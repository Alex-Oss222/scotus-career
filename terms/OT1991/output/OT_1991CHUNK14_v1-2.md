## Decisions and Dispositions

### International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee, No. 91-155

Plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 25, 1992. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Second Circuit, [925 F.2d 576 (1991)](https://static.case.law/f2d/925/cases/0576-01.json). The District Court, 721 F.Supp. 572, granted declaratory and injunctive relief under §1983 against the Port Authority's terminal restrictions. The Second Circuit reversed as to solicitation and affirmed as to distribution. ISKCON seeks restoration of relief against solicitation. The questions are whether the publicly accessible terminal areas are public forums and whether continuous or repetitive solicitation and receipt of funds can be prohibited there. Private airline-leased areas are outside the remaining case. The separately decided companion, Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc., No. 91-339, resolves distribution on this same date; neither decision has priority over the other.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision. Thomas took his oath on October 23, 1991, before argument.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the judgment sustaining the solicitation-and-receipt provision as applied to continuous or repetitive personal requests coupled with immediate payment.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous or repetitive solicitation and immediate personal receipt of funds in the Authority's controlled terminal interiors | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Blackmun, Stevens, Souter | The Second Circuit's denial of declaratory and injunctive relief against this limited prohibition remains; no additional injunction or remand. |

Requests for later support without immediate payment remain outside the sustained construction. The judgment extends no protection for viewpoint discrimination and does not resolve distribution relief.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Judgment without a controlling validity rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Six Justices—Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas—affirm the denial of relief against continuous or repetitive solicitation and immediate personal receipt of funds in the Authority's terminal interiors. The judgment binds these parties within that construction. White's nonpublic-forum reasonableness opinion has four joins including its author. Stone-Zsela and Kennedy separately apply the more protective public-forum time, place and manner standard to the immediate-payment rule; they do not join the nonpublic classification. No five Justices adopt the same validity rationale or forum classification.

**Authority and limits:** Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, permits identification of an actual narrower judgment-supporting ground; it does not combine votes for different grounds into an opinion. Here the nonpublic rationale depends on a different legal classification and demands reasonableness, whereas the other two writings apply Ward's significant-interest, tailoring and alternatives requirements. Their justification predicates and rules are not demonstrated logical subsets simply because one standard is more demanding. Stone-Zsela also expressly reserves classification, while Kennedy classifies the common areas as public. The limited affirmance does not establish either classification or a general public-forum permission to prohibit immediate collections. No controlling alternative merits holding is extracted.

**Limits and issues not reached by a controlling Court rationale.** Neither the general status of airports nor the constitutionality of broader donation appeals, literature distribution, literature sales, airline-leased areas, or secure operational areas is decided by a controlling rationale in this docket. The companion decides its own distribution relief. Religious solicitation remains protected expression under existing law; no new category of unprotected speech is created.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No Supreme Court precedent is overruled or displaced by a controlling rationale. Perry and Cornelius retain their forum framework; this judgment does not settle its airport classification. Ward retains its content-neutral significant-interest, tailoring and alternatives requirements; the Stone-Zsela and Kennedy applications are noncontrolling. Kokinda remains fractured on classification and is not enlarged into a categorical airport rule. Heffron retains its location-sensitive treatment of protected solicitation; Jews for Jesus retains its rejection of an indiscriminate speech ban. Marks is applied to preserve the absence of a common ground. Current Burson, Forsyth County, R.A.V. and Lechmere retain their previously established scopes.

The limited solicitation-and-immediate-receipt restriction survives for these parties from June 26, 1992. The Court adds no reusable airport-classification rule or unified constitutional-validity rationale. Existing forum doctrine, viewpoint neutrality and protected-solicitation law remain in force. White's four-Justice analysis and the separate Ward applications by Stone-Zsela and Kennedy are noncontrolling. The companion independently establishes distribution relief.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, regards the Authority's transport facilities as nonpublic and the immediate-collection rule as a reasonable response to congestion, traveler pressure and monitoring difficulties. Public access and shops do not establish a traditional or deliberately opened forum on that view.

Stone-Zsela assumes public-forum protection and sustains only the conjunctive immediate-payment rule under Ward. Repeated personal transfers interrupt travelers and create operational and fraud concerns. Advocacy and requests for later support inside remain essential alternatives, supplemented by outside immediate solicitation; his analysis does not assume connecting passengers can conveniently reach the sidewalk. He reserves classification and rejects any extension to all financial appeals or distribution.

Kennedy, with Blackmun, Stevens and Souter in Part I, classifies the accessible common areas through their actual physical character, public access and compatibility with expression. Kennedy alone concludes that the narrow immediate-money transaction restriction satisfies Ward while leaving interior advocacy and later-contribution requests available.

O'Connor emphasizes that reasonableness must fit the actual multipurpose environment. She distinguishes immediate monetary collection from passively accepting a leaflet; her classification does not authorize every exclusion.

Souter, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, would invalidate even the immediate-collection ban. On their public-forum view the record does not justify complete interior exclusion where conduct and location rules could address actual obstruction or fraud, and later-payment devices do not adequately replace the live fundraising opportunity.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review of No. 91-155 is complete. Ordinary mandate implementation leaves the Second Circuit's solicitation disposition in effect within the immediate-payment construction sustained here. No new injunction, special remand, retained Supreme Court jurisdiction or special mandate-issuance date is ordered. The separately completed companion supplies the distribution result.

**End of entry: International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee, plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992.**

### United States v. Fordice; Ayers v. Fordice, Nos. 90-1205 and 90-6588

Consolidated plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992

The Court vacates the Fifth Circuit's judgment in both consolidated dockets and remands for further proceedings under Mississippi's continuing duty to dismantle its former legally segregated university system. Argued November 13, 1991. On writs of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Fifth Circuit sitting en banc, [914 F.2d 676 (1990)](https://static.case.law/f2d/914/cases/0676-01.json), which affirmed the District Court's compliance judgment at 674 F.Supp. 1523 (N.D.Miss. 1987). The United States and private plaintiffs challenge the standard for discharging the State's established duty and seek further remedial proceedings; private plaintiffs also seek relief concerning historically black institutions. The Court reviews Equal Protection and the related Title VI duty. The consolidated certiorari grant limits Ayers to its first two presented questions. No entitlement to a particular closure, merger, funding amount or racial quota is established by the questions under review.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision. Thomas took his oath on October 23, 1991, before argument. The full policy-specific and system-wide remand is 8–1: Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support it; Scalia opposes that breadth. All nine agree that the ACT admissions requirements require further review, but Scalia supports only a narrower admission-barrier remand on his own ground.

White writes the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. O'Connor and Thomas each file a separate concurrence without additional joiners. Scalia concurs in the judgment as to further review of discriminatory ACT admissions requirements and dissents from the broader remand; he joins no part of White's opinion. His agreement on admissions supplies no ninth join in the systemic standard.

A State that maintained a legally segregated university system does not discharge its affirmative dismantlement duty merely by adopting good-faith, facially neutral policies. It must eliminate policies traceable to that system that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; the established duty does not require fresh discriminatory intent for each such remnant, and current racial imbalance alone is insufficient.

White's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, eight of nine Justices, controls the Equal Protection rule and the related Title VI disposition in these cases.

The Court holds that opening a university's doors does not necessarily remove the State's old barriers to educational choice. Brown establishes the duty to end legally imposed educational separation. Green requires effective dismantlement rather than reliance on the name given a freedom-of-choice policy. Dowell and the Court's decision in Freeman preserve practical limits and require identification of effects attributable to the established violation. University attendance is voluntary, and differing institutional missions may serve legitimate educational purposes. But the alternatives among which a student chooses remain shaped by admissions requirements, available programs and State-assigned institutional roles. Bazemore does not make those arrangements private choices: its voluntary clubs presented findings that discriminatory practices had ended and choice was unfettered. Mississippi's good faith therefore does not answer whether its inherited machinery still fosters segregation. The Title VI claim follows the same established discrimination duty here. The Court leaves new policies without roots in the dual system to ordinary equal-protection analysis and does not require proportional enrollment or decide unrelated statutory theories.

The Court applies Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, and Brown II, 349 U.S. 294; university systems share the affirmative duty to dismantle established legal segregation.

The Court applies Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430, to effective dismantlement, with voluntary university attendance and legitimate institutional differences preserved.

The Court applies Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237; good faith and practicable removal of vestiges remain distinct requirements.

The Court applies Freeman v. Pitts, the decision of this Court of March 31, 1992, within its actual causal and practicability limits; its school-decree holding is not replaced or mechanically transplanted.

The Court distinguishes Bazemore v. Friday, 478 U.S. 385; genuinely unfettered voluntary club choice does not establish removal of inherited university constraints.

Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp., 429 U.S. 252, remains in force for a new violation involving policies not rooted in the established dual system.

The State bears the burden of demonstrating that it has dismantled its established dual system; a court may not shift that burden to plaintiffs by demanding that they disprove discharge. The court must examine the current effects and practical alternatives of challenged inherited policies, including their interaction, and must tie any remedy to the violation rather than infer liability or relief from racial identifiability alone.

The same eight-Justice opinion of the Court adopts the burden, combined-effects inquiry and violation-linked remedial boundary.

The Court holds that a finding of current good intentions cannot substitute for proof of completed dismantlement. Brown II places responsibility for overcoming the established unlawful system on the State. Green requires examination of whether the chosen measures actually remove its operation, and Swann ties remedial authority to the constitutional wrong. Here the District Court identified substantial unnecessary duplication yet relied on an asserted absence of plaintiffs' proof that changing it would affect enrollment. That approach reverses the burden and isolates a policy that may reinforce admissions and mission differences. The Court's Freeman decision likewise requires attention to causal and remedial connections rather than treating a finding on one question as an answer to all others. Undisturbed historical findings remain evidence; applying the wrong compliance standard does not itself require retrying every fact. On remand the courts must examine the affected policies together where their effects interact, assess educational justification and practical change, and state the findings supporting relief. Neither general racial balance nor unlimited judicial management follows.

The Court applies Brown II, 349 U.S. 294; the State retains the burden of showing discharge of its established duty.

The Court applies Green, 391 U.S. 430; effectiveness is assessed in actual operation, not by formal policy labels.

The Court applies Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1; the scope of the violation bounds equitable relief.

The Court applies Freeman v. Pitts, the decision of this Court of March 31, 1992; causal burdens, practicable compliance and remedial interdependence are retained.

The Milliken v. Bradley decisions, 418 U.S. 717 and 433 U.S. 267, remain in force; remediation must address the proven violation and its consequences.

Where present admissions requirements derive from deliberately exclusionary criteria and continue segregative effects, the State cannot establish dismantlement merely by attributing their later retention to neutral educational purposes. On this record it must adequately justify the differential thresholds and ACT-only method under the continuing-duty standard or replace the unjustified, practicably removable remnants; the Court does not invalidate every use of the ACT or prescribe a substitute score.

White's eight-Justice Court opinion controls this application. Scalia supplies a ninth vote for further admissions review only on his distinct intentional-admission-barrier ground; he does not join the broader rule.

The Court holds that changing a policy's stated purpose does not by itself remove its discriminatory inheritance. Brown and Green require the State to dismantle the exclusionary system actually established. The ACT requirements began as a means of excluding black applicants from historically white institutions, and the lower findings show continuing differences in automatic admission opportunities. The asserted concern about unprepared students does not adequately explain all differences between institutions with comparable undergraduate missions or establish why different missions require these precise thresholds. The refusal to consider school performance alongside test scores also requires more than the general invocation of uneven grading. The record presents an available predictor of success and materially different effects that the courts below did not adequately assess under the State's burden. This conclusion does not replace university admissions expertise with a judicial formula. It requires an explanation adequate to the established remedial duty and examination of practicable alternatives. The State may maintain sound admissions standards that satisfy that duty; no universal ACT prohibition is announced.

The Court applies Brown and Green; inherited discriminatory admission barriers require effective dismantlement rather than a new neutral label.

The Court applies Dowell and the Court's Freeman decision; practical removal and causal attribution govern the continuing compliance inquiry.

The Court applies Florida ex rel. Hawkins v. Board of Control, 350 U.S. 413, to equal access to higher education; no new test-score formula follows.

The corrected inquiry must examine unnecessary program duplication, inherited mission assignments, continued institutional organization, and other properly challenged features including affected funding, with their combined segregative effects and educational justifications. Neither racial identifiability nor fiscal inefficiency alone requires closing or merging a university, and improved funding alone cannot establish dismantlement while unlawful barriers remain.

White's opinion, joined by the same seven Justices, controls the full remand in both dockets; Scalia dissents from this breadth.

The Court holds that the State must examine the structure through which its universities offer educational opportunity. Green supplies the demand for effective removal of the former system, and Swann and Milliken limit remedies to its established consequences. Unnecessary duplication can preserve separate pathways, and mission assignments that carry forward the former institutions' unequal roles may reinforce admissions barriers. Maintaining all eight institutions must also be examined where that organization perpetuates the dual system; expense alone does not resolve the constitutional issue. The present record does not establish that closing any school is required, and changes to admissions or programs may make closure unnecessary. Historically black institutions' educational value and students' interests remain relevant to sound remedial design. The Court rejects an entitlement to additional funding merely to maintain publicly financed separate enclaves, but leaves funding needed for actual dismantlement for remand. The Alabama State Teachers Association summary affirmance concerned an educationally justified new campus; it did not approve every inherited policy. Concrete remedies must follow the corrected findings, not precede them.

The Court applies Green and Swann to effective, violation-linked structural relief rather than a numerical enrollment command.

The Court applies Milliken, 418 U.S. 717, and 433 U.S. 267, to connect practical educational relief to proven consequences.

The Court confines Alabama State Teachers Association v. Alabama Public School and College Authority, 393 U.S. 400, to the issues necessarily affirmed concerning the justified new campus; no blanket university-compliance safe harbor is recognized.

Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, the decision of this Court of January 15, 1992, remains intact in its consent-decree-modification setting; it supplies no different liability or automatic termination standard here.

No precedent is overruled. The Court creates neither a general Title VI disparate-impact cause of action nor a heightened standard of proof. Ordinary intent requirements remain for a new policy outside the established dual system. Freeman's separate disposition concerning school supervision remains intact.

Effective June 26, 1992, Mississippi must establish actual dismantlement by addressing the challenged policies' roots in the former dual system, present segregative effects, educational justification and practicable cure. Neutral wording and good faith alone do not discharge the established affirmative duty in higher education. The State's existing burden continues. Neither racial identifiability nor every difference in funding, programs or admissions establishes a violation. Properly challenged inherited policies must be reassessed with their relevant interactions, subject to causation and educational practicality. Equal Protection and the related Title VI disposition share that duty; the Court establishes no specific closure, merger, funding award or racial assignment rule.

O'Connor joins the Court and separately emphasizes careful examination of the State's claimed educational reasons. On her view, where an essential remnant must remain, the State must counteract and minimize its segregative effects as far as possible. Her additional formulation does not replace the Court's stated rule.

Thomas joins the Court and separately stresses policy-specific liability and sound education. He views tainted origins, continued adverse effect and lack of adequate justification as supporting the inference that the prior discriminatory purpose has not been overcome. He stresses that historically black institutions may have independent educational value and traditions; the decision neither commands their destruction nor guarantees their continued racial identifiability. His intent formulation is not an additional Court test.

Scalia agrees that discriminatory admissions barriers must be removed and that Mississippi has not adequately justified continued ACT requirements. He opposes the broader remand, reading Bazemore to make truly nondiscriminatory access sufficient in voluntary higher education. He objects that the wider effects inquiry gives uncertain guidance and may pressure the State to reduce institutional diversity. He rejects a constitutional entitlement to funding equality while preserving the State's freedom to support lawful institutions.

The cases return through the Fifth Circuit for further proceedings. The courts below must examine traceable policies' current segregative effects, educational justification, practical elimination and interaction, including differential admissions, unnecessary duplication, mission assignments, continued institutional organization, and affected funding and other challenged features. Existing factual findings are not set aside merely because the governing legal standard was wrong. If the corrected inquiry establishes an undischarged obligation, the State must be adjudged in violation of Equal Protection and the related Title VI duty, followed by appropriate remedial proceedings. The Court orders no campus closure, merger, fixed funding award, compulsory assignment or numerical racial balance. Supreme Court merits review is complete in both dockets; the Court retains no supervisory docket and sets no special mandate date or further hearing. No separate Ayers event remains unresolved.

**End of entry: United States v. Fordice; Ayers v. Fordice, consolidated plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992.**

### Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., No. 91-971

Merits decision, June 26, 1992

The Court affirms the Fifth Circuit's judgment on the reviewed secondary-meaning question, 9–0. Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision and all support affirmance.

Argued April 21, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, [932 F.2d 1113 (1991)](https://static.case.law/f2d/932/html/1113-01.html), affirming the Southern District of Texas infringement judgment. The granted question is whether inherently distinctive restaurant trade dress—the restaurant presentation that identifies its source—may receive protection under the applicable pre-1988 Lanham Act §43(a) without acquired secondary meaning, meaning public association with a source. Petitioner seeks reversal because the jury found no secondary meaning. The separate challenge to the nonfunctionality finding is outside the grant.

White delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Scalia also files a concurrence without additional joiners. Stevens and Thomas each concur in the judgment in separate writings without joiners; neither joins White's opinion. All writings support affirmance.

Under the pre-1988 Lanham Act §43(a), nonfunctional restaurant trade dress that is inherently distinctive may receive protection without proof of acquired secondary meaning, provided the remaining infringement requirements, including likelihood of confusion, are met. Protection does not expire after an unspecified period merely because secondary meaning has not developed.

White's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies seven votes at this level of generality. Stevens and Thomas agree with the judgment on separate statutory accounts; the Court's rule does not depend on combining their writings.

The Court holds that a source identifier need not acquire distinctiveness twice. Inwood distinguishes acquired public association from functionality and recognizes the broader protection available under §43(a). Park 'N Fly explains the statutory distinction between descriptive marks requiring acquired meaning and inherently distinctive marks. The same source-identifying distinction applies to the nonfunctional restaurant presentation accepted here. The jury found inherent distinctiveness and likely confusion, yet found no acquired secondary meaning; those findings concern different propositions and are not contradictory. Chevron Chemical properly applies the familiar Abercrombie categories to trade dress, whereas a universal secondary-meaning requirement has no basis in the operative statute. Requiring an initial period of unprotected copying would obstruct source identification and goodwill. The concern that protection might exhaust useful designs is answered by the independent requirements of distinctiveness, nonfunctionality and confusion. A temporary exemption followed by an unwritten deadline is equally unsupported. The Court accepts the findings for this limited review and leaves the excluded functionality challenge and other forms of product design undecided.

The Court applies Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U.S. 844 (1982) for secondary meaning, functionality and §43(a)'s protection beyond registered marks; its distinct contributory-infringement holding is unchanged.

The Court applies Park 'N Fly, Inc. v. Dollar Park & Fly, Inc., 469 U.S. 189 (1985) for the statutory distinction between inherent and acquired distinctiveness and protection of source-identifying goodwill; no registration requirement is imported into §43(a).

Kellogg Co. v. National Biscuit Co., 305 U.S. 111 (1938), retains its concept of acquired source significance; it does not impose secondary meaning on accepted inherently distinctive restaurant dress.

The Court uses the classification in Abercrombie & Fitch Co. v. Hunting World, Inc., 537 F.2d 4 (2d Cir. 1976), to distinguish inherent from acquired distinctiveness; this circuit authority is not represented as an earlier Supreme Court trade-dress holding.

The Court approves Chevron Chemical Co. v. Voluntary Purchasing Groups, Inc., 659 F.2d 695 (5th Cir. 1981), on the presented question concerning inherently distinctive trade dress.

The Court rejects Vibrant Sales, Inc. v. New Body Boutique, Inc., 652 F.2d 299 (2d Cir. 1981), and the contrary categorical reading of the Second Circuit trade-dress cases insofar as they require acquired secondary meaning despite accepted inherent distinctiveness; their distinct functionality applications are not decided.

The accepted jury findings are not retried. No ownership of Mexican cuisine, a general decorative style, useful designs or generic matter follows. The Court decides no categorical rule for other forms of product design, no excluded functionality issue, no new burden of proof and no damages amount. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

Effective June 26, 1992, inherent distinctiveness supplies an alternative to acquired secondary meaning for this nonfunctional restaurant trade dress under the applicable §43(a). The Court resolves the circuit disagreement by rejecting a categorical additional acquired-meaning requirement and an unwritten expiration period. The ordinary civil infringement burden, independent confusion requirement, functionality limitation and remedies outside this review remain unchanged.

Stevens supports the rule through the developed judicial construction of §43(a), its statutory purposes and Congress's subsequent endorsement; he does not regard the original text as having fully specified that development. Thomas supports the same result through §43(a)'s own false-description language and common-law source-identification rules, without importing registration provisions. Scalia finds Thomas's explanation complementary to the Court's opinion. These methodological differences do not limit the seven-Justice holding.

Supreme Court merits review ends with affirmance. The reviewed infringement judgment remains effective. The Court orders no new damages calculation, new remedy or merits remand, and establishes no further nonroutine Supreme Court action or remand condition. The excluded functionality objection and the independently resolved trade-secret and licensing issues are not decided or reopened.

**End of entry: Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., merits decision, June 26, 1992.**

### Franklin v. Massachusetts, No. 91-1502

Merits decision, June 26, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 21, 1992. On direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for Massachusetts, [785 F. Supp. 230 (1992)](https://static.case.law/f-supp/785/html/0230-01.html), which set aside the inclusion of overseas federal employees in state apportionment counts and ordered corrective relief. Massachusetts and its voters challenge that inclusion, the Secretary's residence methodology and the explanation for using home-of-record data after cancellation of a proposed survey. The questions concern standing, review of the Secretary's completed decision under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), constitutional and statutory counting authority, and review for arbitrary or capricious administrative action.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court reverses the District Court's judgment and dissolves its corrective relief.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operative judgment and constitutional claim requiring exclusion of overseas personnel | Reversed; constitutional exclusion claim rejected, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | No Justice opposes | Corrective relief dissolved; no replacement allocation or injunction against the President |
| Review of the Secretary's completed action and statutory and APA merits | Review available; statutory objections and arbitrary-or-capricious challenge rejected by five Justices | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter | White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas oppose APA review and would reach reversal on that threshold ground; they do not vote that the methodology violates the APA | No further factual proceedings or new allocation required |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III | Stone-Zsela | White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | Supports reversal and rejection of the constitutional exclusion claim | Posture, standing for the inclusion injury and constitutional counting rule; nine Justices |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts IV–V | Stone-Zsela | Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter | Supports reversal after APA review and rejection of statutory and administrative objections | Final agency action in Part IV; statutory and administrative merits in Part V; five Justices |
| Opinion concurring in part and in the judgment | O'Connor | White, Scalia, Thomas | Supports the constitutional rule and reversal; opposes APA review | Would hold the Secretary's report nonfinal and the President outside the APA |
| Concurrence | Scalia | No other Justice | Supports standing and the constitutional merits | Applies Montana's express declaratory-redress holding while leaving coercive presidential relief unresolved |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Standing for the continuing apportionment injury

**Controlling proposition:** A State and its voters have standing to contest inclusion of an identified population in the operative apportionment when that inclusion costs the State a seat and authoritative relief concerning the responsible census determination would likely correct the allocation through the prescribed statutory sequence. Likely redress here does not require an injunction against the President or a general assumption that persons outside the litigation follow judicial advice.

**Authority:** Parts I–III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices, control. This holding concerns the proved inclusion injury; it does not find that any particular alternative data source would independently give Massachusetts a seat.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this continuing representational injury can be judicially redressed. Massachusetts would receive an additional seat if the challenged overseas population were excluded. The injury therefore rests on the operative allocation, not an abstract objection to governmental procedure. This Court's Montana decision establishes that authoritative declaratory adjudication can probably correct an ongoing apportionment through the prescribed governmental sequence. Lujan requires a concrete injury, causal connection and likely relief at the pertinent evidentiary stage; its absent-agency application did not command a majority and does not supply a categorical bar here. The statutory sequence and the Government's litigation position support the likelihood of correction following an authoritative determination concerning the census. The Court need not presume a power to command presidential discretion. Nor does it assume that a different residence dataset would necessarily produce another seat: that separate causal proposition has not been established. The inclusion injury and its connected legal challenges suffice for the review undertaken here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, this Court: applied for continuing representational injury and likely declaratory redress through the allocation process; the express unanimous holding remains controlling and its reservation of coercive presidential relief remains intact.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, this Court: applied for injury, causation, likely redress and evidentiary demands; the noncontrolling application concerning absent funding agencies is not promoted into law. Its rule for procedure connected to concrete injury does not require certainty that a new method changes the result.

##### The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable

**Controlling proposition:** The Secretary's completed census determination supplies final agency action under APA §704 even though the President later transmits the resulting apportionment statement. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit the counting decision entirely to unreviewable discretion: the duty to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice to the Secretary.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule while agreeing with reversal on their own reviewability ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the official responsible for the count cannot escape review merely because another official transmits its apportionment consequence. Section 141 assigns the census and its completed population figures to the Secretary. Section 2a directs transmission of numbers ascertained under that census and application of the prescribed formula; it does not confer a new presidential choice of the population to count. Abbott Laboratories supports review of a completed administrative determination producing concrete consequences. Waterman involved a statutory presidential judgment over foreign air routes; that independent decision differs from the prescribed transmission here. Possible correction and general executive supervision do not make every completed agency decision tentative. Japan Whaling confirms that broad administrative discretion remains judicially reviewable when governing statutory commands supply a legal standard. The Secretary retains latitude over census form and method, but must count the proper population under the governing law. Review of that completed administrative choice is sufficient. The Court therefore leaves the President's own APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): applied for reviewability and practical completion of an agency determination with concrete consequences; the statutory transmittal here does not postpone finality.
- Chicago & Southern Air Lines, Inc. v. Waterman Steamship Corp., 333 U.S. 103 (1948): distinguished because Congress there required an independent presidential foreign-affairs judgment; its rule for that decision structure is preserved.
- Japan Whaling Assn. v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): applied for review against statutory limits despite broad administrative judgment; methodological latitude is not absence of law to apply.

##### Temporarily absent residents may be included in the constitutional count

**Controlling proposition:** The constitutional command to count persons in each State permits inclusion of federal personnel temporarily overseas whose enduring ties support residence in a particular State. It does not require a literal census-day physical presence rule, and it does not authorize attribution to a State without a supportable residence connection.

**Authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices, controls with nine votes. The constitutional rule does not itself resolve the separate statutory finality or APA explanation questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that temporary government service abroad does not necessarily sever residence in a State. The constitutional text requires an enumeration of the population belonging to each State, but does not define residence exclusively by bodily presence on census day. The first census legislation treated persons temporarily absent from their usual abode as belonging there. That early implementation gives concrete content to the counting command. The challenged personnel retain identified state connections, and the Secretary may reasonably treat their absence on federal assignment as temporary for this purpose. Wesberry and Karcher enforce equality in drawing districts within a State; they do not dictate this answer to who belongs in an interstate population count. Montana likewise recognizes that interstate apportionment must respect the constitutional structure rather than import every intrastate rule unchanged. Home-of-record information is imperfect, but that does not make every supported attribution constitutionally forbidden. The Court decides this record and rejects mandatory exclusion; unsupported connections and different methodological records remain open.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, this Court: applied for the constitutional structure of interstate apportionment and the distinction from intrastate districting; no new tolerance for unequal districts is adopted.
- Wesberry v. Sanders, 376 U.S. 1 (1964): distinguished as an intrastate congressional district-equality rule; its equal-population command remains effective in its own setting.
- Karcher v. Daggett, 462 U.S. 725 (1983): distinguished on the same interstate/intrastate ground; the present residence decision does not excuse avoidable inequality among a State's districts.

##### The statutory and administrative objections fail on this record

**Controlling proposition:** Section 141 permits the supported inclusion of temporarily overseas personnel with enduring state ties, and the Secretary's comparative choice of supplemented home-of-record information is not arbitrary or capricious on this record. The agency must supply a contemporaneous, reasonably discernible explanation connecting its available information and residence objective to its choice; imperfection alone does not require rejection of every available dataset.

**Authority:** Part V of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. The other four Justices do not join the statutory or APA merits determination.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Secretary made a permissible comparative choice and adequately explained it. State Farm requires examination of relevant considerations and a rational connection between the record and the decision. Bowman Transportation permits an explanation whose path is reasonably discernible even if its expression is imperfect. Cancellation of the proposed survey is a serious objection because improved residence information helped motivate the change. But the contemporaneous record also contains assurances of supplementary personnel information and continuing reasons to recognize temporarily absent residents. Home of record can identify an enduring connection; legal residence may instead reflect taxation, and last duty station may identify employment rather than home. The Secretary could weigh those comparative defects under the statutory counting responsibility. The Court relies on the recorded reasons, not later declarations or a rationale invented by counsel. It does not demand a perfect dataset unavailable to the agency. Nor does it excuse persistence when an agency's only factual premise collapses. Different methodological challenges require their own records.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): applied for consideration of relevant factors, reasoned explanation and review on the agency's actual rationale; no exemption from review accompanies census discretion.
- Bowman Transportation, Inc. v. Arkansas-Best Freight System, Inc., 419 U.S. 281 (1974): applied for a reasonably discernible decisional path despite imperfect exposition; a court may not substitute a new rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached.** No replacement allocation, judicially selected dataset, injunction against the President, or general APA status for the President is decided. No claim that another residence method necessarily restores a seat is found proved. The statutory and administrative holdings are record-specific applications; no earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Effective June 26, 1992, the Secretary's completed census decision is reviewable under APA §704 despite subsequent presidential transmission; the counting obligations supply law for a court to apply. Supported temporarily absent residents may be included in the constitutional count. The statutory and APA merits uphold this inclusion and the home-of-record choice on the contemporaneous explanation. Montana supplies the standing premise, and Lujan applies at the level of its controlling propositions. Waterman remains effective for its distinct structure requiring an independent presidential decision; State Farm and Bowman govern the actual administrative record. The Court creates no presidential-immunity or presidential-APA holding, new evidentiary burden, replacement formula or tolerance for unequal districts.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

O'Connor, joined by White, Scalia and Thomas, views the Secretary's report as an intermediate step because the President transmits the operative apportionment and may seek corrections; they would also exclude the President from the APA's agency definition. They concur in the constitutional rule and reversal but oppose review of the Secretary under §704. Scalia additionally explains that this Court's express Montana standing decision, which he joined, governs likely declaratory redress for the proved inclusion injury; it does not decide whether a court may coerce the President. These positions do not displace the five-Justice APA holdings.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The District Court judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. This judgment orders no replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President. Supreme Court review of these claims ends without a merits remand.

**End of entry: Franklin v. Massachusetts, merits decision, June 26, 1992.**

### Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc., No. 91-339

Plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 25, 1992, with No. 91-155. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Second Circuit, [925 F.2d 576](https://static.case.law/f2d/925/cases/0576-01.json). The Port Authority seeks reversal of the judgment affirming declaratory and injunctive relief against its continuous or repetitive distribution prohibition in publicly accessible, controlled terminal interiors. The District Court had invalidated both solicitation and distribution restrictions; the Second Circuit distinguished them. This case concerns distribution, not an unrestricted privilege to sell or collect immediate payment. The separately decided International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee, No. 91-155, addresses the limited solicitation-and-receipt restriction on the same date; neither companion has priority over the other.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision. Thomas took his oath on October 23, 1991, before argument.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the judgment invalidating the complete literature-distribution prohibition.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Judgment without a controlling invalidity rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter affirm the invalidation of the Authority's complete literature-distribution prohibition. Stone-Zsela applies nonpublic-forum reasonableness on an assumption; O'Connor reaches unreasonableness after classifying the terminals as nonpublic. Kennedy, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter apply public-forum protections. The declaration and injunction bind these parties, but neither forum classification nor one constitutional invalidity rationale receives five joins.

**Authority and limits:** Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, a common result is not enough to create a controlling opinion. The two contextual-reasonableness writings cannot be mechanically combined with the four public-forum votes: a restriction can fail the more protective public-forum test while remaining reasonable in nonpublic property. Conversely, the forum predicates of the published writings are different. No adopted logical-subset rule with majority support is established here. The per curiam expressly preserves the judgment without a unified rationale rather than treating stronger scrutiny as an implicit join in a different test. There is no controlling alternative holding.

**Limits.** The judgment preserves relief against the complete distribution prohibition in the common terminal areas actually at issue. It supplies no categorical license to sell merchandise, solicit immediate personal payment, obstruct circulation, or enter private or secure operational space. Particular viewpoint-neutral conduct and location regulations await their own assessment. No majority doctrine governing sales of literature is established.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Perry and Cornelius remain controlling forum doctrine; no majority airport classification is added. Ward remains the public-forum time, place and manner standard; four Justices' application does not displace nonpublic reasonableness. Schneider's public-street litter rule and Taxpayers for Vincent's distinct treatment of attached signs retain their settings. Jews for Jesus remains authority against a comprehensive airport speech ban; this judgment supplies no broader unified rule. Kokinda's distinction between immediate monetary transactions and taking a leaflet remains relevant through its own published authority, without conversion of its plurality into a majority. Marks is applied to prevent an unsupported synthesis. No precedent is overruled.

From June 26, 1992, the Port Authority remains subject to the declaration and injunction against this complete distribution prohibition. The Court adds no controlling airport classification or reusable constitutional invalidity rationale. Existing forum, reasonableness, viewpoint-neutrality and tailoring rules remain in force. The companion's distinct limited solicitation judgment does not itself validate or invalidate distribution rules.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stone-Zsela finds the ban unreasonable even assuming nonpublic status. A passerby can accept or decline a leaflet without completing a financial exchange. The terminal's ordinary circulation and commercial activities undercut a total claim of incompatibility. Litter, occasional obstruction and the need to distinguish leafletting from solicitation can support appropriate regulation, but on this record do not justify wholesale exclusion. This is contextual reasonableness, not a demand that officials prove every alternative would fail.

O'Connor reaches the same distribution judgment after finding nonpublic status. The Authority's actual multipurpose operation, rather than a statement that transport is its sole purpose, governs the fit of its restriction. She distinguishes the disruption inherent in collecting money from passive distribution.

Kennedy's Part I, joined by Blackmun, Stevens and Souter, treats these accessible common areas as public forums based on physical character, public use and compatibility with expression. On that ground the complete distribution ban lacks adequate tailoring and alternative channels. Souter, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, separately supports that public-forum analysis and this judgment while opposing the solicitation result.

White, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would sustain the distribution prohibition under nonpublic reasonableness. They consider cumulative congestion, conversations prompted by literature, litter and monitoring costs sufficient managerial grounds. They do not equate every leaflet with an immediate-payment transaction; their disagreement concerns how much operational justification reasonableness requires.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review of No. 91-339 is complete. Ordinary mandate leaves the Second Circuit's distribution relief in place. No new injunction term, remedial program, administrative rule, further Supreme Court hearing, retained jurisdiction or special mandate-issuance date is ordered. A future challenge to a particular rule would require its own proceeding. The companion supplies the separate limited solicitation-and-receipt disposition.

**End of entry: Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc., plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992.**

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Controlling proposition:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects the decision to obtain an abortion before viability against prohibition and State-imposed transfer of effective control to another person. The State retains legitimate interests in health and potential life throughout pregnancy and may regulate within the surviving constitutional protections; after viability it may prohibit abortion subject to abortion necessary, in appropriate medical judgment, to preserve the woman's life or health.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “The framework retaining protected choice,” joined at this scope by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Protected-choice foundation and retained boundaries.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Constitution continues to protect the decision whether to end a pregnancy before viability. That protection belongs to the person whose body, health and future the decision concerns. It does not depend on a legislature's willingness to approve her reasons. Before viability, the State may not convert its preference for childbirth into authority to prohibit the protected choice. Nor may it condition care on another person's effective control of that choice. This is a constitutional boundary, not a promise that every regulation affecting abortion is invalid. The separate holdings identify the regulations that this record permits and those that it forbids.

Roe v. Wade supplies the previability protection and the boundary associated with the fetus's capacity for life outside the woman. Doe v. Bolton protects the exercise of medical judgment and rejects unnecessary institutional approval as a condition of care. Together they distinguish a decision protected by liberty from an unrestricted license to practice medicine without standards. A competent patient gives voluntary informed consent, and a qualified clinician evaluates the safety and suitability of treatment. A previability patient need not establish that ending the pregnancy is medically necessary. Clinical appropriateness concerns the care provided, not official permission for her decision. The Court does not adjudicate every postviability restriction in reviewing these particular amendments.

The protection has a constitutional foundation beyond any single arrangement of pregnancy into stages. Griswold v. Connecticut recognizes protection for intimate reproductive decisions. Eisenstadt v. Baird makes clear that the individual does not lose decisional privacy by entering marriage. Carey v. Population Services International applies that protection to reproductive choice while allowing regulation to be evaluated in its actual setting. These authorities answer the contention that the absence of the word abortion from the constitutional text ends the inquiry. Liberty protects decisions concerning bodily integrity and family life even when the text does not list each protected activity. That reasoning does not dispense with identifying the particular protected interest or decide every claim described as personal autonomy.

The State's interest in potential life is real. It may express its preference, support childbirth and provide lawful information about assistance and alternatives. It may protect patients through appropriate professional standards, informed consent and medical regulation. Those interests exist throughout pregnancy. Their existence, however, does not answer whether a particular condition unlawfully interferes with the protected decision. The distinction matters here: accurate medical information and an effective parental alternative raise different questions from compulsory disclosure that gives an adult spouse practical power to prevent care. The Court therefore decides each requirement on its own terms, including its exceptions and actual operation.

Webster v. Reproductive Health Services did not produce a majority replacing Roe's general framework or withdrawing its core protection. The fragmented opinions there cannot be treated as an earlier overruling. This decision likewise identifies agreement by proposition. Six Justices retain the constitutional foundation and the viability and life-and-health boundaries. Their agreement does not make every proposed regulatory formula an opinion of the Court. The dedicated holding on the governing previability standard states the present force of the competing formulations and of earlier regulatory precedents. No fetal-personhood rule, entitlement to public funding, general immunity from medical law or entitlement to admission of a particular drug follows. Those questions are outside this judgment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — The constitutional foundation, previability choice, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection are retained; regulatory implementation is addressed separately.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 — Protection of individualized clinical judgment remains; no new comprehensive immunity is adopted.
- Griswold v. Connecticut, 381 U.S. 479 — Applied for constitutional protection of intimate reproductive decisions.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird, 405 U.S. 438 — Applied to the individual's reproductive choice, including within marriage.
- Carey v. Population Services International, 431 U.S. 678 — Applied for protected reproductive decision-making without mechanically deciding every abortion regulation.
- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services, 492 U.S. 490 — Its fragmentation is recognized; no earlier majority overruling of Roe is inferred.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Controlling proposition:** Lower courts must apply the retained constitutional protection and the direct, provision-specific holdings of this decision, together with earlier rules not displaced here. Roe’s trimester timetable no longer determines when health and potential-life regulation may begin, but neither Stone’s complete six-step framework nor a general purpose-or-effect substantial-obstacle replacement commands a majority; absence of a substantial obstacle is therefore not a general safe harbor for every lesser burden.

**Authority:** Composite statement of the law of the decision, derived from the named opinion parts and their distinct coalitions below. It is not a separately signed unified framework opinion: protected choice and stare decisis have Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; the timetable change has Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; each provision carries its own express joins. The analysis identifies the force and limits of those actual holdings under Marks without adding a new majority formula.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court’s judgment changes some regulatory rules while retaining the constitutional protection of the decision. Lower courts must begin with the proposition actually at issue. Six Justices retain previability choice and the viability and necessary life-and-health boundaries. A different six reject Roe’s trimester timetable as the rule determining when health and potential-life regulation may begin. That negative holding permits pursuit of those interests throughout pregnancy, subject to independently applicable constitutional limitations. It does not supply the affirmative test for every regulation. The Justices who reject constitutional protection altogether are not counted as joining its retention; the Justices who preserve that protection are not all counted as joining the timetable change.

The direct provision holdings supply the next instruction. The present physician-delivery requirement and fixed waiting interval survive under an expressly limited five-Justice permission: the accepted costs and delays do not establish severe restriction of access on this facial record, and medical responsibility and informed deliberation reasonably support these requirements. The contrary applications of Akron are displaced to that extent. Accurate clinical and ancillary information receives its separately stated protection and limits. The Court does not retain a categorical prohibition of the concise accurate notices merely because they may favor childbirth. Its spousal-notice holding, however, forbids the demonstrated transfer of effective control over the protected decision and the dependent notice-compliance reports. Each reporting and parental component must be applied at its own stated scope.

No complete general formula has five joins. Stone proposes an independently impermissible substantial obstacle to protected, medically appropriate previability care and also a meaningful-burden inquiry requiring State-substantiated justification and fit. Stevens joins the substantive first five steps, not the generalized sixth-step facial rule; agreement there is not agreement with every proposed remedy. Blackmun would retain stricter received scrutiny. Souter does not adopt a comprehensive replacement, and O’Connor retains a severity-first inquiry with a possible compelling justification outside independently forbidden control. Kennedy adopts the specific constitutional boundaries and permissions without a universal scale. White, Scalia and Thomas would replace fundamental-right analysis with rational review, but three votes cannot make that the general rule either.

Marks v. United States permits identification of a controlling narrower ground in an appropriate fractured judgment. It does not permit a court to assemble a new formula from incompatible parts. Stone’s invalidating rule is not a logical subset of O’Connor’s: a meaningful lesser burden can fail his justification requirement while passing her reasonable-relation inquiry, and she may accept a compelling justification he would reject for a substantial obstacle to protected care. Comparing permissions might produce a narrower overlap, but his delivery and waiting votes dissent from the sustaining judgments and cannot supply their controlling rationale. Blackmun’s stricter scrutiny may invalidate regulations Stone would permit; those compatible invalidating writers still lack five votes for a complete rule. O’Connor’s general permission and the three rational-review writers likewise supply only four; Kennedy’s particular permissions cannot be expanded into the missing comprehensive fifth rationale. On the actual sustaining components, five Justices directly join the narrow permission, so no Marks construction is needed to supply their authority.

The general substantial-obstacle formulation is likewise not a Court-wide replacement. Descriptive agreement that a particular obstacle is forbidden does not establish that every regulation below that threshold is valid. No joint opinion supplies a five-Justice general substantial-obstacle replacement. Outside the precise changes announced here, surviving Roe, Doe, Danforth, Akron, Thornburgh and the applicable minors’ precedents retain their actual force. Courts must identify their relevant holding, the challenged obligation, its record and the scope of relief. This decision supplies no universal numerical facial threshold or new proof quantum. Where its competing general approaches do not establish a rule beyond those authorities and the direct holdings, that question remains unresolved; the judgment does not authorize invention of a unified test.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — Core protection retained; the trimester timetable no longer determines when health and potential-life regulation may begin.
- Akron, 462 U.S. 416 — Its contrary physician-delivery and fixed-delay applications are displaced to the extent of the direct permissions here; other actual holdings remain.
- Thornburgh, 476 U.S. 747 — Categorical rejection of these concise accurate ancillary notices because they may favor childbirth is displaced; protection against coercion, distortion and unlawful medical interference remains.
- Doe — Its protection of individualized clinical judgment retains its actual force; limited reporting permissions do not create comprehensive immunity or a general replacement test.
- Danforth — Its informed-consent, anti-veto and reporting holdings remain at their actual scopes and supply the specifically identified principles here.
- Bellotti — Its independent maturity/informed-consent or best-interests authorization safeguards retain their force in the minors’ setting.
- Ashcroft — Its parental-consent and adequate alternative-authorization holding remains; this decision does not replace it with a general abortion formula.
- Hodgson — Its parental-involvement holdings and distinct opinion coalitions retain their actual force; no universal regulatory test is inferred.
- Ohio v. Akron Center — Its actual bypass safeguards and facial-review holding are not generally replaced; Stone’s proposed broader facial formulation is noncontrolling.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — Applied to examine the actual rationales and participating votes; no logical subset supplies a complete majority replacement framework.

##### Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation

**Controlling proposition:** A renewed disagreement with Roe's constitutional conclusion, without a sufficient reason to depart from precedent, does not justify withdrawing its workable protected-choice, viability and life-and-health boundaries. The absence of doctrinal or factual displacement and reliance in reproductive planning support retaining that foundation, while a specific defect in a separable regulatory rule may justify changing that rule without overruling the foundation.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation,” joined at this scope by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Ordinary precedent grounds for retaining the foundation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court retains Roe's constitutional foundation because the grounds offered for abandoning it do not justify withdrawing an established liberty. Stare decisis means that a decided legal question carries weight when the Court is asked to decide it again. It does not make every earlier sentence permanent. It requires the Court to identify what rule it would change, why that rule can no longer fairly govern, and what would be disturbed by the change. Mere renewed disagreement with the original decision does not perform that work. A change in the membership of the Court supplies no substitute for a legal reason.

The protected-choice boundary remains workable. Courts can distinguish a prohibition of previability abortion from regulation of the conditions under which lawful care is provided. They can also distinguish regulation from the delegation of practical control over a competent adult's decision. Hard applications do not establish that the boundary has ceased to function. The disagreements in this case concern particular disclosures, delivery arrangements, delays and reporting duties. Those disagreements call for careful identification of the applicable rule; they do not show that constitutional protection of the decision itself is incapable of judicial application.

No intervening controlling decision has removed the foundation on which Roe rests. Griswold, Eisenstadt and Carey continue to protect reproductive decisions and individual autonomy in their respective settings. Webster did not yield a majority overruling Roe's central holding. The parental-involvement decisions recognize distinct interests concerning minors and evaluate the adequacy of an independent alternative; they do not confer comparable authority on an adult spouse. Treating those decisions as a silent repudiation of the underlying liberty would confuse a permitted regulation with abandonment of the interest it regulates. The Court therefore rejects the argument that precedent has already accomplished the overruling now requested.

Nor do the medical facts offered here eliminate the significance of viability or the need to protect necessary care. Medical developments may matter to the justification for a particular clinical regulation and to the suitability of an earlier mechanical rule. They do not establish that the patient's protected decision has become a matter of unrestricted legislative choice. Roe's implementation and its constitutional foundation are separable questions. The Court's precise treatment of trimester machinery, required information, physician delivery and waiting appears in the governing-standard and provision holdings. The reasoning for retaining the foundation does not silently resolve the different coalitions on those questions.

Reliance also extends beyond transactions documented by a contract or a purchased asset. People organize relationships, education, work and family life against a settled ability to make reproductive decisions and obtain lawful care. The Court need not invent a numerical estimate of that reliance to recognize its legal importance. This is not a guarantee that every existing clinical or regulatory arrangement will remain unchanged. An alteration must receive its own analysis of the rule affected, its justification and the resulting remedy. The Court's actual decisions in Hilton and Quill illustrate that adherence and correction require attention to the particular proposition and setting; their statutory and commercial contexts do not dictate the abortion result.

These considerations are sufficient. The Court adopts no independent rule giving a decision extra authority merely because public disagreement is intense, and no rule insulating constitutional error to preserve institutional standing. The obligation is to give a principled account of continuity and change. Here, the arguments for abandoning the protected-choice foundation do not establish unworkability, doctrinal displacement or a factual transformation sufficient to overcome the reasons for retaining it. The specific regulatory holdings remain subject to their separately stated reasoning and limits.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — Retained at the protected-choice, viability and necessary life-and-health boundaries; separable implementation receives separate treatment.
- Griswold — Their reproductive-liberty premises remain in force and support continuity rather than doctrinal displacement.
- Eisenstadt — Their reproductive-liberty premises remain in force and support continuity rather than doctrinal displacement.
- Carey — Their reproductive-liberty premises remain in force and support continuity rather than doctrinal displacement.
- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services, 492 U.S. 490 — Distinguished from an overruling of Roe's foundation; no majority did so.
- Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission, OT1991, December 16, 1991 — This Term’s decision illustrates proposition-specific statutory precedent analysis; it does not dictate constitutional adherence here.
- Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, OT1991, May 26, 1992 — This Term’s decision illustrates separable precedent treatment and setting-specific reliance; its commercial and congressional considerations are not transplanted as abortion rules.

##### The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation

**Controlling proposition:** Roe’s trimester timetable no longer determines when health and potential-life regulation may begin; a regulation is not invalid solely because it operates in the first trimester. The State may pursue those interests throughout pregnancy, subject to independently applicable constitutional limitations.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, part “The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation,” joined at this scope by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 6 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Operative timing change, severed from constitutional-foundation and complete-framework questions.

**Controlling explanation:** Roe supplied a timetable that restricted when maternal-health and potential-life interests could support regulation. The Court removes that timetable as an independent constitutional barrier. It does not hold that every regulation is valid, or that every interest automatically outweighs an independently protected liberty. Danforth's approval of voluntary and informed consent already showed that the first trimester did not exclude every lawful medical requirement. The present change is broader and express: timing alone no longer prevents pursuit of maternal health and potential life throughout pregnancy. The validity of a particular law must therefore be decided under the constitutional rules applicable to that law, without treating its first-trimester operation as sufficient to invalidate it. This proposition neither identifies a complete substitute test nor joins every rationale offered for retaining or rejecting Roe's constitutional foundation. It states the point of operative agreement and leaves other disagreements to their actual opinion and proposition coalitions.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — Its timetable no longer determines when health and potential-life regulation may begin; retained constitutional boundaries have their separate coalition.
- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 — Its consent permission shows that first-trimester care was never immune from every lawful medical rule; the present timing change is broader and express.

##### Medical emergency and timely clinical judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3203 survives the present facial challenge under the protective construction that compliance with the Act may not pose a significant threat to the woman's life or health. The physician may act on timely, good-faith clinical judgment before the danger becomes catastrophic, including in the disputed preeclampsia, inevitable-abortion and premature-membrane-rupture circumstances.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Medical emergency and timely clinical judgment,” joined at this scope by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Prospective statutory construction only; no independent vagueness holding.

**Statutory terms:** §3203 covers a condition which, on the physician's good-faith clinical judgment, necessitates immediate abortion to avert death or for which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 3205(b) requires the physician, if possible before the abortion, to inform the woman of the medical indications supporting the judgment that abortion is necessary to avert her death or to avert substantial and irreversible impairment of major bodily function. The separate §3205(c) information-omission defense requires the physician to establish by a preponderance a reasonable belief that disclosure would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health; it is not a general waiting exemption.

**Record and construction:** The three conditions and their medical consequences come from district findings 152–174. Their inclusion through prospective assessment is the Third Circuit's construction, 947 F.2d 700–701, adopted here; it is not an earlier authoritative Pennsylvania state-court holding. No new patient diagnosis is found. See [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the definition as construed, rather than treating the words as a command to wait for death or irreversible injury to become imminent. The district court identified conditions in which prompt care could be necessary before that point. The Third Circuit read serious risk prospectively and concluded that the legislature intended compliance to cause no significant threat to life or health. That reading answers the concrete medical objection presented here. It permits the physician to assess the developing condition through good-faith clinical judgment and encompasses the three disputed diagnoses without postponing treatment until shock, infection or permanent harm occurs.

Webster illustrates attention to the actual clinical duty when construing a medical requirement. Here, the accepted prospective construction supplies the answer; this permission adopts no comprehensive constitutional medical-usefulness test. The disclosure-specific defense does different work: it protects certain omissions of information, not delay of necessary care. The present judgment neither approves a contrary enforcement construction nor decides every possible degree of health risk. A standalone vagueness theory and its disputed preservation are not independently decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services, 492 U.S. 490 — Applied for attention to the actual clinical obligation when construing a medical requirement; its disparate broader frameworks are not merged.
- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — Their retained necessary-life-and-health protection and clinical-judgment principles support the protected-choice coalition; a Justice who accepts only the statutory construction does not thereby join every constitutional premise.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 — Their retained necessary-life-and-health protection and clinical-judgment principles support the protected-choice coalition; a Justice who accepts only the statutory construction does not thereby join every constitutional premise.

##### Accurate material medical information, including gestational age

**Controlling proposition:** The State may require accurate, nonmisleading information material to voluntary informed consent about the proposed procedure, relevant risks and alternatives, probable gestational age, and the medical risks of continuing pregnancy. Sustaining that content does not by itself sustain a particular speaker requirement or a fixed delay.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Accurate material medical information, including gestational age,” joined at this scope by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Accurate medical content, separated from its delivery and timing.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3205(a)(1)(i)–(iii) requires the performing or referring physician orally to provide procedure or treatment information, risks and alternatives a reasonable patient would consider material, probable gestational age when the abortion will occur, and childbirth medical risks, at least 24 hours earlier. The medical emergency defined in §3203 excuses the ordinary process: good-faith clinical judgment finds immediate abortion necessary to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 3205(c) separately protects omitted information on the physician's preponderance showing of reasonable belief in severely adverse physical or mental-health consequences. Delivery and timing receive separate judgments.

**Record and construction:** District findings 185–189 describe patient-specific informed consent and the inadequacy of printed or recorded information alone; findings 204–213 describe qualified counselors and existing physician contact. Those findings do not enact a universal physician-only rule. See [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes the information a patient receives from the arrangements used to deliver it. A decision can be voluntary and informed while the State identifies facts that a reasonable patient would consider material. Danforth permits written informed consent; it does not make the physician's preferred description the exclusive permissible means of informing the patient. Procedure risks, reasonable alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks can bear directly on the choice and on the care provided.

The district's findings support individualized discussion rather than the substitution of a pamphlet for clinical judgment. They do not establish that these medical subjects are irrelevant or that truthful information itself compels a different decision. The patient remains free to choose, and the clinician may explain qualifications relevant to her circumstances. The holding approves accurate material content, not falsehood, ideological assent or a guarantee that every form of communication is appropriate. The physician-delivery and waiting requirements are separately adjudicated, and the statute's emergency protection and information-omission defense retain their distinct functions.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 — Applied for permissible informed-consent requirements while retaining the patient's decision.
- Akron v. Akron Center, 462 U.S. 416 — They do not compel invalidation of accurate material medical content merely because the State specifies it; delivery, timing and coercion questions remain separately controlled.
- Thornburgh v. American College, 476 U.S. 747 — They do not compel invalidation of accurate material medical content merely because the State specifies it; delivery, timing and coercion questions remain separately controlled.

##### Notice that free state information is available

**Controlling proposition:** The State may require a concise, accurate notice that it publishes information describing fetal development and agencies offering alternatives, that the patient has a right to review it, and that a copy is free if she chooses. The notice does not require endorsement of an agency, acceptance of a pamphlet, reading it or agreement with a governmental viewpoint.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Notice that free state information is available,” joined at this scope by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 8 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Concise factual availability notice.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3205(a)(2)(i) gives this notice duty to the performing or referring physician, or a qualified physician assistant, health-care practitioner, technician or social worker expressly delegated responsibility by either physician, at least 24 hours earlier. The timing question is separately adjudicated. The ordinary process is excused by §3203 medical emergency: good-faith clinical judgment finds immediate abortion necessary to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Under §3205(c), omitted information is protected on a physician's preponderance showing of reasonable belief disclosure would severely adversely affect physical or mental health.

**Record and construction:** District findings 181–184 identify omitted agencies and some listed agencies supplying misinformation or inflammatory material, while allowing clinicians to recommend avoiding a listed agency, recommend another and correct limitations. They do not find every state statement false. See [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the notice of availability as a distinct obligation. Knowing that information and assistance can be obtained may help a patient evaluate the available choices. The notice communicates that the State publishes materials and that the patient may obtain them without charge; it does not require the clinician to endorse every agency or the patient to accept the materials. Danforth permits informed-consent administration, and does not transfer the patient's decision.

The directory findings require care in describing what has been approved. Some agencies were omitted and some listed organizations supplied misinformation. Those facts do not make the fact of availability false, nor do they establish that every printed statement is inaccurate. Clinicians may explain defects and recommend other sources. Akron and Thornburgh do not require categorical invalidation of a truthful availability notice merely because the information may favor childbirth. This holding does not adjudicate every particular pamphlet as accurate, resolve a distinct compulsory-distribution challenge by label, or merge notice content with the separately decided timing requirement.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 — Applied to factual informed-consent administration without transferring the decision.

##### Notice of potentially available medical assistance

**Controlling proposition:** The State may require accurate notice that medical assistance benefits may be available for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care, with more detailed information available in the state materials. **Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Notice of potentially available medical assistance,” joined at this scope by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 8 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Conditional assistance information.

##### Lawful publication and the patient’s choice to receive materials

**Controlling proposition:** The State may publish lawful, accurate information concerning pregnancy, childbirth, assistance and alternatives, and a patient may choose to request and receive it. **Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Lawful publication and the patient’s choice to receive materials,” joined at this scope by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. **Statutory terms:** Section 3205(a)(2)(i) describes the woman's right to review the State's printed materials and obtain a free copy if she chooses; §3205(a)(3) supplies a copy if she chooses to view it. The permission to publish and elect receipt is separate from the adjudication of compulsory clinician duties, delivery timing and particular challenged content. This limited permission neither certifies the accuracy of every existing document nor immunizes a required false or ideological communication. The statutory accuracy standards and the trial's specific directory findings retain their separate roles. Disagreement over a mandatory notice or delivery requirement does not make lawful publication itself forbidden. Conversely, recognition that the State may publish does not resolve those compulsory obligations. ##### Physician delivery of the specified medical information

**Controlling proposition:** Pennsylvania may require the performing or referring physician to deliver orally the accurate medical information specified in section 3205(a)(1): the procedure and its risks and alternatives material to a reasonable patient, probable gestational age, and the medical risks of carrying the pregnancy to term. This permission concerns the designated speaker and the present facial record; the fixed timing receives a separate holding.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, part “Physician delivery of the specified medical information,” joined at this scope by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 5 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Sufficient permission for this physician responsibility on this facial record.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3203 means a condition which, on the physician's good-faith clinical judgment, necessitates immediate abortion to avert death or for which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of major bodily function. The prospective construction covers preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and premature membrane rupture at onset without waiting for irreversible injury. Under section 3205(c), no physician is guilty for failing to furnish subsection (a) information if the physician demonstrates by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have resulted in a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. **Record and construction:** District findings 185–189 and 204–213 support patient-specific dialogue, counselor competence, ordinary physician contact and additional physician-delivery costs. The record establishes that qualified counselors can communicate effectively and that requiring earlier physician contact adds expense and scheduling difficulties. The Court accepts those findings. They do not establish severe restriction of access on this facial record, and the State may reasonably connect personal physician delivery to medical responsibility. This conclusion does not depend on finding counselors incompetent or the physician requirement medically indispensable. Akron's categorical rejection of physician-only delivery does not govern this requirement after today's change. **Controlling proposition:** The First Amendment does not facially invalidate the concise, accurate notices of available materials, potentially available assistance and paternal legal responsibility as limited information about the patient’s options. ##### One-parent informed consent and independent judicial authorization

**Controlling proposition:** A State may provide for one-parent informed consent for an unemancipated minor's abortion when an effective confidential and expeditious judicial alternative independently authorizes care upon maturity, capacity and actual informed consent, or upon best interests. The minor need not first ask a parent, and necessary emergency care cannot await either route.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “One-parent informed consent and independent judicial authorization,” joined at this scope by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. White, Scalia and Thomas sustain the architecture on their separately stated regulatory grounds; the basic judgment is unanimous, but their votes do not adopt the constitutional necessity of the safeguards.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3206 applies to unemancipated minors under 18; its separate adjudged-incompetent guardian rule is not independently adjudicated. In the minor-consent route the physician first obtains informed consent from both the pregnant minor and one parent. A parent or guardian considers only the child's or ward's best interests. When pregnancy results from incest and the father is a party to the incestuous act, only the mother's parental consent is needed; the minor's own informed consent remains required. If both parents have died or are otherwise unavailable to the physician within a reasonable time and in a reasonable manner, guardian consent suffices; if parents are divorced, custodial-parent consent suffices; if neither parent nor legal guardian is available to the physician within a reasonable time and in a reasonable manner, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. The minor may elect judicial authorization without first seeking parental consent, in the court of common pleas of the judicial district where she resides or where abortion is sought. Maturity/capacity and actual informed consent, or alternatively best interests, authorize care. Counsel is supplied unless she chooses private counsel or knowingly/intelligently waives representation; proceedings and records are confidential, her name is excluded from a public docket, the trial court must rule within 3 business days of application and the appellate court within 5 business days after filing of the appeal. Section 3203 medical emergency excuses: good-faith clinical judgment finds immediate abortion necessary to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 3206(f) requires precedence over other pending matters to ensure a prompt decision, written specific factual findings and legal conclusions, and an order sealing the entire case record when the petition is initially filed. Hearings exclude everyone except the applicant and persons whose presence she or her guardian specifically requests. Under §3206(i), the penalty requires knowledge that the patient is a covered minor or incompetent, or reckless disregard or negligence as to that status, together with intentional, knowing or reckless failure to conform to a requirement of the section. **Record and construction:** The statutory alternatives are legal commands. The record supplies no current failure of bypass courts to function, district finding 266. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court preserves the permissible parental structure and the independent route to judicial authorization. Bellotti requires an alternative that respects a minor's maturity and best interests rather than giving a parent an absolute veto. Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron Center confirm the significance of adequate safeguards when the State seeks parental participation. A parent's responsibility for a minor supplies an interest different from a spouse's relationship to a competent adult.

The alternatives here are genuine legal routes, not cumulative obstacles. A mature applicant who has actually consented need not additionally prove that abortion serves her best interests, and an applicant may proceed through best interests without establishing maturity. The statute permits direct resort to court and supplies counsel, confidentiality and expedition. The current trial record does not establish that the courts are unprepared to operate that process. This holding does not promise that future maladministration is immune from review, require a prior request to a parent, or decide an unpresented adult-incapacity claim. The separate attendance objection receives its own disposition.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bellotti v. Baird, 443 U.S. 622 — Applied for independent maturity/informed-consent or best-interests authorization and effective procedural safeguards.
- Hodgson v. Minnesota, 497 U.S. 417 — Applied for the validity of parental involvement coupled with an adequate bypass, without attributing all separate rationales to the same coalition.
- Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502 — Applied to confidentiality, expedition and actual bypass operation; hypothetical administrative failure alone is insufficient.

Section 3203 medical emergency excuses: good-faith clinical judgment finds immediate abortion necessary to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function.

**Record and construction:** Section 3206 requires informed consent but contains no express wholesale incorporation of every §3205 mechanism. The district court at 744 F. Supp. 1382–1384 uses §3205 information to explain informed-parent content. Additional attendance is its distinct construction/operational finding, reserved by Third Circuit footnote 23. For paternal information §3205(a)(2)(iii) says, “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted,” without a reporting condition; legal liability is not guaranteed collection. This identifies the underlying patient-information provision's terms; it does not invent an express wholesale incorporation of its mechanisms into the parental statute. See [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the ordinary informational content of permissible parental participation. A parent asked to consider only the child's best interests may reasonably receive accurate facts about the proposed procedure, relevant risks, alternatives, gestational age and continuing pregnancy. Accurate information about available assistance, legal support responsibilities and materials can also inform that consideration without requiring adoption of a moral viewpoint.

Bellotti and Hodgson recognize a legitimate role for informed adult assistance. This statutory scheme also supplies an independent authorization route; its constitutional necessity receives a separate holding. Information does not become unlawful solely because it could support a decision to continue pregnancy. The Court nevertheless separates content from the mechanisms used to convey it. The district's use of §3205 to explain informed consent does not insert a parent-specific waiting clock, a nondelegable physician-only task or an express clinic-visit sentence into §3206. Nor does preserving informed parental involvement revive a delivery, timing or content duty independently held invalid. The parental structure, its accurate information, its independent safeguards and the distinct attendance objection must each retain their own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bellotti v. Baird, 443 U.S. 622 — Applied for permissible adult guidance with independent authorization safeguards.
- Hodgson v. Minnesota, 497 U.S. 417 — Applied to the legitimate minor-parent informational relationship without treating its separate opinions as one universal regulatory standard.
- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 — Informed-consent principles support accurate information while the particular duties and veto limits remain separate.

##### The separate parental-attendance objection

**Controlling proposition:** The actual facial record does not warrant an additional focused vacatur, remand or interim order on the parental-attendance objection to the permissible informed-parent and independent-authorization structure. The Court accepts the positive findings about intended attendance and resulting burdens, leaves the reserved Pennsylvania-law interpretation open, and does not hold that every unnecessary visit for an already-consenting parent is cured by offering bypass.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, part “The separate parental-attendance objection,” joined at this scope by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 8 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Limited facial-record and construction ground; no universal curative-bypass rule.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3206 applies to unemancipated minors under 18; its separate adjudged-incompetent guardian rule is not independently adjudicated. In the minor-consent route the physician first obtains informed consent from both the pregnant minor and one parent. A parent or guardian considers only the child's or ward's best interests. When pregnancy results from incest and the father is a party to the incestuous act, only the mother's parental consent is needed; the minor's own informed consent remains required. If both parents have died or are otherwise unavailable to the physician within a reasonable time and in a reasonable manner, guardian consent suffices; if parents are divorced, custodial-parent consent suffices; if neither parent nor legal guardian is available to the physician within a reasonable time and in a reasonable manner, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. The minor may elect judicial authorization without first seeking parental consent, in the court of common pleas of the judicial district where she resides or where abortion is sought. Maturity/capacity and actual informed consent, or alternatively best interests, authorize care. Counsel is supplied unless she chooses private counsel or knowingly/intelligently waives representation; proceedings and records are confidential, her name is excluded from a public docket, the trial court must rule within 3 business days of application and the appellate court within 5 business days after filing of the appeal. Section 3203 medical emergency excuses: good-faith clinical judgment finds immediate abortion necessary to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function.

**Record and construction:** District findings 232, 238–259 describe intended face-to-face practice and the burden when a willing parent cannot or will not attend; district 1382–1384 uses §3205 content to explain informed parental consent. It does not establish express incorporation of every §3205 timing/delivery mechanism. Third Circuit footnote 23 leaves the attendance interpretation undecided. These are findings/constructions, not new statutory words. See [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court accepts the district's findings that a parent may agree yet be unable or unwilling to attend, and that an additional encounter can cause expense and delay. Those findings are not an express statutory visit sentence. The Third Circuit left the state-law interpretation open, and this decision does not turn that reservation into a finding that attendance can never be required.

The requested additional focused vacatur and interim order are not justified on this facial record. The permissible informed-parent structure remains accompanied by a genuine independent route to authorization, with the actual statutory safeguards assessed in light of Bellotti, Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron Center. The record does not establish that this route is unavailable or presently malfunctioning. That conclusion is confined to the request and evidence here. It does not announce that an adequate bypass cures every unjustified demand upon an already-consenting family. An actual application or authoritative construction imposing an unlawful additional condition remains challengeable. Nor does the Court create a parent-specific waiting period or nondelegable physician-delivery command.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hodgson, 497 U.S. 417 — Applied for adequate independent authorization as a safeguard in the distinct parental-involvement setting; not a universal cure for needless attendance.
- Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502 — Applied to actual procedural safeguards and the insufficiency of hypothetical maladministration.
- Bellotti, 443 U.S. 622 — Its independent authorization safeguards remain; no prior parental-request requirement is added.

##### Spousal notice and State-enabled control of protected choice

**Controlling proposition:** The State may encourage voluntary marital consultation, but it may not condition protected abortion care on notice that gives a spouse practical control of the patient's decision in the relationships established by this record. Section 3209 and its dependent enforcement, together with §3214(a)(12)'s notice and nonnotice-reason requirements, are invalid; the holding does not announce a general numerical facial-challenge test or an independent equality or informational-privacy rule.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Spousal notice and State-enabled control of protected choice,” joined at this scope by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Protected-choice prohibition of demonstrated practical spousal control.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3209 requires a signed statement, not notarization, that the married patient notified her spouse; it must warn that a false statement is punishable. A signed, nonnotarized substitute carrying the warning suffices if at least one condition is certified: spouse is not father; diligent effort failed to locate him; pregnancy resulted from §3128 spousal sexual assault reported to a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction; or the woman has reason to believe notice likely will result in bodily injury to her by the spouse or another person. Section 3128(a)–(b), quoted in district 268, covers sexual/deviate intercourse through forcible compulsion, threat of forcible compulsion that would prevent resistance by a person of reasonable resolution or with an unconscious spouse; district 297 identifies the incorporated 90-day reporting restriction. Medical emergency independently excuses: §3203 good-faith clinical judgment requires immediate abortion to avert death or delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of major bodily function. The statute does not generally excuse psychological/economic harm, injury to children/others, publicity or divorce/custody retaliation. Violations constitute unprofessional conduct subject to license suspension/revocation through applicable procedures; Section 3209(e) also imposes civil liability to the spouse who is the father of the aborted child for any damages caused by the knowing violation and punitive damages of $5,000; the court must award a prevailing plaintiff a reasonable attorney fee as part of costs. The rape-information permission in §3205(a)(2)(iii) has no reporting condition and is a different provision.

**Record and construction:** District findings 273–308 establish actual family dynamics, coercion and limitations of the substitute statements. The identification of the affected population and the conclusion that State-required disclosure enables practical control are judicial applications to those findings. No finding that all husbands are abusive, that every married patient is prevented from obtaining care, or that patient names become public is made. See [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/cases/1323-01.json); [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/cases/0682-01.json).

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that marriage does not give the State authority to place a competent adult's protected abortion decision under another person's practical control. The Commonwealth may encourage spouses to discuss pregnancy and may respect a husband's serious interest in potential parenthood. Those interests do not authorize a condition that, on this record, enables him to prevent his wife's exercise of the protected choice. The holding concerns the operation of this compulsory condition, not the value of voluntary consultation or the ordinary responsibilities of family life.

The inquiry begins with the people whose conduct the statute changes. Many married patients already consult their spouses. Their voluntary decisions do not answer the constitutional objection of patients whom the law compels to disclose. Nor may the relevant group be defined simply as everyone who ultimately proves a constitutional injury. The statutory condition and the record identify married patients who would withhold notice and whose access is made dependent on disclosure or an available substitute statement. The evidence about that operation supplies the basis for relief, without inventing a numerical cutoff for all facial challenges.

The trial findings establish more than inconvenience or disagreement. Some husbands use physical confinement, threats, control of money, psychological intimidation or threats concerning children to control a woman's actions. Compelled notice can expose the intended abortion and give that control immediate practical force. The fact that a private person supplies the threat does not remove the State's role. The law makes disclosure a prerequisite to treatment and backs it with consequences for the physician. Without the compulsory condition, the patient could seek care without supplying the information that enables the interference.

The statutory substitutes do not adequately remove this operation. Nonpaternity and inability to locate a spouse after diligent effort address different circumstances. The bodily-injury route concerns likely injury to the woman by the spouse or another person; it does not generally cover financial deprivation, psychological harm or injury threatened to children. The spousal-assault route requires a report to law enforcement and incorporates the 90-day restriction identified in the district findings. Reporting itself may expose the woman to the danger she seeks to avoid. The record also explains why some abused women cannot realistically use a danger certification despite the threats they face. Medical emergency addresses necessary care under a separate clinical standard, not every form of coercive marital control.

Danforth supplies the governing distinction. The State cannot give a spouse an absolute veto over the patient's protected decision. A formal right to sign a refusal is not the only way to create that control. A legally compelled disclosure that predictably enables prevention of care can produce the same prohibited result. The State's strongest response—that notice differs from consent—therefore fails on the actual operation established here. Preserving the woman's decision does not deny a husband's concern; it denies the State power to enforce that concern by transferring control over her body and treatment.

Bellotti, Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron Center concern minors and the distinct responsibilities of parents, with independent authorization safeguards. They do not make an adult spouse a guardian. Eisenstadt confirms that constitutional decisional protection belongs to the individual even within marriage. These principles suffice without a new standalone equality, compelled-speech or general informational-secrecy holding. Section 3209 and dependent enforcement cannot stand. The notice and nonnotice-reason report would demand the same protected explanation and must also fall; separately lawful confidential data and the precise state-law severability consequences remain distinct. The Court preserves voluntary marital communication and decides no broader class of disclosure laws.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 — Applied to State-enabled practical spousal control; the absence of a formal signature veto does not save this record's equivalent obstruction.
- Roe, 410 U.S. 113 — Its retained previability decision supplies the protected interest; no universal replacement scrutiny formula is adopted by this holding.
- Eisenstadt, 405 U.S. 438 — Applied for the individual's reproductive decision, including within marriage; no independent general secrecy rule is announced.
- Bellotti, 443 U.S. 622 — Distinguished as minor-parent cases involving separate parental responsibilities and adequate independent authorization; they do not confer comparable authority over a competent adult spouse.
- Hodgson, 497 U.S. 417 — Distinguished as minor-parent cases involving separate parental responsibilities and adequate independent authorization; they do not confer comparable authority over a competent adult spouse.
- Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502 — Distinguished as minor-parent cases involving separate parental responsibilities and adequate independent authorization; they do not confer comparable authority over a competent adult spouse.

##### Notice-compliance reporting and the severable marital-status field

**Controlling proposition:** The notice-given and nonnotice-reason demands in §3214(a)(12) are invalid as dependent enforcement of unconstitutional spousal notice. The distinct confidential marital-status question survives severance; the injunction must remove the notice-compliance fields and their enforcement while preserving that independently operating statistical question.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, part “Notice-compliance reporting and the severable marital-status field,” joined at this scope by Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct joins, an Opinion of the Court for the stated proposition. Complete notice-field invalidation and independent marital-status severance; Stone additionally joins the notice-invalidity proposition only. Stone also joins invalidation of notice-given/nonnotice-reason reporting, making six for that proposition; he joins preservation of the independent marital-status field in the judgment without adding a ground. White, Scalia and Thomas sustain marital data on their primary intact-report premise and cast no conditional severance vote.

**Statutory terms:** Section 3214(a) bars patient names in these procedure reports. District finding 357, describing §3214(e)(1)–(2), states that the reports are not public records; law-enforcement disclosure requires a Court of Common Pleas order after an application showing good cause, and the Department’s statistical report must not disclose any physician, facility or patient’s identity. These procedure reports differ from the public facility reports.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court separates the three questions within §3214(a)(12). A confidential report whether a patient is married does not itself require her to tell a spouse about the abortion. The further questions whether notice was given and why it was not serve the notice condition that the Court invalidates. Confidentiality does not make enforcement of that condition lawful. Danforth permits health records but does not permit reporting to accomplish an unconstitutional restriction. That limit requires removal of the notice-compliance questions and their dependent enforcement. The broad severability clause in Act 64, section 6, permits separation of provisions, words, phrases and applications when the remainder can operate independently. The marital-status question has an intelligible statistical operation without either notice question. Retaining it therefore severs an unlawful obligation without reconstructing the statute. The Court directs that distinction in the remand mandate. It does not invalidate all marital data or permit officials to demand the removed explanations through a differently labeled form.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 — Its prohibition on restrictive use of reporting is applied to invalid notice enforcement.
- Brockett v. Spokane Arcades, Inc., 472 U.S. 491 — Supports relief confined to the invalid obligation while preserving a legally separable remainder.
- The spousal-notice holding in this decision — Controls the notice-compliance dependency, not independently justified marital statistics.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Effective June 29, 1992, the Court retains protected choice and the viability and necessary life-and-health boundaries. A different coalition removes the trimester timetable as an independent barrier to the timing of regulation. No complete replacement formula commands five votes. Lower courts must apply the direct provision holdings and the earlier authority that those holdings leave in force.

Roe supplies the retained constitutional foundation, while its timetable changes under the separately joined timing holding. Akron’s contrary physician-delivery and fixed-delay applications are displaced for the requirements sustained here. Akron and Thornburgh do not categorically invalidate these concise accurate ancillary notices merely because they may favor childbirth; their distinct protections against coercion, distortion and unlawful medical interference remain. Ohio v. Akron Center’s facial-review formulation is not generally replaced. Marks supplies no comprehensive formula and does not turn dissenting application votes into the controlling rationale of sustaining judgments.

The five-Justice delivery and waiting permissions rest on direct joins. Accurate medical information and concise ancillary notices carry their independent permissions and limits. Spousal notice and notice-compliance reporting are invalid; confidential marital-status data remain after severance. The narrow speech, parental and reporting rules carry their own coalitions. No independent emergency-vagueness, general large-fraction, equality, general marital-privacy or drug-admission holding is adopted. The minority proposals remain noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

##### Stone — Separate opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

**Joins and scope:** Stevens joins the proposed substantive framework in Steps 1–5. The complete six-step replacement and focused remands have Stone alone. The separate application parts below have their expressly identified joins.

Chief Justice Stone joins the retained constitutional foundation and proposes replacing trimester machinery with a six-step undue-burden framework. Roe supplies protected choice, Doe clinical judgment, Danforth protection against transferred control, and Carey reproductive liberty. The proposal identifies the precise obligation and protected care; examines objectively established purpose; defines an affected population independently of the ultimate injury; measures causal and cumulative effects; requires justification and fit; and confines relief to unlawful obligations and applications. Poverty, geography, age, abuse and provider scarcity affect the burden without making every preexisting hardship State-caused.

A substantial obstacle to protected, medically appropriate previability care is independently forbidden. A smaller meaningful burden—concrete, nontrivial interference with access, health, privacy or informed decision-making—also fails for inadequate justification, excess relative to demonstrated benefit, or an unexplained practicable and substantially less burdensome adequate alternative. Challengers establish the obligation, group, causation and burden, or independently prove prohibited purpose; the State then substantiates benefit and fit. Reliable predictions may suffice before actual denial, without double counting or speculation. Opposition to abortion alone does not establish prohibited purpose, and lawful persuasion remains possible.

Necessary action against demonstrated medical danger remains permissible. Professional consensus has substantial evidentiary weight, not a veto; competent contrary evidence matters. Stone requires neither perfect policy, scientific certainty nor a new study for every rule. Ordinary proof and factual review remain, with independent constitutional review. Professionally defensible judgment is not punishable solely because of later disagreement, but malpractice, fraud and misconduct remain actionable. Before viability, clinical appropriateness does not require medical necessity or approval of the patient's reasons.

His sixth step permits facial relief for constitutionally prohibited terms or reliably demonstrated unlawful operation in a large fraction of an independently defined restricted group. It requires explanation of prevalence and inadequate narrower relief, supplies no numerical cutoff, and qualifies Ohio v. Akron Center’s no-valid-applications formulation. Stevens joins the substantive first five steps, without the generalized sixth-step rule. No complete framework receives five joins.

Stone would invalidate fixed waiting, unjustified physician exclusivity, automatic public facility releases and blanket referrer identification. He preserves accurate clinical information, concise accurate ancillary notices, limited confidential medical-basis reports and lawful parental involvement. Paternal-support information may be omitted in rape without police reporting (§3205(a)(2)(iii)); payment is not guaranteed. Materials are elective (§3205(a)(3)). He seeks focused materials and parental-attendance vacaturs and remands, with interim injunctions against disputed distribution and additional attendance; he does not read the statute to impose a visit requirement. Waiting invalidation would remain effective during remand. He joins spousal-notice invalidation and its dependent enforcement, while preserving voluntary consultation and independently lawful severable duties. His proposed remedies do not direct wholesale invalidation of the Act.

##### Blackmun — Separate opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

**Joins and scope:** No other Justice joins the complete separate opinion. Agreement with a particular result elsewhere does not constitute a join in this writing.

Justice Blackmun joins preservation of protected previability choice, the viability boundary, necessary life-and-health care, and the Court's reasons for retaining that constitutional foundation. He would also retain Roe's stronger staged scrutiny. His Webster opinion (1989) explains why changing medical facts warrant examination of particular regulations without abandoning protection of the decision. His Ashcroft opinion (1983) distinguishes genuine health regulation without significant impact from unsupported burdens. An independently forbidden obstacle and scrutiny of lesser burdens can provide minimum protection, but adequate fit under a less demanding formula cannot replace those requirements. He therefore does not join the operative abandonment of the trimester timetable.

Blackmun would invalidate the fixed waiting period and unnecessary exclusive physician delivery. Thornburgh (1986) protects voluntary clinical advice while rejecting compulsory ancillary persuasion. He accordingly would invalidate the three ancillary notices and their incorporation into parental consent. Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) says, “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” That permission carries no police-reporting condition, but does not resolve his objection to the mandatory notice. He accepts medically relevant information, including gestational age, lawful receipt certification, State publication and elective receipt of materials. District findings 181–184 identify directory defects; they do not establish that every published item is false. He opposes the proposed focused materials vacatur and interim distribution injunction.

He joins narrow First Amendment permission for actual clinical disclosures, reserving ancillary speech after due-process invalidation. He sustains the parental provision against a facial challenge premised on an assumed attendance command, subject to severance of the invalid ancillary content. The district's operational finding and the Third Circuit's unresolved construction in footnote 23 do not establish that the statute commands a clinic visit. He therefore opposes a special attendance remand and interim injunction, while reserving a properly presented actual enforced-visit challenge.

Blackmun joins invalidation of spousal notice and dependent notice-reporting fields, retaining confidential marital-status data. Actual coercion and interference with protected choice supply the ground; he does not add an independently sufficient equality, general-privacy or spousal compelled-speech holding. He would invalidate the referral-identity field and automatic public facility disclosures because the record shows burdens inadequately connected to their asserted benefits. Adequate confidentiality does not erase the two physicians' recorded refusal to refer.

He nevertheless sustains confidential medical-necessity, emergency and diagnostic-basis reports. Ashcroft recognizes the usefulness of recorded medical reasons; Thornburgh distinguishes useful confidential reporting from exposure and interference. These reports do not authorize advance approval, useless tests or punishment solely for later disagreement with reasonable clinical judgment. His public positions in Foucha and Riggins (May 18, 1992) reinforce attention to actual bodily interference and justification without supplying a universal proof rule. He accepts the protective emergency construction, reserving standalone vagueness and its disputed preservation. His remedial position remains provision-specific severance and ordinary implementation, without wholesale invalidation of the Act.

##### Stevens — Separate opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

**Joins and scope:** No other Justice joins the complete separate opinion. Agreement with a particular result elsewhere does not constitute a join in this writing.

Justice Stevens joins the constitutional foundation and the stated reasons for retaining it. His Thornburgh concurrence (1986) protects childbearing decisions against restriction supported only by majority disapproval; his Webster opinion (1989) requires attention to actual medical usefulness and burden. Hodgson (1990) places the justification for imposed obstacles on the State and distinguishes legitimate parental assistance from needless interference. Those sources support Stone's first five steps: identify the real obligation and affected population, examine purpose and cumulative effects, prohibit substantial obstacles to protected care, and require adequate justification and fit for meaningful lesser burdens.

This is Stevens's substantive proposed alternative, not agreement with every remedy or a generalized facial formula. He does not join Stone's sixth step as a comprehensive large-fraction rule. His Ohio v. Akron Center concurrence (1990), joining Parts I–IV but not Part V, supports careful facial analysis grounded in actual safeguards and demonstrated operation. Nor does he join bare elimination of staged protection when his meaningful-burden alternative has not become controlling law. Constitutional protection cannot depend on a replacement standard that appears only in a minority writing.

Stevens would invalidate the fixed adult waiting period and exclusive physician delivery. Hodgson's treatment of time for a minor's parental consultation does not establish a corresponding adult waiting rule. He nevertheless sustains accurate clinical information, including gestational age, and the three limited ancillary notices. The paternal-support provision permits omission in rape cases without requiring police reporting (§3205(a)(2)(iii)); optional materials need not be accepted, read or endorsed (§3205(a)(3)). Those actual limits distinguish useful information from compelled ideological agreement. Lawful receipt certification may survive severance of invalid requirements.

He joins narrow First Amendment permission for these factual disclosures; Riley (1988) prevents treating compensation as dispositive. He sustains publication and elective receipt while preserving concrete challenges to misinformation. District findings 181–184 concern actual directory defects; they do not warrant an automatic suspension of every challenged item. He rejects the proposed focused materials vacatur and interim distribution injunction. He also sustains the present informed-parent provision, including lawful medical and ancillary content, without reading the statute to require attendance. The district's operational finding and Third Circuit footnote 23 leave an actual visit construction unresolved. He therefore rejects the special attendance vacatur, remand and interim injunction.

Stevens joins spousal-notice invalidation on protected-choice grounds, removal of notice-compliance reporting, and preservation of confidential marital-status information. He would invalidate the referring-identity field because the recorded deterrence lacks sufficient justification. He sustains the three confidential medical-basis reports and the limited public facility reports; clinical documentation and fiscal accountability must be evaluated under their distinct record and statutory conditions. He adds no independently sufficient equality, general-privacy or spousal-speech holding, and reserves standalone emergency vagueness. His Riggins position (May 18, 1992) supports justified protection of bodily decisionmaking; his Quill opinion (May 26) supports examining separable precedents without treating every revision as abandonment of their foundation.

##### Souter — Separate opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

**Joins and scope:** No other Justice joins the complete separate opinion. Agreement with a particular result elsewhere does not constitute a join in this writing.

Justice Souter joins retention of protected choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care. His Payne concurrence (June 27, 1991) explains that disagreement with a constitutional precedent alone does not establish the special justification needed to overrule it. Concrete unworkability or internal tension can support a limited correction. The present grounds do not displace Roe's constitutional foundation. The Court's decisions in Hilton (December 16, 1991) and Quill (May 26, 1992) likewise require attention to the particular proposition and setting; their statutory and commercial considerations do not dictate the abortion result.

Souter accepts prohibition of an obstacle that gives another person effective control over protected care and rejects automatic immunity for every lesser burden. He does not adopt a complete replacement formula. Stone's proposal carefully distinguishes relevant populations, real causal burdens, clinical evidence and actual relief, but its specificity does not itself justify a universal new balancing rule, burden transfer or facial denominator. Souter's Riggins join (May 18, 1992) concerns forced medication; his Medina concurrence (June 22) resists automatic transfer of procedural methods across different constitutional questions. He therefore applies the relevant abortion precedents and the demonstrated facts rather than declaring a new comprehensive test. He also declines operative abandonment of staged protection without identifying the justification for each displaced rule.

Souter would invalidate the fixed waiting period and exclusive physician-delivery requirement because the grounds offered do not sufficiently justify departing from Akron's directly relevant protections. He sustains accurate clinical information, gestational age and the three concise ancillary notices. Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) permits paternal-support information to be omitted in rape cases; it does not require omission or police reporting. Materials remain optional under §3205(a)(3), and receipt certification cannot enforce an invalid prerequisite.

He joins narrow First Amendment permission for these factual disclosures, without universal professional-speech immunity or treating compensation as dispositive. He sustains lawful publication and elective receipt while preserving concrete challenges to misleading execution. The district's directory findings do not establish that every material is unlawful. He opposes the focused materials vacatur and interim distribution injunction. He also sustains the present informed-parent provision, including lawful medical and ancillary content, without assuming an enacted clinic-visit requirement. The operational finding and the unresolved construction identified in Third Circuit footnote 23 warrant reservation of an actual enforced-visit case, not the proposed special attendance vacatur and interim injunction.

Souter joins invalidation of spousal notice and notice-compliance fields while preserving confidential marital-status data. Protected choice and actual coercion provide complete relief, so independent equality, general-privacy and spousal-speech grounds remain undecided. He sustains confidential referral identification, acknowledging the two physicians' predicted withdrawal but finding insufficient demonstrated service obstruction for facial relief. He also sustains the three medical-basis reports and limited public facility disclosures under their distinct statutory conditions. The reports preserve actual clinical standards and do not create prior permission or hindsight control. He accepts the protective emergency construction while reserving standalone vagueness and its contested preservation.

##### O'Connor — Separate concurrence

**Joins and scope:** No other Justice joins the complete separate opinion. Agreement with a particular result elsewhere does not constitute a join in this writing.

Justice O’Connor concurs separately to explain the regulatory standard she would retain and her additional grounds concerning spousal notice. She preserves the constitutional protection for previability choice while rejecting Roe’s rigid trimester machinery. Her Akron and Thornburgh dissents supply a two-stage inquiry: first determine whether the regulation creates an absolute obstacle or severely limits access, without weighing the State’s interests at that threshold; then, if that burden exists, require compelling justification. Ordinary burdens receive rational review. A severe burden is not automatically unconstitutional under every conceivable regulation, although an independently forbidden transfer of the patient’s decision to another person cannot be justified by invoking family consultation. Her Riggins opinion does not transplant its medication-specific justification requirements into every abortion regulation.

She therefore rejects a general rule placing an evidentiary burden on the State whenever any meaningful interference is shown. Professional evidence and workable alternatives matter, but the Constitution does not invariably require the least costly policy. She accepts accurate medical information, physician responsibility for its delivery and the fixed deliberation interval on this record, acknowledging the proven additional visits, expense and delay. The separate ancillary notices communicate facts rather than require ideological allegiance. Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) permits paternal-support information to be omitted in rape cases, without a reporting condition; legal liability does not guarantee payment. Chosen materials and receipt certification do not require reading or assent, and her rejection of present materials relief does not certify every pamphlet or listed agency as accurate.

Hodgson supports one-parent informed consent with an effective independent bypass based on maturity and actual informed consent or, alternatively, best interests. She joins the limited rejection of the attendance challenge and would also sustain the district’s assumed operation with that adequate alternative. She does not read the statute to impose an express visit requirement or a separate waiting period for a parent. She sustains confidential referrer identification, the distinct medical-judgment reports and these public facility reports on their record-specific justifications. Reporting does not itself authorize punishment merely because an official later prefers a different lawful clinical judgment.

Spousal notice fails independently under the protected-choice rule. O’Connor also relies on Hogan’s requirement that a sex classification substantially serve an important governmental objective without resting on stereotyped roles. Biological difference does not justify giving a husband supervisory power over his wife’s medical decision. Her joined Hodgson reasoning additionally protects the narrow sphere of adult reproductive communication from this imposed control. Those are additional grounds, not a general constitutional right to secrecy or a claim that every pregnancy classification is invalid. She reaches no independent spousal compelled-speech or emergency-vagueness holding, and resolves no disputed waiver through silence. Her ordinary stare-decisis analysis protects the constitutional foundation while allowing a demonstrated defect in a particular regulatory rule to be corrected.

##### White — Concurrence in part and dissent in part

**Joins and scope:** O’Connor joins only White’s isolated selection of intermediate scrutiny. Scalia and Thomas join except that tier selection; they assume the tier without deciding and join its application.

White concurs in part and dissents in part. Scalia and Thomas join except for White's conclusion that intermediate scrutiny governs the spousal-notice sex classification; they assume that standard without deciding its applicability and join the analysis under it. O’Connor joins only the isolated selection of that tier and rejects White’s sustaining application. The writing accepts the Court's precise permissions for physician delivery, the waiting interval, lawful information, specified clinical reports and public-funding disclosures at their stated scopes. It also joins removal of Roe's trimester timetable as a barrier to pursuing health and potential-life interests throughout pregnancy. These direct joins adopt the limited reasons given for those permissions, without recognizing a fundamental abortion right or supplying a comprehensive regulatory test.

The three Justices reject the retained constitutional foundation and the Court's reasons for preserving it. White's Roe and Thornburgh dissents and Scalia's Webster and Ohio v. Akron Center writings identify their principal objection: the Constitution does not establish the claimed priority of elective abortion over the State's protection of prenatal life. Thomas joins the source and precedent analysis expressed here. They accept that precedent deserves reasoned consideration, but regard the source defect and continuing doctrinal difficulties as sufficient to reconsider Roe. Their position does not deny every substantive liberty or dispense with independent speech, equality and privacy guarantees.

They would sustain spousal notice because the father's prospective parental interest and marital participation support this notice requirement, while acknowledging the record's coercion and retaliation findings and the actual statutory limits. Under intermediate scrutiny, applied by White and assumed by Scalia and Thomas, they find the information requirement substantially related to an important actual objective. Caban supplies the standard, and White's Michael M. position reinforces attention to that relationship; biological differences alone do not justify legal subordination. Their privacy analysis addresses the particular disclosure to the spouse, not a general denial of intimate privacy. Riley likewise prevents treating all compensated medical expression as commercial or all truthful compelled speech as permissible.

They would also sustain the notice-compliance and nonnotice-reason reports on their primary premise that notice is valid. They cast no conditional vote for removing those fields after the Court invalidates notice. Their broader reporting position preserves confidentiality, distinct data fields and the different public-funding triggers; parental informed consent, assumed attendance and judicial bypass remain separate questions. The writing adopts no universal Salerno rule. It leaves the distinct spousal First Amendment merits and preservation question open for ordinary handling of any genuinely preserved claim, and does not turn the emergency health construction into a standalone vagueness holding.

### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, No. 91-453

Merits decision, June 29, 1992

Argued March 2, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of South Carolina, 304 S.C. 376, 404 S.E.2d 895 (1991), which reversed a trial award of $1,232,387.50. The question is whether a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use requires compensation despite its public-harm purpose, including the effect of a new permit avenue and background ownership limits. Lucas seeks reversal of the no-compensation judgment. All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

The Court reverses and remands, 6–3. Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support reversal. Blackmun and Stevens dissent; Souter would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted, declining to decide this case rather than affirming on the merits.

Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in its entirety by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas. Kennedy concurs in the judgment without joining the Court's categorical rationale. Blackmun and Stevens each dissent, and Souter files a statement favoring dismissal. Each separate writing has no additional joiners. The Court's express five-member opinion controls.

A later discretionary permit procedure does not bar review of compensation for an earlier categorical building prohibition where permission under that prohibition was unavailable and the state supreme court finally rejected the compensation claim on its merits. Any compensable taking must be remedied for its actual duration; the later procedure and unresolved factual issues remain relevant to future use, duration and valuation, and the permanent-value award is not automatically reinstated.

Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, controls with five votes. Kennedy separately concurs in the judgment and agrees that the earlier interval is reviewable, but does not join the Court's writing. The holding rests on the express five-Justice opinion without combining separate rationales.

The Court holds that a new opportunity to seek future permission cannot eliminate a completed deprivation from judicial review. Williamson County and MacDonald ordinarily require a final determination of permitted development so that a court knows the restriction it must assess. Here the Council stipulated that the original Act allowed no building permit, and the state supreme court rejected compensation on a categorical merits ground after the amendment existed. First English establishes that ending a restriction does not eliminate compensation for a period in which it effected a taking. The Council's request for further permitting therefore cannot dispose of the earlier interval. Yee preserves the difference between a distinct unpresented claim and an argument supporting the claim actually decided; the earlier deprivation belongs to the compensation dispute already presented. These conclusions do not establish that a taking occurred or fix its value. The Court leaves the new permit avenue, the extent and duration of actual loss, and the defenses the state supreme court did not decide for further proceedings.

Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank, 473 U.S. 172 (1985), continues to supply ordinary finality requirements. The Court distinguishes an absolute prohibition for an already elapsed period.

MacDonald, Sommer & Frates v. Yolo County, 477 U.S. 340 (1986), continues to require a determination of permitted development. An unavailable original permit need not be sought to test the earlier prohibition.

First English Evangelical Lutheran Church v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304 (1987), requires compensation for an actual temporary taking. The Court applies that rule without deciding whether this regulation effected a taking or setting the amount.

Yee v. City of Escondido, April 1, 1992, supplies the distinction between a presented claim and a distinct unpresented taking theory. Its application here creates no new physical-taking rule.


A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. The express five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rule.

The Court holds that public authority to restrict land and responsibility to pay for its complete economic loss are separate questions. Pennsylvania Coal recognizes that otherwise valid regulation can impose a compensable taking. Agins supplies the complete-use formulation, which the Court now specifies as a categorical rule with an ownership limitation. Penn Central continues to govern ordinary partial burdens. A purpose-only exemption would let the State describe the same sacrifice as preventing harm or conferring a benefit and thereby determine its own compensation duty. Mugler and the older noxious-use cases do not establish that exemption for proved elimination of all beneficial land use. Roth and Scranton explain why the owner's entitlement remains bounded by existing law: prohibiting an entitlement never held takes nothing. The trial finding supplies the premise of this review, but does not settle the Council's preserved factual objections. South Carolina must identify genuine background principles and their application on remand. Changed knowledge may affect nuisance law's application; a new legislative label cannot replace it. The Court decides no parcel-definition rule or percentage shortcut.

Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922), recognizes that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes. The Court applies that principle without attributing today's exact categorical rule to that decision.

Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980), supplies the complete-economic-use formulation that the Court specifies and extends into today's rule and necessary exception. Its distinct legitimate-interest formulation is not adjudicated.

Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978), retains its contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action. No percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.

Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962), retain their actual outcomes and protection of lawful protective regulation. The Court limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation.

Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987), concerned productive enterprise and partial interests. The Court distinguishes that setting without reversing its actual holding or adopting a new denominator for measuring loss.

Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972), supplies existing rules and understandings as the basis for identifying a protected property interest. Applying that principle here decides no procedural due-process claim.

Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900), supplies the principle that title is subject to antecedent limitations. A previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.

Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 (1982), and Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 (1979), retain their protections against physical occupation and interference with exclusion. They illuminate the difference between existing limitations and a newly imposed servitude; this decision concerns regulatory loss.

Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies, Inc. v. Beckwith, 449 U.S. 155 (1980), supplies the prohibition on converting private property into public property by governmental assertion alone, which the Court applies here.


The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

Effective June 29, 1992, proved complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use triggers the categorical compensation rule unless background state ownership law already forecloses the use. The State must identify and substantiate that exception through ordinary adjudication. The rule specifies Agins's complete-use formulation and limits broad purpose-only readings of the noxious-use cases without establishing a percentage rule, parcel definition or heightened proof requirement. Penn Central remains operative for partial burdens; First English governs compensation after an actual taking. Public regulatory authority and ordinary finality requirements outside this setting remain intact. No precedent is overruled.

Kennedy agrees that the earlier interval is reviewable and reversal appropriate, but would test complete-loss restrictions against objectively reasonable investment-backed expectations drawn from the whole legal tradition. Common-law nuisance alone is too narrow, particularly for vulnerable coastal land; actual ability and intent to develop and the duration of loss remain for remand. Blackmun disputes finality and the unsupported premise of complete economic extinction, and rejects the categorical rule in favor of the protective public purpose and contextual precedent. Stevens would withhold premature constitutional adjudication and, on the merits, sustain the general coastal measure under Penn Central, which supplies contextual review, and Keystone, which addressed productive enterprise and partial interests. He questions the zero-value boundary and a rigid nuisance exception. Souter would dismiss because the unreviewable and questionable total-loss premise prevents sound resolution of both the category and its exceptions. These separate positions do not control.

The case returns to the Supreme Court of South Carolina for proceedings consistent with the holdings. The no-compensation judgment cannot rest on public-purpose characterization alone. The state court must address background property and nuisance law and its remaining unaddressed defenses, including the extent and duration of economic loss. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator

**Controlling proposition:** When a weighing State requires its sentencing judge to give great weight to a jury's recommendation, an inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the final sentence even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. The especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel instruction given here lacks the guidance the Eighth Amendment requires; the judge's separate findings do not by themselves cure the recommendation's legal influence.

**Authority:** The per curiam Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, seven Justices, for this rule and its application. White's preference for argument is not a present merits vote. Scalia rejects the weighing premise in dissent. The express seven-Justice majority supplies the controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional guidance must follow the actual allocation of sentencing responsibility. Godfrey, Maynard and Shell require an aggravating category to distinguish the cases to which it applies; the ordinary words given this jury supply no adequate boundary. Stringer and Sochor distinguish eligibility supported by valid factors from the later weighing that selects death. Florida law makes the jury's general recommendation consequential by requiring the judge to give it great weight, whether it recommends life or death. Mills does not permit an assumption that jurors ignored an authorized invalid category, and Walton's presumption that a judge follows the law means the recommendation received the weight state law required. The judge thus cannot be treated as wholly independent of the defective recommendation. Baldwin permits a different allocation of sentencing roles; it does not eliminate an actual duty of deference. The Court does not find that particular jurors selected particular aggravators. It decides the legal effect of the unguided recommendation and preserves the State's ability to structure a constitutionally guided sentencing process.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), and Shell v. Mississippi, 498 U.S. 1 (1990): apply their requirement of meaningful guidance for a capital aggravating category; the instruction here is insufficiently narrowed.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992: applies the distinction between valid eligibility and constitutionally defective weighing; surviving valid aggravators alone do not remove the error.
- Sochor v. Florida, June 8, 1992: applies its weighing-error rule while distinguishing its independent state preservation bar to a different jury claim and its separate factual-invalidity issue.
- Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367 (1988): applies the refusal to presume that jurors disregard an instruction's constitutionally defective route; the Court attributes no factor-specific verdict to the jury.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990): preserves genuine judicial sentencing and the presumption that judges apply legal narrowing; distinguishes a system in which a separate unguided recommendation carries required weight.
- Baldwin v. Alabama, 472 U.S. 372 (1985), and Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447 (1984): preserve States' latitude in allocating sentencing responsibility; that latitude does not validate influential unguided weighing.
- Tedder v. State, 322 So.2d 908 (Fla. 1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988): supply the state-law requirement to give great weight to jury recommendations of life or death.
- Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989): rejects its premise that the jury's advisory designation alone makes this vague instruction constitutionally immaterial.

##### Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the per curiam opinion's remedy instruction. They expressly apply the existing Stringer, Sochor, Clemons and Chapman correction requirements to the two-stage defect found here. No agreement is attributed to White on this unargued merits question or to Scalia on the need for correction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that correction must reach the error actually found. Clemons permits an authorized reviewing court to reweigh valid aggravation against mitigation or determine that an invalid factor was harmless; it does not treat a surviving majority of factors as an answer. Chapman supplies the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness standard. Stringer and Sochor require an actual lawful cure of invalid weighing, and Parker requires attention to the sentencing record that genuinely existed. Here the jury's general recommendation carried legally required weight into the judge's decision. The judge's four valid aggravators and two mitigators therefore do not establish that this indirect error had no effect. Further review must account for that channel of influence, rather than repeat the premise that the judge alone imposed sentence. The Court does not undertake its own balancing, choose a sentence, or decide that Florida law authorizes every proposed appellate cure. It leaves those proceedings to the state court under the stated constitutional limits. The conviction and unrelated petition questions are outside this disposition.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, June 8, 1992: apply actual cure requirements to the recommendation's transmitted influence; no unreasoned harmlessness label suffices.
- Parker v. Dugger, 498 U.S. 308 (1991): applies review grounded in the real sentencing record and mitigation, not an assumed alternative record.

The Court applies the anti-vagueness and weighing rules described above, distinguishes genuine independent judicial sentencing, and requires correction of this indirect influence.
