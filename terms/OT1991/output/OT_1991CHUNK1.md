## Decisions and Dispositions

### United States v. Ibarra, No. 90-1713

Summary disposition, October 15, 1991

Respondent's motion to proceed in forma pauperis and the petition for certiorari are granted. The Court unanimously vacates the Tenth Circuit's judgment and remands for consideration of the Government's suppression appeal. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; Thomas is not yet seated. The Court acts through a per curiam opinion.

#### Controlling Proposition and Authority

A timely motion asking a district court to reconsider a decided question in order to alter the rights adjudicated renders the order nonfinal for appeal purposes until the motion is denied. The full appeal period then runs from denial. The apparent strength of the motion—including that it relies on a ground previously abandoned—does not alter the rule. All eight participating Justices support this holding.

The rule follows *United States v. Healy* and *United States v. Dieter*. Screening a reconsideration motion for merit before fixing appellate timing would destroy the definite boundary those cases supply and encourage protective appeals. Ibarra's notice, filed 27 days after denial, was timely.

The Court reserves bad-faith motions and successive motions. It does not address the modern jurisdictional characterization of the timing provision or the suppression ruling.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment dismissing the appeal is vacated. The Tenth Circuit is to hear and decide the suppression appeal; this Supreme Court proceeding is complete.

**End of entry: United States v. Ibarra, summary disposition, October 15, 1991.**

### Mireles v. Waco, No. 91-311

Grant of certiorari, October 21, 1991

The petition is granted for plenary briefing and argument. The request for summary reversal is not granted. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; Thomas is not yet seated.

White, Blackmun, O'Connor, and Souter would grant and summarily reverse. Stone votes to grant and set the matter for argument. Scalia and Kennedy would deny summary disposition and the petition; Stevens would deny the petition. Four votes grant certiorari, but no participating majority supports summary reversal.

Stone issues a statement respecting the grant, limited to the need for plenary consideration of the unresolved boundary between a judicial command to produce counsel and an alleged distinct command to use unnecessary force. Scalia, joined by Kennedy, notes that he would deny the petition; Stevens also notes that he would deny it. These statements do not decide the merits.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The grant creates no substantive holding and leaves judicial-immunity doctrine unchanged. The Ninth Circuit's judgment remains in place pending this Court's later disposition. The matter remains open for briefing and argument; Thomas may participate only if argument or resubmission occurs after his October 23 judicial oath.

**End of entry: Mireles v. Waco, grant of certiorari, October 21, 1991.**

### Campos v. City of Houston, Application No. A-301

In-chambers disposition, October 29, 1991

O'Connor acts alone as Circuit Justice for the Fifth Circuit under the simulation's governing allotment. The application is not referred to the Court. She denies the requested original injunction stopping the entire municipal election process but grants a stay of the district court's October 12 order insofar as it directs use of Plan #2 for the November 5 district-council elections.

#### Basis and Limits

The application establishes no adequate source for the blanket original injunction. The narrower stay is available under the All Writs Act to protect potential appellate jurisdiction, applying the considerations identified in *Ripon Society*: probable error, irreparable injury, and public interest.

Plan #2 remained unprecleared, and judicial adoption did not alter its legislative character. Implementing it threatened the statutory injury section 5 prevents. The presence of precleared Plan #3 and the availability of genuinely judicial remedies undercut the asserted necessity for Plan #2. O'Connor gives substantial weight to election disruption and ballots already cast but concludes that those considerations do not justify implementation without clearance on this record.

This single-Justice action is not a holding of the Court. It does not select a substitute plan, stop unaffected elections, preclear Plan #2, reinstate the former plan, decide section 2 liability, finally decide the three-judge-court objection, or resolve whether an extreme circumstance could ever justify temporary nonintervention.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Plan #2 direction is stayed pending the Fifth Circuit's disposition or further order. The appeal and related proceedings remain open in their proper courts.

**End of entry: Campos v. City of Houston, in-chambers disposition, October 29, 1991.**

### Hafer v. Melo, No. 90-681

Merits decision, November 5, 1991

On certiorari to the Third Circuit, the judgment is affirmed unanimously. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; Thomas does not participate because the case was argued before his judicial oath. O'Connor delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined by every participating Justice.

#### Controlling Proposition and Authority

State officials sued in their individual capacities are “persons” under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. Official action may be action under color of state law without converting a claim against an officer personally into a claim against the State. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a personal-capacity damages claim, and the official nature of the challenged act alone supplies no absolute immunity.

*Will v. Michigan Department of State Police* concerns States and official-capacity defendants, where the real defendant is the governmental entity. It does not remove individual officers from the statutory term. Concerns about reasonable official conduct are addressed by immunity doctrine rather than by redefining “person.”

#### Limits and Stage

The Court adopts no rule about qualified immunity's content or sequence and decides no liability, causation, damages, patronage issue, or disputed pleading question. Hafer's *Will* defense fails, and the personal-capacity claims return for further proceedings; no remedy is awarded.

**End of entry: Hafer v. Melo, merits decision, November 5, 1991.**

### Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc., Nos. 90-913 and 90-914

Merits decision, December 3, 1991

The Court reverses No. 90-913 and affirms No. 90-914. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter are unanimous; Thomas does not participate because the case was argued before his oath. Stevens delivers the Opinion of the Court.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

Section 1818(i)(1)'s express jurisdictional bar deprives a district court of power to enjoin prosecution of these pending Board proceedings. The Bankruptcy Code neither qualifies nor supersedes that bar: the proceedings fall within § 362(b)(4)'s police-and-regulatory exception, and the cited estate-property provisions do not reach ongoing, nonfinal administrative proceedings.

*Leedom v. Kyne* does not authorize interim review. Section 1818(h)(2) supplies meaningful review of the regulation's validity and application after a final Board order, while § 1818(i)(1) clearly precludes intervention during the proceeding.

The Court does not decide the source-of-strength regulation's validity, the unsafe-practice allegations, or the section 23A charge. It does not decide a case in which the statutory review route is genuinely unavailable.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Both district-court injunctions and the filing procedure resting on them fall. The administrative proceedings may continue; MCorp may obtain review under § 1818(h)(2) after a final order.

**End of entry: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc., merits decision, December 3, 1991.**

### Griffin v. United States, No. 90-6352

Merits decision, December 3, 1991

The Court affirms the Seventh Circuit, eight to zero in the judgment. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; Thomas does not. Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter. Blackmun concurs in the judgment.

#### Controlling Proposition and Authority

Due process does not require setting aside a general guilty verdict on a single multiple-object conspiracy count when the evidence is sufficient for one alleged object but insufficient for another. On this charge, section 371's objects are alternative factual means; proof of the agreement, overt act, and at least one unlawful object suffices. Seven Justices join the Court's rationale, and Blackmun agrees that no due-process violation occurred.

*Yates* and *Stromberg* concern legally invalid routes to conviction, which jurors are not equipped to identify. *Turner* governs a factual route lacking sufficient evidence, an assessment entrusted to the jury. Evidence supported the IRS object.

Blackmun declines to join the common-law survey. He emphasizes the danger of confusion in this complex trial and commends separate counts or special interrogatories.

#### Limits and Stage

The Court does not overrule *Yates*, require withdrawal of every unsupported object, adopt a supervisory rule, or decide Sixth Amendment or statutory unanimity as to an object. The conviction remains intact.

**End of entry: Griffin v. United States, merits decision, December 3, 1991.**

### Estelle v. McGuire, No. 90-1074

Merits decision, December 4, 1991

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit, five to three. White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support the judgment; Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor would vacate and remand for harmless-error review. Thomas does not participate. White delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined by the other four Justices in the judgment majority. O'Connor writes separately, joined by Stone and Stevens.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

All eight participating Justices agree that admitting evidence of repeated nonaccidental injuries did not violate due process. The evidence was probative of whether the fatal injury was intentional even without direct attribution to McGuire, and federal habeas relief cannot rest on a state-law evidentiary ruling.

Five Justices further hold that, in the charge as a whole, there was no reasonable likelihood the jury treated McGuire's commission of the earlier injuries as established or treated the identity inference as mandatory. The conditional wording and the instruction barring character or disposition use preserved the jury's role. Neither asserted error supports habeas relief.

O'Connor, Stone, and Stevens agree with the evidence and state-law holding but conclude that the instruction created a reasonable likelihood of relieving the State of proving identity. They would remand for harmless-error review under *Chapman* and *Rose* rather than direct issuance of a writ.

The Court expresses no view on whether a state propensity-evidence rule would violate due process and does not decide state admissibility or invoke any later-developed habeas standard.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed. The district court's denial of habeas relief stands.

**End of entry: Estelle v. McGuire, merits decision, December 4, 1991.**

### Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni, No. 90-584

Merits decision, December 4, 1991

The judgment of the Ninth Circuit is affirmed unanimously. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; Thomas does not because argument preceded his oath. White delivers the Opinion of the Court.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

A maritime worker in an occupation enumerated by the LHWCA may be a Jones Act seaman because the LHWCA expressly excludes masters and members of a vessel's crew. Enumeration alone does not establish LHWCA exclusivity. Status is a mixed, fact-specific inquiry into the vessel and the worker's relationship to it.

Congress did not require a Jones Act suit to await an administrative coverage determination. Voluntary receipt of LHWCA benefits without adjudicated coverage neither precludes nor estops the suit; the statutory credit prevents double recovery and defeats the claimed detrimental reliance.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Genuine disputes remain over whether the platforms are vessels in navigation and whether Gizoni's connection suffices. The Court does not find him a seaman or decide negligence, unseaworthiness, maintenance, or cure. Summary judgment remains reversed and the case returns for factual proceedings.

**End of entry: Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni, merits decision, December 4, 1991.**

### Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, No. 90-967

Merits decision, December 4, 1991

The Sixth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded unanimously. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; Thomas does not because argument preceded his oath. White delivers the Opinion of the Court.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

A union member seeking the pleaded money damages under LMRDA Title I is entitled to a jury trial. The damages are legal rather than restitution incidental to reinstatement, and the action resembles a personal-injury action. *Teamsters v. Terry* controls.

Section 301(a) extends to an individual union member's suit alleging violation of a union constitution that is a contract between labor organizations when the member is a beneficiary. The statutory word “between” describes the contracts, not the permissible plaintiffs. Wooddell sufficiently alleged an interunion contractual structure and a breach through referral discrimination to establish federal subject-matter jurisdiction.

#### Limits, Remedy, and Stage

Jurisdiction establishes no breach, officer liability, damages, or disposition of the fair-representation and state-law claims. The LMRDA damages claim is jury-triable, the section 301 claim is within federal jurisdiction, and further proceedings will occur below.

**End of entry: Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, merits decision, December 4, 1991.**
