## Law of the decision

**Justiciability holding and operative rule:** Completion of an election does not moot a ballot-access dispute where election results carry continuing party-qualification consequences and the short election cycle presents a reasonably recurring controversy that otherwise evades review.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion joined by seven of eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Moore v. Ogilvie* recognizes that election restrictions may retain legal consequences after ballots are counted. Here several candidates exceeded the five-percent threshold that may alter the party's qualification in later elections, and the challenged rules operate on a timetable too short for complete review before voting. The dispute therefore remains concrete. The Court decides the federal grounds actually used by the Illinois Supreme Court but does not supply an answer to the unaddressed judicial-candidate objection. *Bacchus Imports* supports returning that consequential state-law issue for authoritative construction. The Court neither revives the unappealed water-district candidates nor orders a new election.

**Ballot-access holding and operative rule:** Illinois may require meaningful support within each separate electorate, but it may not categorically prevent authorized candidates from expanding an existing party under its name or disqualify otherwise qualified countywide and city candidates solely because candidates in a different district lack sufficient signatures.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion joined by seven of eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Anderson* requires attention to the burden, the State's precise interests, and whether the restriction is necessary; *Socialist Workers Party* rejects a local signature demand more onerous than the statewide route without adequate justification. Protecting a party from unauthorized appropriation is legitimate, but an absolute geographic name barrier prohibits even authorized expansion and is broader than a permission requirement. Likewise, support in each electorate may be demanded for candidates chosen there, which sustains the suburban exclusion on this record. It does not justify invalidating countywide and city candidates who met their own constituencies' requirements merely because a separate slate component failed. That consequence effectively compounds the signature burden without advancing the stated interest in support for each election. The Court leaves other district structures, cumulative burdens, and actual unauthorized name use open.

**Precedent treatment:** *Moore*, *Anderson*, *Socialist Workers Party*, *Jenness*, and *Bacchus Imports* are applied and preserved.

**Law after decision:** Authorized party-name expansion and independently qualified slate components receive constitutional protection; separate-electorate support requirements remain permissible on this record.
