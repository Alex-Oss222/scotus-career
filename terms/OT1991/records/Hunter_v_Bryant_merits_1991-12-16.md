## Law of the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Qualified immunity from a claim of arrest without probable cause is a legal question for the judge under ordinary summary-judgment constraints. A court must evaluate whether a reasonable officer could believe the arrest lawful on the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; choosing the “more reasonable” interpretation is not the test, but a generalized security concern alone does not establish reasonable belief that the elements of the offense were satisfied.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants and therefore controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** *Harlow*, *Anderson v. Creighton*, and *Malley* protect reasonable mistakes and require early judicial resolution where supported facts permit it. Rule 56 nevertheless requires the court to identify genuine disputes about historical predicates and draw supported inferences for the nonmovant. The agents could consider the assassination references, reported gesture, oral statements, confusing interview, and refusal to elaborate. They also had the letter itself, which repeatedly attributed the supposed plot to an outside group; that feature matters to whether Bryant communicated a serious threat prohibited by §871 or reported an imagined danger. *Watts* supplies the contextual distinction between a true threat and protected expression. The Ninth Circuit erred by asking which interpretation was more reasonable and by suggesting the legal immunity issue belonged generally to a jury. But the opposite error would treat presidential-security concern as dispositive without connecting the accepted facts to the offense. The remand court must decide immunity promptly after specifying any supported material dispute; the Court finds neither guilt, innocence, bad faith, nor a constitutional violation.

**Precedent treatment:** *Harlow*, *Anderson v. Creighton*, *Malley*, *Mitchell*, *Beck*, *Watts*, and *Liberty Lobby* are applied and preserved. No qualified-immunity rule is revised.

**Law after decision:** Qualified immunity remains judge-decided and protects reasonable probable-cause mistakes, subject to Rule 56's supported-fact constraints and offense-specific analysis.
