## Law of the decision

### Corporate assignee’s filing and payment duty

**Holding and operative rule:** An assignee holding all or substantially all of a corporation’s property must make its income-tax return under §6012(b)(3), whether or not the corporation dissolves or the assignee continues its business. Smith’s receipt of the corporate estate property satisfies that category, and §6151(a) requires payment of the tax due with those returns.

**Authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous controlling statutory construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the tax duty follows the statutory assignment, not a narrower bankruptcy label. Section 6012(b)(3) expressly includes an assignee holding all or substantially all corporate property and expressly applies whether or not the property or business is being operated. The plan made Smith that assignee. He therefore must file the returns the corporations would have filed had the plan not assigned their property. The argument that only a conventional operating receiver or a fiduciary winding up a dissolved corporation qualifies cannot be reconciled with the broader enacted term and its express qualification. First National Bank of Greeley and Louisville Property illustrate applications to particular fiduciaries; they do not impose exclusive factual categories. Section 6151 connects the filing duty with payment when due. The Court need not decide whether Smith also qualifies under another statutory label, and it does not compute income, deductions, penalties or a tax amount.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *First National Bank of Greeley v. United States*, 86 F.2d 938 — its winding-up application does not limit the statute to dissolution.
- *Louisville Property Co. v. Commissioner*, 140 F.2d 547 — its operating-fiduciary setting does not exclude a qualifying assignee.

### Fiduciary of the separate liquidating trust

**Holding and operative rule:** A fiduciary of a separate liquidating trust must file the trust’s return under §6012(b)(4) and pay the associated tax under §6151 when the plan and duties meet the governing trust and fiduciary definitions. Here Gould’s estate property passed directly into that trust rather than revesting in Gould, so the asserted grantor-trust theory does not displace Smith’s duty.

**Authority:** Thomas’s unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the trust-specific classification and corresponding payment duty.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Smith’s responsibility for Gould’s transferred property arises from his position as trust fiduciary. The plan created a separate liquidating trust and vested the estate property in it; it did not merely replace Gould as manager of the same bankruptcy estate. Treasury Regulation §301.7701-4(d) recognizes a trust organized to liquidate and distribute transferred assets, and Smith’s powers satisfy §7701(a)(6) and Regulation §301.7701-6. Restrictions on his discretion do not eliminate that fiduciary function. The grantor-trust objection also fails on the actual transfer. In re Sonner involved property apparently returned to the debtor before contribution to a trust; here the estate transferred the property directly and Gould made no such contribution. The Court does not approve or disapprove Sonner’s result on its different facts. Nor does it decide that all liquidating trusts are taxed alike or displace grantor provisions when their predicates actually exist. The corresponding tax amount remains unadjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *In re Sonner*, 53 B.R. 859 — distinguished by revesting and contribution; its result on those different facts is left open.

### A silent confirmed plan does not excuse prospective tax duties

**Holding and operative rule:** Confirmation under §1141(a) of a plan silent about taxes, coupled with the United States’ failure to object, does not excuse the statutory return and payment obligations imposed on this assignee and trust fiduciary after appointment. The binding treatment of properly encompassed preconfirmation claims does not establish an exemption from these later obligations.

**Authority:** Thomas’s unanimous opinion; all nine Justices reject the plan-confirmation defense.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the confirmed plan supplies no prospective exemption from the tax laws. The United States seeks recognition of Smith’s duties as assignee and fiduciary after his appointment, not payment from him of taxes already due before appointment. Section 6151 links the payment obligation to the time prescribed for the required return. Hartman v. Lauchli and Pan American Van Lines confirm that connection. Even assuming §1141(a) binds creditors with respect to claims that arose before confirmation, that premise does not bind them as to the later obligations at issue. The Code’s creditor definition reinforces the distinction. Government silence cannot supply an exemption the plan never stated or adjudicated. Respect for confirmation therefore does not justify the declaration that Smith has no duty at all. Existing tax claims expressly adjudicated through a plan present a different question. Ordinary tax and bankruptcy proceedings must determine the proper amounts and carry out the filing and payment duties.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Hartman v. Lauchli*, 238 F.2d 881 — applied for the connection between the return’s due date and payment duty.
- *Pan American Van Lines v. United States*, 607 F.2d 1299 — applied for the same timing principle.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse in both Nos. 90-1361 and 90-1484 and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare Smith responsible for the prescribed corporate returns as assignee and the relevant trust returns as fiduciary, with accompanying lawful tax-payment obligations after appointment. Leave amounts and implementation to ordinary tax and bankruptcy procedures.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Corporate assignee and trust fiduciary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve both property categories necessary to the declaration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted tax classifications and operative regulations without a bankruptcy-policy exception.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An assignee of substantially all corporate property falls within §6012(b)(3), and the fiduciary of this separately established liquidating trust falls within §6012(b)(4).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The plan assigned the corporate property to Smith; the statute expressly makes business continuation unnecessary. Limiting an assignee to a conventional operating receiver would delete the broader term and its qualification. Gould's assets were vested in a separate trust, not simply left in an unchanged individual bankruptcy estate with a substituted manager. The liquidation purpose and Smith's authority match the regulation's trust and fiduciary descriptions even though the plan limits his discretion. The asserted grantor theory does not overcome the actual transfer and governing tax predicates. Section 6151 supplies the corresponding duty to pay tax required with the returns; the Court need not compute it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No universal rule that every bankruptcy trustee has identical tax duties or that every liquidating trust is taxed without regard to the grantor provisions.

### Issue 2: Silence is not a prospective tax exemption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address confirmation because it is offered to defeat otherwise applicable duties.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §1141(a) to its actual subject.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A plan silent about taxes does not remove statutory duties arising from postappointment trust activity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The government did not agree to an exemption, and the plan did not adjudicate these prospective liabilities. Protecting reliance on a confirmed allocation does not permit the court to insert immunity from a different federal statute. The requested declaration therefore cannot stand even if confirmation binds creditors as to properly encompassed preconfirmation claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preappointment taxes and express adjudication of existing tax claims remain outside this disposition.
