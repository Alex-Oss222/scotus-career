## Law of the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Mandamus is unavailable where the petitioner has not first sought focused relief from the court that entered the operative abeyance order and has not shown that avenue inadequate or futile. Denial may be without prejudice to a renewed petition after the ordinary avenue is pursued.

**Authority:** The per curiam and concurrence together state this ground for all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 20.1, *Kerr*, *Will*, and *Fahey* reserve mandamus for an extraordinary failure to act where no other adequate avenue exists and the entitlement is clear and indisputable. The appeal's extended pendency, unanswered inquiries, and execution stay warrant serious attention. But the August order awaiting related proceedings is the current source of delay, and Washington never asked the panel to modify that specific order. Such a motion would permit the panel to address exhaustion, counsel, the proposed third petition, and a concrete schedule on the current record. Earlier general inquiries and a resubmission request do not establish that focused relief is unavailable or inevitably futile. Denial without prejudice respects both fair habeas review and timely adjudication. The Court does not approve every period of delay, find abuse of the writ, resolve Campbell's sentence, dissolve the stay, or impose an automatic special burden on future capital litigants.

**Precedent treatment:** *Kerr*, *Will*, and *Fahey* are applied; *Barefoot*, *Rose*, and *McCleskey* are not extended.

**Law after decision:** The extraordinary-writ prerequisite remains unchanged; the event creates no habeas-merits rule.
