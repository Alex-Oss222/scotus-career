**Freeman v. Pitts** — No. 89-1290; OT1991.

Argued 1991-10-07; decided 1992-03-31. Certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), reviewing partial withdrawal of supervision under a school-desegregation decree. Eight Justices participated at argument and decision; Thomas took no part.

**Question:** May supervision be withdrawn in completed areas while other decree obligations remain, and what findings and limits must govern that decision?

**Material record and posture:** A 1969 decree required dismantling DeKalb County’s dual school system. On the district’s 1986 dismissal request, the trial court found compliance in student assignment, transportation, facilities and extracurricular activities, but retained supervision over faculty and administrative assignments and educational resources and quality. It attributed the challenged student imbalance to demographic change and praised the district’s progress. The Eleventh Circuit required simultaneous compliance across all Green factors for several years. The factual findings, the legal completeness of the causal inquiry, and whether control in one area remains useful to cure another are distinct questions. Neither the Court’s judgment nor praise for progress establishes final systemwide compliance.

## 6. Controlling holdings

### 1. Partial withdrawal under an unfinished decree

**Holding and operative rule.** A court may withdraw supervision from a completed part of a school-desegregation decree while retaining jurisdiction and supervision elsewhere only after the district shows full and satisfactory compliance and elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable. The court must assess demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree and whether retaining control is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy.

**Authority.** Stone, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: all eight participating Justices support this limited rule.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that completion can justify ending supervision of one obligation without ending the case. Green requires removal of the former dual system’s effects throughout school operations; it does not make each remedial obligation indivisible. Swann supplies equitable flexibility directed to the constitutional injury, and Dowell requires good-faith compliance and practicable elimination of vestiges. Those principles permit partial withdrawal when supported by findings. They also require attention to connections among assignments, personnel, resources and other remaining duties. The court must assess whether retaining control is necessary or practicable to complete compliance elsewhere and preserve an effective remaining remedy. The contrary categorical rule would preserve restrictions that no longer perform remedial work. The opposite shortcut would mistake progress in one area for completion of the whole decree. Rufo’s tailored approach to consent modification is consistent with this inquiry but does not displace the school-specific obligations. The Court orders findings, not final release.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430 — applied: the affirmative duty and operational inquiry continue across the unfinished decree.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 — applied: equitable flexibility remains tied to effective repair of the violation.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 — clarified for partial withdrawal: good faith and practicable elimination must be assessed in that setting.
- Milliken v. Bradley, 433 U.S. 267 — applied: restoration of local control remains consistent with completion of the constitutional remedy.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, current-term decision January 15, 1992 — preserved within its consent-modification scope; it does not replace the Green/Dowell release inquiry.

### 2. Causation and the limits of the remand

**Holding and operative rule.** The district bears the burden of showing that imbalance for which it disclaims remedial responsibility is not traceable to the constitutional violation; genuinely independent demographic change does not require perpetual racial balancing. Existing factual findings receive clear-error review, but findings on one question do not decide unexamined causal or remedial connections; the remand must resolve properly open issues and define the obligations released and retained.

**Authority.** Stone, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: eight votes; the separate opinions’ disputed factual applications are outside this holding.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that an explanation of population change must answer the question on which release depends. Spangler protects a district from endless readjustment for genuinely independent demographic change after it has performed the required remedial work. It does not establish that every residential or enrollment pattern is independent merely because migration occurred. Swann preserves the district’s responsibility to show why remaining imbalance is not an effect it must remedy. At the same time, ordinary appellate review respects factual findings actually made; a legal challenge to the completeness of an inquiry is different from disagreement with a resolved fact. The present remand requires findings on whole-decree good faith and the relationship between proposed withdrawal and unfinished remedies. It does not require relitigation of every demographic finding or endorse every causal conclusion. Specific withdrawal terms and retained jurisdiction allow the court to end unnecessary supervision while continuing to enforce the remaining decree.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Pasadena Board of Education v. Spangler, 427 U.S. 424 — applied with its independent-cause and completed-remedy limits.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 — preserved: the district’s causal showing and violation-linked remedy remain required.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 — applied: good faith requires demonstrated compliance, not an assurance alone.

## 3. Freeman: permissible common public formulation

### Audited common core

A district court may return control over a completed aspect of a school system before every aspect satisfies the desegregation decree, while retaining jurisdiction over the case and supervision of the remaining violations. Before doing so, it must determine that the district has fully and satisfactorily complied with the decree in the area to be released and eliminated the vestiges of the constitutional violation there to the extent practicable. It must consider whether retaining control in that area is necessary or practicable to obtain compliance elsewhere, and must find a demonstrated good-faith commitment to the whole decree and its governing constitutional and statutory obligations. Good faith is assessed through the district's conduct and compliance history, not an assurance alone.

The district bears the release showing. Racial imbalance alone neither proves a present constitutional violation nor establishes entitlement to release. The district must show that imbalance for which it disclaims responsibility is not causally attributable to the prior violation; genuinely independent demographic changes do not require perpetual racial balancing. Existing factual findings receive ordinary clear-error review. A finding made on one factual question does not resolve a different causal or remedial relationship that the lower court did not examine. The reviewing court must distinguish challenges to facts from claims that the governing legal inquiry was incomplete.

The Eleventh Circuit's categorical simultaneous-compliance restriction is vacated. The case returns through that court for further proceedings consistent with these principles. The court must identify issues properly remaining open under the parties' prior presentations and secure the necessary findings on whole-decree good faith and the relationship between proposed release and outstanding remedies. An order of partial withdrawal must specify the obligations and active supervision withdrawn, and those remaining. Jurisdiction and authority to remedy the remaining violations continue. No particular component is released by this Court's judgment, and this opinion makes no new finding that every current imbalance has an independent cause.
