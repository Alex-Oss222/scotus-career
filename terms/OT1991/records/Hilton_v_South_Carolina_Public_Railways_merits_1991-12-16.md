## Law of the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** FELA's established inclusion of state-owned interstate railroads remains enforceable in a competent state court. This rule does not revive constructive waiver of Eleventh Amendment immunity in federal court or establish a general rule for every Article I damages action against a State.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion joined by five of eight participants supplies the controlling rationale; Blackmun supplies the sixth judgment vote.

**Controlling explanation:** *Parden* separately interpreted FELA's coverage of “every” interstate common carrier and treated operation of a railroad as waiver of federal-court immunity. *Welch* displaced the constructive-waiver rationale but expressly reserved statutory coverage. The remaining construction has governed the specialized railroad relationship for decades, Congress retained the relevant language, and States and employees arranged compensation against that background—including South Carolina's exclusion of FELA-covered employees from workers' compensation. *Will* construed a different statutory term and does not silently overrule the specific FELA holding. Statutory stare decisis thus requires retaining the independently grounded coverage rule unless the Court squarely abandons it. Under *Howlett*, a competent state court may not use state immunity to defeat a defendant's liability under valid federal law. The Court does not reopen Hilton's dismissed federal suit, decide negligence, or announce that entering commerce generally waives immunity.

**Precedent treatment:** *Parden*'s FELA coverage holding is reaffirmed; its constructive federal-forum waiver remains displaced by *Welch*. *Will* is distinguished; *Howlett* is applied.

**Law after decision:** State-owned interstate railroads remain FELA defendants in competent state courts under the specific retained precedent.
