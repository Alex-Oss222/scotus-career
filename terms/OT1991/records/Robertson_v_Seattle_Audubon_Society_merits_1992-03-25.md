**Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society** — No. 90-1596; OT1991.

**Material record and posture:** Environmental groups challenged Forest Service and BLM timber activity under MBTA, NEPA, NFMA, FLPMA and OCLA. The fiscal-year 1990 Northwest Timber Compromise both required sales and imposed new protected-area limits. Section 318(b)(6)(A) treated compliance with (b)(3) and (b)(5) as meeting the statutory requirements identified by the pending cases’ captions. Individual-sale compliance remained for judicial determination; some additional BLM areas were to be designated after enactment. The Ninth Circuit held that Congress directed results under unchanged law and could not amend substantive law in an appropriations measure.

## 6. Controlling holdings

### 1. Substitution of governing law

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 318(b)(6)(A) changes the statutory law applicable to the identified timber disputes by making compliance with the new restrictions sufficient to meet the specified old requirements, while leaving actual compliance for courts to decide. That clear substantive amendment is effective although enacted in an appropriations statute and does not violate Article III on the ground decided below.

**Authority.** Thomas, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: a unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that Congress supplied new legal standards and left their application to the judiciary. Before the Compromise, the challenges depended on the identified environmental requirements; afterward, compliance with the two new provisions could satisfy those obligations. Simpson explains why the specific enactment qualifies the more general laws. No provision finds that a particular timber sale meets the new restrictions, and the contemplated later designation of protected areas confirms that judicial application remains necessary. The case captions identify the requirements affected; they do not themselves decide facts. Will permits Congress to amend substantive law clearly in an appropriations act, while TVA’s caution against implied repeal does not defeat this express substitution. Because the premise of the circuit’s Klein ruling fails, the Court need not define Klein’s full reach. It reserves the unraised challenge to narrowly targeted legislation and leaves actual sale compliance for remand.
