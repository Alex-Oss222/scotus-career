# Canonical Decision Record — Campos v. City of Houston (in chambers)

## Event and chronology

- **Matter:** *Campos v. City of Houston*, Application No. A-301; Fifth Circuit No. 91-6100; reported comparator at 502 U.S. 1301 (1991).
- **Event:** In-chambers denial of original injunction and partial grant of stay pending appeal.
- **Effective date:** October 29, 1991, after Thomas's accession and circuit reallotment.
- **Posture:** Emergency application after the district court and Fifth Circuit denied a stay of an order directing Houston to conduct November 5 district-council elections under objected-to Plan #2.

## Participation and entering law

- **Decisionmaker:** O'Connor, Circuit Justice for the Fifth Circuit under the simulation's October 7 and October 23 allotment orders. No referral to the Court is established; no other Justice participates.
- **Law entering:** Supreme Court Rules 22 and 23; 28 U.S.C. § 1651(a); *Ripon Society*, 409 U.S. 1222 (1972) (in chambers); Voting Rights Act §§ 2 and 5; *McDaniel v. Sanchez*, 452 U.S. 130 (1981); *Clark v. Roemer*, 500 U.S. 646 (1991). Earlier simulated holdings are immaterial.
- **Snapshot:** A Circuit Justice may stay the injunctive aspect of an interlocutory order to preserve appellate jurisdiction, considering probable error, irreparable injury, and public interest. A covered jurisdiction may not implement an unprecleared legislative change merely because a court adopts it; any extreme-circumstance qualification remained unresolved.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- O'Connor denies the requested original injunction stopping the entire municipal election process. She grants a stay of the October 12 order only insofar as it directs use of Plan #2 for the November 5 district-council elections, pending the Fifth Circuit's disposition or further order.
- O'Connor issues an in-chambers opinion. It is a single-Justice ruling and not an opinion or holding of the Court.

## Law of the decision

- **Authority:** No Court precedent is created. For this application, O'Connor concludes that the original blanket injunction lacks an adequate source and that the narrower stay is authorized by § 1651(a).
- **Application:** Applicants showed probable error because Plan #2 remained unprecleared and judicial adoption did not change its legislative character; implementation threatened irreparable statutory injury. The precleared Plan #3 and other judicial remedies undercut the claimed necessity for Plan #2. Election disruption and ballots already cast were weighty but did not justify implementation without clearance on this record.
- **Limits:** The ruling does not select a plan, halt unaffected elections, preclear Plan #2, reinstate the old plan, decide § 2 liability, finally decide the three-judge-court objection, or recognize or foreclose an extreme-circumstance exception.
