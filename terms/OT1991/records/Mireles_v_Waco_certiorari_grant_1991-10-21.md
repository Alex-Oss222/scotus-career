# Canonical Decision Record — Mireles v. Waco

## Event and chronology

- **Matter:** *Mireles v. Waco*, No. 91-311, 502 U.S. 9 (1991).
- **Event:** Grant of certiorari for plenary consideration; request for summary reversal not granted.
- **Effective date:** October 21, 1991.
- **Posture and question:** Petition from the Ninth Circuit's reversal of a Rule 54(b) dismissal. The granted question is whether absolute judicial immunity covers allegations that a judge ordered officers to bring counsel before him using excessive force.

## Participation and entering law

- **Participating:** Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. **Nonparticipating:** Thomas, not yet seated. Quorum present.
- **Law entering:** *Bradley v. Fisher*, 13 Wall. 335 (1872); *Pierson v. Ray*, 386 U.S. 547 (1967); *Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978); *Forrester v. White*, 484 U.S. 219 (1988); and related functional-immunity authorities. *Ibarra* was effective but immaterial.
- **Snapshot:** Existing law denied absolute immunity for nonjudicial acts and acts in the complete absence of jurisdiction, while protecting judicial acts despite error, malice, or excess of authority. It did not clearly settle whether the alleged production command and force command were one function or two.

## Stone

- Approved Section II version 1.0 remained compatible with the record and entering law.
- Stone opposes summary reversal and votes to grant plenary review. If merits were reached on the papers, he would affirm narrowly by treating the alleged direction to use unnecessary force as a distinct nonjudicial act. That conditional merits position does not become law at this event.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Public action:** The petition is granted. The case is set for briefing and argument. The request for summary reversal is not granted.
- **Public vote:** White, Blackmun, O'Connor, and Souter voted to grant and summarily reverse. Stone voted to grant and set for argument. Scalia and Kennedy would deny summary disposition and the petition; Stevens would deny the petition on his view of the merits. Four votes granted certiorari; no participating majority supported summary reversal.
- **Opinion topology:** No merits opinion issues. Stone announces a short statement respecting the grant, explaining only that the unresolved functional boundary warrants plenary consideration. Scalia, joined by Kennedy, notes that he would deny the petition. Stevens notes that he would deny the petition. These writings create no merits holding.

## Law of the decision

- The grant creates no substantive holding and does not alter judicial-immunity doctrine. The Ninth Circuit judgment remains in place pending this Court's later disposition.
