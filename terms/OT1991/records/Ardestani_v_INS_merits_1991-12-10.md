# Canonical Decision Record — *Ardestani v. INS*

## Event and chronology

- **Natural key:** *Ardestani v. INS*, 502 U.S. 129 (1991), No. 90-1141; merits decision; December 10, 1991; OT1991.
- **Dates and route:** Argued October 8 and decided December 10, 1991, on certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 904 F.2d 1505.
- **Question and relief:** Whether an administrative deportation hearing is an adjudication under 5 U.S.C. §554 for EAJA §504. Ardestani seeks restoration of a $1,071.85 administrative fee award.
- **Stage:** Plenary merits review; affirmance closes the Supreme Court and administrative fee matter while leaving asylum intact.
- **Dependencies:** *MCorp* reinforces attention to a specific administrative statutory structure but does not decide EAJA. *Hafer* does not govern federal fee liability. The same-day *Simon & Schuster* decision is uncoordinated and uses the common start-of-day baseline.

## Participation and entering law

- Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas did not participate because argument preceded his oath. Eight participants; quorum present; five votes required.
- EAJA §§504(a)(1) and 504(b)(1)(C) cover an adversary adjudication “under section 554” with the United States represented. INA §242(b) supplies the sole and exclusive deportation procedure. *Marcello* holds that the INA scheme supersedes APA hearing provisions. *Shaw* requires statutory authority for monetary liability. *Sullivan v. Hudson* covers some remand work integral to a qualifying judicial action, not every standalone agency hearing. *Pierce* governs substantial justification only after coverage.

## Stone

- Section II version 1 is approved. Stone votes to affirm and joins O'Connor's opinion.
- Fixed rule: a deportation proceeding governed by the INA rather than §554 is outside administrative EAJA §504. Leave asylum and its factual basis undisturbed; do not assess whether INS was substantially justified, restrict a covered judicial EAJA claim, or decide constitutional claims.
- No residual waiver canon is needed after the statutory structure resolves coverage. Only compatible wording and full join used.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Eleventh Circuit affirmed, 6-2. Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support affirmance; Blackmun and Stevens dissent.
- **Opinion:** O'Connor writes for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. Stone assigns O'Connor as Chief Justice in the majority.
- **Dissent:** Blackmun, joined by Stevens, reads the cross-reference functionally to cover a statutorily required, on-the-record adversary hearing where the United States is represented.
- **Remedy:** No administrative fee award under §504. Ardestani's asylum grant remains undisturbed.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding and operative rule:** An administrative deportation hearing governed by the INA's exclusive procedure rather than by 5 U.S.C. §554 is not an adjudication “under section 554” and therefore is outside EAJA §504's administrative fee authorization, even if the hearing resembles a formal APA adjudication.
- **Authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of eight participants.
- **Controlling explanation:** EAJA did not refer generally to formal or trial-type agency hearings; it chose adjudications “under section 554.” *Marcello* establishes that the INA's deportation procedure supersedes the APA hearing provisions and supplies the exclusive statutory source for this proceeding. Functional similarity and agency regulations cannot enlarge Congress's fee authorization. EAJA's remedial purpose supports extending protection through enacted language, but it cannot erase the selected cross-reference. *Sullivan v. Hudson* involved administrative work integral to a qualifying court action and supplies no such predicate for this standalone administrative request. Because coverage fails, the Court does not review whether the agency's position was substantially justified under *Pierce*. The asylum award, judicial EAJA claims with their own predicate, and any separately presented constitutional claim remain open.
- **Precedent treatment:** *Marcello* is applied; *Sullivan v. Hudson* is distinguished; *Shaw* and *Pierce* remain unchanged.
- **Law after decision:** Effective December 10, INA deportation hearings outside §554 governance do not qualify for administrative EAJA §504 awards.

## Continuity

- The Supreme Court and administrative fee proceedings close; asylum remains effective.
- **Published noncontrolling position:** Blackmun and Stevens would treat “under section 554” as a functional category encompassing statutorily required on-the-record adversary adjudications with government counsel. Noncontrolling dissent.
