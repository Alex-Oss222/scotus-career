# CANONICAL DECISION RECORD — Mireles v. Waco

**Natural authority:** *Mireles v. Waco*, 502 U.S. 9; No. 91-311  
**Event and effective date:** Summary merits disposition, October 21, 1991  
**Validation status:** Validated and committed

## Law-Entering Snapshot

The October 15 *Ibarra* holding concerns appellate timing and supplies no immunity rule. The pre-divergence baseline includes *Bradley*, *Stump*, *Forrester*, and *Burns*: immunity is functional; judicial acts remain protected despite grave error, but nonjudicial functions do not.

## Participation and judgment

Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participate; the Marshall line is vacant. Four Justices—White, Blackmun, O'Connor, and Souter—would reverse on judicial immunity. Stone and Stevens would affirm because the complaint plausibly alleges a separable operational direction to inflict unnecessary violence. Scalia and Kennedy would affirm because summary reversal is unwarranted and review should have been denied. The Court is equally divided, 4–4; the Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed without precedential effect.

## Published writings and authority

The Court issues a per curiam order announcing equal division. Stone files an opinion concurring in the affirmance, joined by Stevens: an attendance order remains immune, but a plausibly separable direction to inflict gratuitous violence is operational conduct; liability, proof, and defenses remain open. Scalia files an opinion concurring in the affirmance, joined by Kennedy, stating that the extraordinary case does not warrant summary review. The four votes to reverse produce no controlling opinion. No proposition in any writing is a holding of the Court.

**Law after decision:** The Ninth Circuit judgment stands only between the parties. Existing immunity doctrine remains unchanged. Stone's joined concurrence is a material published noncontrolling position for continuity.
