# Canonical Decision Record — United States v. Ibarra

## Event and chronology

- **Matter:** *United States v. Ibarra*, No. 90-1713, 502 U.S. 1 (1991).
- **Event:** Grant of certiorari, grant of leave to proceed in forma pauperis, summary vacatur, and remand.
- **Effective date:** October 15, 1991. First OT1991 event; no same-day dependency.
- **Posture and question:** On petition from the Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the Government's suppression appeal. The question is whether a timely motion to reconsider makes the original suppression order nonfinal until the motion is denied when the motion reasserts a previously abandoned theory.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding and authority:** A timely motion asking a district court to reconsider a question decided in order to alter the rights adjudicated renders the order nonfinal for appeal purposes until denial; the full appeal period then runs from denial. The apparent strength of the motion, including reliance on a previously abandoned ground, does not alter that rule. Opinion of the Court, eight Justices.
- **Rationale and application:** A merits screen would make appellate timing depend on a preliminary appraisal of the reconsideration motion and encourage protective appeals. Ibarra's notice, filed 27 days after denial, was timely.
- **Limits:** The Court does not decide bad faith, successive motions, whether the timing provision is jurisdictional in modern terminology, or the suppression merits.
- **Precedent treatment:** *Healy* and *Dieter* are applied and their bright-line operation reaffirmed; the Tenth Circuit's additional exception is rejected.
