## Controlling holdings

### Vested entitlement exists before payment or an award

**Holding and operative rule.** A worker whose substantive compensation right has vested is a person entitled to compensation under LHWCA section 33(g), even if the employer disputes liability, makes no current payments and is not subject to an award. When the covered third-party settlement is less than the statutory compensation entitlement, section 33(g)(1) requires prescribed written approval from employer and carrier before execution and filing within thirty days; failure to obtain and file required approval terminates compensation and medical benefits under subsection (g)(2), while judgments and settlements at least equal to compensation entitlement require notification rather than that prior approval.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Six of nine Justices adopt the entitlement interpretation, the necessary relationship between the subsections and the application. No independently sufficient alternative ground or Marks inquiry is needed.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that entitlement concerns an existing legal right, rather than its later acknowledgment or adjudication. Demarest v. Manspeaker requires enforcement of clear statutory language; Congress's addition of subsection (g)(2) expressly makes prior payment or acknowledgment immaterial. Retention of one phrase does not adopt every earlier administrative limitation when the same amendment supplies contrary context. The uses of entitlement in sections 14(h) and 33(f) reinforce that reading: disputed claims can prompt investigation, and third-party recovery can reduce liability before payments begin. Sullivan v. Stroop and Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury support consistent meaning in that setting. The estate's reading would separate those linked protections. Banks v. Chicago Grain Trimmers Association explains why approval guards against a settlement leaving excessive liability with the employer. Notification still serves independent purposes for judgments and sufficiently large settlements, so that requirement is not redundant. Cowart's vested right brings him within the condition despite the missing award and current payments. The Court does not decide whether employer participation independently satisfies or excuses approval, the gross-versus-net comparison, or the effect on final awards to other workers.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Demarest v. Manspeaker, 498 U.S. 184 (1991): applied; contrary administrative practice and later retention of words cannot overcome clear enacted meaning.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984), and K mart Corp. v. Cartier,Inc., 486 U.S. 281 (1988): preserved; no deference may override unambiguous statutory text. No new rule of deference to a changed litigation position is announced.
- Sullivan v. Stroop, 496 U.S. 478 (1990), and Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury, 475 U.S. 851 (1986): applied; the recurring statutory phrase is read consistently where surrounding provisions reinforce that meaning.
- Banks v. Chicago Grain Trimmers Association, Inc., 390 U.S. 459 (1968): relied on for approval's protection against an inadequate third-party settlement; the statutory function remains operative before acknowledgment of liability.
- Northeast Marine Terminal Co. v. Caputo, 432 U.S. 249 (1977), and Voris v. Eikel, 346 U.S. 328 (1953): their remedial-construction principles remain available but do not authorize an exception contrary to this provision's text and structure.
- Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director, OWCP, 449 U.S. 268 (1980): preserved concerning the Board's adjudicative rather than policymaking role; no special deference to the Board supplies a contrary construction.
