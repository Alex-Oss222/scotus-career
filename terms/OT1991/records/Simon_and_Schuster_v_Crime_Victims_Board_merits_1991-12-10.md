# Canonical Decision Record — *Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board*

## Event and chronology

- **Natural key:** *Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board*, 502 U.S. 105 (1991), No. 90-1059; merits decision; December 10, 1991; OT1991.
- **Dates and route:** Argued October 15 and decided December 10, 1991, on certiorari to the Second Circuit, 916 F.2d 777, which rejected the publisher's First Amendment challenge.
- **Question and relief:** Whether New York Executive Law §632-a unconstitutionally burdens speech by escrowing income from a person's account of crime. Simon & Schuster seeks a declaration of invalidity and an injunction against enforcement through §1983.
- **Stage:** Plenary merits review before decision; remand for declaratory and injunctive relief after reversal.
- **Dependency:** *Gibson* protects disputed compelled dues before ideological use but does not replace scrutiny of this content-based financial burden.

## Participation and entering law

- Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas did not participate because argument preceded his oath. Eight participants; quorum present; five votes required.
- *Arkansas Writers' Project* requires compelling justification and a necessary, narrowly drawn means for content-based differential financial burdens. *Leathers* distinguishes general financial rules from selective burdens threatening expression. *Caplin & Drysdale* addresses forfeiture of criminal proceeds rather than lawful compensation for discussing crime. The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern; paid books remain protected expression.

## Stone

- Section II version 1 is approved. Stone votes to reverse and joins O'Connor's Court opinion applying content-based financial-burden scrutiny.
- Fixed limits: invalidate the speech-dependent escrow and remand for appropriate prospective relief; do not award money to Hill, discharge victim claims, protect stolen property, or immunize publishing income from neutral judgments and collection rules.
- Compatible full join; no substantive discretion used.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Second Circuit reversed, 8-0. All eight participating Justices support reversal.
- **Court opinion:** O'Connor writes for Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and herself. Stone assigned O'Connor as Chief Justice in the judgment majority.
- **Separate writings:** Blackmun concurs in the judgment, generally agreeing but emphasizing that the statute is underinclusive as well as overinclusive. Kennedy concurs in the judgment because direct content-based burdens on fully protected speech are forbidden here without the Court's compelling-interest balancing formulation.
- **Remedy:** The case returns for appropriate declaratory and injunctive relief against applying §632-a to the contract. No award to Hill or discharge of victim or creditor claims.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding and operative rule:** A law that selects crime-related expression for a special financial escrow is content based and must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest. Section 632-a fails because it burdens an entire covered contract, including incidental discussion and unprosecuted admissions, while leaving other assets outside its special reach; it therefore may not be enforced against this publishing contract.
- **Authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter; six of eight participants.
- **Controlling explanation:** The escrow obligation arises from what the work says and restrains payment to the source, which affects a publisher's access to protected expression even without a purpose to suppress ideas. *Arkansas Writers' Project* supplies scrutiny for a content-based financial burden, and *Leathers* confirms that general economic regulation differs from selective burdens keyed to expression. Victim compensation and recovery of crime proceeds are compelling at their strongest, but §632-a is not fitted to those interests. It captures all income under a covered contract when crime appears only incidentally and extends to voluntary admissions never followed by prosecution. Excluding victimless crimes and releasing limited production or legal expenses do not cure that breadth. Neutral restitution, forfeiture, attachment, and collection mechanisms show that compensation can be pursued without this subject-matter classification. The Court decides only this statute and leaves materially different laws, stolen property, general judgments, and victim claims open.
- **Precedent treatment:** *Arkansas Writers' Project* and *Leathers* are applied; *Caplin & Drysdale* is distinguished. *Gibson* remains confined to compelled bar financing.
- **Law after decision:** Effective December 10, §632-a's content-based proceeds escrow is unconstitutional; neutral asset-recovery and creditor rules remain available.
