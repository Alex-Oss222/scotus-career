## Law of the decision

### This application is not a new rule under Teague

**Holding and operative rule:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O’Connor; six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Teague protects reasonable reliance on existing law, not a refusal to recognize what that law already required. Butler requires attention both to the rule announced by a later decision and to whether its proposed application extends the precedent in a new way. Godfrey had already rejected an aggravating phrase that gave the sentencer no meaningful guidance. Maynard applied that same prohibition to closely equivalent language. Mississippi’s formal weighing of aggravation against mitigation makes the defect consequential to sentence selection; it does not exempt the system from the preexisting constitutional protection. Zant expressly distinguished its nonweighing arrangement and supplies no affirmative permission to weigh an invalid factor. The State’s finality argument therefore does not make this particular application novel. Clemons identifies lawful methods of correcting the error rather than first creating the right to challenge it. The Court does not hold that every later capital decision applies retroactively, and it decides no Teague exception or unresolved procedural default.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

### A weighing error needs a constitutionally adequate cure

**Holding and operative rule:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s six-Justice opinion; Stone joins the weighing/correction rule with his approved conditional remedy and reserved application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an invalid factor placed on the statutory scale cannot be ignored simply by counting the valid factors that remain. Zant sustained a system in which the invalid factor did not do the formal weighing work present here. Barclay also involved actual review of the sentencing choice and a different kind of invalidity; it does not permit automatic affirmance of this constitutional weighing error. Clemons recognizes that a properly authorized state appellate court can itself weigh valid aggravation against mitigation, or can determine under constitutional harmless-error principles that the error did not affect the sentence. Chapman places the harmlessness burden on the State beyond a reasonable doubt. Bare proportionality review or an assertion that other aggravators exist does not establish either correction. The Court leaves the actual state review and the unresolved procedural-default defense to the lower courts. If habeas relief becomes warranted after those matters are resolved, a conditional remedy may permit lawful state correction; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 — distinguished: duplication in narrowing does not answer vague-factor weighting and cannot supply a prefinality reliance premise.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Does Teague bar relief from a Mississippi death sentence resting on an undefined heinousness aggravator?

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the question and necessary antecedents identified in I.A, subject to its threshold limitations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Teague rather than replace it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Godfrey already prohibited the unguided aggravation at issue when Stringer became final on February 19, 1985; Mississippi’s weighing structure does not make the requested protection a new rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apply Teague rather than replace it. Godfrey supplied a prohibition on unguided aggravation before this sentence became final. The challenged Mississippi factor performs formal work in weighing death against mitigation; that feature strengthens, rather than weakens, the existing requirement of guidance. Zant’s preservation of its distinct nonweighing result cannot become affirmative permission to put an invalid factor on Mississippi’s statutory scale. Maynard describes the same defect in closely equivalent wording. Clemons supplies permissible corrective options, not a new permission to challenge arbitrary sentencing for the first time. The proposed holding is that this particular application is old-rule; it is not that every subsequent capital decision applies retroactively.

The State’s concern about reopening final judgments is real under Teague, but finality does not authorize misidentifying a settled protection as novel merely because a later case explains a remedial branch. Conversely, the existence of the error does not establish that death could never be imposed here. A lawful reviewing state court may perform its own actual weighing if state law authorizes that function; harmless-error analysis asks whether the invalid factor affected this sentencing decision and places the Chapman burden on the State. The sentencing record, including mitigation and the prosecutor’s use of the invalid factor, must support the selected route. Stone proposes no new fact, numerical balancing formula, or presumption that remaining aggravators automatically cure error.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve the ultimate sentencing correction and any unpresented systemic capital challenge. Do not treat an invalid aggravator as necessarily negating death eligibility where valid eligibility findings remain, or conflate eligibility, selection, harmlessness, and reweighing.
