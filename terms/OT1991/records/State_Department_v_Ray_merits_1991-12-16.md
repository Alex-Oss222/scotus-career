## Law of the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Under FOIA Exemption 6, identifying information may be withheld when linking names to sensitive personal interviews would cause a substantial privacy invasion and the identifiers add little demonstrated value to public understanding of agency conduct after reasonably segregable substantive material has been released. A confidentiality assurance informs the privacy interest but does not itself create an exemption.

**Authority:** Stevens's Court opinion; all eight participating Justices support the operative rule and judgment, while Scalia and Kennedy do not join the broader discussion of possible derivative public benefits.

**Controlling explanation:** Exemption 6 requires a practical balance between the invasion of personal privacy and disclosure's contribution to public understanding of government operations. *Rose* supplies that balance and demonstrates that useful agency records may be disclosed after identifiers are removed; *Washington Post* confirms that information about particular persons falls within “similar files.” The released reports disclose the questions, answers, dates, sampling, and reported treatment, allowing scrutiny of the Department's monitoring. Adding names would connect intimate family, employment, and living information—and cooperation given under an accepted assurance of confidentiality—to identifiable returnees. On this record, the requesters identify only a general hope that renewed interviews might uncover inaccuracies, not concrete contradictory information or a demonstrated incremental contribution sufficient to outweigh that invasion. The Court does not require proof of government wrongdoing before invoking FOIA, decide that derivative investigation can never matter, or adjudicate Haiti's safety or any asylum claim.

**Precedent treatment:** *Rose*, *Washington Post*, and *Reporters Committee* are applied and preserved; waived Exemptions 1 and 7(C) are not reached.

**Law after decision:** Effective December 16, the Exemption 6 balance measures the incremental public contribution of identifiers against their concrete privacy cost after segregated release; confidentiality is relevant but not dispositive.
