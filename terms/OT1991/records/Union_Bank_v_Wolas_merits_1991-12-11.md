# Canonical Decision Record — *Union Bank v. Wolas*

## Event and chronology

- **Natural key:** *Union Bank v. Wolas*, 502 U.S. 151 (1991), No. 90-1491; merits decision; December 11, 1991; OT1991.
- **Dates and route:** Argued November 5 and decided December 11, 1991, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 921 F.2d 968.
- **Question and relief:** Whether payments on long-term debt may qualify for the then-current ordinary-course exception in 11 U.S.C. §547(c)(2). Union Bank seeks reversal of the categorical exclusion and review of remaining statutory issues.
- **Stage:** Plenary merits review; reversal and remand to the Ninth Circuit afterward.
- **Dependencies:** *MCorp* concerns bankruptcy's interaction with bank regulatory jurisdiction, not preferences. *Ardestani* concerns a distinct fee cross-reference. Neither alters §547. 

## Participation and entering law

- All nine Justices participated: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Quorum present; five votes required.
- Section 547(b) supplies the preference elements; §547(f) provides a 90-day insolvency presumption; §547(g) places the exception burden on the creditor. The then-current §547(c)(2) requires that the debt be incurred in the ordinary course of both parties, the payment be made in their ordinary course, and payment accord with ordinary business terms. Congress deleted the earlier 45-day limit in 1984. *Ron Pair* gives operative Code text effect unless demonstrably at odds with congressional intent.

## Stone

- Section II version 1 is approved. Stone votes to reverse and remand and joins Stevens's Court opinion.
- Fixed limits: long-term payments are eligible, not presumed ordinary; the Bank retains its statutory burden; the Court does not exempt all interest or commitment fees or decide appellate challenges the categorical ruling bypassed.
- Compatible full join; no substantive discretion used.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0. All nine Justices support the judgment.
- **Opinion:** Stevens writes for a unanimous Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Scalia also writes separately to rest the result directly on the enacted text without extended legislative-history or policy analysis.
- **Assignment:** Stone assigns Stevens as Chief Justice in the unanimous judgment coalition.
- **Remedy:** The Ninth Circuit must review any remaining challenges to application of all statutory elements. The Court does not itself restore judgment for the Bank.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding and operative rule:** Payments on long-term debt may qualify for the ordinary-course exception in the then-current §547(c)(2). Debt maturity is not an additional categorical exclusion, but the creditor must prove every enacted ordinary-course element for the particular payments.
- **Authority:** Stevens's opinion states the proposition for all nine Justices; Scalia's separate writing agrees with the statutory holding and judgment.
- **Controlling explanation:** Section 547(c)(2) contains no distinction between short- and long-term debt. Congress removed the former 45-day restriction and retained three express ordinary-course requirements, so a court may not recreate a maturity bar from legislative purpose or the amendment's perceived motivating examples. *Ron Pair* supplies the obligation to apply operative Code text when the result is not demonstrably contrary to congressional design. Equality of distribution and discouragement of destructive collection races coexist within the enacted preference rule and its exceptions; neither permits omission of the exception's words. Eligibility does not establish that these interest and commitment-fee payments were incurred and paid in both parties' ordinary course and according to ordinary business terms. Those questions, including appellate challenges not addressed under the categorical rule, remain for remand with the Bank bearing §547(g)'s burden.
- **Precedent treatment:** *Ron Pair* is applied. The Ninth Circuit's categorical *CHG International* construction is rejected; no Supreme Court precedent is overruled.
- **Law after decision:** Effective December 11, long-term debt payments are eligible for §547(c)(2), subject to all three contemporaneous statutory elements and the creditor's burden.
