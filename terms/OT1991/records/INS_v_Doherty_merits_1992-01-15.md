# Canonical Decision Record — INS v. Doherty

## Event and chronology

*INS v. Doherty*, 502 U.S. 314 (1992), No. 90-925. October Term 1991. Argued 1991-10-16; decided 1992-01-15. On certiorari to the Second Circuit, 908 F.2d 1108, which required reopening after sustaining the Attorney General's rejection of Ireland as the designated destination. The granted controversy concerns denial of reopening for asylum and withholding of deportation; the INS seeks reversal. Destination selection and detention are outside the disposition.

**Natural key:** INS v. Doherty; 90-925; merits decision; 1992-01-15; OT1991. Historical reporter citation is a locator, not an adoption of the historical decision.

**Material record and disputed premises:** Doherty withdrew his applications while seeking deportation to Ireland. The final administrative decision instead directed deportation to the United Kingdom. The Attorney General denied reopening based on lack of qualifying new material, tactical withdrawal, and independent statutory exclusions. The Irish extradition litigation did not adjudicate the INA exclusions. The exclusion grounds include persecutor activity under §1253(h)(2)(A) and serious nonpolitical crime under §1253(h)(2)(C); this Court makes no eligibility finding.

## Law of the decision

### Disposition and absence of a controlling reopening rationale

**Holding and operative rule:** The asylum portion of the Second Circuit's judgment is reversed; its withholding portion is affirmed by an equally divided Court. The disposition creates no controlling substantive reopening rule on either issue.

**Authority:** The per curiam judgment records eight votes for asylum reversal and the equal division on withholding. No separate rationale receives five of the eight participating Justices; no substantive proposition is treated as controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court disposes of the two forms of relief separately. All participating Justices would set aside the requirement to reopen the asylum application, but they do not unite behind a reason that controls a later case. Three rely on the regulation's new-evidence condition, one on the consequences of tactical withdrawal, and four on lawful discretion to deny asylum as distinguished from mandatory withholding. Those grounds are independent; one is not a logical subset of the others. On withholding, four would sustain the administrative denial and four would return the case for judicial review of the independent exclusion grounds. Under Durant, the equal division leaves the lower judgment in place without adopting its reasoning. Abudu continues to supply the previously established distinctions among reopening grounds. Neither the asylum vote nor the withholding disposition converts any separate opinion into a new rule binding other courts.
