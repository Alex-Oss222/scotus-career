## Law of the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2024(d) contains no implicit reasonableness limit on the duration of otherwise covered military service. An employee must still satisfy the provision's actual coverage, notice, service, and return conditions.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion for all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2024(d) ties protected leave to the period required to perform covered duty, and the Army required the three-year tour associated with King's position. The statutory scheme expressly imposes duration limits on neighboring service categories but omits one here. *Morash* requires provisions to be read as part of the whole enactment; that comparison makes the omission meaningful rather than accidental. *Fishgold* supports protective construction of reemployment rights, although the text and structure—not solicitude alone—control the result. The Hospital's concern about disruption from a lengthy voluntary absence is substantial, but the words “leave,” “employee,” and “return” do not provide an administrable judicial measure for writing a missing cap. The Court rejects both a per se three-year exclusion and a free-ranging reasonableness balance. It does not excuse failure to meet enacted requirements, decide damages, or determine obligations arising after a failure properly to return.

**Precedent treatment:** *Morash* and *Fishgold* are applied; circuit precedent adding a reasonableness condition is rejected. *Wolas* remains statute-specific.

**Law after decision:** Effective December 16, otherwise covered §2024(d) leave has no judicially implied duration limit.
