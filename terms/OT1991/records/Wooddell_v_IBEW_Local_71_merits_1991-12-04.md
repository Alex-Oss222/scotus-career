# Canonical Decision Record — *Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71*

## Event and chronology

- **Natural key:** *Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71*, 502 U.S. 93 (1991), No. 90-967; merits decision; December 4, 1991; OT1991.
- **Dates and route:** Argued October 16 and decided December 4, 1991, on certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 907 F.2d 151.
- **Questions:** Whether Wooddell has a jury right on his LMRDA damages claim and whether LMRA §301 permits his claim as a member-beneficiary alleging breach of an international-local union constitution.
- **Judgment under review:** The Sixth Circuit restored the retaliation claim but denied a jury and sustained dismissal of the union-constitution claim. Wooddell seeks reversal on both reviewed issues.
- **Stage:** Plenary review before decision; remand for adjudication afterward. Same-day baseline shared with the other December 4 matters.

## Participation and entering law

- Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas did not participate because argument preceded his oath. Quorum present; five votes required.
- *Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers Local 391 v. Terry* makes the legal or equitable character of relief the more important jury consideration and recognizes compensatory lost-pay relief as legal. *Plumbers v. Local 334* treats an international union constitution as a contract between labor organizations under §301. *Smith v. Evening News Association* permits individual beneficiaries to enforce covered labor agreements. The Seventh Amendment, LMRDA Title I, and 29 U.S.C. §185(a) supply the governing text.
- Earlier *Hafer* preserves the distinction between statutory coverage and ultimate liability but does not establish §301's scope.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 8-0. All eight participating Justices support both components.
- **Opinion:** White writes for the unanimous participating Court, assigned by Stone as Chief Justice in the majority. No separate writing.
- **Remedy:** Restore the union-constitution claim for §301 proceedings and permit a jury on the LMRDA legal damages claim. No liability or damages are awarded.

## Law of the decision

### Jury entitlement

- **Holding and operative rule:** A union member's claim for wages and benefits lost because hiring-hall referrals allegedly were withheld is a claim for compensatory legal relief carrying a Seventh Amendment jury right. Accompanying equitable requests do not eliminate the jury right on the legal issues.
- **Authority:** White's opinion joined by all eight participating Justices.
- **Controlling explanation:** *Terry* supplies both the historical-analogue inquiry and the more important classification of the remedy. Wooddell seeks replacement for earnings he says retaliation prevented him from obtaining, not return of identified funds held by the union or backpay incidental to reinstatement in a former job. That compensation has the character of legal damages. The combined request for injunctive relief does not permit a court to withhold a jury from the legal claim; the forum instead sequences legal and equitable issues consistently with the Seventh Amendment. The holding resolves entitlement to the factfinder, not whether retaliation occurred or what amount, if any, is recoverable.

### Section 301 plaintiff and contract coverage

- **Holding and operative rule:** Section 301 permits an individual union member who is a beneficiary of a qualifying contract between labor organizations to sue for its alleged violation. An international constitution binding the international and its local retains its interunion-contract character when a member invokes it.
- **Authority:** White's opinion joined by all eight participating Justices.
- **Controlling explanation:** Section 301 identifies covered contracts; it does not confine plaintiffs to the organizations that signed them. *Local 334* supplies the classification of an international constitution as a contract between labor organizations, and *Evening News* establishes that individual beneficiaries may enforce agreements within §301. Uniform federal interpretation serves the same function whether a union or a protected beneficiary brings the claim. Ordinary contract predicates still apply, and fear of trivial disputes cannot add an organizational-plaintiff restriction absent from the statute. The Court does not federalize every internal union dispute, classify standalone local bylaws, or decide whether defendants breached an enforceable promise.

### Precedent and current law

- *Terry*, *Local 334*, and *Evening News* are applied and preserved.
- Effective December 4, compensatory lost-referral damages carry a jury right, and member-beneficiaries may enforce qualifying interunion constitutions through §301. Liability and equitable-remedy questions remain distinct.
