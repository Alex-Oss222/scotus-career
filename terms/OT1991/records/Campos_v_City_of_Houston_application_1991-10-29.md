## Participation, judgment, and order

All nine Justices participate. By 8–1, Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas deny the free-standing injunction but grant the alternative stay insofar as the October 12 order directs use of Plan 2. Scalia would deny all relief.

The order states: “The application presented to Justice O'Connor and by her referred to the Court is granted in part. The portion of the District Court's October 12, 1991 order directing use of 9-5-1 Plan 2 is stayed pending further order of this Court. The application is otherwise denied.” The prohibition on malapportioned Plan 1 remains. The district court may consider a lawful interim plan and scheduling changes; this Court prescribes no map.

## Opinion and controlling proposition

O'Connor writes for the eight-Justice majority. A legislative plan does not become a judicial plan merely by being embodied in an interim order, and scheduling, turnout, and expense do not authorize judicial implementation of a plan subject to an outstanding §5 objection when a nonobjected alternative exists. The Court reaches only entitlement to a stay in aid of appellate jurisdiction. It does not decide §2, discriminatory purpose, permanent districting, Plan 3's ultimate validity, or whether every imperfect interim map is unlawful. Scalia dissents, emphasizing delay, uncertainty, and public disruption.

**Precedent treatment and law after decision:** The Court applies *McDaniel*, *Upham*, and *Clark* to the plan's legislative origin and outstanding objection. The controlling limited-stay proposition enters simulated law on October 29.
