# Canonical Decision Record — Campos v. City of Houston

## Event and chronology
- **Case:** *Campos v. City of Houston*, 502 U.S. 1301 (1991); Application A-301; OT1991.
- **Event:** Full-Court disposition of an application on October 29, 1991. Presented to Justice O'Connor as Fifth Circuit Justice and by her referred to the Court.
- **Posture / relief:** Applicants sought an injunction stopping the November 5 city election and alternatively a stay of the district court's October 12 order requiring Plan 2. The district court and Fifth Circuit had declined a stay.
- **Stage:** Application pending before; denied and closed after. Underlying appellate proceedings remain outside this action.

## Participation and entering law
All nine Justices—Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas—participated after Thomas's oath. Quorum was present; five votes controlled each component. The Court considered authority, likelihood of error, irreparable injury, urgency, and public consequences under §1651, §5, *McDaniel*, *Upham*, *Clark*, and *Ripon Society*. It did not decide permanent districting or the §2 claim.

## Stone
Approved version 1 controls. Stone voted to deny the free-standing injunction and grant only a stay of Plan 2. He authored a dissent from denial of the alternative stay, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, using the approved statutory and equitable ground and exact limits. No plan was prescribed.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions
The application was denied by order of the Court. The broad injunction was denied 9-0. The alternative stay was denied 6-3: White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas denied; Stone, Blackmun, and Stevens would grant the limited stay. The order stated no controlling rationale. Stone dissented from the alternative-stay denial, joined by Blackmun and Stevens. No other writing issued.

## Law of the decision
**Public procedural effect:** The application is denied. An unexplained denial of emergency relief creates no substantive Supreme Court holding and does not adjudicate Plan 2's ultimate validity.

**Law after decision:** No doctrine changes. The district court's order remains operative subject to proceedings in the lower courts.

## Separate positions and continuity
Stone's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, states noncontrollingly that a court may not implement an expressly objected-to legislative plan merely for scheduling, turnout, and expense where a nonobjected-to alternative exists; they would have stayed only the Plan 2 direction while leaving Plan 1 prohibited and lawful remedial alternatives open. This published position is material for future modeling. The application itself is closed; the Supreme Court retains no role.
