# Canonical Decision Record — *Gibson v. Florida Bar*

## Event and chronology

- **Natural key:** *Gibson v. Florida Bar*, 502 U.S. 104 (1991), No. 90-1102; merits decision; December 4, 1991; OT1991.
- **Dates and route:** Argued November 6 and decided December 4, 1991, on certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 906 F.2d 624.
- **Question:** Whether Florida Bar rule 2-9.3 adequately protects compulsory-dues payers who object to nongermane political lobbying.
- **Judgment under review and relief:** The Eleventh Circuit upheld the procedure except that interest must run from collection rather than objection. Gibson seeks prospective declaratory and injunctive protection. A retroactive-refund request first raised on appeal is outside the case.
- **Stage:** Plenary merits review before decision; remand for prospective relief after decision. The continuing assessment and approved bylaw preserve a live prospective dispute. Same-day baseline shared with the other December 4 decisions.

## Participation and entering law

- All nine Justices participated: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Argument followed Thomas's oath. Six constitute a quorum; five votes control.
- *Keller* permits compulsory integrated-bar funding for activities germane to professional regulation and legal services but protects objectors from nongermane ideological subsidy. *Hudson* requires adequate explanation, prompt impartial review, and escrow of reasonably disputed sums. *Ellis* rejects a rebate that permits temporary ideological use, while advance reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow can protect funds. *Abood* allows an objection requirement but does not require an objector to identify each expenditure; the organization bears the chargeability burden after opposition is known.
- *Zatko* concerns judicial filing fees and supplies no compulsory-speech rule.

## Stone

- Section II version 1 is approved by direct user instruction. Stone votes to reverse the delayed-protection and issue-by-issue-objection components, affirm rejection of facial panel disqualification, and remand for prospective relief.
- Fixed rules: known dissenters' reasonably disputed funds must be protected before use; a general objection suffices; advance reduction or adequate escrow remains available; Bar membership alone does not establish panel bias. Preserve the lower court's interest correction. No retroactive damages, universal opt-in system, categorical panel approval, or ruling on every lobbying activity.
- Stone authors the Court's opinion. Only nonmaterial wording within the approved envelope was used.

## Law of the decision

### Timing and scope of protection

- **Holding and operative rule:** Once a compulsory-bar member makes a continuing objection to financing nongermane ideological activity, the bar may not require repeated objections to each announced position or leave the reasonably disputed share available for use until each new objection arrives. The bar must provide adequate advance explanation and protect the disputed share before use through a reasonable reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow; it retains the burden to justify chargeability.
- **Authority:** Stone's opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight of nine participants.
- **Controlling explanation:** *Keller* applies the compelled-dues safeguards to an integrated bar without disturbing compulsory support for activities germane to regulation of the profession and improvement of legal services. *Hudson* requires explanation, prompt review, and protection of disputed funds, while *Ellis* establishes that later reimbursement and interest do not undo temporary compelled ideological financing. Florida collects annual dues before its twice-monthly announcements and does not escrow a disputed share until a member objects to a particular position. Budget estimates and protection of a reasonably sufficient amount can address uncertainty without exact advance prediction. Under *Abood*, a member must make opposition known, but the organization then bears the chargeability burden; repeated 45-day waivers improperly shift monitoring back to the dissenter. The Court leaves accounting detail and injunction terms to remand and adopts no universal opt-in requirement.

### Impartial review

- **Holding and operative rule:** Membership in the compulsory bar, standing alone, does not establish that every arbitrator chosen through a procedure giving the bar and objector one selection each and requiring a mutually selected or judicially appointed chair is constitutionally biased. Actual bias, a direct stake, or obstructive delay remains subject to review.
- **Authority:** Stone's opinion joined on this proposition by all nine participating Justices.
- **Controlling explanation:** *Hudson* requires reasonably prompt impartial review. The rule does not permit the Bar unilaterally to select the panel: each side chooses one member, neither controls the chair, and judicial appointment is available if agreement fails. The shared and indirect incidence of bar dues is not by itself the direct stake in a particular award that establishes facial partiality. This conclusion does not approve an actually biased panel, indefinite delay in constituting it, or a procedure that proves ineffective in operation.

### Precedent treatment and law after decision

- *Keller*, *Hudson*, *Ellis*, and *Abood* are applied and preserved.
- Effective December 4, an integrated bar receiving a general objection must protect reasonably disputed nongermane amounts before use and may not require recurring position-specific objections. Balanced selection of bar-member arbitrators is not facially invalid on membership alone.

## Continuity

- The Supreme Court proceeding closes; prospective remedial proceedings continue below. The existing correction requiring interest from dues receipt remains intact.
- **Published noncontrolling position:** Scalia would permit objection after each disclosed lobbying position followed by the rule's escrow and arbitration, reasoning that uncertain future activity need not be estimated in advance; he agrees that Bar membership alone does not disqualify arbitrators. Noncontrolling except for his join in the latter controlling proposition.
