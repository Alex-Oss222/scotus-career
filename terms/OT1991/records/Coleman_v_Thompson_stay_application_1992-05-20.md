## Holdings

### Stay requirements on the renewed habeas presentation

**Holding and operative rule.** A stay of execution pending certiorari review requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would find the issue sufficiently meritorious to grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, and likely irreparable harm without a stay. Coleman's combined presentation does not establish the necessary possibility of overcoming the surviving independent claim-default and successive-petition barriers; denial decides neither ultimate innocence nor a general freestanding-innocence or hearing rule.

**Authority.** The per curiam opinion is joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific application of Barefoot. Stevens concurs in the denial without joining that opinion. Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Souter dissent.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the prerequisites for a stay have not been shown on this presentation. Barefoot v. Estelle requires a sufficient prospect of review and a significant possibility of reversal as well as irreparable harm. Coleman v. Thompson and McCleskey v. Zant preserve independent claim-default and successive-petition barriers, subject to the miscarriage-of-justice exception recognized in Murray v. Carrier. The informant's disputed recantation, competing accounts of another confession, physical-evidence allegations and conflicting scientific interpretations must be considered together. They do not establish the necessary prospect of overcoming those barriers. The Court does not treat the applicant's choice of an expert as proof of scientific correctness, or the possibility of an adverse credibility finding as a definition of innocence. Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes preserves Townsend's hearing rule for a properly available claim; it does not remove an independent bar to the claim itself. The appellate court separately rejected factual innocence and cause. Execution entails irreparable harm, but that consequence alone cannot supply the missing likelihood requirement. The stay is denied without deciding ultimate innocence or final certiorari treatment.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983): applied; the ordinary capital-stay prerequisites remain distinct and no automatic stay is created.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991): applied; independent and adequate state claim-default rules and their established exceptions remain in force.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991): applied to the independent successive-petition and abuse barriers; its cause-and-prejudice rule and innocence exception are not replaced.
- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478 (1986): applied for the extraordinary miscarriage-of-justice exception; the Court does not require conclusive exoneration.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436 (1986): its plurality formulation identifies the lower court's stated factual-innocence inquiry; no broader controlling rule is attributed to that plurality.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1859: distinguished and preserved; the simulated holding concerns inadequate factual development of a properly available claim, not automatic removal of an independent claim-default bar.
- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963): preserved through Keeney; its material-fact hearing framework is not displaced.

**Limits and questions not reached.** No ultimate innocence finding, conclusive-exoneration requirement, automatic hearing entitlement, freestanding innocence merits holding, reversal of the preterm Coleman decision, or final disposition of petition No.91-8336 is made.
