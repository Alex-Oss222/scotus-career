## Law of the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** INA §242(a) permits a generally applicable release condition forbidding unauthorized employment when the regulation is construed not to reach authorized workers and retains individual status determinations and discretionary relief. That facial statutory ruling does not resolve constitutional objections or particular misapplications.

**Authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion for all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 242(a) authorizes release on conditions prescribed by the Attorney General; it does not restrict every condition to securing physical appearance. The regulation's heading, exception, contemporaneous explanation, and operating instructions establish that its target is unauthorized employment. *Witkovich* requires delegated immigration authority to remain connected to the statutory scheme, and Congress's labor-market protections supply that connection. *American Hospital Association* permits general rules to structure delegated discretion so long as legally required individual determinations remain. Here officials must determine existing work authorization, address colorable citizenship or status claims, and consider the regulation's avenue for discretionary relief. The narrower construction therefore defeats the proposition that every application exceeds statutory authority. The Court does not approve interference with lawful employment, presume disputed deportability, decide the complaint's constitutional claims, or foreclose an as-applied challenge where the represented procedures are unavailable or misused.

**Precedent treatment:** *Witkovich*, *Carlson*, *Seminole Rock*, and *American Hospital Association* are applied and preserved. *Ardestani* is confined to EAJA's §554 cross-reference.

**Law after decision:** Effective December 16, §242(a) supports the narrowed unauthorized-work bond rule with individualized status and relief determinations.
