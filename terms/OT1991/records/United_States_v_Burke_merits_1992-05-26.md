# Canonical Decision Record — United States v. Burke

## Event and chronology

United States v. Burke, No. 91-42, October Term 1991. Argued January 21, 1992; decided May 26, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 929 F.2d 1119, which reversed the Eastern District of Tennessee’s denial of refunds. The United States seeks reversal of the ruling excluding the employees’ Title VII backpay settlements from gross income. The question is whether the identified recovery under the pre-November 21, 1991 Title VII remedial regime is excluded by the then-applicable 26 U.S.C. §104(a)(2) and Treasury Regulation §1.104-1(c). The dates and docket are verified in the official report at 504 U.S.229. Render form: full. The judgment is 6–3 and no opinion commands a majority; the decision has no majority opinion and distinct judgment-supporting grounds.

## Record, posture, questions and reachability

TVA settled allegations that male-dominated salary schedules had been increased while female-dominated schedules were denied increases or reduced. The settlement allocated a $5 million employee fund using service and pay, with these respondents’ distributions subject to withholding. The underlying complaint sought backpay and equitable relief, and the payment’s Title VII source is undisputed. The agreement did not admit discrimination. The federal refund court denied exclusion; the Sixth Circuit’s majority treated the underlying personal right as controlling, while Wellford’s dissent distinguished compensation for earned but unpaid wages from compensation for tort loss. Federal income-tax classification is an independently reviewable legal question. Refund standing and the certiorari channel are established; no material finality or preservation defect bars that question. The original litigation’s counterclaim and settlement arrangements do not supply a separate tort allocation. The FICA issue is analytically distinct and not reached.

## Holdings
### Tax classification of this recovery: judgment without controlling rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Six Justices reverse the Sixth Circuit’s exclusion of the identified pre-1991 Title VII settlement payments from federal gross income. The result governs these parties, but no common five-Justice construction of §104(a)(2), no general remedial-range test, and no health-injury requirement is established.

**Authority:** Blackmun’s plurality, joined by White, Stevens and Kennedy, supplies four votes for its remedial-character rationale. Scalia and Souter concur separately in reversal. Their judgment votes do not add joins to the plurality’s rule. O’Connor, Thomas and Stone would affirm the exclusion.

**Authority boundary:** Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S.188, the narrowest judgment-supporting opinion must express an actual controlling ground, not merely the same result on these facts. Scalia’s health-injury construction changes the statutory class of qualifying injuries. Souter instead accepts the regulation, recognizes competing personal-right and contractual analogies, rejects making intangible remedies indispensable, and resolves uncertainty through narrow construction of exclusions. Neither rationale is established as a logical subset of the plurality’s remedial classification. The six votes therefore support the judgment without a unified statutory rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not resolve the separate FICA definition of wages; treatment of punitive damages, ordinary wage settlements, distinct tort allocations or amended statutory regimes; or the Title VII jury-trial question. The settlement supplies no adjudicated finding of discrimination. No independently sufficient alternative holding commands five votes.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

United States v. Burke, May 26, 1992: Blackmun, joined by White, Stevens and Kennedy, plurality — old Title VII’s backpay/equitable structure fails the tort-type personal-injury classification under §104(a)(2); remedies are material to the claim’s nature, and the holding would be confined to the old regime. Scalia, concurrence in judgment — §104’s personal injuries or sickness context concerns physical or mental health; backpay entitlement does not require such injury, and an inconsistent Treasury interpretation cannot override the statute; he expressly recognizes that the Government did not argue this ground. Souter, concurrence in judgment — no indispensable intangible-remedy requirement; tort and employment-contract analogies both bear weight, and an unclear exclusion fails under the narrow-construction canon. O’Connor, joined by Thomas, dissent — the statutory right against discrimination is tort-type irrespective of the available remedy’s limitation, and compensatory wage measurement does not negate personal injury. Stone, dissent — this identified intentional-discrimination settlement qualifies by the source and object of its payment, while ordinary unpaid wages, other non-tort payments, punitive damages and amended regimes remain outside his rule. All these rationales are noncontrolling; none receives five joins or an established narrowest-ground status.
