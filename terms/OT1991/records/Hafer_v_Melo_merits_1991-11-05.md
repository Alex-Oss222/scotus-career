# Canonical Decision Record — Hafer v. Melo

## Event and chronology

- **Matter/event/date:** *Hafer v. Melo*, No. 90-681, 502 U.S. 21 (1991); merits decision, November 5, 1991.
- **Posture/question:** On certiorari to the Third Circuit. Whether state officials sued in their individual capacities are “persons” under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

## Participation and entering law

- **Participating:** Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. **Nonparticipating:** Thomas, because argument occurred October 15 before his judicial oath. Quorum present.
- **Entering law:** Section 1983; *Will v. Michigan Department of State Police*, 491 U.S. 58 (1989); *Kentucky v. Graham*, 473 U.S. 159 (1985); *Scheuer v. Rhodes*, 416 U.S. 232 (1974); *Monell*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978). Earlier simulated decisions were immaterial.
- **Snapshot:** *Will* excluded States and official-capacity damages defendants from “person,” while the received distinction between official- and personal-capacity claims left officers personally answerable for action under color of law subject to applicable defenses.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Third Circuit affirmed, eight to zero. The personal-capacity claims return for further proceedings; no damages or other remedy is awarded.
- **Opinion:** Opinion of the Court by O'Connor, assigned by Stone and joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding:** State officials sued in their individual capacities are persons under § 1983. Official action may be action under color of state law without converting a claim against the officer personally into a claim against the State. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a personal-capacity damages claim, and official character alone supplies no absolute immunity. Unanimous Opinion of the Court.
- **Rationale/application:** *Will* concerns the identity of the real defendant in an official-capacity claim; it does not remove individual officers from the statutory term. Concerns about reasonable official action belong to immunity doctrine rather than the definition of person. Hafer's asserted *Will* defense therefore fails.
- **Limits:** No rule about the content or sequence of qualified immunity is adopted. Failure of this defense establishes neither violation nor liability.
- **Precedent treatment:** *Will*, *Graham*, and *Scheuer* are distinguished and applied, not altered.
