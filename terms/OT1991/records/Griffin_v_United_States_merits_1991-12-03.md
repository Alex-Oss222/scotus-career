# Canonical Decision Record — Griffin v. United States

## Event and chronology

- **Matter/event/date:** *Griffin v. United States*, No. 90-6352, 502 U.S. 46 (1991); merits decision, December 3, 1991. It shares the start-of-day baseline with *MCorp* and is not dependent on it.
- **Question/posture:** On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit; whether due process requires setting aside a general verdict on a multiple-object conspiracy when evidence is insufficient for one object.

## Participation and entering law

- **Participating:** Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. **Nonparticipating:** Thomas, because argument occurred October 15.
- **Entering law:** 18 U.S.C. § 371; Rule 7(c)(1); *Stromberg*, 283 U.S. 359; *Yates*, 354 U.S. 298; *Turner*, 396 U.S. 398; *Jackson*, 443 U.S. 307. No material earlier simulated rule.
- **Snapshot:** Existing cases distinguished a submitted legal basis on which conviction cannot rest from an alternative factual basis lacking sufficient proof.

## Stone

- Approved version 1.0 remained compatible. Stone affirms, treats the objects as alternative means under § 371, independently adopts the *Yates/Turner* distinction, commends but does not require removal of unsupported theories, and reserves Sixth Amendment unanimity and supervisory rules.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Seventh Circuit affirmed, eight to zero; conviction remains intact.
- **Opinion topology:** Opinion of the Court by Scalia, assigned by Stone, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter. Blackmun concurs in the judgment and agrees no due-process violation occurred, but does not join the common-law survey; he emphasizes the confusion risk and commends separate counts or special interrogatories.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding:** Due process does not require setting aside a general guilty verdict on a single multiple-object conspiracy count when evidence is sufficient for one alleged object but insufficient for another. Section 371's objects are alternative factual means on this charge; proof of the charged agreement, overt act, and at least one unlawful object suffices. Opinion of the Court, seven Justices; Blackmun agrees in the judgment and constitutional result.
- **Controlling rationale:** *Yates* and *Stromberg* address legally invalid routes, which jurors are not equipped to identify; *Turner* governs evidentiary insufficiency, which jurors are equipped to assess. The evidence supported the IRS object.
- **Limits:** The Court does not overrule *Yates*, compel withdrawal of an unsupported object, adopt a supervisory rule, or decide Sixth Amendment or statutory unanimity as to an object.

## Continuity

- Adds the legal-invalidity/evidentiary-insufficiency rule for general verdicts and its § 371 application. It does not govern an instruction that makes a legally impermissible route available; that distinction enters *Estelle* the next day. Matter completed; cursor remains December 3 after the same-day group.
