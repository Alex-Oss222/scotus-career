## Law of the decision

**Ad valorem holding and operative rule:** The General Allotment Act's established fee-land rule and the Burke Act proviso authorize state ad valorem taxation of reservation land validly patented in fee under that Act, including qualifying land later held by the Nation. The tax follows the land, but patent source and any independently applicable federal protection must be determined parcel by parcel.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion joined by eight Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Blackfeet* requires unmistakably clear congressional authorization, and here the County relies on more than alienability or policy inference. *Goudy* construed the General Allotment Act to permit taxation following an ordinary fee patent, while the Burke Act proviso expressly addresses removal of restrictions on sale, encumbrance, and taxation. The Indian Reorganization Act halted future allotments and preserved trust periods but also created a distinct mechanism for restoring land to trust; it does not automatically return every existing fee parcel to tax-protected status. *Mancari* requires compatible enactments to operate together. *Moe* rejects general personal taxing jurisdiction over reservation Indians, but a specific tax on qualifying land does not revive that discarded jurisdiction. *Brendale*'s fractured analysis of tribal regulation of nonmember land supplies no governmental-impact test for this statutory tax question. Remand is necessary because the stipulated record does not establish every parcel's patent source.

**Sales-excise holding and operative rule:** Congressional permission to tax qualifying fee land ad valorem does not unmistakably authorize a tax imposed on an Indian owner's sale of that land; securing the transactional tax with a lien does not change its legal incidence.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion states this proposition for all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The annual levy is triggered by ownership and burdens the realty. The sales excise is triggered by a transaction. Under *Blackfeet*, authorization must be clear at the level of the tax actually imposed. A collection lien cannot transform an otherwise unauthorized transactional charge into a land tax, because that device would allow the State to enlarge congressional permission through its own collection law. *Capoeman*'s discussion of taxation after fee patent arose in the allotment context and does not provide blanket authorization for every tax related to land. The Court therefore gives the allotment provisions their demonstrated property-tax scope without creating general state dominion over the owner or transaction.

**Precedent treatment:** *Goudy* is reaffirmed for qualifying GAA fee land; *Blackfeet*, *Mancari*, and *Moe* are applied and preserved; *Brendale* is distinguished.

**Law after decision:** Qualifying GAA fee land is subject to ad valorem taxation; sales of that land by Indian owners remain outside the identified authorization.
