# Canonical Decision Record — Estelle v. McGuire

## Event and chronology

- **Matter/event/date:** *Estelle v. McGuire*, No. 90-1074, 502 U.S. 62 (1991); merits decision, December 4, 1991.
- **Posture/questions:** Federal habeas review of a California murder conviction; whether prior-injury evidence and the instruction governing its use denied due process.

## Participation and entering law

- **Participating:** Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. **Nonparticipating:** Thomas, because argument occurred October 9.
- **Entering law:** Due Process Clause; 28 U.S.C. § 2254; *Lewis v. Jeffers*, 497 U.S. 764; *Lisenba*, 314 U.S. 219; *Boyde*, 494 U.S. 370; *Winship*, 397 U.S. 358; *Sandstrom*, 442 U.S. 510; *Francis*, 471 U.S. 307; *Chapman*, 386 U.S. 18; *Rose*, 478 U.S. 570; and *Griffin* as effective December 3.
- **Snapshot:** Habeas does not correct state-law evidentiary error. An ambiguous instruction violates due process when there is a reasonable likelihood of constitutionally impermissible application; the State must prove established instructional error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. *Griffin* distinguished legally invalid routes from insufficiently supported factual routes but did not alter these instructional rules.

## Stone

- Approved version 1.0 remained compatible after express revalidation against *Griffin*. Stone would vacate and remand for harmless-error review: he rejects the evidence/state-law ground but finds a reasonable likelihood that the instruction relieved the State of proving identity.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Judgment:** Ninth Circuit reversed, five to three. White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support reversal and reinstatement of the district court's denial of habeas relief. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor would vacate and remand for harmless-error review.
- **Opinion topology:** Opinion of the Court by White, assigned by White as the senior Associate in the judgment majority, joined by Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. O'Connor writes an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Stone and Stevens: all three join the Court's evidence and state-law analysis but not its instructional holding or final judgment.

## Law of the decision

- **Holding 1:** Admission of evidence that the child had sustained repeated nonaccidental injuries did not violate due process; it was probative of whether the fatal injury was intentional even without direct attribution to McGuire, and federal habeas relief cannot rest on a state-law evidence ruling. Opinion of the Court, eight Justices at the proposition level.
- **Holding 2:** In the charge as a whole, there was no reasonable likelihood the jury treated McGuire's commission of the earlier injuries as established or treated the inference of identity as mandatory; the conditional wording and anti-character instruction preserved the jury's role. Opinion of the Court, five Justices.
- **Application/remedy:** Neither ground supports habeas relief. The appellate judgment is reversed and the district court's denial stands.
- **Limits:** The Court does not decide whether a state propensity-evidence rule would violate due process, whether the evidence was admissible under state law, or any later-developed habeas harmless-error standard.
- **Separate position:** O'Connor, Stone, and Stevens conclude the instruction created a reasonable likelihood of burden-relieving application and would remand under *Chapman* and *Rose* rather than grant an unconditional writ.

## Continuity

- Adds both holdings above and preserves *Boyde*'s reasonable-likelihood standard. The proposition-level unanimous evidence holding is distinct from the five-Justice instructional holding. Matter completed; cursor advances to December 4.
