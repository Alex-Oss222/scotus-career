# Canonical Decision Record — *Estelle v. McGuire*

## Event and chronology

- **Natural key:** *Estelle v. McGuire*, 502 U.S. 62 (1991), No. 90-1074; merits decision; December 4, 1991; October Term 1991.
- **Dates:** Argued October 9, 1991; decided December 4, 1991. The decision date is the manifest's historical ordering date and is confirmed by the reporter and docket.
- **Review and question:** On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 902 F.2d 749, which reversed the denial of federal habeas relief. The Court considers whether evidence of prior injuries, or the instructions governing its use, made the trial fundamentally unfair or relieved California of proving McGuire's identity as the killer.
- **Relief sought:** The warden requests reversal; McGuire requests habeas relief from his conviction.
- **Stage:** Plenary merits review before decision. After decision, the Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed and the federal habeas proceeding ends without relief.
- **Chronology dependency:** The December 3 *Griffin* rule concerns a factually unsupported lawful object in a general verdict. It does not govern whether this instruction relieved the State of elemental proof. The December 4 group shares a start-of-day baseline.

## Participation and entering law

- **Argument and decision:** Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas did not participate because argument preceded his October 23 oath.
- **Quorum and threshold:** Eight qualified participants; six constitute a quorum; five votes control the judgment.
- **Channel:** Federal habeas review under then-current 28 U.S.C. §2254.
- **Issues reached:** Federal constitutional effect of the evidence and charge. State-law admissibility alone is not reviewed.
- **Entering law:** *Lewis v. Jeffers* and *Pulley v. Harris* exclude mere state-law error; *Lisenba* and *Dowling* reserve habeas relief for evidentiary use producing fundamental unfairness. *Winship* requires proof of every element. *Cupp* requires whole-charge review; *Francis* and *Sandstrom* forbid relieving the State of elemental persuasion; *County Court v. Allen* separately governs permissive inferences. *Boyde* supplies reasonable-likelihood analysis of ambiguous instructions. *Teague* restricts new collateral-review rules. If constitutional instructional error exists, *Rose* permits harmless-error review and *Chapman* places on the State the burden to prove harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. No later AEDPA or *Brecht* rule is used.

## Stone

- **Approved source:** Section II version 1, expressly approved by the user for this Run. Its status line is superseded by that direct approval.
- **Fixed core:** Vacate and remand for harmless-error review. Reject evidence-alone and state-law grounds; hold that evidence of repeated nonaccidental injuries can establish intentional harm without identifying the abuser, but that this charge created a reasonable likelihood that jurors attributed unidentified injuries to McGuire and inferred the charged killing without independently finding the required facts. Apply established *Winship*, *Francis*, *Sandstrom*, *Boyde*, *Rose*, and *Chapman* rules; reserve a general propensity-evidence rule.
- **Final position:** Stone votes to vacate and remand. He joins the evidence-admissibility proposition of the Court's opinion and joins O'Connor's separate opinion on the instruction and remedy.
- **Discretion envelope:** Only nonmaterial wording and compatible joins were used; no substantive narrowing or additional ground was supplied.

## Public action, judgment, and opinions

- **Public action:** Judgment reversed.
- **Judgment:** 5-3. White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter reverse the Ninth Circuit. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor would vacate and remand for harmless-error review.
- **Remedy:** The federal habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered.
- **Opinion of the Court:** White, assigned by White as the senior Justice in the judgment majority, writes for the Court. Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter join throughout. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor join the evidence proposition but not the instruction or final-disposition portions.
- **Separate writing:** O'Connor, joined by Stone and Stevens, concurs in the evidence ruling and dissents from the instruction holding and judgment. They would vacate and remand for the Ninth Circuit to decide harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on the full record.

## Law of the decision

### Prior-injury evidence

- **Holding and operative rule:** On federal habeas review, evidence of a child's prior nonaccidental injuries may be relevant to prove that the charged death was intentional even when the identity of the person who caused the earlier injuries is unknown. Admission for that nonidentity purpose does not itself violate due process, and a defendant's choice not to contest accident does not remove the State's burden to prove intentional killing.
- **Authority:** White's opinion, joined on this proposition by all eight participating Justices.
- **Controlling explanation:** Habeas relief does not lie for an error of state evidence law alone. The prosecution retained the burden to prove an intentional killing, and the repeated injuries supplied evidence that the fatal injury was not accidental. That inference did not itself identify McGuire as the abuser, but relevance to one required proposition does not disappear because the defense concentrates on another. *Winship* supplies the State's continuing elemental burden; *Lisenba* supplies the narrow fundamental-unfairness boundary. The Court does not create a general constitutional rule governing propensity evidence and does not decide whether California correctly applied its own evidence law.
- **Application and effect:** The Ninth Circuit may not grant habeas relief on the evidence-alone theory.

### Instructional use

- **Holding and operative rule:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. The instruction here did not create such a reasonable likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.
- **Authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants.
- **Controlling explanation:** *Cupp* requires examination of the complete charge rather than isolated words, and *Boyde* requires a reasonable likelihood of unconstitutional application rather than a merely conceivable misunderstanding. The challenged instruction used conditional language and told jurors not to infer guilt from bad character. It did not direct them to find that McGuire caused the earlier injuries or shift the prosecution's burden to prove identity and every offense element. *Francis* and *Sandstrom* remain controlling where an instruction actually creates a mandatory presumption or shifts elemental persuasion, but the whole charge did neither here. Because no constitutional error occurred, harmless-error review is unnecessary. The Court does not hold that every prior-act instruction is valid or decide a general constitutional propensity prohibition.
- **Application and effect:** The instruction supplies no basis for habeas relief; the Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed.

### Precedent treatment and law after decision

- *Lewis*, *Pulley*, *Lisenba*, and *Dowling* are applied; their federal habeas boundary is unchanged.
- *Winship* is applied and unchanged.
- *Cupp* and *Boyde* are applied to whole-charge and reasonable-likelihood review.
- *Francis* and *Sandstrom* are distinguished and preserved.
- *Teague*, *Rose*, and *Chapman* are not needed by the controlling majority because it finds no error.
- **Reusable law effective December 4, 1991:** The reasonable-likelihood whole-charge rule applies to an ambiguous prior-act instruction; relevance to nonaccidental harm does not itself establish identity. A general constitutional propensity-evidence rule remains open.

## Continuity

- The conviction and denial of habeas relief stand. No Supreme Court matter remains open.
- **Published noncontrolling position:** O'Connor, Stone, and Stevens would hold that grouping unidentified grave injuries as acts of “the Defendant,” followed by an invited inference to the charged killing, created a reasonable likelihood of bypassing the identity finding. They would apply harmless review, not automatic reversal. This position is noncontrolling.
