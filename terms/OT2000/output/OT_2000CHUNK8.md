## Decisions and Dispositions

### Duncan v. Walker, No. 00-121

Merits decision, June 18, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 26, 2001; decided June 18, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, which reinstated Walker's federal habeas application because it treated the pendency of his earlier federal application as other collateral review under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2). The question is whether that subsection excludes federal habeas pendency from the one-year limitation period.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower courts must calculate time without the claimed federal-pendency exclusion, address independently preserved and legally available alternatives, and dismiss as untimely if none applies. No writ or automatic extension issues.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Justice Stevens filed a concurrence, joined by Justice Souter; both join the Court's opinion and judgment. Justice Breyer filed a dissent, joined by Justice Ginsburg, that would affirm reinstatement and remand.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Federal habeas pendency is outside section 2244(d)(2)

**Holding and operative rule:** The word State in 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review. A federal habeas application's pendency therefore is not excluded from the limitation period by that subsection, even when the application was timely and dismissed without prejudice because exhaustion was unestablished; a civil-rights pleading accompanying it does not change that classification.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt Holdings 1 and 2 on the shared textual and structural ground. The opinion does not adopt a categorical objection to independent equitable relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2244(d)(2) excludes time spent in a properly filed application for State post-conviction or other collateral review concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. In this coordinated phrase, the sovereign modifier identifies both forms of review. Reading other collateral review to include every federal application would leave State with little independent work. The broader category remains useful because state procedures vary and state custody may arise outside an ordinary criminal conviction. Congress's express distinction between state and federal proceedings elsewhere reinforces that reading.

Federal habeas is collateral review in ordinary usage, as Walker argues. But membership in that broad category does not answer which sovereign's proceedings Congress selected. State collateral litigation can complete exhaustion; federal habeas invokes the forum subject to the filing period. Rose v. Lundy supplies that exhaustion background without changing the enacted exclusion. A delayed federal dismissal can create serious practical difficulty, but this statutory construction does not depend on a promise that equity saves every applicant or on a conclusion that all equitable protection would frustrate the statute.

Lindh v. Murphy preserves provision-specific temporal treatment of the identified pre-enactment application; it does not exempt every later application about the conviction. Slack v. McDaniel preserves the first-application classification of its exhaustion return while expressly retaining independent timing requirements. Artuz v. Bennett distinguishes filing conditions from claim-relief bars; it does not turn a federal application into state review. Thus neither an initial timely filing nor non-successive status permanently satisfies the limitation for a distinct later application. Proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency remain independent requirements even for state proceedings.

##### 2. The statutory error requires reversal, with actual independent alternatives preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's reinstatement cannot rest on excluding Walker's first federal proceeding under §2244(d)(2). On remand the courts must calculate timeliness without that exclusion and adjudicate any independently preserved, legally available commencement or equitable ground under the applicable law and actual record; if none makes the application timely, it must be dismissed as untimely.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices directly join the remedial holding; agreement by Ginsburg and Breyer that independent alternatives remain available if their statutory view fails is not needed to establish its controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Walker's first application was dismissed without prejudice on July 9, 1996 because its allegations did not establish exhaustion. His May 20, 1997 application came after the appellate court's April 24, 1997 transitional deadline. That deadline is the premise of this review, not a ruling on every commencement dispute. The first dismissal left roughly nine months under that calculation, the later claims differed, and Walker did not return to state court. Those facts do not establish that court delay necessarily caused the late refiling. They also do not adjudicate every equitable contention.

The Second Circuit did not reach the equitable argument actually raised. Rejection of a statutory exclusion cannot substitute for adjudication of that independent argument on its own legal and factual terms. The decision establishes no equitable entitlement or finding of diligence, impediment or identical claims and grants no automatic extension. There is no still-pending first application or stay request for this Court to administer. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted application on the merits; it does not require such denial. The Court orders no universal warning, election, dismissal deadline, or stay-and-abeyance procedure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general availability and conditions of equitable tolling, including court misdirection or extraordinary external impediments, are outside the statutory question reviewed and lack supporting findings for a ruling here. The requirements for staying a mixed petition likewise lie outside that question. The underlying constitutional claims, ultimate entitlement to habeas relief, and comprehensive classification of state filing rules are not decided. Adjudicating a preserved alternative on remand is remaining legal and factual work under its own applicable law, not an award of relief. The first dismissal remains nonmerits; no writ, release, resentencing or automatic extension issues.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Rose v. Lundy supplies the exhaustion distinction between state review and federal habeas; its actual exhaustion rule remains unchanged and creates no compulsory stay regime here.
- Lindh v. Murphy retains its provision-specific protection of the identified pre-enactment application; it is distinguished from a permanent exemption for later applications concerning the same conviction.
- Slack v. McDaniel is reaffirmed within its exhaustion-return, non-successive-application scope. That classification supplies no limitations reset and leaves independent timing, exhaustion and default requirements intact.
- Artuz v. Bennett remains controlling on application-filing conditions as distinct from claim-relief bars. It does not decide the sovereign category or excuse a genuine filing defect.
- Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs and United States v. Brockamp do not decide this AEDPA statutory exclusion; their provision-sensitive treatment of equity supplies no ruling here either granting Walker tolling or foreclosing every lawful equitable ground.

#### Current Law

Section 2244(d)(2) excludes qualifying state collateral pendency, not federal habeas pendency. A timely first federal application and a later application's non-successive classification do not by themselves satisfy the later application's limitation period. The Court resolves the sovereign-category question while retaining proper-filing, pertinent-judgment-or-claim and pendency conditions. Independent commencement rules and properly preserved equitable contentions require their own legal and factual grounds; the decision neither grants relief through them nor extinguishes them. Lindh, Slack and Artuz retain their distinct force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Souter, concurs. They join the Court's textual construction and remand, but emphasize that this subsection's omission of federal pendency does not itself extinguish otherwise lawful equitable protection or permissible retention of a federal application while exhaustion is completed. Rose's exhaustion requirement should not be mistaken for a categorical prohibition on lawful protection against loss of a timely federal opportunity. Irwin's treatment of equitable tolling and Brockamp's attention to a particular statutory scheme require examination of the actual source and conditions, not a universal equation between every equitable accommodation and amendment of §2244(d)(2). They establish no entitlement for Walker, compulsory stay rule, warning duty or automatic suspension. Their affirmative safeguard explanation is separate from, and broader than, the Court's preservation of the actual alternative without deciding its general availability.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Ginsburg, dissents. Federal habeas is other collateral review concerning a state judgment, and the competing grammatical and neighboring-statutory arguments do not uniquely require State to govern that separate category. They would read the subsection to exclude qualifying federal pendency so that ordinary exhaustion administration does not randomly consume a timely first opportunity through the duration of a nonmerits federal proceeding. The rule would still require proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim and actual pendency; it would not make one timely filing permanently sufficient. They would affirm reinstatement and remand for the application's independent exhaustion and other remaining requirements, awarding no writ. If the Court's construction governs, they agree that the actual equitable alternative must remain open for lawful adjudication.

#### Source Notes

The [petition, both merits briefs and record](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386019_1654) supply the filings, exhaustion dismissal, claims difference, dates and lower judgment. The respondent's merits brief, page 5, identifies the equitable argument not reached below. [Title 28, 2000 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title28/pdf/USCODE-2000-title28-partVI-chap153-sec2244.pdf) supplies §2244; §2254(b)(2) separately permits merits denial of unexhausted claims. The legal question does not require a finding that an unexamined pleading established exhaustion or equitable entitlement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Second Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the statutory construction and preserved-alternative instructions. The federal habeas proceeding returns for timeliness adjudication; no merits determination or release is ordered. The April 24, 1997 deadline remains the review premise rather than a universal transition holding. If no independently available basis establishes timeliness after the required calculation, dismissal as untimely follows.

**End of entry: Duncan v. Walker, merits decision, June 18, 2001.**

### Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education, 185 F.3d 337; Fifth Circuit Nos. 97-30879 and 98-30132

Merits decision, June 18, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided June 18, 2001. On merits review of the Fifth Circuit judgment affirming an Eastern District of Louisiana injunction against the board's mandatory evolution disclaimer. The question is whether the Establishment Clause permits the complete required statement, which references biblical creation, disclaims an intention to influence or dissuade that account or any other concept, and recognizes a student's choice to form an opinion or retain parental beliefs. The district court found no secular purpose; the appellate court accepted the secular objectives of disclaiming inferred orthodoxy and reducing offense but found an impermissible effect. The constitutional question, not fee calculation, is under review.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6-3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The existing injunction against requiring the complete disclaimer remains in force. The judgment requires no replacement statement and prohibits neither objective religious study nor an otherwise lawful general statement protecting against compelled professions. No damages or additional curricular directive issues.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Scalia filed a dissent, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, that would reverse the federal constitutional ruling and remand.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The board may protect personal conscience but may not make preservation of a religious account part of its instruction

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may distinguish academic instruction from compulsory personal belief, but it may not require teachers, through a targeted curricular statement, to assure students that instruction will preserve a specified religious account. The board's mandatory evolution disclaimer, considered as a whole with its parental-belief and critical-thinking language, performs that prohibited official religious function; the injunction against requiring that statement is affirmed.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt the actual-function holding and affirmance of the unchanged injunction against the complete statement.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon as one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that inquiry, not independent prongs. The governmental act here is the board's own message, required of teachers immediately before evolution instruction. Its official attribution establishes who speaks; its text and actual use establish the prohibited function.

The board identifies biblical creation, assures students that the instruction is not intended to influence or dissuade that account or any other concept, and couples that assurance with their right to form an opinion or retain parental beliefs. The actual word or preserves choice. Evolution remains taught, students need not recite anything, and no prayer, profession, punishment or conversion is shown. Those points support the board's claim of conscience protection, but do not eliminate its selective undertaking to shelter a religious account through science instruction. The references to other concepts and critical thinking do not change that official task. The adoption history, including retention of the biblical reference after the creation-science controversy, confirms the function apparent from the complete policy rather than substituting a motive test for it.

Epperson v. Arkansas and Edwards v. Aguillard distinguish curricular authority from tailoring science instruction to religious doctrine. Settle preserves genuine academic study and legitimate pedagogical limits. Good News Club protects genuinely private religious access; it does not make this mandatory teacher message private. Marshfield requires examination of continuing public conduct, and Children's Healthcare permits a limited nondevotional accommodation rather than every religious preference. Lee and Santa Fe preserve prohibitions on official school religious activity; absence of prayer removes one possible violation but does not resolve this different use of public authority. Once that prohibited function is established, proof of successful persuasion or a completed penalty is unnecessary. Sincere secular objectives cannot justify religious means.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the complete adopted disclaimer. Its introductory paragraph directs teachers; its next two paragraphs are the statement delivered to pupils. Neither the first spoken paragraph by itself nor a severed replacement policy is adjudicated, because that distinct policy is outside the reviewed whole-disclaimer judgment. Mention of the Bible, religious motivation, offense or official attribution alone is not the rule. Objective religious study, academic criticism, private expression and otherwise lawful evenhanded protection against compelled professions remain permissible. A materially different general conscience notice is outside the presented question. No independent fee-calculation question is presented; the existing fee judgment is undisturbed, with no new fee rule. The remedy requires no evidence of conversion, new factual proceeding, damages or replacement curriculum.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton applies unchanged: attribution and actual function, with purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence, determine this official religious activity. No independent Lemon prong is restored.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing only as modified by Agostini; the lower court's independent effect/endorsement method is not adopted.
- Epperson v. Arkansas supplies the restriction against tailoring public science instruction to religious doctrine; its curricular boundary applies within the governing functional inquiry.
- Edwards v. Aguillard supplies the distinction between legitimate instruction and religiously directed curricular action; the present decision does not revive a freestanding purpose test or treat academic discussion of creation beliefs as forbidden.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board preserves genuine academic religious study and legitimate pedagogical requirements, while withholding immunity from religious curricular exclusions.
- Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe retain their official religious activity and school-created pressure protections. This statement is not prayer, and no new coercion finding is inferred from those decisions.
- Good News Club v. Milford Central School is distinguished: its protected access is genuinely private, while this required teacher statement is the board's own curricular act.
- Freedom From Religion Foundation v. City of Marshfield applies the need to examine actual continuing governmental religious conduct rather than rely on labels or possible observer offense.
- Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty v. Min De Parle retains the permission for its bounded secular-care accommodation and actual-function review; it supplies no general authorization to protect favored doctrine through official instruction.

#### Current Law

The existing unified establishment rule applies to mandatory curricular assurances as well as other official religious acts. Schools remain free to teach science, examine religious accounts academically and protect personal conscience, but may not undertake official preservation of a selected religious account through the targeted statement held unlawful here. The holding rests on the complete policy's function, not its mere mention of religion or proof of student reaction. The former independent Lemon tests remain displaced. The existing injunction and dependent fee judgment remain undisturbed; no severed substitute text is adopted.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. They apply Agostini's governing attribution-and-function inquiry but read the complete statement as an assurance that learning the scientific account does not compel personal belief. The actual or permits students either to form their own views or to maintain parental beliefs; the notice refers to other concepts and calls for critical examination while leaving evolution instruction in place. Official speech is conceded, but they find neither adoption of religious truth, religious instruction nor solicitation of adherence in those operative directions.

The retained biblical example and adoption history are substantial contrary evidence. In their view those features do not transform the text's reassurance into religious governance. Scalia's Edwards analysis distinguishes actual curricular commands from inferred motives; Kennedy's official-school-religion concern in Lee and Santa Fe remains, but the representative religious-choice mechanism at issue in Santa Fe is absent here. Their common ground does not turn absence of legal compulsion into universal immunity, allow a parental curriculum veto, prescribe creation teaching, or authorize religious grading. They would reverse the federal constitutional ruling and remand for conforming lawful relief, leaving dependent fee consequences to ordinary proceedings without choosing an amount. The disagreement concerns this policy's function, not revival of the lower court's independent tests.

#### Source Notes

The [original petition and appendices, opposition and reply](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0363) supply the resolution, adoption record, district judgment, Fifth Circuit opinion and rehearing materials. The resolution appears at petition page 2. Its teacher-direction paragraph is not one of the two spoken paragraphs. The final spoken paragraph uses or, a transcription corrected on rehearing; the whole-disclaimer limit appears at appendix 29a note 3. The appellate opinion is reported at 185 F.3d 337 and its fee judgment at issue below awarded $49,444.50.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fifth Circuit's judgment sustaining the injunction is affirmed. The bar against requiring the complete April 19, 1994 disclaimer continues in force; no replacement curriculum, new evidentiary proceeding or separate fee calculation is ordered. Lawful academic instruction, private expression and a materially different general conscience notice are not prohibited by this decree.

**End of entry: Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education, merits decision, June 18, 2001.**

### Idaho v. United States, No. 00-189

Merits decision, June 18, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 23, 2001; decided June 18, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed the district court's quiet-title decree after a nine-day trial. The United States sued as trustee, and the Coeur d'Alene Tribe intervened. The question is whether Congress's prestatehood treatment of the reservation, including authorization of partial-cession negotiations, clearly retained the disputed submerged lands against Idaho's otherwise presumptive title. Idaho concedes executive inclusion and congressional notice and does not challenge the historical findings.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6-3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The existing decree remains in force: United States trustee title; the Tribe's beneficial interest and exclusive use, occupancy and quiet enjoyment; the injunction against Idaho's adverse claim; and denial of the State's counterclaim. The decree applies only within the boundaries described by the March 3, 1891 and August 15, 1894 Acts and excludes the waters and submerged lands claimed by Idaho within Heyburn State Park. Its existing costs provisions remain undisturbed. No new geographic line or general regulatory decree issues.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Kennedy filed a dissent, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, that would vacate the reservation-based judgment and remand for lawful consideration of the preserved aboriginal-title alternative, without directing unconditional title in Idaho.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Congress's timely treatment of the identified reservation retained its unceded submerged estate

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal retention against future state ownership requires both clear inclusion of the submerged lands and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose. With inclusion and congressional knowledge conceded here, Congress's enacted prestatehood treatment of the defined Coeur d'Alene Reservation as an estate protected pending consensual, compensated acquisition clearly retained its unceded beds and banks; Idaho did not acquire the disputed estate at admission on July 3, 1890.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt the rule, its application and affirmance of the actual decree, including its Heyburn exclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Alaska requires separate clarity about inclusion and the intention to defeat state title; geography or purpose alone is insufficient. Shively v. Bowlby recognizes Congress's territorial power to retain beds for an appropriate public purpose. The conceded executive inclusion and congressional notice establish the identified estate, not the second requirement.

Congress's enacted treatment supplies that additional showing. The 1886 authorization concerned cession outside the existing reservation and required ratification. The 1888 railroad measure recognized the reservation, withheld rights until actual survey plats were filed and approved by the Secretary and compensation was fixed and paid, required tribal consent before rights accrued, and protected existing tribal tenure. The 1889 enactment authorized purchase only of nonagricultural portions chiefly valuable for minerals and timber that the Tribe consented to sell, on just and equitable terms and subject to congressional ratification. Read together, those measures preserved a known estate while Congress obtained particular portions through a limited consensual process. The lower lake and river belonged to the unceded remainder. The accepted admission disclaimer corroborates that specific retention; it is not independently sufficient for every Indian reservation.

Idaho correctly distinguishes protected use from title and notes that the agreements were unratified at admission. But the Court relies on the earlier statutes' own operative treatment, not on prematurely binding agreements. The agricultural and resource description defines the authorized purchase; it does not erase Congress's protection of the remainder. Transferring that remainder silently at statehood would defeat the arrangement Congress maintained. Tribal water uses explain the understood object and purpose, but cannot substitute for enacted authority. The Senate's June 1890 vote was not law. The March 1891 ratification confirms the earlier arrangement and does not newly divest a title that vested in Idaho. Alaska's legally effective interim segregation supports examining present legal effect; it does not equate every negotiation with a completed reservation or demand a particular verbal formula.

**Operative reach and remedy:** The affirmed decree quiets title in the United States as trustee, recognizes the Tribe's beneficial interest and exclusive use, occupancy and quiet enjoyment, enjoins Idaho's adverse claim to the covered beds and banks, and denies its counterclaim. It covers the reservation boundaries described by the March 3, 1891 and August 15, 1894 Acts, including the relevant Lake Coeur d'Alene and St. Joe River portions, but expressly excludes waters and submerged lands claimed by Idaho within Heyburn State Park. The existing decree's costs to the Tribe and agreement that the United States and Idaho bear their own costs are undisturbed; no separate new costs disposition is made.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not retain every navigable bed enclosed by an executive boundary, make a Senate vote law, dispense with a ratification condition, or use a general Indian-land disclaimer as an automatic title exception. The prior Coeur d'Alene immunity case did not decide this ownership or bar the United States' own suit. No title to the ceded northern lake, other aboriginal territory or the excluded Heyburn estate is adjudicated, and no new coordinates are supplied. The independent aboriginal-title theory was not decided below or developed on its merits here and may require first-instance factual consideration; affirmance on the reservation ground does not decide it. The suggested ratification relation-back theory supplies no alternative holding because the timely enacted ground suffices; no retroactive divestiture is authorized. Fishing restrictions, taxes, environmental regulation and jurisdiction over nonmembers are outside the title question. Recognition of title adds no damages, forced conveyance or general regulatory injunction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Alaska is applied without weakening either clear-inclusion or clear-title-defeat requirement. Its legally operative reservation and interim-segregation holdings support examination of this different enacted record; its later implementing decree did not change the test or authorize retroactive confiscation.
- Shively v. Bowlby supplies Congress's territorial power to retain submerged property for an appropriate public purpose, subject to the distinct clear-intent inquiry.
- Montana v. United States supplies the strong presumption of state title and the need to distinguish reservation description from intent to withhold navigable beds. The present specifically identified estate and enacted treatment satisfy what mere inclusion would not.
- Utah Division of State Lands v. United States supplies the separate inquiry into timely intention to defeat state title; its insufficient reservoir-control showing does not make a particular verbal formula indispensable here.
- Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho retains its prospective-officer and immunity distinctions. It adjudicated neither ownership nor this separate United States trust-title suit; no title holding is inferred from it.
- Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley remains a rule about the presented nonmember fee-land taxation. It does not resolve title or convert this ownership judgment into general tribal regulatory power.

#### Current Law

Alaska's two independent clear-showing requirements remain unchanged. Congress can manifest the necessary retention through the timely legal treatment of a specifically identified estate; the 1888 and 1889 enactments, read in their full prestatehood setting, do so here. Executive geography, cultural importance, unratified negotiations or a general disclaimer alone remain insufficient. The presently adjudicated beds and banks remain in federal trust for the Tribe under the actual decree, with Heyburn excluded. Ownership and regulatory authority remain distinct; neither an alternative aboriginal-title rule nor a ratification relation-back rule is added.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. They accept the distinct inquiries into clear inclusion and timely defeat of state title, but conclude that these enactments do not satisfy the second. Congress could require consent, compensation and preservation of present tribal tenure while leaving the future State's ownership of navigable beds intact. The 1889 enactment authorized a contingent purchase of designated resources; neither that negotiation process nor the unratified agreements clearly resolved the separate statehood consequence.

Alaska involved operative statehood provisions with identified title-preserving effect. Its protection of a legally effective temporary segregation does not make every contemplated acquisition a comparable withdrawal. The dissent does not require express words or a special formula. It recognizes Congress's knowledge and the Tribe's water uses but considers those insufficient to bridge use and ownership. Postadmission ratification cannot provide a timely consequence absent from the earlier enactments, and the general disclaimer cannot independently cure that deficiency.

They would vacate the reservation-based judgment and remand for lawful consideration of the preserved independent aboriginal-title ground, which was not adjudicated below and was not developed on its merits in this Court. They do not resolve that alternative, decide relation back, direct unconditional quiet title in Idaho, expand the controversy into Heyburn State Park or add an automatic interim injunction. Scalia and Thomas's distinct Alaska position concerning the post-1953 Submerged Lands Act is not a ground governing Idaho's 1890 admission and is not attributed to Kennedy.

#### Source Notes

The [petition, Joint Appendix and merits briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386019_1726) supply the question, unchallenged findings, legal instruments and actual decree. Joint Appendix pages 7-8 contain the August 14, 1998 decree and its repeated Heyburn exclusion; pages 50-51 contain the 1886 enactment, pages 137-140 the 1888 railroad statute, page 144 the 1889 negotiation authority, and pages 370-372 the admission instruments. The [United States merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/idaho-v-united-states-brief-merits) addresses the timely reservation ground. The Tribe's merits brief, page 25 note 12, identifies the unadjudicated aboriginal-title alternative; page 47 note 27 suggests relation back. Neither alternative supplies the Court's holding. The actual decree, rather than a newly drawn description of the lower third of the lake, controls its geographic scope.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed, preserving the district court's August 14, 1998 quiet-title decree at its actual boundaries and with its express Heyburn exception. The decree's title, possession, fitted adverse-claim injunction and counterclaim provisions remain operative. The existing award of costs to the Tribe and the United States' and Idaho's agreement to bear their own costs are undisturbed. No evidentiary remand, new survey, enlargement to ceded land, damages award or general regulatory injunction is ordered.

**End of entry: Idaho v. United States, merits decision, June 18, 2001.**

### Calcano-Martinez v. INS, No. 00-1011

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 24, 2001; decided June 25, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, 232 F.3d 328. The question is whether the permanent 1996 immigration-review provisions bar these lawful permanent residents' direct petitions while leaving district-court habeas available for their legal objection to exclusion from former §212(c) relief. Individual waiver merits are outside this review.

Deboris Calcano-Martinez, Sergio Madrid and Fazila Khan concede covered aggravated-felony removability. Proceedings began in June 1997 for Calcano-Martinez and Madrid and May 1997 for Khan. The Board denied eligibility as a legal matter without exercising waiver discretion. Each filed both direct-review and habeas petitions raising statutory retroactivity and due-process/equal-protection theories; the district courts held the habeas actions pending this review. The Second Circuit dismissed direct review while recognizing the independent habeas route. INS v. St. Cyr is a separately listed related matter; no ruling from it supplies this decision's ground.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6-3, without prejudice to independently proper §2241 proceedings.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing the complete affirmance:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who agree that ordinary direct review is barred but would vacate the unqualified forum disposition and remand for the confined constitutional-exclusion inquiry stated in their separate writing.

All nine agree that these ordinary petitions cannot proceed under the permanent criminal-removability provision. Only the six affirming Justices adopt statutory preservation of habeas. The direct petitions remain dismissed; the separate district-court actions retain their own jurisdictional, procedural and merits questions. No individual immigration relief or change in a stay is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join Part I only | Six support the complete affirmance; all nine support ordinary-review dismissal | Six join all three parts; the other three join only the permanent direct-review conclusion |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Scalia | Kennedy, Thomas | Agrees with Part I; would vacate the unqualified forum disposition for a confined constitutional-exclusion inquiry | Entire separate writing; rejects Parts II and III |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The permanent criminal-removability limitation bars these ordinary petitions

**Holding and operative rule:** When the permanent review provisions apply and the alien concedes removability for a covered aggravated felony, §1252(a)(2)(C) bars an ordinary petition for review seeking correction of the Board's legal exclusion from former §212(c) eligibility. The three petitions must therefore remain dismissed; their challenge to eligibility does not negate the conceded criminal-removability predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt this statutory ordinary-review conclusion, although Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join the complete disposition or the retained-habeas holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Removal proceedings began in May or June 1997, after the permanent system commenced on April 1. The ordinary petition thus falls under §1252(a)(1) and the specific restriction in §1252(a)(2)(C), rather than the saved earlier review scheme. The petitioners concede the relevant aggravated-felony removability. Their legal objection concerns the consequences for eligibility, not whether they belong to the criminal category Congress identified.

Kolster v. INS distinguishes enforcement of the ordinary-review limitation from the independent habeas question. The same distinction applies here. Neither the importance of the eligibility question nor the availability of a different judicial channel restores ordinary appellate review withdrawn by this provision. The government's recognition of review for disputed threshold predicates and substantial constitutional challenges does not concede ordinary jurisdiction over these statutory eligibility claims. Although due-process and equal-protection theories were pleaded, the government disputed their substantiality; this Court does not treat that dispute as an admitted constitutional defect or decide the underlying constitutional merits.

The permanent discretionary-relief provisions in §1252(a)(2)(B) have their own enumerated categories and discretionary-authority clause, including the exception for grants under §1158(a). They are not substituted for the criminal-removability ground applied here. No disputed alienage or offense classification is presented, and the extent of direct jurisdiction in such a different case is not decided.

##### II. Independent habeas remains available for a substantial legal eligibility objection

**Holding and operative rule:** The permanent provisions do not clearly displace §2241 jurisdiction over a substantial statutory or constitutional objection that executive custody or removal rests on an unlawful eligibility rule, when custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates are satisfied. That jurisdiction must be capable of deciding the legal-authority objection and affording effective relief when warranted; it does not authorize general factual retrial or reweighing of lawful discretionary judgments.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining Parts II and III of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court in full supply the controlling statutory ground and remedy. No combination of separate opinions is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** Kolster preserves independent habeas against a withdrawal of ordinary immigration review, and Reno v. Goncalves applies that distinction to an unlawful eligibility exclusion. Those decisions do not automatically decide the permanent scheme. Here §1252(a)(1) supplies ordinary final-order review, subsection (a)(2)(C) withdraws it for the covered criminal category, and subsection (b)(9) consolidates the questions available through that review. Read in that setting, their comprehensive ordinary-review language does not clearly extinguish the separately enacted writ.

Section 1252(c) expressly imposes filing requirements on a petition for review or habeas corpus of a removal order. It does not itself confer jurisdiction, but its recognition of the distinct writ reinforces this reading. Section 1252(e)'s specific, limited habeas rules concern expedited removal under §1225(b)(1), not these ordinary removal proceedings. Repeal of former §1105a's immigration-specific habeas provision does not by itself repeal §2241. Felker v. Turpin, Ex parte McCardle and Ex parte Yerger require attention to the particular channel withdrawn and the independent authority retained. The inquiry concerns enacted meaning; Congress need not use a statutory section number or any indispensable verbal formula to accomplish a clear withdrawal.

The government's contrary construction treats the permanent consolidation language as ending every route. But a challenge to an unlawful eligibility rule is a legal-authority question before discretion is exercised. The Board never weighed these petitioners' waiver equities. Calling the eventual relief discretionary cannot answer that objection. Nor would a nominal detention remedy that must assume the challenged eligibility rule valid provide the retained review described here.

Reyes-Hernandez and Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee retain §1252(g)'s named-action boundary; that provision is not a universal bar to completed-order legality review. The present statutory resolution makes a broader Suspension Clause ruling unnecessary. A statute clearly removing every effective legality-review channel and the constitutional consequences of such a statute are outside the question resolved.

##### III. The direct dismissals do not adjudicate the separate habeas actions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's dismissal of the three direct petitions is affirmed without prejudice to independently proper §2241 proceedings. Each separately filed habeas action remains for its own jurisdictional, procedural and merits adjudication under Part II; no individual eligibility, waiver, custody predicate or entitlement to interim relief is decided here.

**Authority:** The six-Justice coalition stated for Part II directly joins Part III and the complete affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Each petitioner filed a district-court action, held pending the direct-review resolution. Preserving the statutory channel removes the asserted categorical obstacle to those actions; it does not establish facts or satisfy requirements that those courts have not adjudicated. Section 1252(c) requires attachment of the removal order and disclosure of any prior judicial decision upholding its validity. Applicable exhaustion requirements remain, and §1252(d)'s prior-validity-review restriction includes its exceptions for grounds unavailable in the prior proceeding or an inadequate or ineffective prior remedy. This jurisdictional dismissal does not decide the validity of a removal order on the merits.

The petitioners' offense, plea, residence and application histories differ. The Court therefore does not import another litigant's temporal or eligibility facts. Kolster and Goncalves distinguish a lawful opportunity to seek discretionary relief from a right to obtain it. If an independently proper habeas claim succeeds, its court determines effective relief under the applicable law and actual record. This judgment supplies no waiver, release, mandatory agency hearing, automatic conversion or transfer, removal command, new stay or dissolution of an existing stay.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Individual habeas predicates and the temporal and constitutional merits require adjudication in the separate actions, not findings on this direct-review record. Actual discretionary denials and general factual review are outside the granted channel question. No legal entitlement to a favorable exercise of discretion follows from the preserved legality-review rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Kolster v. INS, June 23, 1997 — extended from its earlier review regime to the permanent provisions: withdrawal of ordinary review does not silently eliminate independently proper §2241 review of a substantial authority objection. Its actual-predicate and effective-relief limits remain.
- Reno v. Goncalves, June 3, 1999 — its distinction between legal eligibility and discretionary weighing is applied, and its channel principle is extended to this permanent statutory configuration. Its separate pending-application temporal holding is neither extended nor decided for these petitioners.
- Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996 — applied for channel-specific repeal analysis and genuinely retained habeas authority; it does not establish that every nominal remaining forum is adequate.
- Ex parte McCardle and Ex parte Yerger — applied to distinguish a withdrawn review mechanism from an independent writ; neither makes a particular verbal formula indispensable.
- Heikkila v. Barber — its distinction between ordinary immigration review and habeas informs the separate channels; no general factual-review entitlement follows.
- Reyes-Hernandez v. INS, May 12, 1997, and Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee, February 24, 1999 — their §1252(g) named-action boundary remains intact. The latter's confined constitutional-selection access holding is not extended to create a second jurisdictional ground here.

#### Current Law

The permanent criminal-removability restriction withdraws ordinary review of the presented legal eligibility claims on conceded covered predicates. It does not clearly repeal independently proper §2241 review of a substantial statutory or constitutional challenge to executive authority based on an unlawful eligibility rule. The latter rule now applies to this permanent configuration, with actual jurisdictional predicates and effective relief when warranted, while lawful discretionary weighing and general factual retrial remain outside it. The constitutional consequences of a clear elimination of every effective channel are not decided. The three petitioners receive a preserved judicial route, not a ruling on eligibility or waiver merits.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in Part I and dissents from Parts II and III and the complete affirmance. They agree that the conceded criminal-removability predicates bar the ordinary petitions. They read §1252(a)(1), (a)(2)(C) and (b)(9) together, however, as withdrawing statutory review of the presented removal-related claims, including the proposed §2241 route. The permanent all-questions consolidation provision supplies a textual difference from Goncalves's earlier regime. Section 1252(c)'s procedural reference to habeas supplies no general grant, and the express expedited-removal provision cannot establish unrestricted habeas outside its specified setting. They reject turning the absence of a statutory number into a reason to disregard an otherwise clear withdrawal.

That statutory conclusion does not make an unlawful eligibility rule discretionary or decide whether the review exclusion is constitutionally effective. Their Goncalves jurisdiction join preserves the eligibility/discretion distinction; their separate position in American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee distinguishes interpreting a statute from assessing whether its express exclusion can constitutionally operate in the circumstances. They would vacate the unqualified forum disposition and remand for that confined exclusion inquiry, including the actual availability of effective review, the individual circumstances, and any lawful forum and relief that follow.

The inquiry would not confer jurisdiction over eligibility merits merely by describing them as constitutional. Pleaded due-process and equal-protection theories are not thereby established as substantial or meritorious, and no constitutional entitlement to eligibility review is finally decided. The three would not terminate the separate actions with prejudice, order removal, or transform existing interim relief automatically into a different remedy. Any jurisdiction or interim relief would require its own lawful source and showing. Their permanent-text construction and proposed constitutional-exclusion remand are noncontrolling.

### FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee, No. 00-191

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 28, 2001; decided June 25, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, which affirmed the district court's facial invalidation of the coordinated-party expenditure limitation. The question is whether the First Amendment permits the presented Senate-election ceiling on actually coordinated party spending to prevent circumvention of candidate-contribution limits.

The broader counterclaim remains after the Court's June 26, 1996 judgment protecting the party's genuinely independent advertisements. The developed record includes donor-understanding and fundraising-tally evidence, party-candidate association evidence, denials of personal corruption and the duplication and communication costs of independent spending. The present case does not reopen whether the original advertisements were coordinated.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 6-3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The supporting six reject the categorical facial exemption under the complete contribution-and-circumvention ground. The opposing three would preserve protection for the party's own expression while correcting any broader declaration immunizing genuine contributions and conduits.

The facial invalidation is set aside for conforming proceedings. The earlier independent-advertisement judgment remains intact. No particular transaction is newly found coordinated, and no penalty or other individualized enforcement relief is awarded.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg. Justice Kennedy filed a dissent, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, that would preserve relief for the party's own expression while correcting any broader immunity for genuine contributions and conduits. The dissenters do not join the Court's ground for sustaining the ceiling.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Actual coordinated party spending may be limited to prevent demonstrated circumvention

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may limit expenditures actually coordinated with a federal candidate when the government demonstrates an objectively grounded risk of circumvention of valid candidate-contribution limits and a restriction closely drawn to that risk while preserving meaningful party association and effective advocacy. On this developed record, the presented Senate-election party ceiling satisfies that standard against the categorical facial challenge; party identity, policy agreement, endorsement or ordinary affiliation alone establishes neither coordination nor corruption.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt both holdings, their operative qualifications and the reversal. No combination of separate writings supplies the controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes candidate contributions from independent expression and permits a functional inquiry into resources delivered to a campaign. The earlier Colorado decision protects genuinely independent party advertisements and rejects coordination presumed from party identity. It did not decide this reserved party-specific question. Russell, Shrink Missouri, Daggett and Mariani require the government to establish a sufficiently important, objective interest and close fit; they do not validate a new ceiling by analogy alone.

Actual cooperation with, consultation with, or a request from a campaign can make a purchase serve as campaign support equivalent to a monetary contribution. Larger permissible gifts to parties, fundraising credits, documentary arrangements and testimony concerning tacit donor-candidate understandings establish a practical route around candidate limits. The Court does not find every tally unlawful or every donation corrupt. The evidence supports prevention of circumvention without requiring a completed bribe. Former Senators' denials of personal corruption do not eliminate that demonstrated mechanism.

The party's proposed reliance on express-earmark enforcement is inadequate. General treasuries and tacit allocation understandings can convey candidate benefit without a traceable formal earmark. Restrictions and disclosure governing the hard money used for federal spending do not themselves prevent cumulative benefit through that channel. The Court does not thereby approve unchallenged soft-money practices or find a particular donor's funds attributable to a candidate.

Joint expression is central to party association, and separation can cause duplication, expense and impaired communication. Those burdens require consideration, not a presumption that independent spending cures any restriction. Here the candidate-connected boundary, the allowance for substantial coordinated activity and the developed circumvention evidence together justify the presented ceiling. Independent expression also remains protected. Ordinary political access, equalization and dislike of expensive campaigns supply no independent ground. No finding that a party necessarily corrupts its own candidate is required. The conclusion rejects categorical immunity without declaring every communication a contribution or every future application valid.

**Operative statutory distinctions:** Section 441a(a)(7)(B)(i) requires actual cooperation, consultation or concert with, or a request or suggestion from, a candidate, an authorized political committee or an agent. Section (a)(7)(B)(ii) separately addresses financing dissemination, distribution or republication of candidate-prepared campaign material; it is not the same predicate. Section (a)(8) attributes a contribution directed through an intermediary or conduit to its original source and requires source-and-recipient reporting. The (a)(4) exception for transfers among national, State, district, local and subordinate committees of the same party applies to the limits in (a)(1) and (2); it supplies no categorical constitutional immunity for candidate-connected spending.

Section (d)(1) permits the identified national and State party committees, including subordinate State committees, to make general-election expenditures notwithstanding other contribution or expenditure limits, subject to (d)(2) and (3). Section (d)(3)(A)'s Senate and single-Representative-State allowance is the greater of two cents per voting-age resident or $20,000, increased under (c)'s annual price-index adjustment. Section (d)(3)(B)'s separate $10,000 indexed allowance concerns other House, Delegate and Resident Commissioner races. The present constitutional holding concerns the Senate counterclaim; it does not independently adjudicate those other categories or the presidential allowance, replace the statutory amounts, or erase ordinary contribution and genuine-conduit distinctions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A particular coordinated communication may present a distinct expressive application requiring its own constitutional characterization on an appropriate factual record; that legal question is genuinely unresolved by this categorical challenge. The record does not establish the extent of potentially protected applications needed for a broader facial-overbreadth ruling. A materially different ceiling or application lacking the actual statutory candidate connection is not upheld by this decision. No numerical safe harbor, mandatory evidentiary protocol or universal empirical measure is adopted.

##### II. The facial invalidation is reversed while the independent-advertisement judgment remains intact

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment sustaining facial invalidation of the presented coordinated-party expenditure ceiling is reversed, and the case is remanded for rejection of the party's categorical exemption claim and conforming proceedings. The earlier judgment protecting the genuinely independent advertisements remains effective; any remaining enforcement matter requires its own actual statutory predicates and lawful liability and remedial findings.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly adopt this remedial holding and the complete judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The original advertisements attacked Timothy Wirth before the party selected a nominee and were accepted as independent in the earlier litigation. Losing the broader counterclaim cannot supply the missing coordination or revive an adverse enforcement judgment foreclosed by that decision. The present judgment addresses the categorical claim after record development, not the liability of an identified new communication.

Remaining enforcement issues, if any, must apply the actual coordination and contribution provisions without treating shared ideology or affiliation as proof. The Court imposes no civil penalty, refund, damages, attribution finding or replacement ceiling, and orders no mandatory new trial. The party did not renew the rejected argument that invalid independent-expenditure applications were inseverable from all other applications. That abandoned challenge supplies no question for decision and no remedial instruction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Valeo — applied to the functional contribution/expenditure distinction and justified prevention of contribution-limit evasion; its distinction is retained, without a presumption that every coordinated message is constitutionally identical.
- Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC, June 26, 1996 — its six-Justice independent-expression and actual-candidate-connection holdings remain binding. The previously reserved facial coordinated-spending question is now resolved; neither its narrower plurality nor broader concurrence is converted into prior Court law.
- Russell v. Burris, April 28, 1999 — its government burden, sufficiently important objective interest and close-fit requirement are applied, preserving meaningful association without a fixed dollar floor.
- Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC, January 24, 2000 — its contribution standard is applied; its record-specific amount supplies no monetary safe harbor for this different channel.
- Day v. Holahan, May 15, 1995 — its protection of meaningful intermediary association and allowance for realistic conduit regulation remain intact; availability of another speaker alone does not cure a burden on the chosen association.
- Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices, December 11, 2000 — its affirmative contribution-justification burden applies. Its distinct responsive-grant holdings neither turn independent speech into contributions nor decide this ceiling.
- Mariani v. United States, December 12, 2000 — its distinction between direct candidate resources and differing party-financing arrangements is applied. Neither that decision nor this one validates all unchallenged party practices; the false-source holding remains separate.

#### Current Law

Actually candidate-connected party expenditures are not categorically exempt from closely drawn contribution safeguards. The government must demonstrate an objective circumvention risk, close fit and preservation of meaningful association; it has done so for this facial Senate-election challenge. Independent party expression and the requirement of an actual statutory candidate connection remain protected. The decision resolves the previously reserved facial issue but leaves the constitutional characterization of a particular expressive application for an appropriate record. It supplies neither a universal amount nor automatic approval of other contribution categories, soft-money practices or all future coordinated communications.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. They would preserve the judgment's protection of the party's own Senate-campaign expression, including a jointly developed message, while requiring correction of any declaration extending immunity to ordinary candidate contributions, actual candidate-controlled financial substitutes or genuine donor conduits. This proposed remedy does not reopen the abandoned inseverability argument.

Their earlier Colorado position treats the party's electoral message as its own expression despite cooperation with its candidate. Buckley's contribution distinction remains valid, but the breadth of consultation reaches more than a candidate's bills or financial control. Day protects meaningful association, and Russell, Shrink Missouri, Daggett and Mariani require an operational answer to burdens on the chosen channel. Party-candidate collaboration is central to the party's political purpose; compelled separation entails real duplication, inefficiency and lost coherence. Unlimited independent spending therefore does not itself justify a ceiling on the party's joint message.

The dissent accepts the legitimacy of preventing genuine circumvention and the relevance of the tally and donor-understanding evidence. It finds that evidence insufficient to equate the whole coordination category with candidate-controlled financial benefit or to justify limiting the party's own message at the presented amount. Allocation also reflects electoral prospects and party priorities; the existence of a tally does not establish an illegal earmark. Source restrictions, disclosure and tailored conduit enforcement provide substantial alternatives that, in its view, the government has not adequately answered for this expression category. Neither denial of completed corruption nor the absence of an express bargain is treated as conclusive.

The three do not overrule Buckley, adopt strict scrutiny for every contribution, demand proof of a completed bribe, or immunize direct cash, undifferentiated transfers or sham conduits. Their narrower party-expression rule and limited remedial position are noncontrolling. They would award no penalty, donor-attribution finding, refund or replacement ceiling.

#### Source Notes

The [predecision petition, merits briefs and record](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0412) supply the procedural history, developed financial and association evidence, contrary testimony and parties' positions. The FEC's cited testimony concerning tallying and tacit understandings supports the circumvention mechanism; it does not establish that every tally or donation violates the law. The [2000 §441a text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title2/html/USCODE-2000-title2-chap14-subchapI-sec441a.htm) supplies the separate allowances, indexing, coordination, candidate-material, transfer and attribution provisions. The [reported case](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-431.pdf) supplies the docket, argument and decision dates and the procedural fact that the inseverability argument was not renewed. No specific communication's liability is established by the facial record.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Tenth Circuit for proceedings conforming to reversal and rejection of the categorical exemption claim. The independent-advertisement judgment remains effective. Any remaining enforcement questions require the applicable coordination, contribution, liability and remedial predicates; no new trial, penalty, refund, damages, donor attribution or replacement limit is ordered. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

**End of entry: FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee, merits decision, June 25, 2001.**

### INS v. St. Cyr, No. 00-767

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 24, 2001; decided June 25, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 229 F.3d 406, affirming district-court habeas jurisdiction and the judgment preserving eligibility for former §212(c) consideration. The questions concern the independent habeas channel and the temporal operation of AEDPA and IIRIRA on an otherwise eligible preenactment guilty plea, not whether a waiver should be granted.

Enrico St. Cyr, a Haitian citizen and lawful permanent resident since June 17, 1986, pleaded guilty to selling a controlled substance under Connecticut law on March 8, 1996. He was then deportable but eligible to seek relief. Removal proceedings began April 10, 1997. The agency treated the 1996 changes as a legal bar and did not exercise waiver discretion. His independently filed habeas action challenges that conclusion. Calcano-Martinez, decided earlier in the coordinated consideration of these matters, supplies the controlling forum rule; its petitioners' individual histories do not supply St. Cyr's facts.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing the eligibility disposition:** Scalia and Thomas.

All nine accept jurisdiction in this actual habeas action under controlling Calcano; the three who dissented in that case retain their original construction reservations.

The eligibility judgment stands, and the matter returns through the appropriate agency process for former-law consideration and lawful discretion. No favorable waiver, release, removal, termination of proceedings or change in a stay is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer; Scalia and Thomas join Part I only | Seven affirm eligibility and the complete judgment; nine accept jurisdiction under controlling Calcano | Seven join all parts; Scalia and Thomas join only the present jurisdiction application |
| Concurrence | Kennedy | — | Joins the opinion and judgment | Additional relevant-activity explanation and continued objection to Calcano's original construction |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Scalia | Thomas | Accepts jurisdiction; would reverse eligibility relief | Joins Part I; rejects Parts II and III on the present-relief temporal ground |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Calcano permits adjudication of this actual habeas action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2241 supplies jurisdiction over St. Cyr's properly brought substantial legal claim that the agency erroneously denied authority to consider former §212(c) relief. The permanent review provisions and repeal of the separate immigration-specific habeas provision do not foreclose this action; the court may decide the governing eligibility law and afford effective relief without reweighing immigration discretion.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas apply Calcano as controlling precedent while retaining their published disagreement with its original statutory construction; their present joins do not adopt that original reasoning anew.

**Controlling explanation:** Calcano-Martinez v. INS has resolved the permanent-scheme channel question. Its retained jurisdiction reaches an independently proper legal-eligibility action, subject to custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable requirements. This actual district-court action was adjudicated below, and no supported defect in its predicates or difference in claim type is presented. Calcano's dismissal of ordinary appellate petitions is not a dismissal of this independent action.

Reno v. Goncalves distinguishes eligibility from an exercise of discretion. St. Cyr challenges the Attorney General's conclusion that relief is legally unavailable, not the conviction or the agency's balancing of equities. Kolster and Calcano preserve a channel capable of deciding that objection and granting effective relief if it succeeds. Repeal of former §1105a's separate writ and comprehensive ordinary-review consolidation do not answer the retained §2241 claim.

Calcano's filing and review conditions remain: §1252(c) requires the removal order and disclosure of prior judicial validity review; §1252(d) retains exhaustion of remedies available as of right and its prior-review restriction, except for previously unavailable grounds or an inadequate or ineffective earlier remedy. These provisions do not create jurisdiction independently. Expedited-removal rules concern a different procedure, and §1252(g)'s three named executive acts are not a universal completed-order legality bar. The statutory ground makes a comprehensive Suspension Clause ruling unnecessary. General factual retrial and discretionary reweighing remain outside this claim.

##### II. The 1996 changes do not eliminate eligibility attached to this completed plea

**Holding and operative rule:** Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, AEDPA §440(d)'s new eligibility bar and IIRIRA §304(b)'s repeal of former §212(c) do not eliminate consideration under the former law for a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a guilty plea entered before the relevant change while eligible for that relief. The protected consequence is the opportunity for lawful discretionary consideration, subject to the former eligibility conditions, not a favorable waiver or universal exemption for every preenactment conviction.

**Authority:** Parts II and III of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court are joined by the seven Justices who join the opinion in full. Their direct joins control the operative temporal rule and remedy; the opinion applies existing temporal law to this transaction rather than announcing a new general framework.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products requires examination of Congress's temporal direction and the legal consequences attached to completed events. Neither the new system's comprehensive character, its April 1, 1997 commencement nor the saving provision for earlier proceedings unmistakably directs withdrawal of eligibility attached to this completed plea. Those provisions determine which procedural system governs St. Cyr's April 10, 1997 removal proceeding. They do not themselves resolve the distinct substantive disability. Lindh v. Murphy supports attention to differentiated temporal provisions; it supplies no general exemption from AEDPA.

St. Cyr pleaded guilty on March 8, 1996, before both enactments, while eligible to seek the waiver. The plea completed an exchange of legal rights, including surrender of trial rights, while lawful discretionary consideration remained available. Eliminating that opportunity adds a new legal consequence even though a waiver was never guaranteed. A particular official's promise, subjective-reliance finding or proof that St. Cyr would have chosen trial is unnecessary. The Court makes none of those findings.

The government argues that removal concerns future presence and a present discretionary benefit, which a state plea cannot freeze. But the conviction obtained through this completed exchange supplies the very predicate for the new categorical disability. Protecting the existing opportunity does not bind the Attorney General to grant relief or freeze immigration law generally. Reyes-Hernandez protects a surrendered legal option without making its particular deportability defense indispensable. Kolster expressly distinguished a plea before eligibility; its different result does not defeat this eligible plea. Goncalves protected a pending application and left this later-application and repeal question open.

Both challenged changes are therefore inapplicable to this transaction. Ordinary statutory construction resolves their temporal meaning. Goncalves and United States v. Mead Corp. do not allow an agency interpretation to override that resolved meaning. No independent constitutional invalidation or discretionary entitlement is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Trial convictions, pleas after the relevant change, persons ineligible when they pleaded and unrelated later grounds of removal lie outside this transaction class. Their claims are not adjudicated adversely by implication. No new or later disqualification is adjudicated here. The former law's conditions remain operative, including seven years of lawful unrelinquished domicile and its bar for an aggravated-felony conviction with at least five years served for the felony or felonies; the decision waives no otherwise applicable former eligibility restriction.

##### III. St. Cyr receives consideration under the former law, not the waiver itself

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's judgment recognizing habeas jurisdiction and preserving former-law eligibility is affirmed. The matter returns through the appropriate agency process for consideration of St. Cyr's request under the applicable former §212(c) criteria and, if those criteria are met, a lawful exercise of discretion; the 1996 changes cannot supply the categorical ineligibility ground rejected here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition directly adopts the eligibility disposition and remedy. Scalia and Thomas would reverse that relief while accepting present habeas jurisdiction under Calcano.

**Controlling explanation:** The agency stopped at a legal bar and did not perform the discretionary task the former law permits. A judicial determination of the lawful eligibility rule must have an effective consequence: the agency must entertain the request under that rule. Goncalves supplies the distinction between requiring lawful consideration and substituting a court's assessment of waiver equities.

The record does not supply the factual equities for this Court to grant relief, and the criminal conviction is not under review. Further factual and discretionary work is ordinary administration of the rule, not an unresolved answer to the temporal question. No favorable waiver, release, termination of removal proceedings, retrial of the conviction, extra hearing beyond lawful agency adjudication, new stay, dissolution of a stay or removal direction is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Calcano-Martinez v. INS, June 25, 2001 — applied as the controlling permanent-scheme habeas rule; its ordinary-review dismissal and reserved temporal merits are kept distinct.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, April 26, 1994 — its statutory-direction and completed-event inquiry is applied to an eligible guilty plea; no universal subjective-reliance requirement is added.
- Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997 — its attention to differentiated temporal directions is applied without treating every AEDPA amendment as governed by one transition rule.
- Reyes-Hernandez v. INS, May 12, 1997 — its protection of a completed surrendered option is applied by analogy; surrender of its particular deportability defense is not made indispensable.
- Kolster v. INS, June 23, 1997 — its legality/discretion distinction is applied. Its plea before eligibility is distinguished, preserving its express rejection of a universal reliance prerequisite.

- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001 — its distinction between delegated interpretation and persuasive reasoning remains; it supplies no power to override statutory temporal meaning resolved by ordinary construction.
- Felker v. Turpin, Ex parte McCardle and Ex parte Yerger — their channel-specific principles, as applied in Calcano, remain the statutory background. No fresh constitutional minimum or indispensable repeal formula is announced.

#### Current Law

The retained permanent-scheme §2241 channel applies to this actual substantial legal-eligibility action. The separate temporal rule protects former §212(c) consideration for the defined class of then-eligible preenactment guilty pleas, including St. Cyr's March 8, 1996 plea, against both challenged 1996 eligibility changes. Current commencement of removal proceedings alone does not eliminate that opportunity. Former eligibility conditions and lawful agency discretion remain; no subjective-reliance trial, immigration promise, guaranteed waiver or universal protection of every old conviction is required or supplied.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Kennedy concurs and joins the opinion in full. He continues to disagree with Calcano's original construction of the permanent review text but accepts that controlling decision in this actual action. On temporal reach, the relevant completed activity is the eligible guilty plea and surrender of trial rights. The statute's general commencement does not unmistakably attach the new disability to that transaction. His relevant-activity approach therefore supports the same operative eligible-plea rule, without an open-ended fairness test or individualized promise. Goncalves's absence of a surrendered legal option does not describe this record. He does not join the temporal dissent or treat a guaranteed waiver as necessary.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurs in Part I and dissents from Parts II and III. ### K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District, 196 F.3d 1046

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Review of the Ninth Circuit judgment reported at 196 F.3d 1046 (November 15, 1999). The question is whether adequate and safely accessible alternative services permit the State's express religious-site restriction on secular special education. The independent IDEA onsite-entitlement ruling is not reopened.

K.D.M., a blind child with cerebral palsy, attends Harbor Baptist Church School by his parents' religious choice. The district supplies equipment there and vision instruction at a nearby fire hall, about ninety minutes twice weekly with district transportation. Adequacy and travel safety are stipulated. Onsite instruction would also occur in a separate room. The rule permits secular private-school sites, but the record establishes no actual secular onsite recipient. The district court granted constitutional relief while denying an unconditional specialist entitlement; the Ninth Circuit reversed the constitutional judgment.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**The constitutional disposition is reversed and remanded, 6-3.**

**Supporting reversal and remand:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

Thomas, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer adopt the complete tailored implementation rule; Stone-Zsela proposes the additional directions in his concurrence rather than joining that remedial part in full.

The lower courts must confine relief to the religious criterion, preserving the independent regulatory provisions and lawful neutral decisions. The rejection of an individual statutory onsite entitlement remains undisturbed. No unconditional onsite award or damages issues.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins Parts I–II only | Six support constitutional reversal; five adopt complete Part III implementation | Five join every part; Stone-Zsela joins constitutional grounds and concurs in the remedial judgment |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | — | Supports reversal/remand; proposes additional implementation directions | Parts I–II joined; service continuity and conditional onsite direction stated separately |
| Dissent | Souter | Stevens, Ginsburg | Would affirm the constitutional disposition | Entire complete-service benefit/burden ground; no join in invalidation |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Adequate alternative service does not justify the express religious disqualification

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not exclude a child from consideration for onsite secular special-education services solely because the child's school is religious when the existing program permits comparable secular locations and effective public control can preserve the service's secular character. OAR 581-15-166(3)'s categorical religious-site restriction violates free exercise and equal protection as applied here; neutral educational, logistical and fiscal criteria remain available on equal terms, and no independent statutory onsite entitlement is created.

**Authority:** The six direct joins in Parts I and II of Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court control the constitutional rule and establishment analysis. The dissenting Justices reject constitutional invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah subjects an objective religious disability to compelling justification and narrow tailoring. The rule permits consideration of a secular private-school location but categorically excludes the religious location. That legal difference is established by the rule itself; the absence of a proved actual secular onsite recipient does not erase it. Good News Club likewise distinguishes a legal comparison from a requirement to produce an actual comparator, although this public teaching service is not a private-expression forum.

The stipulated adequacy of the fire-hall service, safe transportation and ability to remain in religious school answer claims of educational deprivation and dangerous travel. They do not make the religious condition neutral. Nor can the Court rely on lost classroom integration: onsite instruction would also occur in a separate room. The injury decided is unequal eligibility for an available delivery option because of religious enrollment, not an invented medical or instructional deficit.

The State's separation rationale does not justify that categorical line under the governing actual-function law explained in Part II. Neutral site criteria and effective controls address lawful administration without the religious disqualification. Equal protection independently rejects the same unjustified differential legal term; the Court creates no blended scrutiny category. Employment Division v. Smith remains applicable to genuinely neutral, generally applicable rules. No finding of subjective hostility or general exemption from such rules is necessary.

##### II. Constitutional permission for controlled secular service defeats the categorical separation defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Religious-school location alone does not make an independently controlled, operationally separate public vision service unconstitutional. The State may enforce actual safeguards against religious teaching and diversion, but it cannot justify this categorical exclusion by presuming that the religious premises turn the secular service into devotion; constitutional permission does not itself create a general duty to furnish onsite services.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly join Part II. The source of equal site consideration is Part I's nondiscrimination rule, not a new funding entitlement inferred from establishment permission.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini v. Felton identifies the public act and its actual religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships within one inquiry. It permits independently administered secular instruction on religious premises under effective public controls. Mitchell v. Helms requires correction of actual financed religious use but rejects religious identity, location, incidental savings and possible diversion as substitutes for that showing.

The contemplated vision service remains the district's instruction, delivered separately from ordinary classroom teaching. The stipulated separate room permits operational separation, and the record identifies no devotional instruction requiring this categorical exclusion. The Court does not find that every future onsite arrangement necessarily complies with every safeguard. Public control and secular performance must remain real; neither neutral eligibility nor public employment immunizes actual religious teaching.

Freiler applies the same unified inquiry to a mandatory official undertaking that preserved a selected religious account. This specialist's secular service is not that undertaking. Official attribution alone identifies the speaker, not a religious function. Children's Healthcare permits its particular secular-care accommodation; it validates neither all religious classifications nor this disability. Zobrest distinguished absence of a federal duty from an affirmative prohibition and supplied no establishment merits ruling. These distinctions answer the State's asserted constitutional necessity without requiring sectarian tuition, inseparably devotional teaching or a new evidentiary audit.

##### III. Relief must remove the religious criterion while preserving lawful service decisions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's constitutional disposition is reversed, and the matter is remanded for a declaration and injunction against using religious enrollment or location alone to disqualify the proposed service site. The decree must preserve lawful neutral service and site decisions, paragraphs (1), (2) and (4) of the regulation, and the undisturbed rejection of an individual IDEA right to onsite delivery; it does not automatically order an onsite specialist.

**Authority:** Part III has five direct votes from the Justices joining the opinion throughout. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but concurs in the judgment on this component, proposing the additional directions stated in his separate writing. His vote is not counted as assent to the complete Part III remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The district decree already disclaimed an unconditional specialist entitlement and permitted equal nonreligious refusals consistent with IDEA. Its formal injunction against enforcement of the entire regulation nevertheless sweeps beyond the religious criterion. Mitchell requires relief fitted to the established defect while preserving independent lawful applications. The injunction must therefore target paragraph (3)'s categorical religious-site exclusion and require a delivery decision under lawful criteria, rather than erase the program's independent rules.

The district may select the same offsite arrangement for genuine neutral reasons. It must not preserve the excluded religious condition under an administrative label. The stipulated adequacy, safety and separate-room facts need no reproof, and no new hearing is mandatory. This constitutional remand does not reopen statutory entitlement, award damages, withdraw equipment or determine fees, RFRA or Oregon constitutional claims.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Paragraph (1) concerns children from age three through kindergarten eligibility whose parents reject an offered free appropriate public education and unilaterally choose private school; paragraph (2) concerns kindergarten eligibility through age twenty-one under the corresponding resident-district offer. Each denies an individual service entitlement in that setting and assigns selection, services and delivery to the responsible public agency under OAR 581-15-171. Paragraph (4) independently requires genuine opportunities for equitable participation consistent with children's number and needs. Those provisions remain effective, subject to the constitutional nondiscrimination rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding protects equal eligibility within the service actually offered, not a universal private-school subsidy or an obligation to reimburse religious tuition. A materially different service inseparable from devotional instruction is outside this record. Specific lawful neutral grounds for the delivery decision remain for implementation; the Court does not invent one. The preserved statutory ruling and the distinction between permission and obligation remain binding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah — applied to the objective religious classification, compelling justification and tailoring; a modest alternative-site burden does not make the explicit disability neutral.
- Employment Division v. Smith — preserved for genuinely neutral, generally applicable rules; no broad exemption regime is created.
- Agostini v. Felton — applied through its unified actual-function inquiry and permission for operationally separate, publicly controlled secular instruction; permission alone supplies no onsite entitlement.
- Mitchell v. Helms — applied to distinguish actual financed religious use from location presumptions and to preserve lawful applications when tailoring relief.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District — its antecedent-law remand and distinction between lack of duty and prohibition remain; no establishment merits holding is inferred.
- Good News Club v. Milford Central School — its legal-comparator distinction informs the religious eligibility inquiry; its private-expression forum is not extended to public teaching services.
- Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle — its limited accommodation and actual-control requirements remain program specific, not approval of every religious classification.
- Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education — its complete-policy religious-function holding is applied as governing method and distinguished in operation; official attribution and location alone do not establish devotion.

#### Current Law

Equal site eligibility within the offered secular service may not be withheld solely because of religious enrollment or location. Adequate safe alternatives remain relevant to administration and remedy but do not validate the express classification. Actual public control and secular operation, rather than premises alone, govern the establishment question. The existing program's independent eligibility, selection and equitable-participation rules survive; the Court requires a lawful delivery decision and fitted relief, not a universal onsite entitlement. Stone-Zsela's additional continuity and conditional onsite commands are not part of the controlling mandate.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I and II and in the judgment, with a distinct implementation position. He joins the constitutional reversal and a tailored injunction against the religious criterion, leaving the rejection of an independent statutory onsite right intact. He would expressly require adequate services to continue during implementation. If no lawful neutral ground supports refusal on the stipulated record, he would require removal of the religious barrier and provision of onsite service on the same terms as comparable secular placements. That condition does not award onsite delivery unconditionally or depend on an invented actual secular onsite recipient. It preserves genuinely neutral educational, logistical and fiscal criteria, effective public control and safeguards against religious instruction. These additional implementation directions are his position, not the Court's five-Justice Part III mandate.

Justice Souter, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg, dissents. They would affirm the constitutional rejection on the stipulated arrangement: the district supplies the complete adequate service, safe nearby transportation and continued religious enrollment. They distinguish the government's location for its own instructional service from denial of a benefit or exclusion of private religious expression. In their view constitutional permission to deliver a service at religious premises does not require making every permitted location available where the actual service and religious schooling remain intact.

They confront the express legal distinction but find no actionable impairment of religious exercise or unequal access on this record. The lack of an actual secular onsite recipient supports that limited conclusion; they do not make an actual comparator indispensable to every challenge to a facial religious disability. They accept Agostini, Mitchell and Freiler's actual-function law and do not presume devotion from location, restore independent Lemon prongs or declare onsite service forbidden. Good News Club's private-access setting and Children's Healthcare's affirmative payment preference differ from the fully supplied public instruction here. Their view would leave the injunction displaced, with statutory obligations intact, while preserving materially different claims involving inadequate, unsafe, inaccessible or unequal services. Their conditional agreement that any contrary injunction must preserve independent regulatory provisions is not a vote for reversal.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for a constitutional decree fitted to paragraph (3)'s categorical religious exclusion and a delivery decision under lawful, generally applicable criteria and actual secular safeguards. Paragraphs (1), (2) and (4) remain in force. The courts need not reopen the stipulated adequacy, safety or separate-room facts, and no mandatory new hearing, damages, equipment withdrawal or specific fee award is ordered. The statutory onsite-entitlement rejection is undisturbed. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

### Nevada v. Hicks, No. 99-1994

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 21, 2001; decided June 25, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed federal summary judgment recognizing tribal jurisdiction and requiring exhaustion of personal-immunity objections. The questions concern tribal tort authority, the §1983 forum and exhaustion after searches for evidence of an off-reservation state offense.

Hicks, a Fallon Paiute-Shoshone member, lived on a retained trust allotment. The August 30, 1990 state warrant required tribal approval; the tribal judge limited it to exterior premises and vehicles, from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., rather than the state's 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. period. The June 12, 1991 state warrant concerned two mounted heads and the property and premises, from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.; tribal approval on June 13 made no modification. Tribal officers accompanied the searches. Hicks alleges damage and excess, not adjudicated violations. The State and official-capacity wardens were voluntarily dismissed; claims against tribal defendants were dismissed. The retained personal claims include the Director in the reviewed posture; a reported motion to drop him establishes no completed dismissal.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

The Ninth Circuit's approval of the reviewed tribal claims is reversed and the case remanded, with distinct component dispositions:

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Retained trust land does not eliminate the nonmember authority inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Montana's nonmember framework applies to the claimed civil authority over these state officers' execution of process on Hicks's retained trust allotment for an off-reservation state offense. Land status is material to that inquiry but does not by itself establish adjudicatory authority; the actual source and relationship must be examined.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices at this case-specific scope. Their common starting point does not establish unanimity about either exception, ultimate tribal authority, personal immunity or the proper return.

**Controlling explanation:** Strate v. A-1 Contractors addressed an authorized public highway where ordinary public use displaced exclusion. This home and retained trust allotment differ materially. Yet the difference does not dispense with identifying authority over the nonmembers and conduct before the Court. Montana's framework requires examining a consensual commercial dealing, contract, lease or other qualifying arrangement connected to the asserted authority, or conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The exceptions are distinct.

South Dakota v. Bourland concerns particular displaced exclusion rights and retained residual authority, not state exclusivity across every reservation. Atkinson applies Montana to its nonmember fee-land tax and requires a meaningful connection; it did not already decide this retained-land setting. The present inquiry applies that source-and-relationship discipline to these enforcement searches. It does not adjudicate every private nonmember activity on tribal land or convert geography into either automatic authority or automatic immunity.

##### II. These state-enforcement searches do not establish tribal tort authority

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tribe lacks civil authority over the personal-capacity tribal tort claims challenging these officers' execution of state process to investigate an off-reservation state offense. Their use of the identified tribal warrants does not establish a qualifying first-exception regulatory relationship for these claims, and the asserted injuries do not establish the qualifying tribal threat or direct effect under the second exception; the approval of tribal tort jurisdiction must be reversed.

**Authority:** Part II has the five direct votes of the Justices joining the opinion throughout. They control final rejection of the reviewed tort authority. Stone-Zsela would affirm that authority; Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer would return it for proper Montana analysis and early federal consideration of defenses.

**Controlling explanation:** Strate requires an adequate source of corresponding governing power for nonmember adjudication and a meaningful nexus to a qualifying consensual relationship. The present warrants reflect cooperation between sovereign authorities in implementing state criminal process. They are not, on this record, a private regulatory undertaking by which state officials become subject to tribal civil control of that enforcement activity. The State's authority to investigate an off-reservation violation does not depend on a tribal civil forum's permission to regulate the execution of its process.

Hicks's strongest response is that the officers actually sought and used tribal judicial authorization at a member's home. The first state warrant expressly required tribal approval, and each search had it. Those facts matter; the Court does not replace them with an assumption that the officers never consulted the Tribe or demand express consent to civil suit in every consensual case. But a close factual connection to a warrant does not cure the absence of the qualifying regulatory relationship. An express state-tribal cooperative contract or superior federal delegation presents a different source not established here.

The alleged damage and excess likewise do not establish that regulating this state-enforcement category is necessary to tribal self-government or internal relations within Montana's second exception. Individual injury has real consequences, but it does not by itself satisfy that separate source of authority. No allegation is adjudicated false or lawful. The rule concerns official enforcement of off-reservation law, not unrelated private ventures by officials or all nonmember conduct on retained land. Personal-capacity pleading preserves the distinction from sovereign immunity but does not supply missing forum power.

##### III. Section 1983 supplies no authority for the asserted tribal federal-claim forum

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 does not itself confer state courts' general concurrent jurisdiction on tribal tribunals, and no adequate independent grant supports the asserted tribal forum for these federal claims. The §1983 claims must be dismissed from the tribal proceeding; further exhaustion cannot supply the missing forum authority, and their possible availability in a competent forum is not decided.

**Authority:** The seven direct joins in Part III control this component. Stevens and Breyer would instead leave possible §1983 cognizability open in an otherwise competent tribal tribunal after the authority and defense inquiries.

**Controlling explanation:** A federal cause of action and a tribunal's adjudicatory power have distinct sources. Section 1983 reaches a person acting under color of State, Territory or District of Columbia law who causes the deprivation of a federally secured right. Its authorization of an action at law, equity or another proper proceeding does not make every sovereign tribunal a proper forum. State-court concurrent jurisdiction cannot simply be attributed to tribal courts.

Hafer v. Melo establishes that a state official sued personally may be a statutory person even when acting through official authority, subject to personal defenses. It does not establish this tribal forum's competence. Likewise, Neztsosie's treatment of a specially assigned federal statutory channel neither creates tribal removal nor supplies an independent grant for this case. The present forum conclusion establishes no universal immunity for state officials and no ruling on the underlying federal violation.

The statute's separate qualification for an injunction against a judicial officer acting in a judicial capacity permits such relief only if a declaratory decree was violated or declaratory relief was unavailable. That qualification concerns a different class; it neither gives these wardens a defense nor supplies tribal jurisdiction. The merits elements, defendants' personal conduct, any lawful filing elsewhere and applicable defenses remain independent questions.

##### IV. No further tribal litigation of these rejected claims is required, and federal relief remains bounded

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's approval of tribal tort jurisdiction and its direction for further tribal exhaustion of the officers' immunity objections are reversed at the scope of Part II. On remand the federal courts must give the no-authority conclusions their proper declaratory and prospective effect, subject to the actual claims, lawful parties, immunity limits and ordinary equitable requirements; this judgment does not itself issue an injunction or award personal immunity.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the same five-Justice coalition that joins Part II. Eight Justices reject the lower court's unconditional further-exhaustion direction, but Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer do so through their distinct early-defense remand, not by joining this complete rationale. Stone-Zsela would require initial tribal presentation of personal defenses on the tort claims he finds within tribal authority; he agrees that the §1983 claims cannot remain there.

**Controlling explanation:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual preserve initial tribal consideration and ordinary prudential exhaustion where authority may exist; completed jurisdictional review cannot create the power absent here. Strate permits a final no-authority determination without requiring litigation of defenses in that unauthorized forum. Neztsosie confirms that exhaustion and jurisdiction are distinct, while its specific federal statutory assignment supplies no general removal procedure.

The federal court must conform relief to the settled jurisdictional conclusions, not repeat an unresolved Montana inquiry the controlling coalition has answered. But jurisdictional error does not waive immunity, identify a lawful defendant for every remedy or satisfy every injunction requirement. Personal qualified immunity remains a defense, not the source of this jurisdictional holding. Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability in a competent forum; Saucier leaves Harlow and Anderson's objective defense and conduct-date inquiry intact, without a controlling universal merits-first sequence. Any notice question would concern the 1990–1991 searches, not later forum decisions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Search legality, warrant excess, causation, damage, personal immunity and the Director's own participation remain unadjudicated. The pending motion to drop the Director is not a completed dismissal. No damages, automatic injunction, new filing deadline, tolling, guaranteed timely alternate forum, waiver of the State's immunity or direction to conduct another search is created. Express cooperative contracts, superior federal authority and unrelated private conduct remain outside the source ruling here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Montana v. United States — applied to this retained-land state-enforcement controversy, preserving distinct consensual-relationship and tribal-effect exceptions; neither title nor state employment alone answers every claim.
- Strate v. A-1 Contractors — its source, meaningful-nexus and independent-relief requirements are applied; its highway holding is distinguished rather than treated as already deciding retained-land searches. No express consent-to-suit formula is imposed for every arrangement.
- South Dakota v. Bourland — its particular displacement and residual-authority rules remain intact; no universal state exclusivity or reservation-boundary change follows.
- Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley — its actual-connection and separate exception inquiries inform this different setting. Its fee-land holding was not a controlling all-land rule.
- National Farmers Union Insurance Cos. v. Crow Tribe and Iowa Mutual Insurance Co. v. LaPlante — ordinary tribal examination and prudential exhaustion remain; they supply neither missing substantive authority nor compulsory defense litigation after the controlling no-authority determination.
- El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie — its source-specific federal priority and independent party, immunity and equitable conditions are preserved; it creates neither general removal nor automatic injunctive relief.
- Hafer v. Melo — its personal-capacity personhood and liability rule remains distinct from tribunal competence and personal defenses; official authority does not itself confer sovereign immunity on the personal defendant.
- Saucier v. Katz, Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton — existing objective personal immunity remains. Saucier supplies no controlling universal merits-first sequence, replacement defense or search-immunity award here.

#### Current Law

Montana applies to these nonmember state-enforcement searches on retained trust land. At the controlling five-Justice scope, the accepted warrants do not establish qualifying tribal civil authority over execution of state process for an off-reservation offense, and the asserted injury supplies no second-exception authority. Seven Justices reject the asserted §1983 tribal forum. Further defense litigation there cannot cure these authority defects. The general treatment of private nonmember retained-land transactions, express cooperative contracts or superior federal authorization is not resolved; state employment does not immunize every private act. Personal liability, immunity and federal coercive relief remain distinct.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices Souter and Thomas, concurs and joins the Court's opinion in full. As in their Atkinson concurrence, they would begin with Montana's general nonmember principle on tribal or trust land as well as fee land. Land status remains important within the exceptions; it does not eliminate the initial source inquiry. They reject a merely formal remand to repeat that inquiry where the character of the state-enforcement relationship and the insufficient qualifying tribal effect already answer it. Their broader starting point does not enlarge the Court's case-specific holding into a decision about every retained-land private transaction.

Justice O'Connor concurs in Parts I and III and dissents from Parts II and IV, joined in her separate remand explanation by Justices Stevens and Breyer. Montana applies, but retained land and the actual tribal authorizations require serious consideration within its exceptions. Cooperation in obtaining tribal process may establish a relevant arrangement; neither permission alone nor state-officer status conclusively resolves it. They would reverse the undifferentiated approval below and return the matter for proper Montana analysis and early federal consideration of the raised immunity defenses, without automatically requiring further tribal exhaustion of those defenses.

They do not find qualifying consent, award immunity, decide the searches lawful or prescribe a universal constitutional-merits-first order. Hafer keeps personal and sovereign defenses separate. Actual Strate and Neztsosie require an independent basis, proper parties and ordinary equitable predicates for any federal protection; the separate position supplies no equivalent of statutory removal or automatic injunction. The 1990 exterior limitation does not govern the unmodified 1991 authorization, and neither warrant establishes a breach or the Director's involvement. National Farmers permits inquiry into that authority; the federal source of the cause is a separate question. They do not sustain this forum now. No state-officer immunity award, automatic injunction or rewriting of §1441 follows.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I and III and dissents from Parts II and IV. The home on retained trust land differs from Strate's public highway, but land alone is not decisive: the officers actually used tribal judicial authorization, the first state warrant required it, and alleged excess or damage in executing those warrants has the necessary direct nexus. Neztsosie's special federal statutory assignment has no counterpart removing these tort claims. He joins dismissal of the §1983 claims from the tribal proceeding and decides nothing about their availability elsewhere. It waives no state immunity and decides no search violation, compensable injury, personal participation or immunity merits.

### New York Times Co. v. Tasini, No. 00-201

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 28, 2001; decided June 25, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 206 F.3d 161, which reversed publisher summary judgment and directed judgment for six freelance authors concerning the challenged database uses. The question is whether §201(c)'s collective-work privilege permits those reproduction and distribution uses without the authors' authorization.

The articles appeared in the New York Times, Newsday and Sports Illustrated. Publishers licensed their contents to electronic services. The products differ: NEXIS and New York Times OnDisc retrieve individual text articles; General Periodicals OnDisc retrieves page images. The statutory question concerns their actual presentation and the proved uses. Newsday and Time do not press the contract defenses rejected below, and the Court does not adjudicate those defenses anew.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer.

The same coalition governs each challenged product.

The Second Circuit's statutory liability disposition stands. The lower courts determine damages and equitable relief on the applicable law and record. No automatic wholesale deletion, compulsory license, fixed damages, willfulness determination or reopening of the unpressed contract defenses is directed.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. The opinion covers the contextual rule, each challenged product, and the bounded remedy. Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justice Breyer; they would reverse the statutory construction, vacate the dependent liability disposition and return the case for further proceedings.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The revision privilege requires a contribution to remain part of the collective work

**Holding and operative rule:** Absent an express transfer supplying different rights, §201(c) permits reproduction and distribution of a contribution as part of the particular collective work, a recognizable revision of that work, or a later collective work in the same series. Source identification alone does not permit separately offering the contribution in a new compendium; the actual presentation must retain the contribution's relationship to the collective work, regardless of medium.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court directly adopt all three holdings and the complete judgment. The dissent rests on a different statutory construction and resulting disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 103(b), 106 and 201 preserve the freelance author's separate copyright while protecting the compiler's original selection and arrangement. Section 201(c) gives the publisher a defined privilege in that separately owned contribution, not ownership of every use. Stewart v. Abend preserves the distinction between underlying material and the work incorporating it; its particular renewal and derivative-work dispute does not decide this revision question.

The publishers correctly argue that a revision may change format, arrangement and contents. The statute does not demand every original item, identical typography or an immutable facsimile. Nor does electronic searching itself destroy the privilege. But a revised collective work must still be what the publisher reproduces and distributes. A bibliographic identifier describes the article's past; it does not establish its present inclusion in the source work or a recognizable revision. Reading every general database as a revision of every represented issue would consume the separate rights Congress preserved.

Campbell v. Acuff-Rose requires application of its own statutory fair-use criteria; Quality King v. L'anza likewise connects a particular exclusive right to its enacted limitation. Their method supports honoring the particular permission here, not importing a fair-use or first-sale defense. Fair use was not asserted, and public display is not decided. Section 201(d)'s transferability alternative need not be decided because the challenged uses exceed the privilege's substantive scope even assuming it can be transferred. The rule preserves actual contractual authorization and genuine electronic revisions without deciding their unpresented terms or interfaces.

##### II. Each of the three challenged products exceeds the privilege

**Holding and operative rule:** NEXIS and New York Times OnDisc reproduce and distribute the articles as separately retrieved text outside a perceptible revision of their source issues; General Periodicals OnDisc preserves particular page images but likewise separates the article pages from the original issue as a collective whole. None of these proved uses is privileged by §201(c), and the contrary summary-judgment defense fails for each product.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join this product-specific application in Part II. The image-based product receives its own factual consideration; it is not treated as text-only.

**Controlling explanation:** NEXIS delivers an individual article with publication information, without its edition's surrounding contents and presentation. New York Times OnDisc likewise delivers individual text articles across editions. Its single-publisher source does not make the delivered article part of a recognizable revision of the particular issue in which it appeared.

General Periodicals OnDisc presents images of the relevant printed pages, preserving photographs, captions, advertisements and other material on those pages. That added context matters, but the article and continuation pages are still delivered without a navigable presentation of the complete original issue. The retained page does not itself establish the requisite collective-work relationship. This conclusion requires neither physical page-turning in every medium nor preservation of every original item in every lawful revision.

The strongest contrary analogy is microform: a reader may focus on one article in a reproduced newspaper or magazine. But microform retains the article within the continuously reproduced issue. These products distribute the selected article apart from that issue. A user's possible search assembling an issue's contents does not show that the publisher itself presents the article as part of its revision. Likewise, an intact intermediate transmission does not answer whether the downstream reproductions and distributions remain within the privilege.

Stewart's separate-ownership principle and the right-specific inquiries in Campbell and Quality King preserve the authors' reproduction and distribution rights at this boundary. Useful archival access cannot supply statutory permission the products lack. The Court resolves these demonstrated uses, not every digital archive, later interface or unpresented article. It adopts no independent library exception, compilation-originality ruling or contributory-liability rule.

##### III. The statutory liability judgment stands, with individualized relief below

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's judgment rejecting the §201(c) defense and directing judgment for the authors on the challenged uses is affirmed. Damages and equitable relief remain for the lower courts under their applicable statutory and equitable requirements; an appropriate injunction may issue, but neither automatic deletion of all freelance material nor a compulsory license follows from this judgment.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join Part III. Stevens and Breyer would reverse the statutory construction, vacate the dependent liability disposition and return for consistent statutory proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** Liability under the presented reproduction-and-distribution theory does not establish the amount or form of relief. The copyright remedial provisions require their own findings and exercise of lawful discretion. The public value of a complete archive can inform that inquiry; it cannot enlarge §201(c), compel a license or confer a right to continued uncompensated use. No damages amount, willfulness finding, universal injunction or negotiated accommodation is established.

The contract defenses supply no additional question for decision here. Newsday's defense was held waived below; Time's was rejected below; neither petitioner presses its defense in this Court. Those rulings remain undisturbed. Whitford's written first-publication and conditional republication terms are background to that posture, not a finding that every author lacked an agreement or a new construction of those terms. The return does not reopen either contract defense.

Campbell and Quality King preserve the separation between correcting or applying a statutory rule and determining relief on the remaining lawful record. Here the statutory liability disposition is affirmed, while the unfinished remedial work remains below. Actual agreements and transactions outside the reviewed uses, later interfaces and unpresented articles are outside this judgment. No new license burden, contract waiver rule or automatic right to final relief on every other issue is announced.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Stewart v. Abend — its protection of separately owned underlying material is applied as statutory background; its renewal and derivative-work holding does not already decide the modern collective-work privilege. No earlier holding is overruled.
- Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc. — its right-specific statutory inquiry and separation of liability from unfinished remedial work remain. The unasserted fair-use defense is not substituted for §201(c).
- Quality King Distributors, Inc. v. L'anza Research International, Inc. — its treatment of the distribution right and the first-sale limitation remains confined to its actual subject. It supports honoring statutory boundaries but grants no privilege to reproduce these articles or reopen independent contracts.

#### Current Law

Section 201(c) now has a controlling contextual rule: the contribution must remain part of the identified collective work, a recognizable revision or the permitted later work in the same series. Medium and search functionality alone do not determine the answer. Original publication data cannot turn separately offered articles into a revised edition, and the three proved products fail that requirement. Actual licenses and genuine electronic revisions remain possible; ownership and the right to reproduce remain distinct. The statutory liability ruling is settled, while individualized relief remains to be determined. Transferability beyond that necessary scope and unpressed contract questions are not decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Breyer, dissents. They would recognize the demonstrated archival uses as revisions only when both conditions are met: each article expressly identifies its source collective work, and substantially the rest of that collective work is readily accessible to the reader of the individual file at the same time. The publisher's editorial selection and issue association must remain available. A detached article with only a source label does not qualify.

In their view the files corresponding to an issue can constitute its revision, and aggregation of those revisions into a searchable archive does not destroy that status. The library and microform analogies demonstrate why a reader's selection of one item need not change the character of the collection. GPO's images add page context, but their conclusion does not depend on images alone. They regard the accessible editorial contents and express issue association as sufficient; the Court instead requires a perceptible collective presentation these interfaces lack. Neither side permits alteration of the contribution itself or use in an unrelated anthology merely because its former source is identified.

The dissent preserves the author's ownership and the compiler's limited privilege under §§103(b) and 201(c). Stewart's different 1909 Act dispute does not erase the modern statutory separation; Campbell and Quality King likewise require giving the actual statutory permission its full effect. Archive completeness and clearance costs reinforce their construction rather than create a free-standing public-benefit exemption or prove inevitable deletion.

On §201(d), they reject complete nontransferability: a publisher may farm out printing or distribution of its own collective work or qualifying revision. That does not permit assignment authorizing the contribution's inclusion in a wholly different collective work. Because the Court and the court of appeals did not decide transferability, they decline further resolution. They would reverse the §201(c) construction, vacate the dependent liability disposition and return for consistent statutory proceedings, without reopening the unpressed contract defenses or guaranteeing final publisher judgment on every other preserved issue. They order no fixed license, universal archive remedy or revision of unpresented agreements.

#### Source Notes

The [predecision record and briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386019_1725), including the Second Circuit opinion, support the three product descriptions, author and publisher relationship, and lower-court contract treatment. The [reported case](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-483.pdf) supplies the docket and argument and decision dates and confirms that neither contract defense was pressed here. Whitford had a written agreement; the decision does not rest on a supposed absence of all written contracts. [Section 201](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title17/html/USCODE-2000-title17-chap2-sec201.htm) supplies the separate contribution ownership, collective-work privilege and divisible-rights provisions. The record establishes the challenged products, not later licenses, altered interfaces or the proper amount of relief.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Second Circuit judgment is affirmed. Ordinary mandate procedure returns the matter for the remaining district-court remedial proceedings consistent with the statutory liability ruling. Applicable damages and equitable standards govern the relief actually requested and supported. Neither Newsday's nor Time's unpressed contract defense is reopened; no new deadline, archive deletion protocol, compulsory license or future interface is ordered.

**End of entry: New York Times Co. v. Tasini, merits decision, June 25, 2001.**

### United States v. United Foods, Inc., No. 00-276

Merits decision, June 25, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 17, 2001; decided June 25, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 197 F.3d 221, which reversed summary judgment sustaining the challenged compulsory mushroom-promotion assessments. The question is whether independent producers may be required to finance this generic advertising under the First Amendment. United Foods sought administrative relief; following its denial, district review and the government's enforcement action were consolidated.

The Mushroom Promotion, Research, and Consumer Information Act funds an industry Council through domestic fresh-market producer and importer assessments. First handlers collect and remit; direct-marketing producers remit themselves. Producers and importers at or below the 500,000-pound annual threshold fall outside the assessed categories, and mushrooms certified by a first handler for export as mushrooms cannot be assessed. Most funds support generic promotion, which United Foods opposes while seeking to advertise its own mushrooms as superior. Producers otherwise retain independent market decisions; this is not a comprehensive cooperative marketing order.

Section 6104 requires approval of plans, projects and budgets before implementation and provides contractual accounting controls. Its ban on spending to influence legislation or governmental action or policy separately permits Council recommendations to the Secretary for order amendments and voluntary grade and quality standards. Section 6105 requires both a majority of voters and a majority accounting, on average, for more than half the voting producers' and importers' annual volume. The mandatory five-year referendum differs from the optional referendum available after three years upon a request by at least 30 percent of producers and importers. These statutory terms do not establish constitutional validity or actual control of the advertising message.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6-3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The challenged advertising obligation cannot be enforced against United Foods on the adjudicated private-subsidy ground. The lower courts must implement the judgment through the statutory review and consolidated enforcement proceedings. Unresolved accounting, severability and monetary relief receive their own lawful treatment; no general refund entitlement or invalidation of independently lawful research assessments is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Justice Stevens concurred separately. Justice Thomas also filed a separate concurrence. Justice Breyer dissented, joined by Justices O'Connor and Ginsburg; they would reverse the constitutional bar and return the case for proceedings under their commercial-speech ground.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The independent-market advertising obligation violates the freedom against compelled promotional support

**Holding and operative rule:** Government may not compel otherwise independent producers to finance the selected private generic promotion here merely because the campaign benefits the industry, overcomes free riding, receives majority approval or leaves producers free to advertise separately. Where no independently sufficient collective function justifies the required association, the advertising cannot supply its own justification; the challenged mushroom advertising obligation therefore violates the First Amendment.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court directly adopt all three holdings and affirmance. The dissent rests on an independent commercial-speech ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Abood v. Detroit Board of Education and Keller v. State Bar require identifying the function that justifies compelled association before asking whether financed expression is germane to it. An advertising campaign cannot meet that requirement merely by declaring advertising to be the association's purpose. Compelled financing can burden expression even when the payer need not utter the message, the objection is commercial and no personal endorsement is attributed.

Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott permits germane nonideological promotion within its substantially collective agricultural market, with no attribution and continued independent expression. This program lacks comparable output and sales restrictions, an antitrust exemption or comprehensive cooperative marketing. The producers remain independent competitors; promotion is the principal activity for which they must associate. Common benefit alone was insufficient under Glickman, which expressly left this arrangement open. Southworth supplies another sufficient function: a broad, neutral educational opportunity for independent private expression. The present rule therefore does not require collective price or output regulation for every valid speech subsidy.

The government has offered an alternative commercial-speech argument, including substantial market interests, advancement and reasonable fit; the Court does not find it waived. Even assuming promotion increases demand and the national scope, small-producer line and denial of brand-advertising credits are reasonable economic choices, those propositions do not cure the separate compelled-private-support defect. They explain how the campaign can serve an economic objective, not the missing independent justification for forcing dissenters to finance its selected message. No empirical Central Hudson ruling or finding that advertising failed is necessary.

United Foods' freedom to promote its own mushrooms as superior does not eliminate that objection. The holding preserves Glickman's comprehensive-program result, actual government speech financed by taxes, and factual commercial disclosures governed by their own requirements. Separate health or safety purposes and independently lawful research or information assessments are outside this adjudicated advertising obligation.

##### II. The operational government-speech alternative is not decided in this review

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines, as an exercise of review discretion, to decide the government's operational government-speech defense, which was neither raised nor addressed below. Its asserted inclusion within the preserved First Amendment controversy does not require the Court to decide a materially different attribution theory or order its development; the private-subsidy dispute is resolved on the ground properly adjudicated here.

**Authority:** The same six Justices join Part II. The dissenting three find government speech unnecessary under their own sufficient commercial-speech ground; their nonreach does not join the Court's review rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Yee v. Escondido and Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. distinguish an included legal argument from a different question and preserve the Court's discretion over review. Lebron permits consideration of an included argument; it does not compel consideration of every late alternative or an operational remand whenever statutory federal control is identified.

The Secretary appoints Council members through the statutory producer/importer nomination process. Plans, projects and budgets require approval before implementation; contracts require the Secretary's prior plan approval and accounting. These are real statutory controls. They do not, by themselves, establish who actually adopted or controlled the messages at issue. The Court neither finds those powers absent nor decides that the Council can never convey governmental speech.

Lebron's determination that an institution bears constitutional duties is different from deciding that a particular financed message belongs to government. Keller preserves that distinction despite the integrated Bar's official status, while Rosenberger separates governmental provision of a benefit from the independently edited private publication it supports. Those distinctions counsel against resolving the new operational theory by label alone. The Court's refusal is prudential, not a categorical forfeiture rule or a general prohibition on financing genuine governmental speech. The disputed Central Hudson presentation is a different matter; its legal answer appears in Part I and does not rest on borrowing this theory's conceded nonpresentation.

##### III. Affirmance bars the challenged advertising enforcement and requires lawful implementation

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment rejecting the challenged advertising assessment is affirmed; the consolidated enforcement proceeding must conform to that constitutional holding. Under §6106(b)(3), a contrary-to-law administrative ruling must be remanded to the Secretary either for a lawful ruling directed by the reviewing court or for the further action the law requires; unresolved accounting, severability and monetary relief remain governed by their own legal and factual predicates.

**Authority:** The same six Justices join Part III. O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the constitutional bar and return for lawful implementation under their commercial-speech ground, without a fixed collection award or a new proof-gathering inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The government may not enforce the unconstitutional advertising obligation merely because administrative review and enforcement were consolidated. Section 6106 provides a petition and hearing, timely district review and a mandatory lawful administrative return when the ruling is contrary to law. The legal defect is now settled; the return implements it rather than postpones it for another effectiveness study or an unadjudicated government-speech inquiry.

Section 6106(b)(4) separately provides that mere pendency of the administrative petition does not impede enforcement under §6107. That rule does not authorize collection of an obligation adjudged unconstitutional or settle the distinct requirements for any properly sought interim relief. The two provisions perform different work.

The Act also authorizes research and information activities. The record does not decide the actual operation and separability of every such expenditure, the accounting attributable to the challenged advertising or the legal predicates for monetary recovery. Their treatment remains for the proper proceedings under governing remedial law. No nationwide refund entitlement, damages amount, collection award, invalidation of every Council function or automatic relief for other producers is ordered. Any independently lawful assessment must be considered on its own operation; no new statute, funding protocol or remedy is invented.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc. — distinguished at its express boundary. Its permission for germane nonideological promotion within the substantially collective program, without attribution and with independent expression preserved, remains binding; neither common benefit nor majority approval alone supplies an independent exception. The reserved advertising-centered independent market receives the new rule stated here.
- Abood v. Detroit Board of Education and Keller v. State Bar of California — their independent common-function and germaneness principles are applied to the compelled advertising. Keller also preserves the distinction between official institutional status and every funded message; neither case supplies an automatic ban on every compulsory payment.
- Board of Regents v. Southworth — its separately justified broad, neutral educational private-expression program remains valid. It demonstrates that comprehensive agricultural regulation is not the only possible independent function; its actual conditions are not replaced by a common-benefit formula.
- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission — its commercial-speech framework remains in its proper field. The Court decides no empirical application of that framework here; asserted commercial advancement and fit do not answer the independently sufficient subsidy objection.
- Yee v. City of Escondido and Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. — their distinction between an included argument and a separate question, and their review discretion, are preserved. Lebron's governmental-actor holding does not determine every message's attribution or require a new operational remand.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia — its distinction between government funding and independently edited private expression remains; the Court adopts no governmental-message finding from the funding label.

#### Current Law

The First Amendment now bars the challenged advertising-centered compelled association in an otherwise independent market because the selected promotion supplies no independent justification for forcing dissenters to fund it. Glickman's integrated-market permission and Southworth's distinct educational program remain intact. Commercial benefit, reasonable economic design, majority approval and freedom to counteradvertise do not cure this separate defect. The Court does not decide that the program failed an empirical commercial-speech test or that the Council can never convey government speech. The operative advertising ruling is settled; administrative implementation and supported residual remedies remain below.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the Court's opinion in full. Compelling payment differs from forcing a person to speak, and not every governmental use of money impairs the same liberty. But requiring an independent producer to finance competitors' preferred advertising still burdens protected choice. The assertion that a useful campaign benefits everyone does not supply the sufficient independent function that justified Glickman's collective regulation or Southworth's broad educational program. He finds the compulsory-subsidy ground sufficient and declines the new operational government-speech inquiry. He decides nothing about a materially different health, safety or artistic purpose and does not announce that every compulsory expression payment is forbidden.

Justice Thomas concurs separately as well as delivering the Court's opinion. He would not reduce the protection of compelled financing because the message is commercial. His Glickman position rejects Central Hudson's discounted framework as the governing answer to that burden. The Court's sufficient compulsory-subsidy rule resolves this case without adopting his broader standard. His observation that failure under a lesser test would also establish failure under a stronger one does not adopt that lesser test, find its elements empirically unsatisfied or supply an additional Court holding. He preserves Southworth's separately justified neutral educational program and does not prohibit every regulatory fee or tax supporting actual government speech. No other Justice joins this stronger-framework concurrence.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Ginsburg, dissents. They accept that compelled financial support can burden expression and that actual Glickman supplies no general economic-regulation immunity. They would nevertheless sustain this assessment under independent commercial-speech scrutiny: government must show a substantial interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit; no issue of unlawful or misleading commercial content is presented.

They would reach that alternative because it concerns the same assessment, constitutional controversy and relief, the government denies waiver, and United Foods addresses its merits. This exercise of discretion does not establish automatic consideration of every reply argument or new evidentiary admission. Government speech's conceded nonpresentation is separate and need not be decided on their sufficient ground.

National agricultural-market maintenance and development, together with the supported collective-action problem, supply substantial interests. The actual promotion serves those interests, and the legislative and program record and reported experience support its practical capacity to advance them. The reported failures of voluntary campaigns support compulsory contributions. Brand advertising seeks a producer's share and need not perform the same market-wide function, so an automatic credit is unnecessary. The exemption at or below 500,000 pounds can avoid disproportionate collection and administration; the reply's numerical illustration is arithmetic, not a verified cost study.

National promotion need not return benefits to each region in proportion to a producer's distribution. Exempt beneficiaries, elastic supply, new entry and possible intermediary capture do not establish that market expansion fails to serve the public objective. Reasonable fit does not require the least restrictive method, identical treatment of every commodity, guaranteed profit for United Foods or a prescribed empirical study. The reported effectiveness submission supports their assessment of the existing record; they adopt no numerical coefficient, new methodology finding, evidentiary-admission order or concession by the producer.

These three reject a conclusive subsidy bar where the government has independently justified the commercial burden. They would reverse and remand for lawful conforming proceedings, without a fixed assessment award or a new evidentiary inquiry. Accounting and other lawful implementation remain; they make no finding that an official authored, adopted or controlled the message.

#### Source Notes

The [predecision record and briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386019_1795) support the statutory program, consolidated posture, competing commercial-speech arguments and separate admission that government speech was not raised or addressed below. The government denied waiver of Central Hudson in reply; that dispute is not the government-speech concession. Its reported effectiveness review is a proffer, not a numerical finding adopted here. [Section 6104](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title7/html/USCODE-2000-title7-chap90-sec6104.htm), [§6105](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title7/html/USCODE-2000-title7-chap90-sec6105.htm) and [§6106](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title7/html/USCODE-2000-title7-chap90-sec6106.htm) supply the enacted control, collection, referendum and review terms. The [reported case](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-405.pdf) supplies the docket and argument and decision dates. Statutory approval powers are not treated as findings of operational message authorship.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit judgment is affirmed. The consolidated review and enforcement proceedings return for implementation of the constitutional holding. Section 6106(b)(3) requires remand of a contrary-to-law administrative ruling to the Secretary for the lawful ruling or further action the reviewing court directs. Accounting, actual separability and any monetary relief remain subject to their own legal predicates. No fresh government-speech or effectiveness inquiry, new filing period, nationwide refund, fixed collection amount or invalidation of unrelated Council activities is directed. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

### Adair v. Sherman, Seventh Circuit No. 99-1736; district-court No. 98 C 3946

Merits decision, June 27, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided June 27, 2001. On review of the Seventh Circuit's August 25, 2000 judgment, 230 F.3d 890, affirming dismissal of James Adair's FDCPA action against Michael Sherman and Sherman & Sherman. The question is whether adequate notice, nonobjection and Chapter 13 confirmation establish actual litigation of the collateral valuation for issue preclusion in this later damages action. The district court relied on claim preclusion; the court of appeals used issue preclusion.

Adair financed a 1995 Chevrolet Lumina with a $16,483.22 loan in March 1997 and filed for Chapter 13 in July. His plan would pay allowed secured claims in full and a percentage of unsecured claims. On September 3, the bank's attorneys filed a proof assigning the car a $19,841.43 value, reported to exceed its purchase price. Adair received notice, did not object, and does not contest the adequacy of the interval before September 15 confirmation. The bank's claim was treated as fully secured.

Adair alleges that the attorneys knowingly inflated value to increase the fully paid secured portion. His June 1998 adversary challenge ended when the bankruptcy case was dismissed. His later damages complaint supplies allegations, not a finding of fraud or a contested valuation adjudication. His subsequent return to Chapter 13 does not itself defeat pursuit of his statutory claim.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9-0, insofar as the Seventh Circuit sustained dismissal through issue preclusion based on the unopposed valuation.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The FDCPA complaint returns for consideration under its actual elements and any independently supported, properly available defenses. The district court's distinct claim-preclusion ground is neither established nor defeated by this ruling. No damages, fraud finding, collateral valuation, plan revision, deadline extension, sanction or revocation is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All parts address actual litigation, preserved statutory consequences and independent defenses, and the bounded return of the FDCPA action. No separate opinion or additional published ground was filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Notice and an unexercised objection opportunity do not establish actual litigation

**Holding and operative rule:** When a debtor receives adequate notice of a preconfirmation proof of claim but does not contest its collateral valuation, deemed allowance and plan confirmation alone do not establish that the valuation was actually litigated for issue preclusion in a later FDCPA damages action. The opportunity to obtain a contested determination cannot substitute for the adjudicative event; a genuinely litigated determination remains subject to ordinary issue-specific preclusion rules.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above controls each holding and the bounded vacatur and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Montana v. United States requires an issue actually and necessarily determined by a court with authority to bind the party. Dodd v. Hood River County likewise distinguishes an actually determined common fact from the separate federal claim and other elements. Dodd's Oregon-law and §1738 setting does not itself govern a federal bankruptcy judgment. The Court applies the distinction it preserves to this federal bankruptcy valuation: fair opportunity is necessary, but it is not actual litigation.

The $19,841.43 value was asserted in the bank's proof of claim by its attorneys. Adair received notice, did not object before confirmation, and does not challenge the adequacy of the available interval. The claim was treated as fully secured. Those facts establish the uncontested allowance and confirmation process, not a contested determination of the car's value or the attorneys' alleged deception. The later adversary proceeding ended when the bankruptcy case was dismissed; it supplies no identified independent merits determination of valuation.

The defendants correctly emphasize that Adair's theory depends on disproving the submitted value. If that value was correct, his pleaded overvaluation theory could not succeed. That overlap explains why preclusion matters; it does not supply its missing actual-litigation element. General concerns about strategic silence and finality likewise cannot merge issue preclusion with every consequence bankruptcy law assigns to confirmation.

This is no fraud exception to an otherwise binding adjudication and no excuse based on inadequate notice. The Court decides the conceded-notice, uncontested-valuation situation fully. It does not decide the effect of an actually litigated valuation, defective notice or a proof first filed after confirmation, and announces no constitutional ground.

##### II. Allowance, evidentiary force and the plan's statutory effects remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 502(a)'s deemed allowance, Rule 3001(f)'s prima facie evidentiary effect and §1327's binding plan and property consequences retain their separate operation; none, solely through this unopposed process, substitutes for actual litigation of valuation. A damages label does not eliminate those consequences or any independently applicable claim-preclusion defense, which must be established under its own governing rule and supported judgment, parties, claims and procedural record.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court adopts Part II. Its preservation of distinct effects decides no independent claim-preclusion defense on this record.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Under §502(a), a claim or interest supported by a proof filed under §501 is deemed allowed unless a party in interest objects; that class expressly includes a creditor of a general partner in a partnership that is a debtor under Chapter 7. Rule 3001(f) gives a proof executed and filed in accordance with the Bankruptcy Rules prima facie evidence of its validity and amount. Under §1327(a), confirmed plan provisions bind the debtor and each creditor whether or not the creditor's claim is provided for and whether the creditor objected, accepted or rejected. Under §1327(b), confirmation vests all estate property in the debtor except as the plan or confirmation order otherwise provides. Under §1327(c), subject to the same plan-or-order exception, property vesting under subsection (b) is free and clear of a claim or interest of a creditor provided for by the plan. Subsection (c)'s creditor-provided-for condition is distinct from subsection (a)'s broader binding provision.

**Controlling explanation:** Each provision can perform its enacted work without a finding that every underlying assertion was contested and adjudicated. Deemed allowance permits the claim's treatment in administration; prima facie evidence supports proof without making it irrebuttably litigated; confirmation binds the parties to the plan's actual provisions and carries the specified property effects. Preserving those consequences does not require enlarging them into every possible preclusive effect.

Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz enforces the specific consequence of failure to object to a scheduled exemption within the applicable §522(l) and Rule 4003(b) period. That period runs thirty days after conclusion of the creditors' meeting or a relevant amendment, whichever is later, absent an extension granted within the period. The claimed exemption became effective even without a colorable statutory basis. Today's rule neither relaxes that deadline nor converts Taylor into a direction to treat every unopposed factual assertion as actually litigated.

Semtek requires identifying the source and particular effect of a judgment rather than giving a procedural label every possible external consequence. Its rendering-state incorporation rule governs federal diversity judgments on state-law claims, not this federal bankruptcy issue. Pioneer and Hartford Underwriters likewise preserve the particular procedural mechanism and statutorily authorized remedy; neither supplies general equitable relief from nonobjection.

The district court's claim-preclusion ground therefore remains analytically distinct. The lower courts must test any properly available defense on its own elements, not simply rename the rejected substitution of opportunity for actual litigation. The Court neither holds that all bankruptcy consequences disappear upon dismissal nor determines the continuing effect of this particular plan through an unexamined general rule.

##### III. The issue-preclusion affirmance is vacated, with the statutory action and distinct defenses returned

**Holding and operative rule:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as it sustains dismissal through issue preclusion based on the unopposed valuation, and the case is remanded for consideration of Adair's FDCPA complaint and independently supported, properly available defenses. Adair must establish the applicable statutory coverage and elements; the judgment establishes no fraud, collateral value, liability or damages and orders no revision of the bankruptcy plan.

**Authority:** All nine join Part III and this complete disposition. No separate rationale or narrower-ground aggregation is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** Heintz v. Jenkins rejects a categorical exemption for attorneys who regularly engage in covered debt collection merely because their activity includes litigation. It does not make every attorney a debt collector or every unsuccessful collection position unlawful. A covered consumer debt arises from a transaction primarily for personal, family or household purposes, whether or not reduced to judgment. The process-server exception concerns serving or attempting to serve legal process in judicial debt enforcement; it is no wholesale attorney exemption.

Section 1692e prohibits false, deceptive or misleading representations or means in collecting a covered debt. Section 1692f(1) prohibits collecting an amount, including incidental interest, fees, charges or expenses, unless the agreement creating the debt expressly authorizes it or law permits it. Those provisions require application to the conduct and any properly supported defenses. Heintz's rejection of categorical attorney immunity does not resolve every bankruptcy-specific displacement or litigation-immunity theory.

The allegations receive the favorable treatment required at this dismissal stage. They are not findings of knowing falsity, the vehicle's true value, collector status or recoverable injury. The broad question whether an FDCPA action may ever rest on a bankruptcy filing was not presented below and is outside the issue-preclusion question reviewed here. The independent claim-preclusion effect of the particular judgment likewise requires its own analysis rather than an assumed answer.

Further proceedings restore consideration under the correct rules, not entitlement to recovery. The Court orders no valuation hearing, damages award, sanctions, revocation, extension of an objection deadline or new bankruptcy plan. Possible bankruptcy misconduct procedures retain their own conditions and are not adjudicated or reopened by this remand.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Montana v. United States — its actual-and-necessary-determination requirement supplies the ordinary issue-preclusion principle applied to the asserted valuation defense; overlap between issues does not satisfy every element.
- Dodd v. Hood River County — its distinction among an actually determined common fact, fair opportunity and an independent federal claim is extended to the present federal bankruptcy setting. Its Oregon-law and §1738 basis remains confined to state-judgment effect and does not itself supply the federal rule.
- Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz — its scheduled-exemption consequence and timely-objection conditions remain intact. It is distinguished from a claim that every unopposed valuation is actually litigated; no equitable deadline exception or fraud exception is added.
- Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. — its insistence on the source and particular judgment effect is applied. Its rendering-state incorporation rule for federal diversity judgments on state-law claims is not transplanted to bankruptcy issue preclusion.
- Heintz v. Jenkins — its coverage of attorneys who regularly engage in covered debt collection, including litigation, remains binding subject to actual definitions, exceptions, elements and defenses. It establishes no violation or categorical resolution of the bankruptcy-specific question here.
- Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates and Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank — their adherence to the particular procedural authority, claimant and remedy is preserved. Neither creates general equitable relief from a missed objection or authorizes a new recovery in this action.
- Hubbard v. United States — its distinct statutory coverage holding leaves Heintz intact; a different statute's judicial-proceeding treatment supplies no categorical FDCPA exemption.
- Randell v. Johnson and New Hampshire v. Maine — their respective custody-review prerequisite and accepted-inconsistent-position rules remain in their own fields. Neither decides bankruptcy issue preclusion, supplies an adjudicated valuation or permits treating an opportunity as actual litigation.

#### Current Law

An uncontested preconfirmation collateral valuation does not become actually litigated merely because adequate notice, an opportunity to object, deemed allowance and confirmation preceded the later FDCPA suit. That rule governs the presented class of notice-and-silence cases, while an actually determined issue requires ordinary issue-specific analysis. Statutory allowance, evidentiary force, binding plan provisions and the qualified property consequences remain operative according to their own terms. Independent claim preclusion and statutory defenses require separate support. The action's ultimate validity, facts and relief remain for further proceedings; no general exception from bankruptcy finality or attorney debt-collection law is created.

### Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly and Altadis U.S.A. Inc. v. Reilly, Nos. 00-596 and 00-597

#### Chronology and Posture

One signed lead contains these parts. Conditional assent to specified failures counts for those operative propositions and does not become endorsement of a reduced commercial-speech tier. The direct majority propositions control without a narrowest-ground synthesis. Stone-Zsela's fit grounds do not supply either independent advancement join.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Covered cigarette advertising is expressly preempted

**Holding and operative rule.** Under 15 U.S.C. §1334(b), a State may not impose a smoking-and-health requirement or prohibition with respect to the advertising or promotion of cigarettes whose packages satisfy the federal labeling requirements. Massachusetts's cigarette applications of §21.04(5)(a)–(b) impose such prohibitions even though they regulate advertising location; general zoning unconnected to smoking and health and independent restrictions on sales to minors are not displaced by this holding.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation.** Cipollone requires examination of the actual duty and its basis, rather than the name given a claim or rule. The amended federal clause includes both requirements and prohibitions and expressly identifies advertising and promotion as the regulated activity. These rules select cigarette advertising to reduce tobacco use and its health consequences. A location prohibition remains a prohibition with respect to that advertising; it does not become unrelated to smoking and health because children are its intended beneficiaries.

The contrary reading properly considers the federal warning scheme but gives inadequate effect to the broader enacted words. Congress did not confine this clause to additional warning language. Treating every placement ban as outside the clause would exclude a direct advertising prohibition while reaching a lesser demand to alter its message. Medtronic and Geier require attention to the particular statutory scheme; they establish neither general tobacco immunity nor an impossibility-only preemption rule. The federal package-compliance condition remains necessary. Because preemption gives complete relief on the covered cigarette-advertising component, the Court does not reach its First Amendment alternative. Neither smokeless tobacco nor cigars becomes directly covered by the cigarette statute.

##### II-A. The outdoor preventive showing establishes material advancement at this limited level

**Holding and operative rule.** On the existing record and the protected-speech assumption, the product-specific evidence and supported preventive inferences establish that limiting youth exposure to smokeless-tobacco and cigar promotion can materially advance prevention of underage use. That conclusion does not establish that the particular 1,000-foot burden reasonably fits the benefit or that every product, medium and application is equivalent.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join; Stone-Zsela's fit rationale adds no independent vote on this ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Central Hudson requires actual advancement rather than an asserted interest alone. Edenfield requires a responsive evidentiary basis; it does not prescribe one exclusive empirical form. The reports, marketing history and experience concerning these products support more than speculation about the connection between youth exposure and underage use. The record permits preventive inference without a precise causal coefficient or a universal controlled experiment.

The products nevertheless require separate attention. Declining reported smokeless use does not eliminate continuing use or historical targeting. The cigar materials must be assessed on their own terms despite the small outdoor expenditures and the absence of identified Massachusetts cigar billboards. Past-month use is not interchangeable with regular use. The conclusion neither relies on a later evidentiary Lodging nor gives an unauthorized federal rule controlling legal weight. The State's supported preventive showing answers this prong at the stated level; it does not discharge the separate obligation to justify the actual communication burden. Four members of this coalition accordingly seek further tailoring proceedings, while O'Connor concludes that the rule already fails reasonable fit.

##### II-B. The categorical outdoor restrictions lack reasonable fit

**Holding and operative rule.** The challenged 1,000-foot smokeless and cigar advertising prohibitions suppress substantially more protected adult communication than the State has adequately justified in relation to its child-protection objective. Their protected-advertising applications must be reversed for conforming relief, without a new trial to justify this categorical scheme or a judicially prescribed replacement radius.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part II-B is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Kennedy; Thomas expressly joins the failure under the lesser test conditionally while independently demanding stronger protection. Those five adopt this operative failure, not one shared endorsement of the ordinary tier.

**Controlling explanation.** Central Hudson and Fox require proportional fit, not the least conceivable restriction. Edenfield, Rubin and Greater New Orleans require justification responsive to the actual means. Even crediting the State's strongest permissible advancement showing, these rules cover different sizes and settings, brands and prices, oral proposals, enclosed stadiums and indoor information visible or directed outdoors. Their substantial urban retail burden is established without attributing every contested map percentage to this rule alone.

The restriction reaches 1,000 feet from a public playground, the playground area of a public park, or an elementary or secondary school; it does not concern every park or educational institution. It has no adult-only outdoor exception. Its breadth matters because other media do not necessarily replace an immediate on-site offer to adults. The struck cigarette and smokeless generic-sign provision supplies no remaining channel. The separate cigar permission supplies only a small generic sign, not brand or price information. A retailer's limited advertising budget does not diminish the constitutional importance of its principal practical outlet.

Reno confirms that protecting children does not eliminate adult communication interests. Existing enforcement and public education do not prove this additional burden proportionate, but the Constitution requires neither exhaustion of every alternative nor a particular substitute program. The defects are sufficient on this record even accepting advancement. Independent proceedings concerning actual deception or unlawful transactions retain their own predicates; the present protected-speech assumption is not a universal factual finding.

##### III-A. The height rule lacks demonstrated material advancement

**Holding and operative rule.** The five-foot advertising prohibition, which applies indoors only at establishments within 1,000 feet of a public playground, a playground area in a public park, or an elementary or secondary school, does not sufficiently demonstrate material alleviation of youth exposure under the ordinary commercial-speech test, even with that geographic limit and its adult-only exception. That independent deficiency requires rejection of the challenged smokeless and cigar applications; it does not establish zero conceivable effect or demand a height-specific experiment in every case.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part III-A is joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; Thomas conditionally adopts this lesser-test failure. These five expressly support the operative ground. Stone-Zsela does not join this independent explanation.

**Controlling explanation.** Central Hudson and Edenfield require a demonstrated relationship between the actual restraint and the asserted benefit. Evidence that minors encounter low signs does not establish the necessary material alleviation from a rule barring any portion of an advertisement below five feet. Older minors are not confined below that line, and younger children can look upward. Advertising above the line remains possible, while the outside-visibility prohibition separately restricts that channel's practical availability.

The geographical limitation and the exception for premises admitting no person under eighteen at any time, even with an adult, are real limits; they do not supply the missing relationship. The State's general evidence about tobacco advertising does not automatically validate this particular device. Unlike staff-mediated physical acquisition, the rule operates through the message's effect on its viewer. Calling it a sales adjunct cannot substitute for the justification required for that communicative restraint. The conclusion is one of insufficient demonstrated advancement, not a prohibition on supported preventive inference.

##### III-B. The height rule also independently fails reasonable fit

**Holding and operative rule.** The undifferentiated five-foot placement requirement also fails to justify its burden on protected adult advertising in reasonable proportion to the asserted reduction in children's exposure. This separate fit defect supports the same smokeless and cigar advertising reversal, without prescribing a different height or display arrangement.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part III-B is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; Thomas conditionally adopts the ordinary-test failure. Six support this operative ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Fox requires a reasonable relationship between means and ends even when the State describes the burden as modest. The any-portion height line insufficiently distinguishes exposure of children from adults' practical access to advertising. Neither the importance of reducing youth tobacco use nor general evidence that advertising affects demand explains this particular placement burden. The retained above-height channel, its separate outside-visibility restriction and the actual adult-only exception must be considered together.

The Court does not require one exclusive statistical method. Maps, experience, testimony, the rule's design and practical adult communication can establish or defeat fit. Here the limited justification does not answer the chosen line. A direct message restraint is not sustained merely because it appears beside valid physical-sales regulation. The Court therefore rejects the rule on fit independently of Part III-A's advancement conclusion and leaves different, unpresented display arrangements outside this decision.

##### IV. Staff-mediated access survives the presented speech challenge

**Holding and operative rule.** Assuming that product display and inspection have a cognizable expressive component, the challenged staff-mediated access requirements validly regulate physical acquisition for reasons unrelated to suppressing expression and impose an appropriately limited incidental burden. They survive for Lorillard and Brown & Williamson, USSTC and the cigar petitioners, with the amended exceptions and practical assisted inspection preserved.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Six adopt this assumed-speech conduct ground; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer concur in the access judgment on their distinct physical-conduct ground.

**Controlling explanation.** The incidental-conduct principles of United States v. O'Brien distinguish regulation of physical acquisition for an expression-independent purpose from suppression of a message. Uncontrolled product access can bypass age checks and facilitate unauthorized taking. Requiring the customer to obtain assistance directly advances the substantial age-verification interest without discounting speech because it is commercial.

The rules retain visible information, informative empty packages, actual products accessible through staff, assisted adult inspection and lawful purchases. For premium cigars, an empty box cannot convey aroma, feel or weight; assisted examination of the actual cigar answers that concern. No prohibition on all pre-purchase inspection is found. The amended adult-only self-service permission and the other applicable access exceptions remain operative. The rule need not eliminate every route by which a minor obtains tobacco to address this route. No finding that most youth tobacco is stolen, that adult-only admission is costless, or that handling goods can never communicate is necessary.

United Foods requires attention to the distinct burden and its independent function. Its compulsory-advertising-finance rule neither converts staff-mediated acquisition into direct message regulation nor supplies a new commercial-speech alternative. The Court decides only the parties' presented access claims; the cigarette challenge supplies no retail-preemption question.

##### V. Sampling and derivative inseverability remain distinct questions

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court declines a separate merits adjudication of the identified cigar-sampling restriction because the complete merits presentation does not develop its distinct constitutional challenge; the lower judgment remains undisturbed subject to the amended adult-only daily exception. USSTC's preserved argument that cigarette preemption requires derivative smokeless relief is also left undecided because neither lower court resolved that state-law consequence and the independent smokeless constitutional ruling supplies relief; any necessary unresolved severability work remains below under ordinary procedure.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part V is joined by all eight other Justices. These are express limits on adjudication, not affirmative merits rulings.

**Controlling explanation.** The actual burdens must be considered separately. United Foods distinguishes the supported ground presented for decision from a different theory that need not be addressed. A developed challenge to staff-mediated access does not itself establish the merits of a free-distribution claim. Sampling was named; the Court does not invent a failure to preserve it or a default. Its amended exception permits adult-only sampling no more than once per person per day, with the product-specific quantity limits retained. No future waiver is declared.

USSTC's inseverability argument is likewise preserved. Section 21.06 and the separate-product operation inform that argument, but a severability clause does not automatically answer it. The earlier cross-product excision of the distinct small-sign provision decides no broader consequence. The Court can resolve the independently presented smokeless speech challenge without deciding this state-law issue in the first instance. Section 22.08 separately governs the cigar regulations; its existence does not collapse the two schemes. No unresolved question here authorizes reopening the unappealed small-sign or cigar-warning relief.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cipollone v. Liggett Group: extends its predicate-duty method to the express advertising-location prohibition; preserves its claim-specific statutory conditions rather than creating industry-wide immunity.
- Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr: applies attention to enacted scope and actual regulatory context; does not control the meaning of this different cigarette clause.
- Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.: preserves scheme-specific analysis; no new implied-conflict ground is needed or adopted here.
- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission: applies the distinct advancement and fit inquiries to the protected-speech applications presented; no tobacco-specific tier is created.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox: applies reasonable proportional fit without requiring the least restrictive conceivable measure.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applies the government's burden to show real advancement through evidence responsive to the restriction; no exclusive experimental format is imposed.
- Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co.: applies scrutiny of the restriction's actual relationship to its stated objective.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applies protection for lawful adult commercial communication and the requirement of adequate justification, without converting separate stronger theories into a common holding.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applies scrutiny of supported advancement and actual fit; no general right to a second causal or tailoring trial follows.
- Reno v. ACLU: applies the continuing importance of lawful adult access when the State seeks to protect minors.
- United States v. O'Brien: applies expression-independent incidental-conduct scrutiny to physical access; does not supply a conduct exception for the direct advertising rules.
- United States v. United Foods, Inc.: distinguishes burdens and their independent regulatory functions; its compulsory-financing holding remains unchanged.

No precedent is overruled. FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee concerns the distinct contribution mechanism and supplies no substitute test for these direct advertising restraints.

#### Current Law

Covered health-based cigarette advertising-location prohibitions fall within the federal express clause when its federal-labeling condition is met. The challenged noncigarette outdoor rules fail reasonable fit; the height rules fail both demonstrated advancement and fit under the precise majority joins stated above. Supported narrower youth protections, deception restrictions and lawful age limits remain available. Staff-mediated product access survives for the parties and provisions actually challenged. The Court establishes no tobacco-wide immunity, new empirical-proof formula, general sampling rule or complete severability rule.

The advertising provisions cover oral as well as identified written and visual proposals under their actual definitions; utilitarian items not exceeding 200 cubic inches remain excluded from the definition. The separate surviving cigar-sign permission allows, but does not require, one sign of no more than 576 square inches, black text on white, stating only that tobacco products are sold there, without brand or price. It is not an adequate substitute for all protected retail communication and is not struck by this judgment.

The remainder of the sales scheme is not invalidated. The amended subsection (4) exceptions retain adult-only self-service, limited adult-only sampling, mail-order sales without coupon redemption or free samples and with at least a valid identification copy from the recipient, and qualified vending in premises licensed for on-site alcohol consumption. Such vending requires staff-controlled lockout without continuous operation, employee observation and exclusive control, and the required notices. Ordinary face-to-face and identification provisions retain the apparent-age exception at twenty-seven. The premium provision concerns the amended branded-premium restriction, not every gift; the amended cigar unpackaging provision concerns little cigars. ### Palazzolo v. Rhode Island, No. 99-2047

Merits decision, June 28, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 26, 2001. Certiorari to the Supreme Court of Rhode Island, which affirmed rejection of an inverse-condemnation action, 746 A.2d 707. The questions concern finality of the coastal agency's determinations, acquisition after the regulations, and the distinction between complete and partial deprivation on the whole-parcel premise presented.

Shore Gardens acquired the property in 1959; Palazzolo obtained personal title in 1978 after the corporation's charter was revoked. The later filling applications included a private beach club. The Council's coastal program protected the salt marsh and denied the application for failure to establish the required compelling public purpose. Section 130A permits an exception only when the applicant demonstrates all three conditions: a compelling public purpose benefiting the public as a whole through public infrastructure, a water-dependent activity generating substantial economic gain to the State, or broad public shore access; all reasonable steps to minimize environmental impacts or use conflict; and no reasonable alternative means or location for serving that purpose. Satisfaction permits an exception; it does not compel one. The stated denial does not establish separate findings about the other two conditions.

At least one upland home and substantial retained value, approximately $200,000, are supported. They are not maximum-use or maximum-value findings. Additional sites, necessary access and their feasibility remain disputed. The State did not waive all additional upland possibilities or concede that every possible access road was forbidden. The proposed 74-lot subdivision supplies the owner's $3,150,000 valuation position, not a submitted application, an adjudicated before-value or an award. The wetlands-only denominator was not presented below or in the petition. The asserted 1986 taking date is the owner's contention, not a finding of injury then or at enactment.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms rejection of the whole-parcel complete-deprivation theory, reverses the finality and categorical post-regulation-acquisition grounds, and remands the existing case for proceedings under the contextual takings standard and ordinary preservation rules.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority common to Parts I–III:** The six-Justice coalition identified in the opinion table directly adopts these parts. **Part IV:** The five-Justice coalition identified there directly adopts the contextual remand. The three threshold dissenters' conditional views are not needed to establish any holding.

##### I. A definitive restriction does not require an equivalent application

**Holding and operative rule.** When the responsible agency has definitively excluded the requested class of use and the established remaining use sufficiently defines the challenged restriction, an owner need not submit materially equivalent proposals to obtain a final decision. These denials and the governing private-filling restriction permit review of the existing compensation claim without another private-filling proposal; they do not fix every permissible upland use or the property's maximum value.

**Controlling explanation.** Suitum separates a completed land-use determination from valuation and other merits consequences: an additional act that cannot change the agency's answer is not a condition of review. Lucas likewise separates reviewability of an established prohibition from proof of a taking. The Council rejected ordinary private wetlands filling under a standard requiring a compelling benefit to the public as a whole. Shrinking the private beach-club proposal would not supply that missing public purpose. The recognized residential upland use also confirms that this is a compensation claim involving retained use, rather than an unresolved request for permission to pursue the same prohibited filling.

The State correctly identifies uncertainty about additional upland sites and access. That uncertainty remains material to the actual burden and the contextual claim; it does not make the settled ordinary-filling restriction provisional. The Court finds no agreed one-home ceiling, universal road prohibition or waiver of all further development. Nor does the unsubmitted subdivision defeat review merely because it supplies a damages valuation rather than a separate permit request. An application remains necessary when its density, design or a genuinely available exception could materially change the relevant agency determination. The rule does not make two denials universally sufficient or permit a litigant to isolate an unpresented parcel.

##### II. Acquisition after regulation is not a categorical defense

**Holding and operative rule.** Acquisition after a regulation's enactment does not by itself bar the claimant's own takings challenge or turn that regulation into an antecedent limitation on property rights. Actual regulatory conditions and acquisition circumstances may bear on reasonable investment-backed expectations within the combined Penn Central inquiry, but notice alone does not extinguish the claim or decide that inquiry.

**Controlling explanation.** Lucas requires an actual background property or nuisance limitation before the State may treat a forbidden use as outside the owner's title; a regulation does not acquire that status merely because title later changes hands. Penn Central evaluates the real economic and legal setting, and Dodd specifically rejects acquisition under an existing restriction as a dispositive substitute for that evaluation. A contrary categorical rule would allow an otherwise compensable public burden to become immune from challenge upon transfer and would burden an original owner's ability to sell.

The Court also rejects the opposite shortcut that regulatory circumstances never matter. The actual conditions, assurances or absence of assurances may affect the reasonableness of expectations when considered with economic impact and governmental character. Mere notice does not receive exclusive weight, create a presumption against later purchasers, cap compensation at the price paid or establish a background-title defense. This rule concerns the claimant's own injury. It does not itself decide when a taking occurred, establish that an earlier owner suffered one, or transfer an accrued compensation claim with title. The separate disposition of that objection belongs to Part IV and Stevens's contrary position.

##### III. Meaningful retained use defeats the total-deprivation theory

**Holding and operative rule.** The whole parcel's meaningful residential upland use defeats the Lucas category requiring complete deprivation of economically beneficial use. That conclusion does not defeat a partial-taking claim under Penn Central, and it requires no finding that one house or approximately $200,000 is the maximum remaining use or value.

**Controlling explanation.** Lucas supplies a categorical compensation rule only when complete economic deprivation has been established; Dodd applies that distinction where profitable use remains. Here the supported upland residential opportunity is substantial, not a nominal interest. Comparing the asserted subdivision value with the retained value does not transform a severe percentage loss into complete deprivation. Neither the owner's proposed before-value nor any particular loss percentage determines compensation.

The case was presented on the whole-parcel premise. A wetlands-only denominator was not raised below or included in the petition and cannot be introduced to supply the missing total-loss premise. The Court adopts no universal parcel-definition rule. The separate contextual inquiry remains necessary where the existing claim calls for it; rejecting the total-loss category establishes neither that environmental regulation is invalid nor that its burden can never require compensation.

##### IV. The existing partial-taking claim receives the combined inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** The existing compensation claim must be considered under Penn Central's combined inquiry into economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action, subject to actual preservation requirements and independently supported lawful grounds. Acquisition after enactment, retained value or a legitimate public purpose alone cannot substitute for that inquiry; no compensation result follows from the present remand.

**Controlling explanation.** Penn Central supplies the governing partial-taking inquiry, and Dodd requires its considerations to operate together rather than as competing categorical shortcuts. The lower decisions addressed that framework. The owner's emphasis on Lucas does not erase the federal contextual question they passed upon or preserve an acquisition-only answer to it. The Court returns that existing case, not a new or unrestricted cause of action.

The facts necessary for liability and relief have not been determined here. Remaining development opportunities, actual value, reasonable expectations, background property law and the timing and ownership of any compensable injury must receive their proper work where material. The Court does not find a taking at enactment or in 1986 and does not assume an assignment of a prior owner's claim. Nor does it resolve the State's disputed nuisance theory merely from its public-interest characterization. Genuine background-law defenses and ordinary limits on claim presentation remain available on their actual predicates. Those are matters of application or preserved independent adjudication under the stated rules; the contextual standard itself is settled.

No precedent is overruled. The holding adds a direct rejection of the categorical successor-acquisition defense and applies existing finality and takings rules to this record. It creates no new parcel formula, price-paid ceiling, automatic compensation, permit entitlement or categorical transfer of an accrued claim.

The contextual takings standard is settled. Its application requires actual findings where material; title history alone supplies neither immunity from compensation nor a transfer of a prior owner's accrued claim. He would nevertheless affirm because he reads this compensation claim as seeking redress for a restriction imposed before the present owner took title. The State's statement that it would permit a valuable home establishes a floor, not a ceiling, and the trial testimony supplies possible additional sites without establishing issued permission. Their approach requires neither endless resubmission of the same rejected design nor an exact maximum valuation. It requires a sufficiently settled regulatory burden for the whole-parcel challenge actually litigated. If the merits were properly reached, they agree that acquisition alone does not bar the owner's own claim, meaningful retained use defeats total deprivation and a preserved partial-taking claim receives Penn Central's combined inquiry. Those conditional views supply no vote for this remand, no new findings about additional uses and no automatic resolution of title, claim ownership or preservation.

### Tyler v. Cain, No. 00-5961

Merits decision, June 28, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 16, 2001. On certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 98-31159, whose June 1, 2000 judgment affirmed denial of federal habeas relief solely on the successive-application requirement. The lower disposition is unpublished under its own stated circuit rule.

Tyler was convicted of second-degree murder in 1976 after a 1975 shooting. Following unsuccessful state and federal collateral proceedings, he raised a Cage reasonable-doubt challenge. The Fifth Circuit permitted the present successive federal application on a preliminary showing. The district court accepted retroactivity under circuit authority but denied relief under section 2254(d). The Fifth Circuit held that the district court still had to enforce section 2244(b)(2)(A), and affirmed solely because Supreme Court action establishing retroactivity had not been shown.

The Court reviews the final judgment after the application was filed; it does not review the initial authorization order. The questions are what Supreme Court action makes a rule retroactive under section 2244(b)(2)(A), whether existing holdings do so for the confined Cage rule, and what follows from the alternative request for a new retroactivity determination. The contemporaneous amicus questions identify the statutory construction, existing-holdings application and alternative retroactivity request presented for decision.

Respondent did not dispute that Cage supplied a new rule previously unavailable to Tyler. Those remain distinct statutory requirements generally, but they are accepted premises here. The Court does not reopen them for fresh proof. The complete two-paragraph charge quoted by the district court includes both repeated reasonable-doubt requirements and disputed grave-uncertainty, moral-certainty, substantial-doubt and good-reason language. It is not treated as a concession that this charge is unconstitutional or as a certified transcript of every instruction at trial.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision. Section 1254 supplies review of this final federal appellate judgment, within the distinction preserved by Felker.

#### Judgment

**Reverse the Fifth Circuit's gate-based judgment and remand for review of the district court's separate merits disposition, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

The Supreme-Court-retroactivity condition is satisfied for the confined rule described below. On return, the lower court must review the complete charge and the actual state adjudication under the applicable section 2254(d) restriction, using Supreme Court law established at the legally relevant time. Neither the disputed words in isolation nor the gate holding establishes the constitutional violation or entitlement to relief. Newness and previous unavailability are not remanded issues. No other allegedly independent prerequisite is preserved merely by a generic formula; any actually raised additional matter requires its own legally available basis.

There is no immediate writ, acquittal, release, retrial or evidentiary hearing. If a properly available claim ultimately establishes the verdict-wide defect and satisfies the remaining merits requirements, Sullivan prevents a harmless-error substitute for the absent valid verdict. Appropriate conditional relief then permits a lawful retrial or termination of custody resting on the invalid conviction. Its terms are for the lawful merits proceeding, not fixed here. Other rejected claims and unrelated instructional theories are not reopened.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins; Parts II–III have the five direct joins identified in the opinion table. The latter coalition adopts the application and remand without aggregating competing rationales.

##### I. Supreme Court holdings may necessarily establish retroactivity together

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2244(b)(2)(A) is satisfied by an express Supreme Court retroactivity holding or by Supreme Court holdings whose necessary legal propositions jointly compel retroactivity; a lower court's persuasive extension, prediction or independent assessment does not suffice. The bar on review of an authorization order does not bar review of this later final judgment, and preliminary appellate permission does not excuse the district court's obligation to verify the statutory conditions.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress assigned the retroactivity determination to this Court. It did not require that all controlling propositions appear in one opinion or that a particular sentence announce their consequence. Necessary propositions supporting holdings are legal determinations of this Court; their conjunction can leave no further retroactivity judgment to make. General principles that merely make a proposed extension attractive are different. The inquiry remains demanding: the identified holdings must determine the result, rather than invite a lower court to choose it.

Felker preserves the distinct work of advance authorization and actual district-court verification. Its review bar concerns the authorization judgment itself, not every later judgment in a properly filed proceeding. Tyler therefore receives review of the court of appeals' final statutory disposition, without making the preliminary permission conclusive. This is an ordinary successive legal claim, not Stewart's previously presented and expressly preserved premature claim, a factual-innocence application or an original habeas petition in this Court. The accepted new-rule and previous-unavailability premises are not transformed into contested conditions merely because the statute requires them generally.

##### II. The verdict-wide reasonable-doubt rule has the required retroactive status

**Holding and operative rule.** Teague's preserved exception for watershed procedures and Sullivan's necessary holding that a verdict-wide unconstitutional reasonable-doubt charge leaves no valid jury verdict jointly establish retroactivity for that confined Cage rule. This claim therefore satisfies section 2244(b)(2)(A)'s Supreme-Court-retroactivity requirement without a separate prior opinion reciting an express declaration about Cage.

**Controlling explanation.** Teague's procedural exception requires both a fundamental protection against inaccurate convictions and a bedrock element of a fair adjudication. Sullivan holds more than that a particular error bears a structural label. When the governing reasonable-doubt standard is destroyed throughout the jury's factual findings, there is no constitutionally valid jury determination of guilt. That necessary premise establishes both the fundamental reliability defect and the loss of the indispensable adjudicative protection. Treating that same defect as insufficiently central would contradict the operative propositions, not simply favor another policy choice.

The warden correctly distinguishes harmless-error review from collateral retroactivity. The Court preserves that distinction. Sullivan expressly left collateral gates unresolved and did not itself decide this statutory requirement. Today's holding applies the necessary propositions of Sullivan and Teague to that separately presented requirement. Reservation of a later question does not remove the legal consequences of the holdings once that question arrives.

The confined result does not make every structural error, due-process violation or instructional mistake retroactive. Neither importance alone nor the absence of harmless-error review is enough. A court must identify the actual Supreme Court holdings and their necessary connection. The district court's reliance on circuit precedent alone was insufficient, but the required Supreme Court basis exists for this rule. The Court changes no constitutional rule governing the earlier state adjudication and does not decide whether Tyler's own charge presents the defect.

##### III. Passage through this gate requires merits review, not automatic relief

**Holding and operative rule.** The Fifth Circuit's sole ground for refusing this application is erroneous; the case returns for review of the complete instruction and the district court's separate section 2254(d) merits disposition. A new retroactivity declaration is unnecessary because existing holdings already supply the required status, and passing this gate neither establishes a constitutional violation nor itself satisfies section 2254(d).

**Controlling explanation.** Felker keeps actual statutory satisfaction separate from authorization, and Sullivan separates a proved verdict-wide defect from a hypothetical reconstruction of a valid verdict. The Court has resolved the contested successive-application condition. The remaining task is to determine the actual charge's constitutional effect and whether the state adjudication permits relief under the applicable habeas restriction. The relevant Supreme Court law must be the law established at the proper time; this gate ruling is not a newly invented constitutional standard applied to the state court.

The Court neither assumes that isolated phrases prove a Cage violation nor treats Sullivan's conceded defect as a concession about Tyler. The complete instruction and the state court's actual analysis must be considered. Newness and previous unavailability are accepted here and receive no new round of proof. The alternative request for a freshly effective retroactivity announcement requires no additional ruling: the existing-holdings conclusion supplies the present application's legal basis. The Court issues no advisory declaration about an unidentified later application and decides no freestanding innocence or Suspension Clause claim, neither of which is presented.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Teague v. Lane: applies the preserved contemporary procedural exception, requiring the fundamental accuracy and bedrock-procedure elements together; no new exception or discretionary equitable substitute is adopted.
- Cage v. Louisiana: identifies the confined reasonable-doubt rule at issue; this decision establishes its statutory retroactivity status without finding that Tyler's own charge violates it.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: applies its necessary no-valid-verdict holding in conjunction with Teague; respects its express reservation of collateral gates and does not make structural classification alone decisive.

- Schiro v. Farley and Caspari v. Bohlen: preserve their distinct treatment of ordinary nonretroactivity defenses and the unchanged contemporary Teague framework; neither supplies a waiver of the enacted successive condition.

#### Current Law

No precedent is overruled. The law now expressly permits necessarily entailing Supreme Court holdings to establish retroactivity and holds that the confined verdict-wide reasonable-doubt rule meets that standard. The distinct constitutional merits inquiry, section 2254(d), and actual verification within the statutory process retain their separate work. The decision changes neither limitations tolling under Duncan nor review of expired predicates under Daniels or Coss, and announces no general dispensation from habeas requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent joins the multiple-holdings construction and the distinction between authorization and actual verification. It rejects the Court's conclusion that the relevant holdings leave no further retroactivity judgment. Sullivan classified a conceded verdict-wide error for harmless-error purposes and expressly reserved collateral gates. Teague's demanding procedural exception requires a rule-specific classification; the seriousness of the defect and its structural treatment do not show that this Court has already made that distinct determination. The majority's reasoning may support making the rule retroactive, but, in the dissent's view, it does not identify existing holdings that necessarily did so.

Their position does not require a single opinion, ritual wording or an express formula. Several holdings can suffice when the consequence is strictly necessary, rather than a persuasive application of general principles. Nor does their gate-based affirmance approve Tyler's charge, declare Cage nonretroactive in every proper proceeding, reopen accepted newness or previous unavailability, or find an additional claim bar from missing earlier-application materials. They would decline an unnecessary new declaration in this proceeding because it would not supply the preexisting Supreme Court action they regard as required for this application. They promise no future vehicle and decide no unpresented constitutional attack on the successive scheme.

That order remains effective because the Fifth Circuit stayed its mandate. The present controversy includes the government's asserted current authority to resume custody. It is neither continuous imprisonment through decision nor an automatically moot dispute.

Ma entered as a Cambodian refugee as a child, received a removal order following an aggravated-felony conviction, and was detained beyond the removal period. The district court released him; the Ninth Circuit affirmed, treating the absence of an existing or pending Cambodian repatriation agreement as decisive. Neither an agreement's absence nor ongoing negotiations alone establishes the actual removal prospect. Neither record establishes harmlessness, current acceptance, deliberate obstruction or compliance with every custody-review requirement.

##### Statutory and administrative setting

Section 1231(a)(1) ordinarily provides a ninety-day removal period beginning on the latest of administrative finality, the final judicial order when judicial review includes a removal stay, or release from nonimmigration confinement. Paragraph (2) requires detention during that period and separately forbids release of the specified criminal and security categories. Paragraph (6) permits detention beyond it for an ordered-removed person who is inadmissible under section 1182, removable under section 1227(a)(1)(C), (a)(2) or (a)(4), or determined to pose a community risk or be unlikely to comply with removal. If released, that person must receive paragraph (3) supervision. Permission to detain and mandatory supervision are distinct.

Paragraph (1)(C) separately extends the period and permits detention when the person fails or refuses to make a timely good-faith application for necessary travel or departure documents, or conspires or acts to prevent removal. A country's refusal establishes none of those triggers. The government's allegations about Lithuanian documents, reporting and citizenship possibilities, and the account of Zadvydas's renewed application, remain unresolved. Any reliance on obstruction requires its actual predicates.

The December 21, 2000 replacement section 241.4 governs the current administrative review described here and expressly covers relevant criminal cases under former section 242. It replaces the old clear-and-convincing release language with the detainee's satisfaction showing and specified findings: immediate removal unavailable, impracticable or contrary to the public interest; present and likely continuing nonviolence; no likely community threat; likely compliance with conditions; and no significant flight risk. Its existence does not establish compliance or satisfaction in either case.

The rule supplies written notice, records review and reasons; an optional district interview; headquarters review; and a personal panel interview if release is not recommended or an initial favorable panel recommendation is not accepted. Two panel members must agree; a split adds a third member and permits a majority recommendation. The final official is not bound by it. A person chosen by the detainee may assist at no government expense if able to attend the scheduled interview, subject to reasonable institutional and panel security concerns. The headquarters director provides translation when that director determines it appropriate; information submitted to the panel must be in English. Current custody decisions have no administrative appeal, except completion of Board cases already pending on December 21 under the transition provision.

The initial pre-expiration review may be waived by requesting additional preparation time. The district may retain responsibility for up to three months after the ninety-day review. On an earlier referral, headquarters review ordinarily occurs by expiration of the removal period or as soon thereafter as practicable; if district responsibility continues and removal has not occurred within three months of its decision, authority transfers and headquarters review ordinarily occurs at that three-month point or as soon thereafter as practicable. Approximately thirty days notice accompanies either headquarters review. Scheduling allows unforeseen circumstances or an emergent situation. Subsequent review ordinarily begins approximately annually; a material-change request may be made no more often than every three months and receives a written response approximately within ninety days. Shorter discretionary reviews and documented postponement for prompt proper removal or other good cause remain. Reasonable care must ensure review once the reason for delay is remedied or anticipated removal does not occur. For detainees already reviewed under the prior procedures, headquarters review follows a records-only review within six months, or is scheduled one year after a review that included an interview. Those transition reviews occur within those periods or as soon as possible thereafter, allowing resource limits, unforeseen circumstances or an emergent situation. The Board completes a case pending there on December 21, 2000; if it affirms continued detention, the next scheduled review occurs one year after its decision. These are not absolute deadlines or automatic annual release trials.

Travel-document prospects must be considered; efforts continue, likely or forthcoming documents may support detention, and the rule preserves impracticable or public-interest immediate-removal exceptions. It dispenses with this custody review when removal is ready to execute and denies release for failure or refusal to cooperate. Placement or sponsorship, when ordered, must be arranged; release conditions, discretionary employment authorization and withdrawal of approval before release remain distinct. Revocation may rest on served release purposes, a condition violation, appropriate enforcement or commencement of removal, or conduct or another circumstance making release inappropriate. It requires reasons and a prompt informal opportunity to respond. Further review evaluates contested facts, with an interview ordinarily expected within approximately three months and subsequent annual review under the ordinary review provisions. None of that administrative language supplies unlimited statutory custody authority.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision. These independently proper section 2241 custody proceedings supply the review channel, with certiorari review of their appellate judgments.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins in Part I, six in Parts II and IV, and five in Part III, with the precise coalitions stated in the opinion table. Each proposition has direct majority support; the controlling implementation does not combine Stone-Zsela's separate view with Part III.

##### I. Habeas permits review of current executive custody authority

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2241 permits these independently proper challenges to the legal authority for detention, including the government's asserted authority to resume custody, without reopening the removal orders. The court must be able to determine that authority and afford effective relief when warranted; the decision does not authorize general factual retrial of removability or reweighing of lawful immigration discretion.

**Controlling explanation.** Calcano preserves an independent habeas action addressing a substantial legal-authority objection when the actual custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates are satisfied. St. Cyr applies that rule to an actual independently filed proceeding. These cases challenge custody's lawful basis, and no supported defect in the applicable habeas predicates is identified. They do not seek another merits review of the removal orders or a favorable discretionary waiver. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas apply Calcano as controlling law while retaining their published disagreement with its original construction. The statutory channel resolves review without a comprehensive new Suspension Clause holding.

##### II. Extended detention must remain reasonably necessary for removal

**Holding and operative rule.** For the previously admitted persons presented here, section 1231(a)(6) authorizes detention beyond the removal period only for a period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future, continued custody under that provision is unauthorized; a final removal order, past crime, danger or continuing diplomatic effort alone cannot supply permanent preventive authority detached from removal.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute regulates removal, permits an extension of detention and expressly provides supervised release. Its listed criminal and risk categories identify people who may remain detained beyond the ordinary period while removal remains a genuine prospect; they do not clearly create lifelong preventive imprisonment. Congress's omission of an express endpoint does not make discretionary authority unlimited.

National Center confines delegated immigration discretion to the statutory scheme. Foucha and Jackson require the nature and duration of custody to remain connected to a continuing lawful ground; additional procedure cannot replace that ground. Flores concerns temporary juvenile care with preserved individual challenges, not universal detention authority. Those principles reinforce the serious physical-liberty concern supporting this available statutory construction. The Court does not independently invalidate the statute, impose a universal psychiatric requirement or dangerousness burden, or find these detainees harmless.

The replacement custody-review regulation strengthens the opportunity to contest danger, flight and continued custody. But considering travel prospects within a discretionary safety review differs from requiring release when the detention statute's removal ground ends. Its procedures cannot cure that missing authority, and no facial invalidation of the regulation is necessary to enforce the statutory limit. Hendricks and Seling keep punishment, present authority and effective relief distinct; no punishment finding is required for this statutory relief and none is made. Previously unadmitted entrants and distinct security or civil-commitment regimes fall outside the ordinary statutory custody question resolved here.

##### III. A rebuttable six-month presumption governs the removal-prospect inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** Six months is a presumptively reasonable period of removal detention, within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety days. After that interval, a detainee who provides good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future requires the government to furnish evidence sufficient to rebut that showing; six months alone neither compels release nor forecloses other properly supported unlawful-custody claims during the interval.

**Controlling explanation.** A uniform rebuttable interval gives the Executive a meaningful initial opportunity to arrange removal while making the statutory limit enforceable. The former six-month removal period supplies a legal reference point, and the current rule's ordinary transition from district review to headquarters after ninety days and up to three additional months supplies an administrative reference point. Neither establishes a statutory ceiling or a mandatory review deadline; the rule preserves extra preparation time, practicable scheduling, shorter review, documented good-cause postponement and its distinct transition provisions. The Court uses six months as a presumption, not as a finding about every removal's duration.

The detainee retains the initial supported showing. The government must then supply a sufficient factual response; the order alone, danger alone, good faith or an indefinite theoretical possibility does not answer it. As detention lengthens, the reasonably foreseeable future becomes correspondingly shorter. Courts consider actual efforts, foreign responses, negotiations, travel documents, receiving arrangements and material detainee conduct, giving appropriate leeway to supported executive judgments, administrative needs and foreign-policy expertise without surrendering the legal-authority inquiry.

No treaty is indispensable, diplomatic uncertainty is not automatically failure, and the detainee need not prove permanent impossibility. Detention may continue after six months until the absence of the required significant likelihood is determined. Where removal remains reasonably foreseeable, crime and flight risks may help justify custody within the reasonable removal interval. Where it does not, the court must afford effective supervised-release relief, subject to the actual posture and an independently lawful custody basis. Paragraph (1)(C)'s distinct obstruction extension requires its own actual predicates; foreign refusal alone does not establish them.

##### IV. Both records require the correct inquiry and effective lawful relief

**Holding and operative rule.** Both appellate judgments must be vacated so the two records receive the required reasonably-foreseeable-removal inquiry, instead of permanent-impossibility or agreement-only proxies. When paragraph (6) no longer authorizes custody and no independent lawful basis applies, effective relief is supervised release; release preserves the removal order and supplies no new admission, immigration status, waiver or immunity from removal.

**Controlling explanation.** The Fifth Circuit's requirement that removal be impossible permits more detention than the statute allows. The Ninth Circuit's reliance on a missing repatriation agreement may overlook a genuine practical prospect. Neither error can be corrected by assuming the facts that the proper inquiry must examine. Zadvydas's effective release and any proposed resumed custody remain distinct; Ma's actual prospects must receive appropriate attention to supported negotiations and elapsed confinement. The Court issues no automatic rearrest command and no new six-month detention entitlement.

Paragraph (3) requires supervision on lawful release: periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examination at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A violation may support return to custody only under actual lawful authority and process. The regulation's revocation grounds and review cannot revive unlimited custody on a rationale the statute no longer supports. Any criminal, civil-commitment or specialized security regime needs its own established authority, predicates and protections; none is invoked or adjudicated here. Questions about terrorism or other special circumstances potentially supporting different preventive authority or heightened deference are outside these ordinary records. The remaining removal-prospect and current-authority determinations are application of the binding standard, not unresolved law or permission to preserve unauthorized custody indefinitely.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Calcano-Martinez v. INS: applies retained independently proper section 2241 review of a substantial authority objection, with actual predicates and effective relief; no broader removal-merits review is added.
- INS v. St. Cyr: applies its use of Calcano in an actual habeas action; its plea-specific temporal holding does not decide detention under former section 242 or every earlier custody episode.
- INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights: applies the requirement that delegated immigration discretion remain within its statutory purpose, without converting its release-condition holding into universal detention authority.
- Foucha v. Louisiana: applies the distinction between a continuing lawful custody ground and a procedure reviewing an insufficient ground; its psychiatric setting does not create a universal diagnostic or dangerousness rule here.
- Jackson v. Indiana: applies the required relationship between custody's purpose, nature and duration.
- Reno v. Flores: distinguishes the temporary juvenile-care regime and preserves its individual unlawful-delay and implementation limits.
- Kansas v. Hendricks: preserves its actual as-applied punishment holding and the noncontrolling status of its diagnostic discussion; it supplies no controlling due-process approval of the Kansas scheme or these detentions.
- Seling v. Young: preserves distinct punishment, current-authority, conditions and custody-relief questions, with no inference of punishment from duration alone.

#### Current Law

No earlier precedent is overruled. Paragraph (6) now receives a controlling purpose-bound reasonable-duration construction for the previously admitted class presented, with the five-Justice rebuttable six-month implementation. Independent legality review must provide effective relief when its predicates are met. The statute's actual commencement, obstruction and supervision provisions remain distinct. The decision resolves neither initial-entry custody nor uninvoked security or civil-commitment regimes, and does not create an automatic release or rearrest date.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurrence in part and in the judgments.** Stone agrees with the removal-purpose limit, retained habeas review, both vacaturs and effective supervised relief when authority ends. He does not join a numerical presumption. Once the removal period has ended and the detainee presents concrete evidence of repeated refusals or comparable obstacles, he would require the government promptly to identify facts supporting a reasonably foreseeable removal prospect. The detainee must have a meaningful opportunity to answer that account. Duration, progress, receiving-country decisions, travel-document history and actual obstruction bear on individualized necessity; ongoing negotiations or theoretical possibility alone are insufficient.

His objection does not characterize the Court's rebuttable rule as an absolute safe harbor. He declines to make a calendar presumption part of his rule because custody may lose its purpose before six months, while genuinely imminent removal can justify custody after the ordinary period. He adopts neither automatic day-ninety-one release nor a mandatory six-month ceiling. Danger may inform custody within a live removal process but cannot preserve that source after its purpose fails. Other preventive detention requires its own authority and safeguards. His different trigger is noncontrolling; the Court's five directly adopt Part III.

**Kennedy, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The dissenters read paragraph (6) to authorize discretionary detention of the covered criminal and risk categories without a judicial foreseeable-removal condition. Congress expressly permitted custody beyond the ordinary period and selected categories serving protective as well as removal purposes, yet supplied no endpoint. Foreign refusal, absence of an agreement or inability to forecast a removal date therefore does not alone terminate authority. The common detention phrase also reaches inadmissible and removable categories, which weighs against implying different meanings, although these cases do not adjudicate every initial-entry setting. They reject both the statutory limitation and the numerical implementation, while applying the governing habeas channel and preserving actual regulatory requirements. Their dispositions would affirm the Fifth Circuit against this general challenge and reverse and remand the Ninth Circuit; neither automatically orders renewed custody.

**Kennedy, dissenting, Part II, for himself.** Kennedy recognizes protection against arbitrary or capricious detention and unlawful punishment for both removable and inadmissible people. Genuine danger or flight may sustain detention even if removal cannot be forecast. The replacement regulation supplies meaningful ways to contest continued custody: notice, reasons, records and interview review, headquarters responsibility and later review. Those requirements matter without establishing compliance in either case. Denial of prescribed process in an arbitrary or categorical manner is subject to habeas challenge.

He would reject this presented general duration challenge, without a new affirmative-current-justification holding or two new constitutional merits remands. A proper concrete case may require deciding the severity of danger needed, whether an actual danger or flight finding is unjustified, the adequacy of its review, or the operation of the burden. Neither the present alien-satisfaction language nor the availability of supervision alone proves such a defect. He imposes no universal hearing calendar, new government burden or automatic adversarial release trial. The distinction between general authority and an unlawful particular application preserves those questions without inventing unfinished claims in these proceedings.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting.** They reject the claimed general constitutional entitlement to supervised release into this country merely because removal after a valid final order is not foreseeable. Their Flores and Foucha positions make the precisely asserted right, adjudicated status and authorized custody channel material. Their use of Mezei as an analogy concerns that asserted right; it is not a finding that these people were never admitted. They accept the enacted discretionary channel and the opportunity for release review but do not adopt Kennedy's separate procedural-liberty explanation or a new affirmative-current-justification rule. Their general-challenge disposition supplies no indefinite remand to develop another theory. It does not authorize torture, unlawful punishment, sham authority or denial of a legally required procedure; no such particular violation is found, and no unpresented remedy is prescribed.
