## Controlling law

#### I. Proven litigation success may establish prevailing status without a decree

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the FHAA and ADA fee provisions, a plaintiff prevails when a legally substantial action materially causes the defendant to provide an enduring, concrete benefit sought in that action. The plaintiff bears the causal showing; timing alone, favorable judicial language, an unrelated policy change or a response to a groundless claim does not establish prevailing status.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, supported in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this holding and Holding II. The four dissenters join neither rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes reward litigation success through reasonable fees to a prevailing party. They do not expressly restrict success to a judgment or consent decree. A substantial suit may secure the concrete relief it seeks when the defendant voluntarily changes the challenged conduct. The absence of a decree does not undo that success, but the plaintiff must prove that the action materially contributed to the relief and that the benefit reflects a legally substantial claim.

Hewitt v. Helms rejects favorable language without personal relief; Rhodes v. Stewart rejects a declaration that benefits neither plaintiff. Neither decides this different case of concrete relief caused by litigation. Texas State Teachers Association v. Garland separates meaningful success from its degree, and Farrar v. Hobby identifies an enforceable nominal judgment as a sufficient route while keeping status distinct from amount. Those decisions do not make their judicial-relief examples exclusive.

The competing interpretation would allow unilateral delivery of the requested relief to defeat fee eligibility for the success the enactments make attainable. That consequence confirms the statutory reading; it is not an independent equitable source of fees. Concerns about weak suits and speculative causation require genuine proof, not categorical exclusion. Courts may examine contemporaneous communications, legislative records, testimony, admissions and the course of litigation. Neither temporal sequence alone nor a statistical study is required or sufficient. A defendant need not admit liability to reform voluntarily.

Here the categorical denial prevented the necessary findings. Repeal following suit does not itself establish causation, legal substance or benefit. Those factual predicates must be determined on remand. This holding governs the two fee provisions presented; a similar application under other prevailing-party language depends on that enactment's text and structure, not automatic extension. The moot merits action is not revived, and the former residency requirement's validity is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Hewitt and Rhodes are distinguished by the required concrete personal benefit; Garland's status/degree distinction and Farrar's status/amount distinction remain in force. None had foreclosed this catalyst route.

#### II. Eligibility does not itself establish a fee award or amount

**Holding and operative rule:** If the plaintiffs prove the prevailing-party predicates on remand, the court must separately determine a reasonable statutory fee through an explained assessment of actual success, excluding unrelated unsuccessful work. Prevailing status does not guarantee a positive amount, and neither a mechanical damages ratio nor automatic compensation for all work supplies the answer.

**Controlling explanation:** Farrar v. Hobby requires an explained Hensley assessment when correcting an eligibility error leaves reasonableness unresolved. The same distinction governs here. Success produced through voluntary compliance must be proved and measured, rather than presumed from the suit's asserted importance or the resources spent. The court may reduce a requested award, including to zero where the explained circumstances justify it. No existing award is reinstated, and this Court fixes no amount. Findings under that governing standard are the ordinary remand work, not an unresolved legal entitlement.

**Precedent treatment:** Farrar and Hensley v. Eckerhart are applied to the separate reasonable-amount inquiry; their limits remain binding after catalyst eligibility is recognized.

It need not classify every settlement device or interlocutory order to reject fees on this voluntary-change record. Affirmance would leave both the fee denial and moot merits dismissal in place.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit must return the fee question for findings under the stated eligibility standard and, if appropriate, an explained reasonable-fee determination. The parties may litigate the actual causal and benefit evidence relevant to that collateral issue. The underlying mootness judgment is undisturbed; no liability finding, fee award or positive minimum follows from this decision.
