## Controlling holdings

### Available original review does not become a renewed section 2255 forum

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2255 does not permit a prisoner to reopen an expired counseled state conviction for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims merely because that conviction enhances a current federal sentence, where meaningful original review was available and the prisoner identifies no external impediment to using it. A genuinely available earlier opportunity lost through unexcused failure is not an unavailable remedy; the Court does not declare the old conviction valid or decide the different case of faultless loss of meaningful original review.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five directly join this available-opportunity rule, its finality explanation, application and affirmance. Each Justice's separate position beyond it remains distinct.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the current federal sentence and the expired predicate have different lawful review histories. Custis limits constitutional attacks at ACCA sentencing, but expressly leaves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases open. Extending its channel distinction into section 2255 requires today's statutory holding; it is not an application of an already decided universal bar.

Section 2255 expressly permits correction of an unlawful current federal sentence. That text does not erase the original judgment's review structure or make every later enhancement a renewed opportunity to litigate constitutional objections that could have been presented in the proper forum. Finality, the original jurisdiction's responsibility and the difficulty of reconstructing old proceedings support enforcing that distinction when a genuine earlier opportunity existed. Administrative inconvenience alone does not establish that every alleged unlawful sentence must escape review.

Daniels's stronger incentive to contest the predicates after committing the federal offense does not establish that the earlier forum was unavailable. His counsel identifies neither a prior attempt to pursue these constitutional challenges while in custody nor an external obstacle preventing one. The Court does not infer deliberate personal choice from every attorney failure; it decides the opportunity and allegations actually presented. Parke preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful proof rules, not an irrebuttable finding that every final plea was valid.

Maleng distinguishes expired independent custody from collateral consequences. Garlotte concerns a continuous unexpired consecutive series and does not convert these wholly expired state sentences into present custody under those judgments. Daniels's current federal custody supplies the section 2255 vehicle, but not an automatic right to relitigate each predicate. His involuntary counseled pleas and deficient-representation allegations therefore cannot proceed through this motion on the review history presented.

### Complete unconstitutional counsel deprivation remains distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A predicate obtained through unconstitutional complete denial of constitutionally required counsel, without valid waiver, remains subject to challenge through section 2255 under applicable procedural requirements and upon a showing of its adverse effect on the current sentence. Deficient representation and an involuntary counseled plea are not that exception; ordinary procedural-default requirements remain, generally including presentation of the counsel-deprivation claim at federal sentencing or a legally sufficient excuse.

**Authority:** The same five Justices directly join the preservation of this exception, its procedural boundaries and its inapplicability to Daniels's allegations.

**Controlling explanation:** Gideon establishes the entitlement to appointed counsel when constitutionally required. Burgett and Tucker preserve the special restriction on subsequent reliance on a conviction obtained through unconstitutional complete deprivation of that protection. Custis retains this distinct exception while refusing to turn every alleged defect in counseled proceedings into an ACCA sentencing issue.

The difference concerns the kind of constitutional deprivation, not a judgment that ineffective assistance or involuntary pleas are unimportant. Daniels alleges defective counseled proceedings; he identifies no complete unconstitutional deprivation. The exception therefore supplies no route for his present motion. Nor does recognition of a qualifying claim dispense with applicable procedure, proof of invalidity or the connection between the predicate and the current enhancement. No such qualifying defect or adverse-effect determination is made here.

### The absent faultless-review issue has no controlling resolution

**Scope and authority:** No Court holding resolves whether section 2255 permits review when, through no fault of the prisoner, meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated. O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas leave that absent situation open. Stone-Zsela affirmatively adopts a faultless-loss exception in his separate access rule. Scalia joins the actual available-review holding but supplies an additional statutory objection to section 2255 as the forum for an outstanding state conviction's non-Gideon defect. A statement about the Court's limited holding does not attribute personal reservation or support for an exception to either concurrence.

Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg advocate a broader express-section-2255 route, while Breyer relies on a distinct proposal to reconsider the original sentencing restriction. Those positions do not establish five direct joins to a common faultless-loss exception, and no Marks synthesis makes one controlling. Daniels alleges no external impediment placing that issue before the Court.

Stone's affirmative exception, Scalia's additional statutory objection and the dissenters' different routes retain their stated noncontrolling status. Independently invalidated predicates, actual statutory classification and other authorized applications retain their own prerequisites and remedies. Custis remains effective at its original sentencing scope, extended only as stated here.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the common available-review holding, counsel exception and affirmance, and additionally adopts an affirmative faultless-loss exception as part of his governing access rule. Section 2255 review should remain available when meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated through no fault of the prisoner, subject to applicable procedure, proof of constitutional defect and adverse effect on present custody. This is his present legal position, not merely a suggestion that an exception might be recognized later.

The distinction is between meaningful first review and repetitive litigation. Custis's forum rule and reservation do not establish that every completed conviction had an effective first opportunity, while Maleng's custody requirement must still be respected. A later enhancement creates a new incentive but does not make an earlier available remedy unavailable. Conversely, administrative inconvenience should not insulate a demonstrably unlawful current sentence from every authorized challenge. He does not equate every attorney failure with the prisoner's deliberate choice. Daniels identifies no complete counsel deprivation or external obstruction to meaningful first review, so even this affirmative exception does not allow his claims. Stone's exception has no controlling majority and is not borrowed from a decision in Coss. He orders no fresh sentencing or predicate invalidation.

**Scalia, concurring.** He joins the actual available-review rule and finality reasoning, but adds a statutory objection: section 2255's remedy for an unlawful federal sentence does not itself authorize adjudicating an outstanding state predicate's non-Gideon constitutional defect. Custis supports distinguishing an operative conviction from a collateral objection, although its actual sentencing holding did not already decide every expired-custody setting. A possible constitutional duty to provide review would concern the rendering jurisdiction and would not identify section 2255 as the remedy. He does not affirmatively endorse a section 2255 faultless-review exception. The Court's statement of what it has not decided is a scope description, not his agreement that such an exception exists. He orders no alternative forum remedy and decides no absent constitutional claim.

**Souter, dissenting, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** Section 2255 expressly authorizes review of the current federal sentence when constitutional error allegedly makes that sentence unlawful. Custis's restriction at the sentencing proceeding supplies no sufficient statutory basis for a new categorical exclusion from this express collateral remedy. Custis's available original-custody setting and reservation make the difference particularly important. The dissent's reading permits examination of an allegedly unlawful enhancement without treating Daniels as still in custody under the old state judgments.

Finality and stale evidence matter through applicable procedure and lawful proof rules; they do not justify the additional categorical bar. Parke preserves regularity and proof, Garlotte separates custody from remaining conditions, and Fiore and Glover distinguish an actual constitutional wrong and its causal effect from access requirements. The dissent preserves applicable defaults and does not automatically import all section 2254 exhaustion requirements into section 2255. It would reverse and remand for authorized consideration, not find either plea invalid, guarantee a hearing or order a lower sentence. The remaining qualifying predicates and the actual effect on lawful federal punishment would still require adjudication.

**Breyer, dissenting.** He also would reverse the categorical denial and remand, but on a distinct statutory and practical ground. Custis should be reconsidered: the sentencing scheme is better read to permit properly raised constitutional challenges to predicates at sentencing under lawful proof rules. His earlier opinion in United States v. Paleo supplies that approach. A prohibition at the original federal sentencing then generates restrictive or increasingly complex collateral exceptions; section 2255 is an awkward substitute for deciding the predicate issue when the enhancement is imposed. His disposition agreement with Souter does not make him an automatic join in every aspect of Souter's construction of section 2255. He proposes reconsideration, not an accomplished overruling, and establishes no plea defect, excuse of default, hearing entitlement or sentence reduction.
