## Holdings

### Part I. The enacted taking class and the required connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The regulated class is the taking of protected wildlife without a commercial element. A landowner's commercial motive, the wolves' past interstate transportation, projected tourism, scientific value and potential trade do not, alone or by accumulation, establish the constitutionally required interstate connection; aggregation measures effects after an eligible connection is identified and cannot supply the missing connection.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II and the judgment but do not join this part's restrictive connection premise. The five direct joins control without aggregation of separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires identification of the enacted class and its actual interstate mechanism before aggregate magnitude can establish power. Home Builders applies that requirement to wildlife taking; an owner's commercial project does not rewrite the class Congress regulated. Morrison preserves the requirement despite extensive evidence of downstream economic consequences.

The present rule prohibits taking experimental red wolves on private land without making commercial activity an element. Mann's concern for cattle therefore does not transform the regulated class into cattle ranching. Actual refuge visitors and scientific work are relevant evidence, but their economic consequences do not by themselves establish the necessary relationship between this taking class and interstate commerce. Nor does the wolves' earlier transport establish that every later local taking is itself interstate commerce. The government must establish an eligible mechanism under the governing framework rather than combine several incomplete connections. The Court does not insert a new commercial element into the regulation or adopt a categorical rule immunizing all noneconomic local conduct.

### Part II. The experimental-population taking rule is an integral part of the Act's valid interstate scheme

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may protect an experimental population established under Section 10(j) of the Endangered Species Act by prohibiting unauthorized taking on private land, where the prohibition is an integral and reasonably adapted component of the Act's valid interstate species-regulation and conservation system. The Constitution does not require proof that each protected animal crossed state lines, that the particular landowner acted commercially, or that the population independently produces substantial interstate economic effects. The red-wolf regulation falls within that permissible federal authority. This conclusion does not enlarge the rule beyond its lawful statutory terms, authorize taking not covered by an exception, eliminate a valid landowner defense, or determine a compensation claim. The challengers have not established that the regulation is unconstitutional on the Commerce Clause ground presented.

**Authority:** Part II of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion and the affirmance have seven direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this application of Home Builders Part II-A while preserving their proposed broader ground for affirmance. Those seven directly adopt the holding and the affirmance; no broader rationale commands a majority.

**Controlling explanation:** Home Builders Part II-A supplies the rule. Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies that regulation, the intrastate class actually covered and a genuine functional relationship between them. The Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of listed wildlife may include unauthorized taking as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system.

Section 10(j) allows the Secretary to establish experimental populations to advance conservation and to adapt the protective rules for them, as the red-wolf rule does through its exceptions for particular livestock threats and other defined circumstances. That tailored structure reinforces the connection. The regulated population is part of the Act's authorized preservation and recovery system, and the special taking rules are a mechanism by which that system operates in the relevant landscape. Validity requires no separate showing that the released wolves migrate across state borders or produce measurable tourism revenue, and it does not depend on the grounds addressed in Part I. The exceptions also show that the question is not a general dispute over unrestricted federal control of private land: a livestock owner's statutory right to act in specified circumstances is separate from Congress's power to establish the protective rule.

The partial dissent contends that treating the Act's integration as sufficient makes a comprehensive statute its own justification. Part II-A answers that concern: Congress cannot establish the relationship by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions, and a challenger may show that an application lies outside the class, that the interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual. The challengers make none of those showings. Lopez and Morrison still bar reliance on generalized downstream effects alone, and Solid Waste's statutory construction supplies no constitutional finding about red wolves. Because necessary inclusion resolves the question, the externality route need not be satisfied.

**Limits and remaining work:** The regulation's exceptions for nonintentional and nonwillful taking, defense of human life, taking while wolves are actually killing livestock or pets, nonlethal noninjurious harassment, and written approval after abandoned capture efforts, with their reporting conditions, remain governed by their own terms; fear of possible future harm is not converted into an automatically applicable exception. The Property Clause, treaty theory, any takings-compensation claim and distinct statutory claims lie outside the granted Commerce question. Mann's earlier conviction is not reopened. Facts needed for a particular future enforcement event are absent and are not found here.

**Limits and remaining work:** The regulation's exceptions for nonintentional and nonwillful taking, defense of human life, taking while wolves are actually killing livestock or pets, nonlethal noninjurious harassment, and written approval after abandoned capture efforts, with their reporting conditions, remain governed by their own terms; fear of possible future harm is not converted into an automatically applicable exception. The Property Clause, treaty theory, any takings-compensation claim and distinct statutory claims lie outside the granted Commerce question. Mann's earlier conviction is not reopened. Facts needed for a particular future enforcement event are absent and are not found here.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied and retained for the enacted-class, eligible-connection, aggregation and fit sequence. Remote movement and generalized economic consequences do not create the required connection; no categorical immunity for noneconomic conduct is added.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied. Part I's enacted-class rule governs Part I. Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule establishes the integral federal-regulatory connection: the experimental-population taking rule is a reasonably adapted, integral means of executing the Act's valid interstate regulation, and no species-by-species, border-crossing or independent-effects proof is required. Part II-B's independent externality route remains available but need not be satisfied.
- **United States v. Morrison:** Applied and reaffirmed. Its rejection of downstream magnitude as a substitute for connection remains intact, as does its refusal to give every failed constitutional theory another evidentiary opportunity. The connection here rests on the Act's integrated interstate scheme, not on downstream effects.
- **Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers:** Its statutory holding is distinguished from the constitutional question here. It leaves the Commerce framework unchanged; the separate bird-population analysis establishes no red-wolf fact or controlling constitutional rule.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon:** Its statutory taking and causation rules remain unchanged. This Commerce decision neither creates a new taking exception nor awards a permit, and does not substitute habitat-injury doctrine for proof of a constitutional mechanism.

## Law After Decision

Under Home Builders Part II-A, Congress may prohibit unauthorized taking of a Section 10(j) experimental population on private land where the prohibition is an integral and reasonably adapted component of the Act's valid interstate species-regulation and conservation system, without proof that each protected animal crossed state lines, that the landowner acted commercially, or that the population independently produces substantial interstate economic effects. The experimental red-wolf regulation is valid against the Commerce Clause challenge presented and remains enforceable within its statutory terms and exceptions. Commercial motive, past interstate transport, tourism, scientific value and potential trade do not themselves establish the connection. The agency retains its statutory duties and the limits Section 10(j) imposes.

## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Breyer.** These Justices join Part II and the judgment. They continue to favor the broader, materially supported substantial-class-effects approach stated in their Morrison dissent. Actual interstate visitation, volunteer activity and organized scientific use depend on the living population, and preventing the class of takings reasonably protects that resource. A supported class-wide relationship does not require a fixed revenue figure or a substantial effect from each wolf. Future pelt speculation and national conservation importance alone are insufficient and unnecessary to their view. That broader ground has not become the Court's rule. Given the controlling connection framework, they join Part II's application of the Home Builders Part II-A rule they joined, without finding the externality route established.

**Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Souter.** These Justices join Part II and the judgment and would also affirm under the same proposed broader class-effects rule. The actual interstate use and study of an irreplaceable living population support a rational, materially grounded substantial-effects judgment, and preventing killing fits protection of that resource. Their view retains substantiality, evidence, reasonable means and judicial review but rejects the Court's additional connection prerequisite, so they do not join Part I. Their earlier view of economic habitat conversion does not establish that direct killing is economic, and their Solid Waste ecological position supplies no interstate red-wolf facts. They do not assert that several incomplete Home Builders theories add up to a complete one; they join Part II because it rests on the Home Builders Part II-A rule they joined.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** These Justices join Part I and would reverse for declaratory or injunctive relief confined to the challenged private-land applications. They accept authority over local conduct genuinely necessary to protect a valid interstate regime, but the offered grounds establish no supported anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship. They adhere to their Home Builders dissent from Part II-A: the Act's integrated structure does not itself supply that relationship, and treating it as sufficient makes a comprehensive statute its own constitutional justification. They also retain their Home Builders objection to territorial incapacity as an independent source of commerce authority. Morrison confirms that economic consequences cannot supply the missing connection and that failure of the offered theories does not entitle the government to search indefinitely for another. Their proposed relief neither invalidates the ESA generally nor authorizes killing contrary to other valid law, revisits Mann's prior conviction, or awards compensation.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit judgment is affirmed, and the experimental red-wolf regulation remains enforceable in the challenged private-land applications, subject to its own exceptions and conditions. The Commerce Clause challenge is concluded; Supreme Court merits review is complete.
