#### Confinement at commencement controls the covered action despite later release

**Holding and operative rule:** Confinement when a federal civil action is brought determines section 1997e(e)'s application to claims for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without the required prior showing of physical injury. Subsequent release and a supplemental pleading recording release do not cure that deficiency in the existing action; dismissal resting solely on it must remain without prejudice to a permissible post-release action and is no adjudication of the alleged misconduct.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, supported in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. These five directly adopt the rule, its complete injury-specific scope and the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute selects bringing the action as the relevant event. Its definition identifies who is a prisoner; it does not move that event to judgment. These six plaintiffs were confined when they commenced this action. Release changes their present status but not the legal consequence Congress attached to commencement.

Rule 15(d) permits supplementation on motion, reasonable notice and just terms, including where the original pleading was defective. It can record a later event; it cannot alter the statute's selected historical condition. Semtek preserves that boundary between procedural rules and substantive statutory consequences. McNeil v. United States likewise requires attention to an enacted prior condition rather than the amount of litigation that follows. Mathews v. Diaz allowed later-application supplementation under the benefits statute there; it does not establish that every commencement condition is curable. Its separately waived exhaustion issue supplies no waiver here.

Avoiding duplicated litigation is a substantial practical argument, but it supplies neither an ordinary release-based cure nor a special exception after unusual investment of judicial resources. The statutory trigger does not change when more work has been done. This conclusion assumes no improper litigant motive and does not determine the disputed procedural adequacy of the actual supplemental paper: even adequate supplementation recording release would not cure this defect.

The appellate court's without-prejudice direction respects the restriction's limited operation. The Court neither determines the alleged shakedown violations nor imposes a single bar on every monetary claim. General treatment of nominal, punitive, prospective or independent economic relief, the physical-injury threshold in other cases and the statute's constitutional validity are outside the accepted temporal question. Limitations, tolling and preclusion in a new post-release action require claims and procedural facts not presented here; no later action is declared timely or successful.

**Precedent treatment:** McNeil and Semtek are applied to preserve the enacted condition against procedural alteration; Mathews is distinguished by its particular application requirement and separate exhaustion waiver. Artuz and Randell do not already decide this temporal rule.

## Law After Decision

For claims within section 1997e(e), the existing action's commencement during confinement fixes the temporal restriction; later release recorded in a supplemental pleading does not remove it. A dismissal based solely on that restriction does not adjudicate the underlying conduct and must not permanently bar an otherwise permissible post-release action. This new temporal holding supplies no universal classification of monetary remedies, physical-injury threshold, jurisdictional rule or timeliness decision for a later suit.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The dissent accepts that the covered action was defective when filed during confinement, but would allow ordinary consideration of a supplemental pleading after release removes the personal disability. Rule 15(d) permits later-event supplementation even where the original pleading was defective, subject to proper motion, reasonable notice, just terms and the district court's discretion. The statute does not, in the dissent's view, foreclose that route once these plaintiffs are no longer confined.

Mathews v. Diaz illustrates later satisfaction of a personal application qualification; its distinct exhaustion waiver is not necessary to that comparison. McNeil protects a different interest: the administrative opportunity Congress required before an FTCA suit cannot be restored after a premature filing. Recording release would not eliminate such an administrative sequence here or create a substantive damages right. Semtek's rulemaking boundary remains respected because supplementation operates within the dissent's reading of the statute. Artuz likewise requires examination of the particular condition rather than reliance on a general label.

The dissent recognizes the majority's argument that commencement is a selected historical event, but considers dismissal and a second filing unnecessary where ordinary lawful supplementation can reflect the removed disability. It does not adopt a special requirement that the case be rare or that dismissal would manifestly waste resources. Litigation progress may inform ordinary discretion; it supplies neither a mandatory threshold nor proof that remand would be futile.

These Justices would reverse only the categorical foreclosure of supplementation for the six released plaintiffs and remand for the actual motion, notice, terms and permission questions, together with ordinary remaining claims and defenses. They would not deem the supplement valid, compel leave, establish physical injury, adjudicate the alleged misconduct or award damages. The separate disposition of the other five plaintiffs and questions outside the accepted injury-specific issue would remain undisturbed.

## Procedure After Action

The en banc judgment remains effective on the temporal issue, including its remand for dismissal without prejudice of the six released plaintiffs' claims barred solely by section 1997e(e). No new remand for discretionary release-based cure is ordered. An independently filed post-release case, if any, would require its own limitations, preclusion and merits analysis; this judgment supplies no outcome for it.
