## Event, record and participation

Research cutoff: 2001-01-17. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 200 F.3d 836, amended at 208 F.3d 1066. The petition asks whether Gasperini displaces the circuit approach restricting district-court discretion to grant a new trial while expanding appellate review. The question concerns the July 23, 1998 conditional new-trial order, not the procedural-due-process liability established in the earlier appeal. The District seeks to overturn reinstatement of the first verdict, sustain that order and obtain further appellate proceedings; Tri County seeks to preserve the first judgment or, if the order is sustained, obtain review of its outstanding second-trial objections.

The first jury awarded $5 million. The trial judge denied judgment as a matter of law but ordered a new trial unless Tri County accepted $1 million within thirty days. His stated grounds concerned mitigation of projected profits, the profit model, excessiveness and reconsideration of excluded evidence. The second jury awarded $100. The court of appeals reinstated the first verdict and vacated the second verdict and judgment. Its April 21, 2000 amendment provides interest at 4% per annum beginning April 20, 1998, computed daily until payment, citing D.C. Code section 28-3302 for the rate and 28 U.S.C. section 1961 for computation. No acceptance of remittitur is established.

The October 15 and December 6, 1993 letters and identified health-and-safety testimony were struck at the first trial; the Siess proffer was excluded. The citation, stop-work order, unpaid fine and appeal defenses were presented to the jury. The operating permit retained testing, inspection and other conditions. It supplied neither an unconditional right to operate nor a finding that later lawful closure would occur. The July 23 order's later reconsideration of excluded proof is distinct from the initial rulings. Evidence first offered at the second trial cannot sustain the earlier order retroactively. The parties dispute appellate preservation; the unexplained rehearing denial establishes neither waiver nor its absence.

## Entering law and reach

The opening law through June 29, 2000 governs together with effective earlier-date decisions. The case-specific entering-law slice supplies the following operative authorities and their limits:

- **Gasperini, June 24, 1996:** Rule 59 gives the district judge the initial new-trial assessment; appellate review examines legal premises independently and reviews supported discretionary assessment for abuse. It does not authorize an appellate court's first-instance valuation. The state compensation measure applied in that diversity action is not the measure for this federal civil-rights suit. The operative register is **State compensation limits within federal new-trial review**, present operation effective June 24, 1996.
- **Williams, May 4, 1992:** The Court may review a federal question expressly decided below despite a party's failure to repeat opposition to binding circuit precedent where adherence did not concede correctness. Permission to review creates no duty to reach and excuses no affirmative waiver. The question was expressly passed upon here; that fact does not eliminate discretionary vehicle concerns.
- **Lebron, February 21, 1995, and X-Citement Video, November 29, 1994:** The first permits consideration of an included legal argument on an established record without requiring that choice; the second permits a respondent's record-supported defense of its favorable judgment without enlarging its relief. The operative register is **Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review**, with those separate effective dates and limits. Neither establishes a disputed filing or a waiver finding here.
- **Carey v. Piphus:** Actual damages require injury caused by the denial of required process. The value of an underlying business and the denial of procedure are distinct; nominal damages remain distinct from proved consequential loss. This principle supplies no factual conclusion about the amount recoverable here.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting from dismissal.** He would reach the question and affirm under ordinary abuse-of-discretion review. Williams permits review of a federal question expressly decided below; the court of appeals expressly applied its more-searching-review formulation drawn from binding circuit precedent. The parties' conflicting preservation accounts and the unexplained rehearing denial establish no waiver finding. The existing first-trial record permits review of each stated new-trial ground without new facts; affirmance leaves the separate second-trial challenges unnecessary to decide.

Gasperini preserves trial-court discretion while permitting independent review of legal error, and forbids a fresh appellate valuation. Its particular state-law compensation measure does not govern this federal civil-rights action. A judge may reconsider an earlier discretionary exclusion; the Chief Justice does not treat the initial ruling's permissibility as a bar to doing so. The reconsidered proof must nevertheless have a lawful relevance and evidentiary foundation.

Here the judge repeatedly sought a lawful mechanism for terminating the permitted operation and found the District's proffer inadequate. He struck the October 15 and December 6 letters and health-and-safety testimony and excluded Siess's expert proffer, while submitting the fine and appeal defenses to the jury. The July order relies on that same unsupported prospect of community resistance and later closure. Community opposition is not itself lawful regulation. The permit retained testing and inspection conditions; the judge assumed compliance at the approved site, and counsel's contrary suggestions and the excluded proffer supplied no adequate foundation. No community or misrepresentation proceeding had occurred. The order did not rely on occupancy review, and the Chief Justice does not decide whether section 3203.1 or Woodley Park could supply a lawful mechanism on a different record.

The order's excessiveness reason particularly, and its speculation reason partly, depend on the same deficient closure premise. Its remaining doubts about inexperience, the failed Virginia operation and falling treatment prices concern disputed valuation evidence the jury was instructed to weigh, including new-business uncertainty and competition. The award was less than half the profit projection. On this record those doubts do not justify displacing the jury's supported assessment.

The categorical premise that future profits cannot be recovered without seeking permit reinstatement is also legal error. Mitigation was for the jury on proof the District bore the burden to supply, taking account of the available route, cost, delay and likely utility. The order did not expressly rest on the October letter as an unanswered invitation to be heard; insofar as failure to seek reinstatement supplies its mitigation ground, the same categorical error remains. The District had an opportunity at the first trial to challenge the expert's premises. Later-trial evidence cannot justify the earlier order. Competent testimony and its factual premises, contemporaneous business evidence, mitigation and causation matter; no separate statistical study is required.

He would preserve the first verdict and the appellate interest amendment without another trial. His reasoning establishes neither automatic lost-profit recovery for permit suspensions nor a new damages formula, and it does not decide the deference owed to a different, properly supported order or materially different record.
