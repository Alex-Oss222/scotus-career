## Holdings

### I. Voluntary abandonment leaves no continuing controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A former licensee who withdraws renewal, abandons the regulated business and establishes no concrete plan to return or surviving claim has no continuing personal stake in prospective review of the licensing procedure. Refusal to promise permanent nonreturn does not restore that stake; this writ must therefore be dismissed as moot without deciding the granted First Amendment question.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both the mootness and equitable-vacatur holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans requires a personal controversy throughout review after the claimant voluntarily leaves the regulated setting. City News withdrew its renewal request, closed the adult business and asserts no present plan to obtain another license. No surviving damages claim is established within the grant. The disabilities from the 1995 violations expired in 2000. The proposed through-2005 disability depends on an additional violation finding the council never made; the council instead permitted operation during state review.

Laidlaw's demanding defendant-cessation rule protects a plaintiff's continuing stake against a defendant's strategic cessation. It does not require Waukesha to prove that this applicant will never change its mind after voluntarily abandoning the activity. Neither a short-lived action reasonably likely to recur for this applicant nor a presently imposed surviving disability is shown. A concrete licensing plan or surviving claim would present different facts, but speculation is not such a showing. The importance of the prompt-review issue and the First Amendment supply no exception to Article III. The Court accordingly decides neither prompt access versus prompt determination nor the distinct question of interim operation during review.

### II. The unfavorable state judgment remains undisturbed

**Holding and operative rule:** Dismissal for mootness does not automatically require vacatur of the judgment under review; the party seeking that equitable relief must justify it in the actual circumstances. City News voluntarily abandoned the activity and application that made review useful and establishes no further sufficient equity, so the Wisconsin judgment is left intact without affirmance of its federal merits reasoning.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion directly adopts the equitable disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** U.S. Bancorp preserves authority to dispose of an unreviewable judgment equitably while rejecting automatic judgment clearing after voluntary loss of review. City News is the reviewing loser that chose to abandon the renewal and business. No further sufficient equitable circumstance is established. Arizonans involved a prevailing claimant's departure followed by lower federal merits adjudication and justified corrective vacatur on those distinct equities. No comparable lower federal jurisdictional error is present here. Article III governs this Court's ability to decide the federal controversy; it does not retroactively govern the Wisconsin court's jurisdiction. Leaving that judgment in place decides neither its correctness nor the licensing rule.
