## Holdings

### Conforming implementation of the two approved agreements

**Holding and operative rule.** In this continuing original action, an implementing decree must incorporate the quantities and limits the Court approved on June 19 while preserving every expressly reserved claim. Clauses A–G conform to that approval and are entered without a new allocation rule or an adjudication of reserved rights.

**Authority.** The Court's supplemental decree controls the parties and the administration of this original action. Its authority is the Court's retained original jurisdiction and June 19 settlement judgment; the unsigned instrument publishes no individual joins or tally.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court's June 19 decision approved the two agreements and required implementing provisions. Those agreements establish the entitlements, releases, and reservations against which the proposal is measured. The aggregate quantities, California entries, and limited boundary-adjustment exception implement that approval. Nebraska v. Wyoming distinguishes enforcement of an established decree right from a request for a new allocation requiring proof of injury; no new allocation showing is required to incorporate these approved adjustments. A provision inconsistent with the approval would require correction. The proposed Fort Mojave dates, however, repeat different dates already present in the general entitlement and California table. Entry preserves that inherited distinction and changes neither priority. The absence of objections supplies no consent beyond the approved agreements. Retained jurisdiction permits enforcement and later adjudication of separately preserved matters; it does not decide them now.

**Settlement scope.** Fort Mojave receives the lesser of an additional 3,022 acre-feet or water sufficient for 468 acres. Its agreement fixes the specified reservation boundary near the Hay and Wood Reserve and bars further reserved Colorado River water claims by the United States and Tribe for Hay and Wood Reserve lands. Competing title and jurisdiction claims concerning the bed of the river's last natural course within that boundary remain reserved, as do private land claims, title, and jurisdiction. The Colorado River Reservation receives the lesser of an additional 2,100 acre-feet or water sufficient for 315 acres. Its agreement bars further reserved Colorado River water claims by the United States and Tribes for California reservation lands. The disputed boundary, including the ambulatory-west-bank and fixed-line theories, and competing riverbed-title and jurisdiction claims remain reserved. Clause C neither enlarges those releases nor decides the reserved boundary.

### The amended boundary proviso preserves the rest of condition (5)

**Holding and operative rule.** Clause C adds its exception only to the boundary-adjustment proviso at the end of the first sentence of the 1979 decree's introductory condition (5). That condition's remaining shortage, calculation, use, and priority rules continue to govern on their existing terms.

**Authority.** Clauses C and F of the Court's supplemental decree preserve the unamended condition; no broader replacement is entered.

**Controlling explanation.** The approved releases remove the western California boundaries of the two named reservations from the proviso for future entitlement adjustments. They supply no basis to discard the rest of condition (5). Under the preserved condition, a determination under Article II(B)(3) that mainstream water is insufficient to satisfy present perfected rights requires the Secretary of the Interior first to satisfy in full the specified rights of the Chemehuevi, Cocopah, Fort Yuma, Colorado River, and Fort Mojave reservations before other nonmiscellaneous present perfected rights. Those other rights retain their priority-date order without regard to state lines. The priority order of miscellaneous rights numbered 7–21 and 29–80 is unaffected. Boundary adjustments outside clause C's exception continue to measure net practicably irrigable acreage by the Master's December 5, 1960 report methods and use these acre-feet-per-acre diversion quantities: Cocopah 6.37, Colorado River 6.67, Chemehuevi 5.97, Fort Mojave 6.46, and Fort Yuma 6.67. Irrigation measures the entitlement's quantity; it does not limit use to agriculture. If water is used for another purpose, total consumptive use may not exceed what the specified irrigation diversions and equivalent supplements would have consumed for the specified acreage and related uses. The condition establishes no priority for other unspecified Indian water claims.

**Questions outside this submission.** The Fort Yuma merits and additional water quantity, the expressly reserved riverbed and private-title claims, the reserved jurisdictional claims, and the Colorado River Reservation's disputed boundary are outside this implementing submission. They receive no adjudication by implication. The separate dates already present in the Fort Mojave provisions are preserved without deciding the source or resolution of that inherited difference.
