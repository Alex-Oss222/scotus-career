## Holdings

### I-A. The mandatory condition can be challenged without a refused request

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff whose concrete intended or continuing activity is subject to an enforceable approval condition may challenge that condition without first seeking and being denied permission. The supported teaching and research activities here permit review of the condition itself; relief must still correspond to the plaintiffs' actual continuing injury under the amended coverage, and no hypothetical future refusal is adjudicated.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this threshold ruling and the general-entitlement holding that follows.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged burden is the necessity of advance written public permission, not only the possibility of a refused request. The record identifies removed Internet assignments, proposed research concerning Victorian poetry and other sexual subjects, scholarly databases, and Smith's website blocking and partial restoration. Published accounts identify summary-judgment submissions for material portions of those claims; this is not merely an unspecified fear of workplace regulation. None of these plaintiffs requested approval, and the record reports no denied request. Those facts limit any refusal claim but do not defeat this condition challenge.

Smith's blocking is not a denied statutory application. Nor does a document labeled a statement of undisputed facts establish that the Commonwealth conceded every assertion. The amended dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements narrow the statute; they do not establish that all the intended inquiry lies outside it. Article III power rests on the supported concrete condition burden, not an assumption of jurisdiction to decide disputed merits. The continuing covered activities and beneficiaries of final prospective relief must be determined at their proper scope.

### I-B. Protected subject matter alone creates no unrestricted employee resource right

**Holding and operative rule:** The lawful or constitutionally protected character of material, its usefulness to an employee's job, and delegated professional discretion do not alone give every state employee a personal right to choose its use through government equipment free of approval. The reversal of the district court's wholesale declaration of invalidity remains in force, without sustaining every possible application to nonacademic employees or resolving the distinct individual scholarly claim.

**Authority:** Part I has all nine direct joins, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring requires identification of the employee's personal interest before balancing and permits lawful institutional control of an assigned instructional production. An employer ordinarily defines its prescribed work, allocates equipment and prevents misuse. The message and task matter; the computer's ownership is not a complete answer to an independently protected personal claim. The professors expressly disclaim personal recreational use, which is outside review.

The district court addressed the earlier, broader content definition and work-related activity across state employment. That reasoning cannot establish the amended law's substantial unconstitutional reach or support the same wholesale decree on these six faculty claims. NTEU preserves the distinction between a supported personal right and relief for unrepresented employees. This ruling does not adopt a rule that one lawful application defeats every First Amendment overbreadth challenge. Genuine institutional decisions remain subject to independent constitutional and other applicable limits, and the individual university question requires its own answer.

### II. Independent scholarly expression requires its own protection and burden inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Independent university inquiry, teaching or communication on matters of public concern may carry a personal First Amendment interest according to its function and context; salary, connection to duties and use of public equipment do not alone make it the institution's prescribed message. Once that interest is established, Pickering and NTEU require the State to justify the actual prospective approval burden, including its effect on scholars and audiences, with a commensurate connection to legitimate operational needs; this rule grants no personal control over curricula, assignments, budgets, professional standards or equipment allocation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: six direct joins. The five Associates also join Part III's limited remand. Stone-Zsela joins this rule but concludes that the present record already establishes a violation requiring relief; his conclusion is not part of the Court's application or mandate. O'Connor assumes an interest without joining this affirmative rule; Scalia and Thomas reject the claimed entitlement.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring decided an assigned secondary-school production and expressly left university academic freedom and a universal duty-speech rule open. It cannot make every scholarly undertaking institutional expression. Keyishian protected individual educators and a library employee against the challenged speech and membership restrictions; its concern with official orthodoxy is not exclusively an institution's right. Sweezy's plurality and separate concurrence support attention to scholarly freedom on different grounds, not a single comprehensive academic-freedom test. Neither decision grants ownership of public research resources.

Pickering and Connick require contextual protection and employment justification. NTEU gives the actual prospective burden on speakers and audiences weight; it does not automatically classify every work activity as personal expression. Givhan confirms that location and private communication do not alone defeat protection. Waters preserves genuine lawful employer grounds but supplies no universal investigation procedure or answer to this statutory condition.

The inquiry must account for the amended content definition, required-use limitation, both approval branches, written approval, public availability and actual administration. Efficiency, resource management and preventing harassment are legitimate interests. The State may use operational evidence and supported predictive judgments; completed harassment, expert studies, automatic strict scrutiny or proof of actual disruption in every case is not required. Conversely, ownership and the availability of approval do not by themselves answer the burden of obtaining public advance permission for protected exploratory inquiry. Delegation to deans or department heads is relevant practice, not an enacted guarantee of universal peer review. Scholarly labels, underinclusiveness and a sexual subject alone establish neither protection nor invalidity. The standard preserves genuine academic governance and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions.

### III. Apply the amended condition to the represented scholarly claims before directing relief

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court's categorical employee-role and exclusively institutional academic-freedom rules bypassed the necessary inquiry. Five reported misuse incidents and complaints support preventive concerns without establishing five harassment judgments or criminal guilt. Prompt approvals with minimal paperwork and blanket permissions support the contention that the condition is modest. They are examples, not statutory deadlines, assurances that every request will succeed or elimination of the written public-approval burden. A research connection does not replace the statutory requirement that access be required for an approved project or other undertaking. The lower courts must assess the actual protected scope and fit under the amended law, using the existing submissions and ordinary procedural rules for any properly authorized further proceedings.

This remand is not based on an evidentiary vacuum or unrecovered original exhibits. It presumes neither that plaintiffs must win nor that every approval system fails. ## Record and legal premises used

The operative statute is the amended law considered by the en banc court. It regulates an agency employee's use of agency-owned or agency-leased computer equipment to access, download, print, or store information-infrastructure files or services with covered content. The content must have as its dominant theme a lascivious description or depiction of the enumerated sexual subjects: sexual bestiality, lewd exhibition of nudity, sexual excitement, sexual conduct, sadomasochistic abuse, coprophilia, urophilia, or fetishism. The incorporated definitions remain those reproduced in the official lower opinion, including the forms of nudity, actual or explicitly simulated sexual conduct, genital arousal, and the specified torture, flagellation, or restraint. The amendment does not turn all covered material into unprotected obscenity.

An agency includes a public institution of higher education but excludes the Department of State Police. Information infrastructure includes telecommunications, cable, and computer networks, including the Internet, the World Wide Web, Usenet, bulletin boards, online systems, and telephone networks. The operative exception permits access only to the extent required in conjunction with a bona fide, agency-approved research project **or other agency-approved undertaking**. The second branch is not restricted to research. Agency heads must give approvals in writing, and approvals must be available under the state public-records law. The statute does not promise approval of everything merely related to work. Nor does it mandate an outside nonacademic decisionmaker or universal academic peer review. Reported delegation to deans or department heads is administrative practice.

## Five-Justice common rationale and remand scope

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer support the following at the same level of generality:

1. A university professor may possess a personal First Amendment interest when engaged in independent scholarly inquiry, teaching, or communication on matters of public concern. The interest depends on the activity's function and context, not the academic title. Salary, duty connection, and public equipment do not alone make that expression the institution's prescribed message. This does not establish a personal right to dictate institutional curricula, funding priorities, assignments, or the allocation of equipment. Genuine institutional decisions and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions remain.
2. Once such an interest is identified, the State must justify the actual burden of this prospective content-based condition under Pickering and NTEU. The inquiry includes the effect on the professor and audience, the narrowed content definition, the required-use limit, both approval branches, written and public approval, and actual administration. The State's efficiency, resource-management, and prevention-of-harassment interests are legitimate. Their weight and the restraint's fit must address the protected work actually affected. Ownership alone is not that justification.
3. The court of appeals cannot reject the claim solely because the inquiry is work-related or because academic freedom supposedly belongs only to the institution. It must apply the amended statute, distinguish independent scholarly activity from institutional output, and assess the condition itself. The record contains supported claims and employer evidence on both sides; it is not an evidentiary vacuum. The earlier district analysis of a broader statute and all state employees does not supply the final answer for this narrower faculty claim.
4. A remand is required because the categorical appellate rule bypassed the activity-specific protection and burden inquiry, while the district judgment addressed a different definition and substantially broader relief. No finding of improper motive, unlawful refusal, or entitlement to unrestricted access is made. No presumption that all approval systems fail or that these plaintiffs must win replaces the required inquiry.

The State may rely on appropriate operational evidence and supported predictive judgments. No completed harassment violation, expert study, special hearing, newly commissioned research, or universal proof protocol is imposed. Conversely, successful approvals do not automatically answer the chilling effect of requiring public advance permission for protected inquiry. A scholarly label does not establish coverage, protection, harm, or victory. Access to unprotected obscenity, personal use, and unrelated misuse are not rights established here.

On remand, ordinary procedural rules govern the existing submissions and any properly authorized further proceedings. Determine which plaintiffs' continuing activities are covered by the amended statute and implicate a personal interest, then decide whether the actual condition is justified at that scope. Any declaration or injunction must redress an established violation and reach no further than the represented rights and effective relief require. The Court does not direct the approval of a particular project, invalidate the public-records requirement in isolation, substitute itself for a dean, or require a particular future enforcement decision.

## Holdings

### I-A. The mandatory condition can be challenged without a refused request

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff whose concrete intended or continuing activity is subject to an enforceable approval condition may challenge that condition without first seeking and being denied permission. The supported teaching and research activities here permit review of the condition itself; relief must still correspond to the plaintiffs' actual continuing injury under the amended coverage, and no hypothetical future refusal is adjudicated.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this threshold ruling and the general-entitlement holding that follows.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged burden is the necessity of advance written public permission, not only the possibility of a refused request. The record identifies removed Internet assignments, proposed research concerning Victorian poetry and other sexual subjects, scholarly databases, and Smith's website blocking and partial restoration. Published accounts identify summary-judgment submissions for material portions of those claims; this is not merely an unspecified fear of workplace regulation. None of these plaintiffs requested approval, and the record reports no denied request. Those facts limit any refusal claim but do not defeat this condition challenge.

Smith's blocking is not a denied statutory application. Nor does a document labeled a statement of undisputed facts establish that the Commonwealth conceded every assertion. The amended dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements narrow the statute; they do not establish that all the intended inquiry lies outside it. Article III power rests on the supported concrete condition burden, not an assumption of jurisdiction to decide disputed merits. The continuing covered activities and beneficiaries of final prospective relief must be determined at their proper scope.

### I-B. Protected subject matter alone creates no unrestricted employee resource right

**Holding and operative rule:** The lawful or constitutionally protected character of material, its usefulness to an employee's job, and delegated professional discretion do not alone give every state employee a personal right to choose its use through government equipment free of approval. The reversal of the district court's wholesale declaration of invalidity remains in force, without sustaining every possible application to nonacademic employees or resolving the distinct individual scholarly claim.

**Authority:** Part I has all nine direct joins, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring requires identification of the employee's personal interest before balancing and permits lawful institutional control of an assigned instructional production. An employer ordinarily defines its prescribed work, allocates equipment and prevents misuse. The message and task matter; the computer's ownership is not a complete answer to an independently protected personal claim. The professors expressly disclaim personal recreational use, which is outside review.

The district court addressed the earlier, broader content definition and work-related activity across state employment. That reasoning cannot establish the amended law's substantial unconstitutional reach or support the same wholesale decree on these six faculty claims. NTEU preserves the distinction between a supported personal right and relief for unrepresented employees. This ruling does not adopt a rule that one lawful application defeats every First Amendment overbreadth challenge. Genuine institutional decisions remain subject to independent constitutional and other applicable limits, and the individual university question requires its own answer.

### II. Independent scholarly expression requires its own protection and burden inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Independent university inquiry, teaching or communication on matters of public concern may carry a personal First Amendment interest according to its function and context; salary, connection to duties and use of public equipment do not alone make it the institution's prescribed message. Once that interest is established, Pickering and NTEU require the State to justify the actual prospective approval burden, including its effect on scholars and audiences, with a commensurate connection to legitimate operational needs; this rule grants no personal control over curricula, assignments, budgets, professional standards or equipment allocation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: six direct joins. The five Associates also join Part III's limited remand. Stone-Zsela joins this rule but concludes that the present record already establishes a violation requiring relief; his conclusion is not part of the Court's application or mandate. O'Connor assumes an interest without joining this affirmative rule; Scalia and Thomas reject the claimed entitlement.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring decided an assigned secondary-school production and expressly left university academic freedom and a universal duty-speech rule open. It cannot make every scholarly undertaking institutional expression. Keyishian protected individual educators and a library employee against the challenged speech and membership restrictions; its concern with official orthodoxy is not exclusively an institution's right. Sweezy's plurality and separate concurrence support attention to scholarly freedom on different grounds, not a single comprehensive academic-freedom test. Neither decision grants ownership of public research resources.

Pickering and Connick require contextual protection and employment justification. NTEU gives the actual prospective burden on speakers and audiences weight; it does not automatically classify every work activity as personal expression. Givhan confirms that location and private communication do not alone defeat protection. Waters preserves genuine lawful employer grounds but supplies no universal investigation procedure or answer to this statutory condition.

The inquiry must account for the amended content definition, required-use limitation, both approval branches, written approval, public availability and actual administration. Efficiency, resource management and preventing harassment are legitimate interests. The State may use operational evidence and supported predictive judgments; completed harassment, expert studies, automatic strict scrutiny or proof of actual disruption in every case is not required. Conversely, ownership and the availability of approval do not by themselves answer the burden of obtaining public advance permission for protected exploratory inquiry. Delegation to deans or department heads is relevant practice, not an enacted guarantee of universal peer review. Scholarly labels, underinclusiveness and a sexual subject alone establish neither protection nor invalidity. The standard preserves genuine academic governance and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions.

### III. Apply the amended condition to the represented scholarly claims before directing relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is vacated insofar as it finally rejects the individual academic claim, and the case is remanded to identify continuing covered personal scholarly activity and apply the stated protection, burden and justification rules. Any declaration or injunction must redress an established violation and extend no further than the represented rights and effective relief require; the Court directs no present injunction, access approval, damages, special hearing or new discovery.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: five direct joins to the whole application and remedy. Stone-Zsela would reverse and direct confined relief now; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the academic disposition. The five-Justice holding requires no combination of opposing rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court's categorical employee-role and exclusively institutional academic-freedom rules bypassed the necessary inquiry. The district court's broader statutory definition and decree do not supply its answer. The existing record contains specific scholarly burdens and Commonwealth evidence. Five reported misuse incidents and complaints support preventive concerns without establishing five harassment judgments or criminal guilt. Two incidents preceded the Act and were reported as addressed under existing policies; that does not prove those policies prevent every future harm. Smith's asserted scholarly purpose and the complaints remain distinct from a statutory refusal.

Prompt approvals with minimal paperwork and blanket permissions support the contention that the condition is modest. They are examples, not statutory deadlines, assurances that every request will succeed or elimination of the written public-approval burden. A research connection does not replace the statutory requirement that access be required for an approved project or other undertaking. The lower courts must assess the actual protected scope and fit under the amended law, using the existing submissions and ordinary procedural rules for any properly authorized further proceedings.

This remand is not based on an evidentiary vacuum or unrecovered original exhibits. It presumes neither that plaintiffs must win nor that every approval system fails. No improper motive, unlawful refusal or unrestricted access entitlement is found. The remaining work applies the stated rule to activity, coverage, burden and remedy; it does not postpone the governing legal standard. No isolated invalidation of the public-records requirement or decision about an unpresented project follows.

## Precedent Treatment

- Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education: applies its antecedent-personal-interest requirement and lawful institutional-output rule; resolves part of the university question it reserved without converting it into a universal duty-speech rule or overruling its assigned-production holding.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applies its requirement of justification commensurate with an actual prospective burden on protected expression and audiences after identifying the personal interest; preserves the independent limits on relief for unrepresented persons and imports no blanket class remedy.
- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563: applies the employment balance to identified protected scholarly activity and the actual condition.
- Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138: preserves contextual public-concern and operational analysis; no occupational label resolves it.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District, 439 U.S. 410: preserves protection despite private communication or workplace location, subject to the actual employment inquiry.
- Waters v. Churchill: preserves its limited majority rules concerning genuine grounds and mistaken accounts; extends neither its plurality's full investigative framework nor any automatic employer immunity to this condition.
- Keyishian v. Board of Regents, 385 U.S. 589: applies its protection of individuals against the challenged orthodoxy, speech-vagueness and membership restrictions as support for personal scholarly freedom; distinguishes its specific defects from a complete entitlement to public resources.
- Sweezy v. New Hampshire, 354 U.S. 234: preserves the plurality's authorization/due-process ground and Frankfurter and Harlan's distinct justification ground; draws support for scholarly freedom without combining them into a new majority test.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves its particular damages-immunity holding and reservation of the underlying constitutional extension; it supplies no adverse merits rule for this prospective academic claim. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The State may control its prescribed institutional work without giving every employee a constitutional choice of materials or equipment use. Independent university expression may nevertheless carry a personal First Amendment interest that requires the actual prospective approval burden to be justified under the stated contextual rule. Six Justices adopt that bounded protection and standard; five directly require application on remand before relief. The mandatory condition may be challenged without a prior denial, but no particular refusal, universal faculty exemption, agency-wide injunction or personal recreational-use right is adjudicated. The amended statutory qualifications and genuine institutional controls remain part of the analysis.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I and II and dissents from the academic disposition.** He recognizes an individual interest in bona fide independent university teaching and research, distinguished by its scholarly function from the State's prescribed message. Boring left that question open; Sweezy and Keyishian support protection against ideological control. Pickering and NTEU then require an adequately justified fit, and he concludes that this record already establishes the violation.

The removed indecency-law assignment, planned Victorian-poetry study, website interference and curtailed inquiry establish concrete activity and burden. The amended definition still reaches protected material studied for scholarly purposes. No denied request or statistical showing of abstract harm is needed to challenge the compulsory condition. The State's efficiency and harassment interests are legitimate, but content-based discretionary permission and public identification of research are insufficiently connected to misuse or unwanted exposure of colleagues. The narrower definition, academic delegation and successful permissions do not remove that burden or justify public approval records. Ordinary professional, curriculum, budget, resource-use and anti-harassment rules remain available.

He would reverse the rejection of the individual academic claim and direct a declaration and injunction against enforcing the prior-approval and associated public-disclosure condition on the plaintiffs' bona fide independent teaching and research. The lower court should identify covered scholarly activities and frame workable terms, not reopen whether the established defect warrants relief. He would not restore the agency-wide employee decree, award damages against the official-capacity defendant, grant a personal-use right or decide a hypothetical refused project. Independent disputes over scholarly versus prescribed institutional activity retain their own facts.

**O'Connor concurs in Part I and dissents from vacatur of the academic disposition.** She assumes a qualifying scholarly interest and applies the case-specific Pickering balance reflected in her NTEU position. She does not adopt the Court's affirmative personal-interest rule or Scalia and Thomas's antecedent-entitlement rejection. Boring reserves university inquiry while preserving institutional control, and Waters recognizes genuine employer operational concerns.

The narrowed dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements, separate research and other-undertaking approval branches, internal administration, prompt permissions and blanket approvals support the actual condition. The record also shows genuine misuse concerns. Preventive regulation need not await actionable harassment or completed injuries. Written and publicly available approvals impose a real accountability burden, but that burden does not outweigh these interests on this record. Administrative delegation is relevant without becoming a statutory universal peer-review requirement; successful approvals are relevant without guaranteeing all future requests. She would affirm the faculty disposition without denying every personal academic interest, sustaining a future discriminatory refusal or adopting a universal rule permitting advance restrictions.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in Part I and dissents from the academic vacatur on two independently sufficient grounds.** First, scholarly value and delegated discretion do not establish the claimed personal entitlement to these publicly supplied research means. Boring's university reservation is not itself an affirmative access right; neither its limited assigned-production rule nor every duty-related statement is mechanically controlling. Keyishian's independent restrictions on orthodoxy and association survive, while Sweezy's separate rationales establish no general faculty authority over public resources. The present limited equipment condition does not itself prescribe an academic conclusion or association.

Second, even assuming a personal interest, the condition survives the employment balance. Their NTEU position gives weight to preventive and administrable restrictions; Waters preserves genuine permissible employer purposes without a free-standing investigative right. The narrower covered category, reported misuse and institutional approval of required research or other undertakings provide adequate protection and accommodation. The written public-approval burden does not demand completed misconduct or expert prediction before prevention is permissible. They would affirm on either ground, preserve independent constitutional prohibitions and decide no future refused project. O'Connor's agreement in result supplies no join to their antecedent-interest ground or to this entire writing.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for ordinary adjudication under the stated standard, using the existing record and any further proceedings properly authorized by ordinary rules. The lower courts must identify continuing activities covered by the amended statute, their personal scholarly character, the actual approval burden and justified operational fit. Relief may follow an established violation and must remain confined to the represented rights and effective remedy. No present injunction, access approval, damages, special hearing or new discovery is directed. The broad district decree remains reversed; particular refused projects and personal recreational use are not decided.
