## Controlling holdings and authority

### Title VI's bounded direct prospective action, recognized in Powell v. Ridge, is available to enforce the Department of Justice section 602 effects regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** Persons actually subjected to a prohibited national-origin effect in a covered federally assisted program may seek prospective compliance with the Department of Justice regulation through Title VI's bounded direct equitable action. Under Powell v. Ridge, a person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action exists within Title VI's particular statutory context; it does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. The existence of the private action is the granted question, and Powell answers it for the claimants. The rule applies to every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within Powell's bounded prospective scope; it is not an exception for English-language testing.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this rule, its explanation, the treatment of Powell, and the application and remedy below. These are five direct joins at the stated level of generality. Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judgment, which is unanimous; the four join no part of the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The action rests on Title VI's statutory context, not on the regulation's own force. Section 601 identifies the protected beneficiaries, and section 602 authorizes implementation of their protection. Cannon recognized private judicial enforcement of the scheme. Guardians' distinction between prospective compliance and retrospective compensation carries decisive remedial weight. Congress legislated, in the 1986 remedies provision and the Civil Rights Restoration Act, where private Title VI enforcement and effects regulations were familiar. Section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures govern the agencies' own powers; they are not the exclusive route to prospective compliance. The claimant must establish what Powell requires. Statistics alone are not conclusive, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. Remedies are confined to declaration, an injunction ending or correcting the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and proportionate supervision. McClellan's contextual, statute-specific inquiry is the one Powell applied. McClellan rejected a new Cable Act damages action in a statute without Title VI's received enforcement background. Franklin governs relief once a valid action exists and supplies no damages for effects alone.

Guardians' distinction between prospective compliance and retrospective compensation carries decisive remedial weight. Congress legislated, in the 1986 remedies provision and the Civil Rights Restoration Act, where private Title VI enforcement and effects regulations were familiar. Section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures govern the agencies' own powers; they are not the exclusive route to prospective compliance. The claimant must establish what Powell requires. Statistics alone are not conclusive, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. Remedies are confined to declaration, an injunction ending or correcting the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and proportionate supervision. McClellan's contextual, statute-specific inquiry is the one Powell applied. McClellan rejected a new Cable Act damages action in a statute without Title VI's received enforcement background. Franklin governs relief once a valid action exists and supplies no damages for effects alone.

### Powell v. Ridge controls and is applied

**Holding and operative rule:** Powell's direct-action holding decides this case and is applied without change. Nothing distinguishes it: not a different agency's regulation, not a different state policy, and not a district-court finding rather than a pleaded allegation of prohibited effects. Powell's separate section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join this treatment of Powell and its statutory stare-decisis explanation. The four Justices concurring in the judgment apply Powell's direct-action holding as controlling statutory precedent in Justice O'Connor's opinion; they do not join the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Petitioners argue that section 602 addresses only agencies, that a valid implementing obligation does not show congressional authorization of a private action, and that an agency's wording cannot create one. Powell considered and answered that argument on prospective relief against state officials last Term. Statutory precedent carries special force because Congress can alter a construction it disagrees with. Congress has not amended Title VI, and no intervening decision undermines Powell. The Court applies Powell at its actual scope and extends it to no statute with a materially different enforcement scheme.

### Application and remedy

The Department receives federal assistance. The regulation's validity and the lower courts' findings of actual discriminatory effects were not independently presented; they are not transformed into newly adjudicated Supreme Court findings. The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the granted private-action question, and its underlying prospective judgment is left undisturbed. The injunction requiring accommodation of non-English-speaking applicants in driver's-license examinations is not vacated for absence of a private cause of action. It remains subject to ordinary lawful modification and to independent review of issues outside the grant.

The Court finds no discriminatory purpose and infers none from the effects finding. The section 601 intentional-discrimination action keeps its intent requirement, the authority and validity limits on section 602 regulations remain, and federal agencies retain the enforcement Congress authorized, subject to section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Powell v. Ridge, April 3, 2000:** Applied and controlling. Its direct-action holding decides this case: the bounded private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid section 602 effects regulation. It is applied at its actual scope, to every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation, and is not distinguished by a different regulation, policy or proved effects. Its separate section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** Preserved at the scope Powell states; applied in its Title VI-derived context, not as universal implied-action authority.
- **Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission:** Preserved as fractured precedent at the scope Powell states. Its distinction between prospective compliance and retrospective compensation informs the remedial bounds; no disputed plurality proposition is treated as an earlier majority holding.
- **Alexander v. Choate:** Preserved; the statutory prohibition and the effects regulations remain distinct standards.
- **Section 601 intentional-discrimination cause of action:** Preserved, with its intent requirement.
- **Authority and validity limits on section 602 regulations:** Preserved. The validity of the regulation here was not presented and is not decided.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992:** Remedies presuppose a valid cause of action. Franklin governs relief within the Powell action and supplies no damages for effects alone.
- **McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc., January 7, 1999:** Stands as decided. Its contextual, statute-specific authorization inquiry is the one Powell applied; its rejection of a new Cable Act damages action is distinguished, not displaced.
- **Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., April 19, 1994:** Stands as decided; its confined received securities action is intact.
- **Blessing v. Freestone, April 21, 1997; Wright v. Roanoke; Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association:** Preserved. Section 1983 enforcement is not presented, and Powell's section 1983 holding is consistent with them.
- **Gebser and Davis:** Preserved at the scope Powell states, without importing their mental-state requirements into effects regulations.

## Law After Decision

Effective April 24, 2001, persons actually subjected to a prohibited national-origin effect in a covered federally assisted program may seek prospective compliance with the Department of Justice section 602 regulation through Title VI's bounded direct equitable action, as Powell v. Ridge held. Powell's direct-action holding applies to every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within its bounded prospective scope; it is not an English-language-testing exception, and it does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action.

The action rests on Title VI's statutory context, requires what Powell requires and supplies declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief, not damages for unintended disparate impact, retrospective compensation without an independent legal source or a private order terminating or withholding federal grants. Powell's separate section 1983 holding remains controlling at its own scope. The validity of the regulation here and the effects findings were not presented and are not decided. Section 601 intentional-discrimination actions, the authority and validity limits on section 602 regulations, and agency enforcement under section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures remain intact. McClellan and Central Bank stand as decided.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judgment. Powell v. Ridge decided this statutory question last Term. Over the opinion she wrote for the four, it recognized a bounded direct action for declaratory and prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid section 602 effects regulation. No supported change warrants reversing the same statutory judgment after one year. Congress has not amended Title VI, and no intervening decision undermines Powell. Neither a different agency's regulation, Alabama's English-only examination policy nor a district-court finding rather than a pleaded allegation of effects changes the statutory authorization question Powell resolved. The four therefore apply Powell's direct-action holding as controlling statutory precedent and concur in the affirmance.

They do not join the Court's opinion, which states as its own explanation the account of congressional authorization their Powell opinion rejected. They keep that view as their own but do not ask the Court to reconsider Powell. They apply Powell at its actual scope: prospective compliance relief only, with relief kept distinct from the antecedent question whether an action exists. They adopt no universal express-action-clause requirement, decide no question of regulatory validity, and leave McClellan's contextual inquiry and Central Bank's received securities action as decided.

No other Justice writes separately, and there is no dissent.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the granted private-action question, and the accommodation injunction is not vacated for absence of a private cause of action; it remains subject to ordinary lawful modification or independent review of issues outside the grant. The Court retains no return condition and directs no remand to relitigate the private action, new factual inquiry, damages proceeding or agency enforcement action.
