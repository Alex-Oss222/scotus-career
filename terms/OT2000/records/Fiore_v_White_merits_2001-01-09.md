## Holdings

### I. The existing missing element defeats both challenged convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not sustain a conviction when its authoritative account of the law in force at the relevant time identifies a necessary element and the State concedes it produced no evidence of that element. Pennsylvania's clarification that actual absence of the required permit was always an element of the owner/operator offense therefore establishes a due-process violation in both of Fiore's challenged no-permit convictions; deviation from a permit's terms cannot substitute for its absence.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both holdings and the bounded reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** In re Winship requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every element. Jackson v. Virginia enforces that protection against a conviction unsupported on a necessary element. State law identifies the offense; the federal Constitution requires its elements to be proved. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's August 21, 2000 answer establishes that Commonwealth v. Scarpone clarified the statute's existing meaning, including its meaning when Fiore's convictions became final. The Commonwealth does not dispute that Fiore possessed a permit. The asserted departure from its conditions thus cannot establish the different no-permit element.

The Third Circuit's premise that a new state rule was unavailable on collateral review cannot survive the authoritative answer. The Court decides no general obligation to apply genuinely new state constructions retroactively: there is no new construction here. Nor can the seriousness of other environmental misconduct supply the missing element. The distinct authorization, transportation-license and permit-compliance provisions do not become substitute offenses in this dispute. The ruling covers the no-permit conviction from each trial, not the other convictions among Fiore's sixty convictions.

### II. The constitutional ruling and the conditions for the writ remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's rejection of the constitutional claim is reversed and the case remanded to resolve any properly preserved, actually undecided statutory prerequisite to habeas relief under the applicable post-April 24, 1996 regime. If those prerequisites permit relief, the habeas order must remove both invalid no-permit convictions and end custody attributable to them, preserving independent convictions and lawful custody.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion directly adopts this remand and the following statutory conditions; the Court makes no finding that an unresolved gate is satisfied or violated.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh v. Murphy distinguishes applications filed before enactment from later applications; Fiore's later filing is not exempt from the applicable amendments. Under section 2254(b), relief requires exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Merits denial despite nonexhaustion is permitted, not required; the State waives exhaustion only through counsel's express waiver. The appellate court expressly left exhaustion and state merits adjudication undecided.

If the relevant claim was actually adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, section 2254(d) requires the statutory inquiry. Williams v. Taylor preserves the independent contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses, the objective legal standard and the restriction to this Court's holdings at the actual state merits decision. Winship and Jackson are preexisting rules, not rules originating in today's decision. The separate statutory factual-review provisions retain their own triggers and force. A finding of constitutional error cannot silently resolve those questions; conversely, remand supplies no permission to reopen the conceded permit fact, the state court's authoritative answer or the decided constitutional issue. The court below must resolve a real preserved prerequisite and implement the resulting conviction-specific relief, rather than infer an unconditional all-count writ.
