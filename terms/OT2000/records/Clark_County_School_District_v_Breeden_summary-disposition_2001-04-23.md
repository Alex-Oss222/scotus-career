## Holdings

**Common authority:** The Court's per curiam opinion states both controlling grounds below. No individual lineup is published.

### 1. This report-review exchange does not support the asserted reasonable belief

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming that objectively reasonable good-faith opposition to a perceived Title VII violation is protected before a violation is proved, this particular isolated exchange during assigned review of an applicant's psychological report cannot support the asserted reasonable belief that an unlawful hostile environment existed. This is a contextual application, not a rule excluding every single incident or requiring employees to wait for completed harassment before raising a reasonable concern.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exchange must be evaluated in its actual work setting. Harris asks whether the conditions are objectively and subjectively hostile, considering their severity, frequency, threatening or humiliating character and interference with work. Psychological injury and lost productivity are not prerequisites. An extremely serious isolated act can qualify; the ordinary rule is severity or pervasiveness, not a mandatory number of incidents.

The employees were performing assigned review of an applicant's report. Breeden did not object to reading the reported remark itself. Her objection concerns the supervisor's response, a coworker's offer to explain it and their laughter. In that context the incident was neither sufficiently serious nor part of an established continuing abusive condition to support the asserted objectively reasonable belief. Subjective offense alone does not supply the objective threshold.

An employee may reasonably complain about a developing unlawful practice without waiting for repeated mistreatment. That principle does not supply a supported pattern, threatened continuation or extreme act absent from this record. Nor does a supervisor's participation answer the antecedent environment question. Faragher's actual-authority rule concerns attribution of an established wrong; it does not turn every supervisory remark into one. The Court assumes the favorable reasonable-belief protection and does not adopt a broader limitation on opposition or participation whenever the underlying claim fails.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The general outer limits of reasonable-belief opposition are unnecessary to this judgment and remain undecided. No separate hostile-environment liability claim or new exhaustion requirement is adjudicated.

### 2. The supplied chronology does not establish retaliatory transfer

**Holding and operative rule:** Implementation of a transfer already contemplated before the relevant decisionmaker learned of suit does not, by timing alone, support retaliation where no evidence shows a retaliatory change or acceleration; the twenty-month interval from the earlier administrative charge likewise supplies no sufficient causal inference by itself here. A preexisting plan is not conclusive immunity, and a long interval is not an absolute bar when other evidence supports causation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the timing must be measured against actual knowledge and the prior plan. Rice contemplated transfer on April 10, before service on April 11, and her unchallenged affidavit states that she learned of the action only after April 11. Completing the transfer in May does not by itself show that the suit caused the already contemplated step. The district-court presentation relied entirely on chronology; it supplied no supported retaliatory alteration, admission or other causal evidence. The August 1995 charge, approximately twenty months earlier, does not fill that gap.

The right-to-sue letter theory appeared only in the appellate reply, not below or in the opening brief. It does not establish Rice's knowledge of the letter or a new protected employee act. The requirement that the employer receive notice of a charge within ten days is a legal command, not proof that Rice or her predecessor actually received it. The Court finds neither receipt nor nonreceipt from that command. Speculation about the letter therefore cannot sustain reversal of summary judgment.

Testimony, explanations, admissions and circumstantial evidence can support a retaliation claim; statistical proof is unnecessary. An initially legitimate action can later be implemented for retaliatory reasons. Those possibilities lack evidentiary support here, and the mere sequence of protected proceedings and completed transfer cannot replace the missing causal link. The Court assumes the formal administrative and litigation activity is protected and adopts no new comprehensive causation formula.

**Limits and matters not reached:** No universal temporal cutoff, automatic notice presumption, categorical prior-plan defense or later retaliation standard is adopted. A materially different supported implementation theory would require facts absent here; none is invented for remand.

Those possibilities lack evidentiary support here, and the mere sequence of protected proceedings and completed transfer cannot replace the missing causal link. The Court assumes the formal administrative and litigation activity is protected and adopts no new comprehensive causation formula.

**Limits and matters not reached:** No universal temporal cutoff, automatic notice presumption, categorical prior-plan defense or later retaliation standard is adopted. A materially different supported implementation theory would require facts absent here; none is invented for remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc.:** Applied contextually to objective and subjective hostility, severity or pervasiveness and the rejection of mandatory psychological injury. The decision does not require repeated incidents regardless of seriousness or completed liability before every reasonable complaint.
- **Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.:** Preserved for attention to social context and the distinction between actionable discrimination and ordinary offensive behavior; no new restriction on protected complaints follows.
- **Faragher v. City of Boca Raton:** Its actual-authority/material-aid attribution rule remains effective for an established wrong. Supervisory status does not supply a missing hostile environment or retaliation link, and no different affirmative defense is imported.

## Law After Decision

Both presented retaliation theories fail on their actual records. Reasonable opposition is assumed protected without requiring ultimate success on the underlying claim; this isolated exchange supplies no objectively reasonable basis of the asserted kind. Transfer timing alone supplies no retaliation inference in light of the prior contemplation and knowledge evidence. Contextual hostility, actual attribution and causal proof remain separate; no complaint-count formula, universal temporal cutoff or conclusive prior-plan defense replaces them.
