**Holding and operative rule:** A recent face-to-face eyewitness report may materially strengthen the reliability of information that a person possesses a gun, but reliability about possession is distinct from a supported inference of criminal activity. ## Event

United States v. Valentine, Third Circuit No. 00-1425; judgment below reported at 232 F.3d 350. Merits submission and decision, May 14, 2001. On review of the Third Circuit's reversal of suppression and remand of the federal firearm prosecution. The questions concern individualized suspicion from the reported gun possession and encounter, including the preserved permitting argument, and whether actual submission can occur during a brief interval before later flight.

At about 1 a.m. on May 8, 1999, a man flagged down two uniformed officers and reported recently seeing a nearby man carrying a gun. He gave a detailed description but refused to identify himself. Officers saw Valentine matching the description nearby, in an area described as having many shootings. The men began walking away. The officer's account describes a command to come over and place hands on the car, Valentine's inquiry about whom it concerned, a charge toward the officer and an attempt to push past his arms, followed by a grab and struggle. The gun was discovered only then.

Valentine asserted an earlier stop and name. The appellate court reported no evidentiary support and a police report apparently inconsistent with that theory, but expressly declined factual determination because it regarded even the assumed account as insufficient submission. The district court's cutoff at the command was a legal ruling, not a finding that Valentine yielded.
