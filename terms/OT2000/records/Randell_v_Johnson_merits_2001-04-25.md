## Controlling holdings

### I. The precise damages claim must necessarily contradict an operative determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Favorable invalidation is not required for a section 1983 claim whose success is compatible with the valid sentence or custody determination, including a claim that officials executed a valid sentence contrary to its terms. Before imposing the prerequisite, the court must identify the operative judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and determine whether success on the actual civil theory necessarily establishes its invalidity; a sentence-administration label neither establishes nor avoids that consequence.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by all eight Associate Justices. All nine directly adopt this classification rule, rejection of the categorical screening ground, and vacatur and remand for the necessary comparison.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the legal consequence of the claim matters more than its label or its connection to custody. Heck distinguishes a necessarily conviction-negating claim from an independent injury compatible with the criminal judgment. Edwards applies that distinction to earned-credit proceedings: calling a claim procedural does not save it if success necessarily defeats an outstanding adjudication, but a genuinely independent injury requires its own analysis. Wolff preserves compatible civil relief, and Preiser's release channel does not turn every damages complaint concerning confinement into habeas.

Randell alleges that officials failed to credit parole-warrant confinement and required him to serve the same time again. That allegation could seek enforcement of a valid sentence, or it could necessarily contradict an operative credit determination. The screening record does not sufficiently identify the determination or establish that relationship. A court cannot assume every alleged excess day negates a judgment, nor assume every administrative-execution claim leaves the controlling custody determination untouched.

The lower court also treated Heck as already deciding a universal former-prisoner bar. The actual rule reserved that population and persons without realistic custody-review opportunity; Spencer preserved those reservations without adopting an affirmative civil exception. Existing precedent therefore did not supply the categorical ground used below. This error requires vacatur, not a finding that Randell was overconfined or already possesses a successful damages claim.

### II. Genuine loss of any realistic custody-review opportunity permits the civil claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When a former prisoner's damages claim necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination, prior favorable invalidation is not required if the claimant never had a realistic lawful opportunity to contest that confinement before custody review became unavailable, or lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. A claimant who lost a genuinely usable opportunity through an unexcused failure remains subject to the prerequisite; release or present federal habeas unavailability alone is neither the exception nor qualifying invalidation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Five directly adopt this new bounded rule within Heck's reservation, its statutory explanation and its unexcused-loss limit. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule coordinating available statutory remedies cannot require a route the claimant never realistically could use. Heck's controlling statutory explanation reconciles section 1983 with a lawful custody-review channel. Preiser supports that coordination where the remedies intersect. Patsy rejects a general requirement to exhaust state procedures before seeking section 1983 relief. The common-law favorable-termination analogy helps implement the statutory arrangement; it does not create an absolute civil disability whenever confinement once occurred.

The defendants correctly object that release cannot reward strategic delay or authorize a person to bypass a real review opportunity. The rule therefore turns on realistic availability and the circumstances of its loss, not custody status alone. An unexcused failure to use a genuine opportunity leaves the prerequisite in place. The exception concerns meaningful first review, not an entitlement to repeat unsuccessful review, and expiration does not invalidate the underlying determination.

The inquiry must use supported facts about custody, notice, court access, timing and the ability to pursue the particular challenge. Neither absence of an earlier successful writ nor the theoretical existence of another procedure proves what opportunity actually existed. An authorized qualifying invalidation route may matter where the prerequisite applies, but requiring proof that every conceivable state process is unavailable would recreate the general exhaustion rule Patsy rejects.

Spencer did not already grant this exception; it decided the live controversy in a habeas case and expressly preserved the civil question. This decision resolves the legal consequences of genuine unavailability and unexcused loss. Randell's actual circumstances remain for ordinary adjudication under that rule, without an assumed finding of diligence, abandonment or deliberate delay.

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit's screening affirmance is vacated and the case remanded to compare the pleaded credit theory with the actual custody determination and, if necessary invalidity is established, decide realistic review opportunity and legally excused loss from supported facts. A compatible claim or one within the no-realistic-review exception may proceed under ordinary civil requirements; an otherwise covered claim lacking qualifying invalidation must be dismissed without prejudice for lack of present cognizability, not as an adjudication that the alleged misconduct did not occur.

Justice Scalia concurs in Part I and in the judgment, joined by Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. They agree that the complaint must be compared with the actual operative custody determination, that an independent execution injury is not automatically barred, and that this screening record cannot sustain categorical dismissal. Scalia's Edwards analysis requires the actual legal consequence, not a procedural label or assumption that every excess day contradicts the judicial sentence.

They retain the common-law-informed ground stated in their Heck concurrence: a damages claim necessarily contradicting an outstanding criminal or custody adjudication ordinarily requires invalidation even after release. Present habeas unavailability is not itself favorable termination. They regard the contradiction of an extant adjudication as distinct from general state-remedy exhaustion, and a remedial gap alone does not remove that concern.

The strongest objection is a claimant whose custody was too short or whose injury became knowable too late to obtain meaningful review. These four do not adopt the Court's affirmative exception on this undeveloped record; they would first resolve the actual determination and necessary-invalidity predicates and reserve a fully established genuinely impossible-review case. Their position does not establish deliberate delay, adequate state review, prior adjudication or actual unlawfulness here. They would remand for classification, preserve ordinary preclusion and defenses only when their predicates exist, and require no impossible universal showing about every possible state procedure. They join neither Parts II–III's new exception nor the conditional directions implementing it.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit for comparison of the civil claim with the actual sentence and custody determination. If the claim is compatible, ordinary civil adjudication may proceed without favorable invalidation. If it necessarily establishes invalidity, the court must determine realistic review opportunity and legally excused loss under the announced exception; an otherwise covered claim without qualifying invalidation is dismissed without prejudice. Ordinary screening, responsibility, causation, injury, immunity, limitations and supported preclusion defenses remain. No habeas conversion, state-court reopening, hearing, counsel appointment, custody recalculation, stay or damages amount is directed, and no Supreme Court return condition is retained.
