## Controlling holdings

### I. Timely review of the later judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A timely petition from the later judgment in the same litigation permits review of an antecedent ruling necessary to that judgment. Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe supply this rule; an earlier appellate mandate binds the district court, but does not immunize the predicate ruling from this Court's proper later review. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this threshold ground in Part I of the per curiam opinion. Stevens does not join it.

### II. The court may not replace submitted factfinding

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may not replace the credibility and factual assessment of a labor arbitrator who is arguably applying the agreed framework within the accepted submission merely because it regards the assessment as seriously mistaken. The court must identify a departure from contractual authority or another recognized enforcement defect; this arbitrator applied the submitted offer-and-withdrawal criterion, so the original award must remain effective.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this review rule and actual award-restoration ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Misco protects contract application and factfinding within the submitted task; Enterprise Wheel preserves the boundary against an award untethered to the agreement. Those rules require examination of what the arbitrator did, not judicial substitution of the preferred factual answer. Here the arbitrator asked whether an actual extension offer existed and was withdrawn through collusion, sought corroboration, and evaluated inconsistent accounts. The court of appeals disagreed with his treatment of Smith's later letter because earlier collusion proceedings had rejected club testimony. But a general finding that the clubs colluded does not prove this specific offer. Disbelief of one aggregate account does not compel acceptance of every later individual assertion.

The Court does not independently endorse the arbitrator's factual accuracy or find that a witness lied. The legal defect is the appellate replacement of the chosen decisionmaker while he applied the agreed criterion. First Options protects antecedent assent and does not create a factual appeal within a conceded submission. Eastern Associated Coal retains the law-grounded limits on labor-award enforcement, and Wright preserves independent statutory claims; neither changes this contractual claim into a new judicial merits inquiry. No corruption, dishonesty or other independently sufficient enforcement defect is established in the described record.

### III. Vacatur does not transfer disputed contractual merits to the court

**Holding and operative rule:** Even when a labor award is properly vacated, a reviewing court ordinarily may not resolve disputed contractual entitlement and order its selected monetary result instead of respecting the agreement's remaining allocation of authority. Further arbitral proceedings depend on what the agreement permits; this rule neither orders another hearing in every case nor creates authority absent from the agreement.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Ginsburg withholds this additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Misco's remedial discussion and American Manufacturing preserve the contractual tribunal's role after a court identifies a valid enforcement defect. A court cannot use vacatur as authority to adjudicate the disputed merits and command the sum it considers inevitable. Garvey's assertion that only one factual result is possible does not itself transfer the submitted task to the reviewing court. This additional ground independently defeats the directed award; because vacatur was improper here, the operative mandate restores the denial rather than requiring a hypothetical second arbitration.

## Event

Major League Baseball Players Association v. Garvey, No. 00-1210. Certiorari grant and summary merits disposition, May 14, 2001. On the petition for review of the Ninth Circuit's December 7, 2000 judgment directing return to arbitration with instructions to award Garvey approximately $3 million. The questions concern review of the arbitrator's credibility assessment and the court's power to direct contractual recovery. A timely challenge to the later judgment also presents the antecedent vacatur ruling. The matter is decided on the petition record without a separately scheduled argued merits hearing.

The accepted LMRA dispute concerns the Framework distributing a $280 million settlement of club collusion claims for the 1985–1987 free-agent markets. A lost-extension claim required a specific offer later withdrawn because of collusion. After the Association denied Garvey's claim, he submitted his testimony and a 1996 letter from former club president Ballard Smith supporting an extension for 1988–1989. The arbitrator requested corroborating documents and rejected the claim, identifying inconsistent accounts and other record concerns. The first appellate panel displaced that assessment; the later panel required the claimed award.
