## Holdings

### I. The association's actual public operation makes this regulation governmental

**Holding and operative rule:** An athletic association's challenged regulatory activity is state action when public-school officials, acting in their official educational capacity, substantially govern and operate it as the common instrument for administering their schools' athletic programs. The demonstrated official operation here establishes governmental responsibility despite private incorporation, voluntary membership and private-school participation; membership percentages, funding or public benefit alone would not suffice.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six Justices directly adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas oppose the attribution ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron prevents government from avoiding constitutional responsibility through a corporate form. Its special-creation and permanent-majority-appointment combination is sufficient, not an exclusive checklist. American Manufacturers requires actual governmental responsibility and protects an independent private carrier's choice; it does not make an institution private when public officials actually operate the challenged function. Tarkanian's national association differs from the collective action of schools within one State. Its discussion of that distinction supports the comparison without itself deciding this case.

About 84 percent of the members are public schools. At the time of the penalties every voting governor was a public-school administrator, selected from principals, assistant principals and superintendents. Their governance of eligibility and competition, official school roles and meetings ordinarily during school hours establish collective public administration. Their official capacity in this work is the Court's legal inference from the established structure and function, not an undisputed lower-court conclusion. No factual remand is needed merely to recount that structure.

Private membership and voluntary selection do not turn this common administration of school athletics into an independent private choice. Public control need not be permanently guaranteed by the voting rules for this actual operation to be governmental. No separate state command to impose the recruiting penalty is required. Gate receipts supply the principal revenue and dues about four percent; staff are not state-paid, retirement eligibility includes no state-paid employer contribution, and State Board representatives do not vote. Those limited contacts are not substitutes for the governance ground. The 1996 recognition and voluntary-membership language replaced the earlier express designation. The district court's finding of unchanged material operation does not erase that amendment; the actual operation nevertheless supplies the necessary connection.

### II. Attribution returns the preserved rights and relief questions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's no-state-action judgment is reversed and the case remanded with the association's challenged athletic regulation treated as governmental. The lower courts must adjudicate preserved constitutional merits and lawful relief; attribution alone neither invalidates the recruiting rule or penalties nor reinstates the district injunction or establishes damages.

**Authority:** The six-Justice coalition identified above directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron separates actor status from violation and remedy. The Sixth Circuit reversed the district court's summary judgment and injunction on attribution rather than resolving all the constitutional grounds. Correcting that premise returns the unreviewed questions under their own rules. The challenged action consists of four years of athletic-program probation, two years of exclusion from football and boys' basketball playoffs, and a $3,000 fine, imposed after recruiting communications about spring football practice. The Court does not decide whether that conduct was protected speech, whether the rule or procedure violated a constitutional right, or what relief any proved violation warrants. Those questions are distinct from the presented attribution issue and remain for ordinary lower-court adjudication; no further evidentiary hearing, damages award or particular injunction is directed.
