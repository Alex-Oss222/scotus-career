# Florida v. Thomas

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Florida v. Thomas. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-391; 532 U.S. 774. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Supreme Court of Florida's judgment. **Simulated Event Date:** June 4, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether New York v. Belton's vehicle-search rule is limited to an arrest in which the officer initiates contact while the arrestee remains inside the vehicle. A separate antecedent question is whether the state judgment is final under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a).

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the granted search question. Federal finality is an antecedent jurisdictional question. Exact petition wording has not been independently verified.

**Material Record:** Police investigating marijuana sales at a home encountered Robert Thomas after he drove into the driveway, parked, and walked to the rear of his vehicle. A license check disclosed an outstanding arrest warrant. An officer arrested and handcuffed him, took him inside the home, then returned alone and searched the car, finding methamphetamine. The criminal charges included possession and related narcotics offenses. [Florida v. Thomas](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-774.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The trial court suppressed the narcotics and paraphernalia. Florida's Second District reversed under New York v. Belton. The State Supreme Court rejected New York v. Belton's application because police contact began after Thomas left the car, but remanded for findings and a determination under Chimel v. California. The record did not permit that court to determine whether officer safety or preservation of evidence justified the search. **Relief Requested:** Florida seeks reversal of the contact-inside limitation. Thomas seeks to preserve the state court's ruling and remand.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The merits briefs did not raise federal finality, but jurisdiction cannot be established by silence or agreement. Florida has not conceded that it loses under Chimel v. California. The State can appeal a renewed pretrial suppression order under Florida's procedural law. This is not yet a conclusive order requiring a trial without the evidence. The arrestee's precise ability to reach the car and the remand findings must not be invented.

**Relevant Dates:** Argument April 25, 2001; review event June 4, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holdings in Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, Illinois v. McArthur, or Arkansas v. Sullivan must be considered for their relevance to the search issue. Their historical outcomes do not supply governing law.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal finality must be established independently of the parties' agreement or their failure to brief it. Florida's available pretrial review procedures matter to whether later federal correction remains possible.

### Issue 1: Finality of the state judgment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Jefferson v. City of Tarrant controls the §1257 issue. A state ruling that finally settles one federal legal question is not automatically a final judgment while substantive proceedings remain. Its preserved Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn routes require practical completion, an issue surviving every further outcome, unavailability of effective later review whatever the outcome, or litigation-preclusive reversal coupled with serious threatened erosion of federal policy. Importance and state law-of-the-case effect do not suffice.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The state-judgment-finality standard preserves those practical-finality routes and requires a concrete account of what proceedings remain and whether the federal issue can still receive effective review. Settlement of an interlocutory legal proposition alone is insufficient.

**Other Governing Law:** Section 1257(a) supplies jurisdiction over the relevant final judgments of a State's highest court. The relevant Florida appeal provisions permit the State to seek review of a renewed adverse pretrial suppression ruling. [Florida v. Thomas](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-774.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn performs the work preserved in Jefferson v. City of Tarrant; it does not supply a free-standing exception merely because an interlocutory federal question matters.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law governs substantive remands and the practical possibility of later review. The unresolved Chimel v. California ground and Florida pretrial appeal route must be tested against that rule.

**Open Point:** No new finality doctrine is needed. The issue is whether this judgment satisfies one of the existing routes on the actual procedural record.

### Issue 2: Search incident to arrest of a recent occupant

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Knowles v. Iowa rejects converting an unused power to arrest into a search incident to a mere citation. Whren v. United States separates objective grounds for initial enforcement from justification of later searches. Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights preserves independent scrutiny of additional custodial steps, but this case involves an outstanding warrant and presents a different asserted defect.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The corresponding search-incident and independent-intrusion standards require a valid source for the actual search, not merely lawful initial contact or an unexercised enforcement option. They do not resolve the first-contact-outside question or dispense with appellate jurisdiction.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment governs the vehicle search. New York v. Belton and Chimel v. California supply the background search-incident doctrines, subject to the independent limits described above.

**Material Other Authority:** The Florida courts' competing New York v. Belton readings are the review controversy. Neither is a controlling Supreme Court holding.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** A lawful arrest does not by itself settle every subsequent search. The existing doctrine's permitted passenger-compartment reach and ordinary safety-and-evidence rationale must remain distinct.

**Open Point:** The precise scope of New York v. Belton for a recent occupant remains open on this record, and the state remand independently leaves Chimel v. California unresolved. A federal merits answer requires appellate jurisdiction first.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The intermediate court applied New York v. Belton's categorical passenger-compartment rule. The State Supreme Court instead made the point of police contact the dividing line, with ordinary Chimel v. California analysis available afterward. Its remand therefore performs substantive work even though its New York v. Belton interpretation is settled for the ongoing state proceeding.

**State's strongest positions:** An occupant can exit before the officer has a chance to make contact, and a rule turning on that happenstance may create arbitrary distinctions and impede safe enforcement. A recent occupant's arrest may justify a contemporaneous passenger-compartment search. On jurisdiction, the State can argue that a definitive federal suppression rule ordinarily will escape review if it must proceed to trial and either obtain a conviction or face double-jeopardy barriers after acquittal.

**Thomas's strongest positions:** Search incident must remain attached to a lawful arrest and the actual justifications supplied by the doctrine. He was already outside, secured, and inside the residence when the officer returned alone. The federal Court should not extend a bright line beyond its existing basis. More immediately, the State has not yet lost the admissibility issue: it may prevail on Chimel v. California or obtain pretrial appellate review after an adverse determination.

**Government participation:** The United States appeared as amicus urging reversal on the search question. Its participation supplies no jurisdictional concession or additional record finding. A disagreement over the desirable merits rule cannot resolve the statutory finality requirement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold path:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction if the judgment fails the preserved practical-finality routes. Further factfinding prevents practical completion. The New York v. Belton issue may become unnecessary if the State prevails under Chimel v. California. Later pretrial review remains possible if it loses. Reversing the New York v. Belton ruling would determine evidence admissibility, not terminate the prosecution, and the ordinary delay inherent in suppression litigation does not establish serious erosion of a distinct federal policy.

**Plausible Holding Scope and Conditional Merits Paths:** Only after identifying a valid jurisdictional route could the Court uphold the contact-inside limitation, reject it and remand for a properly defined recent-occupant inquiry, or adopt another lawful application of search-incident doctrine. A merits reversal would not establish every remaining admissibility ground or guilt. The present remand's factual uncertainty would remain relevant to any noncategorical approach.

**Judgment consequences:** Jurisdictional dismissal neither affirms nor vacates the state merits ruling. It leaves the state proceeding to continue and permits a later petition from a reviewable judgment, subject to then-applicable requirements. No suppression mandate, new trial, conviction, exclusionary-rule exception, or damages remedy is warranted at this stage.

# Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-346; 532 U.S. 811. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Fourth Circuit's reversal of dismissal. **Simulated Event Date:** June 4, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether negligent breach of a general maritime duty of care is actionable under general maritime law when it causes death, as it is when it causes nonfatal injury.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully states the question the judicial opinion identifies; the petition's exact wording has not been independently verified.

**Material Record:** Christopher Garris was injured on April 8, 1997 while sandblasting aboard the USNS Maj. Stephen W. Pless, berthed in navigable waters, and died the next day. His employer, Tidewater Temps, subcontracted for Mid-Atlantic Coatings, which subcontracted for Norfolk. His mother, as estate administrator, alleged negligence by Norfolk and another subcontractor. Norfolk neither employed him nor owned the vessel. These are the pleaded and procedural premises of the dismissal posture, not findings that Norfolk owed or breached every alleged duty. [Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-811.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of Virginia dismissed the federal claim categorically because it recognized no general maritime negligence wrongful-death action for a nonseaman in territorial waters. The Fourth Circuit reversed and remanded, treating the principles of Moragne v. States Marine Lines as supporting the action. **Relief Requested:** Norfolk seeks reinstatement of dismissal; Garris seeks continuation of the federal claim. The separately pursued Virginia wrongful-death claim was resolved against her in state court and is no longer pursued here. This case does not reopen that judgment.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Admiralty jurisdiction was invoked under 28 U.S.C. §1333. No preserved alternative jurisdictional defect is established. The dismissal assumes a maritime duty and negligent breach for the purpose of the categorical remedy question. Liability, causation, available damage categories, and amount remain to be adjudicated; preserved defenses may be presented on remand.

**Relevant Dates:** Injury April 8, 1997; death April 9, 1997; argument April 18, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Lewis v. Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc. concerning maritime procedure must be considered if material to this negligence claim. Its historical disposition does not govern.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** The dismissal tests whether a pleaded negligence death claim exists under maritime law. It does not establish the factual elements of liability or a particular amount of recovery.

### Issue 1: Maritime wrongful death for third-party negligence

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun holds that Moragne v. States Marine Lines's maritime death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state death and survival remedies for nonseafarers in territorial waters outside an exclusive statutory regime. It expressly assumes, rather than resolves, some extensions of Moragne v. States Marine Lines and does not settle the present worker's negligence action or damages. Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co. refuses a parallel survival action where DOHSA governs and Congress has selected the compensation scheme; that rule is bounded by the Act's actual coverage.

American Dredging Co. v. Miller preserves the distinction between state forum administration and substantive maritime obligations and remedies. A procedural label cannot authorize alteration of a characteristic maritime right. Its preservation of Miles v. Apex Marine Corp. demands respect for Congress's applicable remedial choices, not universal statutory exclusivity across all maritime cases.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Maritime law distinguishes forum procedure from substantive rights and preserves the applicable congressional compensation scheme and the state remedies recognized in Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun. Those rules do not turn legislation in an adjacent maritime field into categorical displacement of every third-party negligence claim.

**Other Governing Law:** Admiralty jurisdiction rests on the constitutional and statutory grant, including §1333. The Jones Act concerns seamen; Garris's pleaded status is nonseaman. DOHSA concerns the specified high-seas area rather than this territorial-water death. The Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act supplies employer compensation and vessel-negligence rules while preserving claims against third parties. Those statutory boundaries must be examined before recognizing a maritime action; a judicial remedy cannot contradict an applicable exclusive remedy. [Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-811.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** Moragne v. States Marine Lines supplies the general maritime wrongful-death foundation. Negligence is an established maritime duty; the question is the death remedy's coverage. Moragne v. States Marine Lines's original unseaworthiness facts must not be disguised as a specific earlier decision of this negligence claim.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires respect for the actual boundaries of federal maritime remedial statutes, recognizes general maritime death remedies, and preserves state remedies in Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun's particular setting. It does not make death an automatic defense to every maritime negligence claim or authorize supplementing DOHSA contrary to Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co..

**Open Point:** No controlling decision resolves this exact third-party negligence death claim. Whether Moragne v. States Marine Lines's remedial principle extends to this established duty in a setting outside the asserted exclusive statutory regimes remains the merits question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Norfolk's strongest rule:** Congress has legislated comprehensively for maritime injuries and death. Judicial creation of a new cause of action could disturb the allocation among employer compensation, vessel liability, and state remedies. Moragne v. States Marine Lines involved unseaworthiness, and its broad wording should not be treated as a decision of every different duty. The LHWCA's elimination of some unseaworthiness actions may demonstrate deliberate remedial limits.

**Garris's strongest rule:** The action completes an existing maritime duty and death remedy in a setting Congress has not occupied exclusively. A third party should not escape a maritime negligence remedy solely because the injury proved fatal. The LHWCA's third-party savings provisions protect maritime as well as state claims; its abolition of covered unseaworthiness claims does not abolish negligence. Neither the Jones Act nor DOHSA reaches this claimant and location.

**Below and concessions:** The district court's categorical dismissal did not decide Norfolk's specific duty or breach; the appellate reversal likewise does not establish a damages entitlement. The analogy to Moragne v. States Marine Lines is direct in remedial logic but still requires candid identification of the extension from its unseaworthiness facts. The parties' treatment of the threshold issue does not concede ultimate negligence, a damage measure, or a survival claim.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Available paths:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit if an existing general maritime duty supports a death action and no applicable statute precludes it. Reverse if the relevant congressional scheme forecloses this specific remedy or if Moragne v. States Marine Lines is authoritatively limited in a way that requires denying it. A vacatur for a genuinely unresolved coverage issue would need to identify the issue and preserved factual dispute; generalized unease about common lawmaking is not itself a missing record finding.

**Plausible Holding Scope and Remedy:** Affirmance removes the categorical no-action barrier and returns the case for adjudication of preserved duty, breach, causation, and remedy questions. It does not award damages, hold that Norfolk was Garris's employer or vessel owner, or erase statutory defenses. It does not expand recovery under DOHSA, alter Jones Act seaman remedies, revive the separate state claim, or establish a universal punitive-damages or survival rule.

**Institutional boundary:** General maritime law includes a recognized judicial lawmaking role, but applicable enacted limits remain controlling. Both blanket displacement because Congress has legislated somewhere in the field and unlimited judicial supplementation would exceed what the governing decisions establish.

### Issue 1: A death remedy for an established maritime duty

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the availability of the cause of action and the asserted statutory preclusion. Those are necessary antecedents to allowing this complaint to proceed. Do not adjudicate allegations as proven facts.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a nonseaman's estate may pursue general maritime wrongful death for a third party's negligent breach of an applicable maritime duty in territorial waters when no governing statutory rule excludes that action.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing maritime remedial and statutory-boundary framework and expressly extend Moragne v. States Marine Lines's death remedy to this negligence setting. Its original facts concerned unseaworthiness. The extension changes no applicable statutory limit and overrules no controlling holding.

**Stone's Holding:** General maritime law permits an estate to recover for death caused by negligent breach of an otherwise applicable maritime duty where the actual statutory regime does not preclude that third-party action. The complaint cannot be dismissed solely because the alleged negligent injury was fatal. Duty, breach, causation, defenses, and lawful damages remain subject to proof.

**Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers an established maritime negligence duty in this nonseaman, territorial-water, third-party setting. It neither creates a duty where none exists nor alters exclusive employer compensation or an applicable statutory damage limit.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The same maritime duty should not become unenforceable merely because its breach causes death, unless the governing legal regime requires that outcome. Here Congress's classifications matter: Garris is not a Jones Act seaman, the accident is not within DOHSA's geographic field, and Norfolk is a third party rather than the compensation employer or vessel defendant. A general judicial preference for more recovery would be inadequate, but the established maritime duty, Moragne v. States Marine Lines's principle, and preservation of third-party claims supply the legal basis.

**Precedent, scope, and acceptable alternatives:** Preserve Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co.'s refusal to supplement Congress's actual high-seas compensation choices and Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. The maritime duty and statutory savings structure support this extension. Reinstating the categorical dismissal is not acceptable merely because Congress has occupied adjacent remedial fields.

# United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-157; 532 U.S. 822. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Fourth Circuit's refund judgment. **Simulated Event Date:** June 4, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether an affiliated group filing consolidated returns calculates its product liability loss on a consolidated basis in the first instance or by aggregating product liability losses separately calculated for each member.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the calculation question reviewed; the petition's exact wording has not been independently verified.

**Material Record:** United Dominion's predecessor AMCA filed consolidated returns for 1983 through 1986. In each year its consolidated net operating loss exceeded all members' aggregate product liability expenses. Five subsidiaries nevertheless had positive separate taxable income, subject to immaterial exceptions. The amounts and characterization of their product liability expenses are undisputed; the disagreement concerns whether those expenses count toward the group's loss eligible for the special ten-year carryback. The group comprised 26 members. [United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-822.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The refund claims produced an administrative disagreement, followed by a refund action in the Western District of North Carolina. The district court accepted the consolidated calculation. The Fourth Circuit reversed, requiring separate-member computation and using the regulatory concept of separate net operating loss. **Relief Requested:** United Dominion seeks reversal and treatment of the disputed expenses as part of the consolidated product liability loss; the Government seeks preservation of the limitation. A conflicting Sixth Circuit approach helped frame the issue but is not controlling precedent.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The relevant Code and Treasury regulations are those governing the 1983-1986 tax years, not a later revision merely in effect on the merits date. No jurisdictional or refund-claim preservation defect is established in the available record. The special carryback's mathematical availability does not establish every remaining refund calculation or entitlement outside the litigated expense treatment.

**Relevant Dates:** Tax years 1983 through 1986; argument March 26, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion; related same-term tax matters are addressed below. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Gitlitz v. Commissioner or United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co. concerning tax interpretation must be considered before adjudication. Their historical dispositions do not govern.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** This refund action concerns the legal computation for the litigated tax years. A ruling on the calculation method does not establish an unsupported dollar award or eliminate any preserved issue in the remaining refund accounting.

### Issue 1: Consolidated product liability loss

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. requires courts to enforce enacted terms and actual delegation boundaries before according appropriate effect to a reasonable formally promulgated interpretation. It does not turn any agency litigation position into a binding regulation or cure a gap with administrative labels. Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes informal interpretations from force-of-law rules and preserves persuasion on the merits.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The statutory interpretation and delegated-authority standards require reading the whole operative scheme and identifying actual agency authority. United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Christensen v. Harris County preserve the distinction between an enacted or promulgated rule and the persuasive force of a litigation interpretation. They do not select a financial result before interpreting the law.

**Other Governing Law:** The applicable §172 provisions define product liability loss as the lesser of net operating loss and allowable product liability expenses. Sections 1501 and 1502 permit consolidated returns and delegate their detailed administration. The then-operative regulations calculate consolidated taxable income or consolidated net operating loss using members' separate taxable income, adjusted by items treated at group level. Separate taxable income excludes several items that would enter an ordinary corporation's net operating loss. A separate-net-operating-loss figure also appears for allocation to a member carrying a group loss to a year outside the group. Whether that different-use figure controls the initial group product-liability calculation is disputed. [United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-822.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** The conflicting circuit approaches expose the accounting disagreement but are not controlling precedent. The agency's asserted anti-avoidance concerns must be tied to the operative provisions, rather than assumed to supply an unexpressed restriction.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing framework requires interpretation of the actual tax-year regulations under the Code. It does not invite invalidation of consolidated returns or an assumption that each affiliate possesses a separately defined NOL for every computation. An agency's power to adopt a different prospective lawful rule is separate from what the existing rule says.

**Open Point:** No controlling decision decides whether consolidated product liability loss is calculated using group NOL and aggregate expenses initially or by first limiting each member's expenses through a separate-loss calculation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**United Dominion's strongest rule:** The consolidated-return regulations provide the relevant NOL only at the group level. Once that NOL is calculated, the statute compares it with the group's product liability expenses. Positive separate taxable income is not a separate NOL calculation because it omits items the scheme places at the consolidated level. Importing an allocation rule designed for a member's nonconsolidated year would change its function and add a limitation Treasury did not adopt.

**Government's strongest rule:** Congress tied the special carryback to product liability losses, not every expense arising in a profitable subsidiary. The group approach can use another member's unrelated losses to carry profitable affiliates' expenses back ten years and may invite tax planning. A separate-member calculation better preserves the connection between the expense and the member's economic loss; the allocation regulation supplies an administrable figure.

**Lower reasoning and limits:** The district court accepted group-level comparison where consolidated NOL exceeded total expenses. The Fourth Circuit used member-specific losses and regulatory allocation language. The Government's concerns about duplicate benefits and avoidance are relevant to reading ambiguity, but no concession establishes an actual double deduction or abusive transaction here. # SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Good News Club v. Milford Central School. **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-2036; 533 U.S. 98. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits decision. **Simulated Event Date:** June 11, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Faithful formulation: Whether excluding the Good News Club's after-school meetings from the school's community-use forum violates freedom of speech, and whether avoiding an Establishment Clause violation justifies the exclusion. Exact petition wording was not independently retrieved.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the issue reviewed; it is not a verified transcription of the petition.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** A divided Second Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Milford. The district court had initially allowed the Club to meet by preliminary injunction, then vacated it and treated the proposed activity as excluded religious instruction. **Relief Requested:** The Club requests reversal and equal access; Milford seeks affirmance of its exclusion.

**Material Record:** New York permits school community uses. Milford's 1992 policy admitted instruction, social and civic activities, recreation, and other community-welfare meetings, while excluding religious purposes. District residents Stephen and Darleen Fournier requested weekly meetings for children ages six through twelve. Meetings included Bible verses, songs, games, a Bible story applied to children's lives, and prayer. The Club is privately sponsored; parental permission is required. Milford admitted organizations teaching morals and character, but characterized this integrated program as religious instruction rather than secular matters from a religious perspective. During the injunction the Club met in a school resource/special-education room. The parties accept limited-public-forum treatment. The record's descriptions of recruitment, adult leadership, timing, and very young participants matter to actual sponsorship or pressure; religious content alone supplies neither. [Good News Club v. Milford Central School](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-98.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of summary judgment for the school. An entitlement to ultimate plaintiff judgment must not be inferred from defects in the school's categorical exclusion. Separate equality and RFRA claims were asserted below but are not needed to resolve the reviewed speech and establishment questions. **Relevant Dates:** Application September 1996; denial October 1996, board resolution February 1997; suit March 1997; interim access April 1997 through June 1998; final district judgment August 1998. **Companion or Related Matters:** Same-term religious-benefit cases may affect attribution if effective before this date. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

### Issue 1: Religious instruction and worship in the admitted forum

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District forbids excluding a religious perspective from an admitted instructional subject. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia protects religious viewpoints within its actual private-publication benefit. Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 additionally controls otherwise comparable private worship in a broad after-hours educational and community forum; devotional form alone cannot disqualify it. That holding governs notwithstanding a contrary separate opinion.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The private religious viewpoints standard preserves genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral activity and operational limits. Comparable secular instruction cannot be admitted while its religious instruction is excluded merely by calling it worship. **Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern this public school's access decision. State community-use authorization and the actual adopted policy define the forum without overriding federal equality of viewpoint. **Material Other Authority:** Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association and Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund preserve legitimate subject limits.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law already rejects categorical instruction/worship labels for otherwise comparable uses in this kind of broad forum. **Open Point:** Whether the specific youth-directed program is otherwise comparable under genuine activity limits, rather than merely differently religious.

### Issue 2: Governmental religion and school context

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton supplies the unified, modified inquiry under Lemon v. Doe forbids a school-created representative devotional component and distinguishes genuine private access. Lee v. Weisman independently protects against official organization and substantial practical religious pressure.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Purpose, effects, and entanglement inform the unified inquiry, not independent fallback prongs. Neutral access is not immunity for actual school adoption. **Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause constrains the governmental relationship actually created. **Material Other Authority:** The private, after-hours settings of Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District and Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 are relevant but do not erase the children's ages or operational facts here.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Independent private access is permissible; official devotion and substantial government-created pressure remain forbidden. **Open Point:** Whether this record supports the latter or only ordinary private religious instruction in a community forum.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The court distinguished intensely religious instruction and prayer from secular character development expressed religiously. The distinction was treated as subject exclusion, consistent across religious groups.

**Club's position:** Moral and character formation is admitted. A Christian explanation, including prayer and scripture, remains a viewpoint on that subject. Private sponsorship, parental permission, and voluntary after-school access distinguish official instruction. Its requested rule grants access under the same genuine terms applied to comparable secular groups.

**School's position:** An integrated devotional and conversion-oriented program differs in activity from discussion of morality. Immediate after-school meetings among impressionable elementary pupils can appear school authorized and can carry practical pressure despite a permission form. It seeks authority to exclude the devotional activity while admitting discussion of religious viewpoints.

**Alternative grounds and limits:** Actual official promotion or control could raise a separate establishment problem even when a categorical religious-use rule fails. A school need not open its forum to every activity, but it cannot manufacture a secular counterpart requirement that excludes worship by definition. The entering Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 holding materially narrows Milford's legal defense. No independently decisive Solicitor General submission is identified. [Good News Club v. Milford Central School](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-98.pdf).

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Paths and consequences:** Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand for nondiscriminatory access under valid operational rules. If material facts about sponsorship or government-created pressure remain genuinely disputed, vacate summary judgment and require their resolution before a final injunction. Affirmance requires a genuine activity distinction or supported governmental-function ground consistent with Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10; religious intensity alone is insufficient.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A direct application can cover private moral instruction, including its devotional form, in comparable after-school community use. It need not create a general classroom prayer right, require schools to continue forums indefinitely, or declare parental consent conclusive of every coercion claim. An attribution ruling can distinguish this private permission-based arrangement from a school-selected representative ceremony. Effective relief removes the discriminatory denial; operational supervision, capacity, scheduling, and neutral participant rules survive. No damages or fee amount is established.

**Uncertainty:** A conclusory assertion that all children understand private sponsorship is not a finding. Conversely, the ages of potential observers do not by themselves prove official religious conduct. The summary-judgment posture determines how unresolved supported facts are handled.

### Issue 1: Access and the governmental relationship

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The admitted community subjects and stated religious basis for denial present a speech claim. Because the school invokes its constitutional establishment duty, the permissible governmental relationship must also be decided; it cannot remain an unexamined defense to the remedy. Other independent claims and monetary relief are not reached because the speech ground supplies the judgment.

**Question Stone Resolves:** May a public school exclude comparable private moral instruction because its means are devotional, and does permission-based private use itself establish religion?

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply entering Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 and Agostini v. Felton. This position does not represent Stone's earlier separate disagreement as controlling or adopt silently replacing the Court's access rule.

**Stone's Holding:** Within this broad private community forum, religious instruction and devotional form cannot disqualify otherwise comparable moral and character instruction. Independent adult sponsorship, parental authorization, and after-school operation constitute permissible private access absent actual official adoption or substantial government-created religious pressure. Location, youth, and prayer alone do not establish those prohibited governmental acts.

**Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the requested private Club use on the same valid terms as comparable groups. It does not authorize teacher-led official devotions, compulsory religious participation, or preferential school recruitment. Parental permission is material evidence of voluntary private choice, not an automatic answer to every institutional-pressure claim.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The school has opened moral instruction to outsiders. Its religious/secular distinction removes a viewpoint and its expressive method from an admitted subject. Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 rejects that distinction for comparable worship. The separate establishment inquiry asks who organizes and controls the religious activity and how the school engages participants. A private meeting on school premises is not converted into governmental religious teaching by the content of its story or closing prayer.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** A universal rule for all elementary-school programs lies outside this particular forum question. Any concrete allegation of official recruitment, adoption, or pressure not resolved on this summary-judgment record requires facts the record lacks.

## D. DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT AND FUTURE APPLICATION

Stone distinguishes equal private-forum access from governmental financing or sponsorship of devotion. His earlier separate disagreement in Bronx Household does not displace that decision's controlling access rule, which he now applies to the admitted subject. Young age and immediate after-school timing warrant attention to actual official recruitment or compulsion; they do not justify demanding proof that private religious expression can never influence a child. A remand must resolve a supported material issue. Funding and compulsory-school-devotion cases retain their distinct constitutional questions.

# Kyllo v. United States

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-11. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Kyllo v. United States. **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-8508; 533 U.S. 27. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits decision on a preserved suppression challenge. **Simulated Event Date:** June 11, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Faithful formulation: Whether using a thermal-imaging device aimed at a private home from a public street to detect relative heat within it constitutes a Fourth Amendment search. Exact petition wording was not independently retrieved.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the issue reviewed; it is not a verified transcription of the petition.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit upheld rejection of suppression and the conviction after an earlier remand for an evidentiary hearing. **Relief Requested:** Kyllo seeks reversal of the no-search ruling and suppression of evidence obtained under the ensuing warrant; the United States seeks affirmance.

**Material Record:** On January 16, 1992, at approximately 3:20 a.m., agents used an Agema Thermovision 210 from streets in front of and behind Kyllo's unit in a Florence, Oregon, triplex. The device passively recorded relative infrared radiation. Its images showed unusually warm roof and wall areas; agents inferred indoor cultivation lamps. Informants, utility bills, and the scan supported a later warrant. The physical search disclosed more than 100 marijuana plants. Kyllo conditionally pleaded guilty to manufacturing marijuana. The district court found the device crude, nonpenetrating, and incapable of identifying people or activities; the significance of its heat-pattern inference remains a legal question. The Ninth Circuit relied on lack of efforts to conceal escaping heat and lack of intimate detail. [Kyllo v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-27.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge to the deliberate scan is preserved through the conditional plea. Its constitutional status, the later warrant's untainted support, and the exclusion remedy are distinct. The date of police conduct can matter to a properly available objective-reliance argument, but current law governs merits review of this nonfinal criminal case. **Relevant Dates:** Scan January 1992; argument February 20, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** Any intervening controlling search decision must be applied. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 11, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Illinois v. McArthur and Atwater v. City of Lago Vista may affect general intrusion or remedy reasoning if effective, but neither proposed position supplies a thermal-search rule.

### Issue 1: Targeted acquisition of concealed domestic information

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Pinson already holds that targeted technological examination of a closed home's heat patterns is a search where the reported information reveals otherwise unobservable concealed domestic operation. United States v. Myers applies that rule to an Agema 210 inference of indoor cultivation lights. Passive reception, exterior emissions, and lack of a recognizable interior image do not defeat protection. Neither decision adopts market availability as a constitutional condition.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The targeted thermal examination standard preserves the same rule and requires a warrant absent a recognized exception. Investigative purpose and equipment use alone are insufficient. **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment protects this home; the scan had no advance warrant. **Material Other Authority:** Katz v. United States and United States v. Karo protect concealed information without physical entry. California v. Greenwood concerns discarded garbage; United States v. Place concerns detection confined to contraband. Neither description fits general domestic heat patterns.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The sensor, dwelling, and inference here fall directly within United States v. Myers. The established search rule therefore governs. **Open Point:** No new search rule is necessary on the stated findings; future sensors and ordinary visible observations remain outside this record.

### Issue 2: Consequences for the later warrant and evidence

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Pinson and United States v. Myers distinguish an unlawful scan from exclusion and preserve ordinary preservation limits. United States v. Myers requires consideration of a preserved derivative-warrant claim under supported, procedurally available grounds. **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The thermal standard does not order automatic suppression or an automatic new evidentiary hearing. **Other Governing Law:** The conditional plea preserves the specified suppression issue; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 11(a)(2) governs consequences if the defendant prevails.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Leon requires actual objectively reasonable reliance and retains its deception, neutrality, deficient-affidavit, and facial-deficiency limits. Arizona v. Evans concerns qualifying court-clerical error, not deliberate investigative scanning. **Existing Rule's Reach:** A subsequent entry warrant cannot retroactively authorize the scan. Available untainted probable cause, independent source, and supported reliance remain separate issues. **Open Point:** The record does not establish a final basis for admitting or excluding the derivative evidence.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** No protected expectation existed in escaping exterior heat, and the imager disclosed only amorphous hot spots. The defendant had not hidden those emissions. This reasoning conflicts with entering United States v. Pinson and United States v. Myers unless their factual predicate is genuinely absent.

**Kyllo's position:** The scan was designed to recover concealed facts about domestic activity and succeeded in doing so. The warrant requirement cannot turn on whether officers receive an ordinary photograph or infer activity from technologically revealed patterns. He requests meaningful suppression relief where the later warrant depended on the scan.

**Government's position:** The device passively received heat already outside the house, exposed no identified objects or intimate conduct, and merely strengthened an inference drawn from other evidence. Its rule would permit that external examination. Alternatively, admissibility could survive a search ruling if the warrant or a properly preserved remedial ground independently supports the evidence.

**Concessions and limits:** Protection of this heat pattern does not make every instrument or every visible exterior observation a search. A warrant exception requires an identified lawful justification, not generalized investigative usefulness. No consent or exigency is established. [Kyllo v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-27.pdf).

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Paths and consequences:** Apply United States v. Myers, reject the no-search ground, and remand for resolution of the preserved derivative-evidence claim. Suppression follows if the unlawful scan causally supplies the evidence and no properly available lawful basis defeats exclusion; admission may follow sufficient independent warrant support or another established exception. The court must not assume any such finding. An affirmance of the categorical no-search rule would require expressly changing or materially distinguishing United States v. Pinson and United States v. Myers, rather than disregarding those controlling decisions.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Existing law permits a direct rule for targeted sensing that reveals otherwise concealed domestic operation. A new rule limited by general public use would add a condition absent from entering law and potentially remove protection as devices become common. A rule treating all exterior emissions or every government sensor as protected would exceed the present facts. A remedial mandate can correct the preserved claim without ordering a new hearing, acquittal, release, or plea withdrawal before its legal predicates are established.

**Uncertainty:** The weight of untainted affidavit information and the scope of any preserved alternative admissibility argument require the lower record. The absence of an intimate visual image is established; the absence of information about concealed operation is not.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Tuan Anh Nguyen v. INS. **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-2071; 533 U.S. 53. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of citizenship and removal. **Simulated Event Date:** June 11, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Faithful formulation: Whether the sex-based requirements for transmission of citizenship to a child born abroad outside marriage, particularly timely paternal recognition under 8 U.S.C. § 1409(a)(4), violate the equal-protection guarantee of the Fifth Amendment. Exact petition wording was not independently retrieved.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the issue reviewed; it is not a verified transcription of the petition.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit rejected Nguyen and his citizen father's constitutional challenge after the Board of Immigration Appeals rejected citizenship and sustained deportability. **Relief Requested:** Petitioners seek reversal, recognition of citizenship under a constitutionally corrected statute, and relief from removal; the INS seeks affirmance.

**Material Record:** Nguyen was born in Vietnam September 11, 1969, to United States citizen Joseph Boulais and a Vietnamese mother, who were unmarried. He entered this country in June 1975, became a lawful permanent resident, and was raised in Texas by Boulais. At twenty-two he pleaded guilty to two sexual assaults on a child; removal proceedings followed. In 1998, while administrative appeal was pending, Boulais obtained a parentage order based on DNA testing. Nguyen was then twenty-eight. The timely paternal recognition condition was unmet despite the biological and actual parent-child relationship. The citizen father is a copetitioner with his own equality claim. [Tuan Anh Nguyen v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-53.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Citizenship is antecedent to removal as an alien. The father's presence removes the principal claimant obstacle debated in Miller v. Albright. The 1986 transitional election permits consideration of the earlier statutory version without the later written-support condition; failure of that later condition cannot silently become an independent barrier. Other applicable citizenship prerequisites and statutory vintage still matter. **Relevant Dates:** Birth in 1969 fixes applicable statutory choices; recognition came after both minority and the amended eighteen-year deadline. Argument January 9, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** Other same-term immigration matters address different statutes. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 11, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None is identified as controlling this parent-sex classification. Any earlier effective citizenship or severability ruling must nevertheless be checked before final use.

### Issue 1: Constitutional review of paternal recognition

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Miller v. Albright supplies a fractured affirmance with no controlling merits or citizenship-remedy rationale. Its separate express third-party-standing permission does not establish the validity of § 1409. United States v. Virginia requires a genuine important objective and substantial relationship between it and the sex classification; real differences may matter, while generalized capacity or role assumptions cannot substitute for fit. Exceedingly persuasive justification describes demanding intermediate scrutiny, not least restrictive means.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The sex-classification standards preserve actual-objective and substantial-fit review; the educational-remedy rule is not a citizenship remedy. **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth Amendment's equal-protection guarantee applies to federal classification; Article I supplies national naturalization authority. Section 1409(a) requires proof of blood relationship and paternal citizenship, with recognition during minority through legitimation, sworn acknowledgment, or adjudication. Section 1409(c) treats the unmarried citizen mother differently. The relevant physical-presence requirement must be identified under the applicable statutory version. [Tuan Anh Nguyen v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-53.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** Fiallo v. Bell concerns admission preferences and has not received a controlling simulated extension to all citizenship-at-birth classifications. **Existing Rule's Reach:** Ordinary sex-classification principles govern the claimed inequality; Miller v. Albright establishes no automatic deference or invalidity. **Open Point:** Whether biology and opportunity for relationship justify this additional male-only deadline where parentage can otherwise be conclusively proved.

### Issue 2: Remedy for an unconstitutional citizenship condition

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Miller v. Albright adopts no majority rule either categorically forbidding citizenship relief or requiring extension. **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Effective relief must cure the proved violation while preserving lawful qualifications; a remedy from a different statutory setting cannot be mechanically transferred. **Other Governing Law:** The statutory citizenship structure and severability govern whether the discriminatory condition can be removed while the general transmission rule remains operative.

**Material Other Authority:** INS v. Pangilinan limits equitable naturalization contrary to valid legislation. It does not by itself answer severance of an unconstitutional condition in a claim to statutory citizenship. **Existing Rule's Reach:** No court may simply ignore valid statutory prerequisites. **Open Point:** If the classification fails, whether corrective extension, withdrawal, or another lawful remedy best fits Congress's enacted scheme and the status claimed.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Different biological relationships at birth support different evidentiary and acknowledgment requirements; Congress's citizenship authority reinforces the statutory judgment.

**Petitioners' position:** Biological parentage can be established directly, and a deadline applicable only to fathers adds little to the separate clear-and-convincing proof requirement. A mother need not sustain a relationship, while an actual father who raises his child can lose transmission solely through missing formality. Their proposed rule subjects this sex distinction to demanding review and removes the invalid obstacle without removing citizenship from others.

**Government's position:** Birth itself identifies the mother and provides an opportunity for contact. Paternal recognition during minority supplies both reliable documentation and an opportunity for a real tie to a citizen and this country. The options are modest and administrable; equal protection need not require DNA testing or actual relationship proof in every case. It also contests judicial power to confer citizenship outside statutory terms.

**Alternatives and limits:** Biological proof rules can differ where the evidence differs without validating every legal consequence attached to sex. Invalidating this deadline need not decide the separate support condition or all residency differences. A citizenship conclusion must address lawful remaining prerequisites, not rest on an attractive personal history or the seriousness of the crimes. [Tuan Anh Nguyen v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-53.pdf).

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Paths and consequences:** Affirm if the additional paternal condition substantially serves genuine parentage or relationship objectives under the appropriate scrutiny. Reverse if its male-only temporal formality lacks substantial fit; then decide the lawful corrective remedy. A remedy removing the condition permits determination of citizenship under remaining valid requirements and defeats removal if citizenship is established. Withdrawal of preferential treatment presents different statutory-intent and reliance consequences and would not necessarily give these petitioners citizenship. A categorical no-remedy ruling would require resolving a question Miller v. Albright left open.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The record supports a ruling on timely recognition as a male-only additional condition, not all sex distinctions in nationality law. A constitutionally adequate proof-of-parentage requirement and applicable physical-presence rules remain separable. Relief may be a conditional remand for verification of those requirements rather than immediate judicial naturalization. No criminal conviction is vacated by citizenship relief.

**Uncertainty:** The exact application of the transitional election and remaining statutory prerequisites must be confirmed below if not conclusively established. Neither the later parentage order nor the father's actual care makes the missed statutory deadline timely by interpretation alone.

The granted question about the relationship between unreasonable force and immunity squarely presents the duplication he seeks to correct.

**Legal reason:** Graham's substantive rule already accounts for urgency, perceived threats, reasonable factual mistakes, and operational discretion. A further judicial defense based only on the absence of sufficiently similar precedent can deny the existing damages channel even after the complete substantive standard establishes wrongful conduct. Stone regards that defense as insufficiently tied to the authorized remedy and its legitimate protection of reasonable legal reliance. He states the competing concern fairly: officials need predictable protection against unexpected changes and burdensome litigation. Concrete authoritative permission protects that interest more directly than legal silence.

### Issue 2: Replacement boundary and application

**Stone's Holding:** The court first applies the complete substantive constitutional rule. An individual official then bears the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, genuine reliance, and a reasonable basis for treating that authority as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately conveying such authority can implement reliance. Department custom, a supervisor's unsupported assurance, general job authority, legal silence, or the absence of an identical case does not independently suffice. Reasonable factual mistakes already allowed on the merits are not counted again after a violation has been established.

**Application:** Vice-presidential security, resistance, and the officer's reasonably perceived threat bear on the force merits. They do not themselves identify authoritative permission for the alleged unnecessary shove. The record identifies no such permission. Who performed the disputed act and whether the particular force was justified remain subject to the proper assumed-facts and factfinding rules; the Court cannot infer liability from the current denial of immunity.

### Issue 3: Reliance, transition, and limits

**Reliance and transition:** Stone applies the revised rule to this pending case. An officer's actual reasonable reliance on directly authorizing law receives the new defense; reliance on the broader expectation that unclear law would prevent damages does not justify continuing that very rule. Final judgments are not reopened. Ordinary preservation and preclusion rules continue to govern other litigation. Earlier judgments retain their finality; the treatment of other pending cases follows the effective rule and ordinary preservation requirements.

**Untouched law:** Absolute immunities, proper defendants, existing causes of action, institutional attribution, causation, compensable injury, and applicable sovereign immunity retain their separate rules. The personal defense governs individual damages; it does not decide prospective relief or public compensation. This federal-officer case supplies no new statutory action against a State or federal agency.

## D. DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT AND FUTURE APPLICATION

Stone replaces the legal-uncertainty defense and creates a defined safe harbor for genuine, reasonable reliance on directly authorizing law. That protection answers the legitimate reliance concern without treating silence or the absence of an identical case as permission. He states the displaced rule, the reason for changing it, the new defense, its application to pending litigation, and the continued protection of final judgments. This is a deliberate doctrinal revision, with its reach fixed by the defense's stated elements and the separate prerequisites of liability.

The relationship between operational binding effect and delegated lawmaking effect is disputed. The Court must distinguish interpretation of tariff language, allocation of delegated authority, evidentiary presumptions, and the reviewing court's independent factfinding. **Reasoning Below:** The Federal Circuit treated ruling letters as different from regulations because they lacked public procedures and equivalent general legal force. **Mead's Position:** The power to classify particular imports does not make every classification letter a lawmaking act binding a reviewing court. Mead's objection to binding force does not answer whether the agency offers useful reasons. The Court can resolve the standard and remand application without selecting the tariff category itself. No independent constitutional invalidity claim is needed to decide these questions.

## A. STATUS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit judgment and remand because its rejection of controlling deference did not justify refusing meaningful consideration of Customs' reasons.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require an independent judicial construction of the tariff terms informed by the letter's demonstrated expertise, explanation, and consistency. Do not direct a dutiable classification or a particular refund. The lower court must explain what persuasive weight the actual reasoning earns and decide the classification under the law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Authority before interpretive weight

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both the legal character of the ruling letter and the consequences for judicial interpretation. Each is necessary because the Government asks for controlling treatment and the appellate court denied the alternative form of respect as well. No constitutional ruling or wholesale disposition of unrelated regulatory regimes is necessary. The proper appellate consequence is remand for the lower court to apply the interpretive standard and determine the classification. That allocation of decisional work is a remedy choice, not a claim that the actual judicial record lacks the merchandise evidence.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress made this Customs letter an exercise of authority to establish a controlling legal interpretation, and whether the court may disregard its reasoning after concluding that Congress did not.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.'s requirement of actual delegated authority and Christensen v. Harris County's distinction between controlling and persuasive interpretation. Extend those principles to these Customs letters as a new application, not as a proposition already decided by either case. No alteration of their operative holdings is needed.

**Stone's Holding:** A Customs ruling letter with the transaction-specific and revisable character shown here does not acquire controlling interpretive force from Customs' general responsibility to classify imports. The reviewing court must identify an actual delegation exercised through the relevant mechanism before treating an agency's choice as legally controlling. Without that predicate, the court determines statutory meaning independently and considers the agency's reasoning to the extent its thoroughness, expertise, logic, and consistency justify. These letters fail the controlling-authority predicate but remain eligible for persuasive consideration.

**Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the asserted effect of this classification mechanism and materially equivalent Customs letters. It does not deny that Congress may confer bounded discretion, that a valid regulation may exercise it, or that technical administration can supply persuasive evidence. The number of offices or letters is contextual evidence of the mechanism's function, not an independent numerical disqualification.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Classification responsibility is not identical to authority to bind courts on statutory meaning. The unpublished letter binds Customs operationally in a narrower setting than a generally applicable regulation, and its later detailed explanation does not change the source of its legal power. Conversely, Mead's rejection of binding force leaves Customs' discussion of diary usage and binding mechanisms available for judicial evaluation. The change from earlier treatment may reduce weight if unexplained, but does not make the new explanation nonexistent. Courts must state the reasons for the weight they give, then decide the enacted tariff question. Remand preserves both the judicial responsibility and the possibility that sound administrative reasoning affects the result.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** A universal rule for other agencies' informal adjudications lies outside the Customs question presented.

## D. DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT AND FUTURE APPLICATION

Stone adopts a new application distinguishing these Customs letters from a valid exercise of delegated rulemaking authority. Haggar and Christensen support the distinction; general responsibility for administering a statute does not by itself confer power to bind courts on its meaning. The court therefore decides the tariff question independently while giving the agency's reasons the weight they earn. Stone retains the compatible holdings of Chevron and Auer because this classification mechanism can be resolved without displacing them. That is a reasoned choice of scope, not a prohibition on doctrinal change in a case that presents it.
