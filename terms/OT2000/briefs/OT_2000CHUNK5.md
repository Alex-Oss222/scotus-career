# Alexander v. Sandoval

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Alexander v. Sandoval
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 275; No. 99-1908
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-24 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether private individuals may sue to enforce disparate-impact regulations promulgated under §602 of Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, affirming an injunction requiring Alabama's Department of Public Safety to accommodate non-English speakers in driver's-license examinations under the disparate-impact regulations.
* **Relief Requested:** Alabama officials seek reversal because the asserted implied private cause of action is unavailable. Sandoval and the class seek preservation of the injunction under Title VI's private enforcement scheme.
* **Material Record:** Alabama's Department of Public Safety accepted federal transportation and justice funds. Following a state constitutional amendment declaring English the official language, the Department administered driver's-license examinations only in English. Sandoval represented non-English speakers and challenged that practice under federal regulations prohibiting methods of administration with discriminatory national-origin effects. The district court found a prohibited effect, enjoined the policy and ordered accommodation; the court of appeals affirmed. The claim at issue invokes the regulatory disparate-impact rule rather than proof of intentional discrimination in the statutory prohibition. [Alexander v. Sandoval](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-275.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review is limited to the existence of the private action. For this question, the regulations' validity and the lower courts' disparate-impact determination are assumed rather than adjudicated. The accepted private action for intentional discrimination under §601 is not challenged. A distinct §1983 theory is not the pleaded and granted route to be supplied by this Court. Federal funding, agency authority, a substantive obligation and a judicial remedy are separate inquiries.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.

A request for an injunction does not itself establish a cause of action. Conversely, lack of this cause does not nullify otherwise valid regulations or authorized agency enforcement.

### Issue 1: Private statutory enforcement and regulatory elaboration

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Powell v. Ridge holds that section 602 does not imply a direct private action to enforce Title VI disparate-impact regulations and separately rejects section 1983 enforcement of that effects-based regulatory duty absent an individual statutory right. Its direct-action holding governs the asserted route here unless distinguished or overruled; its separate section 1983 branch is not this granted claim. McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc. requires contextual congressional authorization without a universal express-text requirement. Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. preserves its confined affirmative-assistance action. Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools concerns remedies within an existing action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc. requires contextual analysis of congressional intent while preserving established actions and independently authorized relief. Blessing v. Freestone permits regulatory elaboration of an existing individual federal right in its §1983 setting, but requires identification of that right and does not decide this distinct implied-action question.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title VI §601 states the individual discrimination prohibition. Section 602 authorizes implementing regulations and specified agency enforcement. The 1986 remedies and immunity provision and the Civil Rights Restoration Act are part of the relevant statutory history and structure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Cannon v. University of Chicago recognizes the contextual origin of the Title VI and Title IX private action; Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission and Alexander v. Choate address the statutory and regulatory distinction but do not conclusively settle the precise action now presented.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Powell v. Ridge already rejects the direct regulatory-effects action asserted here. The accepted section 601 intentional-discrimination action and valid administrative enforcement remain available within their own requirements. The broader contextual authorities do not silently displace Powell's specific Title VI holding.
* **Open Point:** No first-impression question remains about whether the existing direct-action rule permits this claim. The merits choice is whether to reaffirm that rule or expressly reconsider it, including whether Title VI's integrated structure warrants a prospective-enforcement exception. Regulatory validity and the unchallenged disparate-impact determination are outside this review.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Title VI's accepted private enforcement and regulatory implementation supported a private equitable action. **Petitioners:** Section 602 addresses agencies and their enforcement duties, and cannot extend the §601 private cause to an independent effects-based wrong; agency language cannot create the missing congressional action. **Respondents:** The action enforces Title VI as lawfully implemented, reflects longstanding judicial treatment and congressional acceptance, and is consistent with the funding scheme. The United States supported that contextual position. Respondents need not show an express action clause, but must demonstrate authorization for this obligation, not merely the §601 action. Petitioners must account for received actions and contextual inference; Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. cannot be cited as categorical rejection of implied liability. Validity of the regulations remains a shared assumption for this review.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reaffirm Powell v. Ridge and reverse the injunction insofar as it rests on the prohibited direct regulatory action, while leaving administrative enforcement intact. Alternatively, expressly reconsider Powell's direct-action branch and affirm if Title VI's integrated structure authorizes prospective enforcement of these valid implementing obligations. That alternative requires a reasoned statutory stare-decisis analysis; it is not mere application of an undecided remedial question. Neither path decides whether the English-only policy is intentional discrimination. A separate preserved action must be identified before it can sustain relief; the Court cannot cure an action defect by inventing §1983 allegations.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Reaffirming Powell can distinguish independent regulatory disparate-impact duties from regulations specifying the content of an existing statutory right, without displacing received securities liability or the §601 action. An affirmative holding must identify the portion of Powell overruled or narrowed and can rest on Title VI's particular history and structure without allowing agencies to create private causes under every implementing grant.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it recognizes the private action for prospective enforcement of the valid Title VI disparate-impact regulations and sustains the injunction on that basis.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the prospective injunction operative on the accepted findings and within the scope reviewed. The validity of the regulations and the unchallenged finding of unjustified disparate impact remain the premises of this review rather than new merits adjudications.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A regulatory duty beyond the received statutory action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the private-enforcement question actually presented. Powell v. Ridge already rejected this direct regulatory action; deciding for Sandoval therefore requires reconsidering that controlling statutory holding. Regulatory validity, the correctness of the impact finding, and a separate section 1983 action are not before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Title VI authorizes a beneficiary to obtain prospective judicial enforcement of a valid section 602 regulation prohibiting unjustified discriminatory effects in the recipient's administration of a federally assisted program.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose overruling Powell v. Ridge's direct-action prohibition to the extent it forecloses this prospective enforcement. Stone joined that prohibition but now rejects its treatment of the integrated statutory scheme. Preserve its distinction between a regulation's validity and a remedy's source, and do not decide its separate section 1983 holding. Reaffirm Cannon's contextual inquiry and distinguish McClellan's unsupported Cable Act damages action.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VI's established private action permits beneficiaries to obtain prospective relief requiring a covered recipient to comply with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 regulations implementing the statutory protection against discrimination. Congress supplies this action through Title VI read as a whole; a regulation cannot independently create an action under an otherwise remediless statute. Powell v. Ridge's contrary direct-action holding should be overruled to this extent. Sandoval may maintain this action, and the reviewed injunction remains supported by that enforcement route.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns prospective enforcement of valid Title VI administration requirements protecting the statutory beneficiaries, including the effects regulation assumed valid here. Plaintiffs must prove the regulation's actual elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation. Statistical imbalance alone is not automatically a violation. Damages for unintentional discrimination and enforcement under section 1983 are not decided.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 601 identifies the protected persons and prohibition; section 602 expressly directs implementation of that protection. The established private action and authorized implementing standards should operate together unless Congress has made the administrative route exclusive. The recipient's obligation does not become privately enforceable merely because an agency wrote a rule, but neither does the agency-facing form of the implementation provision sever every valid regulatory specification from the action Congress accepted. Cannon's interpretive setting, Congress's later express recognition of Title VI suits, and the continued administrative-and-judicial enforcement structure support that conclusion. Those enactments are affirmative context, not an inference from congressional silence alone. The congressional-authorization inquiry carries decisive weight; the practical loss of an effective individual remedy confirms the consequences of the competing readings but does not create jurisdiction or a cause of action. Powell gave insufficient weight to that integrated source and improperly treated the difference between statutory intent liability and valid preventive implementation as dispositive of prospective enforcement. McClellan involved a distinct statute and an asserted damages action without this established remedial background. Central Bank likewise rejects a universal express-text-only rule without itself deciding this claim. Statutory stare decisis deserves substantial weight, and Stone acknowledges his prior join. But Powell's recent channel restriction has not changed the recipients' preexisting duty to obey these regulations; correcting the prospective remedy neither imposes retrospective damages nor disturbs a completed final judgment. Reliance on the absence of a beneficiary's enforcement action cannot outweigh the statute's better-supported remedial arrangement. The present record assumes both valid regulations and a violation. Recognizing this action therefore preserves the remedy for an existing duty rather than judicially prescribing a new language policy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Regulatory validity, a contested disparate-impact merits determination, damages availability, and the separate section 1983 route are outside the granted question. Powell's section 1983 rationale is not silently abrogated.

# Atwater v. City of Lago Vista

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Atwater v. City of Lago Vista
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 318; No. 99-1408
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-24 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourth Amendment permits a warrantless custodial arrest for a fine-only misdemeanor seatbelt offense based solely on probable cause that the offense occurred in the officer's presence?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit en banc, affirming summary judgment for the City and other defendants because probable cause and the absence of extraordinary arrest execution were sufficient under its adopted rule.
* **Relief Requested:** Atwater and her husband seek reversal and adjudication of their §1983 damages claim. The defendants seek affirmance of the probable-cause rule and retain any independently applicable immunity or municipal-liability defenses.
* **Material Record:** In March 1997, Atwater drove a pickup with her three-year-old son and five-year-old daughter in the front seat; none wore a seatbelt. Officer Turek observed the violations and stopped her. On the plaintiffs' supported summary-judgment account, he announced that she would go to jail and berated her. She lacked license and insurance papers, explaining that her purse had been stolen. A friend took the children. Turek handcuffed Atwater, drove her to the station and had her booked; she removed shoes, jewelry and glasses, emptied pockets, was photographed and spent about an hour in a cell before release on bond. She pleaded no contest to seatbelt misdemeanors and paid a $50 fine; other charges were dismissed. The parties did not dispute probable cause for the observed violations. [Atwater v. City of Lago Vista](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-318.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Probable cause for the initial stop is conceded. The disputed additional custody must not be justified circularly by the search-incident rules that presuppose a lawful arrest. Constitutional reasonableness, the officer's conduct-date immunity and liability of the City are separate. The fine-only civil offense in Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights differs from Texas's criminal seatbelt offense, making extension of its rule a genuine legal question.
* **Relevant Dates:** The arrest occurred in March 1997. Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights became effective May 4, 1998, and Knowles v. Iowa on December 8, 1998; neither can itself supply clearly established notice at the arrest date.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.

Her no-contest seatbelt plea establishes no entitlement to unlawful custody and is not itself challenged by this suit.

### Issue 1: Probable cause and additional restraint

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights holds that probable cause and state enforcement authority do not alone justify station transport and confinement for a fine-only civil licensing violation. Added restraint must reasonably serve an actual enforcement function. Its actual application produced no majority finding that custody was lawful or unlawful. Knowles v. Iowa denies the full incident-search exception for a citation without a lawful custodial arrest.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights recognizes identification, appearance, ongoing violation, protection and necessary processing as possible justifications, without presuming them. It creates neither categorical arrest immunity nor a least-restrictive-means or universal citation requirement.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment's reasonableness requirement and §1983 govern the asserted claim. Texas's misdemeanor arrest authorization supplies state power but does not conclusively answer federal reasonableness.
* **Material Other Authority:** Whren v. United States supplies objective grounds for a traffic stop. Wilson v. Arkansas treats historical practice as relevant to reasonableness, not an automatic substitute for actual constitutional analysis. United States v. Robinson and Gustafson v. Florida presuppose a qualifying lawful arrest for their incident-search rules.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law rejects the automatic-custody premise for a fine-only civil violation. It does not yet resolve the effect of a criminal label or adopt a categorical historical breach-of-peace limit for every misdemeanor arrest.
* **Open Point:** Whether the functional custody inquiry extends to an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor, and whether defendants can establish an independent lawful basis for this custody or a separate damages defense.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Probable cause ordinarily supplies the constitutional balance, with exceptional execution concerns treated separately. **Atwater:** Fine-only offenses do not justify jail and booking absent an actual need; the severe additional restraint bore no reasonable relationship to this seatbelt enforcement. Historical misdemeanor limitations reinforce that position. **Defendants:** Officers require clear arrest rules and cannot adjudicate all punishment classifications or alternatives at roadside; probable cause for an observed criminal offense supplies sufficient authority. Missing documents could bear on identification, but their existence must be evaluated rather than presumed dispositive. The United States supported the categorical arrest rule. Atwater's strongest alternative is an actual-function requirement, not immunity from all temporary detention or every arrest for a minor offense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm by limiting Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights to civil matters and adopting an ordinary probable-cause rule for criminal misdemeanors. Vacate and remand by applying functional review to added custody despite the fine-only criminal label. A conduct-date immunity defense may defeat personal damages even if the constitutional rule favors Atwater; municipal liability has independent policy and causation requirements. Neither reversal nor invalid custody automatically invalidates her uncontested seatbelt conviction.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The proposed alternatives concern additional custodial arrest for a known fine-only criminal offense. Functional review would not require citation in every case or alter probable cause for an initial stop. A categorical rule would establish broad misdemeanor arrest authority but still leave extraordinary execution and separately applicable state constraints distinct.

**Material Uncertainty:** The lower court's categorical rule did not establish every fact relevant to identification, appearance risk or necessary processing. Missing documents are in the record; their actual significance and remaining defenses cannot be invented away.

### Issue 1: Extend the actual-function rule to fine-only misdemeanors

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal rule because the defense judgment rests on probable cause alone. A categorical common-law breach-of-peace rule is unnecessary once the actual-function approach answers the stated question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a criminal label alone makes station transport and confinement reasonable for an observed fine-only violation, and what adjudication this record requires.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt an extension of Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights to fine-only criminal offenses. Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights presently controls civil licensing custody, so this application is a new holding rather than a claim that the criminal question was already decided.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only misdemeanor does not by itself justify additional station-house transport and confinement. Such custody must reasonably serve its actual enforcement function, with supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence or necessary-processing grounds considered. Defendants' categorical entitlement to judgment therefore fails.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the added restraints presented here and analogous fine-only criminal enforcement. It preserves initial stops on probable cause and genuine custody needs without requiring the least intrusive imaginable alternative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State's punishment choice and the actual enforcement needs matter to the scale of a seizure. Calling the same modest regulatory violation criminal does not by itself establish that the additional custody reasonably serves its asserted enforcement function. Clear administration is a legitimate interest, but a categorical permission to confine whenever paperwork permits would erase Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights's underlying distinction. Missing papers may justify verification; they do not conclusively establish that every later booking restraint was warranted. Actual facts and practical alternatives must be assessed without hindsight perfection. The rule's merits cannot retroactively establish notice for an earlier officer. The functional inquiry into the seizure's actual enforcement purpose is decisive; the State's choice of a fine-only penalty reinforces the assessment of its intrusiveness. The extension supplies a rule for like cases because a criminal label otherwise defeats the reason for the existing custody limitation. Whren's initial-stop rule remains intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual verification and custody-need findings, conduct-date immunity and municipal policy causation require record determinations absent from the categorical decision; other punishment classes are outside the question presented.

**Case Name:** Sterling v. Borough of Minersville.

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses both questions presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit sustained the denial of personal qualified immunity on the reviewed privacy theory.

**Relief Requested:** The officers seek reversal and protection against damages litigation. The estate seeks affirmance and continued proceedings, not a present damages award. Hoban's factual nonparticipation argument raises a separate interlocutory-jurisdiction limitation.

**Material Record:** On April 17, 1997, police investigated eighteen-year-old Marcus Wayman and a seventeen-year-old companion in a parked vehicle near a closed business. There was no evidence of a burglary. They were arrested for underage drinking. Whether they admitted homosexual activity is disputed. At the station, Wilinsky allegedly instructed Wayman to disclose his sexual orientation to his grandfather or the officer would do so. The officer also delivered a biblical admonition. Nor does rejecting immunity establish that the threat legally caused the death or establish municipal liability. Actual disclosure by the officer did not occur; the distinction between a threat of future wrongdoing and present compelled self-disclosure is central. The adult's privacy claim must not be decided as though police were notifying a minor's guardian of an arrest.

### Issue 1: Constitutional confidentiality and coercion

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County establishes a seven-Justice holding protecting objectively intimate marital and family information against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination when entrusted for a limited criminal investigation and disclosed without sufficient governmental justification. Consent to investigative use is not consent to publication. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority is also a simulated Supreme Court holding: five Justices assumed without deciding a medical-confidentiality interest and sustained the confined, benefits-related internal use before them. That decision neither requires public dissemination for every possible claim nor authorizes unrelated use. Shahar v. Bowers protects actual intimate association against unsupported employment penalties, on distinct predicates. None squarely decides this coercive family-disclosure ultimatum.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The current intimate-information standard preserves the confined protection established in Sheets v. Salt Lake County. Objectively intimate information and a sufficient official justification matter; confidentiality promises, official possession, subjective preference, or reputation alone do not establish a constitutional claim. No universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means, or seven-factor formula has been adopted. Each claim still requires the relevant governmental intrusion, personal responsibility, causation, and injury. Legitimate investigative use does not itself authorize unrelated disclosure.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment supplies the asserted constitutional confidentiality protection and section 1983 the asserted civil remedy. Whalen v. Roe and Nixon v. Administrator of General Services supply the inherited confidentiality background without resolving every disclosure demand.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp. and Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia supply Third Circuit confidentiality authorities. The earlier circuit opinion in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority remains relevant to what officers could know before this conduct; its broader balancing approach was not adopted by the later Supreme Court decision. Walls v. City of Petersburg supplies contrary Fourth Circuit authority concerning a police employment questionnaire about same-sex relations. Its setting and rationale require consideration. These circuit authorities are discussed in the [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies an actual-disclosure rule involving public dissemination and objectively intimate marital and family material. Extending that protection to an operative ultimatum about suspected orientation, directed toward one family member and without actual officer disclosure, requires an express doctrinal step. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority does not foreclose that step merely because disclosure did not occur, but its assumed-right disposition does not decide it. Neither case establishes liability for every offensive remark or a free-standing autonomy guarantee.

**Open Point:** Whether this official ultimatum constitutes a present invasion of protected confidentiality about sexual orientation, and what governmental justification can authorize it, remains unresolved by the cited precedents.

### Issue 2: Qualified immunity and appellate jurisdiction

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier permit separable legal immunity review on properly assumed facts and exclude evidentiary-sufficiency appeals. Hunter v. Bryant requires judicial resolution where the supported record permits, without replacing disputed facts. Elder v. Holloway requires consideration of all relevant conduct-date precedent. Wilson v. Layne preserves particularized fair notice, without an invariably identical-case requirement.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Notice is measured on April 17, 1997. Sheets v. Salt Lake County had already taken effect on April 29, 1996 and therefore belongs in the notice analysis, with its actual-publication limits intact. The Supreme Court decision in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority took effect on May 5, 1997 and cannot supply earlier notice; its earlier circuit opinion is a distinct conduct-date source. Shahar v. Bowers and Wilson v. Layne likewise supply current instructions rather than earlier notice. Subjective awareness alone is not the objective immunity test. No automatic merits-first sequence governs this case.

**Other Governing Law:** The individual damages claim under section 1983 remains subject to objective qualified immunity. The inherited particularity requirement discussed in Anderson v. Creighton requires analysis of the right in the concrete official circumstances, while the interlocutory posture fixes the supported plaintiff-favorable factual assumptions.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp. and Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia predate the conduct. The Third Circuit's earlier opinion in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority was issued in 1995 and is a relevant local notice source, separately from the simulated Supreme Court's subsequent assumed-right application. Walls v. City of Petersburg also predates the conduct and weighs against treating orientation confidentiality as uniformly settled. The dissent's cited threat cases add a separate concern: a threatened violation is not invariably a completed constitutional injury. Later Gruenke v. Seip, Bartnicki v. Vopper, Bloch v. Ribar, and Powell v. Schriver cannot establish April 1997 notice. [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The objective conduct-date standard and distinction between legal and factual appellate issues are settled. Sheets v. ## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Valentine.

**Citation or Docket:** 232 F.3d 350; Third Circuit No. 00-1425. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; disposition on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** May 14, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term.

**Question Presented:**

1. Whether an unidentified informant's face-to-face report of recently observing a handgun, combined with the suspect's presence in a high-crime area late at night and departure upon seeing police, supplies reasonable suspicion for an investigative stop?
2. Whether briefly stopping and giving one's name in response to a police command constitutes submission to authority under the Fourth Amendment when the person subsequently attempts to flee?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses both questions presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed suppression of a handgun and remanded the federal firearm prosecution.

**Relief Requested:** The Government seeks admission based on suspicion before the command or independent conduct before any seizure. Valentine seeks restoration of suppression or reconsideration under a properly identified seizure time. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Material Record:** Around 1 a.m. on May 8, 1999, a man flagged down two uniformed Irvington officers in a marked car. He said he had just seen a nearby man carrying a gun and described his clothes, beard, gold chain and companion. The informant refused to identify himself. Fifty to one hundred feet away, officers saw Valentine, who matched, with two others. The location was described in uncontradicted testimony as having many shootings. The men began walking away. An officer told Valentine to come over and place his hands on the car. According to the officer's account, Valentine asked “Who, me?”, charged toward him and attempted to push past. The officer grabbed him and wrestled him down; a loaded handgun fell during the struggle. No officer saw the gun beforehand. Valentine asserted that he first stopped and gave his name. The appellate court found no evidence supporting that account but also held it legally insufficient even if accepted. [United States v. The informant's recent observation, personal presence and refusal to identify himself have different implications for knowledge and credibility. A reliable report of possession does not alone establish unlawful possession. The Government separately invoked New Jersey's permit requirements and possession presumption; the Third Circuit expressly did not decide that argument. Later struggle and gun discovery cannot justify an earlier completed seizure.

### Issue 1: Reasonable suspicion from the report and encounter

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Illinois v. Wardlow unanimously preserves objective, particularized suspicion under all circumstances and rejects location alone or a universal flight rule. Its five-Justice application holds the actual look-and-flight record insufficient. Florida v. J. L. unanimously rejects a forcible stop resting on an unaccountable gun accusation corroborated only as to observable identity and location. Ordinary firearm danger supplies no exception. Accountable eyewitness reports and other concrete reliability assurances remain open.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Individualized suspicion of wrongdoing is required for an investigative seizure. Local experience and circumstances may inform that assessment but cannot establish an observation absent from the record. Neither permission to ask consensual questions nor the presence of a gun automatically permits a seizure. A protective search requires its own lawful predicates.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and the inherited Terry v. Ohio framework govern the stop and any protective search. The Government also invokes New Jersey permit requirements and a possession presumption as a distinct argument; the lower court expressly did not decide that argument, and its predicates cannot be assumed.

**Material Other Authority:** Ornelas v. United States requires independent review of constitutional sufficiency while protecting historical findings and giving genuine weight to grounded local experience. The historical Third Circuit's categorical account of flight conflicts with the narrower governing holding in Illinois v. Wardlow. The firearm-right standards likewise preclude assuming that all possession is criminal, without themselves deciding the validity of this permitting law.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The settled totality rule governs this encounter. The Court has rejected reliance on identity corroboration, firearm danger, or location alone. Its existing applications do not resolve every recently observed, in-person report by a witness who refuses identification.

**Open Point:** The report's face-to-face delivery supplies a new reliability application, while the particular totality still requires proof of a connection to wrongdoing. The preserved permitting theory remains unadjudicated and may require further factual and legal development.

### Issue 2: Submission before later flight

**Controlling Simulated Law:** California v. Hodari D. preserves the distinction between physical force and actual submission; the cited precedents do not resolve this alleged brief compliance. Ornelas v. United States preserves the division between historical findings and independently reviewed constitutional sufficiency.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual seizure and the facts known at that moment. Later struggle or gun discovery cannot justify an already completed unsupported seizure. Conduct occurring before any seizure may contribute to its justification. Review of the legal effect of assumed facts does not authorize appellate invention of historical findings.

**Other Governing Law:** California v. Hodari D. supplies the inherited distinction between a show of authority and a seizure through physical force or actual submission. An unheeded command alone does not establish submission.

**Material Other Authority:** The lower court offered an alternative no-submission rationale even assuming Valentine's stop-and-name account, while also stating that the record did not support that account. Both aspects must remain separate. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Force and actual submission are recognized seizure mechanisms. Whether an actual stop and answer amount to submission cannot be decided simply by labeling the interval short; the later act's role depends on whether a seizure had already occurred.

**Open Point:** The categorical significance, if any, of subsequent flight after brief compliance requires resolution. Whether Valentine actually complied is a distinct record question for determination under the proper review allocation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Third Circuit emphasized a recent eyewitness account delivered in person, the opportunity to observe demeanor, proximity to the suspect, late hour, neighborhood shootings and departure. It treated these as sufficient before the command. Alternatively, it concluded that even a brief pause and name did not constitute realistic submission, allowing the later charge toward the officer to support suspicion. It did not rely on the gun's later discovery to justify the initial command. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Government's Strongest Position:** A person who approaches police within sight of a nearby suspect takes more personal risk than a remote caller. Immediate eyewitness knowledge, detailed description, prompt matching and contextual danger warrant a short investigation before violence occurs. Even if initial suspicion was insufficient, an unanswered command did not seize Valentine; his attempt to rush past and push the officer supplied new grounds before physical restraint. A real pause must be distinguished from feigned or equivocal compliance.

**Valentine's Strongest Position:** The witness deliberately retained anonymity, supplied no observed offense beyond possession, and was not shown to be accountable. Late presence and ordinary walking away do not identify criminal behavior. A person who actually yields to a command and answers is seized during that interval; later escape cannot retroactively erase that seizure. A minimum-duration condition would let officers use the results of an unsupported detention to justify its beginning.

**Limits on Both Accounts:** The record does not support inventing a reliable informant history, witnessed threat, unlawful transfer, identifiable permit violation, or a finding of continuous compliance. Conversely, subsequent flight is not legally irrelevant if no seizure preceded it. The informant's possible exposure to retaliation is a contextual inference, not proof of veracity.

If initial suspicion independently suffices, the second question may be unnecessary to the judgment, but the lower court's alternative legal rule must be treated explicitly.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance permits further prosecution with the suppression reversal intact. Vacatur can require renewed analysis of the initial basis and seizure point. If an unsupported completed seizure is established, analyze the challenged evidence and any preserved independent grounds under ordinary suppression principles; do not automatically order acquittal or dismissal. The firearm's eventual recovery does not establish the antecedent justification.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The disputed stop-and-name account and the unadjudicated permitting argument counsel against a final finding that every lawful path to admission fails. No universal rule about unnamed in-person witnesses or departure from police is warranted.

**Petitioners and United States:** A person who knowingly distributes an unlawful recording compounds an invasion of private conversation even without causing the original tap.
