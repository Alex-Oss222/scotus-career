### Issue 2: Actual damages and mitigation

* **Other Governing Law:** Carey v. Piphus supplies the received distinction between actual injury and nominal damages for a denial of procedural due process. The applicable evidentiary and mitigation rules remain necessary to any recovery.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Nominal damages may remain available without proved consequential loss. Conversely, uncertainty in the amount of a loss is distinct from absence of evidence that a compensable loss occurred.

# Denius v. Dunlap

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Denius v. Dunlap
* **Citation or Docket:** 209 F.3d 944
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-17 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).
* **Question Presented:** 
  1. Whether a public employer violates the First Amendment by conditioning continued employment on an employee's authorization to disclose confidential attorney-client communications without a sufficient employment-related justification?
  2. Whether the Fourteenth Amendment protects an employee against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to confidential medical and financial records as a condition of continued employment?
* **Question Granted:** Review is limited to the questions stated above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed qualified immunity concerning financial information, reversed the district court’s immunity judgment concerning attorney communications and medical information, and remanded.
* **Relief Requested:** Denius seeks declaratory, injunctive and monetary relief for conditioning contract renewal on the authorization. Dunlap seeks judgment based on lawful employment screening or qualified immunity.
* **Material Record:** Illinois administered the federally funded Lincoln Challenge Program for adolescent school dropouts. Denius taught ordinary academic subjects. In 1996, continued employment was conditioned on a broad authorization covering medical, financial and other records and attorney communications concerning matters in which he had an interest, together with a release. He refused to sign the authorization and lost the position. Dunlap described routine background-screening intentions, but the written terms were broader and the stated limitations were not adequately communicated. The appeal arose from summary judgment, not a completed trial establishing every justification or consequential injury. [Denius v. Dunlap](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/209/944/474129/).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The State’s role as employer establishes governmental action, but the rights invoked remain distinct. Confidentiality of legal consultation is not equivalent to a Sixth Amendment right in every civil relationship. Denius challenges a compelled authorization; actual later public dissemination is not necessary to describe that alleged burden. Damages immunity must be tested against law at the 1996 conduct date, not this 2001 event.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.

### Issue 1: Access to confidential legal consultation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** NAACP v. Button and Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen v. Virginia protect lawful association and access to legal assistance in their settings. They do not constitutionalize every evidentiary privilege. Existing public-employment principles do not permit an employer to impose unrelated burdens on independent protected activity merely because renewal is discretionary.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First Amendment speech and association principles require identification of protected private activity and an actual employment justification. Native privilege law and constitutional association must be distinguished.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Denius v. Dunlap](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/209/944/474129/).
* **Material Other Authority:** Pickering v. Board of Education and Connick v. Myers concern employment-related speech restrictions; their application cannot assume this demand involves speech assigned to the teacher.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No controlling decision makes all attorney-client records immune from lawful investigation or every privilege dispute a constitutional claim.
* **Open Point:** Whether this unrestricted employment condition materially burdens independent legal consultation without a sufficient job-related reason.

### Issue 2: Medical and financial confidentiality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority assumes without deciding a constitutional medical-confidentiality right and sustains limited benefits-related access on its facts. Sheets v. Salt Lake County concerns intentional public dissemination. Neither supplies an already established unlimited acquisition right.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Medical-confidentiality analysis preserves the assumed-right posture and considers purpose, sensitivity, safeguards, and actual governmental use. Acquisition, internal use and dissemination require their own analysis.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment claim concerns compelled access to specified confidential records, not a free-standing right against all background screening.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Seventh Circuit’s existing medical-confidentiality decisions are relevant to objective notice in that jurisdiction, but they do not establish a Supreme Court holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority postdates the challenged 1996 conduct and cannot retroactively clearly establish liability. Financial privacy has a separate notice and scope problem.
* **Open Point:** Whether to recognize a bounded constitutional protection against unrestricted medical or financial demands, and what law gave this official notice when he acted.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Teacher:** Unlimited access to legal, medical and financial confidences is unrelated to classroom duties and chills consultation and private decision making. An undisclosed benign intention does not narrow the signed authorization. **Employer:** Reliable staff screening serves the program’s responsibilities; constitutional confidentiality is uncertain, and privilege can be waived. Even a defective demand does not establish clear 1996 notice for damages. **Necessary response:** A screening rationale must explain these categories and breadth, while a constitutional merits conclusion must not silently decide immunity.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may recognize and apply a confined protection to the unjustified demand while remanding the distinct immunity questions. It may resolve the legal-consultation burden separately and retain the assumed-right approach for information claims. A judgment treating every confidential record identically would erase material differences in protected activity, sensitivity and conduct-date authority.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Unrestricted authorizations imposed as an employment condition without demonstrated relation to actual screening needs; lawful tailored inquiries and claim-specific immunity remain open.

**Material Uncertainty:** Controlling precedent does not establish the full scope of the asserted protection against acquisition of medical records or whether that protection was clearly established in 1996. Financial confidentiality presents an additional unresolved question. These legal uncertainties do not establish disputed facts for either party.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Button and Trainmen’s protection of obtaining legal assistance to this compulsory authorization, preserving lawful investigation and native privilege rules.

### Issue 2: Intimate medical and financial information

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The issue determines the challenged judgment and is properly presented on this record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the Fourteenth Amendment protect against an unrestricted employment demand for medical and financial records?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a bounded acquisition protection; Doe v. SEPTA assumed rather than decided the medical right, and Sheets addressed dissemination.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Yes as to objectively intimate medical information and comparably intimate material in financial records. Compulsory access requires an actual legitimate governmental need and scope, use, and safeguards reasonably fitted to that need. Ordinary nonintimate financial information does not become constitutionally protected solely because its owner regards it as confidential.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The employer must justify the actual demand. A fitness inquiry may support relevant medical verification, but not access to all treatment, family affairs, or unrelated intimate information.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Acquisition itself exposes private life to official power, even before publication. Doe’s confined benefits access therefore differs materially from this unrestricted release. The content and sensitivity of information, explanation of the actual job-related need, access and use restrictions, and feasible confinement of the request inform the balancing inquiry; no universal strict-scrutiny rule or checklist applies. Written terms, testimony, records, and ordinary inferences may establish those matters without statistical proof. The constitutional protection of intimate life is decisive, while the actual purpose and safeguards determine whether access is justified. The court must identify the financial information implicated and decide its intimacy under this rule, not leave the legal right unresolved.

### Issue 3: Individual damages defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The issue determines the challenged judgment and is properly presented on this record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does uncertainty in prior case law alone bar recovery on a proved violation?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Replace the broader clearly-established-law defense preserved by Elder and Crawford-El with a narrow defense of reasonable reliance on authoritative legal permission; do not misdescribe that change as existing law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No. Defendants must establish genuine, objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged demand. Legal silence and routine employer authority do not suffice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The defense concerns personal damages only. It neither supplies liability nor affects a separately authorized live prospective remedy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A remedy for a proved deprivation should not depend on finding a prior case with the same form of intrusive authorization. Constitutional justification and reasonable factual judgment belong in the merits analysis; repeating them as an additional immunity defeats accountability without protecting distinct reliance. Actual authoritative permission receives protection and limits unfair surprise. Stone candidly revises the defense rather than attributing the new acquisition holding to the law known in 1996. The identified record establishes no binding permission for the unrestricted demand. Remand permits adjudication of a properly supported reliance defense and genuine factual disputes.

# Urofsky v. Gilmore

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Urofsky v. Gilmore
* **Citation or Docket:** 216 F.3d 401
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-20 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).
* **Question Presented:** 
  1. Whether the First Amendment permits a State to prohibit its employees from accessing constitutionally protected sexually explicit material on state-owned computers unless they obtain advance approval for a bona fide work-related purpose?
  2. Whether public-university professors possess an individual First Amendment right of academic freedom that protects their teaching and research against that approval requirement?
* **Question Granted:** Review is limited to the questions stated above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Fourth Circuit reversed summary judgment for six public-college and university professors. It treated the regulated access as employee activity rather than protected citizen speech and rejected the asserted individual academic-freedom entitlement.
* **Relief Requested:** The professors seek declaratory and prospective relief against the statutory restriction, broadly for state employees or, alternatively, for academic teaching and research. The Commonwealth seeks a judgment sustaining its control of equipment and official work.
* **Material Record:** Virginia prohibits employees from using state-owned or leased computers to access, download, print or store sexually explicit content except as required for a bona fide agency-approved research project or undertaking. Approval must be written by an agency head and is available under Virginia’s public-records law. After the original district and panel decisions, the definition was narrowed to material whose dominant theme is a lascivious description or depiction of specified sexual subjects. It still reaches some constitutionally protected material. Plaintiffs are six professors at public institutions. They challenge work-related access, expressly disclaiming a right to personal use of state equipment. None requested and was denied permission; the record identifies no denied request. Their objection includes the prospective approval requirement itself, particularly its effect on teaching and research. [Urofsky v. Gilmore](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/216/401/570332/).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A preenforcement challenge must establish an actual protected activity and present burden or credible enforcement threat. Failure to request approval is relevant to the record but does not by itself settle whether the approval condition is unconstitutional. Distinguish ordinary assigned agency output, independent scholarly inquiry, institutional curricular decisions and separate citizen speech. Public ownership of the computer is relevant to the employment relationship but cannot alone resolve every First Amendment claim.
* **Relevant Dates:** The en banc lower decision was June 23, 2000 and addressed the amended definition. The organizationally assigned date is February 20, 2001. No denied approval or later enforcement event is inferred.

### Issue 1: Official resources and protected employee expression

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education permits lawful institutional control of an assigned school production. It expressly adopts no universal rule for duty-related speech and preserves separate citizen expression and independent constitutional prohibitions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education requires an antecedent personal First Amendment interest. Delegated discretion alone does not establish one. Its express limits leave university academic freedom unresolved.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Urofsky v. Gilmore](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/216/401/570332/) define the burden actually challenged. Nonobscene material does not become unprotected obscenity merely because the statute calls it sexually explicit.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. National Treasury Employees Union requires justification commensurate with a broad prospective burden on protected employee and listener expression. Pickering v. Board of Education and Connick v. Myers require contextual examination; they do not establish personal control over every assigned work product.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The State can prescribe genuinely institutional work and regulate its resources, while remaining subject to independent constitutional limits. Neither the citizen-speech cases nor Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education conclusively classifies all academic inquiry.
* **Open Point:** Which covered activities implicate an individual protected interest, and whether the general agency approval process is justified for those activities.

### Issue 2: Individual academic inquiry and the institutional role

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision establishes that university academic freedom belongs exclusively to institutions or that every professor’s work-related activity is individually protected. Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education preserves Keyishian v. Board of Regents and Epperson v. Arkansas as independent limits on educational authority.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The current assigned-production rule cannot be expanded into a categorical university exception to the First Amendment. A claimed personal right must still be identified by the activity’s actual role and the challenged form of control.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment claim concerns public institutions and a statutory condition; contractual or tenure expectations may inform the academic setting but are not automatically constitutional rights.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sweezy v. New Hampshire and Keyishian v. Board of Regents identify constitutional concerns with political or ideological supervision of scholarship. Their particular holdings require careful use rather than attribution of a complete modern academic-freedom code.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing decisions leave the individual interest, its relationship to institutional judgment and the appropriate justification for this content-based condition open.
* **Open Point:** Whether independent teaching and research can receive individual protection against a general nonacademic approval requirement while preserving lawful institutional curricular and resource decisions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Commonwealth emphasizes ownership, workplace efficiency, avoidance of misuse and an approval route accommodating legitimate research. It notes the amended definition and the absence of any denied request. The professors answer that scholarly inquiry often requires encountering sexual material, that agency-head approval is not ordinary professional academic judgment, and that written public disclosure of proposed inquiry can burden protected work before any formal denial. Their broad employee claim must identify personal rights rather than equate every useful work activity with citizen speech. The State’s broad defense must explain why its ownership interest answers a claim concerning constitutionally protected inquiry rather than simply assuming the answer.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could uphold ordinary restrictions on agency work and resources while separately considering faculty research. It could reject a faculty entitlement entirely, but that would resolve an open constitutional question rather than merely apply the narrow school-production holding. Another path would recognize a bounded personal academic interest and remand for classification, present injury, justification and appropriately limited relief. The absence of an actual denial narrows facts available for adjudicating particular exclusions; it does not necessarily answer an attack on the mandatory approval condition itself.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** No general employee entitlement to unrestricted state-computer use. For university inquiry, identify the protected individual activity and test the actual content-based prior-approval burden, without constitutionalizing all employment policy or granting unrestricted control of institutional curriculum.

**Material Uncertainty:** No application establishes how an agency head would treat a particular scholarly request. The amended definition’s actual coverage, plaintiffs’ intended activities, the effect of public approval records and institutional allocation of academic responsibility require careful record treatment.

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the claim that protected content alone creates an unrestricted employee right to state computer use. Reverse the rejection of an individual academic-inquiry right and the approval of this compulsory condition as applied to the professors' independent scholarly work. Remand for relief confined to that established violation.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct a declaration and an injunction preventing enforcement of the challenged content-based prior-approval and associated public-disclosure condition against the plaintiffs' bona fide independent university teaching and research. The lower court must identify the covered scholarly activities and frame workable terms while preserving ordinary curriculum, professional, budget, resource-use, and anti-harassment rules. The existing record establishes standing and the constitutional defect; no denied application or new proof of abstract harm is required. Do not reinstate the district court's agency-wide employee injunction. No damages are awarded against the official-capacity defendant.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Ordinary assigned agency work

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Identify the speech interest before balancing. Plaintiffs disclaim personal recreational computer access; do not decide a claim they have not made.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does constitutionally protected content itself create a personal employee right to use government equipment for any work purpose?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between lawful institutional expression and personal protected expression. Reject an automatic right based solely on the material’s protected status or its usefulness to a job.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Act's approval mechanism may govern access undertaken to produce the State's own prescribed institutional work: protected content by itself supplies no individual entitlement to choose the materials or the use of that equipment. That answer does not extend to independent scholarly inquiry or other independently protected personal expression merely because a state computer is used.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This answers the broad employee theory while requiring distinct treatment of the academic claim expressly included in the grant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An employer may ordinarily define the work it commissions and prevent resource misuse. In that setting the Act regulates the production the State directs, so the material's lawful availability elsewhere does not establish a personal right to select it through the workplace channel. The distinction turns on who controls the message and task, not on a job title or a physical computer. The State's categorical defense fails when it treats the professors' independent inquiry as if it were assigned production; that different activity requires the separate answer below.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Personal recreational use is outside the claim presented and expressly disclaimed.

### Issue 2: Independent university teaching and research

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The professors have standing to challenge the mandatory condition. The record identifies an abandoned indecency-law research assignment, Meyers's planned study of sexually explicit Victorian poetry, a censored scholarly website, and curtailed sexuality research. These are concrete activities, not a generalized wish to disregard workplace rules. The amended lascivious-content definition still reaches protected materials within the intended inquiry, and the enforceable approval obligation produces a present burden traceable to the State and redressable by relief against that condition. No actual denial is required.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can a professor’s independent scholarly inquiry have individual constitutional protection against this general approval condition?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new bounded recognition of an individual interest in bona fide independent university inquiry. Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education leaves this question open.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bona fide independent university teaching and research carry an individual First Amendment interest. Under Pickering and National Treasury Employees Union, adapted to that scholarly function, the State must justify a prospective approval condition's actual burden and fit with its operational needs. This Act fails that standard as applied to the plaintiffs' independent inquiry: it conditions examination of a content category on discretionary approval and public disclosure without a sufficient connection to resource misuse or harassment. The amended definition and the absence of a denied request do not cure that defect.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects inquiry without creating a personal veto over lawful curriculum, professional standards, budgets or generally applicable resource controls. Neither occupational title nor delegated discretion alone decides the classification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Independent examination of sexual themes in literature and law contributes to scholarship rather than transmitting a prescribed government message. I distinguish Boring's assigned school production and extend Sweezy and Keyishian's protection against ideological control to that individual activity. The scholarly function is decisive in identifying the right; Pickering and NTEU then require a justified fit. Here the State's efficiency and harassment interests are legitimate, but requiring advance permission and public identification of bona fide research does not become an adequate response merely because the subject is sexual. The Act targets the content accessed through state equipment rather than distracting conduct or unwanted exposure of colleagues; genuinely work-related inquiry remains burdened. Delegation to academic administrators does not remove the discretionary content condition or explain the need for public approval records. Narrowing the definition to lascivious materials still includes protected material studied for independent scholarly purposes. The actual research plans, changed teaching activity, statutory terms, and ordinary inferences establish the burden without statistics. A history without denied applications addresses eventual permission, not the compulsory condition itself. The State has not justified that condition for this inquiry, so relief follows now; ordinary controls on misuse and harassment remain available.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A particular denied request and personal recreational computer use are outside this record. Individual disputes over whether an activity is bona fide independent scholarship, rather than prescribed institutional production or resource misuse, are governed by the stated functional distinction and require their own facts; no component of the challenged approval condition is left undecided.
