## Standards and Tests selection: Enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees

### Enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees

**Governing question:** What instructions govern enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees?

**Current rule:** Enforcement of an established water-decree right requires no new injury showing; the decree, original opinion, report, record and relevant course of conduct may identify the right. New allocation or modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury. Rule 17.2 uses Rule 56 and its precedents as guides: no genuine material factual dispute and entitlement as law are required. A nonmovant with the ultimate burden must sufficiently support essential elements under the applicable enforcement or clear-and-convincing modification standard. This is distinct from Rufo's institutional-consent-decree framework and creates no general transfer of water rights through silence. A supported allegation of upstream groundwater depletion or changed federal storage administration may proceed only insofar as it materially threatens the decree’s allocation, operation or supporting conditions. Downstream nonirrigation and habitat evidence may be relevant to injury and lawful modification without creating an independent environmental entitlement or priority. Location below the decree’s principal diversion point alone does not foreclose a material decree connection. A State’s own pumping is no categorical unclean-hands defense without relevant conduct and a connection to the asserted harm; independent federal contractual compliance belongs in its lawful separate channel.

## Standards and Tests selection: One-year state-custody habeas filing period

### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Time during which a properly filed application for state postconviction or other collateral review of the pertinent judgment or claim is pending is excluded.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

## Standards and Tests selection: Materiality in financial-deception fraud schemes

### Materiality in financial-deception fraud schemes

**Governing question:** Whether mail, wire and bank fraud require material representations and what materiality means.

**Current rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property financial-deception schemes under §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In transaction context, a representation is material when it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence its targeted decision. Successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss and status as the sole reason for the transaction are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains separate and may execute the scheme without itself containing the falsehood.

**Authority by component:**

- Neder v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes”, 1999-06-10; Thomas Part I, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Neder, June 10, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Both §1344 routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes decided; other differences, omission duties, nonrepresentational schemes and unusual recipient preferences remain open. Section 1014’s different Wells construction remains, and purpose to influence under that section is not automatically fraud materiality.

**Holdings navigation:** [Neder v. United States — I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes](HOLDINGS.md#i-materiality-in-the-charged-mail-wire-and-bank-fraud-schemes-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

### Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

**Governing question:** What instructions govern territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury?

**Current rule:** Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial district design, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the design-only equal-protection claim without racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or another independently cognizable constitutional deprivation. This substantive boundary is not heightened pleading, a universal downstream-loss requirement or an additional injury element for independent statutory claims. Other claims retain their own elements; no predominance scrutiny rule or general replacement for Adarand's common standard follows. This remains so when race actually predominates and traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status or participation injury can be complete in government’s own unequal treatment without a second governmental act, monetary loss or later electoral defeat; shared-electorate composition alone does not establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race. Absence of a statutory command or prior adjudication requiring a particular district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. Shape is evidence, not a necessary gateway or sufficient proof. Purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal consequences remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Personal districting exposure and direct apportionment review

### Personal districting exposure and direct apportionment review

**Governing question:** Standing and the statutory appeal route for a substantial congressional-districting challenge.

**Current rule:** Voters personally subjected to the challenged electoral assignment may litigate asserted unequal treatment when their own exposure, causation and redressability are established, even if their substantive theory fails. Residence alone does not automatically establish standing. A substantial constitutional apportionment challenge adjudicated by the required three-judge district court through a merits denial of injunctive relief falls within §§2284 and 1253.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground](HOLDINGS.md#a-court-may-choose-a-straightforward-nonmerits-jurisdictional-ground-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Public-employee speech and the employer

### Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

**Current rule:** An employer acting on a genuine, reasonable account of speech that would be disciplinable under the governing public-employee-speech rules is protected even if that factual account is mistaken. Conversely, an unreasonable and incorrect account does not defeat a claim when the employee actually spoke protected speech and that speech caused the adverse action. The employee must show protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may establish by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech. These limited propositions do not impose liability for every investigation defect or adopt the plurality’s complete investigative procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- Seven-Justice genuine-and-reasonable-account safe harbor: Waters v. Churchill — A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor, 1994-05-31. O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.
- Six-Justice unreasonable-and-false-account limit: Waters v. Churchill — An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech, 1994-05-31. O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.
- Motive and same-decision proof: Waters v. Churchill — The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment, 1994-05-31. O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Present operation effective:** Waters v. Churchill, 1994-05-31.

**Open question:** Unreasonable belief where the actual speech was unprotected, and a universal investigation checklist, are not resolved. The four-Justice complete reasonableness framework is noncontrolling.

**Holdings navigation:** A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor; An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech; The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

### Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

**Governing question:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause despite another investigative purpose.

**Current rule:** Objective probable cause of a traffic violation supports an ordinary initial stop notwithstanding an unrelated investigative aim. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce that violation absent that aim, or on compliance with customary assignment and enforcement policies. Manner, scope and duration remain independently constrained: a materially greater detention or search requires independent grounds. This rule does not remove purpose-specific conditions from inventory and administrative exceptions; inventories retain standardized criteria or established routine and genuine inventory purposes rather than investigatory rummaging. Purposeful racial selection remains independently prohibited by Equal Protection.

## Standards and Tests selection: Substantial special need for suspicionless candidate drug testing

### Substantial special need for suspicionless candidate drug testing

**Governing question:** Fourth Amendment justification for compelled drug testing as a candidacy condition.

**Current rule:** Compelled urine collection and chemical analysis are searches, and candidacy does not waive bodily privacy. A suspicionless program justified by a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual governmental need, program operation and affected privacy. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not supply the substantial, concrete need required for broad advance-scheduled candidate testing; reduced intrusion or public prominence alone cannot compensate for that deficiency.

**Authority by component:**

- Chandler v. Miller — Candidacy does not waive bodily privacy, 1997-04-15; unanimous Opinion of the Court, holding 1, supplies the no-waiver boundary
- Chandler v. Miller — Georgia has not supplied a special need, 1997-04-15; unanimous holding 2 supplies the concrete-need requirement at this program scope

**Present operation effective:** Chandler, 1997-04-15.

**Exceptions and limits:** Materially different programs retain contextual special-needs review. No universal proof-of-catastrophe, numeric efficacy requirement or exhaustion of all alternatives is imposed.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exploratory tactile examination of closed personal luggage

### Exploratory tactile examination of closed personal luggage

**Governing question:** When touching luggage stored in a shared overhead area becomes a Fourth Amendment search.

**Current rule:** Ordinary shared storage exposes closed personal luggage to movement, incidental contact and associated compression, but not to an added physical examination revealing the shape and density of concealed contents. Objectively exploratory squeezing to acquire that information is a search even without opening the bag. The character of the examination, rather than undisclosed motive or touching alone, governs.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected contents and objectively exploratory contact: Bond v. United States — Exploratory touch can search closed luggage without opening it, April 17, 2000; Souter joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** Bond, April 17, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision prohibits neither ordinary incidental touching nor all compression and creates no new warrant exception or subjective-purpose test.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals

### Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals

**Governing question:** Whether rejection of a private settlement defense permits an immediate collateral-order appeal.

**Current rule:** Ordinary private settlement rights do not have the public importance required for immediate appeal under the collateral-order doctrine. Independently, final-judgment review adequately vindicates this category of contractual rights despite the expense of the intervening litigation. Describing the bargain as a right not to stand trial does not establish appealability. These independently sufficient limits govern the category presented, not every conceivable private agreement; other authorized interlocutory routes, including §1292 certification when its conditions are met, remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- Nine-Justice importance ground: Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc — A private settlement does not supply the required collateral-order importance, 1994-06-06. Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for affirmance.
- Eight-Justice independent adequate-final-review ground: Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc — Independently, final review adequately vindicates this category of settlement rights, 1994-06-06. Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II.

**Present operation effective:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc, 1994-06-06.

**Holdings navigation:** A private settlement does not supply the required collateral-order importance.

## Standards and Tests selection: Final congressional selection on the federal election day

### Final congressional selection on the federal election day

**Governing question:** When a state congressional-election procedure conflicts with then-operative 2 U.S.C. §§1 and 7.

**Current rule:** An election is complete when the legal selection is consummated. A State cannot make an October congressional majority result the final legal choice, leaving no act of selection for the federal November election day, merely by calling the October contest a primary. Compatible preliminary proceedings may precede the federal day. Section 8’s vacancy and failure-to-elect provisions do not treat a successful final October majority as a failure to elect.

**Authority by component:**

- Final October selection and the federal election day: Foster v. Love, 1997-12-02; Ginsburg for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule supplies no comprehensive code for preliminary steps or later runoffs and decides no presidential timing question. Relief must displace the conflicting final-selection feature while preserving compatible state administration and ballot choices.

**Holdings navigation:** Foster v. Love — Federal election timing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Election guidance, authoritative construction and neutral approval thresholds

### Election guidance, authoritative construction and neutral approval thresholds

**Governing question:** Due process and compelled-speech objections when an election provision is authoritatively construed after officials supplied contrary guidance.

**Current rule:** A neutral judicial construction of an ambiguous election provision does not violate due process merely because officials previously issued a contrary advisory interpretation. The actual official inducement, dissemination, reliance and voting circumstances must be examined for fundamental unfairness; a legally possible construction does not by itself answer a supported inducement claim. A neutral referendum threshold may include blank ballots in the approval denominator without compelling political opposition when blanks remain accurately counted and reported as blanks and the rule does not discriminate among identifiable voters. The legal consequence of abstention is distinct from falsely attributing a message.

**Authority by component:**

- Fundamental-fairness inquiry: Canonical Decision Record `citizens_for_a_constitutional_convention_v_yoshina_merits_1999-01-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The record does not establish fundamental unfairness, 1999-01-21; unanimous Part II.
- Neutral denominator and no compelled attribution: Canonical Decision Record `citizens_for_a_constitutional_convention_v_yoshina_merits_1999-01-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. A denominator rule does not compel political speech, 1999-01-21; eight Associate joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-01-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Deliberate misleading directions, pervasive frustration of an effective choice and false attribution of views remain distinct questions. Federal relief does not authorize replacement of authoritative state law merely because informal advice differed.

**Holdings navigation:** [Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina — III. A denominator rule does not compel political speech](HOLDINGS.md#iii-a-denominator-rule-does-not-compel-political-speech-1); [Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina — II. ## Standards and Tests selection: Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit

### Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit

**Governing question:** What justifies direct candidate-contribution limits and differentiated source allowances.

**Current rule:** Candidate contribution limits must be closely drawn to a sufficiently important interest in preventing corruption or its appearance while preserving meaningful political association. No completed bribe is necessary, and equality of political influence is not an independent justification. Amount and inflation comparisons establish no permanent constitutional boundary; actual campaign scale, fundraising burden and cumulative restrictions matter. Under the adjudicated Arkansas classification, a registered committee receiving contributions exclusively from individuals and no more than $25 cumulatively from any individual during a calendar year may receive a $2,500 per-candidate, per-election contribution allowance while ordinary sources face lower ceilings. The actual dispersed-donor structure and its anticorruption fit, rather than a small-donor label, support the distinction.

**Authority by component:**

- Direct-candidate interest and close fit: Russell v. Burris — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Contribution limits require actual anticorruption fit; two candidate categories fail here”, April 28, 1999; O’Connor Part I, all nine Justices.
- Exact dispersed-donor classification: Russell — Holdings, “The exact small-donor qualification supports a different candidate allowance”, April 28, 1999; O’Connor Part IV with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Russell, April 28, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Office-specific dollar applications establish no general floor or safe harbor. Every small-donor qualification remains necessary to the adjudicated distinction; no unlimited candidate-giving right, immunity for sham structures or automatic validity of materially different classifications follows. A failed categorical challenge does not resolve a genuinely different office-specific application.

**Related current rules:** Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association separately governs committee receipt restrictions and actual candidate-conduit fit.

**Holdings navigation:** [Russell v. Burris — Contribution limits require actual anticorruption fit; two candidate categories fail here](HOLDINGS.md#contribution-limits-require-actual-anticorruption-fit-two-candidate-categories-fail-here-1); [Russell v. Burris — The exact small-donor qualification supports a different candidate allowance](HOLDINGS.md#the-exact-small-donor-qualification-supports-a-different-candidate-allowance).

## Standards and Tests selection: Support-sensitive public campaign financing

### Support-sensitive public campaign financing

**Governing question:** Whether competing parties must receive equal public presidential-campaign subsidies.

**Current rule:** The Constitution does not require equal subsidies merely because parties compete or qualify for public financing. A formula tied to prior electoral support and inability to match larger parties’ resources do not alone establish the materially different practical suppression or discriminatory administration needed for a distinct constitutional challenge. Inability to close that subsidy gap under an individual-candidate contribution limit does not alone establish an impairment different from the rejected parity claim; it supplies no permanent dollar safe harbor. Concrete exclusionary administration, viewpoint favoritism and materially different cumulative or prohibitory burdens require their own analysis. A party need not disappear or cease fielding candidates before challenging a substantially suppressive application.

**Authority by component:**

- National Committee of the Reform Party v. Democratic National Committee — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “I. Demonstrated electoral support does not create a duty to equalize subsidies”, 1999-03-17; Thomas Parts I–III, all nine Justices. Part I supplies subsidy treatment; Part II, “The actual contribution-limit argument requires its own narrow answer”, supplies the separate contribution-gap boundary.

**Present operation effective:** Reform Party, March 17, 1999.

**Holdings navigation:** [National Committee of the Reform Party of the United States of America v. Democratic National Committee — I. Demonstrated electoral support does not create a duty to equalize subsidies](HOLDINGS.md#i-demonstrated-electoral-support-does-not-create-a-duty-to-equalize-subsidies-1); [National Committee of the Reform Party of the United States of America v. Democratic National Committee — II. The actual contribution-limit argument requires its own narrow answer](HOLDINGS.md#ii-the-actual-contribution-limit-argument-requires-its-own-narrow-answer).

## Standards and Tests selection: Candidate financing, political-committee thresholds and membership exclusions

### Candidate financing, political-committee thresholds and membership exclusions

**Governing question:** Whether predominantly issue-oriented groups are exempt from FECA political-committee duties for otherwise covered candidate financing.

**Current rule:** Buckley and MCFL supply no categorical organization-wide major-purpose exemption for genuinely covered candidate financing. Section 431(4)(A) reaches a committee, club, association or other group receiving contributions aggregating over $1,000 in a calendar year or making expenditures aggregating over $1,000 in a calendar year, subject to actual definitions, exclusions and constitutional limits. Establish membership, covered transactions, actual candidate connection and thresholds before imposing duties. Where a contribution theory requires cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion, ideological alignment, lobbying or partisan identity alone is insufficient. Independent expression retains its protection.

**Authority by component:**
- No categorical major-purpose exemption: Akins, Akins v. FEC — III. Major purpose is not a categorical exemption for otherwise covered candidate financing, Souter Part III with Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer (five for both candidate-cash/conduit and broader covered candidate-financing subparts; Kennedy joins only the former), 1997-06-25.
- Distinct exclusions and reporting conditions: Akins, Akins v. FEC — IV. Membership exclusions and their distinct reporting proviso govern the remand, seven-Justice Part IV, 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** Akins, 1997-06-25.

**Controlling construction:** Section 431(9)(B)(iii) excludes membership-organization or corporate communications to members, stockholders, or executive or administrative personnel when the organization is not primarily organized to influence federal nomination or election. Its separate proviso requires reporting direct costs of communications expressly advocating a clearly identified candidate's election or defeat when costs exceed $2,000 per election, except communications primarily about other subjects, under §434(a)(4)(A)(i) and, for a general election, (ii). Section 441b(b)(2) separately excludes corporate communications on any subject to stockholders and executive or administrative personnel and their families, and labor communications to members and their families; its nonpartisan registration/get-out-the-vote exclusion covers those respective recipients. Its separate-fund exclusion covers establishment, administration and solicitation for a political separate segregated fund by a corporation, labor or membership organization, cooperative or corporation without capital stock. Its loan exception concerns ordinary-course national or State bank loans under banking law and regulations. Sections 431(8)(B)(vi) and (9)(B)(v) cross-reference activity allowed by §441b without importing the $2,000 proviso.

**Open question:** Particular organizational or donor-disclosure burdens retain supported constitutional challenges. The construction supplies no automatic membership, transaction, threshold or liability finding.

**Holdings navigation:** Akins v. FEC (1997-06-25) — III. Major purpose is not a categorical exemption for otherwise covered candidate financing; Akins v. FEC (1997-06-25) — IV. Membership exclusions and their distinct reporting proviso govern the remand.

## Standards and Tests selection: Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association

### Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association

**Governing question:** Fit of limits on contributions received by political committees and political funds.

**Current rule:** Contributions remain distinct from independent expenditures. A broadly restrictive ceiling on contributions received by political committees or funds must preserve meaningful collective political association and fit an identified corruption or circumvention mechanism. Realistic routes around valid candidate limits may be regulated; an insufficiently fitted ceiling cannot be sustained merely by invoking circumvention. The invalidation of the reviewed $100 aggregate annual ceiling establishes no permanent constitutional dollar minimum. Annual duration, actual recipient function, per-recipient versus aggregate operation, amount and demonstrated effect on collective advocacy must be considered together. Other available committees and aggregate political success do not alone cure a burden on the chosen association.

**Authority by component:**

- This $100 intermediary-contribution ceiling lacks sufficient fit: This $100 intermediary-contribution ceiling lacks sufficient fit, 1995-05-15. Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes, at this stated functional application.

- Russell v. Burris — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “The ordinary-PAC receipt limit survives on this operational record”, 1999-04-28; O’Connor Parts II–IV with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III supplies the actual-channel and operational considerations.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-15; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The reviewed Day recipient class and its exclusions remain as originally stated. Russell supplies no general approval of a $200 intermediary receipt ceiling, no permanent dollar floor, no immunity for sham or materially different structures and no unlimited candidate-contribution authority. Genuine independence and actual candidate-conduit status retain their separate requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** This $100 intermediary-contribution ceiling lacks sufficient fit. [Russell v. Burris — The ordinary-PAC receipt limit survives on this operational record](HOLDINGS.md#the-ordinary-pac-receipt-limit-survives-on-this-operational-record).

**Current wording authority:** Russell, April 28, 1999, applies Day’s meaningful-pooling and actual-fit rule to a demonstrated per-recipient candidate-conduit setting without adopting a general dollar safe harbor.

**Related current rules:** Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit states the distinct direct-candidate and exact dispersed-donor classification rules.

## Standards and Tests selection: Special statutory tribunals for election-financing challenges

### Special statutory tribunals for election-financing challenges

**Governing question:** When a constitutional election-financing claim must enter FECA’s or the Fund Act’s special statutory tribunal.

**Current rule:** A claim lacking Article III standing or presenting a constitutional theory plainly foreclosed by controlling law may be rejected before invoking the extraordinary statutory tribunal. A live substantial constitutional question cannot be bypassed because the court expects it to fail. A substantial qualifying FECA constitutional question brought by an authorized plaintiff follows §437h to the court of appeals sitting en banc. A substantial Fund Act implementation or construction claim follows §9011(b) to a three-judge district court with direct Supreme Court appeal and no administrative-exhaustion requirement. Section 9011(a)’s thirty-day D.C. Circuit review of Commission action remains a separate route. Statutory eligibility to bring suit does not establish Article III standing on each claim.

**Authority by component:**

- National Committee of the Reform Party v. Democratic National Committee — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “IV. The granted claims supply no basis for bypassing or requiring a different statutory tribunal”, 1999-03-17; Thomas, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Reform Party, March 17, 1999.

## Standing State selection: 3. Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.
