#### Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, 503 U.S. 638 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-571.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether an untimely objection may defeat a scheduled exemption lacking a colorable statutory basis

**Holding:** Under 11 U.S.C. §522(l) and the applicable Bankruptcy Rule 4003(b), property claimed as exempt becomes exempt when no party in interest objects within 30 days after the creditors' meeting concludes or a relevant amendment to the exemption list is filed, whichever is later, and the court grants no further time within that period. The trustee cannot later contest the claimed exemption merely because it lacked a colorable statutory basis; this holding concerns the scheduled proceeds claimed here and does not decide a different claim's scope, an unscheduled asset, or an unpreserved fraud or §105(a) theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Eight participating Justices adopt the rule and its explanation at the same level of generality; no fractured-opinion analysis is needed.

**Material application:** Taylor knew of the lawsuit and its potentially substantial proceeds. Uncertainty about value allowed timely inquiry, objection or a request for extension; it did not suspend the deadline until collection. Rule 9006(b)(3) confines enlargement to Rule 4003(b)’s conditions; the August 1, 1991 supplemental-schedule wording does not reopen the expired period.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide §105(a), unpreserved fraud, unscheduled property, differently limited exemption claims, retroactivity, alternative valuation, liens or good faith. Rule 4003(c)’s burden on the objector remains applicable in a properly presented objection proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. Its reversal of the $23,483.75-plus-interest turnover order stands; the scheduled proceeds remain exempt against this untimely theory. No alternative recovery, sanction or new award is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235 — applied: a coherent Bankruptcy Code provision is enforced according to its terms; no general power to replace explicit procedure with an equitable exception follows from bankruptcy administration.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992 — consistent with its text/coexistence rule: substantive exemption eligibility and the procedure for contesting a claim perform different work. Its separate bankruptcy appellate-jurisdiction holding remains unchanged.
- In re Dembs, 757 F.2d 777, and In re Peterson, 920 F.2d 1389 — rejected insofar as their colorability requirement permits a late objection of this kind; that requirement cannot govern the §522(l) consequence decided here.

#### Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates Limited Partnership, 507 U.S. 380 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1695.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### Excusable neglect can include carelessness

**Holding:** For a Chapter 11 proof-of-claim deadline subject to Bankruptcy Rule 9006(b)(1), neglect includes inadvertence, mistake or carelessness as well as circumstances beyond the actor's control, but relief after expiration requires a motion and excusability. Excusability depends on all relevant circumstances, including prejudice to the debtor, length of delay and its effect on proceedings, the reason for delay including reasonable control, and good faith; this authority does not enlarge deadlines governed by Rule 9006(b)(2)'s prohibitions or (b)(3)'s specific-rule limitations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion for the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, April 21, 1992, decision: distinguishes the Rule 4003(b)/9006(b)(3) exemption-objection limit; preserves its strict operation.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): distinguishes the requirement of a supported post-deadline request; does not confine every qualifying neglect to uncontrollable events.

##### Counsel's conduct is attributed to the creditor

**Holding:** A creditor seeking relief under Rule 9006(b)(1) is accountable for the acts and omissions of its freely selected counsel; the court must evaluate the neglect of both client and lawyer together. Reasonable client reliance on counsel does not create an exception from that attribution or independently require an extension.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Court opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five; the dissent does not dispute attorney attribution, but its disagreement about excusability does not create another rationale majority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Link v. Wabash Railroad Co., 370 U.S. 626 (1962): applies attribution of freely selected counsel's conduct to the client.
- United States v. Boyle, 469 U.S. 241 (1985): applies the rejection of reliance on an agent as an automatic filing excuse.

##### This record supports allowing the late proofs of claim

**Holding:** With counsel's conduct attributed to the creditors, the unusually placed and ambiguous bar-date notice, the twenty-day delay, and the undisturbed findings of no prejudice, no adverse administrative effect and good faith support excusing the missed Chapter 11 deadline here. The late claims may be filed; the decision does not establish their substantive validity or amount, excuse ordinary disregard of a clear notice, or give dispositive weight to counsel's office disruption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Court opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five; the Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the corrected attribution analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Link v. Wabash Railroad Co., 370 U.S. 626 (1962): applies full attorney attribution while permitting the separate excusability inquiry authorized by this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9006(b)(2) forbids enlargement under Rules 1007(d), 1017(b)(3), 2003(a) and (d), 7052, 9023 and 9024. Rule 9006(b)(3) permits enlargement only on the conditions of Rules 1006(b)(2), 1017(e), 3002(c), 4003(b), 4004(a), 4007(c), 8002 and 9033. Neither Chapter 7 claims nor Taylor’s exemption-objection deadline gains a general equitable exception. No factor is dispositive: good faith or no prejudice alone is insufficient, and ordinary ignorance or inadvertence ordinarily does not excuse a clear deadline.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm permission to file these late Chapter 11 claims with counsel’s conduct fully attributed. The unusual notice and undisturbed equitable findings support relief; claim validity, priority and amount are not established.

#### Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-409.
**Decided:** May 30, 2000.

##### Section 506(c) gives the independent recovery power to the trustee

**Holding:** Only the trustee, including a debtor in possession exercising the trustee's statutory powers, may invoke section 506(c) in its own right to recover reasonable, necessary costs of preserving or disposing of property securing an allowed secured claim, to the extent those costs benefit the secured claimholder. An unpaid administrative claimant's status and asserted benefit to the collateral do not create an independent section 506(c) action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined in full by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes establish the complete holding and bounded affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court reserves authorized derivative pursuit, assignment, actual trustee recovery, its allocation or recovery before payment of the expense, debtor-in-possession action and genuinely separate remedies on their own predicates. Hartford's Rule 7071/Rule 71 financing-order theory was first raised en banc and rejected as an unpleaded matter outside that limited review; the reservation does not revive it. Ultimate collateral benefit and administrative allowance remain unadjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The en banc direction vacating the surcharge and dismissing Hartford's independent section 506(c) claim stands. Separate estate claims and genuinely authorized alternative procedures remain subject to their own preservation and substantive requirements; this judgment orders none and revives no unpleaded claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Patterson v. Shumate** and **Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz:** Applied for enforcing enacted bankruptcy text and distinct procedures; their actual exclusions, deadlines and reservations remain unchanged.
- **United States v. Noland** and **United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators:** Applied to preserve authorized bankruptcy equity while rejecting a substitute allocation unsupported by the Code; no universal misconduct rule is added.
- **PBGC v. CF&I Fabricators:** Its distinction between underlying entitlement, allowance and distribution supports treating section 503 and section 506(c) as separate questions.
- **Mertens v. Hewitt Associates:** Applied for attention to the statutorily authorized remedy; its ERISA remedy holding does not itself answer the different bankruptcy text.
- **Dewsnup v. Timm:** Distinguished as a confined contextual construction of lien language; it creates no general authority to replace a named claimant with another.

#### Heintz v. Jenkins

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-367.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Regular attorney debt collectors remain covered when they use litigation

**Holding:** The FDCPA applies to an attorney who regularly collects or attempts to collect covered consumer debts owed or asserted to be owed another, including through litigation, subject to the statute's actual definition, exceptions, elements and defenses. There is no categorical attorney-litigation exemption; the decision does not cover lawyers merely because they practice law or establish that this settlement letter violated the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Gardner, December 12, 1994: applies respect for enacted conditions and rejection of an added category restriction; it does not create a new FDCPA proof rule or resolve conduct liability.
- Green v. Hocking, 9 F.3d 18: rejects the lower-court categorical litigation-exemption approach as inconsistent with the enacted definition and attorney-exemption repeal; it supplies no continuing exemption in this Court's law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A covered debt is an obligation or alleged obligation of a consumer to pay money arising from a transaction whose money, property, insurance or services are primarily for personal, family or household purposes, whether or not reduced to judgment. The retained §1692a(6)(D) exception concerns a person while serving or attempting to serve legal process on another in connection with judicial enforcement of a debt; it does not create a whole-attorney exemption. No factual statutory exception is established here.

Section 1692e prohibits false, deceptive or misleading representations in debt collection; §1692f(1) addresses collection of an amount not expressly authorized by the agreement creating the debt or permitted by law. The alleged $4,173 charge remains to be tested under those provisions. Section 1692e(5) concerns threatened action that cannot legally be taken or is not intended; failure to prevail alone does not necessarily satisfy it. No authoritative resolution of those conduct applications is made.

The communication and defense provisions invoked in the objection retain their exact conditions. Under §1692c(c), a consumer's written refusal to pay or written request to cease communication bars further communications except to advise that collection efforts are terminated, to notify that the collector or creditor may invoke specified remedies ordinarily invoked by that collector or creditor, or, where applicable, to notify that the collector or creditor intends to invoke a specified remedy. A mailed notice is complete on receipt. These are alternative permissions, not mandatory notices. Under §1692k(c), the collector must prove by a preponderance that a violation was unintentional and resulted from bona fide error despite procedures reasonably adapted to avoid it; no general immunity for legal error is decided. Under §1692k(e), good-faith conformity with an FTC advisory opinion protects an act or omission even if the opinion is later amended, rescinded or held invalid; staff commentary is not automatically such an opinion. Section 814(d) denies the FTC and the other agencies assigned enforcement responsibility under §814(b) authority to promulgate trade-regulation rules or other regulations concerning collection of debts by debt collectors as defined in the Act. No defense is established on these allegations, and no comprehensive litigation exception or fee entitlement is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's reversal of categorical dismissal is affirmed. The complaint proceeds under ordinary coverage, elements and defenses; regularity, letter liability, contractual authorization and defenses remain unadjudicated, with no damages or fee award.


Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`; heading `Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)`.


#### Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the continuing illness-and-danger basis of insanity commitment; initial verdict-based commitment and rejection of an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling remain intact.
- O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975): applied to the requirement that confinement end when its authorizing basis disappears; the Court does not equate a conditional-release recommendation with a finding of harmlessness.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the required relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose.

##### Whether Louisiana may preserve psychiatric custody without new adequate grounds and proceedings

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Material application:** A counseled hearing about danger alone cannot supply the absent illness predicate. Ordinary civil commitment requires clear and convincing proof of present illness and dangerousness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Vitek v. Jones, 445 U.S. 480 (1980): applied to psychiatric institutionalization's distinct liberty deprivation and need for appropriate grounds and procedures.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the need for an independently authorized commitment proceeding after the initial basis expires.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418 (1979): applied unchanged to the clear-and-convincing burden for ordinary civil commitment; that burden is not made universal for every preventive custody channel.
- Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107 (1966): relied on for the ordinary protections attending a new civil commitment after the prior detention basis ends; no separate majority equal-protection holding is created here.

##### Whether indefinite psychiatric confinement may rest on failure to prove nondangerousness after mental illness ends

**Holding:** Louisiana's scheme permitting indefinite psychiatric confinement of a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee because he has not proved nondangerousness violates due process. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure the absence of a carefully limited substantive authority; the Court leaves materially narrower preventive regimes undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-C, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient rejection of Louisiana's asserted alternative preventive justification. The holding adopts no new general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness proof standard.

**Material application:** Periodic review does not cure an indefinite scheme without carefully limited substantive authority. Salerno’s focused pretrial regime does not justify this post-acquittal confinement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): distinguished because its confined pretrial detention authority and safeguards do not authorize Louisiana's indefinite post-acquittal psychiatric restraint.
- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the absence of authority to punish an insanity acquittee as a convicted offender.


Source: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`; heading `Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504 (1992)`.


#### Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1038.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

##### Whether the 1965 cigarette Act displaces the presented common-law damages claims

**Holding:** Section 5 of the 1965 Federal Cigarette Labeling and Advertising Act bars positive enactments requiring particular cautionary statements on cigarette packages or in advertising; it does not preempt the common-law damages claims presented here. Compliance with the required federal package warning does not itself immunize manufacturers from those claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion on the 1965 Act, joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, and by Stone as to this statutory construction and its application; seven Justices support the controlling construction. Scalia and Thomas also support nonpreemption of liability for voluntary warranty and fraudulent statements, without joining the seven-Justice construction concerning warning duties.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the 1965 Act and the claims presented.

**Material application:** Damages can influence conduct without falling within this Act's prohibition of compulsory cautionary statements. Presented consumer-warning claims, including concealment and conspiracy insofar as they require smoking-health statements to consumers, remain available. Voluntarily made warranty, fraudulent and warning-neutralization statements, and conspiracy predicated on those statements, also escape this Act's statement requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This construction does not create universal immunity from independently established actual conflict, decide design-defect liability, or construe later amendments. Duties genuinely unrelated to advertising are reserved rather than assigned an opposing vote. State-law liability and the existing appellate questions concerning comparative fault and warranty instructions are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the 1965 Act preemption barriers and remand for application of the appropriate statutory periods and remaining state-law proceedings. The 1965 Act took effect January 1, 1966; the successor statute was enacted in 1970 despite its 1969 title. No verdict is reinstated, damages awarded, or fraud, causation or breach found.

**Material judgment:** The warning-duty barrier is reversed 7–2 by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, with Scalia and Thomas opposing. The voluntary-statement barrier is reversed 9–0; that unanimity does not enlarge the narrower proposition-level agreement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218, supplies the presumption preserving traditional state authority absent clear displacement. English v. General Electric Co., 496 U.S. 72, requires attention to the actual statutory operation and rejects displacement from additional tort consequences alone. San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon, 359 U.S. 236, establishes that damages can regulate but does not determine this cigarette provision's scope. Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, June 18, 1992, supplies only its shared rule against automatic preemption from regulatory overlap; its broader separate theories are not adopted.

##### Whether the successor Act preempts advertising warning and warning-neutralization duties

**Holding:** For cigarettes whose packages comply with federal labeling requirements, amended §5(b) reaches common-law duties and preempts liability requiring additional or clearer smoking-health warnings in advertising or promotion, including a duty forbidding advertising because it neutralizes the mandated warning. The duty necessary to liability controls; pleading a warning claim as fraud does not avoid preemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's amended-Act analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, and Scalia's separate express-preemption analysis, joined by Thomas; six Justices expressly support this operative inclusion and displacement. It controls directly across the writings, without treating Scalia's broader theory as a join to Stevens's entire opinion or invoking Marks.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for covered promotional warning duties and federally compliant cigarette packages.

**Material application:** A demand for additional health-warning content and a prohibition on advertising because it neutralizes that warning impose the covered obligation from opposite directions. The amended words concerning state-law requirements or prohibitions in advertising or promotion reach obligations enforced through damages.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No entire amended-Act opinion commands five formal joins. This holding creates no cigarette-wide immunity, constitutional advertising rule or general displacement of independent nonpromotional obligations; it does not decide liability, causation, damages or the effect of later amendments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm preemption of the covered warning and warning-neutralization claims for the successor Act's applicable period, preserving its federal-labeling-compliance condition. Remand for claim-specific application together with the surviving claims described below.

**Material judgment:** Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas sustain the bar, 6–3; Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter oppose it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Garmon, 359 U.S. 236, supplies the treatment of common-law duties as legal requirements without importing labor-law exclusivity. Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., June 1, 1992, is distinguished by its airline language; enforcing that express clause's enacted reach does not establish cigarette-wide immunity here.

##### Whether amended §5(b) preempts voluntarily undertaken express warranties

**Holding:** Amended §5(b) does not preempt an otherwise valid claim enforcing a manufacturer's voluntarily undertaken express warranty because the manufacturer supplies the substantive promise, rather than the State imposing a smoking-health command. Printing the promise in an advertisement does not change that result.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, supplies the controlling narrow ground under Marks v. United States. Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter support restoration on the broader ground that the amended Act preempts no common-law damages claims. Voluntary-warranty claims are a true subset of that broader rule and receive the same result whenever the narrower rule applies; the two judgment-supporting groups total seven. The broader common-law exemption does not control.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for enforcement of voluntarily undertaken substantive promises, at the stated claim-specific Marks scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State law still determines whether a binding warranty exists and whether reliance, breach, causation and damages are proved. This decision makes none of those findings and does not immunize disguised state warning commands.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the express-warranty preemption barrier and remand for the remaining state-law proceedings; no particular verdict or award is reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. American Train Dispatchers Association, 499 U.S. 117, is distinguished: the dependence of contracts on law for enforceability does not turn a manufacturer's voluntary substantive promise into the state-imposed requirement covered by this differently worded clause. Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, supplies only the described component-specific narrower ground.

##### Whether amended §5(b) reaches independently actionable nonpromotional conduct and disclosure

**Holding:** Amended §5(b) does not preempt claims resting solely on testing, research or other conduct unrelated to advertising or promotion, or concealment claims resting on an independently actionable duty to disclose through nonpromotional channels. This boundary neither preserves a disguised demand for additional advertising warnings nor establishes that an asserted state-law duty exists.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, supplies the narrow ground under Marks together with Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter's broader rule exempting common-law damages claims. Claims independently outside the activity boundary form a logical subset of that broader rule, producing the same nonpreemption result whenever the narrower rule applies; the groups total seven. Scalia and Thomas oppose restoration of the presented consumer-warning theories through alternative communication channels but reserve genuinely unrelated nonpromotional duties.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated activity boundary and claim-specific Marks scope; it establishes no particular nonpromotional disclosure obligation.

**Material application:** The necessary liability duty, rather than the pleading label or the use of advertising as evidence of another wrong, determines whether the claim is promotional. Practical compulsion matters when identifying a disguised advertising requirement, but cannot erase genuinely independent nonpromotional obligations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular obligation to report to an agency or breach of such an obligation is found. Genuinely unrelated duties, including the hypothetical agency-disclosure duty, receive no adjudicated opposing vote or liability determination. The lower courts must identify viable allegations and the relevant state-law elements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the presented concealment barrier to permit claim-specific examination under this boundary, without adjudicating independent hypothetical duties or damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** English, 496 U.S. 72, supplies examination of the particular duty and its relation to federally regulated conduct. Morales, June 1, 1992, does not replace this statute's activity limitation with the airline clause's relation language or fare-effect analysis. Marks, 430 U.S. 188, supplies only the narrow surviving category, not a complete nonpreemption theory.

##### Whether intentional material falsehood and concealment conspiracies survive amended §5(b)

**Holding:** Amended §5(b) does not preempt liability for intentional material falsehood under the general duty not to deceive, including a false statement in advertising, when liability does not depend on prescribing additional warning content or punishing warning neutralization. A conspiracy to misrepresent or conceal smoking-health facts survives only insofar as its predicate deceptive duty survives; its label creates no separate exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's claim-specific analysis, joined by Stone, White and O'Connor, supplies the controlling narrow Marks ground together with Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter's broader exemption for common-law damages claims. The protected falsehood and surviving-concealment categories are logical subsets yielding the same restoration whenever they apply; the groups total seven. Scalia and Thomas oppose restoration of the presented amended-Act claims. Blackmun's general exemption remains noncontrolling.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the stated intentional-falsehood and surviving-predicate categories, subject to the warning and warning-neutralization exclusions.

**Material application:** Liability for knowingly making a material false representation need not ask a jury to prescribe warning content. A conspiracy based on independently actionable deception or concealment remains available, but one based on a preempted warning duty does not.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Every state-law element remains to be established; this ruling finds no fraud, causation or damages and supplies no general exemption for claims labeled fraud or conspiracy. Independently established actual-impossibility preemption, industry-wide field immunity and later amendments are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse these preemption barriers, 7–2 with Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter supporting and Scalia and Thomas opposing, and remand for claim-specific proceedings. No existing verdict is reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Garmon, 359 U.S. 236, preserves the regulatory effect of damages without making every damages claim a covered smoking-health requirement. Marks, 430 U.S. 188, supplies the separate narrower grounds for these surviving categories.

#### Sullivan v. Louisiana, 508 U.S. 275 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5129.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993, No. 92-5129, see Federal Courts — Finality of a completed conviction ruling.

##### A charge vitiating the reasonable-doubt standard leaves no valid verdict for harmless review

**Holding:** When an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt charge vitiates the jury’s governing standard for all factual findings, the defendant has received no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. That structural defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate court’s hypothetical verdict cannot substitute for the required jury determination, and this rule does not classify every instructional mistake as structural.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Cage defect is conceded. Different instructional errors, evidentiary sufficiency and collateral-review gates remain unresolved; the distinct penalty-phase relief is not reviewed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment and remand for proceedings permitting a new trial under a valid reasonable-doubt charge. No acquittal; prior penalty-phase relief survives and the original death sentence is not reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358: proof-beyond-reasonable-doubt requirement applied.
- Duncan v. Louisiana, 391 U.S. 145: jury’s constitutional role applied.
- Cage v. Louisiana, 498 U.S. 39: conceded defect taken as the predicate, not relitigated.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18: actual-verdict inquiry preserved; no substitute hypothetical verdict permitted.
- Rose v. Clark, 478 U.S. 570: separable presumption error distinguished.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: structural-defect category applied.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991: contested-charge framework preserved; no reopening of the conceded defect.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: preserved-Doyle habeas rule unchanged; not a Kotteakos constitutional-habeas rule.

#### Schiro v. Farley, 510 U.S. 222

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7549.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### Late presentation of nonretroactivity

**Holding:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted that defense in the lower courts and in its opposition to certiorari and first raised it in merits briefing, the Court may decline to consider it; the Court exercises that discretion here and reaches the presented double-jeopardy claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens also agree with merits reach, but their agreement is unnecessary to the opinion's controlling authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Collins v. Youngblood: applied for the nonjurisdictional character of nonretroactivity and the resulting discretion.
- Teague v. Lane: its substantive nonretroactivity rule and contemporary exceptions are left unchanged; they are not applied to dispose of this case.

##### The initial sentencing phase is not a second prosecution

**Holding:** Considering the intentional-killing aggravator in the initial sentencing phase of this prosecution did not itself place Schiro in jeopardy a second time. Acquittal-like capital determinations remain protected, but the jury's advisory recommendation against death was not a final acquittal of a statutory aggravator or sentence under this scheme.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Salazar-Haro supplies the full protection for qualifying §106 judgments without barring ordinary correctness review.

##### II. Section 440(a) governs this ordinary petition

**Holding:** AEDPA §440(a) withdraws ordinary appellate review of Kolster's final deportation order based on the admitted drug and aggravated-felony grounds, independently supported by the conviction documents, notwithstanding his February 28, 1996 petition. On the established facts, application of the channel restriction imposes no supported new legal disability on his plea, concession or another protected completed event; this conclusion establishes neither a universal actual-reliance requirement nor a rule that pleas or eligibility for discretionary relief can never support nonretroactivity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the dismissal on a different temporal ground.

**Material application:** Deportability was admitted and independently documented; no colorable defense surrendered is identified. Even Kolster's preferred domicile calculation left his September 1992 plea three years short of seven-year eligibility. Those facts establish no protected lost option without findings about subjective motives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A jurisdictional label does not alone determine temporal reach. Section 440(f) times subsection (e), not (a); §440(d)'s substantive eligibility amendment remains undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers supply the provision-specific temporal inquiry. Reyes-Hernandez's actual lost-defense premise is absent, without making surrendered defenses the only protected completed events. Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro reject filing and expectations alone as a basis for preserving the channel. Hughes Aircraft's new private enforcement exposure is distinguished.

##### III. Independent habeas jurisdiction survives for substantial legal and constitutional objections

**Holding:** Section 440(a)'s replacement of a particular INA review channel does not clearly repeal the independent jurisdiction enacted in 28 U.S.C. §2241. When custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates are satisfied, that jurisdiction permits consideration of a substantial legal or constitutional claim that executive custody or deportation lacks lawful authority, including a claim that an unlawful eligibility rule prevented consideration of discretionary relief, and permits effective relief if the claim warrants it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained route must be capable of reaching the substantial authority objection and effective relief when warranted; detention-only review required to assume the disputed eligibility rule valid is insufficient to answer that objection. The Court does not determine Kolster's habeas predicates, make every statutory error constitutional, reopen discretionary weighing or factual review generally, or define all constitutional minima. A demonstrated failure of the retained route remains reserved; §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker's distinction between a withdrawn review channel and retained statutory habeas is extended to §2241 in this immigration setting. McCardle and Yerger support channel-specific repeal analysis; Heikkila distinguishes ordinary immigration review from habeas. The legal-eligibility objection resolves the reservation left in Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro at this conditional scope; Flores's custody safeguards do not themselves answer review of deportation authority.

##### IV. The direct petition ends without a decision on eligibility

**Holding:** The First Circuit's dismissal of the direct petition is affirmed. The Court neither decides the lawful-domicile calculation nor orders a waiver, agency reconsideration, conversion or transfer of the petition, collateral relief, a stay or execution of deportation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The ordinary petition remains dismissed, unanimously. The sentencing recommendation does not bind the immigration decisionmaker. Any collateral challenge must establish its own jurisdictional and procedural predicates before legal eligibility or requested relief is decided.

#### Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1200.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### The selected commercial category lacks the required fit

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading commercial publications concerning lawful activity and newspapers cause the same asserted physical harms through their newsracks, the City must justify why its selected commercial category reasonably fits its safety and aesthetic interests. Cincinnati has not done so: removing respondents' sixty-two racks while retaining approximately 1,500–2,000 physically similar newspaper racks violates the First Amendment; a small reduction is not invalid merely for being small, and no least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven, throughout.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557 (1980): applies the existing commercial-speech test to the accepted classification.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469 (1989): applies reasonable proportional fit and government burden; preserves rejection of least restrictive means.
- Metromedia, Inc. v. San Diego, 453 U.S. 490 (1981): distinguishes the billboard treatment; does not permit an unrelated content distinction without the required fit.

##### The selective prohibition is not content neutral

**Holding:** A newsrack prohibition determined by whether its publication is a commercial handbill is content based when the asserted safety and aesthetic harms do not justify that distinction without reference to the publication's content. Cincinnati cannot sustain this selective ban as a content-neutral time, place and manner restriction merely because its objective is benign; otherwise lawful neutral regulation of newsracks remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applies justification without reference to content; a benign objective alone does not validate the selective line.
- Clark v. Community for Creative Non-Violence, 468 U.S. 288 (1984): preserves valid content-neutral time, place and manner regulation.
- Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc., 475 U.S. 41 (1986): distinguishes different secondary effects; no such difference justifies this rack classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Central Hudson and Fox remain governing. Incremental regulation remains possible; alternatives inform fit but do not impose least restrictive means. No strict-scrutiny rule for all truthful commercial speech, all-sources-at-once duty or commercial/noncommercial equality rule is adopted. Other lawful neutral rack controls remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit and the injunction against the selective commercial-handbill newsrack prohibition.

#### Reno v. Flores, 507 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-905.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### Statutory authority for the juvenile release regulation

**Holding:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits the Attorney General to use the family-custodian and discretionary-release arrangements in 8 C.F.R. §242.24 for temporary juvenile custody pending deportation proceedings; the regulation is not facially beyond that authority because it does not mandate release to every willing responsible adult. Custody remains subject to the statute's reasonable-dispatch limitation, its habeas remedy upon the requisite conclusive showing of unnecessary delay, the regulation's release avenues and the existing conditions agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six votes for this rule. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower statutory and facial-review ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carlson v. Landon, 342 U.S. 524 (1952): applies discretion bounded by the immigration statute; it does not authorize indefinite custody.
- National Center for Immigrants' Rights, December 16, 1991, decision: applies general immigration-release authority with preserved individual safeguards; does not enlarge that decision into universal detention authority.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): applies facial-review limits to the whole-regulation remedy.

##### The facial substantive-due-process challenge fails

**Holding:** The Constitution does not give a juvenile without an available parent, guardian or close adult relative a fundamental right to placement with a willing unrelated adult instead of temporary, nonpunitive government care under this regulation and the binding conditions agreement. On this facial record, the family preferences and other release avenues reasonably serve care and attendance interests; individual unconstitutional confinement and enforcement of the agreement remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. O'Connor and Souter also explain the substantial liberty interest in avoiding institutional confinement in a separate concurrence; Stone-Zsela does not join this formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the distinction between protective juvenile custody and punishment.
- Parham v. J. R., 442 U.S. 584 (1979): applies the special circumstances of juvenile care; does not extinguish a child's liberty interest.
- Youngberg v. Romeo, 457 U.S. 307 (1982): preserves constitutional limits on institutional care.
- Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992, decision: distinguishes psychiatric confinement after its predicate ends; no universal custody test is inferred.

##### Request-based review is not facially inadequate

**Holding:** The described examination, notice and opportunity to obtain immigration-judge review on request do not facially require replacement with an automatic hearing for every detained juvenile. That result depends on the available procedures and the absence of a record establishing general inability to invoke them; it does not approve ineffective notice, denial of requested review, improper delay or unlawful custody in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela agrees with setting aside the universal automatic-hearing remedy but reserves a distinct Mathews-based implementation inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fare v. Michael C., 442 U.S. 707 (1979): applies the recognition that juveniles can make a knowing waiver; no universal finding of comprehension follows.
- The Japanese Immigrant Case, 189 U.S. 86 (1903): applies aliens' entitlement to due process in deportation proceedings.

##### The challenged classifications do not establish facial unequal treatment

**Holding:** The regulation's distinction between juveniles with available family custodians and those without them survives the facial equal-protection objection because family availability bears a rational relation to safe placement and attendance. The separate federal juvenile-delinquency release scheme does not require identical release rules for immigration custody, and this ruling does not resolve unequal administration in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component and adds no ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the legitimate relevance of protective care in juvenile custody; no general exemption from equal protection follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b)(1) requires release in order of preference to a nondetained parent, legal guardian, or adult brother, sister, aunt, uncle or grandparent unless detention is required to secure timely appearance before the Service or immigration court, or ensure the juvenile's safety or that of others. If the only listed custodian is detained, (b)(2) provides discretionary case-by-case consideration of simultaneous release. Under (b)(3), a parent or guardian detained or outside the United States may designate another capable and willing person by sworn affidavit before an immigration or consular officer; that person must agree to care for the child and ensure presence at all future proceedings. Subsection (b)(4) permits other agreeing adult custodians only in unusual and compelling circumstances, at the district director's or chief patrol agent's discretion. These are distinct release avenues, not one undifferentiated exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1987 conditions agreement remains enforceable and is not a finding that each facility complies. Custody review under §1252(a)(1) permits habeas upon a conclusive showing of failure to proceed with reasonable dispatch warranted by the particular facts. For a juvenile not seeking voluntary departure, the described 24-hour process uses a different examiner unless no qualified alternative is available; English/Spanish notice allows review on request and withdrawal of waiver. No universal ruling on voluntary-departure safeguards, 30-day custody cap or approval of defective notice, ineffective assistance, delayed review or unconstitutional individual/systemic implementation follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial juvenile-release and automatic-hearing injunctions, 7–2 in judgment, and remand consistently with the six-Justice rationale. No particular child’s release is ordered; conditions enforcement and properly supported implementation challenges remain.

#### Reno v. Goncalves

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-835; First Circuit judgment, 144 F.3d 110.
**Decided:** 1999-06-03.

##### II. Section 440(d) does not govern this pending application

**Holding:** AEDPA section 440(d)'s new categorical eligibility restriction does not govern a former section 212(c) application filed before AEDPA took effect on April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment. Goncalves's pending administrative appeal must be considered under the former eligibility rule; the holding neither covers applications first filed after enactment nor determines the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopts this rule.

##### III. Agency interpretation cannot override this statutory conclusion

**Holding:** Matter of Soriano receives no controlling weight on section 440(d)'s temporal application because ordinary interpretation resolves that question against the agency's construction; its reasoning is not persuasive enough to overcome the enacted distinctions. The Attorney General likewise cannot conclusively determine the district court's section 2241 jurisdiction, and the Board must decide the pending request without applying section 440(d) as an eligibility bar.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts this rule and its administrative remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kolster v. INS:** Applied to an actual petition satisfying the previously reserved predicates; legal eligibility falls within its retained authority category, while discretionary reweighing remains excluded.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied for channel-specific repeal analysis; withdrawal of one review mechanism does not itself repeal a separate jurisdictional grant.
- **Ex parte McCardle:** Its effect is confined to the appellate route Congress withdrew; it does not establish repeal of every independent habeas route.
- **Ex parte Yerger:** Supports preservation of an independently available writ despite withdrawal of another review channel.
- **Reyes-Hernandez v. INS:** Supplies the named-action boundary under section 242(g); its completed surrender of a defense is a distinct temporal predicate, not a universal prerequisite.
- **Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee:** Supplies the named-action/completed-adjudication distinction. Its separate constitutional section 1331 route is not extended.
- **Hincapie-Nieto v. INS:** Its ordinary-review restriction and rejection of pending status alone remain controlling; they do not decide section 440(d)'s different eligibility change.
- **Salazar-Haro v. INS:** Its review-channel rule remains intact and supplies no general immediate-effect rule for every substantive eligibility restriction.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Supplies the sequence requiring identification of Congress's temporal choice; the Court applies ordinary statutory construction without adopting a universal pending-case exception.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Preserves provision-specific temporal analysis and supplies no agency power to override Congress's choice.
- **Lindh v. Murphy:** Its express/omitted temporal comparison is applied to these provisions, not extended to every AEDPA amendment.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T:** Requires adherence to the enacted bounds of agency authority.
- **NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.:** Preserves agency interpretation only within permissible statutory meaning and delegated authority.
- **INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre:** Retains appropriate immigration deference for authorized unresolved choices; it does not authorize a contrary temporal rule after ordinary construction resolves Congress's choice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The surviving section 2241 route can decide this covered criminal alien's substantial legal eligibility challenge when its actual predicates are satisfied. Applications for former section 212(c) relief already pending on April 24, 1996 remain outside section 440(d)'s new eligibility bar. Eligibility permits the agency to exercise its lawful discretion; it does not predetermine the result. Ordinary-review restrictions, later-filed applications and IIRIRA's distinct repeal remain governed by their own provisions. No precedent is overruled and no general right to review discretionary immigration decisions is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing the eligibility disposition: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All nine sustain district-court jurisdiction over the actual section 2241 petition. The Board shall consider Goncalves's former section 212(c) application under the eligibility law applicable to the pending application when AEDPA took effect and exercise the discretion that law supplies. The immigration judge's earlier discretionary denial does not replace the Board's decision on the timely appeal. No favorable exercise of discretion, waiver, release or termination of deportation proceedings is awarded; equal protection is not adjudicated.

#### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-453.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For reviewability of the original categorical-prohibition period, see Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992, No. 91-453, under Federal Courts — Finality and earlier-period takings claims.

##### Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use

**Holding:** A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. No Marks combination is required or used.

**Material application:** Total loss is the trial finding assumed for the legal question, not a Council concession or appellate affirmance of value. A new legislative declaration of harm cannot replace substantiated background law; changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance principles.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-compensation judgment is reversed and remanded for background property and nuisance law and the remaining defenses, including the sustained extent and duration of loss. Any compensation covers the actual taking period; the $1,232,387.50 trial award is not reinstated automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922): applies its recognition that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes; does not attribute today's exact categorical rule to that decision.
- Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980): specifies and extends its complete-economic-use formulation into the rule and necessary exception stated here; does not adjudicate its distinct legitimate-interest formulation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978): preserves contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action; no percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.
- Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962): limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation; preserves their actual outcomes and lawful protective regulation.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987): distinguishes its productive-enterprise and partial-interest setting; no reversal of its actual holding or adoption of a new denominator follows.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): applies existing rules and understandings to identify the protected property interest; does not decide a procedural due-process claim.
- Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900): applies its antecedent-title-limit principle; a previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 (1982), and Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 (1979): preserve physical-occupation and exclusion protections and illuminate the difference between existing limitations and a new imposed servitude; this decision concerns regulatory loss.
- Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies, Inc. v. Beckwith, 449 U.S. 155 (1980): applies the prohibition on converting private property into public property by governmental assertion alone.

#### Dodd v. Hood River County

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-54; Ninth Circuit No. 97-35124; 136 F.3d 1219.
**Decided:** 1998-11-19.

##### Profitable timber use defeats only the complete-deprivation category

**Holding:** The binding finding that this parcel retains profitable timber use defeats a Lucas claim that the restriction eliminated all economically beneficial use. It does not itself decide partial deprivation under Penn Central, and no background-property limitation need be found when the necessary complete loss has not been established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

##### The independent contextual federal inquiry supports this summary judgment

**Holding:** A partial-taking claim requires consideration together of economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action; retained value, acquisition under an existing restriction or a legitimate regulatory objective alone is not dispositive. On this undisputed record, continued profitable timber use, the existing necessary-and-accessory dwelling condition, nonassuring County communications and the forest-management character of the restriction support rejection of the claim; the restriction also bears the required relationship to the specified forestry purposes under the received Agins inquiry, without a demanded conveyance triggering Dolan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

##### Later permission limits the claimed duration without curing a prior taking

**Holding:** The July 1, 1996 building authorization ends treatment of this dispute as an ongoing prohibition but does not itself defeat compensation for an earlier proved taking. Because the independently considered total- and partial-taking grounds establish no antecedent violation here, the actual-period claim supplies no compensation or basis for new factfinding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The preclusive finding concerns profitable timber use, not every federal element or a precise residual valuation. The judgment is affirmed on the eight-Justice independent federal grounds; no permit, compensation or jury trial is ordered. Later permission limits duration and is not a retrospective cure for an otherwise proved taking.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baker v. General Motors Corp. (January 13, 1998): applies its unanimous lawful-recognition component, not its distinct testimony-process application.
- Allen v. McCurry: applies ordinary issue preclusion to an actually litigated state determination; the entire federal cause is not equated with the state claim.
- Kremer v. Chemical Construction Corp.: preserves the distinction between an adequate available opportunity and failure to employ every procedure.
- Richards v. Jefferson County: preserves constitutional authority to bind and fair opportunity; these participating owners differ from absent litigants without a sufficient binding relationship.
