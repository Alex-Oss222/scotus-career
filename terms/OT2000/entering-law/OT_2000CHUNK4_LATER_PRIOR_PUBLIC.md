### Riggins v. Nevada, No. 90-8466

Merits decision, May 18, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 1992; decided May 18, 1992. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1257 to the Nevada Supreme Court, 107 Nev.178, 808 P.2d535 (March 28, 1991), which affirmed murder and robbery convictions and a death sentence. The granted question concerns forced medication through trial and the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment right to a full and fair trial.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court reverses, vacates the convictions and dependent sentence, and remands for a new trial.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence | Reversed and vacated, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter | Scalia, Thomas | New trial if the State proceeds. Any renewed involuntary medication requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification. |
| Fifth Amendment demeanor/self-incrimination theory | Not reached; all nine agree | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | No Justice opposes nonreach | No independent Fifth Amendment holding; the preserved medication and fair-trial ground resolves the case. |
| Competency-waiver question | Not reached; all nine agree | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | No Justice opposes nonreach | No ruling authorizing trial while incompetent or resolving waiver of competence. |

The distinct unpreserved Eighth Amendment claim is not decided. No finding that Riggins was incompetent, no fixed treatment instruction and no categorical medication prohibition accompanies the new-trial order.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter | Reversal and new-trial mandate | Parts I–IV: record and scope, justification rule, trial-risk holding and mandate; six Justices support these parts |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins the Court | Additionally applies Chapman's harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Kennedy | No other Justice | Joins reversal and new-trial judgment | Distinct extraordinary-showing protection for trial participation |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would affirm | Parts I, II-B and II-C: distinction between personal-liberty remedies and proven trial unfairness |
| Dissent | Thomas | No other Justice | Would affirm | Part II-A: additional objections about proof of involuntariness and the preservation and scope of an independent liberty claim |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The State must justify antipsychotic medication continued over objection

**Controlling proposition:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that clinical acceptability does not itself establish legal authority to override refusal. Washington v. Harper recognizes the substantial liberty interest affected by unwanted antipsychotic medication and permits justified treatment of dangerous, mentally ill prisoners. An accused awaiting trial retains the protected refusal interest recognized in Harper. Riggins initially sought treatment, but continued administration became involuntary after his objection. Medical appropriateness is accepted for this review; necessity is not. The hearing contained testimony that he could remain competent without Mellaril and questions about the high dosage. The trial court's unexplained denial establishes neither an essential safety need nor that less intrusive means could not permit adjudication. The State's legitimate interest in a competent trial cannot replace those missing determinations. The Court does not impose a new documentary ritual, universal proof quantum or strict-scrutiny formula. Medically appropriate treatment essential to safety after considering alternatives remains possible. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, and whether a defendant may insist on becoming incompetent, remain undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### 2. This unsupported medication order requires a new trial

**Controlling proposition:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this trial record cannot establish that the unsupported treatment left the defense intact. Illinois v. Allen and Estelle v. Williams recognize that compelled practices can affect an accused's trial in ways a transcript cannot measure. The doctors' testimony identifies a strong possibility that this dosage affected attention, communication and the substance of testimony, not merely the appearance the jury observed. Expert explanation may help jurors interpret demeanor, but cannot restore a conversation with counsel or a response that medication changed. The contrary demand for a precise account of the trial that would have occurred without treatment would therefore require speculation. Allen permits necessary measures to protect the trial, but this record contains no finding establishing an essential justification for this treatment. Reversal and a new trial follow from this combination of unjustified treatment and supported, unreconstructable trial risk. The Court neither finds Riggins incompetent nor decides that every medication error is structural. Renewed compelled treatment requires a constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

#### Current-Law Effect and Limits

Effective May 18, 1992, an objection to continued trial-related antipsychotic medication obligates the State to establish its medical appropriateness and overriding need with attention to reasonable alternatives. Unjustified treatment with the supported, unreconstructable trial risk present here requires reversal and a new trial. Harper remains governing in its convicted-prisoner setting; this decision adds the stated application for an objecting detainee at trial. No universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula, structural-error category or comprehensive competency-medication test is adopted. Kennedy's extraordinary-showing standard and Stone-Zsela's express Chapman analysis remain noncontrolling supplemental grounds. The unpreserved Eighth Amendment sentencing-display issue and competent-defendant refusal that would produce incompetence remain open. Six direct joins resolve each holding; no Marks construction is necessary.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy concurs in the judgment. He would require an extraordinary showing that involuntary medication for competency presents no significant risk of materially altering the accused's reactions, participation or assistance to counsel. He doubts that the contemporary medical evidence often permits that showing; if competence cannot be restored without such impairment, lawful civil commitment rather than trial may be the available route. This proposed standard is not the Court's rule.

Stone-Zsela joins the Court and separately applies Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): the State bears the burden of showing constitutional trial error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, and the medication record fails that burden. His concurrence supplies no general structural-error classification, categorical ban on competency treatment or order to stop medically necessary care. The Court's holding remains the record-specific unjustified-treatment and trial-risk rule.

Thomas, joined by Scalia except in Part II-A, would affirm. They regard expert testimony and the competence finding as adequate protection of the trial, distinguish a refusal-interest violation remediable in a civil action from an established basis to reverse a conviction, and reject reversal based on possible impairment alone. Thomas alone also disputes proof of involuntariness and whether a separate Harper liberty theory was preserved and included in the grant; Scalia does not join those additional objections.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review is complete. The case returns to Nevada with the murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence vacated, for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed involuntary medication must have a constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification under the Court's holding; a new trial is not an order to withhold medically necessary treatment. The Court does not authorize trial of an incompetent defendant, fix a treatment plan, decide an unpresented sentencing claim, set a new date or retain supervision.

**End of entry: Riggins v. Nevada, merits decision, May 18, 1992.**

### Troxel v. Granville, No. 99-138

Merits decision, June 5, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 12, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Washington Supreme Court in In re Custody of Smith, 137 Wash. 2d 1, 969 P.2d 21. Only the Troxel/Granville visitation dispute is before the Court. The question is whether former RCW 26.10.160(3), as applied over a fit parent's objection, violated parental liberty in directing the children's care and upbringing. The state court rejected a pending-custody-action limitation but sustained dismissal on broader federal constitutional grounds.

The former subsection permits any person to petition for visitation at any time, including but not limited to custody proceedings, and permits an order when visitation may serve the child's best interest whether or not circumstances have changed. After the children's father died, Granville continued contact with the paternal grandparents and proposed one daytime visit monthly and family holidays. The grandparents sought two overnight weekends monthly and two summer weeks; the trial order required one monthly weekend, one summer week and four hours on each grandparent's birthday. The dispute concerns the amount and conditions of continuing contact, not complete exclusion. The published account identifies no allegation or finding of parental unfitness and does not establish concrete harm from Granville's proposal.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy.

Stevens and Kennedy would vacate the constitutional dismissal and remand for further state consideration with proper parental protection. Scalia would reverse that dismissal without adopting the asserted new federal visitation rule. None of the three directs automatic reinstatement of the trial order.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion announcing the judgment; Opinion of the Court in Part I | Breyer | O'Connor and Ginsburg throughout; Stone-Zsela, Souter and Thomas in Part I only | Announces the six-Justice affirmance | I: protected parental-care liberty and prohibition of mere judicial-preference substitution; II: special weight and particular record application, three Justices only |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Affirms invalidation as applied; joins Part I | Serious-harm or comparable concrete legal-need trigger, strong presumption and narrowly fitted relief |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Souter | No other Justice | Affirms dismissal; joins Part I | Unrestricted statutory authorization as authoritatively construed |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Thomas | No other Justice | Affirms dismissal; joins Part I | Fundamental parental right and strict scrutiny |
| Dissent | Stevens | No other Justice | Would vacate and remand | Individualized assessment including the child's relational interests |
| Dissent | Scalia | No other Justice | Would reverse and remand | Refusal to extend established parental domains to this new substantive visitation rule |
| Dissent | Kennedy | No other Justice | Would vacate and remand | Contextual parental protection, actual relationships and extent of intrusion |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Parental liberty forbids displacement based only on a judge's preferred arrangement

**Holding and operative rule:** The protected liberty of a fit parent includes directing the child's care and upbringing. A court may not override that parent's visitation decision solely because the judge considers a different arrangement better for the child; ordinary judicial preference is not a sufficient constitutional justification.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their agreement is at precisely this level; their distinct additional scrutiny, trigger and statutory-scope rules are not combined.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the parent's constitutional interest cannot disappear into an unrestricted comparison of preferred arrangements. Meyer and Pierce establish protected parental direction, while Brown and Curtis preserve parental instruction and lawful educational choices even as they reject particular public-school vetoes. The State's ordinary family-law authority does not make the parent's decision equivalent to a stranger's proposed schedule.

The grandparents' continuing relationship and the benefits of contact are significant context, but a preference for more contact is not itself a sufficient basis for judicial displacement. Recognizing this limit does not establish an absolute parental veto or deny legitimate child protection. Baby Richard recognizes a bounded interest in avoiding serious harm from compulsory custody implementation while preserving the fit parent's entitlement against a preferred-home comparison. A. St. P. C. addresses a distinct complete-contact cutoff. Neither automatically supplies the threshold, proof quantum or remedy for this visitation dispute.

The six Justices sharing this proposition affirm dismissal, but differ about the further constitutional rule and how broadly the statutory authorization must be rejected. Their agreement establishes the prohibition of mere judicial-preference substitution, not a complete governing test for every nonparent-visitation order.

##### The judgment does not adopt a comprehensive visitation standard

**Authority and limits:** The judgment binds the parties and leaves this order unenforceable. Part II's full special-weight and record-application analysis has three votes, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela's serious-harm or comparable-need rule, Souter's statutory-breadth rationale and Thomas's strict-scrutiny ground remain separate. No common scrutiny level, universal harm requirement, parental-unfitness prerequisite, proof burden or general facial invalidation receives a controlling adoption. No Marks synthesis supplies one. Other statutes and materially different nonparent relationships remain open; the Smith and Wolcott disputes and collateral fees are not adjudicated here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meyer v. Nebraska** and **Pierce v. Society of Sisters:** Apply the established protection of parental care and direction; neither supplies the entire ordinary-visitation test.
- **Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.** and **Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth:** Preserve protected family choice and instruction while distinguishing particular public-school veto claims from this intrusion.
- **Baby Richard v. Kirchner:** Preserves its bounded child-transition interest and protection of lawful parental custody against preferred-home substitution; it creates no permanent grandparent-contact entitlement.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C.:** Its distinct clear-and-convincing rule remains intact. That decision concerned a complete cutoff of contact and supplies no universal visitation burden.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg:** Remains a decision concerning the asserted physician-assistance entitlement, without an exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties.
- **Parham v. J.R., Stanley v. Illinois, Prince v. Massachusetts** and **Santosky v. Kramer:** Retain their distinct parental, child-protection, fitness-process and termination principles; their use in the separate analyses creates no new common visitation threshold.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 5, 2000, six Justices directly establish parental-care protection against displacement solely by judicial preference for another arrangement, and six affirm dismissal of this petition. No complete special-weight framework or general serious-harm, strict-scrutiny, facial-invalidation or proof-burden rule is adopted. Existing child-protection, educational-choice, custody-transition and severe-contact-restriction holdings retain their distinct force.

#### Separate Writings

**Part II of the lead opinion, Breyer with O'Connor and Ginsburg.** These three require special weight for a fit parent's judgment, drawing on Parham's presumption that parents ordinarily act in their children's interests. The trial court instead presumed grandparent contact beneficial unless contrary harm appeared, failed to credit Granville's continuing-contact offer, and relied in part on its own favorable grandparent experiences. The written findings establish benefits, not unfitness or concrete injury from her proposal. These circumstances make the order unconstitutional without deciding a universal harm or unfitness requirement. Limited visitation differs from custody termination, but that difference does not permit ordinary better-schedule substitution. Other statutes, relationships and justified protective interventions remain open.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Meyer, Pierce, Prince and Santosky support his proposed extension: a fit parent's reasonable visitation choice receives a strong presumption, and the nonparent must establish particularized serious harm to the child or a comparably concrete legally recognized need before the State may override it. Any order must be narrowly fitted to that need. The order fails because an unbounded best-interests preference supplies neither predicate nor fit. He treats that specification as an additional rule to be adopted, not as a threshold already established by the earlier cases. His rule preserves emergency protection, adjudication of unfitness and tailored relief on the required showing, and creates no absolute veto. No other Justice joins this proposed complete standard.

**Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The authoritatively construed authorization permits any person to seek visitation at any time and leaves protected parental choice exposed to an unrestricted judicial preference for a better arrangement. That breadth supplies his sufficient ground without reaching Part II's particular record assessment. Possible justified applications cannot add restrictions the state court declined to find in the statute. Brown, Curtis and his Glucksberg approach support protection of the actual interest and assessment of the restraint's fit; he does not hold that harm is invariably the sole sufficient justification.

**Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Established parental care is a fundamental right, so its infringement requires strict scrutiny and compelling justification. Benefit-only second-guessing of this fit parent's decision lacks that justification. He joins Part I's exact liberty and anti-substitution rules, but not an unspecified special-weight standard that leaves the required scrutiny unstated. His ground does not transfer the termination proof burden to every visitation case or establish an absolute veto.

**Stevens, dissenting.** Parental protection must coexist with the child's supported interests in important relationships. His Parham position and the bounded interests recognized in Baby Richard and A. St. P. C. support individualized assessment, not a permanent grandparent right. Because a universal harm condition or broad facial invalidation exceeds the established rules, he would vacate and return the matter for state consideration with a presumptive parental role and attention to the child's actual interests. He neither finds the order valid nor commands new evidence or visitation.

**Scalia, dissenting.** He preserves the judicially protected parental domains recognized in Meyer, Pierce, Brown and Curtis but declines to infer this new substantive visitation boundary from those distinct decisions. His Baby Richard and A. St. P. C. positions require an established interest before transferring rules across materially different family-law settings. He would reverse the constitutional dismissal and allow ordinary state disposition without a new federal weighting or harm formula. That position does not deny every enforceable parental liberty, overrule existing family protections or command visitation.

**Kennedy, dissenting.** A categorical harm condition is too broad where an established caregiving relationship and limited intrusion may matter. Brown, Curtis and his Glucksberg opinion require attention to the actual liberty and restraint; the family-law cases do not erase those distinctions. He would vacate and remand for assessment of the order with meaningful parental protection and attention to the actual relationships. His proposed remand does not presume harm, validate the existing order, demand supplementation or reinstate visitation.

#### Source Notes

The [official report, 530 U.S. 57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-530/pdf/USREPORTS-530-57.pdf), and the [Washington opinion, 969 P.2d 21](https://openjurist.org/969/p2d/21/in-re-custody-of-smith-1405279), supply the former subsection, schedules, trial findings and lower-court history. Published recitations establish continued offered contact and the trial court's benefit-based reasons; they do not establish unfitness or concrete harm from the offered schedule. Benefit findings are not findings of necessity, and the absence of a reported fitness allegation is not an adjudication of every conceivable parental concern.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Troxel petition remains dismissed and the visitation order unenforceable. The affirmance permits no renewed contest based merely on a preferred visitation schedule. No new best-interests proceeding, custody transfer, evidentiary hearing, fee award or disposition of the separate Smith and Wolcott controversies is ordered. Materially different visitation laws and relationships remain for cases presenting them.

**End of entry: Troxel v. Granville, merits decision, June 5, 2000.**

### Hunt v. Cromartie, No. 98-85

Merits decision, May 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 20, 1999. On direct appeal from a three-judge District Court, 34 F. Supp. 2d 1029. The court entered summary judgment invalidating North Carolina's Twelfth Congressional District before discovery or an evidentiary hearing. The State disputed the court's inference that race, rather than partisan voting patterns, predominated. The appeal requires the Court to identify the governing constitutional injury before determining which factual disputes matter. The State used a replacement plan for the 1998 elections with district-court approval. Section 1.1 of 1998 North Carolina Session Laws chapter 2 governs that plan's use in 1998 and 2000 subject to this Court's reversal of the judgment declaring the prior G.S. 163–201(a) unconstitutional. That conditional operation leaves a live controversy; the Court does not decide the replacement map's validity or whether this vacatur satisfies the statutory condition.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing or differently aligned Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing summary judgment and injunction | Vacated, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | The summary judgment and dependent injunction cannot stand. |
| Governing remand | Reject predominance alone as a constitutional deprivation, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas would preserve the predominance claim for factfinding | Consider only actually presented independently cognizable claims under their own elements and ordinary procedure. |

No trial is required solely to choose between racial and political motives for the design-only theory. The Court does not finally validate the map, supply a new claim, deem an unknown claim forfeited, or independently decide the State's own summary-judgment motion.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Unanimous vacatur | Common vacatur and ordinary summary-judgment protections. |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts II–III | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Breyer | Governing remand | Constitutional rule and remand limitation. |
| Concurrence in part and in the vacatur, dissenting from the remand limitation | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Joins Part I; opposes the remand limitation | Would retain the predominance claim. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Predominance alone cannot support this injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A districting injunction cannot rest solely on a finding that race predominated in forming a common territorial electorate, even if ordinary districting criteria were subordinated. A plaintiff must establish a separately cognizable denial of equal participation or treatment; remand evaluates any such actually presented claim under its own elements and ordinary procedural rules.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion in Part II and the governing remand in Part III control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that factual disagreement matters only when the governing law makes the fact material. Shaw v. Reno and Johnson v. Miller distinguish racial composition of a shared electorate from unequal civic status, racial eligibility or representation legally limited to one race. Predominance may exist without establishing any of those deprivations. The district court therefore could not invalidate the district merely by resolving the design's motivation against the State. A trial on the same legally insufficient theory would not cure that error. Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson illustrates a concrete burden on political access; it does not turn racial motivation alone into that burden. An independently cognizable injury may be noneconomic and need not arise from a second act or later electoral loss, but its own elements must be established. The Court neither invents such a claim nor rules it forfeited because its preservation is not established here. It leaves narrow tailoring, statutory vote dilution, the applicable scrutiny for an actually supported independent injury and the district's validity against other claims undecided.

##### II. Summary judgment cannot resolve a genuine material inference against the nonmovant

**Holding and operative rule:** When an actually cognizable claim makes motive material, a supported genuine dispute between racial and political explanations cannot be resolved against the nonmoving party on summary judgment. Circumstantial proof can support summary judgment in an appropriate case; neither district shape nor racial and partisan correlation is automatically conclusive.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I at this ordinary procedural level, without agreeing on which constitutional claim may proceed.

**Controlling explanation:** The State offered a supported political explanation for the boundaries. The court could not treat the contrary motive inference as a trial finding on this summary record. Johnson's evidentiary treatment separates proof from legal consequence. Under the Court's substantive holding, no motive trial follows on a design-only theory; under any actually preserved independent claim, ordinary materiality and evidentiary rules govern. The Court does not treat new reply-brief material as evidence that was before the district court or decide the denial of the State's cross-motion as a separate component.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Shaw v. Reno is applied: territorial racial composition alone does not establish the asserted unequal-treatment injury.
- Johnson v. Miller controls both the substantive insufficiency of predominance alone and the distinction between evidentiary dispute and legal materiality; its genuine-injury protections and limits remain.
- Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson is distinguished as an actual political-access burden, not authority to infer such a burden from motive or constituency composition.

#### Law After Decision

The Court applies the existing Shaw/Johnson rule and requires a conforming remand. Predominance alone does not sustain an injunction or require a trial. Ordinary summary-judgment rules remain available for facts material to a properly presented independent claim; the four-Justice proposed predominance trigger is not governing law.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas:** These Justices agree that the existing summary judgment and injunction cannot stand. They maintain their position that predominant racial assignment subordinating ordinary districting criteria should trigger strict scrutiny, and would retain that claim for factfinding. Mere racial awareness, a numerical racial percentage and an unusual shape are not automatic invalidity even under their rule; the State's supported political explanation precludes the present adverse summary judgment. Justification and fit would follow only if the proposed trigger were proved. Their disagreement with the Court concerns the constitutional premise and the remand's foreclosure of the design-only theory, not a vote to affirm the district court's invalidation.

#### Source Notes

The argument date, district-court posture and contested evidence are reported in the [official United States Reports, volume 526, pages 541–558](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/526bv.pdf). The constitutional rule and remand follow Shaw and Johnson; the existence and preservation of an independent claim remain unresolved. The replacement-plan condition is set out in [1998 North Carolina Session Laws chapter 2, §1.1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/SessionLaws/HTML/1997-1998/SL1998-2.html); its application to this judgment is left open.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must proceed consistently with the Court's rejection of the design-only theory and its preserved-claim limits. No replacement map, new election, mandatory motive trial or later Supreme Court event is ordered. The Court orders neither automatic restoration of the predecessor map nor displacement of the replacement map and does not invalidate the completed 1998 elections.

**End of entry: Hunt v. Cromartie, merits decision, May 17, 1999.**

### Christensen v. Harris County, No. 98-1167

Merits decision, May 1, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 23, 2000. On certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 158 F.3d 241, whose decision in No. 97-20796 was captioned Moreau v. Harris County. The questions are whether the Fair Labor Standards Act requires a specific agreement before a public employer may direct use of accrued compensatory time, and what weight the Labor Department's contrary opinion letter receives under the statute and regulations.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Fifth Circuit's final judgment for the County and all other defendants is affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The dissenting Justices would set aside that judgment and remand for actual agreement coverage, terms and validity under a consent requirement.

The employees' categorical declaration and dependent $21,360 fee award remain displaced. The Court orders no agreement-specific proceedings, negotiations, trial, damages or new fee award. The policy remains subject to express statutory protections, binding agreements and other applicable law. Abandoned wrongful-refusal and retaliation claims are not revived.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas throughout; Scalia in Parts I and III | Six Justices support affirmance; Part II's rationale has five | Part I: statutory use and remedy, six. Part II: advisory-letter statutory weight, five. Part III: regulatory textual fit, six. |
| Opinion concurring in part and in the judgment | Scalia |  | Supports affirmance | Joins Parts I and III; withholds Part II. |
| Concurrence | Souter |  | Joins the Court throughout and supports affirmance | Preserves the future-regulation question. |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg and Breyer | Would set aside the judgment and remand | Supports consent-based agreement proceedings. |
| Additional dissent | Breyer | Ginsburg | Supports agreement-specific remand | Explains independently persuasive and conditionally authoritative agency grounds. |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. The statute does not make employee requests the exclusive means of using accrued time

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 207(o) does not categorically forbid a public employer from directing paid use of lawfully accrued compensatory time merely because no agreement specifically authorizes compulsory use. The employer remains bound by lawful agreement restrictions, protected employee requests and the statute's accrual, value and overtime requirements; compelled paid use is not permission to confiscate credits or evade overtime.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, six Justices in all.

**Controlling explanation:** Paragraph (5) protects an employee who requests accrued time: use must be allowed within a reasonable period unless it would unduly disrupt operations. It does not say that a request is the only permissible trigger for use. Paragraph (2)'s agreement requirement governs substituting time for cash overtime, not every later scheduling choice. Moreau preserves its actual agreement routes; neither it nor the statute establishes the terms of this class's agreements.

The accrual cap limits how much time may be accumulated and requires cash for additional overtime at the cap; it does not promise employees the right to reach that cap. The permitted cashout likewise contradicts an absolute right to retain every credit, although cash payment and paid absence remain different transactions. Paid time away from work consumes credits in their defined form: the employee receives the regular rate for hours not worked, which are excluded from hours worked when computing overtime. These statutory reasons suffice without a general presumption that employers control every unregulated workplace question.

The competing consent construction treats the substitute for cash as exclusively employee-controlled. It does not follow from the original agreement requirement or protected request right. An actual lawful term can restrict compulsory use, but none identified here defeats the categorical statutory judgment. That absence does not prove no agreement exists or every employee consented. It supplies no automatic right to new agreement proceedings. The decision leaves constitutional property questions, other labor law and actual wrongful-refusal claims outside this review.

**Operative qualifications:** For the covered state, local and interstate public agencies, a wholly time-based substitute must accrue at least one-and-one-half hours for each overtime hour under the applicable representative agreement or prior individual understanding. For employees outside the applicable representative-agreement route who were hired before April 15, 1986, regular practice on that date itself supplies the agreement; no additional individual agreement is required. Moreau preserves the later-hire individual route where the chosen representative cannot lawfully enter a binding agreement. Unwritten understandings require a record; an express employment condition requires knowing, voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object may evidence assent, but coercion or pressure may not substitute for it. Compatible agreements may mix cash and time or set use terms while preserving the statutory premium; inconsistent terms yield to the Act.

The cap is 480 hours for the specified public-safety, emergency-response or seasonal work, and 240 for other covered work. Additional overtime at the applicable cap must be compensated as overtime. Cashout may occur at any time using the regular rate when paid. Termination requires at least the higher of the final regular rate or the average over the last three years. For termination-rate averaging, employment after a break is treated as a new period when the break was intended to be permanent and post-April 14, 1986 credits were cashed out at the initial separation; if the final period is shorter than three years, averaging uses that period. No termination or rate calculation is decided here.

For employee requests, customary schedules, peak demands, emergency staffing, substitutes and compatible agreement terms inform a reasonable period. An employer must reasonably and in good faith anticipate an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service to establish undue disruption; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and the statute does not guarantee the employee's exact chosen date. Section 553.25 forbids using time to evade overtime or coercing acceptance beyond what can realistically and in good faith be made available within a reasonable request period. Its request provisions exclude separately defined other compensatory time, including pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. The separate court-reporter transcript provision is not presented in this Sheriff's Department case. No violation of these distinct protections is found.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding and operative rule:** The advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual implementing grant permits administration and regulation. It does not make every later advisory response an exercise of authority to bind courts or employers. NationsBank and Smiley involved identified authoritative exercises of delegated responsibility; they do not settle this letter's status merely by identifying its agency. Skidmore preserves its potential persuasive significance rather than discarding it by label. Its construction adds a specific-consent restriction absent from the statutory request trigger and prior-agreement condition. The record establishes no policy change, internal authorization instrument or Government endorsement on which to rest the result. Boston & Maine forbids inventing an agency ground. The Court decides this letter's advisory status on the record, not the validity of every informal agency interpretation.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Auer concerns a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. It does not impose a universal rulemaking-format prerequisite on interpretation, but requires actual textual fit. Section 553.23 permits additional consistent terms; section 553.25 protects requests and practical availability. Neither makes employee requests exclusive or requires an agreement covering every scheduling choice. The letter's interpretation therefore adds a condition rather than resolves pertinent ambiguity. The separate inquiry into statutory authority does not replace this own-regulation analysis. No conclusion here forecloses whatever authority the Secretary may possess to adopt a valid new regulation limiting compelled use.

### American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan, No. 97-2000

Merits decision, March 3, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 19, 1999; decided March 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, 139 F.3d 158. The questions concern attribution of private insurers' initial withholding pending utilization review and an asserted constitutional right to payment before the reasonable-and-necessary character of disputed treatment is established.

The action includes private carriers and distinct public parties. The proposed class was not certified. The additional notice and employee-statement procedures were excluded from petitioners' challenge; individual treatment, prior-order and public-party issues must not be conflated with the two questions presented.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental

**Holding and operative rule.** These private carriers' independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because state law permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis for responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or a function exclusively reserved to government; those predicates are not established for this initial withholding.

**Authority.** The seven-Justice coalition for Kennedy's Part I, identified above, controls this holding.

**Controlling explanation.** Blum and Rendell-Baker distinguish regulation and public support from responsibility for a particular private decision. Lugar requires both the relevant state-created framework and sufficient state involvement; invoking the first does not dispense with the second. Bureau screening for proper invocation and assignment of a reviewer do not substantively approve the carrier's medical objection or order its initial suspension. Later adjudication is a different act.

Edmonson involved private participation in constituting a governmental jury; workers' compensation insurers do not perform that exclusively governmental function. Lebron prevents government from escaping its own constitutional obligations through an institution it creates and permanently controls, but these carriers do not satisfy that institutional-status ground. Mandatory coverage and a statutory source for compensation do not establish the required connection to their particular withholding decision.

This conclusion does not immunize the legislature's own acts or every decision in the public review process. Denver Area requires attention to the actual challenged governmental act, and Tulsa likewise does not dispense with significant governmental involvement merely because a statutory procedure is not self-executing. Actual public obligors, official review decisions or a distinct proved attribution basis require their own analysis. The Court decides only the private-carrier theory supported here.

##### General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service

**Holding and operative rule.** General eligibility for workers' compensation does not by itself establish an unconditional constitutional right to constant payment of every disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. This rule neither extinguishes a protected claim to fair adjudication nor decides against a claimant an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient individual expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review.

**Authority.** The eight-Justice coalition for Kennedy's Part II, identified above, controls this holding. Stevens supports the payment disposition on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennsylvania's substantive promise concerns reasonable and necessary care. General program eligibility therefore does not make every contested invoice presently due without regard to that condition. The facial demand for constant advance payment cannot rest on that inference alone.

Logan protects a statutory claim from arbitrary destruction; it does not equate a right to fair determination with unconditional payment before the substantive condition is established. Loudermill likewise prevents government from defining substantive property away through procedure. The Court preserves that distinction rather than making an insurer's objection conclusive or requiring a new adjudication before every payment.

The allegations concerning earlier orders and ongoing treatment are not resolved by assuming the orders concern different services or that no worker has an existing entitlement. Individual reliance on prior payments also remains open. Nor does reimbursement and interest necessarily replace medical care lost during an interruption. Those limitations prevent the confined negative rule from becoming a universal no-property holding.

The categorical advance-payment/severance requirement is set aside only to this extent. The Court does not decide every procedure adequate under Mathews, impose new exhaustion or proof requirements, or adjudicate unchallenged notice/input. Distinct preserved public-party and individual claims return for ordinary resolution under their own predicates.

- **Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922:** Applied; a state-created procedure and sufficient governmental participation remain separate requirements.

- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium:** Preserved at its actual component holdings; it creates neither universal immunity for permissive legislation nor automatic attribution of every permitted private choice.
- **Tulsa Professional Collection Services v. Pope, 485 U.S. 478:** Preserved; a non-self-executing procedure does not eliminate the need to identify significant governmental involvement.
- **Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422; Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill, 470 U.S. 532:** Preserved for protected claims and the distinction between substantive entitlement and procedure; neither establishes unconditional payment of every disputed bill.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Its procedural inquiry is not replaced or applied to decide every preserved individual or public-party claim.

Private initial withholding requires its own governmental attribution basis. General benefit eligibility alone supplies no unconditional advance-payment right. Actual governmental conduct, fair adjudication of protected claims, prior service entitlements and properly supported continuation interests remain distinct. The decision establishes no universal immunity or absence of property and designs no additional statewide procedure.

#### Separate Writings

**Ginsburg concurs in Part II and in the judgment.** Rejecting constant payment of every medical bill pending the reasonable-and-necessary determination is sufficient; she does not reach private attribution. Claims for medical benefits still require fair adjudication. She agrees with Stevens that the original absence of adequate notice and employee input was deficient and that the revised features answer that particular objection, without deciding every delay, lost-care or prior-award claim.

### Davis v. United States, No. 92-1949

Merits decision, June 24, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

512 U.S. 452. Argued March 29, 1994; decided June 24, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Military Appeals, 36 M.J. 337, affirming the military conviction. The question is whether, following a valid waiver, an ambiguous counsel reference requires cessation or clarification and whether the actual questioning complied. Davis seeks reversal for admission of statements; the United States seeks affirmance.

Davis was convicted by general court-martial of unpremeditated murder under Article 118. His sentence included life confinement, dishonorable discharge, forfeiture of all pay and allowances, and reduction to E-1. The Navy-Marine Corps Court of Military Review affirmed September 16, 1991; the Court of Military Appeals affirmed March 11, 1993. The separate October 20 interview and physical-evidence issue is not reopened.

On November 4, 1988, naval investigators gave oral and written Article 31 and counsel advice and obtained a valid written waiver. About ninety minutes into questioning, Davis said, “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer.” The credited account shows an actual interruption of offense questioning, neutral clarification and a voluntary disclaimer that he was asking for counsel. After questioning resumed and a later break, agents briefly reminded him of his rights; they did not repeat all warnings or obtain a new written waiver. Davis later clearly said that he wanted a lawyer before saying anything else, and the interview stopped. The military judge credited the agents over Davis's differing account; the Court makes no new credibility finding.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participated at argument and decision. No Justice is recused.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Court of Military Appeals, 9–0. A five-Justice majority requires neutral clarification of a reasonable possible present request for counsel after waiver; the actual clarification and final cessation satisfied that rule.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Admission of challenged November 4 statements on the postwaiver invocation theory and resulting military judgment | Affirm, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | None | No exclusion, new trial or remand on this theory; the military judgment remains in force. |

The Court does not adjudicate the separate October 20 issue, invalidate the initial waiver, or decide a new §3501 alternative ground.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg throughout; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in Parts II and III | Unanimous affirmance | I: mandatory neutral clarification after a reasonable possible present request; II: actual lawful clarification and admission; III: clear-request cessation rule |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Joins Parts II and III and affirmance; does not join Part I | Would require an objectively clear request to activate cessation and permit clarification without making it compulsory |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Controlling proposition:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a valid waiver does not permit officers to exploit expressed uncertainty about whether the suspect now wants counsel. Miranda protects an effective choice during custodial questioning, and Edwards makes a request for counsel consequential. Smith distinguishes genuine initial ambiguity from an already clear request that later answers cannot erase. Barrett requires attention to the choice actually communicated; McNeil requires an expression concerning assistance during custodial questioning, rather than an unrelated counsel interest. Those decisions leave this postwaiver ambiguity question open. The contrary clear-request-only rule offers simplicity, but both approaches require officers to assess words in context. A short neutral clarification protects the choice without treating every reference as an irrevocable invocation. It also respects the initial waiver by permitting resumption when the suspect voluntarily clarifies a wish to proceed. The Court adopts this additional safeguard as the rule resolving the presented ambiguity question, not as a conclusion that the investigators' compliance alone logically compels it. Prewaiver conduct and unrelated lawyer references remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### II. The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Controlling proposition:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the military judge's credited account rather than resolving the competing testimony anew. Davis received oral and written advice and executed a valid initial waiver. His first statement, “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer,” expressed genuine uncertainty in its context. The investigators stopped offense questioning and asked whether he was requesting counsel, explaining that questioning would stop if he was. He voluntarily disclaimed a request. Barrett supports respecting the choice actually expressed, and Smith does not prohibit genuine clarification where no initially clear request has occurred. The later break brought a brief reminder of rights, not repetition of all warnings or a new written waiver. The Court does not depend on an invented second waiver. The argument for complete termination at the first remark would disregard the established distinction between uncertainty and a clear invocation. Because the actual exchange was limited and neutral, it satisfied the Court's clarification rule and also fell within the permissible questioning accepted by the concurring Justices.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### III. A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Controlling proposition:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court preserves the distinction between deciding whether a request was made and deciding whether questioning may continue after one. Edwards supplies the cessation rule and its narrow routes for further communication; an earlier waiver does not authorize police to disregard a later invocation. Smith prevents officers from manufacturing uncertainty by relying on answers obtained after an initially clear request. Neither principle requires a suspect to use technical language. The inquiry concerns what the words reasonably communicate in their actual setting. Davis's first remark was uncertain on the credited record, so neutral clarification was permissible. His later statement that he wanted a lawyer before saying anything else was a clear request, and the agents stopped. The Court therefore has no ensuing interrogation to exclude on that ground. This application does not decide a different case involving pressure after invocation, the validity of an unpresented later waiver, or Sixth Amendment attachment; it leaves those questions to their governing rules.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

#### Scope and questions not reached

The Court decides postwaiver invocation within the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules below. It does not independently resolve the Fifth Amendment's direct operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, adequacy of the initial warnings, an invalid initial waiver, or the separate October 20 interview/physical-evidence dispute. The Government did not invoke 18 U.S.C. §3501 below or rely on it as its merits ground. The Court does not decide that statute's military applicability, construction or constitutionality, and it supplies no alternative ground for affirmance. Withrow's collateral-review and independent-coercion rules and Stansbury's objective custody inquiry remain distinct. The exact rule has five direct joins, and the actual application and clear-request protections have nine; no Marks aggregation is used.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

The holding-specific treatments above govern. No precedent is overruled. Miranda's counsel protection is developed for genuine postwaiver ambiguity; Edwards and Smith retain their clear-request safeguards. Barrett and McNeil retain their distinct limits concerning communicated choice and the relevant counsel interest. Withrow remains the rule for Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims; Stansbury remains the objective custody rule. Neither resolves this distinct invocation issue by itself.

Effective June 24, 1994, a valid initial waiver does not permit continued substantive questioning through words reasonably understood as a possible present request for counsel. Officers must pause and clarify neutrally, maintaining the pause while that reasonable uncertainty remains. A clarified voluntary choice to proceed permits resumption, while a clear request invokes Edwards. Unrelated lawyer references and unexpressed wishes do not trigger the new duty. No prescribed legal phrasing is required.

The military framework is accepted as applied below and by the parties. Direct Fifth Amendment operation in the military, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct and the unpressed §3501 theory remain undecided. The four-Justice optional-clarification position is noncontrolling. Actual lawful clarification and honoring a clear final request independently support the unanimous affirmance.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, joins the actual-clarification application and clear-request protections but rejects a mandatory pause for an uncertain reference. Following a knowing and voluntary waiver, these Justices would require words clear enough that a reasonable officer would understand counsel to be requested. Their rule demands no legal terminology or prescribed formula. McNeil requires communication of the particular custodial-counsel interest, and Edwards's cessation protection follows invocation rather than a possible undisclosed wish. The initial waiver remains significant until the suspect objectively communicates a changed choice. Limited clarification is often sensible and is lawful here, but its practical value does not itself establish a legal duty. These Justices accept Smith's prohibition on using later answers to undo a clear request and do not approve coercive persuasion disguised as clarification. They accept the credited first ambiguity, the actual neutral exchange and voluntary disclaimer, and cessation upon the final clear request. They do not rely on a new waiver, decide prewaiver conduct, or adopt the unpressed §3501 theory. Their no-duty rule has four votes; Part I's mandatory rule controls.

### Dickerson v. United States, No. 99-5525

Plenary merits decision, June 26, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 19, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, reviewing 166 F.3d 667 (1999), an interlocutory suppression appeal in a federal bank-robbery and firearm prosecution. There is no conviction or sentence under review. The granted question concerns section 3501's asserted authorization of case-in-chief admission notwithstanding Miranda; the separate challenges to the circuit's taking up that statute and its physical-evidence ruling are outside the grant.

The district court found that Dickerson was in custody during the January 27, 1997 FBI interview and that the disputed statements preceded warnings and waiver. It declined to reopen the record for the Government's later affidavit and handwritten-statement proffer because previous unavailability was not shown; the Fourth Circuit sustained that refusal. The circuit treated the statements as voluntary by inference from the district court's derivative-evidence ruling. That is the appellate court's inference, not a fresh finding by this Court or proof that warnings preceded the statements.

The United States supports Dickerson on the statutory-displacement question and opposes overruling Miranda. Paul Cassell's defending amicus brief presents the principal contrary arguments, including the distinct request for judicial reconsideration. The Government retains its prosecution; its merits position is not an admission that every item of evidence must be suppressed.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded; statement suppression restored, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as section 3501 supplied its basis for reversing statement suppression. The District Court's order suppressing the disputed statement from the prosecution's case in chief is restored, subject to a properly preserved exception, and the prosecution returns for further proceedings consistent with Miranda. The independent physical-evidence disposition is undisturbed. The Court neither reopens the suppression record nor directs a new hearing, dismisses the indictment, acquits Dickerson, orders release or vacates a nonexistent conviction.

#### Holdings

##### I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone

**Holding and operative rule:** Miranda's warnings and waiver requirements implement the constitutional privilege against compelled self-incrimination in custodial interrogation, and Congress cannot authorize case-in-chief use of a statement that those requirements exclude merely because the statement is voluntary under 18 U.S.C. §3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain permissible; no exact verbal formula is indispensable, and an unwarned statement is not necessarily actually coerced for every purpose.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct rationale votes control the constitutional rule, statutory application, qualifications and suppression remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda supplies a prospective safeguard for an informed choice during custodial interrogation, not merely a later inquiry into whether a completed statement was coerced. Its operation in state prosecutions and the constitutional review preserved by Withrow cannot rest on this Court's supervisory authority over federal procedure. Congress may provide effective alternatives, but it may not remove the protection merely by reinstating the voluntariness inquiry Miranda found insufficient.

Section 3501 requires the judge to examine voluntariness outside the jury's presence, consider all circumstances including warnings and counsel, and permit the jury to assess the voluntary statement's weight. These are real protections. Their defect as a replacement is functional: retrospective examination does not itself inform the suspect before speaking. Smith v. Robbins distinguishes freedom to vary a safeguard's form from omission of its protected function; it does not decide this different safeguard's adequacy by analogy alone. Proposed training, civil remedies and warning incentives do not establish an equally effective replacement on this record, and civil relief cannot be assumed available regardless of its cause-of-action and immunity conditions.

The contrary argument relies on Tucker, Elstad, Harris, Hass and Quarles. Those decisions distinguish actual compulsion from warning-based exclusion and preserve particular derivative uses, later warned statements, impeachment or public-safety questioning. They do not make the original unwarned statement generally admissible in the prosecution's case in chief. The Court preserves those distinctions rather than equating every warning failure with actual coercion.

Here the district court found custody and found the disputed statements preceded warnings and waiver. The Fourth Circuit's voluntariness conclusion cannot substitute for the missing safeguard. Its section-3501 basis for reversing statement suppression fails. The statement-suppression order is restored and the prosecution remanded, subject to a properly preserved exception; no new credibility finding is made.

**Statutory qualifications:** Subsection (a) concerns federal or District of Columbia criminal prosecutions. It requires the judge to resolve voluntariness outside the jury's presence and, upon finding the confession voluntary, to allow the jury to hear relevant voluntariness evidence and instruct it to give the confession the weight it deserves in light of all the circumstances. Its admission command remains subject to the constitutional safeguard stated above. Under subsection (b), all circumstances must be considered, including the arrest-to-arraignment interval when applicable, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge concerning silence and use, prior advice of counsel rights, and absence of counsel during questioning and confession. The presence or absence of a listed factor need not be conclusive; consideration is mandatory, conclusiveness is not. Independent voluntariness review survives.

The Court does not invalidate the whole section. Subsection (c) protects a voluntary confession against exclusion solely for presentment delay when its weight is left to the jury and it is given within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the time limit does not apply when the judge finds a longer delay reasonable considering transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. Alvarez-Sanchez's relevant-duty inquiry and unresolved post-six-hour and McNabb–Mallory questions remain. Subsection (d) does not bar voluntary statements given without interrogation or while the speaker is not under arrest or detention; those alternatives remain distinct. Subsection (e) includes an admission of guilt and any oral or written self-incriminating statement. Each provision remains subject to constitutional requirements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic civil liability, general fruit-of-the-statement rule, mandatory warning wording or elimination of recognized exceptions follows. The separate apartment-evidence ruling, car-evidence dispute, refusal to reopen the suppression record and circuit authority to raise section 3501 are outside this review. The Court orders no new suppression hearing, dismissal, acquittal, release or vacatur of a conviction; none exists at this interlocutory stage.

##### II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to replace Miranda's core case-in-chief safeguard with a voluntariness-only rule: congressional preference for that rule and the existing distinctions between unwarned and actually compelled statements do not supply sufficient justification for abandonment here. Retention of the core leaves genuinely effective alternatives and the recognized, separately bounded exceptions available.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the additional stare-decisis ground. Stone-Zsela agrees that the alternative request for judicial abandonment is rejected but adds no ground and does not join Part II. No Marks combination is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** The amicus defending the judgment separately asks this Court to revise Miranda even if Congress cannot displace it. That request requires a judicial justification independent of the statute's existence. Miranda has supplied the governing framework for custodial interrogation, and subsequent decisions have retained its case-in-chief core while defining limits. Existing administration and reliance count against replacing that framework without an adequate reason.

The exceptions do not establish the claimed doctrinal collapse. Elstad distinguishes a later voluntary warned statement from the original unwarned statement; Tucker concerns derivative evidence; Harris and Hass concern impeachment; Quarles addresses public safety. Their tailored consequences can coexist with a constitutional safeguard broader than a retrospective finding of actual compulsion. They do not silently overrule Miranda.

Claims about interrogation costs, improved training and other remedies deserve consideration, but they do not establish that the prospective informed-choice function has become unnecessary or has an adequate substitute here. The Court need not declare every application costless, every warning formulation immutable or every judicial implementation rule beyond revision. The constitutional function and the absence of sufficient grounds to abandon this settled core justify rejecting the requested change.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966):** Its constitutional custodial-interrogation safeguard, case-in-chief exclusion and allowance for equally effective alternatives are retained. Voluntariness alone cannot replace its applicable requirements.
- **Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993):** Its constitutional habeas operation supports Miranda's status; its distinction between a warning claim and separately presented coercion remains. No new collateral-review rule is adopted.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433 (1974):** Its derivative-evidence setting and distinction between safeguards and actual compulsion remain; it does not authorize general case-in-chief use of the original unwarned statement.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298 (1985):** Its treatment of a later voluntary warned statement remains; the original unwarned statement's exclusion is not erased.
- **Harris v. New York, 401 U.S. 222 (1971), and Oregon v. Hass, 420 U.S. 714 (1975):** Their impeachment uses remain subject to their own requirements and do not become ordinary prosecution case-in-chief permission.
- **New York v. Quarles, 467 U.S. 649 (1984):** Its public-safety exception remains limited to its governing conditions; the decision does not replace the general warning rule.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 (1986):** Police coercion remains necessary to due-process involuntariness; absence of warnings alone does not establish that distinct defect.
- **Smith v. Robbins, January 19, 2000:** Its distinction between equivalent form and preservation of the required function informs the substitute inquiry; its appellate-counsel setting does not itself decide confession admissibility.
- **United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 511 U.S. 350 (1994):** Its subsection-(c) relevant-presentment-duty rule, six-hour protection, transportation-and-distance proviso and reservations remain. Today's decision resolves the distinct voluntariness-only displacement question that it reserved.
- **Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318 (1994), and Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99 (1995):** Their objective custody rule and distinction between ultimate constitutional classification and historical facts remain; no new custody or credibility finding is made.
- **Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452 (1994):** Its actual postwaiver clarification, clear-invocation and waiver rules remain; its reservation of the overall section-3501 question is not treated as an earlier answer.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 26, 2000, Miranda's constitutional case-in-chief safeguard cannot be displaced by section 3501's voluntariness-only admission rule. The Court resolves that previously reserved relationship without invalidating the section's distinct lawful functions. Equally effective alternative safeguards remain possible, independent coercion remains a separate inquiry, and recognized exceptions keep their own conditions. Six Justices also reject the separate request to discard the core under stare decisis. No precedent is overruled and no universal rule immunizing every judicial procedure from legislation is adopted.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, dissenting, joined by Justice Thomas.** They would sustain the Fourth Circuit's statutory-admissibility basis against the presented Miranda-displacement objection, provided voluntariness is sustainably determined and the statute's required circumstances are considered. Their ground distinguishes the constitutional prohibition of actual compelled self-incrimination from a categorical exclusion triggered solely by omitted warnings. Tucker, Elstad, Harris, Hass and Quarles demonstrate that the Court already treats warning violations differently from actual compulsion in consequential settings.

The dissent confronts Miranda's application in state prosecutions and constitutional habeas review. It does not claim federal supervisory power over state courts or assert that Congress can override a constitutional rule the dissent simultaneously accepts as binding. Rather, it would judicially reject the asserted constitutional necessity of an irrebuttable case-in-chief bar for a sustainably voluntary statement, and then apply section 3501's federal admissibility framework. That reconsideration is a proposed doctrinal change, not a claim that prior decisions have already overruled Miranda.

Scalia and Thomas regard the tension between actual compulsion and categorical exclusion as sufficient reason for reconsideration despite settled practice and their own prior applications of Miranda doctrine. In their view, institutional familiarity cannot cure a constitutional-authority defect. Their disagreement concerns that premise, not permission to coerce, a finding that warnings preceded these statements or a promise of universally available civil remedies. Subsection (b)'s circumstances remain mandatory considerations, though the presence or absence of an individual factor need not be conclusive. They would leave the prosecution to ordinary further proceedings and decide neither the independent search grounds nor the refusal to reopen the suppression record. The Court rejects their proposed change.

### Lewis v. Casey, No. 94-1511

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

#### Holdings

##### I. Effective access cannot be measured by books alone

**Operative rule:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. It guarantees neither a preferred library program, physical browsing in every case, professional counsel for every prisoner nor the decree's numerical requirements.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join.

**Controlling explanation:** Bounds identifies effective access as the protected object and leaves administrators a choice of adequate methods. Murray preserves that choice without making books an automatic safe harbor. The district findings concerning Bartholic's dismissed action and Harris's inability to file are concrete consequences of inadequate assistance. They cannot be rewritten as an absence of injury simply because a library existed.

An efficient paging system with an inventory and prompt delivery may work for prisoners capable of independent use. Respondents' conditional acknowledgment does not establish that Arizona's actual system worked or that it serves prisoners with different literacy or language needs. The complete arrangement and actual obstacle matter.

The evidence distinguishes findings from testimony and common practices from statewide conclusions. Bartholic is a named representative; Harris supplies witness evidence. No fixed total of injured prisoners follows. Nor must a prisoner prove eventual victory in a case that inadequate access prevented him from pursuing. The opinions differ on the additional threshold described in Part II, not on the reality of these preserved findings.

##### II. The access violation requires a real connection to a qualifying claim

**Operative rule:** A Bounds access-liability claim requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying, nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to the validity of restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing, but a deficient inventory, preferred staffing arrangement or abstract wish for better research does not alone establish the violation.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt this substantive access-liability rule. The additional preliminary Article III merits screen and categorical restrictions in Scalia's concurrence have only four votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Libraries and assistance are means of enabling protected legal claims, rather than independent constitutional ends. The required connection identifies what a deficient arrangement actually prevents. A technical dismissal caused by inability to meet a filing requirement, an inability to file a contemplated qualifying claim, or sufficiently concrete threatened obstruction can establish that connection. A procedural deficiency does not itself make the underlying claim substantively frivolous.

The rule is not a demand for ultimate success or a rigid sequence of preliminary prison tests. Evidence and reasonable inferences may establish the real claim and obstacle before an avoidable filing failure occurs. Lujan's ordinary requirements of personal injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain; today's five-Justice proposition does not additionally require preliminary proof of legal merits as an Article III screening rule.

Direct and collateral sentence challenges and prison-conditions claims fall within the governing doctrine. Genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin's actual substantive-constraint rule. The Court does not adopt a categorical exclusion of all other important retained rights, declare that every affirmative assistance duty ends on filing, or provide a general subsidy for private civil litigation. Properly presented independent communications or petition claims retain their own predicates. Those reservations do not turn every resource request into an access violation.

##### III. The scope of the wrong and the needs of repair control relief

**Operative rule:** Common relief must correspond to a demonstrated common barrier and an identified affected group; individual obstruction or a resource deficiency alone does not establish an unrestricted statewide violation. Relief must respect restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests and give the State a genuine opportunity to devise an effective cure, while permitting measures actually necessary to repair the proved wrong.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this rule and the broader remand. Stevens supports a narrower remedial remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower courts' core-Bounds shortcut allowed resource deficiencies to do the work of proving the full violation. The findings preserve actual obstruction but do not establish the same denial at every institution, or separate broad entitlements for all non-English-speaking or lockdown prisoners. Bartholic's location in a lockdown unit does not itself show injury caused by lockdown status rather than inadequate assistance. This evidentiary limit is not a finding that only two inmates were ever injured.

Common evidence may prove a common wrong; every class member need not already have lost a lawsuit. Geographic and functional reach nevertheless require support. Jenkins permits necessary implementation of a demonstrated violation without making each salary, hour or program measure an independent constitutional right. It does not justify general prison improvement or indefinite control.

Turner sustains restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests, with substantial deference especially relevant to dangerous or specially restricted prisoners. Delay caused by a valid restriction does not become unconstitutional merely because an access injury results. Conversely, asserting security does not prove that every restriction is valid; the actual relationship must be examined.

Arizona had participated and objected, but an opportunity merely to rebut a presumptive statewide Gluth template did not provide the required genuine initial design role. Earlier resistance elsewhere does not establish obstruction here or forfeit that role. The State must now receive that opportunity within enforceable constitutional limits. These conclusions warrant setting aside the unsupported approval while retaining findings, common evidence and lawful targeted relief.

##### IV. The original PLRA governs existing prospective relief and any replacement

**Operative rule:** Section 802 applies amended section 3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996; relief here requires findings that it is narrowly drawn, extends no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation of the particular plaintiffs, and is the least intrusive means necessary, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Relief exceeding official state-law authority or otherwise violating state or local law additionally requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation, and absence of any other corrective relief.

**Authority:** Part IV of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices. The Chief Justice joins the statutory remand disposition without adding a ground. The same eight-Justice authority governs the distinct statutory conditions in Holdings V and VI.

**Controlling explanation:** The enactment expressly reaches existing prospective relief, including this federal prison-conditions decree. Prospective relief includes relief other than compensatory monetary damages. The habeas exclusion does not remove a prison-conditions action merely because the access services may assist habeas litigation.

The existing findings may establish necessary predicates at their actual scope; the statute requires substance, not ritual wording. But reasonableness, modest cost or absence of demonstrated hardship does not replace need, narrowness and least intrusiveness. Effective alternatives and public safety require actual evaluation. Necessary implementation can qualify even when each measure is not itself a freestanding constitutional entitlement.

Section 3626(a)(1)(B)'s three conditions operate separately from (A). Subsection (C) confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable limits; it does not create a new prohibition on every otherwise lawful means. No state-law conflict or prisoner-release remedy is found here.

Any new preliminary relief must be otherwise authorized, narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary for the harm requiring it, and least intrusive, with substantial weight to safety and criminal-system effects and respect for the separate state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the required prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. The old permanent decree is not automatically converted into a new preliminary order.

No constitutional challenge to section 802 is decided. The decree must be reconsidered under these actual terms without erasing established access wrongs or presuming that every old measure must survive or disappear.

##### V. Termination and stays depend on their own statutory predicates

**Operative rule:** The original section 3626 termination provisions do not dissolve relief merely upon enactment: the immediate-termination entitlement for relief entered without the required findings remains subject to written, record-based findings that relief is necessary to correct a current OR ongoing federal-right violation and remains narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary, and least intrusive. The automatic stay requires an actual pending qualifying motion and runs only for the original statutory period described below.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice Part IV coalition identified above; the Chief Justice agrees with the statutory disposition without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Under (b)(1), any party or intervener may seek termination at the applicable two-year period after relief was granted or approved, one year after denial of a termination motion under that paragraph, or, for preenactment relief, two years after enactment. Earlier agreement is permitted. Under (b)(2), a defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination when the necessary findings were absent, subject to (b)(3)'s written findings on the record. The original condition is current OR ongoing, not a requirement that both words separately be satisfied. Section (b)(4) preserves earlier modification or termination otherwise legally available.

Courts must promptly decide modification or termination motions. Under original (e), relief subject to a pending (b)(1) or (b)(2) motion is automatically stayed beginning on the thirtieth day after filing; for a motion under other law, the period begins on the 180th day. It ends when the court enters the final order deciding the motion. No qualifying filing is established here, and the Court creates none.

Consent decrees remain subject to subsection (a). Private agreements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct, and available state-law remedies for breach remain. The statute does not govern relief entered by a state court solely on state-law claims. These distinctions authorize no new settlement or termination in this case.

The present remand follows review of the decree and intervening law. It is not an automatic stay, a ruling on any termination motion or a finding that current necessary relief is unavailable.

##### VI. Continued use of a special master must satisfy the original statute

**Operative rule:** A special master in this prison-conditions remedy must be disinterested and objective, give due regard to public safety, and be warranted by a finding of sufficient remedial complexity; appointments, compensation, work and continued need must comply with section 3626(f)'s separate conditions below. Those requirements apply to the master's statutory role regardless of title and do not themselves establish misconduct or invalidate every prior act or accrued fee.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice Part IV coalition identified above; the Chief Justice agrees with the statutory disposition without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** When an appointment under (f) is necessary, the institution and plaintiffs each submit up to five names. Each side may strike up to three from the opposing list, and the court selects from the remaining names. A party may bring the prescribed interlocutory appeal on partiality. Compensation may not exceed the section 3006A hourly rate for appointed counsel, plus reasonably incurred costs; Judiciary appropriations supply payment.

The court must review continuing need every six months. Authorized hearings and proposed findings must be on the record; ex parte findings and communications are forbidden. A master may assist development of remedial plans and may be removed at any time, but service must end when the relief terminates. The statute is not evaded by calling a person exercising a master's powers something else.

The already ordered opportunity to challenge fees survives. Actual questions about accrued charges and temporal application require determination on remand; the Court orders no repayment and assumes no retrospective invalidity of all work. The district court may retain necessary lawful assistance only on the statutory conditions. No automatic new appointment follows from the complexity of the former decree.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817, and Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539:** Preserve meaningful access and effective methods rather than a constitutional library specification. Bounds is not overruled; four proposed categorical limits do not become Court law.
- **Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1:** Preserves choice among effective assistance arrangements without a general right to appointed counsel for every prisoner.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, and City of Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95:** Preserve personal injury, causation, appropriate prospective exposure and proof at the litigation stage. The Court does not adopt the four-Justice preliminary merits screen or decide standing in gross from a party concession.
- **Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78:** Applies reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, with actual examination and proper deference rather than automatic acceptance of a security assertion.
- **Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995:** Applies violation-linked repair while preserving implementation measures necessary to correct a proved wrong. Each means need not independently violate the Constitution.
- **Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367:** Preserves lawful responsive modification; the new statute's distinct mandatory requirements must also be applied.
- **Sandin v. Conner, June 19, 1995:** Preserves its actual rule for meaningful state-created prison liberty under genuine substantive constraints. No categorical hardship replacement is imported to exclude access to those claims.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc., June 30, 1994:** Preserves connection between remedial terms and their predicate; its speech-specific standard is not a universal least-restrictive-means rule or a substitute for section 3626.

No precedent is overruled. Gluth's earlier remedy supplies background and a proposed template, not a finding of statewide injury in this record.

#### Law After Decision

The controlling access-liability rule requires actual or evidence-supported imminent obstruction of a qualifying nonfrivolous claim, without requiring ultimate success or an impossible completed filing. Five Justices adopt that rule; only four would additionally make preliminary arguable-merits proof a jurisdictional screen, impose their subgroup-standing rule, or categorically confine affirmative Bounds capability to sentence/conditions claims and initial presentation. Those additional propositions are not law.

Effective assistance, security, represented scope and violation-linked relief remain distinct. Supported common proof may justify corresponding common relief, but the existing findings do not sustain this unrestricted statewide decree. The demonstrated access wrong survives. The original PLRA independently controls prospective relief, termination procedures, conditional stays and masters at the exact scopes above; enactment creates no motion or automatic dissolution.

The former decree's operational numbers are not constitutional minima. Its library-placement rule had a 150-person population-or-capacity trigger and exceptions for Aspen DWI, Flamenco, Papago DWI, Maricopa and Picacho; exempt facilities still owed access opportunities. Documented safety/security inability and medical determinations permitted postponement. Requested use was ten actual hours weekly with extra deadline time; opening hours varied between fifty without advance scheduling and sixty, seventy or eighty under population-based scheduling bands. Assistance was needs-based, and librarian qualifications included library-science, law or paralegal degrees.

The phone provision concerned three twenty-minute weekly calls to attorneys, designated representatives or legal organizations, with extra emergency calls, private unrecorded communications, payment or collect calling, exclusions for uncompleted calls and a documented-abuse qualification. ### Pearson v. Hines; Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-17259 and 95-17260, 108 F.3d 265

Decision, June 22, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit’s affirmance of the judgment against Pearson. The questions concern a knowingly false disciplinary accusation retaliating for a prisoner’s lawful grievance, the effect of Superintendent v. Hill’s some-evidence rule, and the asserted need for an additional injury or actual chilling.

The conduct occurred January 19, 1992. Evidence included Hines’s prior grievances, guards’ descriptions of him as a frequent complainer and his statement that morning that he would file a grievance against Pearson. Pearson did not challenge the jury’s finding that his accusation was false. The discipline imposed ten days’ confinement and loss of television. The jury awarded $100 compensatory and $1,000 punitive damages against Pearson. The separate hearing officer’s postverdict judgment is outside this review; qualified immunity is not presented.

All nine Justices participated in the merits determination.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Pearson’s $100 compensatory and $1,000 punitive judgment remains in force in the reviewed respect. No new damages, injunction, release, expungement, credit restoration or judgment against another officer is ordered.

#### Opinions and Joins

Justice Ginsburg delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. All nine join the retaliation, Hill, injury, proof and sufficiency reasoning and the bounded affirmance.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A false retaliatory charge causing concrete discipline violates the protected grievance right

**Controlling proposition:** A correctional officer may not knowingly file a false disciplinary accusation because a prisoner filed or threatened to file a lawful grievance when the accusation causes concrete discipline such as the confinement and television loss established here. Those consequences supply adverse action without additional proof of subjective chilling, abandonment of grievances, a separate due-process deprivation or loss of a nonfrivolous court case.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts both holdings, their stated limits and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Lawful use or threatened use of the prison grievance system is protected petitioning. A knowingly false charge motivated by that activity advances no legitimate penological objective. Perry and Umbehr distinguish the absence of an entitlement to a benefit from a constitutional prohibition on retaliatory withdrawal; the prison setting does not make concrete punishment for protected petitioning harmless.

The actual ten-day confinement and television loss establish consequences. Lewis requires claim-specific obstruction for an access-to-courts claim; it does not add a lost-lawsuit element to this different claim. Sandin addresses meaningful liberty under genuine substantive constraints, through a sufficient and nonexclusive rule; it does not supply the elements of First Amendment retaliation. Edwards concerns claims necessarily invalidating outstanding earned-credit deprivation. No credit loss or sentence extension is established here, and this judgment does not invalidate the disciplinary adjudication.

The decision protects no false grievance, threat or independent rule violation, and does not prohibit lawful discipline or valid prison restrictions. It decides no liability for an accusation causing no material consequence, every adverse comment, trivial inconvenience or subjective offense.

##### II. Hill’s review of the adjudication does not shield the accusing officer’s retaliation

**Controlling proposition:** Hill’s some-evidence standard for a disciplinary adjudication does not defeat a claim that the accusing officer knowingly fabricated the charge in retaliation for protected conduct. Under ordinary proof and review rules, the prisoner must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the officer may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct, but a merely possible lawful explanation is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Hill asks whether evidence supports the disciplinary decision for its due-process purpose. It does not license an official to supply knowingly false accusations for a forbidden reason. Crawford-El rejects special motive-proof barriers and preserves competent circumstantial evidence, while Mt. Healthy supplies the actual same-decision defense.

The jury could consider Hines’s grievance history and reputation, his stated intent to grieve Pearson and the unchallenged falsity finding. Ordinary deference to a jury’s supported findings governs; the Court makes no new credibility determination. Pearson’s proposed some-evidence shortcut cannot replace the actual causation inquiry or supply a special heightened quantum. The verdict stands on the reviewed grounds. The Court decides no qualified-immunity question, converts no later law into notice at the January 1992 conduct date and reopens no separate officer’s judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Superintendent v. Hill:** Its evidentiary minimum for disciplinary adjudication remains intact, but does not shield the accuser’s knowingly false retaliation.
- **Crawford-El v. Britton:** Ordinary direct and circumstantial motive proof governs without a special quantum; its separate conduct-date immunity defense is not adjudicated here.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle:** Its motivating-factor inquiry and defendant’s preponderance same-decision defense apply; possible lawful reasons are not proof of that defense.
- **Perry v. Sindermann and Board of County Commissioners v. Umbehr:** Their distinction between benefit entitlement and prohibited retaliation informs this claim, without importing the contractor-specific balance.
- **Lewis v. Casey:** Its court-access hindrance rule is confined to that distinct claim and adds no lost-lawsuit requirement here.
- **Sandin v. Conner:** Its substantive-constraint/meaningful-liberty rule remains distinct from retaliation; no atypical-hardship test is adopted.
- **Edwards v. Balisok:** Its credit-invalidity prerequisite is preserved at its actual scope; the described discipline establishes no credit loss or sentence extension.

#### Law After Decision

A knowingly false retaliatory accusation causing the concrete disciplinary consequences present here cannot be insulated by Hill’s some-evidence rule or a demand that the prisoner actually abandon grievances. Ordinary proof, causation, the actual same-decision defense and lawful prison restrictions remain. No universal code covering consequence-free accusations or every prison inconvenience is adopted.

The Ninth Circuit’s affirmance of Pearson’s damages judgment stands. No further merits remand is directed, and the other officer’s judgment and the disciplinary adjudication receive no new disposition.

### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., No. 92-1168

Merits decision, 1993-11-09

On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 976 F.2d 733 (1992) (table), which affirmed a Middle District of Tennessee judgment for Forklift after a bench trial. Argued October 13, 1993; decided November 9, 1993. The question is whether sex-based harassment must cause serious psychological injury to create a hostile or abusive environment under Title VII. Harris seeks reconsideration under the correct standard; Forklift seeks affirmance on the findings already made. Harris managed Forklift from 1985 to 1987. President Charles Hardy repeatedly directed sex-based insults and sexual innuendo at her, promised to stop after her complaint, and resumed; she left in October 1987. The trial court treated serious psychological effects and injury as important prerequisites in a close case. No quid pro quo claim is presented.

The Court reverses the Sixth Circuit's judgment and remands, 9–0. Justice O'Connor delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine support the complete rule, its application and limits, and reversal with remand; no Justice opposes the judgment. Justices Scalia and Ginsburg each file a concurrence joined by no other Justice, while also joining the Court's opinion in full.

Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. 

The Court holds that Title VII protects the conditions in which an employee works, without requiring the employee first to suffer a psychological injury. Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson supplies the requirement of discriminatory conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter those conditions. Its examples of extreme mistreatment illustrate the rule; they do not establish a minimum psychiatric consequence. The objective requirement excludes conduct that merely offends, while actual perception of abuse connects the claim to the employee's own working environment. Context determines whether those requirements are met, rather than any mandatory diagnosis or measurable decline in productivity. Forklift accepts that serious psychological harm is unnecessary but contends that the findings independently support judgment. They do not permit that conclusion here: the trial court repeatedly relied on psychological well-being and injury when resolving what it described as a close case. The president's repetition of degrading conduct after Harris complained must be assessed under the proper standard. Reversal therefore requires reconsideration, not an appellate finding of liability. The Court does not decide constructive discharge, damages, a new sex-specific reasonable-person test, or the proposed EEOC regulations.

*Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

Meritor's hostile-environment framework remains controlling, clarified to exclude a psychological-injury prerequisite and to require the objective and subjective assessment described above. Hicks's distinct circumstantial-proof and persuasion rules are unchanged; this decision creates no alternative burden allocation or employer-liability rule.

Effective November 9, 1993, the governing Title VII inquiry is severe-or-pervasive discriminatory alteration of working conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as such, assessed from all circumstances without an indispensable injury or work-loss factor. Psychological consequences remain possible evidence. Mere offense remains insufficient. The decision does not resolve the proposed EEOC regulations, constructive discharge, ultimate liability, damages, or a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation.

Scalia concurs and joins the Court's opinion in full. He emphasizes that the contextual standard cannot mechanically determine close cases. Making work impairment an indispensable element would provide a sharper boundary, but the statutory protection of employment conditions supplies no basis for that restriction. The inquiry is discriminatory alteration of working conditions, not proof of measurable lost output or a separate psychological injury. His reservation about predictability does not withdraw any part of his join.

Ginsburg concurs and joins the Court's opinion in full. Her equality explanation asks whether sex-based conduct imposes disadvantageous working conditions. Difficulty doing the job may demonstrate that alteration without a decline in tangible productivity. That emphasis remains subject to the Court's requirements of objective hostility and the employee's actual perception of abuse; it neither substitutes subjective sensitivity for the objective inquiry nor turns measurable work loss into a required element. These concurrences explain the rule's limits and are noncontrolling additional explanations.

**Source Notes:** Title VII, 42 U.S.C. §2000e-2(a)(1); Meritor, 477 U.S. 57; Sixth Circuit judgment reported at 976 F.2d 733. The [official report, 510 U.S. 17](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep510/usrep510017/usrep510017.pdf), pages 19–23, reproduces the trial findings, relevant statutory text, and the parties' legal-standard dispute. The review question is stated in substance; no broader question is represented.

The Sixth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the stated standard, including reconsideration of the existing record and any proceedings the trial court lawfully requires. No liability finding, damages award, or constructive-discharge finding is directed; those matters are not resolved by this disposition.

### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton, Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878

Merits decision, September 8, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued and decided September 8, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the en banc judgment reported at 111 F.3d 1530, entered April 15, 1997 and amended April 28. The petition was filed June 2; the Court granted review July 14 on four employer-liability questions and received expedited merits briefing through August 29 for this sitting.

The questions concern responsibility for a hostile environment materially aided by supervisory authority outside ordinary employment scope, whether higher-management knowledge is necessary, whether preventive care and unreasonable employee nonuse can supply a complete defense without tangible employment action, and whether a formal policy suffices without effective dissemination or implementation.

Faragher worked intermittently as a City ocean lifeguard during 1985–1990. A bench trial established repeated unwelcome sex-based touching and humiliating conduct by Terry and Silverman, who exercised command over assignments, discipline, training and daily work in a unit remote from higher officials. The City did not communicate its harassment policy to that unit. Faragher complained to Gordon, another supervisor, but not higher management; the City investigated promptly after another former employee's letter. The district court awarded her one dollar on the Title VII claim against the City. The appellate court accepted the hostile-environment finding but reversed that award on employer-attribution grounds. Faragher seeks restoration; the City seeks affirmance.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in the reviewed Title VII respect and remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would vacate the reviewed Title VII disposition and remand for a determination of actual material aid under the correct rule, restoring the one-dollar judgment only if that connection is established and otherwise reaching the preserved negligence and notice grounds.

The Court reverses only the portion of the appellate judgment rejecting Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City. Its mandate requires reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further attribution findings. The separate individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions are outside this review. No new monetary award or fee determination is made.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and relationship to judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full; Stone-Zsela joins Part I only | All eight full joiners support the restoration mandate. Part I's governing actual-authority rule has nine joins; Parts II–IV have eight. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | Author alone | Joins Part I; would require limited correct-rule review of the aid connection and would reject, rather than reserve, a complete preventive-care/noncomplaint defense once that connection is proved. Agrees separately with the policy principles identified below. |

There is a majority rationale for every controlling holding. Stone-Zsela's agreement with the governing rule and particular policy principles is not agreement with the Court's conclusive application or restoration mandate.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion has nine direct joins, as stated above. Those nine directly support this rule. The eight Associates join Parts II–IV and the judgment in full; that common eight-Justice coalition controls the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII protects employment conditions and includes an employer's agent in its definition. Meritor directs an agency inquiry while rejecting both automatic responsibility for every supervisor's act and automatic immunity without management notice. Andrade supplies the required actual-power connection, and Oncale applies it while preserving discriminatory causation, an actionable environment and attribution as separate predicates.

Delegated control over daily working conditions may materially aid the discriminatory conditions even when the misconduct serves a personal purpose and violates employer policy. An employer's prohibition of harassment therefore does not alone answer whether its actual delegation aided the wrong. But employment merely providing an opportunity to encounter the plaintiff is not enough. The plaintiff must prove the connection.

Ordinary scope of employment, apparent authority and employer negligence remain distinct routes. Higher-management knowledge matters to negligence without becoming an additional element of proved actual-authority attribution. Harris and DeAngelis govern the separate contextual hostile-environment threshold; proof meeting that threshold does not itself establish agency or constructive notice. The Court applies the actual-authority route here and leaves the alternative routes undecided.

##### II. The supported operational findings establish the required connection here

**Holding and operative rule:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part II. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry controlled assignments, reprimands and personnel-file discipline and selected recruits subject to higher approval. Silverman controlled daily duties, assignments and fitness routines. Their powers actually governed the working conditions of a geographically separated unit organized through their command. The district court's agency determination rested on those powers and the workplace structure, independently of its disputed constructive-notice ground.

This was more than supervisory capacity accompanied by an opportunity for misconduct. The operative control of Faragher's daily duties and on-site working conditions supplied the authority through which these supervisors maintained the repeated abusive environment. Remoteness and ineffective policy communication reinforce that practical structure; neither becomes a required element for every future plaintiff or a substitute for actual aid. The Court attributes this established course of hostile working conditions, not every personal act of every supervisor.

Historical facts receive clear-error review; their legal significance receives independent review. The Court adds no finding that Faragher feared discharge, received an order to submit, faced a particular threat, lost wages or suffered an employment penalty. Making a firing threat or completed adverse decision indispensable would restore the restriction Andrade rejects.

The strongest remand objection is that a list of powers shows only capacity and that the trial's agency label did not expressly distinguish facilitation from rank. The supported findings go further: they describe the actual organization and exercise of control over the conditions in which the established abuse was maintained. Their legal consequence can be decided without inventing another historical fact. Meritor's unresolved harassment findings and Andrade's unresolved verdict, instruction and sufficiency questions warranted their own remands; neither requires a new proceeding on this adequate bench record. Whether constructive notice or Gordon's knowledge independently establishes liability is unnecessary to decide.

##### III. This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open

**Holding and operative rule:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part III. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees separately that an undistributed policy does not establish unreasonable nonuse and that later correction does not erase earlier attributable misconduct, but would reject the proposed complete defense rather than reserve it.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor does not make a paper grievance policy and noncomplaint conclusive. An uncommunicated procedure does not establish available protection that an employee unreasonably declined. Prompt action after eventual notice matters to the adequacy of that response; it does not establish effective protection during the earlier authority-aided environment or retroactively erase that conduct.

The grant permits consideration of a complete defense, and effective prevention and reasonable avoidance present serious interests. But Andrade already supplies a plaintiff-proved actual-aid limitation, and the City's present contention fails without deciding how genuinely effective measures would affect responsibility. The Court neither adopts a new burden-shifting formula nor rejects every possible complete defense. Nor does it decide a tangible-action exception or a general duty to complain.

No finding is made that no alternative official could ever have been approached, that the policy contained a particular promise, or that Faragher held an unproved belief. Questions about alternative reporting and whether dissemination would have exposed the misconduct belong to their proper agency, negligence or remedial inquiries; they need not be resolved against the City to sustain this independent ground. Failure to disseminate a policy alone does not prove every negligence element or create a freestanding statutory publication duty. Effective procedures, knowledge, correction and avoidable loss retain their possible legal significance where their actual predicates are presented.

##### IV. The existing nominal judgment is reinstated within the grant's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part IV and this mandate. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela instead supports the distinct conditional remand described in his separate writing.

**Controlling explanation:** The sufficient actual-authority ground supports the existing judgment independently of constructive notice, imputation of Gordon's knowledge, ordinary employment scope or apparent authority. Those alternatives remain undecided; a defect in an unnecessary alternative does not require an exploratory proceeding on the sufficient ground.

Landgraf bars attaching the 1991 Act's new §102 compensatory and punitive liabilities to this 1985–1990 conduct; its dependent jury provision creates no new jury right here. Restoration of nominal relief does not enlarge that remedy. The separate $10,000 §1983 judgment against Terry and Silverman and $500 battery punitive award against Terry remain outside the grant, along with other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions.

Farrar separates enforceable nominal relief from the reasonable fee amount under §1988. That distinct statute supplies only a limited analogy; any Title VII fee question requires its own entitlement and reasonableness analysis. The Court fixes no fee, new damages, prospective injunction, constructive-discharge finding or employment reinstatement. The appellate remand implements the existing one-dollar judgment and sets no special deadline or retained proceeding.

#### Law After Decision

The actual-authority route remains a fact-dependent ground separate from negligence, apparent authority and ordinary employment scope. Existing findings of the operational control that materially aided these hostile conditions suffice for the nominal City judgment. A paper policy, noncomplaint to higher management and eventual correction supply no defense on this record. The Court adopts neither a general preventive-care/nonuse defense nor a rule forbidding every such defense; its elements, burden, tangible-action relationship and any distinct remedial limitation remain open. No title-based liability, automatic knowledge imputation or general complaint duty follows.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I but would vacate for a limited agency determination. Meritor's agency instruction and Restatement §219(2)(d)'s aid principle give delegated power distinct work beyond ordinary scope or permission to harass. Assignments, discipline and access can facilitate coercive conditions beyond coworker proximity; actual connection remains necessary. Apparent authority separately requires employer-attributable manifestations and reasonable reliance, not belief that assault was lawfully authorized. Unsupported supervisory claims or proximity do not establish either route.

He rejects mandatory higher-management notice or a firing threat for actual-authority attribution. Harris severity does not establish constructive notice; that asks what reasonable employer care would have discovered. The command findings strongly support aid, but the trial's inference from agency status does not expressly resolve actual facilitation. He would require correct-rule application to the existing findings, with further findings only if a material issue remains. If they establish material aid, the one-dollar judgment should be restored without reopening the accepted environment; otherwise the preserved negligence and notice grounds must be resolved before judgment for either party. Clarification alone requires no new evidentiary trial.

Unlike the Court, he would reject a freestanding complete preventive-care/noncomplaint defense after materially aided misuse is proved. Title VII and Meritor supply no general immunity extinguishing that attributed discrimination. Reasonable care may defeat negligence; appropriate facts may defeat apparent authority or causation or limit recoverable avoidable loss under governing remedial law. Those inquiries do not erase proved actual-authority attribution. An undistributed policy cannot establish unreasonable nonuse, and later correction cannot erase earlier attributable misconduct. Failed dissemination alone is not negligence per se.

Landgraf bars new post-1991 damages. Personal constitutional and state claims remain outside his proposed disposition; collateral fees require their own statutory and reasonableness inquiry. His conditional restoration is not a vote for the Court's present restoration mandate.
