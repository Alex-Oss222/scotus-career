Source: `C_L_Enterprises_v_Citizen_Band_Potawatomi_Indian_Tribe_merits_2001-04-30.md`


## Event

C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma, No. 00-292. Merits decision, April 30, 2001; argued March 19, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Oklahoma Court of Civil Appeals after the Oklahoma Supreme Court denied review. The intermediate court's reconsidered judgment directed dismissal of the confirmation action for tribal immunity. The question is whether the integrated arbitration, incorporated rules, choice-of-law and judicial-entry provisions clearly waive immunity from the contemplated state-court confirmation proceeding.

The Tribe proposed the standard construction agreement for a roof on an off-reservation, nontrust commercial property. After changing the roofing material and hiring another contractor, it declined arbitration while asserting immunity and communicating substantive defenses. The arbitrator awarded C & L $25,400 plus fees and costs, and the trial court confirmed. This review concerns consent to that judicial process, not a new adjudication of breach or the award's validity.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The immunity judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Immunity does not bar the confirmation proceeding contemplated by this agreement. Remaining properly preserved award defenses and ordinary confirmation prerequisites must be resolved in further proceedings. The Court does not itself confirm every award item, expand recovery or authorize attachment of particular tribal assets.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

### The integrated agreement clearly consents to confirmation

**Holding and operative rule:** A tribe clearly waives immunity from the contemplated state-court confirmation proceeding when its integrated agreement requires binding arbitration, expressly provides for judicial entry of the award, incorporates rules providing for that enforcement, and selects an applicable regime under which a competent state court may enter judgment. This agreement supplies that limited consent without a prescribed formula referring to sovereign immunity; commercial activity, a generic choice-of-law clause, or arbitration standing alone does not establish the same waiver.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices, controls the rule, its application and the limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Kiowa preserves tribal immunity for commercial and governmental contracts both on and off reservation land, while recognizing clear waiver. Santa Clara Pueblo requires unequivocal consent, and Potawatomi separates the underlying obligation from amenability to suit. The Court applies those principles to what this agreement promises. Its final-arbitration undertaking expressly ends in judicial entry under the selected Oklahoma enforcement law. Treating the competent-court qualification as requiring an entirely separate immunity waiver would deny that express judicial undertaking its operation. The qualification preserves ordinary court competence; it does not undo the consent the agreement itself conveys.

First Options requires actual agreement and attention to its scope. The Tribe tendered the form, but that conduct supports assent to the words rather than independently waiving immunity. Clarity concerns the undertaking's legal content, not special immunity vocabulary. The agreement links the arbitral decision and judicial confirmation under an identified regime. Enforcing that bounded forum choice respects both sovereign immunity and the Tribe's power to consent.

The commercial location and perceived fairness of requiring payment cannot independently supply waiver. The Tribe's refusal to participate in arbitration establishes neither default nor the correctness of the award. Ordinary confirmation defenses remain for their own adjudication after removal of the immunity bar.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding creates no otherwise absent federal subject-matter jurisdiction. Execution against particular assets and unrelated claims are outside the confirmation question. Signatory authority and former 25 U.S.C. §81 were not aired below and fall outside the accepted issue. The Court need not decide whether tribal and state immunity-waiver doctrines are parallel. Incorporated arbitration rules supply no new holding about delegation of arbitrability.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc.:** Applies the clear-waiver exception without changing immunity for governmental or commercial contracts, on or off reservation land.
- **Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez:** Applies unequivocal relinquishment without adding a prescribed verbal formula.
- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe:** Preserves the distinction between substantive obligation and amenability; the obligation alone remains insufficient.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Applies actual assent and instrument-specific scope without expanding its delegation rule.

## Law After Decision

The combined undertaking here clearly authorizes the contemplated confirmation action. The general tribal-immunity rule remains intact. Other contracts must be construed on their own terms under the same clear-waiver requirement; the Court adopts no commercial-activity exception or automatic waiver from arbitration alone. Remaining award and enforcement issues require their independent legal predicates.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for confirmation proceedings consistent with the limited waiver. Immunity may not support dismissal of this contemplated action. Ordinary properly preserved defenses remain, without a Court order increasing the award or authorizing execution against unspecified assets.

Source: `Cooper_Industries_v_Leatherman_Tool_Group_merits_2001-05-14.md`


## Event

Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc., No. 99-2035. Merits decision, May 14, 2001; argued February 26, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. Review is expressly limited to the standard for appellate review of a trial court's ruling on a constitutional challenge to punitive damages, including the Seventh Amendment allocation.

Cooper modified a Leatherman tool and used its photographs and a touched-up drawing to promote the ToolZall before production was complete. Some materials continued circulating after a recall effort. The jury found passing off, false advertising and unfair competition and awarded $50,000 compensation and $4.5 million punitive damages. The district judge sustained the punitive amount, and the court of appeals affirmed under abuse-of-discretion review. Its separate functionality ruling made copying the useful appearance lawful and removed the design injunction; that distinct judgment remains outside this review.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The appellate punitive-damages judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent constitutional review.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 7–2. Supporting: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg. The Ninth Circuit must independently evaluate constitutional excessiveness while respecting supported historical findings. The Court fixes no sum, ratio, remittitur or new trial and does not hold the $4.5 million award unconstitutional. The $50,000 compensation and the separate functionality disposition remain undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Vacatur and remand, 7–2 | Independent constitutional review and factual protection; BMW treated as binding law without renewed endorsement of its foundation |
| Concurrence | Scalia | Thomas | Joins the complete Court opinion and judgment | Preserves objection to BMW's substantive foundation and availability of reconsideration in a proper case |
| Dissent | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela | Would affirm on the limited review-standard question | Meaningful abuse-of-discretion amount review with independent correction of legal error |

## Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** An appellate court must independently determine whether the punitive award exceeds the constitutional limit established by BMW, while respecting supported historical findings concerning conduct, culpability, causation and harm. The Seventh Amendment does not require abuse-of-discretion review of that constitutional determination because deciding the legal boundary of punishment does not retry those historical facts.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, directly controls the review allocation and its factual limits. Scalia and Thomas join on the premise that BMW governs this limited question, without affirmatively endorsing its substantive due-process foundation.

**Controlling explanation:** BMW supplies the existing substantive limit; this grant concerns how an appellate court enforces it. Bajakajian separates independent constitutional proportionality review from deference to historical findings. Ornelas likewise distinguishes ultimate constitutional evaluation from factfinding. Their task-specific reasoning supports independent application of the punitive limit without a second trial of the conduct, injury or intent.

Gasperini's deferential review of the trial court's supported compensatory valuation does not settle this distinct constitutional task. A jury's selection of punishment reflects a moral assessment and deterrent judgment; the constitutional limit on that punishment remains a legal question. The Seventh Amendment protects the facts underlying the assessment, not a district court's discretionary authority to settle the federal constitutional boundary. Oberg requires meaningful scrutiny of amount, and the existing substantive limit cannot become whatever amount an individual district judge finds tolerable. Independent review therefore corrects the allocation used below while preserving the jury's and trial judge's actual factual work.

The Court does not itself reweigh this award. The independent review must distinguish intentional deceptive promotion from product copying that the separate judgment holds lawful. Supported actual or potential harm and the adjudicated wrong govern; invented losses or wealth unconnected to that wrong cannot replace the required relationship. Those are existing-law boundaries for the required review, not findings that the lower courts committed every asserted guidepost error or a new determination of the maximum permissible amount.

**Limits:** Reopening BMW's substantive validity, Oregon's allocation of part of the award, and fixing a permissible sum are outside the limited grant. No rule is adopted for materially different legislatively fixed multiples or exact deterrence schemes. Supported factual findings remain protected; independent legal review creates no appellate factfinding power or universal standard for every mixed question.

## Precedent Treatment

- BMW: applied as the binding substantive premise; guideposts and their qualifications remain unchanged, without a fresh majority endorsement of their constitutional foundation.
- Bajakajian: extends its factual/legal division by analogy to this constitutional punitive-review task; its forfeiture holding retains its scope.
- Ornelas: applies task-specific independent constitutional evaluation with factual protection; no universal mixed-question command follows.
- Gasperini: distinguishes compensatory valuation and leaves its deferential appellate allocation intact in that setting.
- Oberg: preserves meaningful review of amount and its adequate-functional-substitute qualification; no numerical ceiling is added.
- TXO: preserves its record-specific procedural holding and absence of a controlling numerical or potential-harm formula.
- TrafFix: preserves lawful useful copying, which cannot be punished merely by relabeling it advertising misconduct.
- Buford: distinguishes its practical Guidelines task rather than displacing this constitutional allocation.

## Law After Decision

Appellate courts must independently apply the existing constitutional limit to ordinary civil punitive awards, while preserving supported historical findings. The independent legal task does not create appellate power to find new facts or require repeated valuation of compensation. The amount here awaits that review; no fixed ratio or constitutional dollar maximum is announced. BMW remains controlling at its existing scope without a fresh decision on its substantive constitutional foundation.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in the complete opinion and judgment on the premise that BMW supplies the binding substantive limit for this limited grant. Their agreement concerns the division between legal constitutional review and historical factfinding. They retain their opposition to BMW's substantive due-process foundation and willingness to reconsider it in a case properly presenting that issue. The Court's bounded reasoning does not require them to endorse that foundation or a universal rule of independent review for every mixed question.

Ginsburg, joined by Stone-Zsela, would affirm on the standard-of-review question. Appellate courts independently determine the governing law, correct impermissible premises and unsupported factual assumptions, and require actual review of amount. They may nevertheless review the trial judge's supported punitive assessment for abuse of discretion. The jury's appraisal of culpability, deterrence and the course of misconduct depends on the record heard by it and the trial judge. Gasperini's institutional respect for supported valuation informs this allocation despite its compensatory setting. Bajakajian's governmental forfeiture and Ornelas's search determination concern distinct tasks and do not command a second independent civil punitive valuation.

The dissent preserves BMW's substantive boundary despite disagreement with its foundation and Oberg's demand for meaningful amount review. An award is not immune merely because a judge sustained it. Disregarding guideposts, punishing lawful copying, inventing threatened losses or substituting wealth for a connection to the wrong remains correctable. But the limited grant establishes none of those supposed errors as facts and does not authorize selection of a preferred punitive figure. The ninety-to-one ratio alone does not decide whether this supported assessment must be replaced. The dissent would leave the reviewed judgment operative without deciding a new dollar ceiling or Oregon's share of the award.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit for the independent constitutional evaluation required here, with supported historical findings protected. The Court's mandate itself supplies neither a replacement award nor an order for a new trial and leaves the distinct compensation and functionality judgments intact.

Those materials support selection of the review standard without establishing a final appraisal of threatened harm or the constitutional maximum.

Source: `Major_League_Baseball_Players_Association_v_Garvey_summary_2001-05-14.md`


## Event

Major League Baseball Players Association v. Garvey, No. 00-1210. Certiorari grant and summary merits disposition, May 14, 2001. On the petition for review of the Ninth Circuit's December 7, 2000 judgment directing return to arbitration with instructions to award Garvey approximately $3 million. The questions concern review of the arbitrator's credibility assessment and the court's power to direct contractual recovery. A timely challenge to the later judgment also presents the antecedent vacatur ruling. The matter is decided on the petition record without a separately scheduled argued merits hearing.

The accepted LMRA dispute concerns the Framework distributing a $280 million settlement of club collusion claims for the 1985–1987 free-agent markets. A lost-extension claim required a specific offer later withdrawn because of collusion. After the Association denied Garvey's claim, he submitted his testimony and a 1996 letter from former club president Ballard Smith supporting an extension for 1988–1989. The arbitrator requested corroborating documents and rejected the claim, identifying inconsistent accounts and other record concerns. The first appellate panel displaced that assessment; the later panel required the claimed award.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in consideration and decision.

## Public Action

Certiorari is granted on the arbitration-review and remedy question, and the judgment is summarily reversed and remanded. The grant's individual votes are not publicly disclosed. The public merits positions and separate writings concern summary disposition.

## Judgment & Remedy

Summarily reversed and remanded, 8–1. Supporting summary reversal: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing summary disposition: Stevens, who would obtain plenary briefing and argument before deciding the merits. His dissent supplies no ultimate merits affirmance.

The Ninth Circuit's directed recovery and its erroneous vacatur predicate are set aside. The case is remanded with instructions to restore the district court's denial of Garvey's motion to vacate. The arbitrator's denial remains effective. No new arbitration or evidentiary hearing is required because of the perceived credibility error; independently authorized contractual proceedings are neither created nor prohibited.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court (per curiam), Parts I and II | The Court; unsigned | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Summary reversal and award restoration, 8–1 | Timely antecedent review; limited review and actual application |
| Opinion of the Court (per curiam), Part III | The Court; unsigned | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Independently rejects directed monetary recovery | Bounded remedial rule after proper vacatur |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Ginsburg | No other Justice | Joins Parts I–II and summary reversal | Withholds the additional hypothetical-remedy ground |
| Dissent | Stevens | No other Justice | Opposes summary reversal | Would decide the disputed authority boundary and remedy after plenary consideration |

## Holdings

### I. Timely review of the later judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A timely petition from the later judgment in the same litigation permits review of an antecedent ruling necessary to that judgment. Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe supply this rule; an earlier appellate mandate binds the district court, but does not immunize the predicate ruling from this Court's proper later review. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this threshold ground in Part I of the per curiam opinion. Stevens does not join it.

### II. The court may not replace submitted factfinding

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may not replace the credibility and factual assessment of a labor arbitrator who is arguably applying the agreed framework within the accepted submission merely because it regards the assessment as seriously mistaken. The court must identify a departure from contractual authority or another recognized enforcement defect; this arbitrator applied the submitted offer-and-withdrawal criterion, so the original award must remain effective.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this review rule and actual award-restoration ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Misco protects contract application and factfinding within the submitted task; Enterprise Wheel preserves the boundary against an award untethered to the agreement. Those rules require examination of what the arbitrator did, not judicial substitution of the preferred factual answer. Here the arbitrator asked whether an actual extension offer existed and was withdrawn through collusion, sought corroboration, and evaluated inconsistent accounts. The court of appeals disagreed with his treatment of Smith's later letter because earlier collusion proceedings had rejected club testimony. But a general finding that the clubs colluded does not prove this specific offer. Disbelief of one aggregate account does not compel acceptance of every later individual assertion.

The Court does not independently endorse the arbitrator's factual accuracy or find that a witness lied. The legal defect is the appellate replacement of the chosen decisionmaker while he applied the agreed criterion. First Options protects antecedent assent and does not create a factual appeal within a conceded submission. Eastern Associated Coal retains the law-grounded limits on labor-award enforcement, and Wright preserves independent statutory claims; neither changes this contractual claim into a new judicial merits inquiry. No corruption, dishonesty or other independently sufficient enforcement defect is established in the described record.

### III. Vacatur does not transfer disputed contractual merits to the court

**Holding and operative rule:** Even when a labor award is properly vacated, a reviewing court ordinarily may not resolve disputed contractual entitlement and order its selected monetary result instead of respecting the agreement's remaining allocation of authority. Further arbitral proceedings depend on what the agreement permits; this rule neither orders another hearing in every case nor creates authority absent from the agreement.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Ginsburg withholds this additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Misco's remedial discussion and American Manufacturing preserve the contractual tribunal's role after a court identifies a valid enforcement defect. A court cannot use vacatur as authority to adjudicate the disputed merits and command the sum it considers inevitable. Garvey's assertion that only one factual result is possible does not itself transfer the submitted task to the reviewing court. This additional ground independently defeats the directed award; because vacatur was improper here, the operative mandate restores the denial rather than requiring a hypothetical second arbitration.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Mercer v. Theriot; Hamilton-Brown Shoe Co. v. Wolf Brothers & Co.:** Apply review of antecedent rulings through a timely petition from the later judgment.
- **United Paperworkers International Union v. Misco, Inc.:** Applies limited merits review and its distinct remedial restraint, with the different joins identified above.
- **United Steelworkers of America v. Enterprise Wheel & Car Corp.:** Preserves the actual contractual-authority boundary without treating factual disagreement as abandonment of the submission.
- **United Steelworkers of America v. American Manufacturing Co.:** Applies the contractual allocation against a court-selected merits result.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Preserves assent and gateway review without creating a credibility appeal after accepted submission.
- **Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp.:** Preserves independent statutory claims; this accepted contractual compensation claim presents none.
- **Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America:** Preserves section 301 award review and law-grounded public-policy limits; no contrary public-policy defect is established here.
- **Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams:** Its FAA employment exclusion remains distinct from this accepted LMRA route.
- **C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe:** Its express confirmation-waiver holding concerns actual judicial consent and tribal immunity, not independent review of an accepted labor arbitrator's factual findings.

## Law After Decision

Labor-award review remains confined to the actual submission and recognized enforcement limits. Disagreement with credibility findings made in applying that submission is not a new factual appeal. The additional remedial rule ordinarily forbids the reviewing court from deciding disputed contractual entitlement and commanding its selected award after vacatur; any remaining contractual proceedings require authority under the agreement. The actual disposition restores the original arbitral denial without requiring a second arbitration or deciding an independent statutory claim.

## Separate Writings

Ginsburg joins Parts I and II and the judgment. The sufficient ground is that the original award should not have been disturbed: the arbitrator was applying the submitted framework, and the court replaced his factual assessment. Restoring the denial of vacatur removes the directed award. She therefore withholds Part III's additional discussion of the remedy after a genuinely proper vacatur and does not independently endorse the arbitrator's factual accuracy.

Stevens dissents from summary disposition. The boundary between serious factual error and abandonment of the agreement, and the remedy after a valid finding of that abandonment, warrant plenary briefing and argument. First Options, Wright and Eastern Associated Coal preserve contractual limits without eliminating those questions. He would engage the lower court's record-based reasoning before deciding that it crossed the review boundary and would not declare the arbitrator free from serious error on this treatment alone. His position seeks fuller consideration, not an ultimate affirmance of the monetary award.

## Procedure After Action

Review is granted and resolved by the same summary disposition. The case returns to restore the district court's denial of vacatur. The approximately $3 million judicially directed award cannot stand. Further contractual proceedings arise only from an independent contractual basis; the mandate orders no new arbitration or fresh evidentiary hearing.

## Source Notes

The Framework, proceedings, later judgment and petition posture appear in [the official United States Reports, 532 U.S. 504](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-504.pdf). The question is stated in substance. The record describes no arbitral dishonesty and supplies no independent statutory claim; the disposition does not make new findings about witness truthfulness.

Source: `Rogers_v_Tennessee_merits_2001-05-14.md`


## Event

Rogers v. Tennessee, No. 99-6218. Merits decision, May 14, 2001; argued November 1, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Tennessee Supreme Court. The question is whether due process permits application of that court's abolition of the year-and-a-day murder limitation to conduct occurring while the limitation remained state law.

Rogers stabbed the victim on May 6, 1994. The undisputed death interval exceeded a year and a day. Tennessee's highest court held that the limitation survived the 1989 statutory revision, abolished it judicially, and applied the abolition to sustain Rogers's second-degree murder conviction. The causal medical account and the legal time limitation are distinct. Notice must be evaluated from law existing when Rogers acted.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining the murder conviction through retrospective abolition is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

The state court must vacate the murder conviction resting on retrospective abolition and determine any lawful remaining disposition. The undisputed interval cannot be repaired by a new opportunity to prove earlier death. The Court orders no particular lesser conviction or sentence, automatic retrial, or release independent of other lawful custody. Tennessee may abolish the limitation for later conduct.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas | Reversal and remand, 5–4 | Shared case-specific insufficient-warning ground, actual state-law premise and bounded remedy |
| Concurrence | Scalia | Stevens, Thomas | Joins the complete Court opinion and reversal | Additional objection to avowed retrospective removal of a substantive offense restriction |
| Concurrence | Breyer | No other Justice | Joins the Court's reversal | Expressly adopts the common-law due-process framework stated in the dissent, while rejecting its application here |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Would affirm | Unexpected-and-indefensible framework and conclusion that this abolition was foreseeable |

## Holdings

### I. This acknowledged limitation could not be removed retrospectively without sufficient warning

**Holding and operative rule:** When the state court acknowledges that the year-and-a-day offense limitation remained in force when this defendant acted, its later abolition cannot sustain his murder conviction merely because the limitation had become medically obsolete, was infrequently used, or had been abandoned elsewhere. On this record those reasons support prospective reform but do not supply sufficient warning that the conceded limitation would be removed for earlier conduct.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia and Thomas, directly controls this case-specific fair-warning rule, its application and the bounded remand. Agreement does not depend on universal equivalence between judicial and legislative change.

**Controlling explanation:** Bouie protects against unexpected retrospective enlargement, and Lanier makes the law's reasonably apparent conduct-time reach the source of warning. The wrongfulness of Rogers's assault does not answer whether the law then permitted murder liability after the recognized interval. Tennessee's authoritative survival determination forecloses reliance on the general homicide statute as if it had already removed the limit. Medical causation and the legal temporal restriction are different matters.

The State offers substantial reasons for abandoning an antiquated limitation now that causation can be proved after a longer interval. That justification does not establish warning of retrospective removal. Rare litigation does not itself erase an existing rule, and reform elsewhere does not itself change Tennessee law. Ordinary judicial interpretation, foreseeable clarification and prospective common-law development remain permissible. The present conclusion concerns an acknowledged substantive boundary actually removed after the conduct.

Neither subjective reliance on the rule nor innocence of the assault is required. Later authorities, including Ruane in 1995, cannot supply notice in May 1994. No unsupported numerical survey of preconduct state law supplies the result. Accepting the medical causal link does not make the former limitation disappear. Its conceded survival and the undisputed interval require removal of the murder conviction's retrospective basis while leaving any independently lawful remaining disposition for the state court.

### II. The independently supported common-law framework

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process permits ordinary common-law development but forbids a retrospective judicial enlargement that is unexpected and indefensible by reference to the law expressed before the conduct. It does not automatically incorporate every Ex Post Facto Clause category governing legislation into the review of judicial development.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg state this framework in the dissent; Breyer expressly adopts the same framework in his concurrence, supplying five direct agreements at that level. It is independently majority-supported despite disagreement about the judgment. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia and Thomas do not join this general formulation through their vote to reverse. No Marks synthesis or implied judgment coalition supplies it.

**Controlling explanation:** Bouie's due-process protection turns on fair warning and the legal landscape preceding the conduct. The Ex Post Facto Clause's legislative categories, preserved by Calder and Collins, do not mechanically describe every ordinary judicial clarification or common-law development. Lanier likewise requires reasonably apparent prior law without demanding a factual twin for every permissible application. This framework preserves objective notice while allowing legitimate interpretation and foreseeable development. Its adoption does not decide the application in the State's favor: four supporters regard this abolition as foreseeable, while Breyer concludes that the admitted former limitation and the reasons offered for removing it provide inadequate warning here.

### Limits and remaining disposition

The Court's case-specific insufficient-warning conclusion controls the murder conviction. No majority adopts a universal rule forbidding every judicial change that could not be imposed by legislation. Carmell's distinct legislative proof-sufficiency holding is not itself a direct Ex Post Facto ruling on courts. Fiore's clarification of an existing element does not resolve this acknowledged abolition. Lesser-offense availability and its form lie outside the presented federal question and require their own lawful predicates; neither a specified lesser judgment nor broader immunity from punishment follows.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Bouie v. City of Columbia:** Applies objective fair warning to the retrospective removal of this conceded offense restriction while preserving ordinary foreseeable interpretation.
- **United States v. Lanier:** Extends its conduct-time apparent-law principle to this acknowledged substantive state-law limit; wrongfulness alone does not replace the specific offense boundary.
- **Carmell v. Texas:** Preserves its legislative prohibition on reducing the legally required quantum of proof. Its concern for existing legal conditions does not directly apply the Ex Post Facto Clause to judges or prescribe a lesser-offense remedy here.
- **Fiore v. White:** Distinguishes clarification of an existing element from actual abolition; its existing-element holding remains intact.
- **Calder v. Bull; Collins v. Youngblood:** Retain their legislative categories without automatic transposition to every judicial development under the independently supported due-process framework.

## Law After Decision

Rogers's murder conviction cannot rest on retrospective abolition of the acknowledged year-and-a-day limitation. The case-specific insufficient-warning conclusion and the independently majority-supported unexpected-and-indefensible common-law framework both have controlling force at their stated scopes, through different direct coalitions. Ordinary interpretation, foreseeable clarification and prospective reform remain permissible. The stronger structural concurrence is not a universal Court rule. Any lawful remaining state disposition requires its own legal and record predicates.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Stevens and Thomas, joins the Court's bounded fair-warning holding and adds that an acknowledged substantive offense restriction cannot be erased retrospectively merely because reform was foreseeable. Bouie protects the criminal law governing the act, not only an estimate of what judges may later wish to change. Carmell reinforces respect for existing legal conditions while remaining a legislative case. Ordinary application of existing law and prospective reform remain permissible. These three also agree that, even under the competing foreseeability framework, medical obsolescence, infrequent litigation and change elsewhere supply inadequate warning here. Their additional position does not make disputed historical theories of common-law judging necessary to the judgment.

Breyer's concurrence expressly adopts the unexpected-and-indefensible due-process framework stated by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg and rejects automatic incorporation of every legislative category. He nonetheless finds the warning inadequate. A sound reason for future reform does not show that the existing Tennessee limitation had ceased to govern Rogers's earlier conduct. His concurrence rejects the State's application while preserving the legitimacy of ordinary common-law development; it does not join the stronger structural concurrence.

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, would affirm. Their framework asks whether the common-law development was unexpected and indefensible by reference to preconduct law. They accept the limitation's survival but regard its limited local use, obsolete causal-proof rationale and supported preconduct legal landscape as making abandonment foreseeable. Prior existence and constitutional unexpectedness are distinct questions. Lanier's notice inquiry and the difference between legislative and judicial change permit this application in their view. They do not rely on the rejected statutory-repeal theory, the assault's immorality alone, subjective reliance, or later authorities to establish prior notice.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to vacate the murder conviction insofar as it rests on the retrospective abolition and to determine any lawful remaining disposition under the former limitation and other applicable law. No new proof opportunity concerning the conceded interval, specified lesser offense, automatic retrial or independent release order is authorized. Prospective abolition is unaffected.

The decisive notice date is May 6, 1994; later developments cannot supply earlier warning. No comprehensive numerical preconduct state-law survey or adjudicated lesser-offense disposition is established here.

## Event

United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative, No. 00-151. Merits decision, May 14, 2001; argued March 28, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. The live judgment reversed the refusal to modify a federal injunction and directed consideration of a medical-necessity exemption. The questions concern the Cooperative's asserted statutory defense for manufacture and distribution and its alternative reliance on equitable discretion.

California law removed specified state prohibitions for patients and primary caregivers possessing or cultivating marijuana on a physician's recommendation or approval. The Cooperative operated a dispensary with medical screening and membership identification. The United States obtained an injunction against manufacture, distribution and associated possession with intent; the Cooperative did not appeal the initial order. The separate contempt appeal later became moot. After the appellate ruling, the district court adopted medical criteria for exempted distribution, and a separate government appeal followed. State permission, physician recommendations and federal authorization remain distinct.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participated. Breyer took no part in the consideration or decision; no reason for his absence is stated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment recognizing the medical-distribution exemption is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 8–0 among participating Justices. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Not participating: Breyer.

The statutory or equivalent equitable medical-distribution rationale cannot sustain the requested modification, including the later exception based on that rationale. Lawful discretion over the necessity, scope and means of equitable enforcement and ordinary treatment of independently preserved constitutional objections remain. The mandate does not independently decide the separate government appeal, revive moot contempt, impose criminal punishment or select findings about individual patients.

## Opinion Topology

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Holdings

### I. The distributor has no statutory medical-necessity exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** The Controlled Substances Act forecloses the Cooperative's medical-necessity defense to its prohibited manufacture, distribution and associated possession with intent to distribute marijuana. Medical recommendations and state permission do not supply federal authorization, and even satisfaction of the lower court's medical criteria does not create this distributor's exception.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those eight directly adopt both bounded holdings and their remedy; Breyer does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 841(a)(1) prohibits the identified conduct except as authorized by the subchapter. Schedule I treatment and the specified research route give operative content to that authorization structure; the Cooperative claims no research authorization. The conclusion rests on Congress's enacted classification, not a judicial finding that marijuana can never benefit a patient. Rutherford supports adherence to the chosen medical-regulatory scheme. Bailey supplies necessity's ordinary background and attention to lawful alternatives, but does not permit a court to override the particular medical-distribution policy Congress prescribed.

The Cooperative's strongest argument is the need of seriously ill patients after other treatment fails. The district modification required four conjunctive conditions: a serious medical condition; imminent harm without cannabis; need for cannabis to treat or alleviate the condition or associated symptoms; and no reasonable legal effective alternative because all legal alternatives tried were ineffective or caused side effects the patient could not reasonably tolerate. Those conditions must be accurately understood, and the order does not establish that every recipient satisfied them. Even assuming satisfaction, however, medical need does not furnish the statutory authorization for this distributor. Neither the absence of express necessity-defense words nor sympathy decides the issue; the prohibition, classification and actual authorization channels together do so.

### II. Equitable discretion does not supply the same substantive exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 882(a) preserves lawful discretion whether and how to enjoin violations, but a court may not grant the same prohibited medical-distribution exemption by recharacterizing its own balance of medical benefits and statutory policy as injunction modification. The court may tailor authorized enforcement means consistently with the Act; it may not displace the substantive distribution rule.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Opinion of the Court directly controls the independent equitable ground and its preserved discretion.

**Controlling explanation:** Hecht and Romero-Barcelo preserve traditional choice and tailoring in equitable enforcement. Meghrig requires that this discretion operate within the enacted remedial scheme. Grupo Mexicano likewise distinguishes an actual source of remedial authority from a desired result; its disputed general-equity question does not decide every statutory injunction. Section 882(a) authorizes relief without commanding an injunction in every case or its broadest possible form. A court may therefore consider whether relief is needed, whether its wording exceeds a violation, and how lawful compliance should be ordered. It may not authorize continued prohibited distribution because it gives the medical advantages of nonenforcement greater weight than Congress did. That would supply the statutory exception rejected above through the remedy alone. These limits resolve the independently offered equitable ground for affirmance without deciding all injunctions affecting individual patients.

**Limits:** No ambiguity justifies inserting this distribution exception through constitutional avoidance. That conclusion does not resolve the underlying Commerce Clause, substantive due-process or patient-liberty objections left unadjudicated below; their ordinary preservation and merits requirements remain. Individual possession without distribution, individual-patient necessity and injunction questions, and necessity under materially different federal statutes are outside this case. No general doubt about necessity doctrine or automatic maximal injunction follows.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Rutherford:** Applies respect for the medical-regulatory scheme against a judicial treatment exemption.
- **United States v. Bailey:** Preserves necessity's ordinary framework and lawful-alternative limits without recognizing or rejecting a universal defense under all federal statutes.
- **Hecht Co. v. Bowles; Weinberger v. Romero-Barcelo:** Preserve choice and tailoring of lawful equitable enforcement, not authority to contradict a substantive statutory restriction.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc.:** Applies source-specific remedial limits without creating a universal ban on monetary equity or injunction modification.
- **Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc.:** Applies the distinction between actual remedial authority and a desired result at its proper scope; its unsecured-creditor rule does not decide all statutory injunctions.

## Law After Decision

The Act permits no medical-distribution exemption of the kind claimed by the Cooperative through statutory necessity or the same equitable balance. This construction reaches its manufacture, distribution and associated possession with that intent. Lawful enforcement discretion and independently established constitutional limits retain their distinct predicates. No rule about individual possession, individual-patient necessity or injunctions, or necessity under materially different federal statutes is announced; no medical finding that marijuana can never benefit a patient follows.

## Event

United States v. Valentine, Third Circuit No. 00-1425; judgment below reported at 232 F.3d 350. Merits submission and decision, May 14, 2001. On review of the Third Circuit's reversal of suppression and remand of the federal firearm prosecution. The questions concern individualized suspicion from the reported gun possession and encounter, including the preserved permitting argument, and whether actual submission can occur during a brief interval before later flight.

At about 1 a.m. on May 8, 1999, a man flagged down two uniformed officers and reported recently seeing a nearby man carrying a gun. He gave a detailed description but refused to identify himself. Officers saw Valentine matching the description nearby, in an area described as having many shootings. The men began walking away. The officer's account describes a command to come over and place hands on the car, Valentine's inquiry about whom it concerned, a charge toward the officer and an attempt to push past his arms, followed by a grab and struggle. The gun was discovered only then.

Valentine asserted an earlier stop and name. The appellate court reported no evidentiary support and a police report apparently inconsistent with that theory, but expressly declined factual determination because it regarded even the assumed account as insufficient submission. The district court's cutoff at the command was a legal ruling, not a finding that Valentine yielded.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at merits submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Third Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for correct legal treatment of individualized suspicion, the preserved permitting theory and the first actual seizure on the existing record.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The court below must apply the stated suspicion and submission rules to the supported chronology and consider properly preserved independent grounds under ordinary remedial law. No suppression, acquittal, dismissal, new hearing or reopening of proofs is commanded. The reported continuous-charge account remains a possible basis for admission if no earlier seizure occurred. The gun's eventual discovery supplies no antecedent justification.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacatur and bounded remand, 5–4 | Individualized totality, bare-presumption limit, actual submission and existing-record alternatives |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would affirm | Supported no-prior-submission sequence and concrete conduct before the first grab; reserves initial-tip and permit questions |

## Holdings

### I. Reliable possession information must be connected to wrongdoing

**Holding and operative rule:** A recent face-to-face eyewitness report may materially strengthen the reliability of information that a person possesses a gun, but reliability about possession is distinct from a supported inference of criminal activity. The reported possession, late hour, generalized neighborhood shootings and ordinary walking away do not, without a supported further connection, establish individualized suspicion on this record; all actual circumstances must be evaluated together.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, directly controls all three holdings, their stated limits and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Wardlow requires particularized suspicion and rejects categorical reliance on location or departure; its actual application found a more substantial look-and-flight record insufficient. J. L. distinguishes accurate identification from reliable indication of wrongdoing and rejects an ordinary-gun exception. Personal delivery and immediacy have reliability features absent from the remote report there. The Court adopts no categorical prohibition on unidentified eyewitness reports. But greater assurance of possession does not itself establish an offense. This witness reported no threat or illegal transfer. Ordinary walking away and generalized danger cannot fill the missing connection by assumption.

The totality rule considers circumstances together, including grounded local experience and conduct capable of an innocent explanation. It does not demand that every observation independently indicate crime or that officers prove an offense. Ornelas requires independent constitutional evaluation while preserving historical findings and genuine grounded inferences. It does not permit imagined wrongdoing or assumed licensing facts. Consensual inquiry remains available, and a more particularized report or supported circumstances may justify immediate restraint. The preserved permitting argument and any conduct before the first actual seizure require their own assessment below; the Court does not find that every possible ground for admission fails.

### II. A bare evidentiary presumption is not a particularized fact

**Holding and operative rule:** A state-law evidentiary presumption that a handgun possessor lacks a permit cannot, by itself, replace the Fourth Amendment's requirement of particularized suspicion of wrongdoing. Officers may draw grounded inferences from concrete circumstances actually known before seizure, including supported licensing circumstances; the Government's complete permitting argument must be evaluated under that ordinary totality standard.

**Authority:** The same five Justices directly join this legal limit and its preservation of additional actual licensing circumstances. The four dissenters do not supply implied agreement by reserving the issue.

**Controlling explanation:** The strongest contrary argument relies on New Jersey's strict permit requirements, investigation and evidentiary presumption as making reported possession indicative of illegality under the modest reasonable-suspicion threshold. Those actual regulatory circumstances may matter when properly supported and applicable. A rule allocating evidentiary responsibility, however, does not itself establish a fact about this person or give officers unreported licensing knowledge. The presumption's trial function cannot substitute for evaluation of the circumstances supporting the investigative inference.

J. L. supplies the distinction between credited information and authority to intrude, not an already decided licensing rule. Wardlow requires particularized totality, not a prescribed third fact. No officer must invariably check a database, observe a completed offense or obtain a separately specified item of proof. Reasonable suspicion remains below probable cause and permits contextual inference. The Government's complete theory was not adjudicated below; its properly supported restrictive-licensing and investigation circumstances remain available. Absence of their detail from the published account is not a finding that officers knew none. The remand therefore preserves full ordinary application while rejecting automatic substitution of the bare presumption.

**Limits:** The Court does not decide Valentine's permit status, the presumption's validity as a trial-evidence rule, or the licensing scheme's constitutionality. It does not find permits common or rare or treat all public possession as lawful. The existing arms decisions distinguish coverage from regulatory validity and do not decide this licensing theory.

### III. Genuine submission can be brief

**Holding and operative rule:** Objectively manifested yielding to a police command can constitute a seizure even if brief; there is no fixed minimum duration, and later flight does not erase a completed seizure. A hesitation, inquiry or equivocal movement that does not manifest yielding remains distinct, and an unheeded command alone is not a seizure.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion directly adopts the rule and the existing-record return.

**Controlling explanation:** Hodari D. identifies physical force or actual submission as the boundary. Duration can help determine whether yielding occurred, but it creates no interval in which genuine submission lacks constitutional effect. The lower court therefore could not reject the assumed stop-and-name account solely because it was momentary. Conversely, its legal assumption establishes no actual pause. Its published account found no supporting evidence and described a report apparently inconsistent with the theory, while declining factual determination under the erroneous alternative rule. The district court's cutoff at the command likewise supplies no finding of submission.

On the reported inquiry, charge and attempted-pushing sequence, the first seizure may be the grab; the preceding concrete conduct can contribute to suspicion with the immediate gun report. If actual yielding occurred earlier, later conduct cannot retrospectively justify its inception. The gun found during the struggle supplies no earlier justification. Any independent exclusionary-rule or attenuation ground requires its own law and predicates. Proper review of the existing record may resolve admission without new testimony. The Court finds neither a period of compliance nor an evidentiary conflict requiring another hearing.

### Remedy and scope

The judgment is vacated for application of these rules to the supported first-seizure chronology and preserved independent grounds. Correct legal treatment, rather than a new factual finding for either party, governs the return. The Court neither commands suppression nor decides guilt, acquittal or dismissal. Remaining factual, licensing and remedial work is ordinary adjudication under the rules announced, not a reservation about whether brief actual yielding counts as seizure or whether a bare presumption alone supplies individualized suspicion.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Illinois v. Wardlow:** Applies its actual individualized totality rule and prohibition on universal location or flight shortcuts; the lower court's broader account does not govern.
- **Florida v. J. L.:** Applies the distinction between identification and wrongdoing and the absence of an ordinary-gun exception. In-person reliability is evaluated on its actual features, without a categorical anonymous-eyewitness rule.
- **California v. Hodari D.:** Applies force or actual submission and clarifies that genuine brief yielding is not categorically excluded by later flight.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Applies independent constitutional evaluation with protection for historical findings and grounded experience; it creates no missing facts or compulsory hearing.
- **Illinois v. McArthur:** Preserves justification at the actual intrusion. Its conceded probable cause and residential warrant-preservation setting do not supply this street encounter's predicates.
- **Atwater v. City of Lago Vista:** Its actual-enforcement limit on additional station custody after probable cause remains at that distinct scope. It does not decide the antecedent Terry suspicion or submission question, and this case imports none of its traffic-custody facts or application positions.

## Law After Decision

Reliable eyewitness information of gun possession remains subject to individualized suspicion of wrongdoing, evaluated under all supported circumstances. A bare evidentiary presumption of nonlicensure cannot itself provide the necessary particularized factual connection, while actual licensing context and other known circumstances may support grounded inference. Genuine submission has no minimum duration and is not erased by later flight. The existing-record remand preserves the no-prior-seizure charge-and-pushing alternative, the fuller permitting theory and independently supported remedial grounds without ordering suppression or new proof.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, would affirm on the supported account showing no submission before the first grab. The response about whom the command concerned, charge toward the officer and attempted pushing do not objectively manifest yielding. With the recent in-person gun report, that concrete conduct supplied a particularized basis for restraint at the grab. Hodari D. distinguishes an unheeded command from seizure; their Wardlow positions permit grounded contextual appraisal of these specific actions rather than a categorical flight rule.

They acknowledge that genuine earlier submission would change the timing inquiry, but regard the reported sequence and absence of supporting evidence for the alleged pause as sufficient to resolve this case without inventing a factual dispute. They reserve initial-command sufficiency, the complete permitting theory and the legal effect of actual prior yielding; those reservations are not joins in the Court's holdings. They do not adopt the panel's minimum-duration premise, infer justification from the discovered gun or suggest that every charge or flight in a high-crime area suffices. Their difference with the Court concerns the proper disposition on this existing record. Scalia retains his objection to Ornelas's allocation while applying that governing rule here.

## Procedure After Action

The court below must identify the first seizure through force or actual submission and evaluate the information known then under the announced rules, including the properly supported permitting circumstances. The continuous-charge account may sustain admission if no earlier seizure occurred. If an earlier unsupported seizure is established, suppression and any preserved independent remedial theory require ordinary analysis. No new hearing, proof opportunity, suppression order, acquittal or dismissal follows automatically.
