#### Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-780.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette, decided June 29, 1995, No. 94-780. see Federal Courts — Mootness and seasonal permits (The expired permit dispute remains reviewable).

##### Ohio’s categorical Establishment Clause defense cannot sustain this exclusion

**Holding:** Ohio may not exclude this genuinely private religious display from Capitol Square’s actual, generally available forum on the categorical ground that its religious message and proximity to the Statehouse necessarily establish religion. The judgment concerns the proposal presented, including its offered private-attribution safeguard; it neither finds the later sign adequate nor creates an unconditional right to every unattended display.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Parts II–III, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: seven votes for this bounded speech and access holding. Those seven do not adopt one comprehensive Establishment Clause test.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The offered readable clarification of private sponsorship had negotiable wording; it is distinct from the later actual sign and the injunction's terms. No finding establishes approved final wording or the sign's adequacy. Government-sponsored displays, actual religious favoritism and future rules remain open. Parts II–III have seven direct joins; Part IV's four-vote contextual explanation and the three-vote private-access rationale supply no controlling comprehensive test or Marks subset. No categorical forum immunity or universal disclaimer requirement is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Widmar v. Vincent: applies protection of religious expression in a genuinely open forum, while preserving examination of an actual establishment defense.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District: applies its controlling protection against exclusion of a religious perspective from comparable private access; its contextual reasoning is not converted into categorical forum immunity.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman and County of Allegheny v. ACLU: not overruled; the Court does not replace existing establishment law with a comprehensive test in this case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit at the original scope of the Count One merits judgment: the December 21–24, 1993 permit period remains expired. No new permit, extension, sign condition, removal order, damages or remand is entered. Unappealed rally, damages and other matters remain outside review.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1047.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller, decided June 29, 1995, No. 93-1047. see Federal Courts — Mootness and seasonal permits (The recurring access dispute remains live, and only the Rotunda claim is reviewed).

##### Georgia’s categorical defense cannot sustain summary judgment excluding the private religious display

**Holding:** A State that actually opens a designated forum to comparable private unattended expression may not exclude an otherwise eligible religious viewpoint merely by asserting that its Capitol location necessarily establishes religion. On this record that categorical defense does not justify judgment for Georgia; lawful access must be provided on the neutral terms governing comparable private expression, with the actual form of relief left for proceedings below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight votes for this bounded judgment and speech holding; Stevens dissents from its application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The indoor designated forum actually admits comparable sizeable unattended exhibits for several days. The Court does not endorse all the en banc reasoning or guarantee an unconditional display, and comparable secular access cannot be retained while excluding the otherwise eligible religious viewpoint.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: applies the actual designated-forum status and preserves lawful neutral management and prospective forum changes.
- Widmar v. Vincent and Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District: apply protection of comparable private religious expression and the prohibition on religious viewpoint exclusion.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: applies the bounded rejection of a categorical establishment defense, not a universal forum exemption.

##### Interior location does not itself make clearly private expression governmental religion

**Holding:** Neutral access does not establish religion merely because a genuine private-expression forum lies inside a state capitol, when the actual context makes clear that the religious expression remains private rather than governmental. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preferential treatment or obscured private sponsorship can change that conclusion; neither a forum label nor any unspecified sign automatically resolves attribution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part III, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes for this contextual rule. The additional decree-specific inquiry proposed in Part IV has only O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; it is not a five-Justice mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The identifying greeting names Chabad but does not expressly disclaim state sponsorship; an offered disclaimer is not a finding of an effective completed safeguard. The four-Justice additional decree-specific attribution inquiry is not part of the mandate. Neither a universal sign duty, preferential religious access nor a duty to keep the forum open is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- County of Allegheny v. ACLU: preserves contextual examination of apparent governmental affiliation, rather than title alone.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: preserves its shared access holding and distinct attribution rationales; no categorical immunity is drawn from its separate opinions.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: remains in force; no comprehensive replacement establishment test is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit reversal of State summary judgment on Count II for further proceedings and lawful relief on neutral terms. No Supreme Court permit, retrospective 1991 display, fixed sign, permanent seasonal entitlement, damages amount or compulsory forum closure is ordered. Count I remains undisturbed; lawful physical, safety, scheduling and identification rules and genuinely prospective viewpoint-neutral forum closure or redefinition remain possible.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms. It concerns financing, not merely facility access; it neither decided Agostini's public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### Robinson v. City of Edmond

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit Nos. 94-6237 and 95-6008; 68 F.3d 1226 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

For recurring-contact standing, see *Robinson*, March 31, 1997, Nos. 94-6237 and 95-6008 (Federal Courts — Standing and personal injury).

##### Whether a privately designed but officially adopted civic seal is municipal expression

**Holding:** A municipality's selection and adoption of an official seal used to authenticate its communications, personnel, and property makes the seal its own expression, despite private origin. Attribution does not by itself make every governmental religious acknowledgment unconstitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part II, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pinette* and *Chabad-Lubavitch of Vermont v. Burlington* preserve neutral private access but distinguish official adoption. *Rosenberger*'s protection of independently edited private expression does not convert the City's identifier into private speech.

##### Whether this continuing civic identifier establishes an impermissible religious affiliation

**Holding:** Government violates the Establishment Clause when the full context of its continuing civic expression identifies it with a particular faith. An assumed secular historical purpose and accompanying secular images do not necessarily cure that effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; six Justices.

**Material application:** The recognizable Christian cross occupies an identifying quadrant in the composite seal used across official municipal operations. The design, history, uses, and lack of church control are considered together; favorable reactions and age do not settle the constitutional inquiry.

**Independent alternative holding:** A city may not deliberately and pervasively make a recognizable faith symbol a permanent identifying element of its present civil authority. Edmond's actual combination of official adoption, institutional-identification function, and continuing use independently violates that rule.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part IV, unanimous. The Court directly adopts the bounded distinction from Kennedy's *Allegheny* separate writing; nine direct votes, rather than a combination of incompatible rationales, establish its authority here.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The alternative requires the actual institutional-use combination, not merely governmental ownership or religious subject matter. It requires no proven evangelical motive, church control, added proselytizing inscription, compelled profession, denied benefit, or visual dominance. Religious place names, historical buildings, inherited coats of arms, holiday acknowledgments, exhibits, and private symbols retain their separate contexts; none is categorically approved or invalidated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The effects ground demands neither an offense poll nor coercion and creates no context-free graphic prohibition. Purpose and entanglement need not be independently decided. No new credibility finding or universal constitutional-fact review rule is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Lemon* supplies the independent effects inquiry without being replaced. *Allegheny* supplies contextual affiliation analysis and the distinction adopted in the alternative holding. *Lee*'s school grounds remain distinct; coercion is not the exclusive municipal-seal inquiry. *Kiryas Joel*'s denominational civil-authority rule is not enlarged into a universal display prohibition.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

##### What prospective relief follows from unconstitutional present official use

**Holding:** Relief must end use of the cross-bearing seal as the City's current official identifier within a reasonable implementation period set by the District Court, while permitting accurate contextual preservation of historical records and displays.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V, unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The granted Establishment Clause judgment is affirmed. The Court prescribes no replacement design, destruction of artifacts, suppression of private speech, or fixed deadline. Longevity does not justify indefinite continuing official use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Personal liability, immunity, nominal damages, unappealed Free Exercise and state-law claims, attorney-client production, and independent fees or costs are outside this merits determination. Existing proper relief and fee proceedings are not displaced, but no new fee award is made.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-371.
**Decided:** 1998-06-25.

##### The consideration requirement is not facially invalid under the First Amendment

**Holding:** In this competitively selective arts-grant program, §954(d)(1)'s requirement to consider general decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values, alongside artistic excellence and merit, is facially permissible because it prescribes neither a categorical viewpoint exclusion nor fixed disqualifying weight. The consideration is mandatory; an invidious discriminatory application, a penalty on independently financed expression or coercive suppression remains separately reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer). The narrow facial-validity part is directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on a broader ground; their votes are not added to this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The five-Justice rule adopts no categorical viewpoint veto, fixed weighting, panel-composition safe harbor or universal subsidy exemption. Private art is not treated as government speech. Section 954(d)(2)'s obscenity prohibition remains distinct; the supplementary spending theory has only four votes. Settled individual grant claims are not revived and no particular award is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rosenberger's program-specific viewpoint rule and Forbes's genuine-selection and viewpoint-review requirements remain intact; Denver Area is not extended into categorical grant immunity.

##### Comparative artistic standards do not establish facial vagueness here

**Holding:** In this selective grant scheme, the decency and respect considerations, read with artistic excellence and merit, are not facially void for vagueness merely because comparative artistic judgment cannot be reduced to precise objective criteria. This conclusion concerns discretionary arts patronage, not a criminal prohibition, a license required to speak or an objective entitlement to payment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer). Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the narrow vagueness part. Scalia and Thomas independently reject the claim through their broader subsidy rationale. Stone-Zsela does not decide it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual discriminatory applications remain reviewable. The decision awards no grant and supplies no immunity for coercive licensing or criminal speech restrictions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The speech-protective concern with uncontrolled coercive discretion is preserved; its demands are applied in light of this program's actual grant function rather than transferred unchanged from criminal or licensing rules.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment and the facial injunction are reversed. The case returns for implementation of that disposition. No application-specific judgment or new grant decision is commanded, and the settled claims remain settled.

#### Board of Regents of the University of Wisconsin System v. Southworth, 529 U.S. 217 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1189; Seventh Circuit Nos. 97-3510 and 97-3548.

**Decided:** March 22, 2000.

##### Whether a broad independent student-expression program may use a general compulsory fee without ideological refunds

**Holding:** A public university may require a generally applicable student activity fee to support a broad program of independent extracurricular student expression serving its educational mission when eligibility, allocation and administration are objective and viewpoint neutral. Within that program, a student's disagreement with a recipient's political, ideological or religious viewpoint does not require an ideological opt-out or pro rata refund.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes. Stevens, Souter and Breyer concur in the judgment without joining the framework.

**Material application:** Independent student control, broad educational opportunity and stipulated viewpoint-neutral administration justify the ordinary program together. Absence of attribution, majority approval or enrollment alone is insufficient. Optional contributions and refunds remain permissible choices.

##### What viewpoint neutrality requires within the actual student-expression program

**Holding:** The protection for a compulsory independent-expression program requires viewpoint-neutral eligibility, allocation and administration; neither an educational label nor popular approval permits selection by viewpoint. Reasonable neutral limits on program scope, financial accountability, performance and fraud prevention remain available; the rule does not require equal grants or funding every activity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes. Stevens, Souter and Breyer concur in the judgment without joining the framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This rule does not impose forum neutrality on every curricular choice, faculty instruction or governmental message, and it confers no immunity from other constitutional requirements. Neutral access to religious editorial expression does not itself authorize governmental worship or devotional instruction.

##### Whether the binding funding referendum is validated by the ordinary-allocation stipulation

**Holding:** A university cannot distribute access to its compulsory private-expression program according to majority approval of a group's viewpoint. The binding referendum is outside the ordinary-allocation neutrality stipulation, so the Seventh Circuit's general compulsory-subsidy judgment must be reversed and the case remanded only to determine the referendum's actual operation and appropriate relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes. Stevens, Souter and Breyer concur in the judgment without joining the framework.

**Material application:** The referendum may allocate, terminate or veto a group's support and is treated as binding. Its actual operation, not an assumed neutral final override, must be examined. The Court holds neither that every ballot is unconstitutional nor that popularity substitutes for neutrality.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The operative remand does not reopen a late partisan-policy challenge foreclosed by the reiterated ordinary-allocation stipulation, and no written restriction is assumed rescinded, amended or universally valid. The separate limits on GSSF lobbying or primarily political groups are not exceptions to a different religious-activity provision. The decision does not adjudicate every outside expenditure or every written exclusion. Off-campus location alone supplies no categorical constitutional disposition of an otherwise eligible student expressive activity; the actual program relationship and proper claim remain necessary.

No Establishment, free-exercise, RFRA or state claim is revived or decided. Religious editorial viewpoint is not identical to governmental worship or devotional instruction. No actual devotional purchase is established here, and the ruling does not authorize one. The two reported WISPIRG sums are not treated as the same accounting measure or used to calculate relief. Unchallenged program components remain outside the judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the general compelled-subsidy liability judgment and remand only for the referendum and relief its actual operation warrants. Do not restore the previously removed all-students injunction or detailed administrative scheme. No fixed refund, damages, particular appropriation, universal opt-in mechanism or revived late partisan-policy challenge follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Abood v. Detroit Board of Education; Keller v. State Bar of California:** Their protection against compelled nongermane ideological support remains effective in exclusive-representation and integrated-bar settings. Their representative-duty inquiry does not itself decide this different educational expression program.
- **Lehnert v. Ferris Faculty Association:** Its union chargeability framework and divided applications remain intact; the statutory-duty separate account is not converted into a universal Court rule for universities.
- **Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc., June 25, 1997:** Its specified permission for germane nonideological promotion within collective agricultural regulation is distinguished, not expanded into a prior approval of ideological student subsidies. Its recognition that financing can burden expression remains controlling at that scope.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995:** Its private-publication viewpoint equality and controlled printing-benefit holdings remain intact. #### Mitchell v. Helms, 530 U.S. 793 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1648.

**Decided:** June 28, 2000.

##### 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function

**Holding:** A publicly administered instructional-resource program violates the Establishment Clause when, in the application proved, it actually finances religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under the recipient's control as the funded instructional function. Neutral eligibility, a secular label, public title or allocation tied to school enrollment does not alone cure that defect; genuine individual benefits and independently controlled secular services remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Agostini's unified governmental-authority-and-function inquiry remains operative. An independently sufficient specific prohibition or established permissible relationship is addressed first; otherwise unresolved legally sufficient allegations receive ordinary attribution and function analysis. Per-capita enrollment allocation does not alone establish individual direction of the funded religious use.

##### 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof

**Holding:** The actual financed religious instruction or demonstrated religious use in this application requires correction and may not be treated as a mere hypothetical risk on remand. Religious location or identity, recipient control, fungibility, potential diversion and incidental institutional savings, separately or merely by accumulation, do not alone establish such a violation in another application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public title, content restrictions, independent provision and supplement-not-supplant requirements can support lawful administration; they are statutory commands whose compliance must be distinguished from their existence. The content restriction, the actual funded use and the supplement requirement do different work, and every statutory violation is not automatically a constitutional violation. The actual defect here cannot be reduced to religious affiliation or to a historically recalled book incident already corrected. Conversely, the established use proves neither the prevalence of misuse nor the failure of all controls. No universal failsafe-monitoring regime, separate corporate structure, wholly different personnel or mandatory new audit is imposed. Effective separation must address the actual financed function with measures appropriate to the application.

##### 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition

**Holding:** Public provision of equipment or library books to a religious school is not unconstitutional as a category when the actual relationship reliably preserves lawful secular use or another independently protected benefit. Meek and Wolman cease to control insofar as their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns such a relationship by reference to recipient character or presumed diversion; their unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Source, path, control and practical instructional operation remain relevant evidence. The Court does not approve every historical practice, equate neutral eligibility with permissible direct institutional support, or derive a universal entitlement to religious uses from individual-benefit precedents.

##### 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope

**Holding:** The reviewed federal declaration is vacated and the case remanded to determine the extent and severability necessary for effective prospective correction of the established financed religious use. The lower courts must preserve independently lawful applications and may use the existing record where sufficient; they may not require reproof of the established defect or continue it merely because every provider's use has not been quantified.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings but favors limited affirmance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The federal Chapter 2 declaration returns through the Fifth Circuit for effective relief against the established actual financed religious use, limited by provider, item, application and lawful severability as the record supports. The actual-use determination is not reopened. Existing materials may suffice; no new hearing is mandatory merely because some remedial extent remains to be determined. Any state-program consequence requires demonstrated legal dependence on the reviewed federal disposition. No unrelated program, separate docket, damages award or recoupment is decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The established actual use supplies neither quantitative prevalence nor a finding of inseverability. No automatic new trial, school closure, categorical exclusion, statewide or nationwide invalidity, damages or recoupment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Agostini v. Felton governs through the modified, unified Lemon inquiry, with actual operational control and secular-service qualifications. Religious location and incidental savings do not supply a presumption, while nominal neutrality does not excuse actual devotional financing.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman remains in force only as modified in Agostini; its former independent prongs do not return as an alternative test.
- Jackson v. Benson supplies the actual financed-function prohibition and the conflict-specific remedial inquiry, preserving lawful separable applications rather than treating all benefits to religious education alike.
- Strout v. Albanese preserves that financed-function boundary in its tuition relationship and expressly protects genuine secular separation; it is not a universal voucher judgment.
- Meek v. Pittenger and Wolman v. Walter are displaced insofar as their conclusive equipment-category reasoning condemns reliably lawful secular relationships by religious character or presumed diversion. Unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards are not erased.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia retains its combined private-editing, independent-attribution and controlled-payment safeguards; it does not generally authorize devotional purchases.
- Board of Regents v. Southworth retains its actual independent-expression setting and does not make instructional loans a speech forum merely because allocation is neutral.
- Mueller v. Allen and Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind retain their actual individual-benefit holdings. Per-capita institutional allocation does not establish every predicate of those relationships.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District remains an antecedent-law remand without an Establishment Clause merits holding; no unadjudicated interpreter theory is treated as current law.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

- Mitchell v. Helms; No. 98-1648. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function; 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof; 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition; 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope, 2000-06-28; Breyer Parts I–III with all eight other Justices; Part IV with all seven other Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Mitchell, June 28, 2000, for equipment/library-book category displacement and proof-and-remedy limits; Agostini’s June 23, 1997 unified inquiry and Jackson’s March 16, 1999 financed-function components retain their original force.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. Benson — Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction](HOLDINGS.md#parental-routing-does-not-justify-the-demonstrated-purchase-of-integrated-devotional-instruction-1).

**Equipment-aid limits:** A statutory control and its actual observance are different, and not every statutory violation is constitutional. The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. It decides no universal voucher rule or entitlement to religious uses of public benefits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship does not establish religion. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

In a broad after-hours educational and community forum, an otherwise comparable private meeting cannot be excluded solely for its worship form; exact secular sacramental counterparts are not required. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and activity limits remain available. An admitted instructional subject likewise cannot exclude comparable religious instruction merely while permitting religious discussion. Independent private worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion; actual governmental adoption must be shown rather than inferred from religious identity or location.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official religious identification in governmental symbols

### Official religious identification in governmental symbols

**Governing question:** A government's use of religious symbolism as its continuing civic expression and present civil identifier.

**Current rule:** A municipality's adopted seal used to authenticate its communications, personnel and property is its own expression despite private design. Government violates the Establishment Clause when the whole context of its continuing civic expression identifies it with a particular faith; secular historical purpose and surrounding secular imagery alone do not cure that effect. Independently, a city may not deliberately and pervasively use a recognizable symbol of a particular faith as a permanent identifying part of its badge of present civil authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Robinson v. City of Edmond — II. The adopted municipal seal is the City's own expression, 1997-03-31; Part II, all nine, supplies attribution
- Robinson v. City of Edmond — III. Context establishes an impermissible official religious affiliation, 1997-03-31; Breyer Part III with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg (six) supplies the contextual-effects ground
- Robinson v. City of Edmond — IV. Making a faith's badge the City's permanent civil identifier is independently forbidden, 1997-03-31; Part IV, all nine, supplies the independent present-civil-identification prohibition
- Robinson v. City of Edmond — V. Relief must end the present official use and preserve the distinction from history, 1997-03-31; Part V, all nine, supplies the prospective-use/history distinction

**Present operation effective:** Robinson v. City of Edmond, 1997-03-31.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** Relief must end the unconstitutional current official use, allowing a reasonable implementation period; accurate contextual preservation of history remains permissible.

**Exceptions and limits:** Attribution alone does not invalidate every government acknowledgment of religion. Private equal-access expression, historical presentation and materially different acknowledgments retain their distinctions. Standing does not extend to a general claim of offense at a distant symbol.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory student fees for independent extracurricular expression

### Compulsory student fees for independent extracurricular expression

**Governing question:** When a public university may use a generally applicable fee to support independent student expression.

**Current rule:** A public university may finance a genuine broad educational program of independent extracurricular expression through a general student activity fee when eligibility, allocation and administration are objective and viewpoint neutral. Disagreement with a recipient’s political, ideological or religious views then requires neither an ideological opt-out nor a pro rata refund. Majority approval cannot determine access by viewpoint, including through a binding referendum. Neutral limits on program scope, financial accountability, performance and fraud remain permissible; neither equal awards nor funding every activity is required.

**Authority by component:**

- Program justification and absence of a required ideological exemption: Board of Regents of the University of Wisconsin System v. Southworth — The genuine educational private-expression program may be supported by a general fee, March 22, 2000.

Protection for religious editorial viewpoints does not authorize government worship or devotional instruction; the distinct Establishment, free-exercise and RFRA rules retain their own operation.
