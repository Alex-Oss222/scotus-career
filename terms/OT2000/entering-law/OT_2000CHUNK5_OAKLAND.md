## Standards and Tests selection: Present endangerment and RCRA citizen-suit abatement remedies

### Present endangerment and RCRA citizen-suit abatement remedies

**Governing question:** A private action under 42 U.S.C. §6972(a)(1)(B) and requested recovery of cleanup costs.

**Current rule:** Waste must potentially present an imminent and substantial endangerment when suit begins. A past contribution can cause that present threat, whose physical effects may occur later; danger wholly eliminated before filing does not qualify. The citizen-suit powers authorize restraint and necessary affirmative abatement, not a general private reimbursement action for past cleanup labeled damages or equitable restitution. Remaining danger at commencement does not itself authorize reimbursement of earlier expenses, including emergency cleanup. Partial cleanup does not defeat prospective relief if a qualifying threat remains and the other statutory conditions are satisfied.

**Authority by component:**

- Meghrig — filing-time threat, 1996-03-19; Souter for all nine.
- Meghrig — remedial construction and exact cost reservations, 1996-03-19; Souter and all seven other Associates (eight) adopt the full limit; Stone-Zsela also joins the narrower completed-cleanup-only rejection.

**Present operation effective:** Meghrig, March 19, 1996.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Section 6972(b)(2) ordinarily requires ninety days after notice to EPA, the affected State and alleged contributors; a subchapter-III violation action may begin immediately after notification, without a general emergency waiver. Government-action bars require abatement/restraint of the acts or conditions contributing to waste activities potentially presenting the alleged endangerment. Federal bars cover commenced and diligently prosecuted §6973/CERCLA §106 actions, actual §104 removal, costs initiating a §104 remedial investigation/feasibility study plus diligent remedial action, or a court order (including consent decree) or §106/§6973 administrative order under which a responsible party diligently performs removal, investigation/feasibility study or remedial action. An administrative-order bar lasts within its scope and duration only. State bars cover commenced and diligently prosecuted §6972(a)(1)(B) actions, actual §104 removal, or costs initiating §104 investigation/feasibility study plus diligent CERCLA remedial action.

**Open question:** Whether a private injunction may require payment of cleanup costs arising after a properly commenced action, or costs paid after invocation of RCRA’s statutory process may otherwise be recovered, remains unresolved. Invocation is not equated with filing; no notice-stage expense classification is decided.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 6972(f) preserves existing remedies without creating or guaranteeing another claim. Section 6972(e) permits appropriate litigation costs, including reasonable attorney and expert fees, in a final order for a prevailing or substantially prevailing party; those are not cleanup-cost recovery. EPA’s separate §6973 reimbursement authority, CERCLA remedies and petroleum classification retain their independent scope.

**Holdings navigation:** Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc., 516 U.S. 479.

## Standards and Tests selection: Unsecured-creditor prejudgment asset restraints

### Unsecured-creditor prejudgment asset restraints

**Governing question:** Whether general federal equity permits freezing unencumbered assets to secure a purely monetary unsecured claim.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not use general equitable authority to freeze unencumbered assets before judgment for a general unsecured creditor suing solely for money damages without a lien, equitable interest, statutory authorization or other recognized source of prejudgment control. Likely success and threatened collection difficulty do not supply that power. Rule 65 regulates procedure; Rule 64 preserves independently authorized security routes without creating the missing substantive authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “General equity does not supply this unsecured-creditor asset freeze”, 1999-06-17; Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** Grupo Mexicano, June 17, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Specific property in dispute, statutory freezes, independently authorized fraudulent-transfer relief, bankruptcy orders, Rule 64 attachment and postjudgment enforcement retain their own bases. No bond liability, damages or lien is established.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** A distinct challenge to legal authority during the preliminary period may remain live after final contract success and permanent conversion when asserted preliminary injury and an existing bond preserve consequential relief; a bond alone is not conclusive. Grupo Mexicano Part I, all nine Justices, supplies this bounded continuation.

#### Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc., 516 U.S. 479

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-83.
**Decided:** March 19, 1996.

##### Present endangerment and the independent limits on private RCRA cleanup-cost recovery

**Holding:** Section 6972(a)(1)(B) requires waste that may present an imminent and substantial endangerment when suit begins. Past contribution may produce a present threat and physical effects may occur later, but a danger completely eliminated before filing does not satisfy that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous. This ground independently requires dismissal of the pleaded claim.

**Material application:** KFC alleged completed cleanup years before commencement and no remaining danger. Continuing off-site disposal expenses alone did not allege continuing endangerment; partial abatement would not defeat an otherwise proper prospective action if a qualifying threat remained. This is a statutory pleading defect, not an Article III ruling or a finding on contribution, liability, unjust enrichment or reasonable expenditure.

**Independent alternative holding:** Section 6972(a)'s restraint and necessary-action powers authorize prohibitory and affirmative abatement relief, not a private reimbursement action for a past cleanup merely labeled damages or equitable restitution. Remaining endangerment when a proper action begins does not itself authorize general reimbursement of earlier expenses, including emergency-cleanup expenses; the two reserved categories below are unresolved rather than authorized exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct votes for the complete remedial construction. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the independently sufficient narrower holding rejecting this action solely for completed-cleanup costs and the reservation concerning expenses arising during a properly commenced abatement action; he does not join the broader earlier-expense proposition or the separate post-invocation reservation.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The Court reserves whether an injunction may require another party to pay cleanup costs arising after a citizen action properly begins, and, separately, whether cleanup costs paid after invocation of RCRA's statutory process may otherwise be recovered. Invocation is not equated with filing; classification of a notice-stage expense remains open. Neither remaining danger nor an emergency label reserves all earlier expenses. EPA reimbursement under §6973 is undecided. Section 6972(f) preserves existing statutory and common-law rights without creating a cause or guaranteeing recovery; §6972(e)'s appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party litigation costs do not create cleanup-cost entitlement. No universal monetary-equity prohibition, petroleum classification, limitations period, contingency-plan burden, laches rule, or adopted restitution elements follow. Notice compliance, state preclusion and mootness supply no independent dismissal ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for dismissal of the RCRA claim as pleaded. Independently pleaded or otherwise lawful claims retain their own requirements; no payment, discovery or trial is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gwaltney's different Clean Water Act ongoing-violation language does not displace RCRA's coverage of past contributors causing present danger. Hallstrom preserves the statutory notice and waiting conditions: ordinarily ninety days after notice to EPA, the affected State and alleged contributors; a subchapter-III violation may be sued on immediately after notification, with no general emergency waiver. Porter, Hecht and Franklin v. Gwinnett County preserve equity within congressional limits; Sea Clammers and Transamerica support respecting the specified enforcement scheme. Mertens preserves traditionally equitable categories without treating all money as damages. No earlier holding is overruled.

### Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc., No. 98-231

Merits decision, June 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 31, 1999; decided June 17, 1999. Certiorari to the Second Circuit, 143 F.3d 688, reviewing approval of a preliminary injunction. The questions are whether a live controversy survives the later permanent conversion and whether general equity permits an unsecured creditor seeking money damages to freeze assets in which it claims no proprietary interest.

The district court entered the preliminary restraint December 23, 1997, with a $50,000 bond after findings of near-certain recovery and threatened noncollection. On April 17, 1998, it entered an $82,444,259 money judgment, ordered turnover and converted the restraint to a permanent injunction. The turnover appeal is separate; conversion was no longer challenged. This review concerns the preliminary period.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The preliminary-period authority controversy remains live

When a preliminary restraint is challenged as beyond the court's legal power during the period before judgment, later success on the underlying contract and conversion to a permanent injunction do not necessarily extinguish that distinct controversy. Here the asserted preliminary-period injury and existing bond preserve a consequential authority question, while actual bond liability and damages remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I is joined by all nine Justices and controls at this limited level of generality.

Camenisch distinguishes a surviving case from a surviving preliminary-injunction issue. The Court applies that distinction rather than treating the mere presence of a bond as conclusive. The investors' success on their contract may justify relief after judgment without establishing authority to restrain an unsecured debtor beforehand. GMD's acceptance of the permanent conversion therefore does not resolve whether the earlier restraint was legally authorized. A ruling on that distinct question can matter to the asserted loss during the preliminary period. This is not review of the final money judgment or an adjudication of the separate turnover appeal. Whether a proper bond claim ultimately proves wrongful restraint, causal loss and an amount, and whether independent defenses defeat it, remains for the appropriate proceedings. No universal rule preserves every appeal involving an expired injunction and a bond.

##### General equity does not supply this unsecured-creditor asset freeze

A federal court may not use general equitable authority, at the request of a general unsecured creditor suing solely for money damages, to freeze a defendant's unencumbered assets before judgment when the creditor has no lien, equitable interest, statutory authorization or other recognized source of prejudgment control. Likely success and threatened inability to collect do not supply the missing power.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the complete rule, explanation and remedy.

The historically conferred equity jurisdiction permits flexibility within its sources and objects. It does not authorize creation of a security interest for an unadjudicated general debt. The judgment and proprietary prerequisites protect the debtor-creditor relationship; merger of law and equity did not erase them. Rule 65 regulates injunction procedure, and Rule 64 preserves authorized prejudgment security routes. Neither makes the inadequacy of collection itself a new source of power. The bondholders sought to secure payment from assets that were not the subject of their contract claim. The strong findings favoring them therefore address the usefulness of a restraint, not its legal availability.

Deckert involved equitable rescission and preservation in that setting. First National City Bank involved federal tax collection and statutory authority. Their preservation reasoning does not create the omitted private-creditor predicate. De Beers confirms that preliminary relief must remain within recognized equitable authority related to the available final remedy. Expanding general equity here would supply security and affect other creditors outside enacted attachment and bankruptcy arrangements. The Court does not restrict injunctions protecting specific property in dispute, statutory freezes, independently authorized fraudulent-transfer remedies, bankruptcy orders, Rule 64 attachment or postjudgment enforcement. Nor does it decide bond recovery, create a lien, establish fraud or adjudicate the rights of absent Mexican creditors.

#### Precedent Treatment

- University of Texas v. Camenisch: applies the distinction between a surviving case and the precise preliminary issue; the distinct legal-power consequence here survives without a present bond-liability adjudication.
- De Beers Consolidated Mines v. United States: applies its limit connecting preliminary relief to recognized authority and the permissible final remedy; it supports rejection of this unsecured-creditor freeze.
- Deckert v. Independence Shares Corp.: distinguishes its equitable rescission setting; its preservation authority remains intact within that setting.
- United States v. First National City Bank: distinguishes its federal-tax and statutory predicates; personal jurisdiction and preservation language do not alone establish private unsecured-creditor power.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: preserves its statutory equitable-category rule; it does not itself govern every private injunction or prohibit all equitable monetary remedies.
- Varity and Peacock: preserve their ERISA rules concerning appropriate individual equity and an independently violated obligation; neither supplies this private-creditor power.
- Department of the Army v. Blue Fox: preserves the distinction between an equitable label and an actual legal entitlement within its sovereign/APA setting.
- West v. Gibson: leaves intact the EEOC's specifically enacted compensation authority; it creates no general private-creditor injunction power.

#### Law After Decision

General federal equity supplies no prejudgment security freeze on the stated unsecured, purely monetary claim without an independent recognized source of control. Equity's flexibility, legally authorized attachment and statutory or proprietary remedies retain their respective scopes. Postjudgment enforcement remains distinct. A surviving preliminary-period authority issue may remain reviewable despite later final relief, but neither a bond nor success on the contract automatically determines bond liability.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, concurs in the live-controversy ruling and dissents from reversal. General equity's traditional preservation function, in their view, permits this provisional restraint given near-certain contractual recovery and the concrete threat that asset disposition and insolvency would defeat a judgment. They accept that the particular freeze was not historically granted but regard equitable function as adaptable without creating ownership or a lien. Deckert and First National City Bank support preservation by analogy despite their different predicates; De Beers does not foreclose relief directed to making the eventual money judgment effective. They would affirm this order without adopting the Second Circuit's entire preliminary-injunction test, allowing routine freezes for ordinary debts, treating necessity alone as authority, or resolving other creditors' rights and the separate final orders.

#### Record Limits

The record establishes neither bond damages, an adjudicated fraudulent transfer nor a lien in the restrained assets.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The reviewed preliminary-injunction judgment returns to the Second Circuit under the Court's legal-power ruling. Any bond proceedings remain distinct and require their own adjudication. The final money judgment, unchallenged permanent conversion and separate turnover appeal are outside this mandate. No new remedial entitlement or further Supreme Court event is established.
