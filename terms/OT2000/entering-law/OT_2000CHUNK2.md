## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.

**Authority by component:**

- Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment: Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.
- Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur: Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-08; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No heightened proof quantum or exhaustive list of exceptional circumstances is adopted. This settlement rule does not turn loss of review through circumstances outside the parties’ control into automatic vacatur.

## Standards and Tests selection: State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

### State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

**Governing question:** The measure and allocation of compensatory-excessiveness review in a New York diversity action.

**Current rule:** The district court applies CPLR §5501(c)'s material-deviation-from-reasonable-compensation measure through Rule 59, retaining federal new-trial procedure and allocation of functions. Pertinent state decisions guide compensation, without fixed prices or an imported state appellate hierarchy. The court of appeals reviews for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment; the Seventh Amendment permits that bounded review but forbids a first-instance appellate valuation of jury-tried compensation. A remittitur must offer the plaintiff reduced recovery or a new jury trial, not simply impose a substituted amount; federal additur remains forbidden.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

### Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

**Governing question:** State-remedy exhaustion and express waiver in §2254(b).

**Current rule:** Relief for a person in custody under a state judgment may not be granted unless state remedies have been exhausted, no available state corrective process exists, or circumstances render that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights. The court may deny on the merits despite nonexhaustion; it is not required to do so. A State is neither deemed to waive exhaustion nor estopped from relying on it unless its counsel expressly waives it.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Merits-relief restriction, factual presumption/rebuttal and cumulative hearing conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(2)–(4), 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)–(e), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court holdings and temporal limit, and objective legal inquiry: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by the seven other Associates.
- Materially truncated or objectively indefensible mitigation assessment: Terry Williams — III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- An additional Fretwell unfairness demand in an ordinary lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with the governing rule, with actual independent grounds still requiring examination: Terry Williams — VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part III with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Applicant-attributed deficient diligence and cumulative hearing exceptions: Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams) — I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure, April 18, 2000; Ginsburg’s Part I, unanimous. Reasonable available efforts and unknown concealed relationships: the same decision, III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead; all eight Associates.

**Present operation effective:** The two Williams v. Taylor decisions, April 18, 2000, for the stated legal-review and diligence constructions; the enacted factual presumption and other unchanged components retain their original authority.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Unresolved interaction:** Neither exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses commands a majority in Terry Williams; no Marks synthesis supplies one. No universal unreasonable-extension or refusal-to-extend rule is adopted. Section 2254(d)(2), §2254(e)(1), and the hearing conditions retain distinct work. Michael Wayne Williams does not replace the former-law Keeney rule outside the amendment’s temporal domain. Satisfaction of a statutory gateway does not itself establish the underlying constitutional violation or authorize relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — A continuing personal controversy is absent, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the continuing-controversy and unavailable-damages limits
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — Certification cannot restore the controversy, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the certification boundary

**Present operation effective:** Arizonans for Official English, 1997-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no categorical express-pleading requirement for nominal damages and does not decide a legally available personal-capacity damages claim. Limited authority to correct lower-court jurisdictional error is distinct from merits authority; judgment vacatur remains equitable under the existing rule. No automatic initiative-sponsor standing or mandatory certification rule is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (1997-03-03) — Federal Courts / Mootness, corrective jurisdiction and equitable vacatur.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

### Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

**Governing question:** Whether a privately owned federally chartered lender shares the United States' exception to §1341.

**Current rule:** Borrower-owned production credit associations suing for their own commercial interests do not receive the sovereign's federal forum merely because they have a federal charter, instrumentality designation or claimed substantive tax immunity. If a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists, §1341 bars their federal injunction and declaration action. Institutional identity, substantive tax immunity and federal-forum entitlement are separate questions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground](HOLDINGS.md#a-court-may-choose-a-straightforward-nonmerits-jurisdictional-ground-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

## Standards and Tests selection: Voluntary cessation and continuing controversy

### Voluntary cessation and continuing controversy

**Governing question:** Whether the defendant's postfiling compliance or cessation ends an initially live controversy.

**Current rule:** A defendant invoking voluntary cessation must make it absolutely clear that the challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. Compliance sufficient to defeat an injunction does not by itself meet that burden or negate standing established at commencement. Claimed permanent closure requires evaluation of the actual facts and legal restraints; neither retained legal permission nor dismantling alone determines recurrence.

**Authority by component:**

- Demanding cessation burden and distinction from initial standing or equitable relief: Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, Public Projection / Holdings / Later compliance does not automatically establish mootness; the closure evidence must be assessed, January 12, 2000; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Laidlaw, January 12, 2000, states the applicable instruction without replacing initial-standing requirements.

**Exceptions and limits:** Fees alone do not preserve a controversy. The rule does not determine that permanent cessation occurred or resolve the reserved survival of a penalty claim after it is proved.

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. ## Standards and Tests selection: Special federal corporations as governmental actors

### Special federal corporations as governmental actors

**Governing question:** First Amendment responsibility of a corporation specially created and governed by the Federal Government.

**Current rule:** A corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes when the Federal Government creates it by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains power to appoint a majority of its governing directors. A statutory nonagency label cannot defeat that constitutional status, and ordinary managerial discretion does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act. Charter, subsidy, regulation, governmental property, public benefit, a single appointment, temporary investment or present share ownership alone does not satisfy the combined rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental: Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental, 1995-02-21. Justice Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight Justices directly support the same permanent-majority-appointment rule and its application; Justice O'Connor's conditional view of a question she declines to reach is not a join.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-02-21; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule decides constitutional actor status, not the underlying speech claim, forum classification or relief. It supplies no universal agency, immunity, Treasury-liability, tort-liability or charter-jurisdiction classification.

## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

### Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

**Governing question:** Intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination of objectively intimate information entrusted for a limited criminal investigation.

**Current rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects this confined intimate marital and family interest against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination without sufficient governmental justification. An assurance of confidentiality informs the objective expectation but does not alone create a federal right; consent to investigative use is not consent to general publication. Ownership or authorship is not indispensable to a person's own intimate interest. Public inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not automatically establish prior dissemination, voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of distinct official facilitation of publication. The protection concerns intimate personal information, not reputation alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined protected interest and justification: I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty, 1996-04-29; Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Sheets, 1996-04-29.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure retain their separate rules. There is no universal strict-scrutiny or least-intrusive-means test for personal, financial or business information, general deletion right, inherited privacy claim, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author’s speech. Independent municipal attribution and conduct-date qualified immunity remain. The unobjected trial intent instruction does not establish a universal new mental-state rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private payment choices and governmental attribution

### Private payment choices and governmental attribution

**Governing question:** Whether statutory permission and public review mechanisms make a private compensation carrier’s initial withholding state action.

**Current rule:** Private carriers’ independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because the State permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis of responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or an exclusively governmental function. Screening for proper invocation and assigning a reviewer do not by themselves substantively approve the medical objection or order the initial suspension.

**Authority by component:**

- Choice-specific attribution and limited significance of permission and review: Canonical Decision Record `american_manufacturers_v_sullivan_merits_1999-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier’s payment choice governmental, 1999-03-03; seven direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** The legislature’s own act, a public obligor, an official review decision or a distinct proved attribution basis requires its own inquiry. The ruling does not make all acts in the compensation system private.

**Holdings navigation:** [American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan — Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental](HOLDINGS.md#statutory-permission-does-not-itself-make-the-private-carriers-payment-choice-governmental-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal detention credit, initial computation and conditional release

### Federal detention credit, initial computation and conditional release

**Governing question:** Who initially computes credit under 18 U.S.C. §3585(b), when the computation occurs, how time credited to another sentence is treated, and where conditional release ends and official detention begins for credit eligibility.

**Current rule:** The Attorney General through the Bureau of Prisons must initially calculate §3585(b) credit after the federal sentence commences; a district court may not calculate that credit at sentencing. Time already credited against another sentence when the federal term begins cannot also receive federal credit merely because the federal sentencing hearing occurred first. Administrative review and proper subsequent judicial review remain available. Under the federal Bail Reform Act, a conditional-release order is not official detention under §3585(b), however severe its restrictions; no conditions-based credit inquiry converts that release into detention. Qualifying uncredited detention resulting from the offense sentenced or another charge for which arrest occurred after that offense retains the statutory credit command.

**Authority by component:**
- Actor, timing and review: United States v. Wilson, March 24, 1992, “BOP makes the initial credit computation after commencement.”

## Standards and Tests selection: Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The constitutional justification federal, state or local government must supply when it changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

In K–12 public schooling, supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation may constitute an important lawful objective. An existing desegregation decree is not a prerequisite to that objective, and its absence proves neither a remedial duty nor invalidity. Demographic percentages, annual classifications or a beneficial label do not establish the educational condition, substantial fit or justification of individual burdens; no duty to adopt preferences or presumption validating numerical targets follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. **Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. #### Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join the remaining holdings and the judgment.

##### II. Two supported interstate mechanisms can authorize regulation of noneconomic intrastate conduct

**Holding:** Congress may regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when its inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. Congress may also regulate such conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's regime rule is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Ginsburg and Breyer join both rules as the appropriate inquiry given the Court's independently controlling Part I, while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government must identify and support the mechanism; legislative latitude does not supply missing facts

**Holding:** The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. Courts independently determine whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally eligible; Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive and empirical questions, magnitude and practical regulatory design once an eligible mechanism is identified, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For the regime route, the Government must identify the valid interstate regulation and explain with support why excluding the challenged class would permit evasion or materially impair it. For the externality route, it must support each part of the cross-border mechanism and fit described in Holding II. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. The obligation concerns support for the invoked mechanism; it does not presume that every individual act must cross a state boundary or produce a separately substantial effect.

##### IV. The judgment must be vacated for application of the two routes to the existing record

**Holding:** Where the judgments sustaining these challenged applications have not adequately determined whether the existing legislative and adjudicative record supports either eligible interstate mechanism, the proper disposition is to vacate and remand for that inquiry. The lower courts must identify the mechanism actually asserted and determine whether the record materially supports its required connection and reasonable fit; neither categorical invalidation nor judicial invention of missing facts is permitted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sweet Home requires significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

The distinct threatened-species protections, section 7 agency and incidental-take provisions, scientific permissions, experimental-population provisions and enforcement channels receive no new application here. Sweet Home's reserved section 6(g)(2) question remains reserved; deciding its terms or application would require the operative official text and applicable State-agreement record. The Court inserts no commercial element into section 9 and announces no categorical immunity for traditional State subjects.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 5–4; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer support the disposition. The existing legislative and adjudicative record must be examined for material support of either eligible mechanism; neither categorical invalidation nor judicial invention of missing facts is permitted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied and developed. Its connection-before-magnitude sequence, enacted-class fit, independent judicial eligibility inquiry and no-insertion rule remain controlling. The Court resolves its open question concerning a materially supported substantial interstate externality; generalized downstream effects remain insufficient.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Applied as the first route for necessary inclusion of local conduct in a valid interstate regime. Its anti-evasion and material-impairment requirement is preserved; statutory comprehensiveness alone does not establish it.
- **United States v. Rybar, November 24, 1997:** Preserved as an application of Hale to the demonstrated relationship between possession and prohibited machinegun stock; that relationship is not inferred for the ESA.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for the same fitted regime relationship and the absence of any judicially added interstate element; no machinegun premise is transferred to species protection without support.
- **Wickard v. Filburn:** Preserved within its properly defined economic-class scope. Aggregation measures effects after a sufficient connection; it does not manufacture the connection missing from a noneconomic taking class.
- **Perez v. United States:** Preserved for class-wide assessment of connected economic conduct without requiring every act independently to cross a state boundary.

#### United States v. Morrison / Brzonkala v. Morrison, 529 U.S. 598 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-5 and 99-29, consolidated.
**Decided:** May 15, 2000.

##### Commerce authority for the enacted private-assault action

**Holding:** Congress's findings of the serious economic consequences of gender-motivated violence do not supply the constitutionally adequate interstate connection needed to sustain §13981's presented private-assault action. The enacted class is not directed at an actual interstate market, shown reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of an identified valid interstate regulatory regime, or supported as a substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize; downstream economic magnitude cannot replace that missing connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for both constitutional holdings and their stated limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent on Commerce grounds and reserve the Section 5 alternative; their reservation is not a vote sustaining that alternative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical economic-conduct requirement, universal jurisdictional element, individual border crossing, numerical threshold or findings checklist is adopted. National prevalence alone does not establish a noninternalizable interstate externality, and inclusion in a broad federal package alone does not prove anti-evasion necessity.

##### Section 5 and the relationship to discriminatory state protection

**Holding:** Section 5 does not sustain the presented nationwide action against a private assailant for gender-motivated violence when its defendant, triggering private wrong and relief lack a sufficient remedial relationship to the unconstitutional state denial invoked to support it. A private assailant's gender animus does not itself establish the State's constitutional denial, and Congress's account of discriminatory state justice does not, without that fit, convert liability for the private assault into enforcement of equal state protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for both constitutional holdings and their stated limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent on Commerce grounds and reserve the Section 5 alternative; their reservation is not a vote sustaining that alternative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State action includes official administration, judicial action and legally operative custom. The holding requires remedial fit, not individual proof of a completed violation in every case, a universal bar on private defendants, a geographic restriction, a sunset or a findings quota. The four dissenters reserve Section 5 rather than vote to uphold this alternative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The action concerns a crime of violence committed because of or on the basis of gender and due at least in part to gender animus. The referenced violence includes acts or a series constituting a felony against the person, or a felony against property presenting a serious risk of physical injury, within the State or federal offenses described in 18 U.S.C. §16; it also includes otherwise qualifying conduct excluded only because of the relationship between actor and victim. The definition does not require occurrence in federal maritime, territorial or prison jurisdiction. Gender motivation must be shown by a preponderance of the evidence; random violence unrelated to gender is excluded. No prior criminal complaint, prosecution or conviction is required.

Section 13981(c) includes a person acting under color of state statute, ordinance, regulation, custom or usage and provides compensatory and punitive damages, injunctions, declarations and other appropriate relief. Federal and state courts share jurisdiction. Neither §13981(c) nor 28 U.S.C. §1367, by reason of this claim, confers federal jurisdiction over state-law claims seeking a divorce, alimony, equitable distribution of marital property or child-custody decree. None of these distinct provisions supplies an omitted interstate-commerce or unconstitutional-state-denial element.

Hypothetical color-of-law applications and unpresented severability are not resolved. Title IX litigation, state remedies and other VAWA provisions remain outside the judgment. The Court adds neither an interstate-commerce element nor a state-failure element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the presented private-assault claim in both consolidated dockets. Neither asserted source of power sustains it; no finding of assault, animus, causation or liability, damages award or new proof opportunity follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Lopez (April 26, 1995):** Applied its enacted-class, eligible-connection and fit sequence and its rejection of downstream magnitude as a substitute for connection. The Court declines the dissent's proposed revision; the rule remains controlling.
- **American Life League v. Reno; United States v. Bird:** Distinguished direct interference with an actual interstate medical-service market from the enacted class of private assault. Their service-market holdings remain effective.
- **United States v. Robinson; Reno v. Condon:** Distinguished operating business assets with an enacted commerce element and the identified interstate information market. Neither makes a victim's eventual spending or a crime's costs a sufficient connection.
- **United States v. Hale; United States v. Kirk; Hiley v. Barrett; Gillespie v. City of Indianapolis:** Preserved their fitted anti-evasion or material-impairment relationships to valid interstate supply regimes. The Court finds no analogous identified relationship for this action; domestic violence alone was not their constitutional mechanism.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied its independent substantial-cross-border-externality route and evidentiary/fit requirements. The route remains available for noneconomic conduct; national prevalence is not its substitute, and its particular existing-record remand created no general right to another evidentiary chance.
- **United States v. Hinds:** Distinguished its statutory property-use inquiry from the constitutional mechanism and text of §13981. It supplies no judicially inserted element here.
- **The Civil Rights Cases, 109 U.S. 3 (1883); United States v. Harris, 106 U.S. 629 (1883):** Applied the boundary between state constitutional denial and unsupported private wrong, retaining their recognition of official administration and appropriately adapted corrective legislation. Neither is read as a categorical private-defendant bar. The 1883 Harris is distinct from the later Commerce case bearing that name.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Applied its congruence-and-proportionality requirement and prohibition on replacing the substantive right while retaining preventive coverage beyond individually proven violations. No geographic, temporal or findings-count prerequisite is added.
- **Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank:** Distinguished its upheld preventive patent-property remedy, tied to the States' deprivations, exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative redress. That holding remains intact.
- **College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board:** Applied the requirement of an actual constitutional injury rather than a statutory tort alone.
- **Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents:** Its reservation of Section 5 supplies no decision of this question.
- **Saenz v. Heldenfels Brothers, Inc. (May 9, 2000):** Distinguished an official's own affirmative removal of protection from private harm not prevented by the State. It supplies neither such an act in this complaint nor a holding on Congress's preventive enforcement power.

#### Reno v. Koray

**Decided:** June 5, 1995.

##### Official detention and federal conditional release

**Holding:** Time under a federal Bail Reform Act conditional-release order is not official detention under § 3585(b), even when the restrictions are severe, so Koray’s release period requires no conditions-based credit inquiry. This rule concerns the presented federal release/detention distinction, preserves qualifying uncredited detention and judicial review, and does not decide state custody or require physical BOP control for every creditable day.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven directly adopt the status rule and its application without an independently sufficient administrative-deference holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mandatory credit remains for qualifying detention resulting from the offense sentenced or another charge for which arrest occurred after that offense, if not credited against another sentence. Administrative practice does not supply an independent deference holding.

- United States v. Wilson, March 24, 1992: applies BOP initial computation, no duplicate credit and subsequent lawful review; Wilson did not itself define official detention.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Third Circuit's conditions-inquiry judgment and remand consistently with exclusion of this federal conditional-release period from section 3585(b) credit. #### Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1393.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to obtain a favorable application of a substantive rule subsequently rejected does not establish Strickland prejudice where the lost benefit is one the governing law does not recognize and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. This limited rule does not replace ordinary reasonable-probability analysis, excuse deficient performance, or require an additional showing of unfairness in the usual ineffective-assistance case.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): applied and clarified for loss of a subsequently rejected substantive rule; ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains the usual test.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986): applied by analogy for the distinction between a changed outcome and loss of a legally protected benefit; no finding of perjury by Fretwell is made.
- Kimmelman v. Morrison, 477 U.S. 365 (1986): distinguished; a procedural bar on direct habeas consideration does not eliminate a meritorious underlying claim, unlike the substantive premise here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 (1988): applied for constitutionally sufficient narrowing at the guilt stage despite duplication at sentencing.
- Collins v. Lockhart, 754 F.2d 258 (8th Cir. 1985), and Perry v. Lockhart, 871 F.2d 1384 (8th Cir. 1989): Collins's displaced double-counting rule supplies no cognizable benefit; Perry's rejection of that rule supplies the accepted litigation premise. Whether Collins could independently be revived is not decided.

##### Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry

**Holding:** Teague's limitation on new rules supporting collateral relief does not require a federal habeas court to disregard a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State when deciding whether an omitted objection caused cognizable Strickland prejudice. Performance remains assessed from counsel's perspective at the time, while the prejudice inquiry here does not preserve an entitlement to the rejected Collins rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989): distinguished from an affirmative entitlement to an obsolete rule; its collateral finality limitation is not made reciprocal.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): preserves the contemporaneous perspective for evaluating performance without mechanically fixing the prejudice inquiry to every favorable rule then available.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice remains the usual rule; no separate unfairness requirement is added in ordinary cases. Deficient performance is conceded. The Court does not revive Collins, decide the Solicitor General’s separate §2254(a) theory or determine the unresolved penalty-preparation claims. Thomas’s state/federal hierarchy observation is not a majority holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit grant of relief on the Collins-only ground. Unresolved independent claims retain their own treatment; no new sentence, stay or execution is ordered.

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. No new trial, hearing, release, sentencing proceeding, or determination of every admissibility ground follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ornelas*'s review allocation is applied unchanged. *Kimberlin* permits circumstantial proof when motive is an underlying element without adding one here. *Armstrong* remains confined to federal charging and discovery; its requirements and unresolved discrimination remedy do not become police-stop rules.

#### Kansas v. Hendricks; Hendricks v. Kansas

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1649 and 95-9075.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** Consolidated cross-petitions concern the same commitment and receive one affirmance on the as-applied Ex Post Facto ground.

##### The civil designation of personal commitment remains rebuttable by clearest proof of punishment

**Holding:** In assessing whether Kansas's civil commitment of Hendricks constitutes punishment for Ex Post Facto purposes, the Court first determines the Legislature's intended civil or criminal character; an intended civil scheme is treated as punishment only upon the clearest proof that its purpose or effect is punitive. The Act's civil designation is relevant but not conclusive, and neither confinement, a connection to crime, indeterminate duration, criminal-style procedural protection nor treatment difficulty alone decides that demanding inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory care, review and release obligations are relevant but do not prove actual compliance. Clearest proof does not demand successful cure or make each treatment deficiency punishment; no burden shift, mandatory alternatives inquiry or exhaustive checklist is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ursery’s civil-intent and clearest-proof method is extended from its specified forfeitures to this personal-confinement question, without transferring its forfeiture outcome or Double Jeopardy limits. Ward preserves demanding intent-and-effect analysis; Mendoza-Martinez permits cumulative assessment without a conclusive single indicator.

##### The cumulative original operation makes this retrospective commitment punishment

**Holding:** The cumulative circumstances of Hendricks's original commitment—additional confinement after his sentence, selection tied to earlier sexual offenses, prisonlike restraint, and initially inadequate treatment sustained on the prospect of a future specialized program despite acknowledged possible treatment—provide the clearest proof of punitive operation in this application. Because the Act imposes that additional punishment for conduct predating its enactment, its application to Hendricks violates the Ex Post Facto Clause, and the judgment invalidating this commitment is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Material application:** At the October 1994 hearing, specialist contracts remained unsigned and the dedicated program was not established; commitment nevertheless continued on the prospect of a future program. Procurement efforts, direct-care staff, general hospital professionals, planned evaluation and an earlier prison-treatment offer remain part of the contrary evidence. The cumulative conclusion does not rest on total absence of clinicians or treatment opportunities.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No failed-bid motive, deliberate withholding of effective therapy, complete absence of a clinical plan, rejected adequate alternative, sham release process or later treatment regimen is found. No prospective facial invalidity, automatic rule for later genuinely civil programs, or validity of another commitment route is decided. Double Jeopardy is not reached. The non-dispositive due-process discussion supplies no holding on diagnostic vocabulary, treatment-related process, broader propensity definitions, personality disorder, absolute inability to control conduct or a new instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Kansas judgment invalidating this commitment in both dockets, 5–4. Hendricks may not remain confined under this application for pre-enactment conduct. No new factfinding remand, release date, stay, hearing schedule, clinical protocol or alternative custody authorization is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sahhar’s rejection of a hypothetical sentence ceiling presupposes independently lawful civil custody and does not classify this Kansas confinement or import federal procedures. Foucha, Addington and Heller are unchanged; non-dispositive discussion creates no new due-process rule. Morales remains confined to its parole-opportunity subject, Lynce supplies exhaustion only, and Blockburger and retained Grady are not applied.

#### Bond v. United States, 529 U.S. 334 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-9349.
**Decided:** April 17, 2000.

##### Exploratory touch can search closed luggage without opening it

**Holding:** Placing closed personal luggage in a shared overhead area exposes it to ordinary movement, incidental contact and associated compression, but does not expose concealed contents to an added physical examination of their shape and density. **Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven votes for the rule, its application and the bounded remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary repositioning and incidental contact remain outside this rule. No general canine-sniff or supported safety-search question, detention-duration claim or all statement-specific consequences are decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is reversed for implementation of the actual drugs-and-confessions suppression motion. The drugs cannot rest on the rejected no-search theory, and the Government's disclaimed subsequent-consent ground cannot be reopened. No independent admission ground is found preserved; unreviewed causal and statement-specific issues remain. The mandate orders no automatic acquittal, release or new trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz and Place are applied to privacy retained in closed luggage despite travel in a public conveyance.
- The independent-luggage rule is applied: a bag's separately established privacy does not disappear with the surrounding vehicle's public accessibility.
- Dickerson is applied for the boundary between authorized contact and added exploration; its protective-frisk setting is not imported as search authority.
- Hicks is applied to additional physical acquisition of concealed information rather than mere observation.
- Ciraolo and Riley are distinguished because they concern information exposed to public observation; their objective approach remains intact.
- Flippo preserves the distinction between lawful presence, a visible exterior and concealed contents; no general presence-based exception is created.
- Wardlow preserves the sequence of objective justification and forbids reliance on later discoveries to justify the earlier intrusion.
- Robinette retains the separation between consent voluntariness and independence from prior illegality; no consent ground is adjudicated here after the Government's nonreliance.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). **Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. **Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. **Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. #### Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1525.
**Decided:** February 21, 1995.

##### Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental

**Holding:** When the Federal Government creates a corporation by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains authority to appoint a majority of its governing directors, the corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes notwithstanding a statutory declaration that it is not a governmental agency. Amtrak satisfies those combined conditions; ordinary operating discretion in its corporate officers does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- San Francisco Arts & Athletics, Inc. v. United States Olympic Committee, 483 U.S. 522 (1987): distinguishes federal charter and assistance to a private institution from special creation coupled with permanent governmental majority-appointment authority.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 (1991), and Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992): preserve attention to actual governmental legal machinery in their jury-selection settings; neither makes billboard advertising an exclusively governmental function or supplies a general rule for all publicly benefited corporations.

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., decided November 14, 1994: keeps the distinct inquiry into an entity's state sovereign immunity separate; absence of immunity does not imply constitutional privacy.
- American National Red Cross v. S. G., 505 U.S. 247 (1992): preserves its express charter-jurisdiction rule, which supplies no constitutional actor classification.

#### United States v. National Treasury Employees Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1170.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Scope of public-employee speech relief (The existing ban is unenforceable throughout the certified class; The injunction does not adjudicate the claims of unrepresented senior employees; Crane retains his individual relief without a grade-only remand).

##### A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden

**Holding:** When the Government broadly restricts future compensated expression by its employees acting as citizens, Pickering requires a justification commensurate with the restriction's actual reach and burden on employees and listeners. Section 501(b)'s honoraria ban is unconstitutional as applied to the represented lower-level employees' protected expression unrelated to their employment because the record does not adequately connect that burden to actual or apparent misuse of office; narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563, supplies the employment balance, applied to the breadth of a prospective restriction. Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138, supplies public-concern principles without limiting protected subjects to politics or workplace criticism. Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994, retains its distinct particular-speech and reasonable-account rules. Simon & Schuster v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, supplies the relevance of financial burdens. Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC, June 27, 1994, preserves substantial but reviewable legislative predictive judgments; it does not decide this employment case.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 501(b) prohibits receipt of an honorarium by a Member, officer or employee; §505(2) excludes special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202. Section 505(3) covers money or anything of value for an appearance, speech or article; a series falls within the definition if its subject directly relates to official duties or payment is because of governmental status. That parenthesis does not already restrict every single honorarium. Actual and necessary travel expenses for the individual and one relative are excluded to the extent another person pays or reimburses them, and otherwise deducted to the extent they are not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses mean transportation, lodging and meals away from residence or principal employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received, subject to a $2,000 ceiling on each payment and no financial benefit to the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child or dependent relative. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and a civil penalty up to the greater of $10,000 or compensation received. Under §504(b), a written opinion from an entity identified by §503 protects its recipient and another covered person whose facts are materially indistinguishable from subsection (a)'s sanction only for conduct after issuance, in good faith and in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings. These are qualified provisions, not general exemptions. The separate §501(a) high-level outside-income ceiling is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. Nonenforcement of the existing ban throughout the certified below-GS–16 class is preserved by five votes; Crane retains individual relief by six; relief for unrepresented senior employees is removed. Congressional and judicial coverage, §501(a)'s outside-income ceiling and valid independent ethics restrictions are not invalidated. No penalty or compensation award is entered.

##### Assigned instructional production and the asserted personal speech right

**Holding:** A public-school teacher's selection, production, and direction of material as part of the assigned school instructional program constitute institutional curricular expression subject to the school system's lawful control; the artistic discretion entrusted to the teacher does not, on this claim, create a personal First Amendment right against curricular and related employment control. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Settle v. ### Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College — 2000-11-27

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The dissent would recognize an employee's direct claim when attributed expression protected by the public-employment rules substantially motivates an adverse employment action, subject to the employer's proof that it would have acted for an independent lawful reason. The First Amendment constrains the punitive use of expression against the person officials target; mistaken identity does not remove that person's injury or justify suppressing protected criticism. Actual authorship would therefore be unnecessary. Pickering–Connick would determine protection and legitimate employment interests, and Mt. Healthy would govern causation and the same-decision defense. Erroneous attribution or an imperfect investigation alone would prove neither liability nor the other elements. Waters's limited protection for genuine reliance on a reasonable account of disciplinable speech would remain; it would not protect hostility to protected criticism. Letters, notices, chronology, testimony, and competent circumstantial evidence could establish the actual ground under ordinary Crawford-El procedure, without a statistical requirement.

The dissent would also replace Harlow's defense insofar as lack of sufficiently similar precedent defeats damages after an underlying violation is established. An official would instead have to prove actual and objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly permitting the challenged conduct. Legal silence, custom, or general supervisory power would not suffice. In the dissent's view, §1983's remedy and the protections for reasonable factual mistakes within the substantive inquiry make that narrower reliance defense adequate. Elder's independent consideration of relevant law and ordinary procedural protections would survive, as would independent absolute immunity. This would change existing immunity doctrine, including positions Stone-Zsela previously accepted. He would reverse and remand to determine protected status, adverse action, motive, causation, any supported reliance defense, and compensation for proved injury. No damages judgment would follow now, and prospective relief would require an actual continuing threat rather than a presumed revival of the expired notice.
