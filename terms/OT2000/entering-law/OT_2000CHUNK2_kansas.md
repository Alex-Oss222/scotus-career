### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground](HOLDINGS.md#a-court-may-choose-a-straightforward-nonmerits-jurisdictional-ground-1).

#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 91-543, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-558, County of Allegany, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-563, County of Cortland, New York v. United States et al. The same component-specific relief applies in all three dockets.

For the ripe-review holding in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563, see Federal Courts — Ripeness of future statutory obligations.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to the distinction between an authorized conditional arrangement and compulsory state regulation; it supplies no blanket Guarantee Clause justiciability rule.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.

#### Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503.

**Decided:** June 27, 1997.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Both consolidated dockets are reversed, 5–4, with declaratory relief for Printz and Mack and remand for entry of conforming judgments.

For the officers' concrete-injury holding in Printz/Mack, June 27, 1997, Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503, see Federal Courts — Official injury and statutory commands.

##### Part I — Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons

**Holding:** Congress may regulate private conduct within its enumerated powers, preempt conflicting state law and use federal institutions to enforce federal obligations. But the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses do not authorize Congress to command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative investigative, screening, record-processing or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons. The constitutional defect is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not the number of hours required or the absence of federal reimbursement. Section 922(s)(2)'s mandatory duties for chief law-enforcement officers are therefore unconstitutional insofar as they compel the petitioning officers to administer the interim federal firearm-transfer system.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Those five adopt the rule as it governs affirmative investigative, screening and enforcement duties, including the records research and implicit required acceptance of the dealer's notice and the purchaser's statement that §922(s)(2)'s mandatory inquiry entails, and its application to that inquiry. O'Connor does not join Part I insofar as its rule reaches purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements; that reach has four joins and is stated separately below. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the categorical rule.

**Material application:** Structural federalism carries decisive weight, and a categorical distinction between direct federal regulation and compulsory state executive administration implements it; no case-by-case balancing of burdens and benefits applies. Section 922(s)(2) commands local officers to undertake federal transaction checks. Reasonableness governs the effort required, not whether the office must act, and the designee option does not let the office decline the assigned responsibility. The statutory constraints on duration, effort and use of information do not make the duty voluntary, and the State is not merely complying with a direct law regulating its own activity. The commands, with the acceptance of the dealer's notice and the purchaser's statement that the mandatory inquiry implies, fall within the prohibited category. The officers' budget and unauthorized-expenditure assertions illustrate an assignment of federal responsibility without state choice, but the holding does not depend on proving a cost, a state-law prohibition or personal liability, and an assumed promise of reimbursement would not cure the defect.

**Material statutory qualifications:** Section 922(s)(2) directs reasonable effort within five business days after the specified dealer notice, including research in available state and local systems and the designated national system. It does not require an arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Section (6)(B) concerns a transmitted purchaser statement: unless the officer determines the transaction unlawful, the statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement; disclosure is limited to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use is limited to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Section (6)(C) separately requires written reasons only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. With the mandatory checks removed, these conditional provisions compel no participation from officers who decline the program, and they are not separately declared invalid. Their operation after voluntary participation is not decided, no officer receives a general license to retain, use or disclose information, and no transaction-specific trigger is found satisfied or unsatisfied here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule governs affirmative federal conscription of unwilling state executive personnel for administration of federal regulation of private actors. It does not prohibit direct federal regulation of state activity, generally applicable laws, duties assigned to state courts as courts, voluntary cooperation or otherwise valid conditional federal spending, and it does not limit Congress's authority to regulate the underlying transactions. It is not a physical-occupation rule, an individual-rights scrutiny standard or a totality test, and it rests on structural federalism without any Take Care or unity-of-the-executive holding. The Court leaves unresolved duties under constitutional grants specifically involving state governmental action, including federal elections; actual consent to cooperative arrangements; statutory compensation agreements; and the complete rights of nonparty dealers. No individual Second Amendment question is decided, and the decision creates no compensation rule and no new substantive severability rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New York v. United States: extended from compulsory state legislation and ownership/liability to compulsory executive administration of federal private-party regulation. The extension is stated as such; New York itself did not decide commands to executive officers. Its lawful-inducement and generally applicable regulation boundaries remain, and its severability rule applies without change.
- United States v. Lopez: preserved; authority to regulate the underlying transaction does not independently establish authority to select state executive personnel as administrators. Its connection-before-magnitude requirement and prohibition on adding a missing element are unaffected.
- United States v. Harris and United States v. Hale: retain their direct-instrumentality and integral transfer/stock grounds, respectively. Valid commercial regulation does not itself make every means used to implement it proper.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority and South Carolina v. Baker: preserved; generally applicable federal regulation of state activity is distinct from directing States to govern private actors for Congress. No immunity for every traditional governmental function is revived.
- Testa v. Katt and Howlett v. Rose: preserved for state-court adjudication under the Supremacy Clause; state courts applying federal law as courts are not executive officers administering a federal program.
- FERC v. Mississippi: preserved within its bounded regulatory and adjudicative setting; it supplies no general authorization for compulsory executive field administration.
- Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson: retains its congressional-election and presidential-registration holdings at their source-specific scope. Duties under federal elections and other express constitutional assignments are reserved.
- Alaska Airlines v. Brock: preserved on severability; the Court need not determine every consequence for nonparty dealers or other unchallenged provisions, and it does not decide whether independent dealer transmission or waiting duties continue.

##### Part I — Reach to purely ministerial record-processing (opinion of four Justices)

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Kennedy, would extend the categorical prohibition to purely ministerial record-processing duties that Congress commands unwilling state executive officers to perform as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons, even when those duties involve no investigation, screening or enforcement. O'Connor joins every other part of the opinion but not this reach; her concurrence reserves purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements. With four joins the proposition is not a holding of the Court, and Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the categorical rule altogether.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The proposition decides no application in these cases; the judgment rests on Part I's five-Justice holding as applied to §922(s)(2)'s investigative and screening commands. Whether purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements imposed on unwilling state executive officers fall within the prohibition has no Court answer.

##### Part II — Reversal, declaratory relief and conforming judgments

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm; Souter would vacate and remand on his fiscal ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** In both dockets, reverse the Ninth Circuit's constitutional sustainment of the compulsory chief-law-enforcement-officer duties. Declaratory relief is entered that the federal government may not compel Printz and Mack to perform the interim checking obligations of §922(s)(2). On remand, the lower courts must enter conforming judgments protecting the officers against enforcement of those duties. No damages or reimbursement award follows; the officers do not need to prove an amount of fiscal loss to establish the structural violation, and there is no remand for a budgetary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not invalidate independent federal duties imposed on firearm dealers, private purchasers or federal personnel, decide unpresented dealer claims or rewrite the statute's complete severability scheme; the permanent federal system is not invalidated. The remand directs conforming judgments, not further constitutional adjudication of the conditional provisions or of absent parties' duties. Relief runs to Printz and Mack, while the holding carries ordinary precedential force; no nationwide injunction, class relief or new federal administrator is ordered. The criminal-vagueness, unraised civil-vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment matters outside the grant remain undisturbed.

**Material judgment:** Reversed in both dockets, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support reversal; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the judgments sustaining the interim duties, and Souter would vacate them and remand for consideration of the officers' budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections. All nine Justices join the jurisdictional section of Thomas's opinion. O'Connor concurs, reserving purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements. Stevens dissents, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer; Breyer also dissents separately. Souter dissents separately; his fair-value position is joined by no other Justice and is not law.
