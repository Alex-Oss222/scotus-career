#### Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, 512 U.S. 504

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-120.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The redistribution provision reasonably limits newly shifted educational costs

**Holding:** Under the governing rule giving an administering agency's interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with that regulation, the Secretary reasonably construes 42 C.F.R. §413.85(c) to bar increased reimbursement resulting from transferring previously medical-college-borne educational costs to the hospital's patient-care account, even when the underlying clinical education is generally eligible. That construction sustains the challenged denial for the reporting year ending June 30, 1985; it does not establish a universal historic-dollar ceiling, make every newly measured expense ineligible, or decide the separate community-support ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. Five participating Justices directly adopt this regulatory construction, its application and its limits.

**Material application:** Section 413.85(a)(1) provides that allowable cost may include the net cost of approved educational activities under paragraph (g), apportioned for Medicare utilization under §413.50. Permission to include those costs is not an unconditional payment command. Paragraph (g) deducts tuition revenue and includes trainee stipends, teacher compensation and other direct and indirect educational-activity costs under Medicare cost-finding principles. The recodified paragraph (a)(2) directs payment for approved residency programs in medicine, osteopathy, dentistry and podiatry to §413.86 for cost reporting periods beginning on or after July 1, 1985. That later-period exception does not itself disallow the reporting year ending June 30, 1985, and the Court fixes no later-period payment or base-year amount.

Section 413.17(a), subject to the separate exception in paragraph (d), includes the cost of services, facilities and supplies furnished by organizations related through common ownership or control at the related organization's cost, capped at the price of comparable items that could be purchased elsewhere. It does not independently make an otherwise excluded educational cost allowable. No party's entitlement under paragraph (d) is decided, and its separate exception is not a premise of this holding. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)'s reasonable-cost system takes direct and indirect costs into account within authorized methods and included-item rules, excludes unnecessary costs in efficient delivery, and bars shifting Medicare costs to non-Medicare patients or the reverse. It does not guarantee payment of every asserted actual expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co., 325 U.S. 410 (1945): applies controlling weight to a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation; the plainly-erroneous-or-inconsistent limit remains operative.
- Udall v. Tallman, 380 U.S. 1 (1965): applies the same own-regulation standard; an alternative reasonable reading does not by itself displace the Secretary's permissible construction.
- Gardebring v. Jenkins, 485 U.S. 415 (1988): preserves the limit where an alternative reading is compelled by the regulation's plain language or other indications of the Secretary's intent at promulgation; no such compulsion is established here.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144 (1991): applies its account of reasonable own-regulation interpretation and administrative expertise; its allocation of interpretive authority under the Occupational Safety and Health Act remains specific to that statutory structure.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves reimbursement within lawful cost methods and limitations, including authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods; actual expenditure alone does not establish entitlement here.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., June 17, 1994: preserves the requirement to respect operative statutory limits; its mandatory-tariff holding does not invalidate this textually supported regulatory restriction or replace the own-regulation standard.
- Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: preserves the distinction between delegated authority and its actual exercise; its treatment of a general reasonable-doubt policy does not render every statement of regulatory intent nonoperative or create a Medicare proof burden.
- Ohio State University v. Secretary, Department of Health and Human Services, 996 F.2d 122 (6th Cir. 1993): rejects its contrary construction insofar as it excludes this transfer of otherwise eligible clinical-education costs from the redistribution provision; its separate community-support analysis is not resolved.

##### The actual agency ground survives the asserted inconsistency

**Holding:** The Administrator's expressed redistribution ground and the established history of college-borne costs support this denial; a memorandum's silence about redistribution and an intermediary's prior allowance do not, on this record, establish an authoritative contrary interpretation that defeats it. A considered, otherwise permissible interpretive change is not automatically invalid, but contrary text, genuine inconsistency and the required agency explanation must be examined, and a reviewing court may not substitute a new ground or missing finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this answer to the consistency and actual-ground objections; it supports application of the redistribution holding rather than furnishing an alternative reimbursement bar.

**Material application:** Letter 78-7 requires that faculty-service costs would be allowable if incurred directly, be allocated consistently among recipient components, comply with related-organization and comparable-price limits, and satisfy the applicable patient-care relation, documentation, reasonable-allocation and reasonable-amount requirements. Its identified additional nonfaculty costs are limited to direct and space costs. It does not permit duplicative additions where the hospital maintains its own corresponding library, administrative structure, office or clerical support. Those conditions survive; the Court does not read silence about redistribution as an unconditional promise of all overhead. The record does not explain the failure to claim reimbursement before 1974, and the Court does not turn that silence into a finding of community support or an estoppel rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court affirms on redistribution alone. It neither endorses nor rejects the separate construction treating all non-Medicare funding as community support; decides no merits of the Administrator's additional university general-and-administrative-cost ruling under §§413.9, 413.85 and Letter 78-7; and orders no reimbursement, penalty, recoupment, reopening of the settled faculty-compensation amounts, or relief on the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim. The original $2,861,247 disputed difference is not adjudicated as an amount remaining after settlement. Materially different retrospective applications, later §413.86 calculations and entitlement under §413.17(d) remain unresolved. No independently sufficient alternative holding is adopted, and no fractured-rationale synthesis is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: applies its rule that considered interpretive change is not automatically disqualifying while preserving scrutiny of text, delegation and reasons; unsupported litigation positions receive no automatic controlling weight.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: applies the requirement of a discernible actual agency rationale; the express redistribution ground satisfies it without a new judicial explanation.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943): preserves review on the agency's own ground rather than a substitute invented on review.
- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): preserves required reasoned agency decisionmaking and the prohibition on appellate rationalizations supplying missing findings.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): preserves the rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; this decision rests on the Administrator's stated rationale and decides no new retroactive-rulemaking question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The challenged reimbursement denial is affirmed, 5–4, on redistribution alone. No additional reimbursement or remand is ordered. The separate community-support construction and general-and-administrative-cost ruling are neither independently sustained nor rejected; settled faculty amounts and the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim remain untouched.

#### Auer v. Robbins

**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

For the separate questions in Auer v. ##### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

Stinson distinguishes interpretation from amendment; Martin identifies responsible interpretive authority; Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. None is overruled or given an employer-factfinding effect.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

For the compensatory-time use holding in Christensen, May 1, 2000, No. 98-1167, see Labor and Employment — Public compensatory time and compulsory paid use.

#### American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-2000
**Decided:** 1999-03-03.

##### Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental

**Holding:** These private carriers' independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because state law permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis for responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or a function exclusively reserved to government; those predicates are not established for this initial withholding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven direct joins control.

##### General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service

**Holding:** General eligibility for workers' compensation does not by itself establish an unconditional constitutional right to constant payment of every disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. This rule neither extinguishes a protected claim to fair adjudication nor decides against a claimant an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient individual expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight direct joins control. Stevens supports the payment disposition on a different ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991; Rendell-Baker v. Kohn, 457 U.S. 830:** Applied to distinguish regulation or public support from governmental responsibility for the particular private act.
- **Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922:** Applied; a state-created procedure and sufficient governmental participation remain separate requirements.
- **Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614:** Distinguished for actual participation in the governmental jury function; its attribution inquiry remains effective.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Preserved for governmental institutional creation and permanent control and for the proper scope of an included question; it supplies no finding of such carrier status here.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium:** Preserved at its actual component holdings; it creates neither universal immunity for permissive legislation nor automatic attribution of every permitted private choice.
- **Tulsa Professional Collection Services v. Pope, 485 U.S. 478:** Preserved; a non-self-executing procedure does not eliminate the need to identify significant governmental involvement.
- **Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422; Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill, 470 U.S. 532:** Preserved for protected claims and the distinction between substantive entitlement and procedure; neither establishes unconditional payment of every disputed bill.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Its procedural inquiry is not replaced or applied to decide every preserved individual or public-party claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private initial withholding requires its own governmental attribution basis. General benefit eligibility alone supplies no unconditional advance-payment right. Actual governmental conduct, fair adjudication of protected claims, prior service entitlements and properly supported continuation interests remain distinct. The decision establishes no universal immunity or absence of property and designs no additional statewide procedure.

**Operative remedy or transition:**

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Other positions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private carriers' initial withholding | Attribution ruling reversed, 7-2 on reaching this ground | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Ginsburg and Stevens would not decide this additional ground; neither holds that the carriers are state actors. |
| Categorical advance-payment/severance requirement | Set aside, 9-0, on divided grounds | All nine | Eight join the limited entitlement rule; Stevens relies on fair governmental process. |
| Unchallenged notice/input and distinct preserved issues | Left undisturbed or returned for ordinary resolution as applicable | The mandate preserves their actual scope | No new merits ruling on matters outside review. |

On remand the supported private-carrier constitutional theory must be rejected absent a distinct established attribution basis. No immediate payment follows solely from general eligibility. The Court orders no wholesale dismissal of public-defendant claims, damages, class certification, new discovery or additional procedural design. Only actually pleaded, preserved and unresolved claims remain; the Court creates none.

#### Carter v. City of Philadelphia

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 98-1581; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** March 13, 2000.

##### Whether the Philadelphia District Attorney's own-prosecutor administration is local policy and protected by Pennsylvania's immunity

**Holding:** Pennsylvania's sovereign immunity does not bar the claims challenging this Philadelphia office's training, supervision and administrative policy. For training and supervision of the office's own prosecutors, the District Attorney supplies final local policy under Pennsylvania law; neither conclusion classifies every prosecutorial function or establishes authority over police.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Material application:** Pennsylvania law, local office control and the City's primary responsibility for judgments establish the relevant local institution. Election, title, territory, funding or final discretion alone would not suffice. The County-officer designation has a home-rule and optional-government exception; the decision does not depend on that label. Defined state intervention for resource insufficiency, conflict or judicially established failure or refusal amounting to abuse does not establish ordinary state control of training.

##### What fault, notice and causal connection a municipal training or supervision omission requires

**Holding:** Carter must prove a federal deprivation attributable to a municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate or conscious training or supervision omission amounting to deliberate indifference, and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing his injury. Notice may arise from an established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligence, employee wrongdoing and hindsight that more training might help do not satisfy municipal fault or cause. Perjury's obvious wrongfulness alone does not show an obvious need for specialized instruction. Any police-control theory requires actual authority and causal proof; cooperation alone creates no employment relationship.

##### Whether heightened pleading can be demanded for this municipal-policy claim

**Holding:** The then-operative Rule 8 pleading standard governs this municipal claim without a judge-created demand for heightened factual particularity about internal policies before discovery. The alleged pattern and policy-related omissions suffice against the categorical dismissal grounds presented, while the substantive liability elements and ordinary procedural controls remain fully applicable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 11, managed discovery and Rule 56 remain available. Municipal deliberate-indifference allegations do not automatically defeat personal qualified immunity, which depends on clearly established law at the time of the conduct. A municipality cannot borrow personal immunity; otherwise protected advocacy does not lose its functional protection merely through an allegation of dishonesty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No misconduct, innocence, actual policy, policymaker knowledge, pattern, supervisory control over police, deliberate indifference, causation or damages is found. The Court does not classify all Pennsylvania officials or all District Attorney functions, decide a new Rule 54(b) rule, resolve the annotated State supersession procedure, or impose particular personnel safeguards. The personal and state-law rulings outside the two granted questions receive no new judgment here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reinstatement of the municipal-policy claim within the two granted questions and remand for ordinary proceedings. Liability, damages, any trial and particular safeguards remain unestablished; personal and state-law matters outside the grant receive no new disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **McMillian v. Monroe County:** Applies its final-authority and governmental-identity inquiry to Pennsylvania's own legal structure. Its Alabama application does not determine every State or every prosecutorial function.
- **Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp.:** Applies the complete structural inquiry; treasury exposure remains highly probative rather than indispensable.
- **Regents of the University of California v. Doe:** Distinguishes a State's primary obligation with collateral indemnity from this independent local office's obligations; the antecedent structural inquiry remains necessary.
- **Monell v. Department of Social Services:** Preserves the requirement of the municipality's own policy or legally effective custom and rejects employer-based liability.
- **City of Canton v. Harris:** Applies deliberate indifference, notice, an identified deficiency and actual causal connection; no universal identical-incident prerequisite or relaxed fault standard is added.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserves the separate inquiries into a federal deprivation and municipal responsibility.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Applies the distinct deprivation, attribution, fault and causal requirements and the separation between personal immunity and municipal liability.
- **Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit:** Applies ordinary municipal pleading under the Rules and preserves distinct discovery and proof controls.
- **Conley v. Gibson:** Supplies fair-notice pleading under the Rules in force for this action.
- **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons:** Preserves the functional distinction between investigation or administration and protected advocacy; classification of an individual act remains separate.
- **Fletcher v. Kalina:** Preserves otherwise protected advocacy and separate personal evidentiary acts, without a general dishonesty exception or automatic loss of qualified immunity.
- **Chalfin v. Specter, Specter v. Moak, and Duggan v. 807 Liberty Avenue:** Supply their stated Pennsylvania structural classifications; none alone decides federal attribution or every office function.
- **Walker v. City of New York:** Its lower-court elaboration does not become a separate binding Supreme Court test; Canton governs.

#### Troxel v. Granville, 530 U.S. 57 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-138.
**Decided:** June 5, 2000.

##### The protected parental choice and absence of a complete visitation standard

**Authority of the judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3, leaving the petition dismissed and the visitation order unenforceable. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support affirmance; Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy oppose it. No controlling comprehensive visitation rationale exists under Marks.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The protected liberty of a fit parent includes directing the child's care and upbringing. A court may not override that parent's visitation decision solely because the judge considers a different arrangement better for the child; ordinary judicial preference is not a sufficient constitutional justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their agreement is at precisely this level; their distinct additional scrutiny, trigger and statutory-scope rules are not combined.

**Current force:** The parental-liberty and anti-substitution proposition controls at its stated level of generality; no complete visitation test follows from agreement in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds the parties and leaves this order unenforceable. Part II's full special-weight and record-application analysis has three votes, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela's serious-harm or comparable-need rule, Souter's statutory-breadth rationale and Thomas's strict-scrutiny ground remain separate. No common scrutiny level, universal harm requirement, parental-unfitness prerequisite, proof burden or general facial invalidation receives a controlling adoption. No Marks synthesis supplies one. Other statutes and materially different nonparent relationships remain open; the Smith and Wolcott disputes and collateral fees are not adjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Troxel petition remains dismissed and the visitation order unenforceable. No new best-interests proceeding, custody transfer, evidentiary hearing, fee award or disposition of the separate consolidated controversies is ordered. The decision leaves materially different visitation laws and relationships for cases presenting them.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Meyer v. Nebraska** and **Pierce v. Society of Sisters:** Apply the established protection of parental care and direction; neither supplies the entire ordinary-visitation test.
- **Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.** and **Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth:** Preserve protected family choice and instruction while distinguishing particular public-school veto claims from this intrusion.
- **Baby Richard v. Kirchner:** Preserves its bounded child-transition interest and protection of lawful parental custody against preferred-home substitution; it creates no permanent grandparent-contact entitlement.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C.:** Preserves the distinct clear-and-convincing rule before La. Rev. Stat. 9:364(D)'s mandatory complete-contact cutoff based on parental sexual abuse; it supplies no universal visitation burden.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg:** Remains a decision concerning the asserted physician-assistance entitlement, without an exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties.

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### Whether the unsupported treatment and unreconstructable trial risk require a new trial

**Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Material application:** The supported risk concerned attention, testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel as well as demeanor. Expert explanation cannot restore an altered conversation or response, and an unmedicated trial cannot reliably be reconstructed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

#### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-357.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, No. 92-357, see Federal Courts — Review of federal preclearance determinations.

##### Territorial racial design alone does not state the asserted deprivation

**Holding:** Alleged deliberate or predominant consideration of race in drawing territorial constituencies, even with departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself state this equal-protection claim where no racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation is alleged. This is a substantive disposition of the design-only theory, not heightened pleading, a universal requirement of additional downstream loss, or an added constitutional-injury prerequisite for an independent statutory claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary notice pleading applies; no expert proof or replacement map is required at pleading. Independent exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, civic-status and statutory claims retain their own elements. No statewide racial-strength defense, general replacement of Croson, predominance test or added constitutional-injury gate for §2 is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the design-only state claim (5–4); no injunction, new map or trial on that theory. Other properly presented claims and any properly sought amendment remain subject to ordinary procedure and preclusion. Leave federal-official dismissal undisturbed (9–0) on its separate channel and nonreviewability grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United Jewish Organizations v. Carey, 430 U.S. 144: its fractured reasoning supports a limited distinction, not a comprehensive controlling test or a statewide-white-representation defense.
- Wright v. Rockefeller, 376 U.S. 52: distinguished; racial purpose was not established there, so it did not decide the present assumed-purpose question.
- City of Richmond v. Croson, 488 U.S. 469, and Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1: personal equality retained; the Court declines to equate territorial design alone with Croson’s racial allocation of opportunities.
- Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399: official racial ballot labeling remains prohibited without proof that an election winner changed.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339: racial municipal disenfranchisement remains prohibited; Whittaker’s broader concurrence is not adopted as a rule covering every racial district adjustment.
- White v. Regester, 412 U.S. 755, and McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents, 339 U.S. 637: impaired participation may be unconstitutional despite formal access; no universal downstream-loss requirement.
- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41; Leatherman, March 3, 1993: ordinary notice pleading retained, no demand for expert proof or a replacement map.
- Growe and Voinovich: statutory prerequisites, state primacy and unresolved coalition/influence questions remain distinct; no additional constitutional-injury gate imposed on § 2.

#### Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The appellate alignment is Johnson v. Miller; the three docket aliases identify one constitutional challenge and one reviewed judgment for Georgia.

For the other questions in Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson), decided June 29, 1995, Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929. see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury (Personal exposure and standing are distinct from success on the merits); Federal Courts — Direct review of three-judge merits judgments (The merits denial of an injunction is directly reviewable).

##### Racial predominance alone does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, even when ordinary districting criteria are subordinated, does not by itself establish the asserted equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. **Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-517, 93-527 and 93-539.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### The State may not select a denomination as the recipient of general governmental authority

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause forbids a State deliberately to allocate general civil authority to a community selected as a religious community, even when the immediate recipients are elected public lay officials rather than clergy. Chapter 748 violates that rule because its design and context use denominational membership as the organizing basis of the special school district; religious homogeneity or coincidence with an existing village line alone would not establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II introduction and II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg: five Justices. This is an independently sufficient ground. O'Connor does not join that portion, and Kennedy's separate judgment rationale is not counted as a join.

**Material application:** Chapter 748 grants all powers of a union-free school district through a board of five to nine members elected by village voters for terms not exceeding five years. It contains no special-education-only limitation, sunset, religious voter or office qualification, or rabbinical appointment. The secular public program and professional staffing do not narrow that general statutory grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Larkin v. Grendel's Den, Inc., 459 U.S. 116: applies its civil-authority boundary by structural analogy to deliberate denominational allocation through a lay public board; it does not treat this board as a church or clergy veto.
- Larson v. Valente, 456 U.S. 228: applies denominational neutrality to the State's choice of recipients of general public authority; no general disability on religious communities follows.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: preserves equal political citizenship and distinguishes religious officeholders from governmental allocation by denomination.

**Independent alternative holding:** Chapter 748 independently violates religious neutrality by conferring an anomalously particular benefit of general school-government power on this selected denominational community without neutral terms or dependable assurance of equal treatment for comparable communities. The defect follows from this enactment's design and context, not a rule that every special act is invalid, every general statute is neutral, or an unproved finding that another religious community has already been refused.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This is an independently sufficient alternative ground; Kennedy expressly declines it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Larson v. Valente: applies the prohibition on denominational preference to the particular governmental benefit and its allocation.

**Holding:** A State may accommodate religiously grounded needs beyond what free exercise independently requires, but accommodation does not authorize this denominational allocation of general governmental power. Affirmance leaves the children's statutory special-education rights and lawful neutral local public services intact, without commanding a particular placement, validating the adequacy of earlier services or prescribing a replacement district.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II-C and the implementing limits in Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This holding rejects the accommodation defense and defines the remedy's limits; Kennedy reaches the judgment on his separate ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos: preserves permissible accommodation beyond free-exercise compulsion, with its denominational-neutrality and application limits.
- Zorach v. Clauson, 343 U.S. 306: preserves lawful accommodation of private religious needs without authorizing this governmental franchise.
- Wolman v. Walter, 433 U.S. 229: preserves the possibility of properly administered neutral-site services; no particular replacement is approved.
- Board of Education v. Wieder, 72 N.Y.2d 174, 527 N.E.2d 767: respects the state court's recognition of local educational discretion without turning permission into a compulsory separate school.

The Court does not replace Lemon, overrule Aguilar or Ball, create an aid-merits holding in Zobrest, revise Shaw, decide the state constitutional issue, or find religious voter qualifications, religious instruction, mandatory assimilation, a denied request by another faith, permanent religious demographics or a free-exercise entitlement to a separate district. **Operative remedy or transition:** Federal invalidation of Chapter 748 is affirmed, 7–2, in all three dockets. Implementation must preserve statutory educational duties and lawful neutral services. Felton, Holding II, and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale has no contrary force at that scope. Ball's completed judgment, Community Education and unaddressed holdings remain undisturbed. #### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

#### Pearson v. Hines

##### A false retaliatory charge causing concrete discipline violates the protected grievance right

**Holding:** A correctional officer may not knowingly file a false disciplinary accusation because a prisoner filed or threatened to file a lawful grievance when the accusation causes concrete discipline such as the confinement and television loss established here. **Material application:** The actual ten-day confinement and television loss establish consequences. Lewis requires claim-specific obstruction for an access-to-courts claim; it does not add a lost-lawsuit element to this different claim. Sandin addresses meaningful liberty under genuine substantive constraints, through a sufficient and nonexclusive rule; it does not supply the elements of First Amendment retaliation. Edwards concerns claims necessarily invalidating outstanding earned-credit deprivation. No credit loss or sentence extension is established here, and this judgment does not invalidate the disciplinary adjudication.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision protects no false grievance, threat or independent rule violation, and does not prohibit lawful discipline or valid prison restrictions. It decides no liability for an accusation causing no material consequence, every adverse comment, trivial inconvenience or subjective offense.

- **Sandin v. Conner:** Its substantive-constraint/meaningful-liberty rule remains distinct from retaliation; no atypical-hardship test is adopted.
- **Edwards v. Balisok:** Its credit-invalidity prerequisite is preserved at its actual scope; the described discipline establishes no credit loss or sentence extension.

##### Hill’s review of the adjudication does not shield the accusing officer’s retaliation

**Holding:** Hill’s some-evidence standard for a disciplinary adjudication does not defeat a claim that the accusing officer knowingly fabricated the charge in retaliation for protected conduct. Under ordinary proof and review rules, the prisoner must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the officer may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct, but a merely possible lawful explanation is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Material application:** The jury could consider Hines’s grievance history and reputation, his stated intent to grieve Pearson and the unchallenged falsity finding. Ordinary deference to a jury’s supported findings governs; the Court makes no new credibility determination. Pearson’s proposed some-evidence shortcut cannot replace the actual causation inquiry or supply a special heightened quantum. The verdict stands on the reviewed grounds. The Court decides no qualified-immunity question, converts no later law into notice at the January 1992 conduct date and reopens no separate officer’s judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s affirmance of Pearson’s damages judgment stands. No further merits remand is directed, and the other officer’s judgment and the disciplinary adjudication receive no new disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Superintendent v. Hill:** Its evidentiary minimum for disciplinary adjudication remains intact, but does not shield the accuser’s knowingly false retaliation.
- **Crawford-El v. Britton:** Ordinary direct and circumstantial motive proof governs without a special quantum; its separate conduct-date immunity defense is not adjudicated here.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle:** Its motivating-factor inquiry and defendant’s preponderance same-decision defense apply; possible lawful reasons are not proof of that defense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public title, content restrictions, independent provision and supplement-not-supplant requirements can support lawful administration; they are statutory commands whose compliance must be distinguished from their existence. The content restriction, the actual funded use and the supplement requirement do different work, and every statutory violation is not automatically a constitutional violation. The actual defect here cannot be reduced to religious affiliation or to a historically recalled book incident already corrected. Conversely, the established use proves neither the prevalence of misuse nor the failure of all controls. No universal failsafe-monitoring regime, separate corporate structure, wholly different personnel or mandatory new audit is imposed. Effective separation must address the actual financed function with measures appropriate to the application.

##### 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition

**Holding:** Public provision of equipment or library books to a religious school is not unconstitutional as a category when the actual relationship reliably preserves lawful secular use or another independently protected benefit. Meek and Wolman cease to control insofar as their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns such a relationship by reference to recipient character or presumed diversion; their unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Source, path, control and practical instructional operation remain relevant evidence. The Court does not approve every historical practice, equate neutral eligibility with permissible direct institutional support, or derive a universal entitlement to religious uses from individual-benefit precedents.

##### 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope

**Holding:** The reviewed federal declaration is vacated and the case remanded to determine the extent and severability necessary for effective prospective correction of the established financed religious use. The lower courts must preserve independently lawful applications and may use the existing record where sufficient; they may not require reproof of the established defect or continue it merely because every provider's use has not been quantified.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings but favors limited affirmance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The federal Chapter 2 declaration returns through the Fifth Circuit for effective relief against the established actual financed religious use, limited by provider, item, application and lawful severability as the record supports. The actual-use determination is not reopened. Existing materials may suffice; no new hearing is mandatory merely because some remedial extent remains to be determined. Any state-program consequence requires demonstrated legal dependence on the reviewed federal disposition. No unrelated program, separate docket, damages award or recoupment is decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The established actual use supplies neither quantitative prevalence nor a finding of inseverability. No automatic new trial, school closure, categorical exclusion, statewide or nationwide invalidity, damages or recoupment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

#### Auer v. Robbins

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-897.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

For the separate questions in Auer v. Robbins, No. 95-897 decided February 19, 1997, see Federal Courts — State immunity and local public bodies (The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri); Administrative Law — Interpretation of agency regulations (The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds).

##### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith penalties for major-safety infractions are permitted where the rule prevents serious danger to the plant or other employees.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

**Material application:** Residency discipline is not a major-safety penalty: that exception concerns serious danger to the plant or other employees. Salary amount and the appellate duties determination are uncontested, and no rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Holding:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or general compliance presumption is established. Competing examples and evidentiary-burden contentions remain disputed submissions; the Court directs no general reopening and leaves ordinary preservation rules intact. The later residency rule made termination exclusive but did not repeal all disciplinary authority.

##### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of the Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Correction does not transform a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. General deductions when work is unavailable indicate absence of salary-basis intent and defeat exemption during that period.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance is expressly conditioned on actual reimbursement and a future-compliance promise before disposing of the affected salary claim on restored exemption. Neither act is found complete; without both, that ground remains unavailable and lawful compensation consequences must be determined. No timetable, fixed overtime amount, restoration of the earlier $9,284 award or reopening of duties is ordered.

##### I. The statute does not make employee requests the exclusive means of using accrued time

**Holding:** Section 207(o) does not categorically forbid a public employer from directing paid use of lawfully accrued compensatory time merely because no agreement specifically authorizes compulsory use. The employer remains bound by lawful agreement restrictions, protected employee requests and the statute's accrual, value and overtime requirements; compelled paid use is not permission to confiscate credits or evade overtime.

Moreau preserves the later-hire individual route where the chosen representative cannot lawfully enter a binding agreement. **Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional property question or blanket employer power is decided. The absence of a demonstrated agreement term defeating the categorical claim is not a finding that every employee consented or that no agreement exists.

## Standards and Tests selection: Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury. For a training or supervision omission, the plaintiff must establish a deliberate or conscious municipal choice amounting to deliberate indifference, an identified deficiency closely related to the federal injury, and actual causation. Notice can come from an established pattern or an obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably necessary. Negligence, a single employee’s error, general supervisory responsibility and hindsight that additional training might have helped are insufficient. Responsibility for police conduct requires actual supported authority over that conduct rather than cooperation alone.

**Exceptions and limits:** There is no entitlement to a preferred library, physical browsing in every case, counsel for every prisoner or numerical program levels. ## Standards and Tests selection: False disciplinary charges in retaliation for prisoner grievances

### False disciplinary charges in retaliation for prisoner grievances

**Governing question:** When a correctional officer’s knowingly false disciplinary charge constitutes actionable retaliation.

**Current rule:** An officer may not knowingly fabricate a disciplinary accusation because a prisoner filed or threatened a lawful grievance when the accusation causes concrete discipline. Such discipline supplies adverse action without proof of subjective chilling, abandoned grievances, a separate due-process violation or loss of a nonfrivolous court case. The prisoner must show protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the officer may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. A possible lawful explanation alone is insufficient.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

- Mitchell v. Helms; No. 98-1648. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function; 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof; 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition; 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope, 2000-06-28; Breyer Parts I–III with all eight other Justices; Part IV with all seven other Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Mitchell, June 28, 2000, for equipment/library-book category displacement and proof-and-remedy limits; Agostini’s June 23, 1997 unified inquiry and Jackson’s March 16, 1999 financed-function components retain their original force.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. Benson — Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction](HOLDINGS.md#parental-routing-does-not-justify-the-demonstrated-purchase-of-integrated-devotional-instruction-1).

**Equipment-aid limits:** A statutory control and its actual observance are different, and not every statutory violation is constitutional. The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. It decides no universal voucher rule or entitlement to religious uses of public benefits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions prospective relief

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires that federal law require the state-law violation, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

**Authority by component:**

- Enacted conditions, scope and all-relief transition: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §802, original 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1)–(2), (c)–(d), (g) and §802(b)(1), April 26, 1996.
- Existing-decree application and substantive findings: Lewis v. Casey — Holdings IV–VI, Holding IV, June 24, 1996; Kennedy Part IV for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins only the statutory disposition. 
- Bounded constitutional operation of termination, not a change to the present entry’s remedial conditions: Plyler v. Moore, Plyler v. Moore — II. The prospective-relief rule preserves judicial adjudication, June 23, 1997; see the termination entry’s component authority.

- Federal-law requirement replaces federal permission; effective on enactment and in pending cases: Prison Litigation Reform Act amendments — §123(a)(1)(A) and (b), amending §3626(a)(1)(B)(i), 1997-11-26.

**Present operation effective:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123, 1997-11-26, for the federal-requirement condition; the other statutory conditions and Lewis qualifications retain their original dates.

**Controlling construction:** Prospective relief is all relief other than compensatory monetary damages. A §3626 civil prison-conditions action excludes habeas proceedings challenging the fact or duration of confinement. A prison includes federal, state and local juvenile or adult criminal detention facilities. Consent decrees include relief based wholly or partly on consent or acquiescence and must comply with subsection (a). Private settlements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct; otherwise available state breach remedies survive. Section 3626 does not govern relief a state court enters solely on state-law claims.

**Effective transition:** Amended §3626 applies to prospective relief originally granted or approved before, on or after April 26, 1996. Existing findings may meet the necessary predicates at their actual scope; ritual wording, modest cost or reasonableness alone neither establishes nor defeats compliance. Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states Plyler’s June 23, 1997 resolution of the presented Article III and due-process objections to original §3626(b)(2), with the full §3626(b)(3) preservation condition. Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin apply the amended conditions within the bounds stated in Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays; no automatic-stay constitutional holding follows.

**Related current rules:** Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states the separate actual-motion triggers; Prisoner-release orders and prison crowding supplies additional release-order requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996). Plyler v. Moore (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prison access and prospective relief.

**Former-rule note:** Before November 26, 1997, §3626(a)(1)(B)(i) required federal permission rather than a federal requirement for the departure from state law. Section 123 applies the revised term from enactment, including pending cases.

## Standards and Tests selection: Grant, vacatur and remand after a material development

### Grant, vacatur and remand after a material development

**Governing question:** When a changed premise warrants reconsideration without a Supreme Court merits decision.

**Current rule:** Under §2106, an intervening or sufficiently recent development makes grant, vacatur and remand potentially appropriate when there is a reasonable probability that the lower decision rests on a premise that court would reject on reconsideration and reconsideration may affect the ultimate outcome. The Court must independently weigh finality, lower-court independence, delay, cost and manipulative litigation. Government advocacy alone does not justify vacatur. The receiving court decides the development's relevance, authority and effect and may reach the same result; reconsideration establishes neither merits error nor entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Probability and independent equities: Lawrence — A material agency development can justify reconsideration without a merits decision, January 8, 1996; per curiam for Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six). Stone-Zsela's reasonable-possibility formulation is noncontrolling.

**Present operation effective:** Lawrence, January 8, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No automatic deference to a changed agency view, automatic relief upon Government concession, or conclusive presumption of error from an unexplained judgment is created. Stutson's same-day procedural disposition does not expand Pioneer beyond its decided scope.

**Holdings navigation:** Lawrence v. Chater, 516 U.S. 163.
