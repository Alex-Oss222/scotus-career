### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The constitutional justification federal, state or local government must supply when it changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

In K–12 public schooling, supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation may constitute an important lawful objective. An existing desegregation decree is not a prerequisite to that objective, and its absence proves neither a remedial duty nor invalidity. Demographic percentages, annual classifications or a beneficial label do not establish the educational condition, substantial fit or justification of individual burdens; no duty to adopt preferences or presumption validating numerical targets follows.

**Exceptions and limits:** The standard is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality, and it supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Sex-equality doctrine and other source-specific protections are not replaced; Shaw's design-only districting rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched.

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, Wygant and Paradise, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Material application:** The lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence. The Tenth Circuit's recorded stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and the concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority keep their ordinary force in their actual scope. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings, and a stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. The remand directions require the court to identify the actual program and preserved challenge; assess the supported objective and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories and scope to it; examine presumptions, certification routes and review in operation; examine burdens and concrete, practicable alternatives; and examine continued justification. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails, and relief addresses the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

#### Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 98-2503, 197 F.3d 123; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** May 30, 2000.

##### I. The actual transfer restriction must satisfy the common standard

**Holding:** A public school's racial transfer classification must satisfy Adarand's common substantial-justification standard: the government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, and justification for its coverage and actual individual burdens, including a response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Countywide percentages, administrative convenience, an inclusive label or racial allocation for its own sake cannot supply that showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer with Stone-Zsela, seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas separately acknowledge the standard's binding force but dissent from this application and remedy.

**Material application:** Race alone supplied the stated reason for this transfer denial. The actual restriction, including possible exclusion from the magnet lottery, imposes an individual burden requiring justification. The policy's unique-personal-hardship exception was used, including for siblings, but establishes neither this applicant's eligibility nor the policy's validity. Demographic figures alone establish neither the educational condition nor the denial's fit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and allocation for its own sake remain impermissible. A changed legal standard preserves supported findings and creates no automatic right to supplement the evidence.

##### II. Supported educational benefits of K–12 integration may qualify

**Holding:** Concrete educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation in K–12 public schooling can be an important lawful objective on a supported record. That permissible objective neither requires racial preferences nor validates this transfer mechanism; the government must still establish its educational claim, substantial fit and justified individual burdens under the common standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy expressly reserves this affirmative holding; Scalia and Thomas reject it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An adjudicated segregation violation is not the only possible basis for the objective, but the absence of a decree establishes no current remedial duty. No racial target, permanent policy or general obligation to use preferences is approved; the educational objective remains unproved here.

##### III. Correct the injunction inquiry without deciding final relief

**Holding:** The displaced legal premise requires vacatur of both appellate injunction directions and remand to apply the common standard to the preserved challenge and the ordinary requirements for preliminary and any properly available final relief. Further proceedings require an actual unresolved material issue under ordinary rules; the remand neither mandates new evidence nor establishes a right to a seat, final policy redesign or automatic denial of protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has the same seven direct joins as Part I; Kennedy supports this standard-and-remedy resolution without joining Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ordinary preliminary-relief inquiry retains its independent equitable requirements. Existing findings may suffice, and the lower courts must identify any unresolved material issue requiring further proceedings. Neither current enrollment, cessation of exposure nor loss of the controversy is presumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns for assessment under the correct constitutional and period-appropriate equitable standards. Both appellate injunction commands are set aside. Any interim arrangements must account for actual enrollment facts and ordinary procedural authority; no current placement, removal, stay, additional hearing or final policy is dictated by this judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Adarand Constructors v. Peña:** Applied unchanged to the school transfer restriction, including evidence, coverage, burdens, alternatives and ordinary-remand limits; its displaced strict-scrutiny formulation is not restored.
- **Hopwood v. Texas:** Its individual-opportunity and fitted-justification rules are applied. Its university-specific recognition of supported educational benefits is extended to the bounded K–12 objective adopted in Part II; its original reservation is not described as already deciding that extension.
- **Texas v. Lesage:** Applied to distinguish unequal-process injury from entitlement to a seat and other relief; no race-free-only counterfactual is imposed where controlling law permits justified consideration.
- **Adarand Constructors v. Slater:** Applied to preserve actual future-exposure and equitable predicates without assuming cessation, present enrollment or a vanished controversy.
- **Blackwelder Furniture Co. v. Seilig Manufacturing Co.:** The period-appropriate preliminary-relief framework used below remains the procedural setting; no later injunction formula is substituted.

### Sinkfield v. Kelley — 2000-11-27

The per curiam opinion requires a districting plaintiff to establish personal exposure to the challenged electoral treatment, causation and redressability. Residence in a majority-white district adjoining majority-minority districts and the neighbors' influence on map design do not alone establish personal racial-assignment injury; this record supplies no additional allegation or evidence connecting these plaintiffs to that treatment. Direct or circumstantial proof may suffice. There is no expert-study requirement, numerical racial threshold, electoral-defeat requirement, universal district-shape bar or residence-based entitlement to standing.

Johnson v. Miller's separate personal-exposure and shape-evidence holdings apply; shape can support an inference linked to actual treatment. Bush v. Vera preserves the personal-stake requirement. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (June 2, 1997) requires jurisdiction before contested merits; Ruhrgas permits bounded sequencing of nonmerits questions, not assumed injury. Shaw's substantive design-only rule remains unchanged. Personal unequal treatment may confer standing despite failure on the merits; independently supported dilution, exclusion or unequal participation is outside this record. Both judgments are vacated and the seven-district injunction dissolved for jurisdictional dismissal, without deciding the design-only merits, certifying a map or ordering a replacement. No tally or individual lineup is published.
