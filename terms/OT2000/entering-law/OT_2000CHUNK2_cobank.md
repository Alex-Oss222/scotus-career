### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

### Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

**Governing question:** Whether a privately owned federally chartered lender shares the United States' exception to §1341.

**Current rule:** Borrower-owned production credit associations suing for their own commercial interests do not receive the sovereign's federal forum merely because they have a federal charter, instrumentality designation or claimed substantive tax immunity. If a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists, §1341 bars their federal injunction and declaration action. Institutional identity, substantive tax immunity and federal-forum entitlement are separate questions.

**Authority by component:**

- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas — I. These associations cannot claim the sovereign's federal forum, 1997-06-02; Breyer and the seven other Associates (eight total) on complete rule; Stone-Zsela joins identity, adequacy and injunction grounds

**Present operation effective:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services, 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other federal agencies, special statutory jurisdictional grants and genuinely inadequate state remedies remain unresolved. The forum dismissal does not decide the substantive exemption.

**Holdings navigation:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas (1997-06-02) — Federal Courts / State-tax equitable relief.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

#### Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1918.
**Decided:** June 2, 1997.

##### Private production credit associations cannot borrow the sovereign's forum exception

**Holding:** Borrower-owned production credit associations suing in their own right and commercial interest do not acquire the United States' exception to §1341 merely from a federal charter, instrumentality designation or asserted substantive tax immunity. With the conceded plain, speedy and efficient Arkansas remedy, their federal injunction and declaration action must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous on institutional identity, adequacy and injunction; all eight Associates join the declaratory-relief application, with Stone-Zsela concurring in that judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The entity-specific rule establishes no universal United States-joinder requirement for every agency and decides no Federal Reserve or FDIC suit. The United States' amicus appearance does not make it a plaintiff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Department of Employment involved the sovereign and Red Cross as co-plaintiffs. Moe involved a separate statutory jurisdictional ground. Nash-Finch involved governmental regulation under §2283. None gives these commercial lenders the sovereign's exception.

##### Conceded state-remedy adequacy requires forum dismissal without tax merits

**Holding:** The associations' express concession that Arkansas supplies a plain, speedy and efficient remedy satisfies §1341's remedial condition on this record. Dismissal determines the federal forum only; the associations' substantive tax privileges and the validity of the taxes remain undecided in this action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous on adequacy and nonreach; eight Associate joins in the separate declaratory application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arkansas §§26-18-507 and 26-18-406 remain separate procedures with distinct conditions; no deadline is supplied. Section 2077's instrumentality designation and obligation-specific tax privileges do not confer a sovereign forum, and their substantive application remains undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for dismissal of the federal injunction and declaration action, removing the federal merits judgment. No refund, mandatory joinder, new factfinding or disposition of the parallel state action is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Private Truck Council's actual-adequacy limit and Reich's protection against withdrawal of an apparent postpayment remedy survive; neither defect is disputed here.
