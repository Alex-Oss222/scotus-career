### Artuz v. Bennett — 2000-11-07

Ginsburg's unanimous opinion affirms: under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a state collateral application is properly filed when delivery and acceptance comply with the rules governing filing the application. Claim-specific mandatory relief bars do not defeat otherwise proper filing. New York Criminal Procedure Law §440.10(2)(a) requires denial after merits appellate adjudication unless a subsequent retroactively effective change in controlling law occurred; paragraph (c) requires record facts sufficient for adequate appellate review and omitted review caused by an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or raise the issue on a perfected appeal. Both govern claim relief, not delivery and acceptance. Neither provision is found actually applicable to a particular Bennett claim.

Clerk acceptance does not cure a genuine filing defect. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, fees and filing preconditions remain distinct; the Court does not classify every rule without its terms. Whether deadline exceptions can preserve proper filing of a late application remains open. The Court fixes no pending period or tolling duration and makes no finding about receipt or absence of the written order; absence was an assumption below based on Bennett's allegation. Transitional timing, exhaustion, default and habeas entitlement remain outside the grant. Lindh's provision-specific temporal rule survives; it neither exempts this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA nor answers proper filing. The one-year framework and other habeas defenses remain effective; the remand awards no writ or release.

### Fiore v. White — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The existing missing element defeats both challenged convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not sustain a conviction when its authoritative account of the law in force at the relevant time identifies a necessary element and the State concedes it produced no evidence of that element. Pennsylvania's clarification that actual absence of the required permit was always an element of the owner/operator offense therefore establishes a due-process violation in both of Fiore's challenged no-permit convictions; deviation from a permit's terms cannot substitute for its absence.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both holdings and the bounded reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** In re Winship requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every element. Jackson v. Virginia enforces that protection against a conviction unsupported on a necessary element. State law identifies the offense; the federal Constitution requires its elements to be proved. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's August 21, 2000 answer establishes that Commonwealth v. Scarpone clarified the statute's existing meaning, including its meaning when Fiore's convictions became final. The Commonwealth does not dispute that Fiore possessed a permit. The asserted departure from its conditions thus cannot establish the different no-permit element.

The Third Circuit's premise that a new state rule was unavailable on collateral review cannot survive the authoritative answer. The Court decides no general obligation to apply genuinely new state constructions retroactively: there is no new construction here. Nor can the seriousness of other environmental misconduct supply the missing element. The distinct authorization, transportation-license and permit-compliance provisions do not become substitute offenses in this dispute. The ruling covers the no-permit conviction from each trial, not the other convictions among Fiore's sixty convictions.

#### II. The constitutional ruling and the conditions for the writ remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's rejection of the constitutional claim is reversed and the case remanded to resolve any properly preserved, actually undecided statutory prerequisite to habeas relief under the applicable post-April 24, 1996 regime. If those prerequisites permit relief, the habeas order must remove both invalid no-permit convictions and end custody attributable to them, preserving independent convictions and lawful custody.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion directly adopts this remand and the following statutory conditions; the Court makes no finding that an unresolved gate is satisfied or violated.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh v. Murphy distinguishes applications filed before enactment from later applications; Fiore's later filing is not exempt from the applicable amendments. Under section 2254(b), relief requires exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Merits denial despite nonexhaustion is permitted, not required; the State waives exhaustion only through counsel's express waiver. The appellate court expressly left exhaustion and state merits adjudication undecided.

If the relevant claim was actually adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, section 2254(d) requires the statutory inquiry. Williams v. Taylor preserves the independent contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses, the objective legal standard and the restriction to this Court's holdings at the actual state merits decision. Winship and Jackson are preexisting rules, not rules originating in today's decision. The separate statutory factual-review provisions retain their own triggers and force. A finding of constitutional error cannot silently resolve those questions; conversely, remand supplies no permission to reopen the conceded permit fact, the state court's authoritative answer or the decided constitutional issue. The court below must resolve a real preserved prerequisite and implement the resulting conviction-specific relief, rather than infer an unconditional all-count writ.

**Precedent treatment:**

- In re Winship: applies its proof-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt requirement to an existing state-law element; the seriousness of other conduct cannot supply the omitted proof.
- Jackson v. Virginia: applies its protection against conviction without evidence sufficient to establish each necessary element.
- Fiore v. White, November 30, 1999: completes the reserved federal inquiry after the authoritative state answer; the earlier certification itself supplied neither state-law meaning nor a federal merits judgment.
- Lindh v. Murphy: preserves its filing-date distinction; this post-enactment application falls outside its pre-enactment exemption.
- Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams): applies its independent statutory clauses and objective, date-bounded Supreme Court-law inquiry if an actual state merits adjudication triggers section 2254(d); no exclusive allocation between the clauses is created.

### Seling v. Young — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. Civil designation does not foreclose proof of actual punishment

**Holding and operative rule:** A court first identifies whether the legislature intended a civil or criminal commitment scheme; civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that the challenged commitment's purpose or effect is punitive. Actual design and operation must be assessed cumulatively, including contrary civil evidence: a civil label is not conclusive, but confinement, connection to prior crime, indeterminate duration, criminal-style safeguards, deficient treatment or an individual treatment failure alone establishes neither punishment nor a right to release.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt both holdings, the specified limits and the scoped affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Hendricks permits proof of punitive operation in a nominally civil commitment and invalidated the demonstrated retrospective application there. Hudson preserves design-and-operation review and supplies no universal rule confining the inquiry to statutory wording. The Court must therefore distinguish an inadequately administered but genuinely civil program from custody that actually imposes punishment.

The superintendent's concern that day-to-day conditions should not transform a scheme's classification supports a demanding burden and careful remedial distinctions. It cannot make proved punitive operation legally irrelevant. Clinical testimony, treatment records and plans, staff and patient accounts, confinement practices, program design and other reliable direct or circumstantial evidence can bear on the cumulative question. No statistical study or successful cure is required, and officials cannot defeat strong combined proof merely by isolating every circumstance. Conversely, employee misconduct or poor care alone does not establish that the custody is punishment.

Young's allegations concerning security, confinement, abuse, staffing, therapy and practical release remain allegations. The separate Turay injunction and reported progress are evidence within their actual scope, not conclusive proof of punishment, compliance or cure of an earlier unlawful application. Statutory examination, review and release mechanisms are relevant civil evidence, but their existence does not prove their operation for Young. The Court neither finds punishment nor rewrites those mechanisms. The supported claim requires adjudication under the governing burden, rather than categorical foreclosure by the civil label.

#### II. The existing punishment remand stands, with distinct constitutional and remedial predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's reviewed punishment-remand portion is affirmed, preserving the opportunity to establish Young's supported claim under the former chapter 153 habeas framework applicable to his 1994 application. If the clearest proof establishes punishment and the challenged custody retroactively adds it for conduct predating enactment, custody under that unlawful application must end through appropriate habeas relief, while independent lawful custody is preserved; a conditions violation that leaves custody lawfully civil instead requires relief through its proper conditions channel.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion adopts the remand, temporal rule and remedy distinction. The separate due-process, equal-protection and commitment-procedure rulings Young did not seek to review remain undisturbed.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh's application-date rule leaves this pre-April 24, 1996 application under former chapter 153 law; amended section 2254(d) does not govern its merits. Separately directed provisions and ordinary habeas requirements retain their operation. The Ninth Circuit already allowed an opportunity to prove punitive operation by the clearest proof. That disposition is proper under Hendricks and Hudson, so correcting the account of governing precedent requires instructions rather than vacatur of the opportunity itself. The remand creates no universal entitlement to an evidentiary hearing and no present writ or release order.

Punishment and retrospectivity require separate findings. The existence of a conditions remedy cannot defeat a proved unlawful-custody claim; conversely, conditions defects do not automatically require release. The court must consider the supported record and material genuine factual disputes under the former habeas rules. No replacement confinement authority is supplied.

Double Jeopardy remains distinct. A punitive character alone proves neither the relevant prior jeopardy nor that the challenged confinement is a second punishment for the same offense. Those matters require separate determination under the governing attachment and offense-identity law if the preserved claim remains necessary after the punishment and retrospective-custody findings. Hendricks did not decide them. The Court establishes no independent double-jeopardy entitlement here and need not add a second ground if Ex Post Facto relief fully resolves the custody claim. The remaining punishment, chronology, same-offense and custody determinations are adjudicative work under the stated rules, not permission to treat a civil label or shared past misconduct as conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kansas v. Hendricks: applies its rebuttable civil-intent and clearest-proof framework, cumulative operational inquiry and retrospective-punishment consequence; its particular Kansas facts are not transferred to Young.
- Hudson v. United States: applies its preserved design-and-operation inquiry; it did not replace actual-operation review with a statutory-face-only rule or overrule Halper in its personal-monetary-penalty field.
- United States v. Ursery: preserves the demanding rebuttal of civil classification without transferring its forfeiture outcome to personal confinement.
- United States v. Ward and Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez: preserve the intent-and-effect inquiry and cumulative use of punitive indicators, without a conclusive single factor or new exhaustive checklist.
- Lindh v. Murphy: applies the pre-enactment application rule, preserving separately directed provisions rather than exempting every habeas issue from every amendment.

### Buckman Co. v. Plaintiffs' Legal Committee — 2001-02-21

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Buckman_Co_v_Plaintiffs_Legal_Committee_merits_2001-02-21.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Buckman_Co_v_Plaintiffs_Legal_Committee_merits_2001-02-21.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Agency-fraud claims cannot privately supply the absent federal predicate

**Holding and operative rule:** A state claim existing solely by virtue of alleged fraud in the FDA clearance process is preempted where, as here, no authoritative agency fraud determination and resulting agency-action predicate exists and the claim requires private adjudicators to supply the fraud finding and hypothetical clearance denial. This federal-dependent claim displaces Congress's government-enforcement allocation; the rule does not decide a materially different claim supported by authoritative agency findings and action or one enforcing a genuinely independent traditional state duty.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this confined rule and reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 337(a) assigns enforcement of the federal regulatory relationship to the United States, subject to the distinct State-food exception. These claims allege a wrong to the FDA and require juries to determine that the agency was defrauded and would otherwise have denied clearance. No authoritative FDA fraud finding or withdrawal supplies that predicate. Private reconstruction would therefore perform the regulatory judgment that the enacted arrangement assigns to government. The absence of agency findings and action is central to this conflict; the Court does not declare those predicates immaterial in a different case.

Medtronic preserves independent traditional duties that genuinely require the same conduct as federal law. It does not create private enforcement of the FDCA. Calling the present theory fraud or compensation for injury does not make its asserted regulator-directed duty independent. Geier permits an independently established conflict while rejecting overlap and federal compliance as substitutes. Section 337 supplies the enacted allocation here; agency convenience, predicted information overload, or concern that useful treatment might be discouraged does not decide the case.

Substantial-equivalence clearance is not a determination of substantive safety. Truthful submissions remain required, and lawful off-label treatment does not authorize deception. Independent negligence or warning claims are not displaced merely because federal evidence is relevant. Nor does the Court find the allegations proved or reject ordinary causation on its merits. The different agency-established-predicate case lies outside this record; the presented private-substitution theory is resolved completely.

**Statutory limits:** Section 337(b) allows a State, in its own name and jurisdiction, to civilly enforce or restrain violations of sections 341 and 343(b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (k), (q) and (r) concerning food in that State. Proceedings must wait 30 days after notice to the Secretary, or 90 days if the Secretary begins informal or formal enforcement concerning that food within the first 30 days. They are barred while the Secretary diligently prosecutes the pertinent court proceeding or after settlement of that proceeding or the related informal or formal enforcement action. The State may intervene as of right in the specified court proceeding. This exception provides no private device action.

The Court does not decide section 360k express preemption as an additional ground, invalidate the clearance, adjudicate every off-label use or dismiss every independent product claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Medtronic v. Lohr, June 26, 1996: preserves actual comparison, the clearance/approval distinction and genuinely parallel independent duties; distinguishes the present federal-derived regulator duty from the patient-protection duties there.
- Geier, May 22, 2000: applies independent actual-conflict analysis through an identified enacted allocation, without creating compliance immunity or relying on regulatory inconvenience.
- United States v. Locke, March 6, 2000: preserves its subject-specific statutory allocation; its vessel-law field does not establish a medical-device field immunity.

### Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. — 2001-02-27

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Semtek_International_v_Lockheed_Martin_merits_2001-02-27.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Semtek_International_v_Lockheed_Martin_merits_2001-02-27.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Federal law ordinarily adopts rendering-state preclusion for diversity judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal common law governs the claim-preclusive effect of a federal diversity judgment on state-law claims and ordinarily incorporates the preclusion law applied by the courts of the State in which the rendering federal court sits, except when that law conflicts with a specifically demonstrated federal interest. No such conflict exists in this California limitations dismissal, so Maryland must apply California's claim-preclusion law rather than an automatic nationwide rule.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both holdings and the stated remand.

**Controlling explanation:** A federal judicial act requires a federal rule governing its effect. That source does not require a uniform rule extinguishing the underlying state claim everywhere. Erie and Hanna explain why removal into diversity should not change substantive consequences in a way that encourages forum selection and unequal treatment. Incorporating the rendering State's preclusion law avoids giving the same limitations judgment greater reach solely because the defendant removed the action.

O'Melveny and Atherton reject general uniformity as sufficient reason to displace state-created rights while preserving exceptional federal fields. The effect of a federal judgment supplies the federal field here; a particular conflict remains necessary to justify different substantive content. A state's rule might conflict with a federal interest in protecting federal judicial processes, but this judgment rests only on the State's limitations period. Lockheed's general appeal to finality does not establish such a conflict. The final judgment retains its lawful effect without receiving every additional consequence Lockheed requests.

Section 1738 and the Full Faith and Credit Clause concern state judgments and do not directly dictate the effect of this federal judgment. Baker illustrates the distinction between lawful adjudicative effect and an additional forum consequence, rather than supplying a direct statutory command here. California's actual preclusion rule was not determined below and remains for application on remand; the Court does not promise a second opportunity to litigate the tort merits. Federal-question judgments and materially different process-sanction dismissals fall outside the presented diversity limitations dispute.

#### II. Rule 41(b)'s merits designation does not supply universal claim preclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 41(b)'s default merits operation governs the dismissal's rendering-court effect and does not itself dictate the claim-preclusive consequences in every other forum. The default yields when the dismissal order specifies otherwise and excludes dismissals for lack of jurisdiction, improper venue or failure to join a party under Rule 19; a merits designation under the default does not replace the federal rule stated in Holding I.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly adopts this interpretation and rejects Maryland's contrary automatic-preclusion rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 41(b) allows dismissal for failure to prosecute or comply with the Rules or a court order and describes the default operation of covered dismissals and dismissals not otherwise provided for. Its language need not decide every later court's treatment to perform that procedural work. Rule 41(a)'s distinction between dismissals with and without prejudice reinforces the rendering-court function; neither subdivision purports to supply a complete national preclusion code for state claims.

Reading a procedural merits designation as worldwide extinction of the state claim would also create a serious Rules Enabling Act problem: section 2072 forbids procedural rules from abridging, enlarging or modifying substantive rights. The narrower reading gives the Rule effective work without that unsupported consequence. The label therefore does not establish that Maryland must dismiss regardless of California law.

The California judgment is valid and final, and this external-effect decision grants no permission to return to that court. The separate Rules Enabling Act question whether a federal court may bar refiling in circumstances where a state court would dismiss without prejudice is not decided; a direct challenge to that rendering-court consequence is outside this review. The rule governing other forums is resolved without adjudicating that distinct issue.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins: applies the allocation protecting state-created rights from a removal-driven change in substantive consequences.
- Hanna v. Plumer: applies the concerns with forum selection and unequal administration while respecting the actual scope of the federal procedural rule.
- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: preserves exceptional federal fields but rejects general uniformity as sufficient reason for displacement; the federal judgment supplies the source here.
- Atherton v. FDIC: applies the need for an identified federal conflict before substituting uniform substantive content.
- Baker v. General Motors Corp.: distinguishes lawful judgment effect from additional forum consequences; its section 1738 holding concerns state judgments and is not extended as a direct command for federal judgments.

### Egelhoff v. Egelhoff — 2001-03-21

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Egelhoff_v_Egelhoff_merits_2001-03-21.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Egelhoff_v_Egelhoff_merits_2001-03-21.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Automatic substitution of the beneficiary is preempted for both benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** ERISA section 514 displaces this state divorce-revocation rule to the extent it automatically substitutes the recipient of covered benefits for the plan's operative beneficiary designation. That rule defeats both identified revocation-based claims, including recovery of the already paid insurance from its recipient; the payment does not create a legally independent source of title, and pension anti-alienation is not the ground for the insurance holding.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Seven Justices directly adopt the rule, its application to both benefit demands, its limits and the confined reversal/remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that choosing the beneficiary is a central act of plan administration. Section 514, read with the plan-document duties, prevents the State from replacing the operative designation through this automatic divorce rule. Its application to nonplan property does not remove the instruction it gives when applied to covered benefits. An opt-out still requires affirmative plan drafting and attention to differing state rules to preserve the otherwise operative designation.

The children's strongest answer is that the statute supplies background validity law where the plans leave a gap. The investment plan's invalid-designation fallback makes that question real, but does not authorize every state rule declaring a designation invalid. The actual documents establish no continuing-marriage condition or adoption of this revocation rule. Insurance-plan silence likewise does not establish such adoption. The challenged rule changes entitlement because of a subsequent divorce, rather than identifying a demonstrated defect in execution, capacity or expression of the designation.

Travelers, Dillingham and De Buono distinguish ordinary costs and background effects from direct benefit or administrative commands. UNUM preserves that distinction even when an administrative reassignment uses ordinary-law terminology. The statute's defeasibility, notice protections and permission to withhold payment limit liability but do not eliminate its substitute-beneficiary operation. The result rests on that direct conflict, not disfavor toward family law or conjectured administrative inconvenience.

The insurance payment changes possession, not the legal source of the children's asserted entitlement. Their claim still depends on statutory revocation rather than an independent later promise or transaction. Boggs directs attention to that source, but its pension anti-alienation rule cannot be transferred to welfare insurance. Section 514 independently supplies the present insurance result. Unknown pension-payment status therefore does not prevent resolution of either legal claim.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The decision preempts these revocation-based demands, not every state rule governing capacity, execution, interpretation, marriage, inheritance or distributed money. Unlawful-killing disqualifications, simultaneous death, actual fraud, a valid domestic-relations order and independently arising postdistribution obligations lie outside the presented claim. No alternative beneficiary, actual redesignation, applicable exception, federal survivor entitlement, notice liability, benefit waiver or award is found. Ordinary implementation must follow the actual documents and governing federal protections, with properly preserved independent grounds retained.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Travelers; Dillingham; De Buono:** Their distinction between ordinary background or economic effects and direct benefit or administrative commands is applied, not displaced. General applicability is no automatic exemption, and family-law subject matter is no automatic ground for preemption.
- **UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward:** Its concrete administrative-connection rule is applied to beneficiary substitution. Ordinary interpretive principles are not categorically preempted, just as UNUM did not establish a universal federal agency code.
- **Boggs v. Boggs:** Its attention to the asserted source of benefit entitlement is applied; its pension and survivor holdings retain their actual scope. The present paid-insurance conclusion rests independently on section 514 and does not extend pension anti-alienation to welfare insurance.
- **Mackey v. Lanier Collection Agency & Service, Inc.:** The distinction between pension restrictions and welfare-benefit treatment remains effective. Ordinary recipient obligations are not categorically displaced merely because property once came from a plan.

**Resulting law:** Automatic divorce revocation cannot substitute the beneficiary of these covered pension or life-insurance benefits, including through a recipient recovery resting on that same statutory source. The decision adds this concrete application of section 514 without expanding remote-effects preemption or insulating all distributed assets. The actual plan documents, applicable federal protections and genuinely independent preserved grounds govern implementation; payment status does not change the source of the claims decided here.

### Daniels v. United States — 2001-04-25

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Daniels_v_United_States_merits_2001-04-25.md#holdings).

#### Available original review does not become a renewed section 2255 forum

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2255 does not permit a prisoner to reopen an expired counseled state conviction for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims merely because that conviction enhances a current federal sentence, where meaningful original review was available and the prisoner identifies no external impediment to using it. A genuinely available earlier opportunity lost through unexcused failure is not an unavailable remedy; the Court does not declare the old conviction valid or decide the different case of faultless loss of meaningful original review.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five directly join this available-opportunity rule, its finality explanation, application and affirmance. Each Justice's separate position beyond it remains distinct.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the current federal sentence and the expired predicate have different lawful review histories. Custis limits constitutional attacks at ACCA sentencing, but expressly leaves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases open. Extending its channel distinction into section 2255 requires today's statutory holding; it is not an application of an already decided universal bar.

Section 2255 expressly permits correction of an unlawful current federal sentence. That text does not erase the original judgment's review structure or make every later enhancement a renewed opportunity to litigate constitutional objections that could have been presented in the proper forum. Finality, the original jurisdiction's responsibility and the difficulty of reconstructing old proceedings support enforcing that distinction when a genuine earlier opportunity existed. Administrative inconvenience alone does not establish that every alleged unlawful sentence must escape review.

Daniels's stronger incentive to contest the predicates after committing the federal offense does not establish that the earlier forum was unavailable. His counsel identifies neither a prior attempt to pursue these constitutional challenges while in custody nor an external obstacle preventing one. The Court does not infer deliberate personal choice from every attorney failure; it decides the opportunity and allegations actually presented. Parke preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful proof rules, not an irrebuttable finding that every final plea was valid.

Maleng distinguishes expired independent custody from collateral consequences. Garlotte concerns a continuous unexpired consecutive series and does not convert these wholly expired state sentences into present custody under those judgments. Daniels's current federal custody supplies the section 2255 vehicle, but not an automatic right to relitigate each predicate. His involuntary counseled pleas and deficient-representation allegations therefore cannot proceed through this motion on the review history presented.

#### Complete unconstitutional counsel deprivation remains distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A predicate obtained through unconstitutional complete denial of constitutionally required counsel, without valid waiver, remains subject to challenge through section 2255 under applicable procedural requirements and upon a showing of its adverse effect on the current sentence. Deficient representation and an involuntary counseled plea are not that exception; ordinary procedural-default requirements remain, generally including presentation of the counsel-deprivation claim at federal sentencing or a legally sufficient excuse.

**Authority:** The same five Justices directly join the preservation of this exception, its procedural boundaries and its inapplicability to Daniels's allegations.

**Controlling explanation:** Gideon establishes the entitlement to appointed counsel when constitutionally required. Burgett and Tucker preserve the special restriction on subsequent reliance on a conviction obtained through unconstitutional complete deprivation of that protection. Custis retains this distinct exception while refusing to turn every alleged defect in counseled proceedings into an ACCA sentencing issue.

The difference concerns the kind of constitutional deprivation, not a judgment that ineffective assistance or involuntary pleas are unimportant. Daniels alleges defective counseled proceedings; he identifies no complete unconstitutional deprivation. The exception therefore supplies no route for his present motion. Nor does recognition of a qualifying claim dispense with applicable procedure, proof of invalidity or the connection between the predicate and the current enhancement. No such qualifying defect or adverse-effect determination is made here.

#### The absent faultless-review issue has no controlling resolution

**Scope and authority:** No Court holding resolves whether section 2255 permits review when, through no fault of the prisoner, meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated. O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas leave that absent situation open. Stone-Zsela affirmatively adopts a faultless-loss exception in his separate access rule. Scalia joins the actual available-review holding but supplies an additional statutory objection to section 2255 as the forum for an outstanding state conviction's non-Gideon defect. A statement about the Court's limited holding does not attribute personal reservation or support for an exception to either concurrence.

Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg advocate a broader express-section-2255 route, while Breyer relies on a distinct proposal to reconsider the original sentencing restriction. Those positions do not establish five direct joins to a common faultless-loss exception, and no Marks synthesis makes one controlling. Daniels alleges no external impediment placing that issue before the Court.

#### Remedy and other boundaries

The Ninth Circuit judgment denying this section 2255 motion is affirmed. The 176-month federal sentence remains operative against these claims; the Court orders no new sentencing hearing, state-predicate invalidation, evidentiary hearing or sentence reduction. The constitutional validity of the 1978 and 1981 pleas is not reached because the claims fail the governing access rule.

Statutory classification, counting and exclusions concerning a predicate are distinct from the constitutional attack decided here. No such objection is newly adjudicated or revived. A predicate independently invalidated in proper proceedings presents a different application question; any later request must identify its authorized vehicle, applicable prerequisites, effect on the remaining qualifying predicates and available relief, without guaranteed reopening. No current sentencing-fact jury claim is presented. Coss uses a different state-prisoner vehicle and receives its own decision; no companion result supplies a premise here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Custis v. United States, May 23, 1994:** Its ACCA sentencing-forum distinction and complete-counsel exception are extended to the section 2255 available-review circumstances decided here. Its expired-custody reservation was not already resolved, and the absent faultless-review question receives no common answer today. Custis itself is not overruled.
- **Gideon v. Wainwright:** Its constitutional appointment entitlement and valid-waiver boundary supply the preserved complete-deprivation exception.
- **Burgett v. Texas; United States v. Tucker:** Their special treatment of convictions obtained through unconstitutional complete denial of counsel remains; they do not make these deficient-representation allegations complete deprivation.
- **Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992:** Its rebuttable regularity and lawful collateral proof rules are preserved. It establishes neither universal constitutional validity nor an additional review forum.
- **Maleng v. Cook:** Its distinction between an expired independent sentence and present custody survives. An enhancement consequence does not itself supply custody under the expired judgment.
- **Garlotte v. Fordice, May 30, 1995:** Its continuous-consecutive-series custody holding remains, distinguished from these independently expired state sentences. Custody, procedural access and merits remain separate.
- **Nichols v. United States, June 6, 1994:** Its limited permission for a valid fine-only conviction's particular Guidelines use remains. It does not decide a forum for constitutional attacks on allegedly invalid predicates.
- **Fiore v. White, January 9, 2001:** Its established missing-element violation and distinct statutory habeas prerequisites remain. It creates no universal forum for expired-predicate review.
- **Glover v. United States, January 9, 2001:** Its rejection of minimum-duration and offense-level prejudice thresholds remains. A cognizable sentencing injury does not itself supply this missing review route.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States, March 24, 1998:** Its aggravated-reentry element holding and retained collateral-review distinction remain; no new indictment or jury claim is supplied in this case.

**Resulting law:** The section 2255 channel does not reopen expired counseled predicates for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims when meaningful original review was available and no external impediment is alleged. Complete unconstitutional deprivation of required counsel remains distinct, with ordinary procedural and causal requirements. The governing rule concerns access, not a conclusive presumption that all final convictions are valid.

No controlling rule adopts or rejects a section 2255 exception for faultless loss of meaningful original review. Stone's affirmative exception, Scalia's additional statutory objection and the dissenters' different routes retain their stated noncontrolling status. Independently invalidated predicates, actual statutory classification and other authorized applications retain their own prerequisites and remedies. Custis remains effective at its original sentencing scope, extended only as stated here.

### Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss — 2001-04-25

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Lackawanna_County_District_Attorney_v_Coss_merits_2001-04-25.md#holdings).

#### Binding judgment without a controlling access rationale

**Judgment and operative mandate:** The Third Circuit judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded for the Court of Appeals to determine whether any pursued objection that the 1986 convictions did not increase the sentence selected within the range may be heard or was waived and, if it may be heard, to apply proper appellate review; and, only if an identified requirement of former 28 U.S.C. §2254(b) is shown to remain actually unresolved, to resolve that requirement on the materials bearing on it. The existing deficient-performance, trial-prejudice and consideration determinations keep their lawful scope unless lawfully displaced. The judgment directs no denial on a newly assumed absence of sentencing effect, no automatic release and no freestanding vacation of an expired judgment.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the exact judgment. Breyer's opinion announcing it is joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; its reasons have four joins. Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment. The mandate binds the parties, but the fifth judgment vote does not establish a controlling procedural rationale or access rule.

**Explanation of the four-Justice opinion:** The petition challenges Coss's current imprisonment, not custody under the independently completed 1986 sentences. Maleng permits that construction while leaving the extent of indirect review distinct. Custis limits the ACCA sentencing forum and leaves the materially different absence of meaningful earlier review undecided. Neither decision makes a mention of prior convictions conclusive proof of increased imprisonment or makes an unchanged numerical range conclusive proof of no increase.

The existing record presents two causal questions: the effect on the range and the effect on the sentence chosen within it. The challenged misdemeanors were removed from the prior-record score, but the lower courts found consideration in selecting the sentence. The published account of the lower briefing distinguishes an argument about the range from an objection to the within-range effect. Proper treatment of an actually pursued objection therefore requires a determination whether it may be heard and, if so, appellate review of the actual findings and inferences. The Court does not itself decide waiver, effect or a contrary factual finding.

Former section 2254(b) requires exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, or circumstances rendering that process ineffective to protect the prisoner's rights. The petition's September 1994 filing places it under the former framework. That law does not justify treating an unidentified prerequisite as outstanding. Any inquiry under the mandate must first identify a requirement actually left unresolved and use the materials bearing on it. The old petition's unexplained pendency does not itself establish prisoner fault, faultlessness or satisfaction of every procedural condition. Existing performance, trial-prejudice and consideration determinations are not erased by the return.

#### Fragmentation and remaining work

**No controlling access rule under Marks:** Stone-Zsela's affirmative no-fault access rule, the broader current-sentence route supported by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, and Breyer's refusal to decide an additional constitutional exception are different legal positions. Stone-Zsela and the three broader-route Justices supply only four affirmative access positions even if his proposed category is treated as a subset of theirs. Breyer supplies no affirmative exception to complete that coalition. The lead's procedural explanation has four joins, and Stone-Zsela's judgment-only vote supplies no shared rationale. Marks cannot turn the five votes for this mandate into five votes for an additional review exception, an exclusive faultlessness condition or a general indirect-review bar.

The Court makes no present finding of responsibility for the failed original review, waiver, abandonment, constitutional validity beyond the existing determinations' lawful scope, or adverse sentencing effect. The preservation question and any properly available causal review are work under the mandate; a former section 2254(b) inquiry arises only upon the stated actual-unresolved-requirement condition. No unconditional responsibility inquiry or new evidentiary hearing is ordered. The broader access question lacks a majority rationale. A newly discovered actual-innocence claim is outside the claim presented. No automatic resentencing, retrial, fixed reduction or release follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Maleng v. Cook, 490 U.S. 488 (1989):** Its distinction between a challenge to current custody and a freestanding attack on an independently expired sentence remains. The accepted current-sentence construction does not itself settle indirect review's reach.
- **Custis v. United States, May 23, 1994:** Its ACCA sentencing-channel restriction and qualifying complete-denial-of-counsel exception remain at their existing scope. No majority here extends that restriction into a general section 2254 bar or resolves the reserved absence-of-meaningful-review category.
- **Garlotte v. Fordice, May 30, 1995:** Its continuous, unexpired consecutive-sentence rule remains distinct from these independently completed sentences; it does not supply direct custody over them.
- **Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984):** Its distinct deficient-performance and trial-prejudice inquiries remain. The existing lower determinations retain their lawful scope rather than being discarded by the remand.
- **United States v. Tucker, 404 U.S. 443 (1972), and Glover v. United States, January 9, 2001:** Their requirements concerning unconstitutional sentencing inputs and actual causal prejudice remain. Glover excludes an added minimum-duration or offense-level threshold; it does not establish that these convictions increased this sentence.
- **Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992:** Its collateral regularity and lawful proof rules remain; they establish neither that this pending challenge received a decision nor that every old conviction is constitutionally valid.
- **Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997, and Fiore v. White, January 9, 2001:** Lindh's filing-date distinction preserves the former framework here. Fiore's separation of constitutional merits from actually outstanding habeas conditions remains useful at that scope; its later-filed petition's statutory rules are not transferred wholesale to this case.
- **Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 (1977):** The judgment does not yield a controlling access rationale by combining an affirmative rule with a Justice's refusal to decide it. No precedent is overruled.

**Resulting law:** The exact vacatur and remand govern this case. No new general rule authorizes or forbids indirect review of expired convictions through section 2254, and no additional exception or exclusive no-fault gateway acquires controlling force. Existing custody, collateral-review and former habeas requirements retain their own operation. The Court decides neither that consideration necessarily increased punishment nor that removal of prior-record points necessarily eliminated within-range influence. Existing determinations remain within their lawful scope unless lawfully displaced, and the conditional appellate work cannot be enlarged into an automatic obligation to relitigate every predicate.

### Randell v. Johnson — 2001-04-25

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Randell_v_Johnson_merits_2001-04-25.md#holdings).

#### I. The precise damages claim must necessarily contradict an operative determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Favorable invalidation is not required for a section 1983 claim whose success is compatible with the valid sentence or custody determination, including a claim that officials executed a valid sentence contrary to its terms. Before imposing the prerequisite, the court must identify the operative judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and determine whether success on the actual civil theory necessarily establishes its invalidity; a sentence-administration label neither establishes nor avoids that consequence.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by all eight Associate Justices. All nine directly adopt this classification rule, rejection of the categorical screening ground, and vacatur and remand for the necessary comparison.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the legal consequence of the claim matters more than its label or its connection to custody. Heck distinguishes a necessarily conviction-negating claim from an independent injury compatible with the criminal judgment. Edwards applies that distinction to earned-credit proceedings: calling a claim procedural does not save it if success necessarily defeats an outstanding adjudication, but a genuinely independent injury requires its own analysis. Wolff preserves compatible civil relief, and Preiser's release channel does not turn every damages complaint concerning confinement into habeas.

Randell alleges that officials failed to credit parole-warrant confinement and required him to serve the same time again. That allegation could seek enforcement of a valid sentence, or it could necessarily contradict an operative credit determination. The screening record does not sufficiently identify the determination or establish that relationship. A court cannot assume every alleged excess day negates a judgment, nor assume every administrative-execution claim leaves the controlling custody determination untouched.

The lower court also treated Heck as already deciding a universal former-prisoner bar. The actual rule reserved that population and persons without realistic custody-review opportunity; Spencer preserved those reservations without adopting an affirmative civil exception. Existing precedent therefore did not supply the categorical ground used below. This error requires vacatur, not a finding that Randell was overconfined or already possesses a successful damages claim.

#### II. Genuine loss of any realistic custody-review opportunity permits the civil claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When a former prisoner's damages claim necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination, prior favorable invalidation is not required if the claimant never had a realistic lawful opportunity to contest that confinement before custody review became unavailable, or lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. A claimant who lost a genuinely usable opportunity through an unexcused failure remains subject to the prerequisite; release or present federal habeas unavailability alone is neither the exception nor qualifying invalidation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Five directly adopt this new bounded rule within Heck's reservation, its statutory explanation and its unexcused-loss limit. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule coordinating available statutory remedies cannot require a route the claimant never realistically could use. Heck's controlling statutory explanation reconciles section 1983 with a lawful custody-review channel. Preiser supports that coordination where the remedies intersect. Patsy rejects a general requirement to exhaust state procedures before seeking section 1983 relief. The common-law favorable-termination analogy helps implement the statutory arrangement; it does not create an absolute civil disability whenever confinement once occurred.

The defendants correctly object that release cannot reward strategic delay or authorize a person to bypass a real review opportunity. The rule therefore turns on realistic availability and the circumstances of its loss, not custody status alone. An unexcused failure to use a genuine opportunity leaves the prerequisite in place. The exception concerns meaningful first review, not an entitlement to repeat unsuccessful review, and expiration does not invalidate the underlying determination.

The inquiry must use supported facts about custody, notice, court access, timing and the ability to pursue the particular challenge. Neither absence of an earlier successful writ nor the theoretical existence of another procedure proves what opportunity actually existed. An authorized qualifying invalidation route may matter where the prerequisite applies, but requiring proof that every conceivable state process is unavailable would recreate the general exhaustion rule Patsy rejects.

Spencer did not already grant this exception; it decided the live controversy in a habeas case and expressly preserved the civil question. This decision resolves the legal consequences of genuine unavailability and unexcused loss. Randell's actual circumstances remain for ordinary adjudication under that rule, without an assumed finding of diligence, abandonment or deliberate delay.

#### III. The remand applies the rule without deciding overconfinement or damages

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit's screening affirmance is vacated and the case remanded to compare the pleaded credit theory with the actual custody determination and, if necessary invalidity is established, decide realistic review opportunity and legally excused loss from supported facts. A compatible claim or one within the no-realistic-review exception may proceed under ordinary civil requirements; an otherwise covered claim lacking qualifying invalidation must be dismissed without prejudice for lack of present cognizability, not as an adjudication that the alleged misconduct did not occur.

**Authority:** All nine support vacatur and the classification return in Part I. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer directly join Part III's complete conditional directions, including application of Part II's exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal habeas concerning the disputed confinement was unavailable when Randell filed on June 18, 1999. That fact does not resolve earlier opportunity, a previously adjudicated credit issue, a usable qualifying invalidation route or the legality of the confinement. The court must permit ordinary consideration of pertinent allegations and supported facts, without assuming an unexcused bypass or imposing general state-remedy exhaustion.

Where the invalidation prerequisite continues to apply, Heck recognizes reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal or a federal habeas writ calling the determination into question. Failed review and mere expiration do not qualify. The covered claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation, so the proper remedy for present noncognizability is dismissal without prejudice, not conversion to habeas, an exhaustion stay or an invented tolling regime. Accrual and limitations for a claim outside that prerequisite remain for their independently applicable law.

Section 1915(e)(2) still requires dismissal at any time, notwithstanding any fee paid, for an untrue poverty allegation or an action or appeal that is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim or seeks money from an immune defendant. Correcting the legal failure-to-state-a-claim ground does not decide any other actual screening ground. Nor does failure to satisfy a legal prerequisite establish factual frivolousness.

Individual responsibility, constitutional violation, causation, injury, immunity and properly applicable preclusion remain separate. The requested $1,000 per day establishes no measure or amount of damages. The Court orders no custody recalculation, release, state reopening, new evidentiary hearing, stay, appointed counsel or present award.

#### Scope of the decision

The governing no-realistic-opportunity standard is resolved for the former-prisoner category presented; its factual application is not postponed as an unresolved legal question. The Court adopts neither a categorical bar against all former prisoners nor an unconditional exception whenever federal habeas is presently unavailable. Ordinary proof, pleading, defenses and limitations retain their force. No finding establishes actual overconfinement, deliberate delay, a prior adverse credit adjudication or an adequate remaining state procedure.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Heck v. Humphrey, June 24, 1994:** Its necessary-invalidity comparison, qualifying invalidation routes, deferred accrual for covered claims and without-prejudice dismissal are applied. The Court now resolves part of its express reservation by adopting the bounded former-prisoner no-realistic-review exception and retaining the prerequisite after unexcused loss of a usable opportunity. Its original in-custody holding is not overruled.
- **Edwards v. Balisok, May 19, 1997:** Its legal-consequence analysis governs sentence-credit claims; procedural wording does not avoid necessary invalidity, and genuine independent injuries remain distinct. Its invalidation and accrual rules remain for claims still covered.
- **Spencer v. Kemna, March 3, 1998:** Its habeas mootness holding and preservation of the civil-remedy reservations remain. Its discussion did not itself create today's affirmative exception or a universal former-prisoner bar.
- **Preiser v. Rodriguez:** Its special channel for release from custody supplies the statutory coordination premise; this damages action is neither automatically converted into habeas nor denied solely because it concerns custody.
- **Patsy v. Board of Regents:** Its rejection of general section 1983 state-remedy exhaustion remains controlling. The realistic-opportunity inquiry cannot be converted into exhaustion of every conceivable state procedure.
- **Wolff v. McDonnell:** Its preservation of compatible civil relief informs the independent-claim boundary; factual overlap with custody does not itself require invalidation.
- **Carey v. Piphus:** Its common-law remedial guidance remains subordinate to the federal statutory remedy, and actual compensable injury remains necessary; no automatic per-day award follows.

**Resulting law:** A civil claim compatible with the valid custody determination does not require favorable invalidation. If success necessarily contradicts that determination, a former prisoner may proceed without invalidation when meaningful lawful custody review never realistically existed or was lost through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. Unexcused loss of an actually usable opportunity leaves the prerequisite in force; release and present federal habeas unavailability alone do not satisfy it.

Courts must identify the actual custody determination and review opportunity. This rule resolves the legal consequences of availability and unexcused loss, while leaving Randell's facts and ordinary civil requirements for adjudication. For a claim remaining subject to invalidation, Heck's qualifying routes, deferred accrual and without-prejudice dismissal continue. No general state-remedy exhaustion, automatic damages right or compulsory reopening follows.

### New Hampshire v. Maine — 2001-05-29

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/new_hampshire_v_maine_merits_2001-05-29.md#holdings).

#### Judicial estoppel protects an accepted adjudicative position

**Holding and operative rule.** A court may prevent a litigant from advancing a clearly inconsistent position after obtaining judicial acceptance of the earlier position when repudiation would confer an unfair advantage or impair the integrity of the earlier adjudication. Clear inconsistency, actual judicial acceptance, and resulting advantage or detriment guide this equitable inquiry without forming an exhaustive formula or inflexible prerequisites; a supported genuine mistake or material change in law, policy, or essential fact can affect its application.

**Authority.** The signed opinion by Kennedy, joined throughout by the other seven participating Justices, states the Court's rule and application. All eight directly adopt this holding; no narrower-ground aggregation is involved.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that judicial acceptance, rather than negotiation alone, gives the earlier position its force here. Davis v. Wakelee supplies the principle against obtaining relief on one position and later repudiating it for an inconsistent advantage. Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. requires attention to the actual contradiction and its explanation; different legal labels do not establish inconsistency by themselves. Here New Hampshire urged that the same 1740 boundary language meant the middle of the main navigable channel. The Court accepted that construction when rejecting the Special Master's geographical-middle approach and directing entry of the earlier consent decree. New Hampshire now uses the same language to claim the river to the Maine shore. The offshore and inland remedies differ, but those interpretations cannot both be the basis of the State's judicial demands.

The State's assertion of an insufficient earlier historical inquiry does not establish genuine mistake. The earlier materials examined historical and river-boundary authorities, and the new interpretation rests on materials available then. No intervening enactment or newly occurring essential fact explains the reversal. A State invoking this Court's adjudicative power is subject to the same protection of judicial integrity. That conclusion does not settle the effect of ordinary changes in government policy in another case. Dismissal bars this inconsistent claim without turning every compromise statement into a binding admission.

#### Dismissal does not enlarge the earlier boundary decree

**Holding and operative rule.** New Hampshire's present shore-boundary complaint is barred by judicial estoppel and is dismissed. The 1977 decree remains effective within its existing marine-boundary scope; this dismissal supplies no new inland survey, independent title adjudication, or affirmative geographic decree.

**Authority.** The same eight-Justice Court opinion directly adopts the limited disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that Maine's motion can be resolved from the accepted earlier judicial position. New Hampshire v. Maine, 426 U.S. 363, supplies the acceptance of the States' interpretation; the decree at 434 U.S. 1 supplies its enacted geographic terms. The fact that those terms did not survey every inland segment does not permit repudiation of the interpretation that obtained relief. Conversely, preventing that repudiation cannot itself draw coordinates the earlier decree did not establish. Maine's separate claim- and issue-preclusion theories are therefore unnecessary to decision. A materially different boundary theory would require its own pleadings and record; no such claim is resolved here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Davis v. Wakelee: applied for the received rule against successfully asserting inconsistent judicial positions; its protection of adjudicative integrity remains in force.
- Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp.: applied for examination of actual inconsistency and a supported explanation; it is neither displaced nor converted into an automatic estoppel rule.
- New Hampshire v. Maine, 426 U.S. 363, and the decree at 434 U.S. 1: given effect according to the accepted interpretation and the decree's marine-boundary scope; their geographic reach is not enlarged.

**Resulting law:** Judicial estoppel now expressly governs a State's repudiation of a clearly inconsistent interpretive premise accepted as a basis of this Court's relief, with contextual consideration of genuine mistake and material changed circumstances. The rule is distinct from claim and issue preclusion. The present complaint is dismissed; the earlier decree retains its existing scope, and no additional boundary line is established.

### Penry v. Johnson — 2001-06-04

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/penry_v_johnson_merits_2001-06-04.md#holdings).

#### Psychiatric claim: the statutory ground resolves this application

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2254(d)(1) does not permit relief on this psychiatric claim: the state court's distinction between a defense-requested earlier competency report used in cross-examining a defense mental-condition expert and the compelled, uncounseled capital examination in Estelle v. Smith is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of then-established federal law. The adverse psychiatric certificate disposition is affirmed without deciding a general direct-review admissibility or privilege-waiver rule.

**Authority.** Part I of the signed opinion by Souter is joined by all nine Justices. Its sufficient statutory ground controls; it makes no alternative finding about actual sentencing effect.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the statutory limitation must be applied to the actual examination setting. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses and an objective inquiry. Estelle v. Smith protects against the compelled capital examination in that case; Buchanan v. Kentucky distinguishes a defense-initiated evaluation and responsive use. The Peebles report originated in defense counsel's request for a competency examination in an unrelated 1977 prosecution. Dr. Price reviewed it among fourteen reports while testifying for Penry about mental condition. Cross-examination elicited its dangerousness statement over objection, and the prosecutor repeated the statement in closing. Those uses present a substantial privilege concern, but they do not eliminate the materially different origin and use that supported the state court's distinction.

The applicable constitutional-law cutoff is the state merits decision of February 22, 1995; rehearing was denied June 7, 1995, and no later merits adjudication changing that cutoff is established. A preferred broader exclusion rule on direct review cannot replace the statutory standard. The Court adopts no universal subject-matter waiver, no rule admitting every report whenever mental condition is discussed, and no adverse finding based on absence of an objection. The statutory ground suffices; no general direct-review privilege decision or psychiatric harmlessness determination is necessary.

#### The mitigation charge failed to provide an effective lawful verdict

**Holding and operative rule.** When supported capital mitigation can reduce culpability independently of the factual special issues, the jury must have an authorized, coherent way to give that judgment effect in a life verdict. An instruction allowing a negative answer for mitigation does not satisfy that requirement when the unchanged verdict form requires the jury to certify reasonable doubt about a factual proposition it may believe proved; on this complete charge and trial context there is a reasonable likelihood of unconstitutional restriction, and the state's acceptance was an objectively unreasonable application of the established rule.

**Authority.** Part II has seven direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas dissent from both the constitutional application and the unreasonable-application conclusion. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted on this component.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that admitting mitigation is insufficient when the verdict mechanism cannot lawfully express its effect. Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect relevant culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh requires an effective vehicle for the evidence at issue; Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas establish before the 1995 state adjudication that this vehicle cannot require a false factual answer. Boyde v. California requires a reasonable likelihood of restriction in the complete charge and trial, not a mere possible misunderstanding.

The jury could believe intellectual impairment and childhood abuse reduced culpability while still finding deliberateness, future dangerousness, and lack of reasonable provocation. The supplement permitted a negative answer if life fit that culpability judgment. But the attached form still required a negative answer to certify that at least ten jurors had reasonable doubt about the factual issue. An affirmative answer required unanimity beyond reasonable doubt. The conflict therefore concerned the lawful meaning of the signed answer, not simply awareness that life was possible.

Each eventual juror received a largely similar supplemental instruction and a judicial explanation during selection; counsel also explained its operation, and the defense renewed the explanation at penalty closing. Those circumstances favor the State and must be considered. They instructed jurors how to answer negatively without reconciling the final factual certification. The interval and intervening trial stages do not prove forgetting, and the prosecution's emphasis on oath, evidence, and law is not a separately imposed mitigation prohibition. The final packet nevertheless retained the conflict. Its acceptance exceeded the reasonable range under the pre-1995 effective-vehicle rule. Neither favored weight, a prescribed fourth question, nor a categorical disability exemption is required. The form's ten-juror language is considered as part of the conflict; no separate unanimity claim is decided.

#### A confined State-borne harmless-error rule governs the remaining effect inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the complete penalty record, that the constitutional defect did not contribute to the death sentence. If the State cannot make that showing, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; guilt remains unaffected.

**Authority.** Part III has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stevens and Kennedy join the corrective return but reject this effect standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation judgment and state a different effect position conditionally. This is a majority holding, not a synthesis of those separate positions under Marks.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court expressly extends Chapman's State-borne beyond-reasonable-doubt standard to this defined original-charge category. Calderon v. Coleman applied that allocation to commutation misinformation; it did not already settle every capital habeas error. Weeks v. Angelone left its response-error effect rule, burden, and uncertainty treatment unresolved. Neither holding had already classified the contradictory original-charge mechanism here. The present extension addresses reliable selection of death after an established denial of a lawful mitigation route and assigns uncertainty about the error's contribution to the State.

The State may rely on the entire penalty record, including testimony, instructions, and the clarifying explanations. The established error alone does not prove actual contribution or require automatic relief. Conversely, affirmative answers to the special issues do not establish what an unconflicted jury would have done with independent culpability mitigation. The reported excerpts do not permit a completed whole-record determination by this Court. The lower court must undertake that inquiry under the adopted standard.

The extension changes the present remedial rule for this bounded category; it does not become clearly established constitutional law at the 1995 section 2254(d)(1) cutoff, create a new retroactivity exception, or govern every habeas claim. The constitutional gateway has already been satisfied through the pre-1995 mitigation rule. No structural-error classification or presumption that the error actually contributed is adopted.

#### Certificate review, corrective return, and the limit on immediate relief

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court may review the Fifth Circuit's judicial denial of a certificate of appealability under section 1254(1). The established mitigation violation and unreasonable application require reversal of that component of the certificate disposition and an issue-specific appellate return; they do not themselves constitute a present conditional writ or immediate sentence vacatur before the remaining effect inquiry.

**Authority.** Part I's reviewability framework has nine joins. Part II's mitigation certificate eligibility and corrective return have the seven direct joins identified above. Part III supplies the five-Justice standard binding the effect inquiry on remand.

**Controlling explanation.** Hohn v. United States permits review of the judicial certificate denial. Slack v. McDaniel keeps the substantial-showing threshold distinct from ultimate entitlement to relief and requires issue-specific treatment. The psychiatric statutory disposition remains adverse. The mitigation claim satisfies certification and the constitutional and section 2254(d)(1) questions are resolved in Penry's favor. Those conclusions bind the return; further proceedings are not an invitation to relitigate the decided gateway. But the certificate is not itself a writ, and the record does not establish actual harmlessness or harmfulness. The Court therefore returns the matter for the issue-specific appeal and full-record effect review. Conditional sentence correction follows if the State fails the adopted standard; immediate resentencing, release, or a fixed deadline is not ordered now.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Estelle v. Smith: distinguished on the defense-requested origin and responsive use of this earlier report; its compelled-examination protection remains intact.
- Buchanan v. Kentucky: applied for the defense-initiated evaluation and rebuttal distinction, without a universal waiver or admissibility rule.
- Williams v. Taylor: applied for separate statutory clauses, objective reasonableness, and the relevant state-merits cutoff; no exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority is adopted.
- Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma: applied for consideration and effect of relevant culpability mitigation.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applied to require a workable mitigation vehicle for evidence extending beyond the factual special issues.
- Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas: applied as pre-1995 controlling authority requiring independent culpability effect through a lawful answer; both remain binding within their stated effective-vehicle scope.
- Boyde v. California: applied to the reasonable likelihood of restriction in the complete charge and trial context.
- Buchanan v. Angelone and Weeks v. Angelone: distinguished in assessing present continuity and the adequacy of explanatory directions; their later holdings do not supply newly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff. Weeks's unresolved response-error effect question remains unresolved by this original-charge holding.
- Chapman v. California: extended expressly to the defined preserved original-charge capital-mitigation category, allocating the beyond-reasonable-doubt showing to the State.
- Calderon v. Coleman: its commutation-misinformation holding remains intact; its allocation rationale supports the new bounded extension without becoming a preexisting universal habeas rule.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, O'Neal v. McAninch, and California v. Roy: their existing category-specific operation is not converted into a universal effect rule or silently displaced outside the present extension.
- Hohn v. United States and Slack v. McDaniel: applied for reviewability and issue-specific certification distinct from ultimate relief.
- Shafer v. South Carolina: its later direct-review parole-information holding is not backdated or treated as the rule deciding this mitigation mechanism.

**Resulting law:** The pre-1995 effective-vehicle rule controls materially equivalent contradictory special-issue mechanisms on supported mitigation records. General permission to consider mitigation is insufficient when the required signed answer still certifies a factual doubt the juror need not have. The new five-Justice rule assigns the State the burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that a preserved original-charge restriction in this category did not contribute to death. It controls the remand and later applications of this defined remedial category; it does not alter every habeas effect standard. The psychiatric claim remains barred by section 2254(d)(1) on its own ground. The mitigation claim proceeds with its constitutional and statutory gateway conclusions settled, but no writ issues before the remaining full-record inquiry.

### Wood v. Meadows — 2001-06-11

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Wood_v_Meadows_merits_2001-06-11.md#holdings).

#### I. The continuing deadline dispute remains fit for decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Completion of the 1994 election does not end this challenge to the unchanged nomination deadline, where Wood's continuing voter and candidate interests and the documented repeated litigation over prospective enforcement establish a reasonably recurring controversy on an election timetable too short for complete review. The later signature amendment does not moot the surviving deadline question.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice coalition joining Souter's opinion directly adopts this threshold holding. Stevens and Ginsburg also reach the merits before disagreeing with the disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this case concerns prospective application of a continuing restriction, not a request to reopen a completed election. Norman v. Reed applies Moore v. Ogilvie to election disputes with continuing consequences and recurrence that would evade full review. Wood's identified voter and candidate interests, the unchanged June deadline and the actual repeated enforcement litigation provide that connection here. The earlier petition's account of signature gathering is an allegation, not a judicial finding of a particular future candidacy. The recurrence conclusion does not depend on inventing such a promise. Changing signature totals and distribution did not remove the challenged date. Independent validity of the amended signature provisions and the separate contempt proceedings are outside the accepted question.

#### II. This accessible nomination system proportionately supports the June deadline

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may close independent Senate nomination on its June primary date when the complete scheme affords a substantial practical petition period, reasonably attainable support requirements, multiple petition signing, retained primary participation and time after primary candidates file to organize independently, and when common nomination timing and orderly preparation proportionately justify the actual burden. Virginia's demonstrated system satisfies that standard; calling a restriction reasonable does not eliminate scrutiny of the relationship between burden and asserted interests.

**Authority:** Souter's complete opinion is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, including its author. Those seven direct joins control the application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Anderson's examination of actual injury, precise interests and fit. Burdick requires compelling-interest narrow tailoring for severe restrictions and permits proportionate important regulatory interests for reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions. Timmons permits preventive nomination rules within an accessible complete system without granting them immunity from constitutional review.

Wood's exclusion involved 14,865 signatures, including 200 per congressional district. Collection began January 1; signers could support multiple candidates and still vote in a primary, and candidates needed no prior party disaffiliation. Primary candidates filed sixty days earlier, while other party nomination methods ended by June. Independents thus received meaningful time after primary candidates filed, although no opportunity to wait for the primary result. That lost opportunity is a real burden, but it does not establish a severe barrier within this system.

The Commonwealth's asserted interests include verification, demonstrated support, ballot management and establishing the general-election field at the primary. Evidence that processing requires about thirty days defeats a claim that verification alone needs all 150 days; it does not defeat the additional supported interest in a common nomination endpoint and an orderly general-election campaign. Storer supports orderly candidate selection, and the Constitution does not require the latest mechanically possible filing date. Neither formal equality nor a primary-day label is decisive. The Court rejects the lower court's categorical suggestion that fit ceases to matter for reasonable burdens, yet sustains the judgment after undertaking that inquiry. It imposes no per se safe harbor, entitlement to await every later preference, or least-restrictive-calendar test.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Anderson v. Celebrezze: applied as the actual burden, precise interest and fit inquiry; its earlier presidential deadline does not mechanically decide this Senate system.
- Burdick v. Takushi: applied without changing its calibrated scrutiny or importing an absolute rule from its write-in application.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party: applied for proportionate preventive nomination administration in an accessible system; it remains neither pure rational-basis review nor permission to suppress competition.
- Norman v. Reed and Moore v. Ogilvie: applied to the continuing election controversy and meaningful access; support screening remains permissible within the actual electorate.
- Storer v. Brown: applied for the legitimate role of orderly candidate selection, without validating every deadline.

**Resulting law:** The June independent Senate deadline remains enforceable in the demonstrated Virginia scheme. Lower courts must continue to examine actual burdens, asserted interests and their proportional relationship even when the restriction is reasonable and nondiscriminatory. Primary-day coincidence is neither an automatic safe harbor nor a constitutional defect. Materially different cumulative barriers require their own application of the same settled standard; the separate amended signature-distribution question is not decided.

### United States v. Mead Corp. — 2001-06-18

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md#holdings).

#### I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding and operative rule:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Authority for Holdings I and II:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion have eight direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author. Scalia does not join either proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to administer and classify does not make every administrative interpretation controlling law for a reviewing court. Haggar requires actual delegated authority and statutory fit before acceptance of a reasonable choice within genuine ambiguity. Its formally promulgated Customs regulation does not establish the same authority for the different mechanism here. Christensen distinguishes controlling authority from an advisory interpretation's useful reasoning without deciding that all informal actions have the same status.

Customs letters guide particular transactions, may be modified, issue from numerous offices and do not bind unrelated importers as a generally applicable regulation would. Their place in the protest and judicial-review scheme confirms that administrative operation is distinct from delegated interpretive lawmaking. Formal rulemaking or adjudication can be important evidence of delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite. Independent judicial factfinding likewise does not itself defeat lawful deference; Haggar remains intact.

The Solicitor General's brief, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, represents this as Customs' official position. The Court accepts that representation. It identifies whose interpretation is defended, but does not establish that Congress gave this ruling mechanism controlling legal effect. Headquarters' developed reasons and the responsible institution's endorsement strengthen the claim to consideration, not the statutory delegation. The 1993 action remains governed by its own legal regime; later procedures for changing rulings do not supply retrospective authority. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations are outside this grant.

#### II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding and operative rule:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an agency can offer useful reasons without possessing authority to bind the court's answer. Skidmore provides that separate inquiry, and Christensen preserves it when controlling statutory deference is unavailable. Customs addressed dictionary meaning, commercial usage and whether a ring mechanism binds pages. Those reasons bear on the actual tariff language and cannot be discarded by attaching a nonregulatory label to the letter.

Mead correctly points to the change from earlier duty-free treatment. That change may weaken a consistency claim; it does not eliminate every reason the agency offers. Expertise, national responsibility and official endorsement likewise do not automatically make the position persuasive. The court evaluates the explanation on its merits within the enacted tariff boundaries. Neither the fact that Customs changed course nor the fact that it administers a complex scheme decides the tariff question in advance. The distinction preserves meaningful independent judicial interpretation rather than converting persuasion into a lesser form of mandatory deference.

#### III. The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with the persuasive consideration required by Holding II. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appellate judgment cannot stand on an inquiry that wholly excluded the permissible persuasive contribution of Customs' interpretation. The goods are described as ring-bound planners containing calendar and memorandum pages; no missing product fact supplies the reason for return. Nor is there a general entitlement to have a lower court decide every interpretive question first. The complete tariff question is presented, but neither proposed definitive construction commands a majority: independent construction sustaining the category and controlling acceptance of Customs' reasonable construction rest on distinct noncontrolling grounds. The seven-Justice disposition instead requires the corrected judicial inquiry without predetermining its answer.

The arguments remain concrete. Mead contends that removable pages and prospective scheduling are outside bound diaries. Customs answers that rings bind pages and that diaries include dated prospective entries. The reviewing court must decide the statutory meaning, consider the actual agency reasons and implement the resulting classification through ordinary tariff procedures. The disputed tariff meaning is genuinely undecided here for want of a majority construction; assessment calculations following the eventual lawful classification are ordinary implementation, not a second unresolved deference rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

**Resulting law:** Materially equivalent Customs ruling letters do not command controlling Chevron treatment through this mechanism. Courts must independently interpret the tariff while giving the agency's actual reasons the persuasive consideration they earn. Other genuine delegations, including valid regulations governed by Haggar, retain their own authority. Official endorsement does not collapse the delegation and persuasion questions. Neither ultimate classification of these planners commands a majority; the governing judicial inquiry is settled, and the tariff answer returns for decision under it.

### Daniels v. United States — 2001-04-25

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the common available-review holding, counsel exception and affirmance, and additionally adopts an affirmative faultless-loss exception as part of his governing access rule. Section 2255 review should remain available when meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated through no fault of the prisoner, subject to applicable procedure, proof of constitutional defect and adverse effect on present custody. This is his present legal position, not merely a suggestion that an exception might be recognized later.

The distinction is between meaningful first review and repetitive litigation. Custis's forum rule and reservation do not establish that every completed conviction had an effective first opportunity, while Maleng's custody requirement must still be respected. A later enhancement creates a new incentive but does not make an earlier available remedy unavailable. Conversely, administrative inconvenience should not insulate a demonstrably unlawful current sentence from every authorized challenge. He does not equate every attorney failure with the prisoner's deliberate choice. Daniels identifies no complete counsel deprivation or external obstruction to meaningful first review, so even this affirmative exception does not allow his claims. Stone's exception has no controlling majority and is not borrowed from a decision in Coss. He orders no fresh sentencing or predicate invalidation.

**Scalia, concurring.** He joins the actual available-review rule and finality reasoning, but adds a statutory objection: section 2255's remedy for an unlawful federal sentence does not itself authorize adjudicating an outstanding state predicate's non-Gideon constitutional defect. Custis supports distinguishing an operative conviction from a collateral objection, although its actual sentencing holding did not already decide every expired-custody setting. A possible constitutional duty to provide review would concern the rendering jurisdiction and would not identify section 2255 as the remedy. He does not affirmatively endorse a section 2255 faultless-review exception. The Court's statement of what it has not decided is a scope description, not his agreement that such an exception exists. He orders no alternative forum remedy and decides no absent constitutional claim.

**Souter, dissenting, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** Section 2255 expressly authorizes review of the current federal sentence when constitutional error allegedly makes that sentence unlawful. Custis's restriction at the sentencing proceeding supplies no sufficient statutory basis for a new categorical exclusion from this express collateral remedy. Custis's available original-custody setting and reservation make the difference particularly important. The dissent's reading permits examination of an allegedly unlawful enhancement without treating Daniels as still in custody under the old state judgments.

Finality and stale evidence matter through applicable procedure and lawful proof rules; they do not justify the additional categorical bar. Parke preserves regularity and proof, Garlotte separates custody from remaining conditions, and Fiore and Glover distinguish an actual constitutional wrong and its causal effect from access requirements. The dissent preserves applicable defaults and does not automatically import all section 2254 exhaustion requirements into section 2255. It would reverse and remand for authorized consideration, not find either plea invalid, guarantee a hearing or order a lower sentence. The remaining qualifying predicates and the actual effect on lawful federal punishment would still require adjudication.

**Breyer, dissenting.** He also would reverse the categorical denial and remand, but on a distinct statutory and practical ground. Custis should be reconsidered: the sentencing scheme is better read to permit properly raised constitutional challenges to predicates at sentencing under lawful proof rules. His earlier opinion in United States v. Paleo supplies that approach. A prohibition at the original federal sentencing then generates restrictive or increasingly complex collateral exceptions; section 2255 is an awkward substitute for deciding the predicate issue when the enhancement is imposed. His disposition agreement with Souter does not make him an automatic join in every aspect of Souter's construction of section 2255. He proposes reconsideration, not an accomplished overruling, and establishes no plea defect, excuse of default, hearing entitlement or sentence reduction.

**O'Connor's Part III, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.** They would independently reverse because the challenged convictions did not increase the actual six-to-twelve-year sentence. They accept consideration but regard the adverse-effect inference as clearly erroneous. They rely on the combined removal of score and range effects, the present aggravated assault, independent supported juvenile and adult history, and the sentencing judge's stated concerns with the present crime, community protection, criminal disposition, rehabilitation, testimony and continued lawbreaking.
