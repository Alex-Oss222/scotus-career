## Standards and Tests selection: One-year state-custody habeas filing period

### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Time during which a properly filed application for state postconviction or other collateral review of the pertinent judgment or claim is pending is excluded.

**Authority by component:**

- Four alternative latest-event triggers and pending-state-review exclusion: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §101, 28 U.S.C. §2244(d), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, 1996-04-24.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exhaustion-return applications under former habeas law

### Exhaustion-return applications under former habeas law

**Governing question:** Whether an application renewed after a without-prejudice state-exhaustion dismissal is second or successive under the former habeas rules.

**Current rule:** When an initial federal application was dismissed without prejudice solely to complete state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, its renewal remains a first application even if additional grounds are included. Former Rule 9(b)’s new-ground abuse branch does not eliminate the rule’s second-or-successive predicate in that setting. Independent exhaustion, adequate state default, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain applicable; the classification creates neither an unlimited amendment right nor immunity from a genuinely applicable abuse rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Exhaustion-return classification, additional claims and retained independent requirements: Slack v. McDaniel — IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds, April 26, 2000; Ginsburg joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Slack, April 26, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** Former Rule 9(a) separately permits dismissal when delay prejudices the State’s ability to respond unless reasonable diligence could not have revealed the grounds before prejudice arose. Rule 9(b) permits rather than commands dismissal on its predicates. Former §2244(b)’s prior-merits permission and its conjunctive exception for an unadjudicated ground without deliberate withholding or other abuse are not invoked by an exhaustion dismissal without a merits adjudication.

**Open question:** Slack decides no universal rule for every nonmerits dismissal or later claim, and no amended §2244 authorization determination or exception. The amended statute retains its own applicability and classification predicates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religion and public schools

## Religion and public schools

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Mitchell, June 28, 2000, for equipment/library-book category displacement and proof-and-remedy limits; Agostini’s June 23, 1997 unified inquiry and Jackson’s March 16, 1999 financed-function components retain their original force.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. **Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. ### Official school worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially organized school worship, delegated representative religious choices and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

A school may not make an invocation an official representative component of its own ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that component includes religious exercise. Such a mechanism is not transformed into private access by student authorship, optional attendance, possible silence or secular speech, or nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits. The defect does not require a prayer already delivered or official editing of its words. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independently chosen student expression, lawful secular messages and neutral private access remain protected; the holding adds no separate religious-pressure test.

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship does not establish religion. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

In a broad after-hours educational and community forum, an otherwise comparable private meeting cannot be excluded solely for its worship form; exact secular sacramental counterparts are not required. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and activity limits remain available. An admitted instructional subject likewise cannot exclude comparable religious instruction merely while permitting religious discussion. Independent private worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion; actual governmental adoption must be shown rather than inferred from religious identity or location.

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.

### Denominational selection for general governmental authority

**Governing question:** When creating a special public district impermissibly allocates civic power on a denominational basis.

**Current rule:** The State may not select a religious denomination as the recipient of general governmental authority, even when lay officials formally exercise the power. Independently, an anomalous particular grant aligned with a denomination and lacking a neutral basis assuring comparable treatment violates the required governmental neutrality. Religious accommodation may preserve equal educational access but does not cure denominational allocation of civic power. Demographic religious concentration alone does not establish the violation, and neither rationale makes every special statute or every accommodation invalid.

### RFRA review of prospective enforcement

**Governing question:** Original RFRA within its surviving valid field, including threatened enforcement of preexisting laws and commercial religious exercise.

**Current rule:** Within a valid field of RFRA operation, the claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The inquiry applies to threatened future implementation of a preexisting law. Commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and labeling a law antidiscrimination does not itself justify its application. Direct transactional and dignitary injury and legally material alternatives or exemptions may be considered; availability of alternative housing is not an automatic answer to direct injury.

RFRA also governs later federal enactments unless Congress expressly excludes application by reference to RFRA; FACE contains no such exclusion.

RFRA's universal State and local command cannot rest on Fourteenth Amendment §5: its substitution of the substantial-burden/compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means standard for Smith across the field lacks the required congruent and proportional enforcement relationship. Prospective State and local RFRA claims relying only on that power cannot proceed after June 25, 1997. Completed judgments under Church of Scientology and Swanner retain finality; their independent grounds and statutory reasoning survive within a valid field of operation. Federal RFRA, including its later-federal-enactment rule, remains outside this power holding.

**Authority by component:**

### Ministerial selection as a substantive constitutional defense

**Governing question:** Title VII claims whose adjudication or relief controls selection of religious leaders.

**Current rule:** The Religion Clauses bar applying Title VII to ministerial advancement and retention claims when adjudication would subject a church’s selection of religious leadership to civil review. The bar includes reinstatement, compelled reconsideration and damages resting on that same selection, including race, sex and retaliation theories. It is a substantive defense: a court with jurisdiction over a substantial Title VII claim under §706(f)(3) and §1331 must enter a merits dismissal, not deny subject-matter jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Ministerial selection claims encounter a constitutional defense: Ministerial selection claims encounter a constitutional defense, 1995-01-17. Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this claim-specific rule; their formal descriptions of the judgment are distinguished below.
- The defense defeats the claims on their merits, not statutory jurisdiction: The defense defeats the claims on their merits, not statutory jurisdiction, 1995-01-17. Justice Souter's opinion, this proposition, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the jurisdiction-and-merits distinction; eight vacate the jurisdictional judgment as a whole, while the Chief would vacate the jurisdictional characterization and affirm the dismissal on the merits.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-17; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule neither enlarges §702’s religious-preference permission nor exempts every religious employee or unrelated secular conduct. An independently pleaded salary, property, tort or contract claim remains outside this defense if it can be resolved without doctrine, ministerial fitness or selection. No comprehensive minister definition, fraud-or-collusion exception, RFRA disposition or new requirement to exhaust the denomination’s ultimate hierarchy is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** Ministerial selection claims encounter a constitutional defense; The defense defeats the claims on their merits, not statutory jurisdiction.

### Genuine pedagogy and religious perspectives in graded assignments

**Governing question:** A student’s challenge to rejection of a religious research topic within a teacher-assessed curricular assignment.

**Current rule:** Teachers may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns; religious conviction creates no exemption. But categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal ban on academic religious subjects, is not legitimate pedagogy merely because so labeled. Schools retain authority to select knowledge, method or perspective taught and to distinguish objective inquiry from testimony or devotional advocacy; no universal curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule follows. Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible academic justification where supported evidence permits a material inference of impermissible religious causation. The student must establish protected conduct and a substantial or motivating role; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent it.

**Authority by component:**

- Legitimate-pedagogy boundary and actual causal proof: I. Curricular authority must rest on genuine, legitimate pedagogy and II. The affirmative record leaves a material causal dispute, 1996-06-25; Souter’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Settle, 1996-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary genuine factual or evaluative errors may remain within pedagogical discretion; the rule creates no classroom forum, automatic topic approval, compelled grade, independent investigative duty or conclusive liability from an impermissible factor where the same-decision defense is proved.

**Holdings navigation:** Settle v. Dickson County School Board.

### Official religious identification in governmental symbols

**Governing question:** A government's use of religious symbolism as its continuing civic expression and present civil identifier.

**Current rule:** A municipality's adopted seal used to authenticate its communications, personnel and property is its own expression despite private design. Government violates the Establishment Clause when the whole context of its continuing civic expression identifies it with a particular faith; secular historical purpose and surrounding secular imagery alone do not cure that effect. Independently, a city may not deliberately and pervasively use a recognizable symbol of a particular faith as a permanent identifying part of its badge of present civil authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Robinson v. City of Edmond — II. The adopted municipal seal is the City's own expression, 1997-03-31; Part II, all nine, supplies attribution
- Robinson v. City of Edmond — III. Context establishes an impermissible official religious affiliation, 1997-03-31; Breyer Part III with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg (six) supplies the contextual-effects ground
- Robinson v. City of Edmond — IV. Making a faith's badge the City's permanent civil identifier is independently forbidden, 1997-03-31; Part IV, all nine, supplies the independent present-civil-identification prohibition
- Robinson v. City of Edmond — V. Relief must end the present official use and preserve the distinction from history, 1997-03-31; Part V, all nine, supplies the prospective-use/history distinction

**Present operation effective:** Robinson v. City of Edmond, 1997-03-31.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** Relief must end the unconstitutional current official use, allowing a reasonable implementation period; accurate contextual preservation of history remains permissible.

**Exceptions and limits:** Attribution alone does not invalidate every government acknowledgment of religion. Private equal-access expression, historical presentation and materially different acknowledgments retain their distinctions. Standing does not extend to a general claim of offense at a distant symbol.

**Related current rules:** Lemon test states the governing Establishment Clause inquiry as updated in Agostini v. Felton (June 23, 1997, Holding I), in which purpose, effects and entanglement serve as evidence; Robinson's Part III contextual-affiliation ground was decided under Lemon's then-independent effects inquiry, and its Part IV civil-identifier prohibition is an independently sufficient ground.

**Holdings navigation:** Robinson v. City of Edmond (1997-03-31) — First Amendment / Official municipal religious identification; Robinson v. City of Edmond (1997-03-31) — Whether this continuing civic identifier establishes an impermissible religious affiliation.

### Genuine moments of silence at official university functions

**Governing question:** When may a public university designate silence, including as a replacement for prayer?

**Current rule:** A genuine moment of silence is permissible when officials leave participants free to reflect, pray or do neither and do not prescribe or direct religious profession or devotional exercise. Replacing earlier prayer does not alone make implemented silence worship. Actual administration directing devotion removes that justification, while genuinely private prayer during silence is not governmental merely because of its timing.

**Authority by component:**

- Nondevotional silence and private-choice boundary; unanimous: Chaudhuri v. Tennessee — Genuine silence leaves religious thought to the participant, 1998-04-27.

**Present operation effective:** Chaudhuri v. Tennessee, 1998-04-27.

**Related current rules:** Lemon test governs attribution and function; Official school worship and independent religious pressure supplies the separate prohibition on government-organized devotion.

**Holdings navigation:** Chaudhuri v. Tennessee — University prayer and genuine moments of silence.

### Religious music in public-school academic programs

**Governing question:** When religious choral material or a religious venue makes a school’s academic performance governmental worship.

**Current rule:** Selection, rehearsal and performance of religious choral music, including in religious buildings, for legitimate musical and educational purposes do not themselves violate the Establishment Clause. Religious words, venue and teacher identity alone do not make academic activity worship. An artistic label or secular objective nevertheless cannot excuse separately identifiable official organization of worship, prescribed religious observance or solicitation of adherence through public authority. Concrete earlier official conduct may be relevant to a continuing devotional-use allegation and cannot be excluded categorically merely because it predates the challenged school year.

**Authority by component:**

- Holdings II and IV: academic/devotional distinction and relevant continuing-course allegations: Bauchman v. West High School — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** The genuine option to decline songs without lost grades, required profession or worship defeats the original compulsion theory at its stated scope; sham options, lost credit, exclusion, stigma or actual worship requirements are not approved.

**Holdings navigation:** Bauchman v. West High School — Graduation, amendment and dependent supplemental jurisdiction; Bauchman v. West High School — Academic sacred music, official devotion and compelled singing.
