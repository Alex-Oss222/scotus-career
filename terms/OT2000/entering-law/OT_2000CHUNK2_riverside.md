# Received authority: Riverside Bayview

United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes, 474 U.S. 121 (December 4, 1985), upheld the Corps' reasonable construction covering wetlands adjacent to navigable waters. Frequent flooding by the neighboring water was unnecessary; groundwater saturation and the actual adjacent-wetland findings sufficed. The ecological relationship, statutory language and structure supported the interpretation. Congressional consideration of jurisdiction in 1977 corroborated that construction; silence alone was not its basis. Section 404(g)(1)'s express adjacent-wetland language supported inclusion without conclusively deciding the definition throughout the Act.

Footnotes 2 and 8 expressly reserve nonadjacent wetlands. Footnote 9 permits a reasonable category even if some adjacent wetlands contribute little; permitting can address particular uses. That reasoning does not decide coverage of isolated nonwetland ponds. The permit requirement itself was not necessarily a taking, and an available compensation remedy defeated the asserted need to narrow coverage on that ground.

White wrote for a unanimous Court; Stevens and O'Connor were the participating current Associates. This received holding supplies no personal position for Associates who joined later. Source: [full opinion and notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/474/121).
