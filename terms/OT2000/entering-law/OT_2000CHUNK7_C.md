## Standards and Tests selection: Actual receipt and the Interstate Agreement on Detainers trial period

### Actual receipt and the Interstate Agreement on Detainers trial period

**Governing question:** What instructions govern actual receipt and the Interstate Agreement on Detainers trial period?

**Current rule:** For a prisoner serving a term with an untried charge subject to a detainer in another participating State, Article III(a)'s 180-day period begins when both the prosecuting officer and appropriate court actually receive the required written notice and disposition request, not delivery to the custodian alone. Necessary or reasonable continuance is permitted for good cause shown in open court with prisoner or counsel present. Article VI(a) separately tolls time during inability to stand trial as determined by the court with jurisdiction. Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice for violation of the applicable period. Prompt forwarding and Article III(c) notice remain duties; the receipt rule supplies no remedy for their breach or automatic grace period.

Counsel's authorized, unqualified express agreement before the Article III(a) period expires to a specified later trial date binds a represented prisoner as to that date, unless a contemporaneous objection or reservation, conflict, or lack of authority in that proceeding defeats the agreement. Acceptance and proposal have the same effect. Personal attendance, repeated assent, a personal colloquy, an Article III citation and proof of deadline-specific advice are not required for this scheduling decision. This agreement is distinct from statutory continuance and inability tolling. An actual violation without effective agreement or statutory exception still requires Article V(c) dismissal with prejudice and loss of the underlying detainer's force, without additional defense impairment, bad faith or harmlessness requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period: Fex v. Michigan — Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period, 1993-02-23. Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six votes for this rule and its application.

- Counsel's date agreement and preserved statutory conditions/remedy: New York v. Hill, Public Projection / Holdings / Counsel may bind a represented prisoner to an expressly agreed trial date; Counsel's January 9 agreement supplies the required assent; A date agreement does not replace the compact's statutory conditions; The agreement defeats this dismissal ground, while the statutory remedy remains mandatory for an actual violation, January 11, 2000; Stevens for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** New York v. Hill, January 11, 2000, for the express date-agreement rule. Fex's actual-receipt, continuance, inability and dismissal rules remain effective from February 23, 1993.

**Holdings navigation:** Fex v. Michigan, 507 U.S. 43 (1993).

**Exceptions and limits:** The agreement permits neither wholesale surrender of the compact nor indefinite, bad-faith or post-agreed-date delay. Post-expiration assent, withdrawal of consent, coercion, misunderstanding, ineffective assistance and actual conflict retain their separate questions. No automatic motion-pendency tolling, grace period, Article IV(c) holding or surrender of anti-shuttling protection follows. Presence alone is not good cause for a statutory continuance.

## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. When the complete access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate interests, including primary finality and prevention of evasive candidacies or party raiding. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**
- Governing inherited framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden”, June 8, 1992; White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas (five), applying Anderson and the earlier simulated Norman rule.
- Limited-burden write-in presumption: Burdick, “2. Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid in its reasonably accessible system,” June 8, 1992; same five Justices.
- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the write-in presumption and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute validation of all write-in bans follows. Materially more restrictive access systems may impose severe burdens. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

**Holdings navigation:** Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Authorized party expansion and independently qualified slate components

### Authorized party expansion and independently qualified slate components

**Governing question:** Whether a State may deny ballot access through a geographic party-name barrier or failure of candidates in a different electorate to qualify.

**Current rule:** A State may require meaningful support in each separate electorate. It may not categorically bar authorized candidates from expanding an existing party under its name, or disqualify otherwise qualified countywide and city candidates solely because candidates in a different district lack sufficient signatures. Protection against unauthorized appropriation of a party's name does not justify a geographic barrier that also prohibits authorized expansion; support requirements for one electorate do not justify invalidating candidates who qualified in their own constituencies.

**Authority by component:**

- Authorized name expansion, independent qualification, and separate-electorate support: Norman v. Reed — Ballot-access holding and operative rule, January 14, 1992; Souter's opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy.

**Present operation effective:** *Norman*, January 14, 1992.

**Open question:** Other district structures, cumulative burdens, and actual unauthorized name use remain outside this holding.

**Holdings navigation:** Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances; a legal coercion defense may be reviewed on proper historical assumptions without selecting the disputed account. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).
- Conditional assumed-fact rule: Behrens — Holding 2, February 21, 1996; unanimous Part II. Claim-specific effect of another surviving claim: Holding 3, Part III for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join. Stevens and Breyer join these conditional rules while opposing authorization of the successive appeal.

- Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts: Clanton v. Cooper, 1997-12-29; Ginsburg Part I for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Behrens, February 21, 1996; Johnson and Kimberlin retain their original component authority.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

**Holdings navigation:** Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal; Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule; Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299. Clanton v. Cooper — Collateral appeals from immunity denials; Clanton v. Cooper — Material official deception and qualified immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Lanier — Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted, 1997-03-31; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies conduct-time notice and the limit on reliance on bad purpose
- United States v. Lanier — Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition supplies the permissible legal landscape
- United States v. Lanier — Obvious applications need no factual twin, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition rejects a factual-twin requirement while preserving unsettled boundaries
- United States v. Lanier — The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition identifies the shared clarity protection without merging criminal liability

**Present operation effective:** Lanier, 1997-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Willful bad purpose cannot cure an unknowable constitutional prohibition. The Government must still establish constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness and every other element under the criminal burden. Due-process rights and direct official assaults outside custody are not categorically excluded, but no particular count's merits or notice is predetermined.

**Related current rules:** Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review retains its civil review, timing and preservation mechanics.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lanier (1997-03-31) — Criminal Procedure / Criminal civil-rights liability and fair warning.

## Standards and Tests selection: Timely direct challenges to adjudicators

### Timely direct challenges to adjudicators’ appointments

**Governing question:** Relief for a litigant’s timely substantial constitutional appointment objection on direct review.

**Current rule:** A litigant who timely challenges a substantial constitutional defect in the appointment of officers adjudicating his own case and pursues it on direct review is entitled to fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed officers. The de facto officer doctrine cannot validate the defective panel’s judgment against him. A higher review that lacks the displaced proceeding’s fact weighing, credibility, contested-fact and sentence-review functions does not replace those lost opportunities.

**Authority by component:**

- The higher court did not supply the missing Article 66 review: The higher court did not supply the missing Article 66 review, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; all nine adopt this case-specific rejection of cure and preserved-argument limit.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-12; the component authorities identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Appointment of civilian Coast Guard appellate judges

**Current rule:** Section 323(a), read with Article 66's civilian-judge provision, permits the Secretary of Transportation as department head to appoint civilian Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals judges; assignment and appointment remain distinct. ## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs regulations in tariff litigation

### Delegated Customs regulations in tariff litigation

**Governing question:** Whether the Court of International Trade’s own-record review displaces Chevron treatment of a promulgated Customs regulation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999.

**Open question:** Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided. The particular regulation’s ultimate reasonableness and the importer’s exemption are not established by the delegation holding.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.


## Holdings selection: criminal-procedure / New York v. Hill, 528 U.S. 110 (2000)

#### New York v. Hill, 528 U.S. 110 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1299.
**Decided:** January 11, 2000.

##### Whether counsel may bind the represented prisoner to a specified date beyond Article III(a)

**Holding:** When defense counsel expressly agrees, before expiration of Article III(a)'s period, to a specified trial date beyond that period, the represented prisoner may not obtain dismissal because the agreed date exceeded the deadline, absent a contemporaneous objection or reservation when the date is set, a conflict, or a showing that counsel lacked authority in the particular proceeding. The prisoner need not personally attend, repeat the agreement, or undergo a waiver colloquy for this scheduling act; the rule does not authorize counsel to override an express timely objection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule requires genuine, unqualified agreement within counsel's authority. It does not surrender the compact wholesale, permit indefinite or bad-faith delay or delay beyond the agreed date, decide consent first given after expiration, or decide coercion, misunderstanding, ineffective assistance or an actual conflict.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Taylor and Jones allocate professional trial management to counsel while retaining the accused's basic personal choices. Brookhart preserves the not-guilty defense against express contrary surrender. Olano requires right-specific relinquishment; Mezzanatto supplies no general statutory-waiver presumption, and Godinez supplies no inferred personal assent.


##### Whether acceptance of the prosecutor-proposed date has the same binding effect as proposing it

**Holding:** Counsel's express January 9, 1995 agreement to the May 1 trial date binds Hill as to that date. Express acceptance has the same effect as proposing the identical date; neither an Article III citation, proof of deadline-specific advice nor separate personal authorization is required for this scheduling decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion.

**Material application:** The accepted computation showed 167 nonexcludable days at the January 9 agreement. The April 17 motion preserved the legal issue but was not a contemporaneous objection or reservation. The rejection is on the merits, not forfeiture.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general consent-withdrawal rule or requirement of deadline-specific advice is adopted.


##### Whether an agreed date changes the distinct statutory continuance or tolling requirements

**Holding:** An effective agreement to a particular trial date is distinct from an Article III(a) continuance or Article VI(a) tolling. Article III(a) permits a necessary or reasonable continuance only for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present; Article VI(a) separately tolls time while the prisoner is unable to stand trial, as determined by the court with jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presence alone does not establish good cause. No independent good-cause continuance or inability finding is made, and accepted motion-related exclusions establish no general automatic tolling rule. Article IV(c), anti-shuttling and other compact protections retain their separate predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fex retains actual receipt by both prosecutor and appropriate court as the 180-day trigger; delivery to the custodian is insufficient. Its qualified continuance and inability provisions are unchanged. Libretti preserves substantive statutory limits despite consent.


##### What remedy follows from an actual Article III violation and from this effective agreement

**Holding:** Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice when the applicable trial period is violated without an effective agreement or applicable statutory exception; no additional showing of defense impairment or bad faith, and no harmlessness exception, is imposed. Because Hill's agreement defeats the asserted Article III(a)/Article V(c) ground, the Court reverses the judgment dismissing the indictment, reinstates the conviction as against that ground, and remands for any preserved state or federal issue not resolved below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article V(c)'s distinct refusal-or-failure-to-accept-custody ground is unpresented. A dismissed charge's detainer loses force. The Court decides no independent constitutional speedy-trial or ineffective-assistance claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Reed concerns collateral relief and supplies neither a direct-review prejudice requirement nor an independent waiver finding here.


## Holdings selection: criminal-procedure / United States v. Lanier

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.


## Holdings selection: civil-rights / Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991)

#### Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1440.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Who decides qualified immunity from an arrest-without-probable-cause claim and what record governs at summary judgment

**Holding:** Qualified immunity from a claim of arrest without probable cause is a legal question for the judge under ordinary summary-judgment constraints. The court must determine whether a reasonable officer could believe the arrest lawful on the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; choosing the “more reasonable” interpretation is not the test, and a generalized security concern alone does not establish a reasonable belief that the offense's elements were satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; these five Justices supply the controlling majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** The agents could consider assassination references, a reported gesture, oral statements, the confusing interview, and refusal to elaborate. They also had the letter's repeated attribution of the supposed plot to an outside group, bearing on whether Bryant communicated a serious threat under §871 or reported an imagined danger. The Ninth Circuit's comparison of the relative reasonableness of interpretations and its general assignment of legal immunity to a jury were erroneous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither guilt, innocence, bad faith, nor a constitutional violation. It neither directs immunity nor orders an automatic trial. The separate warrant ruling remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is vacated and the case remanded. The remand court must specify any supported dispute over material historical facts and decide immunity promptly under the correct standard.

**Material judgment:** Vacatur and remand are supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter, 6–2. White would direct immunity; Stevens would affirm, and Scalia would reverse and direct immunity. These differing remedial positions do not enlarge the five-Justice controlling rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow, Anderson v. Creighton, and Malley supply protection for reasonable mistakes and early judicial resolution when supported facts permit it. Watts supplies the contextual distinction between a true threat and protected expression. Mitchell, Beck, and Liberty Lobby are applied and preserved. No qualified-immunity rule is revised.


## Holdings selection: civil-rights / Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.


## Holdings selection: civil-rights / Plakas v. Drinski

#### Plakas v. Drinski

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1824.
**Decided:** January 9, 1995.

##### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 (1989): applies objective reasonableness under the complete contemporaneous circumstances from the reasonable officer's on-scene perspective, with allowance for tense and rapidly developing events; no subjective-malice or hindsight test is substituted.
- Tennessee v. Garner, 471 U.S. 1 (1985): preserves the prohibition on deadly force against a nondangerous fleeing person and the serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning conditions; this supported immediate armed attack differs from nondangerous escape.
- Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991): preserves the supported nonmovant account and complete contemporaneous record at summary judgment; this case neither selects immunity nor uses Hunter as notice predating its decision.
- Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994): preserves consideration of relevant law on a preserved immunity issue, ordinary record limits and conduct-date notice; no immunity disposition or mandatory merits-first sequence is added.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earlier-beating theory was not pleaded and lacks the asserted causal showing; the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. Gilmere's different provocation theory is neither approved nor disapproved. The Court does not decide a case in which the threat ended before the shot or a supported safe alternative makes the force unreasonable. The merits ground supplies no general immunity for tactical decisions.

##### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

**Holding:** A municipality's §1983 liability requires a cognizable federal deprivation attributable to municipal policy or training, the applicable culpability and a causal connection; officer personal immunity is not municipal immunity, and reasonable final force alone does not answer every independent municipal-created-danger theory. The county is entitled to summary judgment here because the actual equipment-and-training contention establishes neither a constitutional equipment-list duty nor a developed independent deprivation with the required causal and culpability showing; a purely derivative force theory also lacks a predicate when the force itself is reasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the county judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserves municipal policy/custom responsibility and causation for a federal deprivation; employment or vicarious responsibility alone is insufficient.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): applies the required failure-to-train culpability and causal connection; it does not create a substantive right to every device or training method.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992): applies the separation between deprivation and municipal attribution; its voluntary-employment holding does not decide every affirmative municipal injury.
- City of Los Angeles v. Heller, 475 U.S. 796 (1986): applies to a derivative theory resting solely on constitutionally reasonable force; it does not supply a universal bar to a distinct municipal wrong.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993): preserves ordinary municipal pleading and distinct Rule 56 proof; it neither relaxes Monell/Canton elements nor supplies personal immunity to a municipality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment for Drinski and Newton County is affirmed on the presented force and municipal claims. No trial, damages or personal-immunity ruling is ordered.

## Holdings selection: constitutional-structure / Edmond v. United States

#### Edmond v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-262.
**Decided:** 1997-05-19.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable are included under Rule 12.4; all six judgments receive the same disposition.

##### Congress authorized these appointments by the Secretary

**Holding:** Section 323(a), read with Article 66's provision for civilian military appellate judges, authorizes the Secretary of Transportation as department head to appoint the civilian officers serving on the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals. Article 66 assignment is distinct from appointment, but no additional statute repeating the office's name in an appointment clause is required for these existing statutory offices.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Weiss's commissioned-officer/germane-assignment rule left civilians open; Article 66 and §323(a) now supply the separate appointment basis. Freytag preserves proper appointment; Ryder does not cure an earlier defect through continued service.

##### The combined statutory hierarchy establishes inferior status here

**Holding:** These civilian military appellate judges are inferior officers because the combined structure subjects their assignments and administration to the Judge Advocate General, permits removal from judicial assignments and prescription of rules, and places their decisions under superior review by presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed judges; they lack final authority free of that system of direction and review. Their significant duties and protected adjudicative independence do not make them principal officers, and neither appellate review nor removal alone is held sufficient for every office.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Souter concurs on a distinct fuller classification rationale.

In a Judge Advocate General referral, it need take action only on the issues he raises; on the accused's petition, it need take action only on issues specified in the grant. Those minimum issue obligations do not convert legal review into guaranteed plenary factual rehearing. Neither review nor removal alone is held sufficient for every office; removal does not authorize unlawful decisional interference. Article III status and different offices with unreviewable final authority remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley preserves officer status for significant adjudicative duties. Morrison and Freytag require the actual office inquiry without a universal single-factor rule. Ryder's fresh Article 66 fact-and-sentence adjudication remedy remains for qualifying timely substantial defects; Article 67 review is no substitute, but the defect premise is absent here.

## Holdings selection: election-law / Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices control the complete rule. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent also accept the calibrated framework without making their contrary application controlling.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, preserves actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements, including its protection against cross-component exclusion. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate state interests. Hawaii's party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the rationale and affirmance. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy disagree with this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the complete access system reviewed and its reasonable burdens.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions support the limited-burden finding. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every later preference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently validate every surrounding access rule or decide a prohibition within materially more restrictive access rules. Candidate qualifications and reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere remain open; no absolute write-in right or guarantee of competitive races is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit; its reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction or replacement election-administration scheme issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, permits primary winnowing and preventive regulation without proof of a particular past disruption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and restrictions against circumvention. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, governs this limited-burden application; the decision creates no exception to its inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, controls. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent expressly agree on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual access analysis separately stated above. Political expression outside the ballot and every restriction on eligible candidates in a different system are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief from the prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.

## Holdings selection: federal-courts / Reed v. Farley, 512 U.S. 339

#### Reed v. Farley, 512 U.S. 339

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5418.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

##### The compact supplies federal law and a federal habeas question

**Holding:** The congressionally sanctioned Interstate Agreement on Detainers is federal law, and a state prisoner's claim that custody violates its trial-period provisions presents a federal statutory question within 28 U.S.C. §2254(a) as in force at decision. Jurisdiction to consider that question does not itself establish entitlement to collateral relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. These five Justices directly adopt the jurisdictional conclusion; the dissent also accepts the compact's federal character but does not join the opinion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cuyler v. Adams, 449 U.S. 433 (1981): applied to the congressionally sanctioned compact's federal-law character; voluntary state participation does not remove that character.
- Fex v. Michigan, February 23, 1993: its application of the compact as federal law remains intact; its Article III actual-receipt trigger does not replace Article IV's arrival trigger or decide collateral entitlement.
- Stone v. Powell, 428 U.S. 465 (1976): its restriction concerning the Fourth Amendment exclusionary remedy is not extended to decide this statutory claim.
- Withrow v. Williams, April 21, 1993: its refusal to extend Powell merely from a fair state opportunity to a different safeguard is preserved; its Miranda holding does not itself decide the distinct statutory-relief threshold here.

##### The assumed timing violation does not establish the required collateral defect on this record

**Holding:** An ordinary nonconstitutional statutory error asserted under §2254 must satisfy the collateral standard of a fundamental defect inherently resulting in a complete miscarriage of justice, or an omission inconsistent with the rudimentary demands of fair procedure; the §2255 standard applies to this claim despite the different source of the conviction. Assuming Article IV(c) was violated, Reed's presented record—including his predeadline written invocations, the absence of a specific contemporaneous objection to the scheduled dates, and no established defense impairment from the initial overrun—does not establish that defect, so the federal denial of habeas relief is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly join the standard and this record's denial. The stronger-demand discussion in Part IV has three votes and supplies no additional Court holding; no combination of separate opinions is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than decides the Article IV(c) breach and does not adopt automatic motion-pendency tolling, find a valid continuance, or resolve the competing statutory-merits positions. It does not impose a required oral formula or article-number citation, find complete silence or knowing waiver, or decide that an adequately presented demand necessarily produces habeas relief. No independent Sixth Amendment claim is presented. Barker and Doggett retain their complete constitutional inquiry, including Doggett's qualified treatment of presumed defense impairment; neither a 120-day overrun alone nor absence of separately proved prejudice alone answers that inquiry. The transfer-hearing and habitual-offender issues are outside the question decided. No categorical bar to every IAD claim, affirmative entitlement in an aggravated case, or general rule governing every statutory habeas claim is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424 (1962): its ordinary nonconstitutional-error threshold is extended from §2255 to the presented §2254 claim; its distinction between formal omission and fundamental defect remains operative.
- United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780 (1979): its collateral distinction is applied to this timing claim without treating every statutory entitlement as a technicality or deciding every aggravated case.
- Davis v. United States, 417 U.S. 333 (1974): its statutory no-offense holding remains available; this procedural timing claim does not establish that Reed's conduct lay outside the criminal prohibition.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: its retention of Chapman for the preserved constitutional Doyle claim is distinguished, not displaced; this case decides statutory collateral entitlement rather than the harmlessness of an established constitutional trial error.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's denial of habeas relief is affirmed, 5–4, without adopting the lower courts' distinct statutory-merits grounds. No writ, dismissal, remand, new trial or release is ordered; the conviction and sentence remain undisturbed by this claim.

#### Johnson v. Jones

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-455.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

##### Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal

**Holding:** A denial of qualified immunity is not immediately appealable under §1291 insofar as it determines only whether the pretrial evidence presents a genuine factual dispute about the official's participation in the alleged conduct. Mitchell's immediate appeal remains available for the separable legal question whether the conduct, on the relevant assumed facts, violated clearly established law; the present excessive-force participation dispute does not meet that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A proper legal immunity appeal uses the district court's assumed facts. If unstated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949): applies separateness as a cumulative collateral-order condition.

## Holdings selection: federal-courts / Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299

#### Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1244.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen's cumulative conditions and Mitchell's legal-immunity category apply. Digital Equipment's importance and category discipline remains; Johnson supplies no numerical ceiling and retains its factual boundary.

##### An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, unanimous. Stevens and Breyer condition agreement on an otherwise authorized appeal and do not find successive authorization here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson and Kimberlin preserve assumed-fact review without factual selection. Elder requires relevant conduct-date law but creates no jurisdiction or retrospective notice. Swint rejects convenience-based expansion; Doe's factual-sufficiency exclusion remains.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No immunity, constitutional violation, property interest, factual truth, evidentiary sufficiency or damages issue is decided.

##### A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join; Stevens and Breyer continue to oppose successive appeal as of right.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell protects against defending the claim to which immunity applies; Swint prevents a proper appeal from carrying unrelated matters and leaves its necessary-connected-review reservation open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the categorical Ninth Circuit dismissal and remand to identify proper assumptions favorable to the nonmovant and any actually reviewable legal issue, dismissing factual-sufficiency and otherwise unavailable issues. The summary-judgment denial is not reversed, immunity is not awarded, and neither the separate United States negligence claim nor earlier substitution ruling is reopened. No stay or permission is granted.

### Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Brewer_v_West_Irondequoit_Central_School_District_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Brewer_v_West_Irondequoit_Central_School_District_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Brewer_v_West_Irondequoit_Central_School_District_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The actual exclusion requires substantial justification

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-school program that changes a child's transfer opportunity because of race must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and the classification's coverage, operation and actual individual burdens. It must give a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage; unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, demographic imbalance or a beneficial label does not discharge that burden.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has seven direct joins. Part III has the same coalition. Scalia and Thomas accept the governing standard's binding force but reject this application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the common justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications. Eisenberg applies it to school transfers and requires justification of the individual denial. Haak received written acceptance that was withdrawn after officials verified that she was white. Her lost opportunity is an actual racial burden, unlike Sinkfield's neighboring-district injury theory. The program must explain how its directional transfer restriction serves the educational conditions it identifies and why that child's exclusion has the required relationship to the objective. Brown's prohibition of state-imposed racial caste remains intact.

The inquiry does not demand a commissioned study, fixed ratio, statistical demonstration or proof that every conceivable substitute fails. Testimony, program records, historical experience, admissions, comparisons, observed operation and reasonable inferences may support the relationship. Concrete alternatives must receive the consideration the governing standard requires; an abstract preference for race-neutrality cannot replace that inquiry. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and allocation for its own sake remain impermissible.

#### II. Supported voluntary integration is a permissible educational objective

**Holding and operative rule:** Supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation in public elementary and secondary schools can constitute an important lawful objective without a prior finding of de jure segregation. That permission neither requires racial preferences nor validates a numerical target or the particular transfer restriction: the government must still prove its educational claim, substantial fit and justified individual burdens.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion has six direct joins: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy does not join this affirmative objective holding; his acceptance of Eisenberg's binding force is not an additional affirmative vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Eisenberg recognizes the educational objective and rejects a categorical decree prerequisite. Meaningful educational contact and access can matter independently of an existing judicial dismantlement duty. Absence of a decree establishes neither such a duty nor invalidity. The program's interdistrict design may operate differently from a restriction based solely on county percentages. Its longevity, limited scale and changing demographics are relevant evidence, but do not conclusively establish either constitutional adequacy or the absence of every educational benefit. A potentially lawful objective likewise cannot justify Haak's exclusion without evidence about the program's actual operation. The Constitution permits the supported objective within lawful governmental authority; it supplies no presumption validating this program.

#### III. Correct the preliminary-relief inquiry without ordering placement

**Holding and operative rule:** The vacatur of the mandatory preliminary-admission injunction is affirmed, the appellate judgment is vacated insofar as its substantive directions employ the displaced constitutional standard, and the matter is remanded for prompt application of the common standard and ordinary equitable requirements. Existing supported findings and concessions retain their scope; further evidence or proceedings require an identified unresolved material issue, and any placement order requires current enrollment, continuing injury, educational-disruption and other material equitable facts.

**Authority:** Part III carries the seven-Justice coalition identified for Part I; Scalia and Thomas oppose this disposition because they would treat the preliminary merits prerequisite as already established against the exclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand and Eisenberg require correction of a displaced legal framework without automatically reopening proof or awarding individual relief. The preliminary record does not establish the program's complete justification, but neither does it conclusively disprove its claimed interdistrict educational benefits. State funding requires a qualifying program designed to reduce racial isolation and an annual demonstration. The appellate account of inevitable funding loss is not an express statutory prohibition on every outward transfer by a white city pupil. That distinction bears on the asserted necessity of the categorical eligibility line.

The mandatory-injunction merits requirement and other equitable prerequisites remain independent. The existing irreparable-harm determination does not alone establish entitlement to a mandatory seat order. The earlier conditional protection lasted only through 1999–2000 and only if Haak had begun attending; it creates no January 2001 placement right. The original mandatory injunction is not reinstated, and this Court orders neither admission nor removal. Final program findings and current interim relief are ordinary application and remedial work under the stated rules. Unappealed contract and estoppel grounds, and other claims outside the equal-protection question granted, are not decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: applies its common substantial-justification standard and bounded-remand rule unchanged; no displaced strict-scrutiny formulation is restored.
- Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools: applies its school-transfer standard, supported K–12 educational objective and independent equitable-remedy limits to this interdistrict program; its objective holding is reaffirmed by six Justices.
- Brown v. Board of Education: retains its prohibition of state-imposed racial caste; voluntary integration is not a license for racial hierarchy.
- Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County: applies the requirement of a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives without requiring rejection of every conceivable substitute.
- Sinkfield v. Kelley: distinguishes a claim lacking personal exposure from the withdrawal of this child's actual educational opportunity; the personal-injury requirement remains in force.

### Denius v. Dunlap — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling law](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following public holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### Independent legal consultation cannot be burdened by an unjustified employment condition

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may not condition continued employment on a blanket authorization to disclose confidential communications used to obtain independent legal advice without an adequate employment-related justification for that burden. The rule protects access to legal assistance; it does not make every evidentiary privilege a constitutional right or bar lawful investigation and genuinely pertinent, confined inquiries.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the rule and affirm restoration of the equitable claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Button and Trainmen protect the ability to obtain legal assistance. Shelton's employment-disclosure principle and Pickering's employment balance prevent use of a public job to burden protected independent activity without an adequate reason. The Court applies those protections to the actual demand: the attorney clause reaches independent consultation unrelated to Denius's teaching and burdens his future ability to seek advice. No employment reason for that additional access has been established. Loss of employment for refusing the condition supplies a concrete injury even without proof that officials already obtained a confidential communication.

The employer answers that the earlier cases concerned litigation and association, rather than a general civil privilege. The Court does not turn the privilege into an absolute constitutional shield. Confidential consultation enables people to identify and assert their legal rights; a blanket employment condition reaches that protected activity directly. Ordinary privilege rules and independently lawful investigation continue to operate. A privilege exception does not itself justify an unrelated employment demand.

The form's actual terms govern the condition imposed. An uncommunicated intention to conduct only screening already authorized does not confine those terms. The equitable claim therefore returns for adjudication under this rule. This is First Amendment protection of independent legal assistance, not a Sixth Amendment right without an attached criminal proceeding. No final equitable entitlement or compensable injury is established by recognition of the right alone.

#### An employment demand for intimate medical information requires actual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. The court must identify the actual covered information, its intimacy, the governmental need, and the scope, use and safeguards of the demand; the rule creates no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or mandatory-factor test.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this acquisition protection and conditional equitable remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects objectively intimate personal information against unjustified official dissemination. The personal interest can also be burdened when the government demands access as the price of retaining employment. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection. Doe sustained a confined benefits-related inquiry while assuming the asserted right; it did not authorize access detached from that function. The Court now recognizes the bounded acquisition protection necessary to decide this case.

Relevant fitness verification can serve a legitimate employment need. That possibility does not itself justify unlimited access to treatment, family affairs or other unrelated intimate information. The actual connection and protections must support the demand imposed, and undisclosed benign intentions cannot replace them. Conversely, an administrative imperfection does not alone prove a constitutional violation, and every datum need not be indispensable.

The authorization's one-year duration limits time, not the breadth of its subject matter. Its opening language is broad, but medical records are not expressly enumerated. The district court must determine whether the authorization actually reaches them. If it does, the court applies the rule to covered intimate material and the supported employment justification. Coverage, personal responsibility, causation and appropriate relief remain factual or remedial work under the announced law; they do not make recognition of the right provisional.

#### Financial records are protected only to the extent they reveal objectively intimate matters

**Holding and operative rule:** Financial information falls within the protected personal interest only to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. A court cannot establish constitutional coverage from the financial label, the breadth of an authorization, or the owner's preference for secrecy alone; once protected content is identified, the employer's demand is governed by the same actual-need, scope, use and safeguards rule as other intimate information.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices vacate the categorical financial-privacy determination while preserving adjudication of the equitable claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets grounds protection in objectively intimate life, rather than a general right to keep every record secret. That principle supplies the relevant boundary here. An ordinary loan, credit rating or business record is not constitutionally protected merely because it is financial or confidential. The same kind of record may reveal intimate personal or family matters in a particular instance, and its content then requires the justification and protection the Court has described.

Denius has identified no particular protected financial item. The loss of employment makes his challenge concrete, but does not establish coverage of every financial record. The Court of Appeals therefore could not infer a categorical constitutional interest from the authorization's breadth alone. Its determination is vacated at that point, and the equitable claim returns for identification of the information and application of the governing rule. This is an identified content boundary, not a reservation whether objectively intimate financial material can ever be protected. Other undeveloped informational categories and the separate release-of-liability theories were not preserved for this review and are not decided.

#### Conduct-date qualified immunity bars the attorney and financial damages claims

**Holding and operative rule:** An official remains protected from personal damages when all relevant law at the time of the challenged conduct did not give sufficiently particularized objective notice that the conduct violated the asserted right; an identical prior case is unnecessary, but later recognition of a right supplies no retrospective notice. The 1996 law did not sufficiently establish these legal-consultation and intimate-financial acquisition extensions on this authorization, so the judgments granting personal damages immunity on those two claims are affirmed.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all eight Associate Justices adopt the rule and both immunity judgments. Stone-Zsela dissents on these components.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve the objective, particularized notice inquiry. Elder requires independent consideration of all relevant preconduct authority rather than only the cases a party happened to cite below. Crawford-El preserves that legal defense alongside ordinary proof, and Wasson confirms that an unsettled constitutional extension does not become actionable for damages merely because a present court could adopt it.

Button's access-to-counsel cases did not sufficiently establish this general employment-condition application in 1996. The financial precedents likewise did not give adequate notice of the protected reach now identified. Sheets's intimate-publication holding did not automatically decide acquisition or every financial record. Neither the 1997 Doe and Crawford-El decisions nor the present decision can supply 1996 notice.

The Court rejects a requirement that officials prove actual reliance on binding authority directly permitting their conduct. That would replace the objective immunity defense, not apply it. Constitutional violation, contemporaneous notice and injury remain distinct. The retained defense protects these two damages components; it does not justify dismissal of a separately available equitable claim, convert the demand into lawful conduct, or supply immunity to an entity or claim outside its personal-damages scope.

#### The medical damages claim presents a different notice question

**Holding and operative rule:** If the authorization reaches objectively intimate medical information, the existing summary-judgment record does not support personal damages immunity merely because no previous decision addressed this exact form. The cumulative law before the 1996 demand gave notice that an unlimited civilian-medical access condition, unsupported by an articulated countervailing employment purpose, could not be justified solely by public-employer status; medical coverage and genuinely supported factual or independent defenses remain for adjudication.

**Authority:** Part IV of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt this conduct-date notice ground. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh vote for the conditional medical damages remand but does not join this rationale. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** Elder requires consideration of the cumulative conduct-date law. Pesce recognized the constitutional significance of employment-conditioned confidential disclosure while sustaining justified child-abuse reporting. Schaill recognized substantial medical confidentiality while sustaining limited disclosure to an appropriate official for a concrete program need. Anderson v. Romero distinguished the recognized civilian medical interest from the unsettled prisoner claim on which it granted immunity. Considered with Whalen and the then-effective Sheets holding, those decisions gave notice of the no-justification boundary relevant here.

The contrary argument correctly observes that those cases involved different circumstances, often upheld disclosure, and did not decide this unexecuted authorization. Those limits do not erase the distinction between justified, confined medical access and an unlimited demand supported only by the existence of public employment. The Court does not treat the circuit cases as controlling Supreme Court holdings or assert that they adjudicated this form.

Actual medical coverage is disputed, and no personal liability follows before it is established. The undisclosed narrow screening intention cannot supply justification for the broader written condition. A genuinely supported material employment justification, personal causation, injury and other proper defenses remain subject to ordinary proceedings. The judgment restores the medical damages claim for that work; it neither finds a violation after trial nor awards compensation. The rule announced today is not itself the source of historical notice.

#### Precedent treatment

- NAACP v. Button and Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen v. Virginia: extend protection of obtaining legal assistance to this unjustified compulsory employment authorization; ordinary privilege and investigation rules remain distinct.
- Shelton v. Tucker: applies the principle that an employment disclosure condition requires justification for its actual burden on protected independent activity.
- Pickering v. Board of Education: applies the relationship between the employee's protected activity and the employer's actual functional justification.
- Connick v. Myers: preserves contextual public-employment analysis; supplies no categorical permission to demand all independent legal confidences.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applies its requirement that a public employer justify the actual unrelated burden; the present rule does not import a universal higher test for every advance employment policy.
- Waters v. Churchill: preserves its genuine-ground distinction and separate causation and immunity inquiries; an undisclosed intention does not alter the written condition at issue.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County: preserves its intimate-publication holding and objectively intimate-content boundary; develops protection against an unjustified employment demand for access without converting Sheets itself into an earlier acquisition holding.
- Whalen v. Roe: preserves legitimate safeguarded collection; its reservation of possible abuses does not foreclose the bounded acquisition right recognized here.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority: preserves its assumed-right disposition of confined benefits administration; distinguishes that supported function from the unrestricted additional access demanded here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the Court declines to replace it with an actual-authoritative-reliance defense.
- Elder v. Holloway: applies independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date authority, without requiring an identical case or importing later notice.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserves ordinary proof, actual causation and lawful procedural screening alongside immunity; its later date supplies no 1996 notice.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves distinct present constitutional merits and contemporaneous notice; its unresolved attributed-speech question does not decide the different medical-law notice record here.
- Pesce v. J. Sterling Morton High School District 201, Schaill v. Tippecanoe County School Corp., and Anderson v. Romero: considered as preconduct circuit authority for the cumulative civilian-medical notice inference, with their justified-disclosure and prisoner-specific limitations retained; none becomes a controlling Supreme Court precedent by that use.

#### Law after decision

Public employment cannot be conditioned on an unjustified blanket surrender of independent legal consultation. Compulsory access to objectively intimate medical information and comparably intimate content in financial records requires an actual legitimate employment connection and reasonably fitted scope, use and protection. General financial secrecy, a disclosure label and a confidentiality preference do not suffice. Actual coverage and content must be identified.

This decision recognizes the bounded acquisition protection left undecided in Doe and distinguishes it from Sheets's public-dissemination holding. The ordinary conduct-date qualified-immunity defense remains in force. Attorney and financial damages immunity is affirmed, while the medical damages claim returns on the distinct cumulative notice ground and actual-coverage condition. Personal immunity does not eliminate live equitable claims. No fractured-decision question or new mandatory scrutiny formula is created.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The particular preservation need and limited execution justify this restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers may temporarily restrict unaccompanied entry into a home while obtaining a warrant when particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual risk of evidence destruction, a restraint reasonably confined to preservation and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization together justify that intrusion. The approximately two-hour restriction here satisfies those conditions in combination; probable cause, refusal of consent, awareness of an investigation, disposable drugs, jailability or duration alone creates no automatic exception or safe harbor.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Stone-Zsela dissents; Stevens would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted and, because the Court reaches the merits, would affirm suppression. Souter's additional concurrence does not limit his full join.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton preserves the home's special warrant protection. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not authorize a greater residential intrusion, and Bond requires an independent basis for an added intrusion despite lawful presence. Edmond addresses suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints; its program-purpose threshold supplies no rule for this individualized preservation measure.

Probable cause was uncontested. McArthur's wife reported seeing him slide drugs under the couch; he knew officers were investigating and had ready access to the identified disposable evidence. These particular circumstances reasonably supported a destruction concern without requiring police to wait for an observed attempt. Refusal to consent supplies neither guilt nor an independent exigency. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another restrained unaccompanied reentry. McArthur made two or three entries for cigarettes and telephone use with observation just inside the doorway. The officers conducted no evidentiary search before the warrant. Their approximately two-hour effort was diligent and the actual restriction reasonably preserved the evidence pending a magistrate's decision.

Place gives duration and diligence independent importance; no fixed two-hour allowance follows. Welsh requires attention to offense gravity. These offenses were modest but jailable: up to thirty days for the marijuana count and one year for paraphernalia. That distinguishes Welsh's civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest setting without making jailability sufficient. The substantial home interest and modest offenses weigh against restraint, but the actual preservation need and limited warrant-directed execution make this measure reasonable.

Segura's independent-source result does not validate every earlier seizure, and its broader Part IV discussion had only two votes. The Court relies on the justification before restraint, not the later discoveries. Limited observational consent is not general search consent or abandonment of the home-access objection. No general power to occupy or search homes, police-created unlawful exigency, requirement to exhaust every imaginable alternative or universal warrant-exception checklist is decided.

#### II. The presented suppression ground cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Illinois appellate judgment is reversed insofar as suppression rests on this temporary restriction of residential access, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings. Any actually preserved independent warrant, admissibility or exclusionary-rule issue remains governed by its own law; none is found established or newly created here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court treated the temporary restraint as an unlawful seizure and suppressed the later warrant evidence on that ground. Because the actual restraint was justified, that ground fails. The Court does not give the later warrant retroactive force, independently resolve every feature of that warrant or determine guilt. Correction of this suppression ground requires further proceedings, not an automatic conviction, dismissal of charges, release, new trial or new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: preserves the home's warrant protection; no general residential-entry authority is created.
- Whren v. United States: preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis and independent limits on greater intrusion, scope and duration; traffic probable cause is not residential authority.
- Bond v. United States: applies the requirement of independent justification for an added intrusion; lawful presence and limited observation do not imply consent to search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond: distinguishes suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints from this individualized preservation measure; its program-purpose holding remains intact.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: applies attention to actual duration and diligence in temporary preservation; no fixed permissible interval is transferred from luggage to a home.
- Welsh v. Wisconsin, 466 U.S. 740: preserves the importance of offense gravity and home protection; distinguishes its civil, nonjailable nighttime arrest without making every jailable offense sufficient for restraint.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796: preserves its independent-source holding while declining to treat it as approval of every initial seizure; the broader securing-premises discussion in Part IV remains a two-Justice position, not a majority holding.

### Cook v. Gralike — 2001-02-28

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Cook_v_Gralike_merits_2001-02-28.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Cook_v_Gralike_merits_2001-02-28.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Official congressional ballots cannot impose policy-compliance penalties

**Holding and operative rule:** The Elections Clause does not authorize a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage candidates because they refuse a prescribed position or pledge concerning future federal legislation. Adverse labels triggered by noncompliance with the State's selected policy exceed election-manner authority even when the candidate remains eligible and the name stays on the ballot.

**Authority:** Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt both the constitutional source rule and its application. Thomas joins the application on the parties' accepted source premise, giving the application eight supporters without adding a vote for the source conclusion. O'Connor supplies no structural vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Thornton establishes that States cannot add congressional qualifications and distinguishes genuine administration from substantive candidate restrictions. Its national-office source rule requires authority over congressional balloting to come from constitutional delegation, rather than a reserved power over federal offices that did not previously exist. The seven Justices joining that explanation apply it here; Thomas accepts the parties' premise for this litigation while preserving his contrary original position.

The manner power is broad enough to administer election machinery, but not to favor candidates who support a State's desired constitutional amendment and disfavor those who do not. Rednour protects actual nomination procedure, not every rule called procedural. These labels single out compliance with a political program at the moment of voting. They neither neutrally identify candidates nor protect ballot integrity. The absence of complete exclusion distinguishes this instrument from Thornton without making the disadvantage lawful. No quantified proof of lost votes is necessary to identify the official penalty.

Anderson v. Martin illustrates how the official ballot can impose a particular electoral disadvantage. The sufficient ground here is lack of delegated ballot authority, not a general public-forum theory or a separate Court speech holding. The labels fail even assuming the enactment could otherwise exercise the manner power, so the referendum/legislature dispute need not be resolved. The Court decides no general prohibition on private advocacy, petitions, memorials or genuinely nonbinding political instructions, and no rule against unrelated neutral ballot information.

#### II. The reviewed injunction remains in force at its actual scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment preserving the injunction against Article VIII sections 15–19 is affirmed. Because the State defended the enactment in its entirety and sought no alternative severance if that defense failed, the Court leaves the reviewed decree undisturbed rather than substituting a new label-only decree or independently deciding the validity of every nonbinding instruction.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly support this decree and its stated scope ground. Stone-Zsela directly supports the label relief and agrees only in the judgment as to the residual sections 15–16 injunction. The disposition is unanimous; this complete remedial explanation has eight direct supporters.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court enjoined sections 15–19, and the court of appeals declined a proposed severance retaining other provisions after removing sections 17–19. The State's defense here presented the enactment as a whole. Affirmance therefore preserves the decree that was actually reviewed. It does not silently replace it with a newly selected set of invalid provisions.

Section 19's candidate and voter label-review routes and its clear-and-convincing-evidence requirement have a function distinct from section 20's automatic repeal upon ratification of the specified federal amendment, section 21's forum provision and section 22's severability clause. The existence of a severability clause alone selects no new remedial division. The Court announces no fresh Missouri-law severability rule and does not convert the absence of an alternative request into a concession that each section is independently unconstitutional. Private and genuinely nonbinding advocacy remain outside the constitutional prohibition stated in Holding I.

**Precedent treatment:**

- U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton: extends the distinction between genuine election procedure and substantive candidate disadvantage to adverse policy-compliance labels; its exclusive-qualifications and national-office source holdings remain in force.
- Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour: distinguishes genuine support and nomination procedure from the official political penalty here; ordinary access regulation retains its separate constitutional review.
- Anderson v. Martin: applies the official ballot's capacity to impose a candidate disadvantage; the Court's sufficient rule here rests on the Elections Clause.
- Burdick v. Takushi: preserves calibrated review of actual electoral burdens and legitimate administration; it supplies no authority for official favoritism among substantive political positions.

### United States v. Hilario — 2001-03-19

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/United_States_v_Hilario_merits_2001-03-19.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/United_States_v_Hilario_merits_2001-03-19.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Independent departmental authority supports the appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** An asserted defect in the United States Attorney's appointment does not invalidate this appeal where an independently appointed assistant and the Department's own litigation authority support the notice and appeal. The assistant's section 542 appointment, section 516's departmental authority, the Solicitor General's criminal-appeal authority and Rule 3(c)'s absence of a required personal United States Attorney signature defeat Hilario's notice objection independently of Gil's appointment validity.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion supplies the Court's notice holding, which is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of this objection without joining its ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Hilario treats the challenged United States Attorney as the only possible source of appellate authority. The notice also bears the assistant's signature, and section 542 makes the assistant's appointment independent. Section 516 reserves federal litigation to Justice Department officers under the Attorney General, subject to statutory exceptions; the Solicitor General controls the criminal-appeal decision under 28 C.F.R. 0.20(b). Rule 3(c) does not make the United States Attorney's personal signature indispensable. The alleged appointment defect therefore does not defeat this properly supported appeal. This conclusion concerns appellate authority alone; it establishes no independent authorization of the indictment and no later ratification.

#### The judicial appointment continues until the vacancy is filled

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the contemporaneous section 546(d), when the Attorney General's interim appointment expires after 120 days under section 546(c)(2), the district court may appoint a United States Attorney to serve until the vacancy is filled. Section 541's ordinary four-year term does not displace that express endpoint, and Gil's judicial appointment did not terminate merely because four years passed.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support the direct section 546(d) holding in the opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified distinct appointment routes and stopping conditions. Section 546(c) limits the Attorney General's appointee to the earlier of qualification of a presidential appointee or 120 days; subsection (d) then permits judicial appointment until the vacancy is filled. Importing the ordinary four-year term into that route would replace its explicit duration. The concern that prolonged vacancies may delay nomination and confirmation is serious, but it does not authorize a court to rewrite the endpoint. The ordinary presidential appointment route and presidential removal under section 541(c) remain available throughout. Section 546(b)'s prohibition on an Attorney General appointment of a person whom the Senate rejected remains distinct; the Court neither extends nor eliminates it. The judicial appointment order must still be filed with the clerk under subsection (d); no filing defect is presented. Later amendments have no part in this construction.

#### Independently, section 541's holdover defeats automatic expiration under the proposed four-year reading

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming section 541(b)'s four-year language applies to the judicial appointee, the same subsection directs the United States Attorney to continue performing the office's duties until a successor is appointed and qualifies. An anniversary alone therefore does not terminate authority on Hilario's proposed reading when no successor has replaced Gil.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this independently sufficient statutory alternative in the Court's opinion. Stone-Zsela does not join this alternative ground; he joins the direct vacancy-endpoint holding and judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The broad introductory language on which Hilario relies cannot be read apart from its express continuation command. Applying the four-year clause would also require giving effect to the holdover clause, which prevents the claimed automatic cessation. This alternative does not expand the Attorney General's 120-day appointment power or replace section 546(d)'s separate endpoint. It supplies an additional answer to the particular asserted expiration, without making continued service a cure for an unconstitutional appointment.

#### Actual combined executive controls make this office inferior

**Holding and operative rule:** United States Attorneys, including this judicial appointee, are inferior officers under the actual statutory structure because the Attorney General directs their duties and litigation, may take or reassign cases, and the President retains removal. Substantial district-level prosecutorial duties and long tenure do not make the office independent of those combined controls; no single supervisory, review or removal power is held sufficient for every federal office.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the Court's combined-control rationale. Souter supports inferior status through the distinct ground in his concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** Edmond requires examination of the office's actual statutory hierarchy, rather than treating significant duties or an interim title as decisive. Sections 516 and 519 place litigation and the prosecutor's duties under the Attorney General's direction; section 518 permits executive takeover or reassignment of a case. Section 541(c) gives removal to the President, not a general discharge power to the Attorney General. Together, these controls leave the United States Attorney's substantial authority subject to executive superiors. Long service does not withdraw any of those powers. Edmond's military-review arrangement is not a checklist transplanted to prosecution; the legal controls appropriate to this office do the work. Weiss preserves the distinction between lawful appointment and later assignment. The ordinary nomination route remains available, but the office's inferior status rests on its actual subordinate organization rather than an assumption that replacement will occur promptly.

#### Judicial selection does not transfer prosecution to the courts

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may vest appointment of this inferior executive officer in the district court under the Appointments Clause's court-of-law option when selection gives the judges no charging, litigation, removal or supervisory authority over the officer's execution of duties. The court's later hearing of cases brought by its appointee and the absence of automatic statutory disqualification do not alone establish unconstitutional partiality or an impermissible merger of judicial and executive power.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the Court's interbranch holding, including its distinct judicial-integrity analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Constitution expressly permits Congress to choose courts of law to appoint inferior officers. Morrison and Ex parte Siebold support that option within their holdings, while Morrison and Mistretta require attention to incompatibility and judicial integrity. Section 546(d) differs from Morrison's arrangement: appointment belongs to the district court as a court, and the statute does not automatically exclude appointing judges from later cases involving the appointee. Those features warrant consideration but do not give judges control of prosecutions or a continuing partisan interest in their outcome. Appointment selects the officer; execution remains subject to executive direction and presidential removal. Functional examination confirms that separation rather than substituting a balance of convenience for constitutional limits. No actual judicial direction, coercion or bias is established here. A supported claim of that kind remains governed by its own facts and ordinary disqualification law; this holding creates no exemption from those rules.

#### Prolonged tenure alone does not invalidate the appointment

**Holding and operative rule:** More than six years without presidential replacement does not by itself invalidate a judicial appointment that remains within section 546(d)'s vacancy condition and the office's constitutionally sufficient executive controls. Passage of time neither converts this regular inferior office into a principal office nor authorizes a new judicially imposed term limit.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support rejection of the duration-only as-applied challenge; Souter reaches the classification premise through his separate fuller inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The challenge concerns a regular office whose powers remain subordinate, not an independent principal office temporarily occupied by an acting inferior officer. The vacancy endpoint, presidential removal and ordinary appointment process continue to operate after six years. Institutional concern about delay cannot erase those controls or amend the statutory duration. This conclusion neither excuses a genuine change in the office's legal control nor validates an independently defective act. Ryder preserves meaningful relief for qualifying timely appointment defects; it does not make longevity a cure or automatically require dismissal of an indictment issued by a prosecutor. Because the appointment is valid on the presented grounds, the Government's preserved argument against dismissal even assuming invalidity is unnecessary to decide. No independent indictment authorization, ratification or harmlessness finding is made.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Weiss preserves the distinction between appointment and assignment; its commissioned-officer holding does not itself authorize this separate office.
- Edmond's actual combined-control inquiry is applied to the prosecutorial hierarchy. The Court adopts no universal single-factor classification rule.
- Morrison and Ex parte Siebold support Congress's court-appointment option within their scope; Morrison and Mistretta also require the distinct judicial-integrity inquiry applied here.
- Ryder retains meaningful relief for a qualifying timely substantial appointment defect. It supplies neither a long-service cure nor automatic dismissal of a prosecutor's indictment; no defect requiring its remedy is established.

### Wallis v. Spencer — 2001-04-02

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Emergency removal requires an immediate danger and a response confined to it

**Holding and operative rule:** Unilateral, nonconsensual removal of children from parental custody without prior judicial authorization requires specific, articulable information giving objectively reasonable grounds to believe that imminent serious bodily harm requires action before authorization can reasonably be obtained; the removal's scope and duration must be reasonably directed to that danger. Genuine disputes about the information, urgency and scope here prevent summary judgment for the City on the preserved removal claim, without establishing a constitutional violation or municipal liability.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II.A and III, and concurs only in the judgment on Parts II.B and IV. All nine directly adopt this removal holding and support the complete partial affirmance, partial vacatur and remand. The removal rule states necessary protections for the intervention presented, not an exhaustive test for every parental-right claim; no combination of separate rationales supplies its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The children's Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable seizure and the parents' Fourteenth Amendment family interests are distinct. Troxel protects parental direction against displacement by ordinary governmental preference but supplies no complete custody test. McArthur requires attention to actual need, execution, duration and available judicial authorization; its limited residential restraint does not itself authorize this greater bodily and familial intrusion.

The reported threat to kill Jessie at the equinox was grave. Transmission through a therapist and protective-services personnel could support prompt protective action without certainty of abuse. Neither the report's later disproof nor the aunt's psychiatric treatment alone defeats that possibility. But the disclosed reliability problems, earlier unfounded report, disputed therapist communications, investigation actually performed and children's observed condition cannot be resolved against the family on summary judgment. No judge issued a removal order. Whether CPS reported one or requested removal, and what the officers knew, remain disputed.

The allegation did not implicate Becky or threaten Lauren. Those differences and practical protective alternatives bear on the intrusion's scope; they do not establish that another placement was actually available. A date-specific threat cannot by itself justify indefinite separation. Permission to enter the home does not establish consent to removal or examinations.

Courts determine the governing law and may decide sufficiency on undisputed facts. The Court does not assign every reasonable-cause question to a jury, impose a fixed corroboration category, require exhaustion of every imaginable alternative, or determine credibility here. Nor does it establish a universal unfitness prerequisite, strict-scrutiny framework or equivalence of the two Amendments. The legal consequence of mistaken reliance on a reported order remains unresolved; the described appellate omission establishes no forfeiture at every stage.

**Precedent treatment:** Troxel's protected parental direction is applied at its controlling scope; its separate broader frameworks remain noncontrolling. McArthur's attention to actual need, scope and duration is applied without transferring its particular residential authorization to child removal.

#### II.A. Municipal responsibility requires the City's own actionable policy, fault and causal connection

**Holding and operative rule:** A section 1983 municipal claim requires a federal deprivation caused by the City's own attributable policy or legally effective custom and the fault required by the theory actually presented; a training, supervision or safeguards omission requires deliberate indifference, supported notice and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing the injury. The testimony connecting an asserted settled removal practice to this intervention defeats the categorical conclusion that no policy evidence exists; the remaining elements are unproved.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices directly join this holding and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Collins separates a federal deprivation from municipal responsibility. Monell rejects employer-based federal liability. Carter applies Canton to deliberate municipal omissions: notice may arise from an established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances. Prior identical incidents are not invariably required. Negligence, employee error and hindsight that further training might have helped do not suffice.

The City correctly argues that an officer's mistake or outside misinformation alone cannot establish its responsibility. Here, however, a supervisor and the designated knowledgeable officer described a settled practice of accepting reported orders without verification. Evidence connects reliance on the supposed order to removal and the absence of further investigation. That permits the policy claim to proceed; it does not establish that the practice was unconstitutional, that every witness possessed final policymaking authority, or that the City caused every later injury.

An affirmative policy and an omission remain different theories. The latter requires proof of deliberate indifference, notice, the particular deficiency and actual causation; the duration of a practice alone proves none of those elements. Final authority depends on the legally effective allocation of power for the challenged function, not operational discretion alone. Interagency cooperation does not make county personnel or a physician City employees. No universal paper-order inspection requirement or prohibition of telephone communication follows.

Ordinary pleading and proof controls continue, without a special requirement to plead inaccessible internal operations in heightened detail.

**Precedent treatment:** Collins and Monell preserve separate deprivation and attribution requirements. Carter and Canton govern omission fault, notice and causation; McMillian governs function-specific final authority. Leatherman preserves ordinary municipal pleading.

#### II.B. The City cannot borrow personal or state immunity against section 1983

**Holding and operative rule:** The City cannot defeat its own federal municipal liability by borrowing an individual's personal qualified or absolute immunity or by invoking state immunity against section 1983. Rejection of those categorical defenses is affirmed without deciding any absent individual's immunity or liability.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in this disposition without adding grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Carter separates municipal responsibility from the personal immunity an officer may possess. A defense protecting an individual's performance of a function does not exempt the City's own actionable policy from federal law. State immunity likewise does not displace the federal municipal claim.

The City is the only remaining defendant in this appeal. Its inability to borrow these defenses does not adjudicate an officer's immunity or restore a personal claim. Denius preserves particularized objective notice from the relevant law when the individual acted; an identical earlier case is unnecessary, but Ferguson in 2001 cannot supply September 1991 notice. The Court resolves only the City's categorical defenses, leaving the constitutional deprivation, institutional fault, causation and injury requirements intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Carter's separation of municipal responsibility and personal immunity is applied. Denius's conduct-date notice rule remains intact and supplies no retrospective notice or municipal immunity.

#### III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible

**Holding and operative rule:** The independent City examination-policy adjudication and its associated constitutional rules are vacated insofar as they conclusively govern the remand; the lower court must first determine that theory's actual presentation and scope under ordinary procedural rules before adjudicating its independent merits. Examination injuries caused by a proved removal-policy violation do not invariably require a second examination policy, but the underlying wrong, actual causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must still be established separately for each claimed consequence.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this threshold and causation holding. No independent examination merits rule is decided. Stone-Zsela's concurrence states his own conditional bodily-integrity rule and receives no other join.

**Controlling explanation:** The examinations were substantial bodily intrusions. The district court addressed them, the report identified the City as requesting agency, and testimony concerned the detective and hospital contract. Both questions were granted. These circumstances support arguments about presentation and attribution; they do not resolve the specific objection that an independent City examination-policy claim was never properly presented. Review does not amend a complaint. Neither a lower dissent nor the absence of original filings here establishes forfeiture.

The proper course is to determine that threshold without conclusively imposing the lower court's independent examination, notice, opportunity-to-object or parental-presence rules. Even its presence rule permitted exclusion for a valid reason with nearby presence instead. Today's judgment finds neither preservation nor waiver and neither constitutional nor unconstitutional examinations.

Ferguson requires an adequate justification for the particular prosecution operation it addressed, while preserving genuinely different clinical functions and consent issues. It supplies no universal mixed-purpose prohibition or three-permission formula. Denius's public-employment information ruling supplies no bodily-examination protocol. The present record establishes no urgent medical need, imminent evidence loss, valid substitute consent or waiver; protective custody alone cannot establish those missing predicates. The Court nonetheless leaves the independent merits question for a properly presented claim. The 1998 California examination enactment does not authorize the 1991 acts.

The removal claim's possible consequential-injury route remains distinct, as the lower opinion's note 15 recognized. Failure of a separate examination-policy theory would not automatically extinguish that route; sequence alone does not prove it. Initial removal, prehearing examinations and posthearing separation require their own causal analysis. A later medical report and judicial intervention neither automatically sever nor establish City responsibility for the entire two-and-one-half-month separation. Ordinary proceedings supply this inquiry, not a mandated new evidentiary hearing or permission to manufacture a new claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Ferguson and Denius are preserved within their distinct operational and informational scopes; neither decides this unadjudicated independent claim. Monell and Carter continue to require an actual municipal causal connection even when a second policy is unnecessary to recover consequential injury.

#### IV. The state claims return under their own liability and immunity rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The surviving California claims return within their existing scope for ordinary application of state liability and immunity law; federal restrictions on municipal respondeat-superior liability and borrowed personal immunity do not replace that separate state inquiry. Disputed CPS enlistment and the character of the challenged acts prevent disposition here under Government Code section 820.2, while the lower court's rejection of the reply-only section 821.6 theory is not reopened.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this limited state-law ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in the state-law remand without adding grounds; no independent constitutional examination holding is supplied through it.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 815.2(a)'s employee-based state liability and section 815.2(b)'s derivative immunity operate under their own terms. Section 820.2 protects an act or omission resulting from vested discretion even if that discretion is abused. Newton and Alicia T., as applied below, protect child-abuse investigation and public employees reasonably enlisted to assist, including the decision to investigate and actions necessary for a meaningful investigation. Protection does not extend to nondiscretionary acts or at least some intentional torts, including battery and false imprisonment under Newton. Whether CPS enlisted these officers, whether particular conduct involved discretion, and which pleaded tort claims fall outside protection remain unresolved. A claimed mandatory policy does not become discretionary merely because the setting involves child protection.

The lower court left the tort-specific limits for initial determination below. This Court neither extends that immunity nor decides battery, abduction, assault or intentional-infliction liability. Its rejection of the section 821.6 contention rested on reply-only presentation and the City's disclaimer of participation in the dependency prosecution; neither is reopened. Preserved state examination issues remain governed by their actual scope and state law, without importing the vacated independent federal examination rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Newton v. County of Napa and Alicia T. v. County of Los Angeles retain their stated state-law roles in the remand; neither becomes a new federal immunity rule. Monell's federal rejection of respondeat superior does not displace the distinct California statutory route.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Troxel v. Granville, 530 U.S. 57:** Applies protected parental direction against displacement by ordinary governmental preference. No complete custody framework, unfitness prerequisite, general harm formula or scrutiny level is imported from its separate positions.
- **Illinois v. McArthur, decided February 20, 2001:** Applies attention to actual justification, scope, duration and judicial authorization; its confined residential restraint supplies no automatic authority for removing children or examining their bodies.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115:** Applies the distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility; institutional fault cannot substitute for a constitutional injury.
- **Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658:** Applies the municipality's own policy or legally effective custom and causal responsibility; section 1983 supplies no respondeat-superior liability.
- **Carter v. City of Philadelphia, decided March 13, 2000:** Applies policy, omission fault, notice, identified deficiency and causation separately; a municipality cannot borrow personal immunity, and institutional cooperation alone creates no employment relationship.
- **City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378:** Preserves deliberate indifference and actual causation for training or supervision omissions; neither negligence nor hindsight suffices, and identical prior incidents are not universally necessary.
- **McMillian v. Monroe County, decided June 2, 1997:** Preserves the inquiry into legally effective final authority for the challenged function; operational discretion alone does not establish it.
- **Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163:** Preserves ordinary municipal pleading without heightened internal-policy particulars; ordinary discovery and proof requirements remain.
- **Denius v. Dunlap, decided January 17, 2001:** Preserves particularized conduct-date personal-immunity notice and the distinction between justified intimate-information acquisition and an unrestricted employment demand. Neither supplies a bodily-examination protocol or municipal immunity.
- **Ferguson v. City of Charleston, decided March 21, 2001:** Preserves the confined actual-operation ruling and unresolved consent inquiry; it establishes no categorical mixed-purpose prohibition, universal three-permission rule or independent holding on these examinations.
- **Newton v. County of Napa, 266 Cal. Rptr. 682; Alicia T. v. County of Los Angeles, 271 Cal. Rptr. 513:** Retain their state-law treatment of discretionary child-protection investigation and enlisted assistance, with the nondiscretionary and tort-specific limits reserved below. Neither alters federal municipal liability.

**Resulting law:** Unilateral emergency removal must rest on particularized grounds of imminent serious bodily harm requiring action before judicial authorization can reasonably be obtained, with scope and duration fitted to that danger. This necessary protection does not establish a comprehensive family-law scrutiny standard. The City's own actionable policy or omission, fault and actual causal responsibility remain separate from both a constitutional deprivation and an individual's immunity.

No Supreme Court merits rule governs the independent examination claim from this disposition. Its presentation must be determined before adjudication. A separate examination policy is not invariably needed for examination injury actually caused by a proved removal-policy violation; neither chronology nor judicial intervention alone resolves causation. The California claims retain their separate statutory liability and immunity rules. Stone-Zsela's conditional examination standard remains a concurrence.

### Atwater v. City of Lago Vista — 2001-04-24

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Atwater_v_City_of_Lago_Vista_merits_2001-04-24.md#holdings).

#### Additional custody for a known fine-only criminal offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor does not, by itself, justify additional station-house transport and confinement. That added custody must reasonably serve an actual enforcement function, considering supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence-protection or necessary-processing needs; the examples are nonexclusive, are not presumed, and require neither the least intrusive imaginable alternative nor citation whenever conceivable.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to this rule, the rejection of categorical summary judgment and the common mandate. The rule extends Ricci's civil-licensing holding to the fine-only criminal enforcement presented here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority to stop a person and the reasonable extent of custody are different questions. Ricci requires an actual enforcement connection for added station custody after a fine-only civil violation. The criminal label does not, by itself, justify the same added restraint where imprisonment is unavailable as punishment. The State's penalty choice reinforces the need to assess the scale of the seizure, without granting immunity from arrest.

Whren preserves an ordinary initial stop on objective probable cause while retaining independent limits on greater restraint. Knowles keeps search consequences separate from the antecedent lawful-arrest question. Robinson and Gustafson presuppose a qualifying lawful arrest; their incident-search rules cannot supply the missing justification for disputed custody. Wilson v. Arkansas treats relevant history as part of reasonableness without constitutionalizing every state authorization. A categorical historical breach-of-peace rule is unnecessary to decide this actual-function question.

Clear administration is a legitimate interest. But optional booking paperwork cannot conclusively establish the need for the custody that creates it. The inquiry permits practical judgments without hindsight perfection or proof that every alternative would fail. The lower court's probable-cause rule omitted that inquiry.

The record must include both the missing physical documents and the supplied license number, address and known local residence. The earlier warning followed recognition that the child was belted; it does not establish another seatbelt offense. The friend took the children. These facts cannot be replaced by assumptions of failed verification, flight risk, continuing danger or compulsory station processing. Neither berating nor a routine booking preference alone supplies an enforcement need. The correct rule defeats defendants' categorical entitlement to judgment, while the Justices differ on the ultimate application and the remaining record determinations.

#### The common judgment does not establish a controlling application conclusion

**Judgment and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit judgment sustaining the categorical custody summary judgment is vacated, and the custody claim returns for proceedings under the actual-enforcement rule. The return permits genuinely supported disputes and preserved defenses, requires no new evidentiary hearing, invents no enforcement justification and reopens no established fact by speculation; it does not invalidate the uncontested seatbelt convictions or award damages.

**Authority and application limits:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer expressly support this vacatur and remand. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer also conclude that the supported plaintiff-favorable account does not establish a sufficient custody function and would reverse on that ground. Stone-Zsela does not join that application: he requires actual verification and custody-need determinations under the rule. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the arrest reasonable on their stated grounds and would affirm. There is no majority holding that this custody was constitutional or unconstitutional; the four-Justice application and the distinct concurrence cannot be combined through Marks into one.

Personal qualified immunity remains a separate conduct-date inquiry using March 1997 law; Ricci, Knowles, McArthur and this decision supply no retrospective notice. Municipal liability separately requires an attributable policy or custom, applicable fault, causation and injury. No final finding resolves those predicates. Other punishment classes are outside the presented question. Ordinary application of the rule and liability work under it are distinct from the Court's lack of a controlling answer on this custody's ultimate reasonableness.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights, May 4, 1998:** Its actual-enforcement requirement is extended from fine-only civil licensing custody to the added custody for a known fine-only criminal offense. Its rejection of categorical arrest immunity, least restrictive means and a universal citation duty remains. Its record-specific application fracture supplies no automatic application answer here.
- **Whren v. United States, June 10, 1996:** Its objective initial-stop rule is preserved and its independent limits on greater restraint applied. Hostility does not replace the objective enforcement inquiry.
- **Knowles v. Iowa, December 8, 1998:** Its distinction between actual lawful custody and search consequences is applied; it supplies no authority to justify an arrest by its ensuing search or booking effects.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their incident-search rules remain conditioned on a qualifying lawful arrest. Neither decides the contested antecedent custody authority here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas, May 22, 1995:** Relevant history remains part of constitutional reasonableness, without turning state arrest authorization into a complete federal justification.
- **Illinois v. McArthur, February 20, 2001:** Its particularized residential-preservation holding remains at that scope. Concrete need and fitted execution inform the comparison; its home setting and approximate duration create no traffic-booking permission or safe harbor.
- **Wilson v. Layne; Wallis v. Spencer:** Conduct-date immunity and independent municipal responsibility remain distinct from constitutional merits. Neither supplies a present finding of immunity, City policy, fault or causation here.

**Resulting law:** Ricci's actual-enforcement rule now governs added station transport and confinement for known fine-only criminal offenses as well as its prior civil setting. Probable cause and local arrest permission alone do not establish reasonableness; genuinely supported enforcement needs and the scale of restraint must be assessed. Neither a universal citation requirement nor categorical arrest immunity follows.

The Court supplies no controlling application conclusion about this custody, immunity or municipal liability. The four-Justice conclusion that the accepted account is constitutionally insufficient remains noncontrolling; the actual-function rule and bounded corrective mandate control further proceedings. Ordinary fact, defense and remedial work must respect the supported record rather than manufacture new justification.

### Sterling v. Borough of Minersville — 2001-04-24

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Sterling_v_Borough_of_Minersville_merits_2001-04-24.md#holdings).

#### I. An operative official ultimatum can invade intimate confidentiality

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer violates the Fourteenth Amendment protection of intimate confidentiality by imposing a credible official ultimatum requiring an adult to disclose suspected sexual orientation to a family member or face disclosure by the officer, when no legitimate governmental purpose supports either course. The present infringement is operative compelled surrender of an objectively intimate confidence; completed officer disclosure is unnecessary, but an idle insult or nonoperative threat does not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the right, application on the supported assumptions and stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an officer cannot evade protected confidentiality by making the citizen perform the disclosure. Sheets protects objectively intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius recognizes that an official disclosure condition can itself burden intimate information before publication occurs. Today's bounded extension concerns an arrest-backed command to reveal an adult's intimate life to family, rather than every threat or a general right to keep all information secret.

The supported account describes an operative choice imposed through official arrest authority: Wayman must disclose his suspected orientation or the officer will disclose it. The alleged demand therefore does more than predict possible future misconduct. Its coercion presently targets the protected confidence. No supported drinking-enforcement purpose connects disclosure to his grandfather with this adult's arrest. Religious admonition supplies no policing justification. Disputed admissions do not establish that the information was already public.

Doe preserves the distinction between confined, justified benefits administration and an unrelated use; it neither requires public dissemination for every claim nor itself decides this extension. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection, and Nixon recognizes personal privacy without making ownership decisive. Their foundations and the actual holdings of Sheets and Denius support this confined coercion rule, not an all-purpose informational guarantee.

The officers' strongest objection distinguishes an unexecuted threat from a completed deprivation. That distinction remains valid for an idle remark or a demand without operative effect. It does not answer a credible, arrest-backed command to surrender the confidence itself. The plaintiff must still prove the actual demand, responsible actor, coercive effect, causation and compensable injury. This legal ruling finds none of those disputed historical facts after trial.

#### II. The supported no-purpose ultimatum defeats legal immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** On the supported plaintiff-favorable account, the law effective on April 17, 1997 gave sufficiently particularized objective notice that using arrest authority to compel an adult's intimate family disclosure for no official purpose was unconstitutional; Wilinsky is not entitled to qualified immunity on that legal ground. An identical orientation-and-ultimatum case is unnecessary, but operative coercion and the complete absence of an official disclosure purpose are material limits, and subjective bad faith alone does not defeat immunity.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to the notice ground and affirmance. O'Connor and Kennedy join the present merits rule but not this conduct-date conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson retain an objective, particularized notice test. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law, not merely an identical earlier case or the authorities cited below. Sheets had taken effect in April 1996. Its completed-publication setting differs from the present ultimatum, but it established protection for objectively intimate information against exposure unrelated to the official purpose for access. The officer cannot avoid that boundary by coercing the person to perform the surrender.

The earlier Third Circuit authorities reinforce the distinction between confidential information and a genuine official need. Westinghouse addressed intimate medical material; Fraternal Order of Police addressed justified employment inquiry and disclosure safeguards; the 1995 circuit Doe opinion evaluated an actual health-plan monitoring interest. Those cases do not themselves decide this ultimatum, and their full balancing formulations are not adopted. Their justified-use boundaries, together with Sheets, make this no-purpose demand sufficiently apparent.

Walls is substantial contrary authority, but its employment-screening inquiry differs from an arrest-based command to disclose to family without a policing purpose. Decisions distinguishing ordinary threats from completed injuries likewise do not authorize operative compelled self-disclosure. The Court does not derive notice simply from the demand's offensiveness or an officer's subjective understanding.

Denius supplies present merits law, not April 1997 notice. The Supreme Court's May 1997 Doe decision, subsequent Shahar and Wilson v. Layne decisions, and still later authorities supply no retrospective notice either. The relevant cumulative law already existed when the alleged act occurred. Denial of the legal defense permits ordinary proceedings; it does not prove participation, causation of death or damages, and it orders no automatic trial.

#### III. Hoban's factual participation challenge is outside immediate review

**Holding and operative rule:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal cannot determine whether pretrial evidence sufficiently establishes an officer's participation in the alleged conduct. Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention is dismissed from immediate appellate review; a genuinely separable legal defense may be decided only on the proper supported assumptions, without choosing between disputed accounts.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson distinguishes immediate review of a separable legal immunity question from review of evidentiary sufficiency concerning participation. Behrens preserves that boundary within an otherwise authorized appeal; legal phrasing cannot bring the factual dispute within the appellate court's power. Hunter leaves legal immunity for judicial decision under ordinary summary-judgment constraints, and Elder requires consideration of relevant law without authorizing factual retrial. Hoban's denial of actual participation therefore does not permit appellate selection of the true account. His role remains for the proper trial-level proceedings, while any properly separable assumed-fact legal issue retains its own lawful footing.

#### Remaining limits and ordinary proceedings

The case concerns an adult and an operative no-purpose ultimatum. It decides neither an emergency protective disclosure on facts absent here nor a general right to conceal evidence of criminal conduct. No universal privacy test, strict scrutiny, least restrictive means or automatic threat liability is adopted. Whether the ultimatum occurred, each officer's responsibility, coercive effect, causation of Wayman's death, compensable injury and lawful damages remain for proof. Municipal responsibility, other constitutional claims and state-law claims retain their own predicates and actual procedural posture. Recognition of the legal rule and denial of immunity establish no damages amount or liability for every defendant.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Sheets v. Salt Lake County, April 29, 1996:** Its objectively intimate-information protection is extended from unjustified official dissemination to the operative coerced family disclosure presented here. Its actual-publication holding remains; it did not itself decide threats. Its date makes its confined principle relevant to April 1997 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap, January 17, 2001:** Its recognition that a compulsory disclosure condition can impose a present burden supports the extension, with actual intimacy and official justification required. Its differentiated immunity analysis preserves conduct-date notice; its present holding supplies no earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority, May 5, 1997:** Its assumed-right disposition of confined, justified benefits administration is preserved, distinguished from the unrelated ultimatum. It supplies no April 1997 notice. The distinct 1995 circuit opinion is relevant conduct-date authority at its own scope.
- **Whalen v. Roe; Nixon v. Administrator of General Services:** Their confidentiality foundations and approval of legitimate safeguarded governmental uses remain. Neither is treated as having decided this coercive demand.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald; Anderson v. Creighton:** Objective, particularized qualified immunity remains; it is neither replaced by a reliance-only defense nor defeated by subjective malice alone.
- **Elder v. Holloway; Hunter v. Bryant:** Independent consideration of relevant contemporaneous law and judicial immunity review under proper factual assumptions are applied. Neither permits appellate fact selection or retrospective notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its particular earlier notice determination and rejection of a universal identical-case requirement remain. Its 1999 decision is not itself April 1997 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones; Behrens v. Pelletier:** Their factual-sufficiency exclusion and distinct assumed-fact legal-review channel govern Hoban's contention.
- **Westinghouse; Fraternal Order of Police; the 1995 circuit Doe opinion:** Considered as preconduct circuit confidentiality authorities concerning actual official functions and safeguards; they are not elevated into Supreme Court holdings or transplanted wholesale.
- **Walls v. City of Petersburg:** Its employment-inquiry setting and contrary notice significance are considered and distinguished from this no-purpose arrest-backed family disclosure demand; its existence is not ignored.

**Resulting law:** Protected intimate confidentiality reaches the credible official ultimatum compelling an adult's family disclosure without legitimate governmental purpose. Present coercion can supply the infringement even without completed officer publication; an idle insult or nonoperative threat cannot. The bounded rule governs claims meeting those conditions and requires proof of actual responsibility, effect, causation and injury.

The ordinary objective qualified-immunity test remains. On this supported no-purpose ultimatum, the April 1997 authorities defeat the asserted legal defense without making all later privacy extensions retrospectively clear. Hoban's factual participation challenge remains outside immediate appellate review. No municipal liability, causal death finding, damages amount or general informational-secrecy right follows.

### Chen v. City of Houston — 2001-05-21

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Chen_v_City_of_Houston_merits_2001-05-21.md#holdings).

#### Total population is a permissible measure

The Court holds that a municipality may apportion ordinary representative council districts by total resident population even when the districts have materially unequal citizen voting-age populations. Equal representation of all residents is a constitutionally permissible objective; the differing eligible electorates alone do not require replacement of that denominator or a new election.

**Authority:** The opinion of Stevens, joined in full by all eight other participating Justices, controls this rule, its explanation and its limits.

Representatives serve children and noncitizens as well as eligible voters. Counting those residents distributes representation and constituent demands on a coherent basis. Reynolds and Avery require equality in representative districting, but do not resolve every choice of population base. Burns permits a population choice in its particular setting; it does not command a citizen voting-age denominator here. The voters correctly identify differing numerical influence among eligible electorates. That difference does not establish that equal representation of residents is forbidden. No finding that both measures could or could not be equalized is needed to answer the categorical claim.

Voinovich's deviation inquiry follows selection of a permissible population measure. It cannot select citizen voting-age population as mandatory by applying the ten-percent threshold to that disputed measure. Nor does Houston's 8.63-percent total-population deviation create immunity from every constitutional claim. Department of Commerce permits constitutional review despite a range of lawful legislative choices; this judgment rests on the merits, not a political-question dismissal. Preclearance does not adjudicate the constitutional claim.

The Court neither requires total population in every setting nor decides whether a different proposed denominator would be lawful. Those alternatives and the separate racial claim are outside the accepted question. Intentional exclusion or a separately established dilution mechanism is not approved by this holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Reynolds v. Sims and Avery v. Midland County: applied as the equality requirement for representative districts; neither becomes a command to count only eligible voters.
- Burns v. Richardson: its population-base permission is preserved, without converting permission there into a required denominator here.
- Voinovich v. Quilter: its deviation and justification inquiry remains effective within the legally relevant population measure; a deviation below ten percent is not categorical immunity.
- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana: applied to preserve judicial review; its interstate allocation holding does not displace intrastate equality.
- Easley v. Cromartie and Smallwood v. Cromartie: their racial-districting rule remains distinct and is not reconsidered in this population-base review.

**Resulting law:** Total resident population is an available constitutional base for these municipal representative districts. Future applications must still distinguish the population base from departures within it and from independently proved discrimination. No exclusive denominator for every setting is announced.

### Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA — 2001-05-29

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Appalachian_Power_Co_v_EPA_merits_2001-05-29.md#holdings).

#### 1. A completed directive with legal consequences is reviewable

The Court holds that this settled national monitoring directive is final agency action reviewable under Clean Air Act section 307(b). EPA has completed its position and implements it through the statutory permit-objection system, in which its objection prevents permit issuance; a disclaimer cannot remove those present legal consequences.

**Authority for all three holdings:** The opinion of Scalia, joined in full by all eight other participating Justices.

Bennett requires both completed agency action and legal consequences. Whitman applies that distinction to a settled Clean Air Act implementation framework before an individual sanction or plan determination. Both requirements are present here: EPA concedes its central position is settled and enforces that position through its permit authority. Expense, anticipated compliance or influence alone would not establish finality. Individual permit review does not displace the statutory route for this national action. The facial legal issue can be decided without choosing the monitoring terms of any particular permit and is ripe for that bounded review.

#### 2. Guidance cannot remove the regulation's condition for added monitoring

The Court holds that section 70.6(a)(3)(i)(B) authorizes added periodic monitoring when the applicable requirement lacks periodic testing or instrumental or noninstrumental monitoring, including qualifying recordkeeping. It does not authorize universal sufficiency review that replaces already prescribed recurring monitoring merely because EPA considers it inadequate; that substantive enlargement requires the applicable rulemaking procedures.

Paragraph (A) incorporates applicable monitoring and testing, permitting streamlining only with at least equivalent compliance assurance. Paragraph (B) requires reliable, representative compliance data and terms, methods, units, averaging periods and other statistical conventions consistent with the applicable requirement; recordkeeping may suffice. Paragraph (C) requires necessary use and maintenance provisions and, where appropriate, installation provisions. Paragraph (c)(1)'s compliance-assurance requirement is expressly consistent with (a)(3). Reading assurance of compliance as unlimited authority to replace existing periodic monitoring would erase that structure and the absence trigger. Missing periodic testing, unspecified frequency and one-time startup tests remain genuine gaps that may be supplemented.

Auer and Thomas Jefferson require weight for responsible, fair and considered interpretations that fit the regulation. Christensen's regulatory-text holding forbids amendment through interpretation. EPA's expertise and practical compliance objective deserve consideration, but neither deletes this express condition. The 1992 explanation's contemplation of future rulemaking to repair deficient standards confirms that reading. The holding does not rest on the guidance label, forbid interpretive change generally, or impose notice and comment on every technical explanation. The regulation resolves the case without deciding the outer limit of Congress's delegation or the alternative challenge to the 1992 rule.

#### 3. The whole intertwined guidance is set aside

The Court sustains vacatur of the entire guidance because its challenged and remaining directions are intertwined and the record gives substantial reason to doubt independent adoption of the remainder. This record-specific conclusion establishes no universal severability formula.

The supported integration finding defeats partial vacatur here. Existing emissions limits and monitoring requirements, the 1992 regulation, lawful gap-filling and independently authorized stricter state requirements remain effective. EPA may proceed through lawful future rulemaking; the Court prescribes no substitute rule, monitoring frequency or individual permit condition.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bennett v. Spear: applied through its separate completion and legal-consequence requirements; practical influence alone remains insufficient.
- Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc.: applied to finality of a settled Clean Air Act framework and to preserving connected legal commands; its nondelegation holding does not decide this regulatory dispute.
- Auer v. Robbins and Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala: preserved for fair and considered own-regulation interpretations within permissible text; neither authorizes amendment.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its regulatory-text holding supplies the limit on interpretive enlargement. Its distinct treatment of a nonbinding advisory letter is not converted into a categorical rule about all guidance.
- Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. United States Army Corps of Engineers: its authority inquiry is consistent with this result; it supplies no general rule invalidating informal administration.

**Resulting law:** National agency guidance may be reviewed when completed action has the required legal consequences under the governing review statute. EPA's existing monitoring regulation permits its specified gap-filling, but the 1998 guidance cannot supply a universal replacement-monitoring duty. The guidance is vacated as a whole; existing law and lawful future rulemaking remain available.

### Arkansas v. Sullivan — 2001-05-29

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Arkansas_v_Sullivan_summary-merits_2001-05-29.md#holdings).

#### 1. Federal motive rules do not dispense with separate authority for each intrusion

The Court holds that an ordinary traffic stop supported by objective probable cause is not invalid solely because the officer also investigates another offense. State courts must follow this Court's controlling federal constitutional interpretation; greater protection must rest on an actual independent state-law ground, not a different reading of the same federal rule.

**Authority for all three holdings:** The per curiam opinion is joined by all nine participating Justices.

Whren supplies the ordinary stop rule while preserving independent limits on detention and search. Oregon v. Hass distinguishes a state's own protections from its duty to follow controlling federal law. The lower court's motive-only federal rationale and asserted power to disregard that hierarchy cannot stand. The reported federal basis, rather than an invented independent state ground, supports review; Quarles supplies the analogous otherwise-evading-review route. Correcting that legal error does not establish the validity of every subsequent act.

#### 2. Added fine-only custody requires a supported enforcement function

The Court applies Atwater: added station transport and confinement for a known fine-only criminal offense must reasonably serve a supported actual enforcement function. Probable cause alone does not justify that added restraint; identification, appearance, safety, a continuing violation, evidence protection and necessary processing are nonexclusive possible functions, not presumed facts.

Atwater extends Ricci's actual-enforcement inquiry to this criminal setting. It requires neither the least restrictive means nor a citation whenever one is conceivable. Actual offense classifications and supported arrest grounds matter where relevant. This record does not conclusively establish that every asserted offense was fine-only, that the hatchet supplied a valid weapons predicate, that identification failed, or that processing required the restraint. Those matters remain for the proper court on the actual preserved record. The Court neither finds the custody unlawful nor treats investigative interest as an independent source of arrest authority.

#### 3. Impoundment and inventory require their own lawful grounds

The Court holds that impoundment and a claimed inventory must independently satisfy their lawful authority, caretaking purpose and standardized-practice requirements. Investigative interest does not invalidate every otherwise lawful act, but a search that is an investigatory ruse fails the inventory exception itself; a policy label does not establish genuine compliance.

Bertine and Wells supply the inventory conditions that Whren preserves. Knowles keeps a search's justification distinct from the antecedent encounter and requires actual lawful custody for its search-incident rationale. Drugs found later cannot supply antecedent probable cause. The reported pretext finding does not necessarily decide every inventory condition under the correct rule. Remand must address actual preserved custody, impoundment, inventory and independent state-law grounds. The Court orders no new hearing, speculative reopening of settled facts, admission or suppression on a newly assumed ground, conviction, acquittal, or unsupported search-incident result.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Whren v. United States: applied to reject a motive-only objection to the ordinary probable-cause stop while preserving independent detention and inventory limits.
- Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights and Atwater v. City of Lago Vista: applied to require a supported enforcement function for added fine-only custody; neither becomes a universal citation duty or a final finding about Sullivan's custody.
- Colorado v. Bertine and Florida v. Wells: preserved as independent authority, caretaking and standardized-practice limits on inventories.
- Knowles v. Iowa: applied to keep search authority distinct and to reject automatic search consequences from a lawful stop.
- Oregon v. Hass: applied to the state court's federal-law duty while preserving genuine independent state protections.
- New York v. Quarles: supplies the analogous jurisdictional treatment of a finally settled federal issue otherwise likely to evade review.

**Resulting law:** The ordinary stop, added custody, impoundment and inventory remain distinct constitutional inquiries. A federal pretext theory cannot replace them, and probable cause for the stop cannot supply their missing predicates. The remand must apply the actual-function custody rule and genuine inventory requirements.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

**Souter concurs and joins the Court opinion in full.** Exigency and permissible scope depend on the risk at the time of the proposed action. In his assessment, while McArthur was inside with immediate access to the hidden drugs, the actual destruction risk could have justified prompt entry and search. Once McArthur stepped outside and police could control access, the same immediate necessity for a greater warrantless evidentiary intrusion was absent. Temporary control of reentry while pursuing a warrant respects the preference for a magistrate's authorization and affords a reasonable opportunity to secure it. Police need not let an occupant return simply to recreate the emergency that would support a greater intrusion. This analysis distinguishes the risk and available response at each point; it does not find that a greater search occurred, infer an unreported occupant or turn the later discoveries into the earlier justification. The conditional greater-intrusion view belongs to his concurrence and is not an additional Court holding.

**Stone-Zsela dissents.** Control of access to a home is a seizure requiring an adequate warrant exception. The wife's contemporaneous observation of concealment strengthens probable cause and bears on risk, but hiding an existing stash does not itself show imminent destruction. McArthur could see his wife confer with police and leave with an officer; those facts support anticipation of a search, but add no particularized urgency beyond that awareness and the disposable character of drugs. Treating that combination as sufficient would authorize a similar restraint in nearly every residential drug investigation. An actual destruction attempt is not invariably required, but the inference must concern imminent loss rather than probable cause alone.

The two-hour duration and accompanied entries reduce the burden but cannot supply the missing exception. Segura does not validate every initial seizure because a later warrant produces evidence. Welsh gives the modest offenses real weight without misclassifying them as nonjailable. Contextual reasonableness evaluates a demonstrated exception; it cannot replace the warrant boundary. Stone-Zsela would affirm suppression for the search resulting from the unsupported restraint, subject to any independently preserved exclusionary-rule issue actually left unresolved. He would not invalidate the later warrant on an unpresented ground or dismiss independently supported charges.

**Stevens dissents.** He first would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because these slight possession offenses make an unsuitable occasion to define the limits of restraints on residential possession. Because the Court decides the merits, he would affirm. Welsh requires serious attention to offense gravity, and his understanding of Segura gives deprivation of home possession independent significance despite an eventual warrant. Even accepting probable cause, diligent warrant pursuit, accompanied entries and the comparatively limited restraint, he concludes that the substantial interest in private use of the home outweighs this modest preservation interest. His position concerns these facts; it neither denies that the offenses permit imprisonment nor categorically prohibits every misdemeanor preservation measure. He does not demand an observed destruction attempt in every case.

## Prior-term public continuity: OT1999

### Christensen v. Harris County, No. 98-1167

Merits decision, May 1, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 23, 2000. On certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 158 F.3d 241, whose decision in No. 97-20796 was captioned Moreau v. Harris County. The questions are whether the Fair Labor Standards Act requires a specific agreement before a public employer may direct use of accrued compensatory time, and what weight the Labor Department's contrary opinion letter receives under the statute and regulations.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Fifth Circuit's final judgment for the County and all other defendants is affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The dissenting Justices would set aside that judgment and remand for actual agreement coverage, terms and validity under a consent requirement.

The employees' categorical declaration and dependent $21,360 fee award remain displaced. The Court orders no agreement-specific proceedings, negotiations, trial, damages or new fee award. The policy remains subject to express statutory protections, binding agreements and other applicable law. Abandoned wrongful-refusal and retaliation claims are not revived.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas throughout; Scalia in Parts I and III | Six Justices support affirmance; Part II's rationale has five | Part I: statutory use and remedy, six. Part II: advisory-letter statutory weight, five. Part III: regulatory textual fit, six. |
| Opinion concurring in part and in the judgment | Scalia |  | Supports affirmance | Joins Parts I and III; withholds Part II. |
| Concurrence | Souter |  | Joins the Court throughout and supports affirmance | Preserves the future-regulation question. |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg and Breyer | Would set aside the judgment and remand | Supports consent-based agreement proceedings. |
| Additional dissent | Breyer | Ginsburg | Supports agreement-specific remand | Explains independently persuasive and conditionally authoritative agency grounds. |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. The statute does not make employee requests the exclusive means of using accrued time

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 207(o) does not categorically forbid a public employer from directing paid use of lawfully accrued compensatory time merely because no agreement specifically authorizes compulsory use. The employer remains bound by lawful agreement restrictions, protected employee requests and the statute's accrual, value and overtime requirements; compelled paid use is not permission to confiscate credits or evade overtime.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, six Justices in all.

**Controlling explanation:** Paragraph (5) protects an employee who requests accrued time: use must be allowed within a reasonable period unless it would unduly disrupt operations. It does not say that a request is the only permissible trigger for use. Paragraph (2)'s agreement requirement governs substituting time for cash overtime, not every later scheduling choice. Moreau preserves its actual agreement routes; neither it nor the statute establishes the terms of this class's agreements.

The accrual cap limits how much time may be accumulated and requires cash for additional overtime at the cap; it does not promise employees the right to reach that cap. The permitted cashout likewise contradicts an absolute right to retain every credit, although cash payment and paid absence remain different transactions. Paid time away from work consumes credits in their defined form: the employee receives the regular rate for hours not worked, which are excluded from hours worked when computing overtime. These statutory reasons suffice without a general presumption that employers control every unregulated workplace question.

The competing consent construction treats the substitute for cash as exclusively employee-controlled. It does not follow from the original agreement requirement or protected request right. An actual lawful term can restrict compulsory use, but none identified here defeats the categorical statutory judgment. That absence does not prove no agreement exists or every employee consented. It supplies no automatic right to new agreement proceedings. The decision leaves constitutional property questions, other labor law and actual wrongful-refusal claims outside this review.

**Operative qualifications:** For the covered state, local and interstate public agencies, a wholly time-based substitute must accrue at least one-and-one-half hours for each overtime hour under the applicable representative agreement or prior individual understanding. For employees outside the applicable representative-agreement route who were hired before April 15, 1986, regular practice on that date itself supplies the agreement; no additional individual agreement is required. Moreau preserves the later-hire individual route where the chosen representative cannot lawfully enter a binding agreement. Unwritten understandings require a record; an express employment condition requires knowing, voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object may evidence assent, but coercion or pressure may not substitute for it. Compatible agreements may mix cash and time or set use terms while preserving the statutory premium; inconsistent terms yield to the Act.

The cap is 480 hours for the specified public-safety, emergency-response or seasonal work, and 240 for other covered work. Additional overtime at the applicable cap must be compensated as overtime. Cashout may occur at any time using the regular rate when paid. Termination requires at least the higher of the final regular rate or the average over the last three years. For termination-rate averaging, employment after a break is treated as a new period when the break was intended to be permanent and post-April 14, 1986 credits were cashed out at the initial separation; if the final period is shorter than three years, averaging uses that period. No termination or rate calculation is decided here.

For employee requests, customary schedules, peak demands, emergency staffing, substitutes and compatible agreement terms inform a reasonable period. An employer must reasonably and in good faith anticipate an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service to establish undue disruption; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and the statute does not guarantee the employee's exact chosen date. Section 553.25 forbids using time to evade overtime or coercing acceptance beyond what can realistically and in good faith be made available within a reasonable request period. Its request provisions exclude separately defined other compensatory time, including pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. The separate court-reporter transcript provision is not presented in this Sheriff's Department case. No violation of these distinct protections is found.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding and operative rule:** The advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual implementing grant permits administration and regulation. It does not make every later advisory response an exercise of authority to bind courts or employers. NationsBank and Smiley involved identified authoritative exercises of delegated responsibility; they do not settle this letter's status merely by identifying its agency. Skidmore preserves its potential persuasive significance rather than discarding it by label. Its construction adds a specific-consent restriction absent from the statutory request trigger and prior-agreement condition. The record establishes no policy change, internal authorization instrument or Government endorsement on which to rest the result. Boston & Maine forbids inventing an agency ground. The Court decides this letter's advisory status on the record, not the validity of every informal agency interpretation.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Auer concerns a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. It does not impose a universal rulemaking-format prerequisite on interpretation, but requires actual textual fit. Section 553.23 permits additional consistent terms; section 553.25 protects requests and practical availability. Neither makes employee requests exclusive or requires an agreement covering every scheduling choice. The letter's interpretation therefore adds a condition rather than resolves pertinent ambiguity. The separate inquiry into statutory authority does not replace this own-regulation analysis. No conclusion here forecloses whatever authority the Secretary may possess to adopt a valid new regulation limiting compelled use.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Moreau retains its distinct earlier-hire practice and later-hire legally capable representative rules; it supplies no finding about this class's actual scheduling terms or general bargaining right.
- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

#### Law After Decision

Section 207(o) supplies no categorical employee veto over compulsory paid use of valid accrued time. Actual binding agreements and every identified statutory protection retain their force. This advisory letter supplies no controlling statutory restriction and cannot add a condition inconsistent with the regulation. The Court decides no future rule's validity, blanket employer-control principle, confiscation power or constitutional property question.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia joins the statutory construction and regulatory textual-fit ruling but withholds the Court's no-Chevron ground. Advisory form alone should not exclude authoritative consideration of a responsible agency's construction within a genuine implementing delegation. Even assuming the claimed authority here, however, the separate accrual-agreement and request provisions cannot reasonably bear the letter's additional restriction. His judgment does not depend on finding an unproved endorsement or internal delegation.

Justice Souter joins the Court in full and emphasizes that the decision does not foreclose the Secretary's authority to issue a valid future regulation restricting compelled use. The present statute-and-letter dispute does not decide that different exercise of power.

Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, would remand for agreement-specific proceedings. The compensatory-time scheme is a conditioned, consensual substitute for ordinary cash overtime. Prior agreement, protected availability and an express cash-redemption power support requiring an agreement covering compulsory consumption. Cash payment and forced leisure are different transactions; authorization of one does not itself authorize the other. The agency's experienced reading reinforces that construction. The writing does not require negotiation of every optional term, identical class agreements or a new bargaining right. Actual earlier-hire practice and later-hire agreement routes govern the remand, and neither the categorical declaration nor its fee award would automatically revive. Breyer adopts these statutory reasons as persuasive support, without declaring this the only permissible judicial reading.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Ginsburg, additionally explains that Skidmore remains independently available. The agency's experience and the statutory reasons supporting consent make this construction persuasive; if authoritative Chevron treatment applies, the construction is reasonable within the actual implementing delegation. This bounded alternative does not require a final finding that the letter conclusively has authoritative status or a universal rule about informal agency forms. The own-regulation argument remains distinct and subject to Auer's responsibility and textual-fit predicates. These grounds support agreement-specific remand without presuming the letter's full contents or any particular agreement terms.

## Prior-term public continuity: OT1996

### Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, No. 95-1608

Merits decision, April 28, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 4, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, Twin Cities Area New Party v. McKenna, 73 F.3d 196 (January 5, 1996). The court below reversed summary judgment for state officials and held Minnesota's old prohibition unconstitutional as applied to the New Party's nomination of Andy Dawkins, a consenting, supported candidate already seeking the Democratic-Farmer-Labor nomination. The DFL did not object. The grant concerns that minor/major-party combination under the First and Fourteenth Amendments, not the separate two-major-party prohibition. Minnesota Laws 1996, chapter 419, conditionally changed the statute and expressly makes reversal consequential.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

All nine reach the live controversy. The old prohibition is sustained in this application; the remand must account for chapter 419's express expiration and prior-law revival upon reversal. The completed 1994 election is not reopened, and no particular intervening filing or election is adjudicated.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Breyer; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg in I and III | Reversal and remand | Five join merits Part II; all eight Associates join live-controversy Part I and statutory-transition Part III; Stone-Zsela agrees to reach the merits without an additional statutory rationale |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg throughout; Souter in I–II | Would affirm the as-applied constitutional judgment | I–II: nomination interest and inadequate fit; III: broader objection to preserving the established parties' competitive position |
| Separate dissent | Souter | No other Justice | Would affirm | Joins Stevens I–II; reserves a demonstrated stability justification connected to effective governing |
| Dissent | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would affirm on contextual, nonsevere-burden review | Substantial but not severe burden; inadequate fit; prospective and format/consent limits |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The conditional enactment leaves a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge to the old prohibition remains live where the officials seek reversal of its invalidation and an enacted provision expressly revives that prohibition upon reversal. Chapter 419 supplies that concrete consequence; neither its replacement scheme nor completion of the 1994 election makes this dispute advisory.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined in Parts I and III by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela also votes to reach the merits without an additional chapter-specific ground.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Mesquite distinguishes a genuine end to a challenged restriction from repeal that leaves its recurrence consequential, while keeping mootness separate from entitlement to an injunction. The Minnesota officials continue to contest the same party's asserted right, and chapter 419 conditions revival of the old law on the judgment they seek. That express condition makes adjudication affect legal rights even without finding a present stay or mandate recall. Norman preserves a continuing election challenge despite completion of the immediate election. The new scheme's existence is therefore relevant but not conclusive. Dawkins's earlier consent and the DFL's lack of objection do not establish compliance with every later written-chair-consent requirement. The Court does not decide the independent constitutionality of that replacement scheme or determine which law governed an unexamined intermediate filing.

##### II. This limitation is justified within the complete nomination system

**Holding and operative rule:** Minnesota may prevent this otherwise qualified minor party from placing its consenting nominee on the ballot under its name when he also seeks or holds the other major party's nomination, where the complete system leaves the candidate available and the party able to nominate other candidates, endorse and campaign. In this application the burden is less than severe and proportionate interests in ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and a stable electoral system justify the restriction under Anderson and Burdick.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer: five direct joins in Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson and Burdick require attention to the actual burden, precise interests and their connection; severe restrictions require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest, while reasonable nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. The Party's nomination choice is real association, not an arbitrary protest message, and endorsement is not literally the same legal act. But the rule excludes neither this actual candidate nor the Party itself from meaningful electoral participation. It limits an additional nomination designation within the State's selection machinery. Norman barred unrelated obstacles to authorized party expansion and independently qualified candidacies; it did not require every overlapping nomination of an available candidate. Colorado Republican protects independent campaign expression, a different mechanism from official ballot selection. The State may use general preventive nomination rules to maintain coherent candidacies and stable election administration without proving a past disruption in this particular alliance. Storer supports orderly candidate-selection rules; Jenness and Munro preserve reasonable screening and prevention without a demand for elaborate empirical proof. Candidate consent and qualifying signatures lessen some concerns but do not compel the State to redesign its nomination structure whenever an alliance proposes a workable alternative. This holding does not depend on voter confusion, an asserted incapacity of voters or proof of past fraud. Nor does it approve suppression of competition merely to protect incumbents, pure rational-basis immunity for ballot laws, or every anti-fusion restriction in a more restrictive system.

##### III. Reversal has the enacted transition consequence, without a retrospective election ruling

**Holding and operative rule:** Chapter 419 §10 makes its amendments effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter, suspends them while the McKenna judgment is stayed or its mandate recalled, and provides that reversal causes the amendments to expire and prior law to revive. This reversal engages that expiration-and-revival condition; the remand must respect it without assuming an unproved stay, recall or continuous intervening statutory regime.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted condition links the state-law consequence to reversal, not to a finding about an earlier filing. Recognizing that consequence does not adjudicate the replacement provisions' independent validity or establish that their requirements were satisfied in 1994. The existing case asks whether the old ban may be applied to this nomination, so sustaining that application permits a conforming remand. It supplies no basis to reopen the completed election or allocate rights in an unexamined intervening candidacy. Any such distinct question would require its actual filing facts and relevant stay or mandate history.

##### Statutory scope and limits

The challenged 1994 §204B.06 subdivision 1(b) barred an affidavit when another affidavit was on file for any office at the same primary or next general election. Section 204B.04 subdivision 2 barred petition nomination for the same office by a primary candidate, subject to two distinct exceptions. Under §204D.10 subdivision 2, if any major-party primary candidate received ten percent of the average vote for the party's state officers at the last state general election in the relevant district, its highest-vote candidates became nominees; if none met that threshold, none were nominated and all could seek petition nomination under §§204B.07–.09. The relevant state officers were governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, state auditor, state treasurer and attorney general. The §204B.13 subdivision 4 nonpartisan-vacancy exception allowed a petition within one week of the vacancy and no later than four calendar days before election; an eligible voter could sign despite voting in the primary or signing another petition. Neither exception applied to Dawkins. The distinct ban on nomination by two major parties is not decided.

The conditional replacement permitted a major-party and one-or-more-minor-party nomination for the same partisan office with written consent of the parties' state chairs; petition nomination also required the candidate's written consent. Losing the major-party primary forfeited the simultaneous minor-party nominations, and a party candidate could not also file as an independent. It retained the nonpartisan-vacancy exception, barred a defeated major-party primary candidate except to fill a §204B.13 vacancy, and repealed the old primary-turnout provision. Its affidavit rule referred to any other office, required petition acceptance and the consent forms, and contained a distinct dual-office exception for a soil-and-water district supervisor outside Anoka, Hennepin, Ramsey and Washington counties and a mayor/council or town-supervisor office in a city or town of no more than 2,500 people wholly or partly within the district. That different-office exception is not fusion. A multiply nominated candidate's name appeared once with party names; major/minor nomination used the major-party position and multiple-minor nomination the first filing party's position. Votes for multiply nominated candidates were excluded from the specified party-status qualification or retention counts. These features neither establish compliance in this case nor receive independent constitutional approval.

The Court recognizes no right to duplicate votes, separate party vote totals or a particular ballot design and adjudicates no independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support or deadline rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Anderson v. Celebrezze supplies the actual-burden, precise-interest and fit inquiry.
- Burdick v. Takushi supplies calibrated scrutiny within the complete access system; its protection against forced counting of arbitrary messages does not make this genuine nomination a fictional vote.
- Norman v. Reed retains protection of authorized party expansion and independently qualified candidates, distinguished from this overlapping nomination; its continuing-election principle also supports reaching the controversy.
- Colorado Republican retains its protection for independent party campaign expression and its distinct statutory connection requirement; it does not impose campaign-expenditure scrutiny on ballot designations.
- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut preserves protected party association; that principle does not itself decide the proportionality of this nomination restriction.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee preserves party choice and expression without making every official ballot mechanism compulsory.
- Storer v. Brown supports orderly candidate-selection rules within an election system.
- Jenness v. Fortson preserves meaningful support requirements and reasonable electoral screening.
- Munro v. Socialist Workers Party permits preventive election regulation without proof of actual past disruption, within the calibrated inquiry.
- City of Mesquite v. Aladdin's Castle prevents automatic mootness where repeal leaves a concrete prospect of recurrence, while keeping injunctive entitlement separate.

#### Law After Decision

The old anti-fusion restriction is constitutional in this minor/major-party application. Reversal engages chapter 419's expiration of its amendments and revival of prior law. The governing constitutional method remains contextual Anderson-Burdick review, with no automatic strict scrutiny, universal validity rule, actual-confusion premise or permission to insulate incumbents from competition. The replacement scheme's independent constitutional questions remain undecided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens dissents, joined by Ginsburg throughout and Souter in Parts I–II. A willing, sufficiently supported standard bearer is a central associational choice, and endorsing him or choosing someone else does not replace the Party's legally operative nomination. Even without declaring every such burden severe, they find inadequate fit: no defeated candidate seeks reentry, the candidate consents, the other party does not object, support requirements have been met, and another party designation adds no new individual to the race. Norman, Tashjian and Eu require attention to that actual association; Burdick permits reasonable prevention but does not make every general interest sufficient. Stevens and Ginsburg further reject preserving established parties' competitive position as a reason to exclude this consensual alliance. They would affirm at the as-applied scope, without mandating duplicate votes, separate party totals or a court-designed ballot.

Souter also dissents separately. Less-than-severe burden does not end the inquiry into fit. He agrees that the present interests are inadequate, but does not join a categorical rejection of every stability justification. A demonstrated threat to effective governing and a connection between fusion and that threat might justify a restriction; he leaves that question open. His Norman and Burdick positions preserve support requirements and primary finality, neither of which explains this supported nomination of the primary winner.

Stone-Zsela dissents. The burden is substantial but not severe, and the Eighth Circuit's strict-scrutiny reasoning should not control. Losing the qualified party's legally operative nomination and distinct identity affects effective electoral choice even though endorsement and campaigning remain possible. Applying Anderson-Burdick, he finds the absolute ban insufficiently fitted to this consenting, supported nomination: neutral labels, single counting and access requirements address the identified concerns, while the record supplies no demonstrated confusion, manipulation or splintering sufficient for this burden. Norman protects authorized party identity, and Colorado Republican confirms that shared candidate support does not erase a smaller party's independence without itself deciding fusion. Political stability cannot simply mean protecting established competitors. He reserves a properly supported stability limit and whether the other party's consent may be required. His proposed relief is prospective and leaves lawful design, consent, counting, fraud-prevention, deadlines and support requirements available. He recognizes no right to a separate line, multiple vote credit, general ballot expression or a particular format, and would not reopen 1994.

## Prior-term public continuity: OT1991

### Burdick v. Takushi, No. 91-535

Merits decision, June 8, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 24, 1992; decided June 8, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. § 1254(1), reviewing its replacement opinion filed June 28, 1991, [937 F.2d 415](https://static.case.law/f2d/937/cases/0415-01.json). The March 1, 1991 opinion at 927 F.2d 469 was withdrawn. Hawaii's Supreme Court construed state law on July 21, 1989 to prohibit write-ins. The District of Hawaii entered summary judgment and an injunction for Burdick on May 10, 1990, 737 F.Supp. 582; that injunction was stayed and the Ninth Circuit reversed. The lower court rejected standing and certification/full-faith-and-credit objections. The argument and decision dates appear in the [official report, 504 U.S. 428](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep504/usrep504428/usrep504428.pdf).

The questions are whether Hawaii's complete prohibition violates First and Fourteenth Amendment electoral-choice rights when considered within its access system, and whether the Constitution independently requires an official tally of arbitrary protest votes. Burdick seeks prospective relief from the prohibition; Hawaii seeks affirmance. The Court reaches primary and general-election applications within the challenged system, but does not independently adjudicate every surrounding access rule.

In 1986 only one candidate filed for Burdick's legislative district, and he wished to vote for someone who had not filed. Hawaii requires new parties to obtain signatures from one percent of registered voters 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Party and nonpartisan candidates file 15 signatures for legislative or county office, or 25 for statewide or federal office, 60 days before the primary. A nonpartisan candidate advances with ten percent of the primary vote or the vote sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower. A voter chooses one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions frame the constitutional burden.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas all participate at argument and decision. Thomas took his seat before argument.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Ninth Circuit and sustains Hawaii's write-in prohibition within the access system reviewed.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electoral-choice challenge to the complete write-in prohibition | Affirm, 5–4 | White, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy: reverse and remand | No prospective injunction issues; the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. |
| Claimed right to official count and publication of arbitrary protest messages | Rejected, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | — | No relief on this component. |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | White | O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas | Affirmance; five votes for judgment and complete controlling rationale | Complete opinion. |

| Dissent | Stone-Zsela | — | Reverse and remand with prospective relief confined to legally eligible candidates | Separate dissent; agrees in rejection of the arbitrary-message claim. |

The Court's rationale commands a majority. The dissenters' agreement on calibrated scrutiny and rejection of a general protest tally does not constitute a joint opinion of all nine Justices.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden

**Controlling proposition:** A court reviewing a restriction on voting or candidate access must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State’s precise asserted interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not apply to every election rule.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and controls. Kennedy’s dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone-Zsela’s separate dissent also accept this calibrated framework; their agreement does not make their contrary application controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional review must account for the regulation and the election system in which it operates. Anderson v. Celebrezze supplies the inquiry into the actual injury, the precise state interests, and the need for the particular burden. Norman v. Reed preserves that inquiry while recognizing that States may require support in the electorate a candidate or party seeks to represent. Storer v. Brown explains why orderly elections require substantial regulation of candidate selection. Treating every restriction as severe would disable the primary procedures and qualification rules those decisions permit. The claimed injury here therefore cannot be measured simply by observing that Hawaii forbids write-ins. The Court must examine the other means of obtaining access and the function the prohibition serves within that system. This approach preserves demanding review when access is severely restricted and meaningful scrutiny when a lesser burden is asserted; it does not make ordinary administration immune from constitutional review.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780 (1983): applied as the governing burden, interest, and necessity inquiry; no categorical strict-scrutiny rule replaces it.
- Norman v. Reed (January 14, 1992): applied for actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements; its protection against cross-component exclusion remains effective.
- Storer v. Brown, 415 U.S. 724 (1974): applied for the State’s authority to organize candidate selection; severe barriers remain reviewable.

##### 2. Hawaii’s complete write-in prohibition is valid in its reasonably accessible system

**Controlling proposition:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a prohibition on write-in voting is presumptively valid and may be sustained by interests proportionate to that burden. Hawaii’s party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its write-in prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries; the electoral-choice challenge therefore fails.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, and Kennedy disagree with this application and its resulting judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Hawaii’s prohibition imposes a limited burden when assessed with its nomination rules. Small signature requirements and party and nonpartisan routes permit access before the election, although the nonpartisan route requires a primary showing of support and a choice of primary ballot. Jenness v. Fortson permits reasonable demonstrations of support, and Munro v. Socialist Workers Party permits primary winnowing without proof of a particular past disruption. The Constitution does not require the State to accommodate every preference arising after its lawful selection deadlines. Storer v. Brown supports preserving the primary’s role in settling candidacies. The prohibition also prevents candidacies that would evade that selection process, discourages primary raiding, and complements the designation of certain unopposed primary victors. The contrary position underestimates these available access routes and treats the inability to vote for an unlisted choice as decisive. The Court sustains this system without deciding whether the same prohibition would survive materially more restrictive access rules.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Jenness v. Fortson, 403 U.S. 431 (1971): applied to permit reasonable candidate-support requirements; it does not validate every access barrier.
- Munro v. Socialist Workers Party, 479 U.S. 189 (1986): applied to permit primary winnowing and reasonable preventative regulation without an empirical-disruption prerequisite.
- Storer v. Brown, 415 U.S. 724 (1974): applied to primary finality and restrictions preventing circumvention of candidate selection.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780 (1983): applied to the complete Hawaii system; this decision supplies a limited-burden application, not an exception to the inquiry.

##### 3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages

**Controlling proposition:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require the State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of political expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not establish a constitutional entitlement to a write-in option.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas, controls. Kennedy’s dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone-Zsela’s separate dissent expressly agree in rejecting this broader claim. All nine reject this component, although four would grant relief for genuine electoral choice.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the ballot’s purpose limits the claimed expressive entitlement. Voting carries political meaning, but an official election need not become a general forum in which the State records and disseminates any message a voter wishes to send. Storer v. Brown recognizes elections as a means of selecting officeholders through an orderly process, and the access inquiry under Anderson v. Celebrezze protects the ability to participate in that process. Neither principle requires an official tally for fictional figures or arbitrary statements. Burdick’s interest in expressing dissatisfaction therefore does not itself invalidate the prohibition. His distinct interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual analysis stated in the preceding holdings, and the Court has conducted it. Rejecting the broader theory does not diminish political expression outside the ballot or decide every restriction on votes for otherwise eligible candidates under a different access system.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Storer v. Brown, 415 U.S. 724 (1974): applied to the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780 (1983): distinguished from the demand for an arbitrary-message tally; meaningful electoral participation remains protected.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Effective June 8, 1992, Anderson's calibrated review includes the Court's holding that a write-in ban is presumptively valid when the complete access system imposes reasonable burdens, with Hawaii's arrangement sustained on this record. No absolute right to a write-in option or arbitrary-message tally exists. This does not establish that every prohibition is valid: a materially different access system may impose a severe burden requiring narrow tailoring to a compelling interest.

Norman v. Reed (January 14, 1992), which protects against unjustified name and cross-component disqualification, retains that protection; Hawaii's accessible selection routes present a different application. Burson v. Freeman (May 26, 1992) supplies a strict-scrutiny proposition concerning campaign-content restrictions on ordinary public sidewalks; that proposition is neither extended to all ballot rules nor displaced. Burson's fractured judgment does not supply a controlling validity exception here. No precedent is overruled, limited in force beyond these distinctions, or restored after displacement. Candidate qualifications, reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere, and independent challenges to other Hawaii provisions remain open.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, would reverse and remand. They accept contextual scrutiny and reject the ballot-as-general-expression theory, but consider the total prohibition a substantial burden in the actual nomination system. Low signature requirements do not answer the need to select one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices, the advancement rules, or the inability to support a real alternative in an unopposed contest. They reject a presumption derived from the surrounding rules’ validity. Sore-loser prevention does not explain a primary ban and reaches only some general-election candidates; party-raiding and uncontested-primary interests do not justify the prohibition’s entire reach. Their position does not establish a general constitutional write-in entitlement.

Stone-Zsela separately would reverse and remand for workable prospective relief from the complete prohibition within this system. Fifteen signatures make initial filing accessible, but the primary-ticket and advancement conditions must enter the burden inquiry. An actual single-candidate contest presents electoral choice even though the Constitution guarantees neither competition in every race nor all late candidacies. Sore-loser prevention cannot explain the primary ban; raiding does not justify excluding genuine support for a candidate on the selected primary ticket; and an election omitted under the uncontested-winner rule has no general-election ballot needing protection. He rejects arbitrary fictional-message tallies and confines relief to legally eligible candidates. He reserves reasonable write-in procedures, valid qualifications, narrow anti-raiding and sore-loser restrictions, and materially different systems. He would not prescribe statewide filing, certification, or counting machinery or independently invalidate other access rules.

Both writings are noncontrolling on the electoral-choice application and remedy. Their agreement on contextual scrutiny and rejection of general protest expression does not make their contrary application or proposed remedy controlling.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review is complete. The mandate follows ordinary procedures and returns the matter below consistently with affirmance; the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction issues from this Court. The Court orders no special timing, election administration scheme, special remand, retained jurisdiction, or nonroutine follow-on condition.
