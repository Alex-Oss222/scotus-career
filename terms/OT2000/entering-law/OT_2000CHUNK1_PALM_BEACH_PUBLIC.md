# Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board, No. 00-836; 531 U.S. 70.

Source Record: `../records/Bush_v_Palm_Beach_County_Canvassing_Board_merits_2000-12-04.md`. Effective date: 2000-12-04.

## Event

**Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board**, No. 00-836; 531 U.S. 70. Argued December 1, 2000; decided December 4, 2000. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) to review the Florida Supreme Court's November 21 certification judgment. That judgment reversed the trial orders and supplied a final binding certification directive with no rehearing or further merits adjudication in that proceeding. The submitted federal questions concern alleged post-election alteration under due process, then-effective 3 U.S.C. §5, and the legislature's Article II authority. No independent equal-protection question is presented here.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the November 21 judgment and remands for immediate clarification of the identified state-law grounds and federal-timing premise, followed by lawful further proceedings in the state court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The state court must clarify two matters: first, whether the late-return discretion limits and selected acceptance date independently implement the pre-election code and what role state constitutional power plays; second, whether a particular federal electoral requirement, including §5, was understood to require that date or the full-participation condition and how that understanding affected the remedy. The Court directs no particular answer, tally, ballot rejection, elector selection, or winner. This disposition supplies no independent stay and prohibits no lawful recount.

## Holdings

### The state judgment requires two precise clarifications before the federal merits can be decided

**Holding and operative rule.** When a final state judgment's unresolved legal dependence prevents sound resolution of the federal challenge to that judgment, the Court may vacate and remand for authoritative clarification of the material state grounds. Here the state court must identify the statutory and constitutional basis for its late-return restrictions and chosen acceptance date, and the role of its federal-timing understanding, before the submitted Article II, §5, and due-process merits can be resolved.

**Authority.** Parts I, II, and IV of the per curiam opinion have all nine Justices' joins and directly support the clarification disposition under §§1257 and 2106 and Minnesota v. National Tea. Part III's distinct due-process explanation has the eight Associate Justices' joins; Stone-Zsela joins that component's disposition only.

**Controlling explanation.** Florida's opinion contains substantial statutory reasoning: it distinguishes tabulation from the tabulating system, reconciles mandatory filing language with the later permissive late-return provision, preserves the county filing deadline, and considers the pre-existing manual process. The Court cannot treat that analysis as nonexistent or revise state law merely because it would read the code differently. The concrete gap concerns the further two-condition restriction on exclusion and the equitable acceptance date. The opinion does not definitively identify whether those terms rest independently on construction of enacted law or on constitutional power to displace a legislative restriction, nor whether a particular federal requirement compelled the remedy. Those dependencies matter to the actual federal challenge. McPherson supplies Article II's legislative-direction setting, but Article II does not categorically exclude ordinary state judicial interpretation. Smiley concerns a different congressional-election setting and does not settle this application. Immediate invalidation would assume legislative displacement; immediate affirmance would assume that the disputed remedial terms independently construe legislative law. National Tea permits the defined antecedent clarification needed to avoid either assumption. The state court receives the case for clarification and lawful further proceedings; the Court does not retain jurisdiction while awaiting an answer.

**Known state-law predicates.** The following distinctions govern the scope of the clarification; they are not newly enacted federal recount rules:

| Provision or state-court construction | Material terms |
|---|---|
| Manual-recount request, §102.166 | An eligible candidate, political committee, or party may make a reasoned request before county certification or within 72 hours after midnight of election day, whichever is later. The board may authorize a sample of at least three precincts and at least one percent of the relevant vote, with precincts selected by the requester. |
| Qualifying sample error | An error that could affect the outcome requires one of three statutory responses: correction and a system recount of remaining precincts, software verification, or a manual count of all ballots. It does not invariably require the third. Public counting, statutory team procedures, and voter-intent procedures apply; an indeterminate ballot goes to the board. No rule makes every undervote an ascertainable vote. |
| County filing and late-return consequences, §§102.111–102.112 | Both retain the seventh-day, 5 p.m. filing command. The state court gives the later, more specific provision's permission to exclude late returns controlling effect over the earlier mandatory language; accepting a late amended return is therefore not automatically displacement of an unambiguous exclusion command. |
| Personal fines | Section 102.112 supplies daily fines and administrative appeal. The further exception for compliance with other code provisions, administrative rules, or other good cause is the state court's construction, not an enacted list. No particular fine is adjudicated here. |
| Rejection of late amended returns | The state court permits rejection only when lateness prevents a statutory contest or full participation in the federal electoral process, and requires a legally supported explanation and reasonable, necessary exclusion. The legal source of these restrictions is part of the clarification. |
| Equitable acceptance date | The November 21 remedy requires acceptance through November 26 at 5 p.m. if the office opens for that purpose, otherwise through November 27 at 9 a.m. Its state stay lasts through that period and its certification directive includes accepted amended returns. This is a judicial remedy, not an enacted date; no later office opening, return, or total is found here. |
| Separate overseas-ballot rule | It concerns otherwise proper federal-office ballots of overseas electors, postmarked or signed and dated by election day and received within ten days. It is not an unconditional extension for every ballot or county return. The five-day protest and 72-hour manual-request periods also remain distinct. |

### Federal timing and fundamental fairness remain distinct from the state-law clarification

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 5 offers conclusive federal-count treatment for a qualifying final determination made under pre-election law at least six days before the electors meet; it does not itself prohibit every count after that date. The due-process question separately remains governed by Yoshina's distinction between authoritative construction of ambiguity and fundamental unfairness, and is returned with the other federal components for consideration on clarified state-law predicates.

**Authority.** Part II's description of §5 and limited timing remand has all nine joins. Part III's due-process ground is the direct holding of the eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with its remand disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** The supplied federal dates are December 12 and December 18. The availability of conclusive treatment does not itself make December 12 an absolute state-law end date or establish that Florida has secured or forfeited the benefit. Foster addresses premature final congressional selection and expressly leaves presidential timing outside its holding. It supplies no automatic stopping rule here. On due process, Yoshina rejects the proposition that a neutral authoritative construction becomes fundamentally unfair merely because an official previously advised otherwise. Actual guidance, dissemination, inducement, reliance, and voting circumstances matter without becoming a rigid new checklist. This source establishes conflicting post-election advice, not a particular pre-election assurance or detrimental reliance; that limit proves no litigant default. The separate contention that a clear election rule was displaced rather than an ambiguity construed depends partly on the legal basis still requiring clarification. The Court therefore neither rejects the entire due-process question for lack of reliance facts nor treats interpretive change alone as sufficient for relief.

**Questions not decided.** The Court enters no final Article II or due-process violation, no §5 qualification ruling, and no categorical prohibition on state constitutional interpretation. Those merits depend on the unresolved authoritative state-law premises identified above. Later contest procedures, ballot standards, recount feasibility, elector totals, and the lawful winner lie outside this certification case.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Minnesota v. National Tea:** Applies authoritative clarification of materially uncertain state grounds before resolution of a federal challenge.
- **McPherson v. Blacker:** Preserves Article II's legislative-direction setting without resolving whether these remedial terms construed or displaced that direction.
- **Smiley v. Holm:** Distinguishes its congressional-election lawmaking setting; it is not treated as a complete answer to presidential appointment.
- **Foster v. Love:** Preserves its congressional-timing holding and express presidential-timing limit; it creates no December 12 counting prohibition.
- **Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina, January 21, 1999:** Applies its fundamental-fairness distinction to the separate due-process component, without equating post-vote construction or advisory disagreement with a constitutional violation.

## Law After Decision

The November 21 judgment is vacated for the defined clarification. Ordinary state interpretation remains available subject to enforceable federal limits, but no final Article II, due-process, or §5 merits rule is announced for this judgment. The federal benefit's conditions remain distinct from a prohibition on later counting.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed. Stone-Zsela's limited join in the due-process component supplies no separate ground.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns immediately to the Florida Supreme Court for the two identified clarifications and lawful further proceedings. Federal jurisdiction is not retained awaiting an answer; later federal review requires its own supported procedural posture. Vacatur neither establishes the Secretary's substantive authority nor mechanically reverses every subsequent administrative act. This decision stays no recount, directs no certification, and presumes no later state response.
