## Reading selection: Arizona v. California, 530 U.S. 392 (2000)

Source: [federal-courts](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md), exact heading `Arizona v. California, 530 U.S. 392 (2000)`.

#### Arizona v. California, 530 U.S. 392 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 8, Original.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### I. The belated Arizona I defense is unavailable in this proceeding

**Holding:** In this continuing original action, the State parties forfeited their Arizona I claim-preclusion defense by omitting it when the enlarged Fort Yuma demand was presented in 1979 and again during the 1982 proceedings, despite the opportunity to assert it, and first presenting it in 1989 after the litigation had proceeded on the different grounds and boundary-review course described here. The Court overrules that exception without deciding whether a timely defense would have barred the enlarged claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The later reopening order and the Master's consideration of the defense do not require disregarding the earlier forfeiture. Limited judicial authority to raise preclusion to avoid repeating an actual prior adjudication supplies no mandatory rescue here: the disputed title predicate was not actually decided. The Court adopts no general prohibition on appropriate judicial initiative and does not deny that claim preclusion can reach unlitigated theories.

This is forfeiture from the supported litigation course, not a finding of intentional waiver, estoppel or a concession in an unavailable response. Interior's changed legal view is not newly discovered fact defeating otherwise applicable preclusion. The unresolved title, cession, boundary, irrigable-acreage and water-quantity predicates require adjudication before any additional Fort Yuma water may be awarded.

##### II. The compensation compromise does not decide the reserved-water claim

**Holding:** Docket 320's consent judgment finally settles the United States' and Tribe's claims against one another within that docket's reciprocal release, but it neither actually nor by a clearly intended binding determination resolves the disputed title predicate and does not release the distinct reserved-water demand against competing users. The Master's asserted consent-judgment bar therefore fails, while the compromise's lawful claim-finality effect remains intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous. O'Connor and Thomas retain their different original-allocation ground opposing the Fort Yuma return.

**Material application:** The $15 million judgment compromises alternative invalid-cession, trespass and uncompensated-taking theories. The disposition of those alternatives does not establish that one particular title theory was adjudicated. International Building requires attention to what a compromise actually determined before assigning issue-preclusive force. The reciprocal release addresses claims between the United States and Tribe; its no-admission clause reinforces the absence of an intended title adjudication without nullifying the judgment or its final releases. The appeal waiver also stands.

**Operative remedy or transition:** O'Connor and Thomas's agreement on the separate Docket 320 and section 22 questions does not make them supporters of the Fort Yuma return: their original-allocation ground would independently end that enlarged water demand. The Court awards no additional Fort Yuma water, fixes no Fort Yuma boundary and resolves no disputed cession or title merits. Further original-jurisdiction proceedings, and implementing decree provisions faithfully incorporating the two approved agreements, remain necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Arizona v. California, 373 U.S. 546 (1963), and the 1964 decree:** Preserve the original allocation. The Court does not decide the merits of the asserted bar arising from that allocation, and no Fort Yuma quantity is changed now.
- **Arizona v. California, 460 U.S. 605 (1983):** Applies its recognition of finality in the continuing original action while preserving its boundary-litigation reservation. Its omitted-acreage holding is not overruled or declared inapplicable to a timely Fort Yuma defense.
- **The 1979 and 1984 supplemental decrees:** Give effect to their conditional all-five-reservations adjustment language and its role in the litigation course; they do not themselves adjudicate the present preclusion merits or establish a final boundary.
- **United States v. International Building Co., 345 U.S. 502 (1953):** Applies its distinction between a compromise disposition and an issue actually determined; the ambiguous title predicate gains no issue-preclusive force from compromise alone.
- **Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996:** Preserves lawful consent-judgment effect and the separate binding prerequisites. Its section 1738 rules are not directly transposed to the federal Docket 320 judgment.
- **Baker v. General Motors Corp., January 13, 1998:** Applies the distinction between an instrument's actual adjudicative effect and an additional effect against distinct rights; its rendering-state inquiry supplies no duty to revive a forfeited defense.
- **Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793 (1996):** Preserves the need for a lawful basis to bind distinct rights; shared subject matter alone supplies neither claim identity nor binding scope.

For the distinct section 22 bar and the particular settlement terms, see Arizona v. California, No. 8, Original, June 19, 2000, under Federal Indian Law, reserved water and compensation judgments.

## Reading selection: Lindh v. Murphy

Source: [federal-courts](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md), exact heading `Lindh v. Murphy`.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

## Reading selection: Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863

Source: [federal-courts](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md), exact heading `Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863`.

#### Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-405.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### A private settlement does not supply the required collateral-order importance

**Holding:** An order refusing effect to an ordinary private settlement does not qualify for immediate appeal under §1291 merely because the agreement promises freedom from trial. Assuming conclusiveness and separateness, the category lacks the public importance required to excuse final-judgment review; irretrievable litigation burdens and the particular agreement's value, clarity or alleged violation do not enlarge appellate jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for affirmance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541: applies its narrow category of collateral decisions rather than a general hardship exception.
- Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay, 437 U.S. 463: applies the cumulative conclusiveness, separateness, importance and effective-unreviewability requirements.
- Lauro Lines s.r.l. v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495: applies category-based finality to a contractual claim despite practical burdens of litigation; its importance rationale distinguishes private bargains from recognized public immunities.
- Abney v. United States, 431 U.S. 651: distinguishes the constitutional interest against a second criminal trial, leaving its immediate-review rule intact.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: distinguishes the public-law immunity from suit without expanding its substantive coverage.
- Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, January 12, 1993: preserves immediate review of qualifying Eleventh Amendment immunity denials; a private settlement is a different category.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even apart from the dispute over importance terminology, ordinary settlement rights of this category remain adequately vindicable after final judgment and therefore fail the collateral-order effective-unreviewability requirement. Review of protection against ultimate liability, together with lawful contract enforcement and district-court protections when their own requirements are met, is adequate in this setting despite the irretrievable experience and expense of litigation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither alternative ground resolves the validity of settlement rescission, actual fraud, breach damages, sanctions, the district court's power to reopen, or trademark liability. Section 1292(b) remains separate: it requires written district certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, possible material advancement of termination, timely application and appellate permission. No satisfaction of those conditions is found and no such appeal is certified here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lauro Lines s.r.l. v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495: applies adequate postjudgment vindication despite loss of an asserted contractual litigation advantage.
- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541: applies effective unreviewability as a distinct limitation on immediate review.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, May 16, 1994: preserves the independent jurisdictional requirements for contract enforcement and its reservation of Rule 60(b) reopening; no breach remedy is found available on these facts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the interlocutory appeal is affirmed. No settlement-rescission merits ruling, contract remedy, trademark judgment or §1292(b) certification or permission is entered.

## Reading selection: Arizona v. California, 530 U.S. 392 (2000)

Source: [federal-indian-law](../../../state/holdings/federal-indian-law.md), exact heading `Arizona v. California, 530 U.S. 392 (2000)`.

#### Arizona v. California, 530 U.S. 392 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 8, Original.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### III. Section 22 does not establish the asserted third-party bar from this payment

**Holding:** Payment of this alternative-theory compromise does not itself establish a taking, extinguishment of tribal title or release of dependent reserved-water claims against all persons under section 22 of the Indian Claims Commission Act. The distinct statutory discharge asserted by the State parties therefore does not bar the Fort Yuma claim on this record; differently supported judgments with an actual title or extinguishment predicate remain open for their own analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of this independent statutory component and adds no ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 22(a) gives a Commission report determining entitlement to recovery, when filed with Congress, the effect of a final Court of Claims judgment and authorizes payment; payment discharges the United States from claims and demands touching the controversy. Section 22(b) separately makes an adverse final determination, made and reported under the Act, a bar to further claims against the United States arising from that matter. The recovery/payment trigger and the adverse-determination trigger are distinct, and both provisions identify claims against the United States.

##### IV. The two submitted settlements are approved on their particular terms

**Holding:** The Court approves the Fort Mojave and Colorado River Reservation agreements recommended by the Master and permits their incorporation into implementing decree provisions at the terms stated below. Approval rests on these agreements and this record under the Court's existing original-jurisdiction authority; it establishes no general approval test and resolves no additional title or water question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous on both independent settlement approvals.

**Material application:** **Fort Mojave terms:** The agreement specifies the reservation boundary near the Hay and Wood Reserve. It permits the lesser of an additional 3,022 acre-feet or enough water for 468 acres. The United States and Tribe may make no further reserved Colorado River water claims for lands within the Hay and Wood Reserve. Competing claims to title and jurisdiction over the bed of the last natural course of the Colorado River within that boundary remain reserved, as do private land claims, title and jurisdiction. Approval does not leave the specified reservation boundary wholly undetermined, but it does not decide those reserved riverbed and private rights.

**Colorado River Reservation terms:** The agreement permits the lesser of an additional 2,100 acre-feet or enough water to irrigate 315 acres. The United States and Tribes may make no further reserved Colorado River water claims for reservation lands in California. The disputed boundary remains unadjudicated in this proceeding, including the competing ambulatory-west-bank and fixed-line theories; competing riverbed-title and jurisdiction claims remain reserved. The agreement requires both the Master's and Court's approval; the Court supplies its approval here on the Master's recommended agreement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new release, additional water number, private-title adjudication, universal settlement framework or present final Fort Yuma decree follows. The Master shall continue the unresolved Fort Yuma proceedings and provide appropriate implementing decree provisions for the approved agreements, subject to the Court's further action. No objection deadline or final supplemental-decree text is entered by this decision.

For the separate Arizona I forfeiture, Docket 320 preclusion holdings and Fort Yuma return, see Arizona v. California, No. 8, Original, June 19, 2000, under Federal Courts, preclusion in continuing original actions.

## Reading selection: Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson

Source: [business-and-commercial-law](../../../state/holdings/business-and-commercial-law.md), exact heading `Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson`.

#### Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1001.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### Section 2 continues to govern covered agreements in state court

**Holding:** FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court, making them valid, irrevocable and enforceable save upon grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. The Court retains Southland's state-court holding; Alabama may apply generally applicable contract defenses but may not deny enforcement solely because its law singles out predispute arbitration agreements for a categorical disability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1: retains the substantive state-court application of §2 against the express request to overrule it; its commerce and general-contract-defense conditions remain.
- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483: applies its rejection of a state judicial-forum exception directed at arbitration while preserving generally applicable contract defenses.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Junior University, 489 U.S. 468: preserves enforcement of an actual contractual choice of state arbitration rules; a forum-wide hostility to arbitration is not itself that choice.

##### Coverage turns on actual commerce within Congress's power

**Holding:** Section 2's phrase involving commerce reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full commerce authority Congress constitutionally possesses, and coverage turns on the transaction's actual interstate commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity when contracting. The multistate service operations and interstate procurement of treatment and repair materials establish the necessary commerce here, requiring reversal of Alabama's coverage ruling; this statutory construction does not define the outer constitutional boundary or make remote interstate history alone conclusive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bernhardt v. Polygraphic Co. of America, Inc., 350 U.S. 198: distinguishes a record failing to establish the requisite commerce; its transaction predicate survives, without a newly attributed contemplation requirement.
- Southland, 465 U.S. 1: applies its substantive enforceability rule after identifying the actual covered transaction; general hostility to arbitration does not substitute for the statutory commerce inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded under the actual-commerce rule. Formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other generally applicable contract defenses remain for ordinary adjudication to the extent properly presented and preserved below, together with party assent, clause scope and appropriate implementing procedure. Coverage is not a final order compelling all parties to arbitrate every claim. Section 1's separate employment exclusion is not presented by this service bond. The Court does not decide the tort merits, damages, a particular fraud-in-the-inducement defense, a contractual choice of state rules not established here, or wholesale state-court incorporation of §§3–4.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Supreme Court is reversed and remanded to apply the actual-commerce rule while addressing properly presented assent, clause scope, general contract defenses and implementing procedure. Coverage does not compel every party to arbitrate every claim or decide tort liability.

## Reading selection: First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan

Source: [business-and-commercial-law](../../../state/holdings/business-and-commercial-law.md), exact heading `First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan`.

#### First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-560.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Antecedent assent and delegation

**Holding:** Courts independently determine agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Presenting an objection while denying panel jurisdiction does not itself establish delegation; company submission does not itself establish an owner's personal assent. Where parties actually delegate arbitrability, limited award review governs that decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer on the antecedent-assent rule and application; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer alone on the additional conditional actual-delegation review rule (Stone-Zsela reserves that proposition) directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Broad clauses, incorporated rules and independent nonsignatory-binding theories are not resolved. The antecedent-assent rule has nine joins; the conditional limited-review rule for actual delegation has eight, excluding Stone-Zsela. No judgment-only or fractured-decision aggregation supplies either coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, Inc. v. Communications Workers, 475 U.S. 643: applies clear-and-unmistakable delegation.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees, 489 U.S. 468: applies arbitration's contractual foundation.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: federal coverage supplies no missing assent.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc., March 6, 1995: actual integrated contract terms preserve the antecedent-consent inquiry.

##### Ordinary appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review factual findings for clear error. Arbitration supplies no additional appellate deference to the district judge's independent arbitrability determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, 475 U.S. 643: preserves decisionmaker/merits distinctions.
- Volt, 489 U.S. 468: actual agreement explains arbitral deference; it insulates no district-court legal conclusion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Third Circuit on arbitrability and appellate review. Vacatur of the award against the Kaplans personally remains effective; MKI's separate corporate submission remains distinct. No new FAA merits-vacatur ground or rehearing of agreed arbitration merits is authorized.

## Reading selection: Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

Source: [civil-rights](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md), exact heading `Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510`.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

## Reading selection: Crawford-El v. Britton

Source: [civil-rights](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md), exact heading `Crawford-El v. Britton`.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.
- Siegert v. Gilley: preserves early examination of a cognizable constitutional deprivation without converting its threshold holding into a special motive burden.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby: applies a valid governing substantive burden at summary judgment, without authorizing courts to invent a new one.
- New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: distinguishes the constitutional speech basis of its defamation burden from this retaliation action against an official.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: applies the claimant's protected-conduct and motivating-factor burden and the defendant's separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board: applies its actual-causation distinction, not school-specific substantive rules; a possible lawful reason is insufficient by itself.
- Pell v. Procunier and Turner v. Safley: preserve prison-access limitations and supply no unrestricted press-conference right.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: preserve the distinction between legal immunity review and disputed factual sufficiency; no new interlocutory-review right is created.

## Reading selection: Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

Source: [criminal-procedure](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md), exact heading `Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806`.

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. ## Reading selection: Neder v. United States

#### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

**Holding:** Placing closed personal luggage in a shared overhead area exposes it to ordinary movement, incidental contact and associated compression, but does not expose concealed contents to an added physical examination of their shape and density. - The independent-luggage rule is applied: a bag's separately established privacy does not disappear with the surrounding vehicle's public accessibility.
- Dickerson is applied for the boundary between authorized contact and added exploration; its protective-frisk setting is not imported as search authority.
- Hicks is applied to additional physical acquisition of concealed information rather than mere observation.
- Ciraolo and Riley are distinguished because they concern information exposed to public observation; their objective approach remains intact.
- Flippo preserves the distinction between lawful presence, a visible exterior and concealed contents; no general presence-based exception is created.
- Wardlow preserves the sequence of objective justification and forbids reliance on later discoveries to justify the earlier intrusion.
- Robinette retains the separation between consent voluntariness and independence from prior illegality; no consent ground is adjudicated here after the Government's nonreliance.

## Reading selection: Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

Source: [election-law](../../../state/holdings/election-law.md), exact heading `Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)`.

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices control the complete rule. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent also accept the calibrated framework without making their contrary application controlling.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, preserves actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements, including its protection against cross-component exclusion. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate state interests. Hawaii's party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the rationale and affirmance. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy disagree with this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the complete access system reviewed and its reasonable burdens.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions support the limited-burden finding. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every later preference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently validate every surrounding access rule or decide a prohibition within materially more restrictive access rules. Candidate qualifications and reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere remain open; no absolute write-in right or guarantee of competitive races is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit; its reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction or replacement election-administration scheme issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, permits primary winnowing and preventive regulation without proof of a particular past disruption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and restrictions against circumvention. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, governs this limited-burden application; the decision creates no exception to its inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, controls. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent expressly agree on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual access analysis separately stated above. Political expression outside the ballot and every restriction on eligible candidates in a different system are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief from the prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.
