### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.

**Authority by component:**

- Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment: Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.
- Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur: Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-08; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No heightened proof quantum or exhaustive list of exceptional circumstances is adopted. This settlement rule does not turn loss of review through circumstances outside the parties’ control into automatic vacatur.

**Holdings navigation:** Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment; Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur.

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — A continuing personal controversy is absent, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the continuing-controversy and unavailable-damages limits
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — Certification cannot restore the controversy, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the certification boundary

**Present operation effective:** Arizonans for Official English, 1997-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no categorical express-pleading requirement for nominal damages and does not decide a legally available personal-capacity damages claim. Limited authority to correct lower-court jurisdictional error is distinct from merits authority; judgment vacatur remains equitable under the existing rule. No automatic initiative-sponsor standing or mandatory certification rule is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (1997-03-03) — Federal Courts / Mootness, corrective jurisdiction and equitable vacatur.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. ### Voluntary cessation and continuing controversy

**Governing question:** Whether the defendant's postfiling compliance or cessation ends an initially live controversy.

**Current rule:** A defendant invoking voluntary cessation must make it absolutely clear that the challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. Compliance sufficient to defeat an injunction does not by itself meet that burden or negate standing established at commencement. Claimed permanent closure requires evaluation of the actual facts and legal restraints; neither retained legal permission nor dismantling alone determines recurrence.

**Authority by component:**

- Demanding cessation burden and distinction from initial standing or equitable relief: Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, Public Projection / Holdings / Later compliance does not automatically establish mootness; the closure evidence must be assessed, January 12, 2000; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Laidlaw, January 12, 2000, states the applicable instruction without replacing initial-standing requirements.

**Exceptions and limits:** Fees alone do not preserve a controversy. The rule does not determine that permanent cessation occurred or resolve the reserved survival of a penalty claim after it is proved.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

#### Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc., 528 U.S. 167 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-822.
**Decided:** January 12, 2000.

##### Whether supported curtailment of local river use establishes personal injury without separate environmental damage

**Holding:** Supported curtailment of identified local recreational and aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing mercury discharges is a concrete personal injury; Article III does not require separate proof of environmental damage when the asserted injury is this loss of use and enjoyment. General interest in compliance or unexplained subjective concern alone remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The supported current local-use interests and curtailment differ from indefinite distant-travel intentions. Childhood cessation for another reason, limited prior visits and scientific no-harm findings retain their actual weight; none is converted into proof of frequent historical use or automatically negates reasonable present-use loss.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permit violation injures everyone downstream, no property valuation is decided, and no separate Sierra Club standing determination is needed for the identical request supported by other organizations' injured members.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lujan requires concrete personal interests and stage-appropriate support. Brown Group separates representative enforcement authority from members' personal stakes.


##### Whether Treasury civil penalties can redress threatened personal injury through deterrence

**Holding:** A statutorily authorized civil penalty payable to the Treasury can provide Article III redress when it is likely to deter continuing or reasonably recurring violations affecting the plaintiff's supported personal interests. Payment to the plaintiff is unnecessary for that preventive effect, but punishment of wholly past conduct without a remaining remedial connection, satisfaction from punishment and fees alone are insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Violations were ongoing at filing, with subsequent mercury exceedances. The statutory penalty can deter recurrence affecting these use interests despite existing governmental enforcement and other litigation exposure; certainty is unnecessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. is distinguished from its post-cure suit with no continuing or imminent violation alleged. Its express deterrence reservation applies, while its completed-injury limitation remains.


##### Whether compliance and later facility closure establish mootness

**Holding:** A defendant relying on voluntary cessation must make it absolutely clear that the challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. Substantial compliance sufficient to defeat an injunction does not automatically satisfy that burden or erase standing that existed at filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The lower court must assess actual closure, dismantling, sale, retained permit and consent-agreement limits. The agreement stopped incineration while allowing approved equivalent treatment capacity subject to permits and reconstruction conditions. Neither permit retention nor dismantling alone decides recurrence; alleged reporting problems do not establish resumed mercury discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not newly affirm or increase the $405,800 penalty, adjudicate the preserved diligent-prosecution defense, award fees, reopen the unappealed denial of an injunction, or decide the unpresented Article II question. The dispute over whether the liability ruling itself was declaratory relief does not establish a separate declaratory decree in the record. Fees neither cure missing standing nor alone prevent mootness. Broader survival of a properly initiated penalty claim after proven permanent cessation is reserved by the Court.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the appellate vacatur and dismissal direction. The district penalty judgment returns to ordinary unresolved appellate review, including diligent prosecution, closure and penalty questions, without affirmance of its merits or amount. Ordinary mandate and actual stay rules govern collection; no immediate execution, new injunction, fee award or special stay follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The established voluntary-cessation burden remains distinct from entitlement to prospective equitable relief.
