#### Bufferd v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue, 506 U.S. 523 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7804.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Which return starts limitations on the shareholder’s individual assessment

**Holding:** For an assessment of an S corporation shareholder’s individual income tax, the return that starts the ordinary § 6501(a) limitations period is the shareholder’s return, including applicable extensions and statutory exceptions. Expiration of the period associated with the corporation’s § 6037 information return does not bar correcting pass-through items in a timely shareholder assessment; the corporate return remains operative for corporate-level taxes to which it applies.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices. All nine Justices adopt this rule and its explanation; it controls by a direct majority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Automobile Club of Michigan v. Commissioner, 353 U.S. 180, 188 (1957), applied by analogy: a filing insufficient to compute the relevant taxpayer’s liability does not trigger that taxpayer’s assessment period; the holding is not a rule that informational completeness alone transforms one person’s return into another’s.
- Germantown Trust Co. v. Commissioner, 309 U.S. 304 (1940), distinguished in scope: a sufficient return for the same taxpayer may trigger limitations despite its form; it supplies no corporate-return substitute for a shareholder’s own return.
- Holywell Corp. v. Smith, February 25, 1992, left undisturbed: distinct fiduciary and trust filing duties do not decide the limitations question here.
No Supreme Court precedent is overruled. The contrary corporate-period rule applied in Kelley v. Commissioner, 877 F.2d 756 (9th Cir. 1989), cannot govern this shareholder-assessment question. No broader C corporation rule is decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ordinary three-year period remains subject to the shareholder’s applicable extensions and statutory exceptions. The corporate return retains its corporate-tax function. Special audit regimes, the unpressed wording objection to the individual extension and any general C corporation rule are not decided; later unified-audit rules do not govern the 1979 liability.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Second Circuit: the timely shareholder assessment is not barred by expiration of the corporate-return period.

#### United States v. Hill, 506 U.S. 546 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1421.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Mineral-interest basis under former §57(a)(8)

**Holding:** For tax years governed by former § 57(a)(8), the adjusted basis offset against percentage depletion is the adjusted basis of the taxpayer’s § 614 interest in the mineral deposit, determined without that year’s depletion deduction; it excludes separately depreciable tangible drilling and development improvements identified in Treasury Regulation § 1.612-4(c)(1). Lawfully capitalized intangible costs recoverable through depletion remain in depletable basis; this classification does not erase distinct statutory provisions governing other costs or later taxable years.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices. All nine Justices adopt this rule and its explanation; it controls by a direct majority.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for liabilities governed by former §57(a)(8), including the 1981–1982 years adjudicated. The Energy Policy Act of 1992, Pub. L. 102-486 §1915(a), (d), enacted October 24, 1992, removes §613A(c) depletion deductions from amended §57(a)(1) for taxable years beginning after December 31, 1992. Its coordinated §56(g)(4)(F)(ii) exception likewise removes those deductions from clause (i) and subparagraph (C)(i) for those taxable years; clause (i)’s general cost-depletion rule retains its separate condition for property placed in service in a taxable year beginning after December 31, 1989. The holding does not supply a contrary rule for the amended years.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Energy Policy Act of 1992, Pub. L. 102-486 §1915(a), (d), 106 Stat. 3023–3024; enacted October 24, 1992; applicable to taxable years beginning after December 31, 1992; prospective depletion-preference and adjusted-current-earnings exceptions. This is noncase-law scope information, not a judicial holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- INDOPCO, Inc. v. Commissioner, February 26, 1992, left undisturbed and distinguished by issue: capitalization can be required without creating a separate asset; Hill concerns allocation among already distinct depreciation and depletion accounts. No restrictive separate-asset prerequisite is adopted.
The controlling classification follows the coordinated statutory and regulatory text. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled or made into a general rule against multiple tax benefits. Technical Advice Memorandum 8314011 supplies no precedent under then-§ 6110(j)(3); its § 616 deferred-development setting also has an express basis direction different from the costs here. The memorandum is not a judicial holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former-law classification does not combine depreciation and depletion accounts because assets are commercially integrated. Properly capitalized depletable intangible costs remain in mineral basis; no general ban on multiple tax benefits is adopted. Technical Advice Memorandum 8314011 is nonprecedential and its §616 provision has a distinct express basis direction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Federal Circuit and remand for implementation of the mineral-interest basis calculation for the 1981–1982 liabilities. The prospective 1992 amendment does not govern those years; no new factual hearing is directed.

#### Commissioner v. Keystone Consolidated Industries, Inc., 508 U.S. 152 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1677.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### The established tax meaning includes property exchanged for release of a funding duty

**Holding:** In §4975(c)(1)(A), the established tax meaning of sale or exchange includes an employer’s transfer of property in satisfaction of an enforceable pension-funding obligation. An unencumbered transfer meeting that condition is not excluded merely because it is called a contribution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Part III-A; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helvering v. Hammel, 311 U.S. 504: settled tax treatment of obligation-discharging transfers applied.
- Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury, 475 U.S. 851: consistent tax-language construction applied.
- Atlantic Cleaners & Dyers, Inc. v. United States, 286 U.S. 427: contextual qualification preserved; it does not justify a different meaning here.
- Commissioner v. Lester, 366 U.S. 299: internal consistency of tax language supports the established-meaning rationale.
- Albernaz v. United States, 450 U.S. 333: prior settled construction informs the meaning Congress selected.

##### Discharge supplies consideration even without importing a technical tax meaning

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently of a settled technical tax meaning, transferring property to discharge an employer’s pension-funding duty is an exchange under §4975(c)(1)(A): the plan receives property and the employer receives release from its obligation. Section 4975(f)(3)’s inclusion of property subject to a mortgage or similar lien assumed by the plan or placed by a disqualified person within ten years does not exclude other exchanges supported by consideration.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Blackmun’s Parts III-C and III-D; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The settled-tax-meaning and ordinary-exchange grounds are independently sufficient. Gratuitous contributions, unpresented exemptions, correction conditions, valuation and computation remain distinct; no agency-deference holding is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand under the correct transfer classification and applicable tax conditions; unencumbered status alone supplies no exclusion. No automatic second-tier assessment or final tax computation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- This independently sufficient ground rests directly on §4975(c)(1)(A) and (f)(3); it requires no additional treatment of an earlier decision.

#### Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1402.
**Decided:** March 18, 1997.

##### Whether income-funded administration expenses invariably reduce the estate-tax deduction

**Holding:** Administration expenses charged to postmortem income of a marital bequest do not automatically reduce its estate-tax deduction dollar for dollar. Under the then-operative §20.2056(b)-4(a), valuation must reflect a material limitation on the spouse’s income rights; the effect on the qualifying beneficial interest governs rather than the income-allocation label alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt both holdings, without a fractured-rationale synthesis.

**Material application:** The regulation’s net-value direction and material-limitation qualification both apply. Its administration-income example permits application rather than demanding automatic subtraction; neither lawful allocation nor exclusion of postmortem income from the gross estate proves immateriality. The Commissioner’s categorical interpretation would remove the operative qualification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No valuation-date-only theory, complete calculation method, numerical threshold, percentage safe harbor or mandatory factor list is adopted. Principal-funded expenses retain their separate reduction treatment, and §642(g) and distinct qualification rules remain unresolved. The charitable deduction receives the same disposition because the parties treat this issue alike, not because §§2055 and 2056 or their regulations are identical.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Thomas Jefferson University and Auer require weight for a fair, considered and permissible own-regulation interpretation, but not removal of an operative qualification. Smiley preserves delegated interpretive authority within statutory limits without supplying a tax formula. Revenue Ruling 93-48 informs the deferred-interest comparison without deciding every administration expense or wholly withdrawing Revenue Ruling 73-98.

##### Whether the Commissioner’s burden and preserved categorical theory permit reversal

**Holding:** Tax Court Rule 142(a) places the burden on the Commissioner for this expense adjustment omitted from the original deficiency notice. Because the Commissioner pursued inevitable reduction rather than maintaining a quantitative-materiality ground against the Tax Court’s finding, failure of that legal theory supplies no basis to reverse or reopen proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), the same seven-Justice coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This does not reverse the ordinary taxpayer burden, make total estate value the decisive denominator or deem approximately $1.5 million immaterial in every estate. Trust qualification was stipulated and is not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining both deductions is affirmed without a new calculation, hearing, deficiency amount or evidentiary opportunity.

#### O'Gilvie v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-966 and 95-977.

**Decided:** December 10, 1996.

For the other questions in O'Gilvie v. United States, Nos. 95-966 and 95-977, decided December 10, 1996, see Federal Courts - Appellate jurisdiction and notices of appeal (The Government's appeal in the father's action was timely).

##### These noncompensatory punitive receipts are taxable

**Holding:** Under the applicable pre-1989 §104(a)(2), the taxpayer must satisfy both the tort-or-tort-type-right requirement and the independent requirement that the particular damages be received on account of personal injuries or sickness; amounts attributable to §213 deductions allowed in a prior year remain excepted. The Kansas punitive component serves punishment and deterrence rather than compensation for injury, fails the independent causal requirement, and is included in gross income under §61.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compensatory receipts, mixed allocations, materially different payment functions and later statutory texts remain outside the holding; Burke supplies no general remedial-menu or health-only test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schleier's receipt-specific causal rule is extended to this wholly punitive Kansas award, without pretending its reserved question was already decided. Burke's fractured judgment remains without a general controlling statutory rationale.

##### Receipt of the refund checks starts the children's recovery period

**Holding:** For these refund checks, a refund is made for §§6532(b) and 7405(b) when the taxpayers receive payment, rather than when the Government merely writes or mails the checks. Receipt on July 9, 1990 makes the recovery actions filed July 9, 1992 timely under the two-year period.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. The three tax dissenters do not reach this defense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns these received checks, not every payment mechanism. No equitable-tolling rule or new deadline exception is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wurts's payment-and-recovery reasoning is applied to the receipt question it did not itself decide; administrative allowance remains distinct from payment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both dockets, 6-3: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine find the father's appeal timely; only the six tax-majority Justices reach the children's refund defense. No amount, additional recovery or required recomputation is ordered.

#### Atlantic Mutual Insurance Co. v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-147.

**Decided:** 1998-04-21.

##### The transition permits Treasury's specified net-addition rule

**Holding:** Section 1023(e)(3)(B) does not confine reserve strengthening to increases caused by changes in computational methods or assumptions; Treasury may implement the fresh-start exclusion through the specified net-addition rule. That permission depends on the actual transitional statute and its interpretive room, not a general power to deny generous deductions or protect revenue.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, supplies a unanimous controlling statutory proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statute permits this construction without making it uniquely correct or authorizing general revenue-based denial of deductions. No misconduct, artificial reserve or changed-method prerequisite is imposed.

##### The reasonable construction receives controlling deference

**Independent alternative holding:** Within the interpretive room left by this transition provision, Treasury's reasonable specified net-addition construction controls under Chevron. Deference supplies no authority to contradict a clear statutory command, disregard the regulation's exceptions or reopen unrelated final tax determinations.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct joins and independently controls this rationale.

##### The calculation retains its exceptions and timing conditions

**Holding:** For an unpaid-loss reserve for an accident year before 1986, strengthening equals the excess of the reserve at the end of the taxable year beginning in 1986 over the preceding year-end reserve reduced by attributable claims and loss-adjustment payments during that 1986-beginning year; the amount by which that reserve is reduced as a result of reinsurance ceded in that year counts as a loss payment. Subject to the exceptions and timing conditions below, net strengthening is excluded from the fresh start and treated as occurring in the first taxable year beginning after December 31, 1986, without an additional requirement of unreasonable reserves or taxpayer discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the rule's application and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The calculation is confined to the specified pre-1986 accident-year reserve and transitional taxable years. No exception is established on this record; no new accounting facts, alternative hypothetical calculation, unrelated liability or avoidance finding is adjudicated.

The regulation measures the specified reserve rather than wrongdoing. It excludes a loss amount reported from a mandatory state or federal assigned-risk pool when that amount is not discretionary with the taxpayer. It also excludes payments concerning reinsurance assumed during a taxable year beginning in 1986 and additions for a line of business's reinsurance assumed then, but only up to a hypothetical reserve calculated with the assumptions, other than assumed interest rates, used for that line's reinsurance assumed in a taxable year beginning in 1985. Reinsurance assumed or ceded in a 1985-beginning year is treated as occurring in the succeeding year if the appropriate unpaid-loss reserve is not adjusted until then. Section 845 separately applies when avoidance of the strengthening limitation is one purpose of a reinsurance transaction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the challenged construction is affirmed unanimously; the objection supplies no basis to defeat the reported deficiency. No penalty, new hearing or independent recalculation is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hill is applied for the distinction between a federal tax category and commercial accounting labels; it supplies no general ban on multiple tax benefits.
- Sweet Home preserves statutory-boundary analysis and the separately sufficient deference ground within actual ambiguity; its two grounds remain distinct.
- MCI requires enacted authority before deference and remains a bar to replacing clear statutory commands with agency policy.
- Chevron and Smiley govern the eight-Justice deference rationale for this permissible classification; neither makes the agency's reading uniquely correct.
- Cottage Savings supports reasonable administration of the tax laws within their statutory terms.
- Regions Hospital supplies a statute-specific comparison and retains its closed-payment finality limit; this decision creates no general reopening power.
- Auer is distinguished because interpretation of an agency's own regulation is not the statutory-delegation question presented here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Breyer. They would require a reduction because income diverted to estate administration no longer passes as beneficial value to the spouse or charity. The net-value direction and express administration-income example permit the Commissioner to treat a limitation consequential to value as material without an additional unspecified substantiality threshold. Thomas Jefferson University and Auer support that permissible interpretation. Qualification does not answer valuation, and bookkeeping allocation cannot eliminate the economic loss. Revenue Ruling 93-48’s distinct deferred-tax-interest treatment did not abandon the administration-expense position.

They do not finally adopt dollar-for-dollar subtraction. The proper measure, including any discounted-value calculation, was insufficiently resolved and should be determined on remand. The Commissioner retains the burden for this adjustment; their disagreement concerns the legal need for an additional quantitative threshold, not a shifted burden or invented evidence. Breyer particularly emphasizes that future payment and present value may differ. A justified administrative simplification could conceivably be reasonable, but no such explanation or final arithmetic is supplied here. Both dissenters would reverse and remand without a new deficiency amount.

#### Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would exclude the punitive receipts. The physical injury is a legal cause of the damages, even though the award's purpose is punishment. The former text says on account of personal injury, while neighboring provisions use compensation language; the dissent rejects adding a compensation-only restriction. It distinguishes Schleier's employment backpay and injury-independent willfulness payment from a traditional tort award legally dependent on the physical injury. The dissent applies both statutory conditions and does not claim every tort-type receipt is excluded. Because the children prevail on that merits ground, the dissent does not decide their limitation defense; it also regards a choice between receipt and later clearance as unnecessary once mailing is rejected. All three agree that the father's appeal is timely.

Scalia separately retains the construction expressed in his Burke concurrence: the exclusion concerns physical or mental health injuries. This wrongful-death recovery satisfies that premise, unlike the employment entitlement in Burke. He joins the shared textual and causal dissent without converting his health-injury construction into the view of O'Connor or Thomas. Both dissents are noncontrolling.
