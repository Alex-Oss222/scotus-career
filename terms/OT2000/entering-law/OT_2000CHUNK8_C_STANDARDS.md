## Standards and Tests selection: Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

### Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

**Governing question:** Repeated/new claims, appellate authorization and district verification under amended §2244(b).

**Current rule:** A claim in a second or successive §2254 application already presented in a prior application must be dismissed. A new claim must also be dismissed unless it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court, or both its factual predicate was not previously discoverable through due diligence and its facts, if proved and viewed with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense. Before district filing, the applicant must seek appropriate appellate authorization; a three-judge panel may authorize only on a prima facie showing of these requirements and must act within thirty days. A grant or denial is not appealable and cannot be the subject of rehearing or certiorari. Authorization is not final entitlement: the district court must dismiss unless the applicant establishes the statutory conditions.

A Ford claim actually presented in the initial merits application, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved for later consideration is not second or successive when renewed after prematurity ends. Its first merits consideration requires no circuit authorization, subject to other habeas requirements. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) does not bar ordinary review of a circuit’s distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary; the classification itself remains reviewable and cannot evade the statute through a label.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate repeated/new claim restrictions and two-stage authorization/verification: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §106(b), 28 U.S.C. §2244(b)(1)–(4), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.
- Post-enactment authorization-review bar and distinction from original filing: The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction; Temporal operation of the channel rule; Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion, 1996-06-28. Felker Part I-A and II, all nine; the channel temporal result has all nine, its Part I-B explanation Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

- Reviewable threshold classification and previously presented/preserved premature Ford claim: Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal — Holdings I–II, 1998-05-18.

**Present operation effective:** Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal, 1998-05-18, for the specified claim classification and review distinction; the statutory requirements and Felker construction retain their earlier dates.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** A Ford claim first presented after an earlier application was rejected, other later-arising claims and other nonmerits dismissals remain unresolved. This rule guarantees no live hearing, relief or stay.  A request to announce a new rule is not reliance on an existing Supreme Court rule made retroactive. Schlup’s gateway and §2244(b)(2)(B)’s cumulative conditions are not interchangeable. The district authorization mechanism does not govern Supreme Court original filing; direct mandatory force of paragraphs (1)–(2) there remains reserved.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996). Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal — Previously premature competency claims and successive habeas review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

### Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

**Governing question:** The retained original-writ channel and extraordinary showing following §2244(b)(3)(E).

**Current rule:** The statutory ban on ordinary certiorari review of a circuit’s successive-application authorization decision does not repeal §2241’s distinct Supreme Court original habeas authority. An original petition receives independent consideration rather than appellate review of that authorization judgment. Circuit permission to file in district court is not a prerequisite to filing here. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform original-writ discretion; whether they bind it directly remains unresolved. Rule 20 separately requires an explanation for not seeking district relief, how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, exceptional circumstances warranting the Supreme Court’s discretionary exercise and lack of adequate relief in another form or court. Retained jurisdiction alone does not warrant the writ, further proceedings or an execution stay. Section 1651 aids independently held jurisdiction rather than supplying a freestanding new source.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate jurisdiction and original-writ discretion: The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction; Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion; The separate constitutional innocence claim and original relief, 1996-06-28; Felker Parts I-A, II and IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Felker, 1996-06-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Felker’s as-applied constitutional rejection rests on actual independent consideration and an adequate traditional original-writ ground, not approval of eliminating every meaningful forum. Constitutional innocence’s universal merits threshold, other supervisory routes, and the constitutionality of all AEDPA restrictions remain open.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Reserved federal claims and preclusion of litigated state facts

### Reserved federal claims and preclusion of litigated state facts

**Governing question:** Whether reservation of a federal cause displaces §1738 issue preclusion following state compensation litigation.

**Current rule:** Reservation of a federal cause does not exempt an identical factual issue from the rendering State’s lawful preclusion rules when the issue was actually and necessarily litigated with a full and fair opportunity by parties bound in proceedings that State recognizes. England’s protection following federal abstention does not create a unilateral right to contradict such a necessary finding in separate state compensation litigation. Preclusion remains issue-specific; a binding fact does not automatically preclude the entire federal claim, every federal element or a distinct legal assessment.

## Standards and Tests selection: A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error

### A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error

**Governing question:** What instructions govern a vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error?

**Current rule:** An unconstitutional reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the governing standard for all factual findings leaves no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate hypothetical verdict cannot replace the jury determination. Other instructional mistakes are not thereby classified as structural, and collateral-review gates remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

### Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

**Governing question:** Whether a State may continue psychiatric confinement under insanity-acquittee authority after conceding that the acquittee is no longer mentally ill.

**Current rule:** The State may not continue that confinement solely because the acquittee cannot prove harmlessness. The acquittal supplies neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric authority after the illness predicate disappears. Continued psychiatric custody requires constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing its own proper grounds; ordinary civil commitment requires current mental illness and dangerousness proved by clear and convincing evidence, and additional procedure cannot supply a missing substantive ground. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure an indefinite psychiatric regime requiring a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee to prove nondangerousness. Any independently lawful restraint must rest on its own authority and procedures; the holding does not prescribe a universal preventive-detention burden or scrutiny tier.

**Authority by component:**
- Ended predicate and absence of punitive authority: Foucha v. Louisiana, “1. The insanity judgment no longer authorizes this psychiatric confinement”, May 18, 1992; White Part II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).
- Appropriate new grounds and procedures; ordinary civil-commitment burden: Foucha, “2. Psychiatric custody requires appropriate grounds and procedures,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (five). Addington's existing burden is retained within that channel.
- Limits of the asserted preventive alternative: Foucha, “3. Public safety does not sustain this unbounded dangerousness regime,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).

**Present operation effective:** Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992.

**Open question:** Materially narrower preventive regimes, maximum duration of an otherwise lawful commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses remain undecided. The equal-protection plurality supplies no controlling rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment

### Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment

**Governing question:** Whether legislatively civil commitment constitutes punishment for Ex Post Facto purposes.

**Current rule:** First determine the legislature's intended civil or criminal character. Civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that purpose or effect is punitive. A civil designation is relevant but not conclusive; confinement, crime connection, indeterminate duration, criminal-style safeguards or treatment difficulty alone does not decide the question. Assess supported operation as a whole without converting a treatment deficiency into an automatic criminal penalty, demanding successful cure or making a single indicator conclusive. If the scheme imposes additional punishment for conduct predating enactment, that application violates the Ex Post Facto Clause.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

### Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions?

**Current rule:** For truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech about lawful activity, the government must establish real harms, material advancement and a reasonable fit between its chosen restriction and its interests. Where commercial and newspaper racks cause the same physical harms, the commercial category requires justification tied to those harms; benign objectives alone do not make content-selected regulation content neutral. Incremental regulation is not invalid merely because its reduction is small, but alternatives bear on fit without a least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement. In professional solicitation the government must demonstrate advancement in the relevant professional setting, without an invariable empirical-study requirement. Deceptive or coercive solicitation remains regulable. Central Hudson and Fox remain the framework; no general equality of commercial and noncommercial speech or categorical vice exception follows. When the State otherwise makes records available, a condition denying access and prohibiting use because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation receives this same review. Absence of a general right to every government record does not remove a speech-selected condition from First Amendment scrutiny; neutral access, retrieval-fee and privacy-redaction rules retain their lawful scope.

## Standards and Tests selection: Alcohol regulation and freedom of speech

### Alcohol regulation and freedom of speech

**Governing question:** Whether the Twenty-first Amendment reduces the First Amendment protection owed to speech concerning alcohol.

**Current rule:** The Twenty-first Amendment does not qualify the First Amendment's protection of speech or supply an additional presumption of validity for liquor-related speech restraints. ## Standards and Tests selection: Cigarette-labeling preemption by statutory period and predicate duty

### Cigarette-labeling preemption by statutory period and predicate duty

**Governing question:** Whether the 1965 or amended 1969 cigarette-labeling provisions displace common-law damages obligations.

**Current rule:** Original §5 of the 1965 Act bars positive enactments requiring particular cautionary statements on cigarette packages or in advertising; it does not displace the presented common-law damages claims. Federal warning compliance does not itself confer immunity.

For federally compliant packages, amended §5(b) reaches common-law duties imposing additional or clearer smoking-health warnings in advertising or promotion, including liability premised on advertising's neutralization of the mandated warning. Identify the duty necessary for liability rather than its pleading label. The clause preserves: voluntarily undertaken express warranties, because the manufacturer supplies their substantive promise; claims resting solely on testing, research or other nonpromotional conduct or an independently actionable nonpromotional disclosure duty; and general intentional material falsehood, including advertising falsehood, when liability does not prescribe additional warning content or punish warning neutralization. A conspiracy claim survives only insofar as its predicate deceptive duty survives. Advertising as evidence does not necessarily make the underlying duty promotional, but assigning consumer warnings to an alternative channel does not by itself establish an independent duty.

**Authority by component:**

- Original-Act construction: Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., “The 1965 Act does not displace these common-law damages claims”, June 24, 1992; Stevens, Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- Covered warning and neutralization duties under the successor clause: *Cipollone*, “The successor clause reaches covered warning and warning-neutralization duties”; Stevens, Stone, White and O'Connor plus Scalia and Thomas expressly support that operative proposition.
- Warranty, independent nonpromotional duties, intentional falsehood and surviving conspiracy: *Cipollone*, “Voluntarily undertaken express warranties survive,” “Independently actionable nonpromotional conduct remains outside the clause,” and “General intentional falsehood and surviving concealment conspiracies are not barred.” The record identifies Stevens's four-member narrower grounds as controlling under Marks, with Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter's broader no-common-law-preemption grounds; each surviving category is a logical subset producing the same result, with seven judgment supporters. No whole amended-Act opinion or blanket common-law exemption is adopted.

**Present operation effective:** *Cipollone*, June 24, 1992, construing each Act for its own statutory period.

**Exceptions and limits:** Preserved claims still require an actual state-law duty and all applicable elements. No agency-disclosure duty, design-defect ruling, later-amendment construction, cigarette field immunity or generic bar on independently established actual-conflict preemption is supplied. Gade's negative overlap rule and Morales's separate airline language retain their own scopes.

**Holdings navigation:** Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., 505 U.S. 504 (1992) — Federal preemption.

## Standards and Tests selection: Juvenile release avenues and facial custody review

### Juvenile release avenues and facial custody review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern juvenile release avenues and facial custody review?

**Current rule:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits temporary juvenile custody under §242.24 without mandatory placement with every willing unrelated adult. Under (b)(1), release is required in preference order to a nondetained parent, guardian or adult sibling, aunt, uncle or grandparent unless detention is necessary for timely appearance or the juvenile's or others' safety. If the only listed custodian is detained, (b)(2) permits discretionary case-by-case simultaneous release. Under (b)(3), a parent or guardian detained or abroad may designate a capable willing custodian by sworn affidavit before an immigration or consular officer; the custodian must agree to care and ensure future appearances. Other agreeing adults qualify under (b)(4) only in unusual and compelling circumstances at the district director's or chief patrol agent's discretion. The family classifications and temporary nonpunitive care survive the facial liberty and equality challenges, subject to the binding conditions agreement. Examination, notice and requested immigration-judge review are not facially inadequate on the record establishing no general inability to invoke them. Section 1252(a)(1) permits habeas upon a conclusive showing that deportability is not being determined with reasonable dispatch warranted by the facts. ## Standards and Tests selection: Immigration transition, completed adjudication and AEDPA review limits

### Immigration transition, completed adjudication and AEDPA review limits

**Governing question:** Ordinary judicial review and §212(c) eligibility during the AEDPA/IIRIRA transition.

**Current rule:** From April 1, 1997, corrected §306(c)(1) gives §242(g) cross-temporal reach. Subject to review provided in §242, and notwithstanding other law, the withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General’s identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders. It does not automatically encompass every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Former §106 review is not textually provided in new §242; §309 preserves it only for claims outside §242(g)’s specified withdrawal. AEDPA §440(a) independently withdraws the listed criminal-alien ordinary appellate channel even for pending petitions, unless application attaches a supported new legal disability to a protected completed event. Filing, conviction, hardship and review expectations alone do not preserve that channel. Where an alien surrendered a colorable deportability defense before enactment to use the then-available waiver-and-review route, neither §440(d) nor §440(a) clearly withdraws original-claim waiver eligibility or ordinary review on account of that completed choice. That surrendered defense is one protected-event route, not a universal reliance requirement; pleas and eligibility interests are not categorically excluded. Section 440(a) does not clearly repeal independent §2241 jurisdiction. Subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates, §2241 permits substantial legal or constitutional objections to executive authority to detain or deport, including an unlawful eligibility rule foreclosing discretionary consideration, and effective relief if warranted. Mere reweighing of discretionary equities is distinct, and §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

Section 1252(g)’s named-action withdrawal includes a selection challenge to commencement or adjudication; a constitutional label or potential later final-order petition does not move it outside those acts, and continuing proceedings is not a fourth enumerated act. Nevertheless, §1252(g) does not foreclose existing §1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim where the claimant has a concrete personal controversy, the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate that claim, and the applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive its necessary factual record. Constitutional or collateral labeling, disagreement with discretion and preference for earlier review are insufficient. The original basis is §1331 and the existing constitutional action against federal officers, not a jurisdictional grant in §1252(f)(1)’s individual-relief exception. Retained access does not itself establish interim or final relief. Section 1252(f)(2)’s clear-and-convincing condition concerns injunction of removal under a final order, not proceedings without such an order. AEDPA §440(d)’s categorical restriction does not govern a former §212(c) application filed before April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment, including a pending administrative appeal. That provision-specific construction requires no surrendered defense, invented plea reliance or vested right to a favorable exercise of discretion. AEDPA §401(e), §440(a) and IIRIRA §309(c)(4)(G) do not clearly repeal the independent §2241 channel for a substantial legal eligibility challenge satisfying its ordinary predicates; permanent §242(b)(9) is not imported wholesale into transitional proceedings. ## Standards and Tests selection: Finality of an unchallenged scheduled exemption

### Finality of an unchallenged scheduled exemption

**Governing question:** Whether a trustee may contest a scheduled exemption after the applicable Bankruptcy Rule 4003(b) deadline because the claim lacked a colorable statutory basis.

**Current rule:** Under 11 U.S.C. §522(l) and the applicable Rule 4003(b), property claimed as exempt becomes exempt if no party in interest objects within 30 days after the creditors' meeting concludes or a relevant amendment to the exemption list is filed, whichever is later, and no further time is granted within that period. Rule 9006(b)(3) allows enlargement only on Rule 4003(b)'s conditions. Lack of a colorable substantive exemption supplies no exception permitting a later objection. Uncertainty about a scheduled lawsuit's value permits timely inquiry and objection; it does not require waiting for collection. Properly supported misconduct remedies retain their own conditions and do not supply an adjudicated late-objection exception here.

**Authority by component:**
- Deadline, extension, no-objection consequence and rejection of a colorability exception: Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, April 21, 1992, “Untimely objection to a claimed exemption,” Thomas for Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, eight votes.

**Present operation effective:** Taylor, April 21, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The applicable historical deadline governs; the later supplemental-schedule wording does not revive its expiry. Rule 4003(c)'s objector burden remains for timely, properly presented proceedings. This decision concerns the scheduled proceeds claimed and does not determine the scope of differently limited exemption language.

**Open question:** Section 105(a), unpreserved fraud, unscheduled assets, respondents' alternative valuation/lien/good-faith theories, sanctions and retroactivity remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, 503 U.S. 638 (1992) — Exemption objections.

## Standards and Tests selection: Excusable neglect under Rule 9006(b)(1)

### Excusable neglect under Rule 9006(b)(1)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern excusable neglect under Rule 9006(b)(1)?

**Current rule:** For a Chapter 11 claim deadline subject to Rule 9006(b)(1), neglect includes inadvertence, mistake and carelessness as well as events beyond control. Post-expiration relief requires a motion and excusability assessed from all relevant circumstances, including debtor prejudice, delay and administrative effects, reason including reasonable control, and good faith. No consideration invariably decides the issue. Freely chosen counsel's conduct is fully attributed to the client; reliance alone is no exception. Under then-effective (b)(2), enlargement is barred for Rules 1007(d), 1017(b)(3), 2003(a) and (d), 7052, 9023 and 9024. ## Standards and Tests selection: Independent recovery of collateral-preservation expenses under section 506(c)

### Independent recovery of collateral-preservation expenses under section 506(c)

**Governing question:** Who may independently seek a section 506(c) surcharge.

## Standards and Tests selection: Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

### Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

**Governing question:** Compensation for a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use of the relevant property.

**Current rule:** Complete deprivation effects a taking requiring just compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the prohibited use from the owner's title. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate a background limitation that independently prohibits the use in the property's circumstances. A new legislative declaration that the use is harmful is insufficient. Apply ordinary adjudicative proof requirements, without a special heightened quantum. Changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance law; legislative labeling cannot substitute for an antecedent title limit. Compensation concerns any actual taking period, not an assumed permanent loss.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical compensation, background-law exception and allocation: Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, “Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use”, June 29, 1992; Scalia, Stone, White, O'Connor and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lucas*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Penn Central's contextual analysis remains for partial deprivation. No percentage shortcut, parcel-definition rule, automatic valuation, right to anticipated profits or finding that every environmental restriction is invalid follows. Valid authority to regulate remains distinct from responsibility to compensate.

**Holdings navigation:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992) — Complete economic deprivation of land.

### Review of an elapsed categorical building prohibition despite later permitting

**Governing question:** Whether a later discretionary permit route defeats review of compensation for a completed period during which no building permission was available.

**Current rule:** A later discretionary procedure does not bar review of an earlier categorical prohibition when permission during that period was unavailable and the state supreme court finally rejected the compensation claim on the merits.
