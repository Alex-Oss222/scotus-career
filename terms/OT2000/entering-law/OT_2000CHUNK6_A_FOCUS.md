#### Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-854.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance

**Holding:** The established private action under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 permits recovery from a person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct, subject to proof of that fraud, the defendant’s qualifying participation, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. This is accessory responsibility within the received investor action, not a finding that the assistant made a primary misrepresentation or a rule imposing liability through professional status or control alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the existence of the confined action and its received-remedy rationale. No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6 — applied as recognition of the received private action; it did not itself decide accessory liability.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — applied for bounded development of the received action and distinguished as to its actual contribution holding; shared liability must still be established before allocation.
- Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Curran, 456 U.S. 353 — used by analogy for the significance of an established judicial remedy; its different statutory setting does not establish congressional enactment of this action.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — applied: the underlying wrong must involve the required manipulation or deception, not unfairness or fiduciary misconduct alone.
- Pinter v. Dahl, 486 U.S. 622 — distinguished: its construction of express seller liability under §12 does not settle this received §10(b) action; its own statutory holding remains intact.
- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Hochfelder itself did not decide that question.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Huddleston itself did not decide that question.

##### Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance

**Holding:** For affirmative substantial assistance, the defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the fraudulent character of the activity and the assisting role of its conduct; severe recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. No independent disclosure duty is required for that affirmative participation, but negligence, valuation concerns alone, professional status, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assistance, or mere silence without a relevant duty do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its exclusion of negligence remains controlling; its recklessness reservation is resolved only for this private affirmative-assistance setting.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — its deception-or-manipulation requirement prevents ordinary investment risk and professional error from becoming securities fraud.
- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — its confined affirmative-assistance and extreme-recklessness analysis is adopted for the bank claim; inconsistent knowledge-only or categorical duty requirements for such affirmative assistance cannot govern after this decision. Its separate rulings concerning Pring are outside this review.

##### Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award

**Holding:** Viewed favorably to the purchasers at summary judgment, the appraisal warnings, internal concerns and agreement postponing an already demanded independent review until after the offering permit a genuine dispute over severe recklessness and substantial affirmative assistance; the Tenth Circuit’s reinstatement of the pleaded bank claim is affirmed and the case remanded. The purchasers must still prove the underlying violation, the bank’s qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss under the ordinary civil burden; neither the default nor the bonds’ face value establishes fraud damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — affirmed insofar as it reverses summary judgment on the purchasers’ affirmative-assistance claim against Central Bank; triable issues are not findings of fraud or damages.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — the ordinary preponderance standard is preserved; neither institutional status nor information held by the defendant shifts that burden.
- Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 — its reliance requirement is preserved. Today’s decision establishes no presumption for these municipal bonds and, as part of the retained accessory action, requires reliance on the underlying fraud rather than on the assistant’s concealed participation.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — its shared-liability prerequisite and contribution limits remain effective; this remand establishes no entitlement or allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the full rules for duty-based omissions, duty-free inaction, agency attribution, a universal securities recklessness rule, other enforcement or statutory settings, or Stone's additional concurrence formulations. The §20(a) defense for a controller acting in good faith without directly or indirectly inducing the violation remains distinct. Reliance on the underlying fraud is required; investors need not personally rely on concealed assistance, and no reliance presumption is established for these bonds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit insofar as it reinstated the pleaded bank claim and remand, 5–4. The bank's summary judgment cannot rest on the rejected categorical grounds or the disputed assistance and culpability record. No liability, damages, automatic indemnity, unpleaded primary claim or adjudication of another defendant's claim is supplied.

#### United States v. O'Hagan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-842.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### I. Deceptive use of entrusted information in securities trading can be primary §10(b) fraud

**Holding:** A person who, with scienter, owes an information source a duty of loyalty and confidentiality, feigns fidelity, and secretly uses material nonpublic information entrusted in that relationship for securities trading commits deception in connection with the transaction when the securities use consummates the concealed disloyalty. The source need not be the trading counterparty; a qualifying duty, deception and transaction connection are indispensable, and full disclosure of the trading plan to every person owed the relevant duty removes this theory's deception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh judgment vote on his narrower source-participation ground, which is not a limitation adopted by the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A source's participation in an actual or contemplated securities transaction is not an additional majority requirement. Status, innocent receipt, informational inequality or another person's fraud does not establish primary liability. Disclosure to one principal may leave duties to another; eliminating this securities theory does not authorize conduct prohibited elsewhere.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical rejection of counts 21–37 is reversed, 7–2 in judgment, without deciding preserved instruction, sufficiency or error-effect objections.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chiarella supplies the duty requirement for deceptive silence; Carpenter supplies source-property and fraud principles without settling every securities connection. Central Bank's confined private affirmative-assistance action retains its distinct predicates and supplies neither this primary crime nor a criminal mental-state shortcut.

##### II. Section 14(e) authorizes the bounded preventive rule in this tender-offer setting

**Holding:** Section 14(e)'s express authority to prescribe means reasonably designed to prevent tender-offer fraud permits Rule 14e-3(a), in the source-misappropriation setting presented, to prohibit the covered trading without proof of a fiduciary breach as an additional element. The rule's actual commencement, information, source, knowledge, instrument and public-disclosure conditions remain necessary; the delegation does not authorize treating every informational disparity as fraud or regulate outside the tender-offer and antifraud domain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, Stone-Zsela and Scalia: eight direct votes. Thomas dissents from this application while accepting that the statute permits some preventive rules.

**Material application:** A person must have taken a substantial step or steps to commence, or commenced, a tender offer. The trader must be someone other than the offering person and must possess material information relating to the offer, knowing or having reason to know both that it is nonpublic and that it was acquired, directly or indirectly, from the offering person, the issuer, or an officer, director, partner, employee or other person acting on behalf of either. The prohibition covers purchasing, selling or causing purchase or sale of the securities sought or to be sought in the offer, securities convertible into or exchangeable for them, and options or rights to obtain or dispose of those securities. Its disclosure alternative requires the information **and its source** to be publicly disclosed a reasonable time before trading. Private disclosure to the source, which can eliminate §10(b) deception, does not itself satisfy that separate public-disclosure alternative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Principal-authorized warehousing and every other application are not decided. Preserved pre-offer scope, due-process and trial objections remain; unpreserved contentions are not decided initially by the Court.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical invalidity ruling on counts 38–54 is reversed, 8–1, subject to the rule's actual conditions and unresolved challenges.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** MCI requires action within enacted authority and is distinguished by §14(e)'s express prevention delegation. Chevron is neither generally replaced nor subjected to a new categorical exception.

##### III. The criminal-punishment qualification and unresolved trial questions remain operative

**Holding:** Section 78ff(a) requires a willful violation for criminal punishment and bars imprisonment for that rule violation when the defendant proves lack of knowledge of the rule. That lack-of-knowledge protection does not erase the underlying rule, create general immunity from fines or require the government to prove knowledge of the rule as an element of every violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** This qualification is part of Part II and has the same eight-Justice coalition. It states the limit under which the criminal Rule 14e-3(a) ruling is sustained, not a factual adjudication of O'Hagan's knowledge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule's knowledge or reason-to-know conditions about information and source remain distinct from willfulness and the defendant's lack-of-rule-knowledge protection. O'Hagan's actual knowledge and punishment eligibility are not determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Central Bank's private accessory severe-recklessness standard and Princeton's copyright willfulness rule are not criminal securities standards.

##### IV. The mail-fraud reversal cannot rest on the rejected securities premise

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit's reversal of mail-fraud counts 1–20, insofar as it rested on the securities theories' legal invalidity, must be reversed now that the Court rejects that premise. The case is remanded for consideration of preserved alternate grounds not reached below; money-laundering counts 55–57 were not presented for review and remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer and Stone-Zsela: seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas join the mail-fraud reversal and remand, making that judgment unanimous, but not this entire rationale; Thomas's independent Carpenter ground is separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Mail-fraud reversal and remand are unanimous in judgment, with seven adopting the Court's rationale. Reinstatement of reviewed convictions is appropriate only to the extent they survive preserved instruction, sufficiency, harmless-error and other lawful challenges. No conviction is unconditionally restored; the unreviewed laundering reversal stands.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Thomas's separate Carpenter ground is not a controlling independent alternative holding, and Scalia's agreement in result is not a join of either complete rationale.

#### United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, 503 U.S. 442 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-860.
**Decided:** March 31, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *United States Department of Commerce v. Montana*, March 31, 1992, No. 91-860: whether congress’s equal-proportions apportionment choice is judicially reviewable (Federal Courts — Political questions and constitutional review); whether count i reversal disposes of the distinct automatic-apportionment claim in count ii (Federal Courts — Scope of appellate disposition); whether count i presents a continuing representation injury redressable by review of the allocation formula (Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury).

##### Whether equal proportions satisfies the presented interstate population-equality challenge

**Holding:** In allocating the statutorily fixed House among States using whole Representatives and respecting each State's constitutional minimum, Article I, §2 and the Fourteenth Amendment permit the equal-proportions method's population-based choice of relative representational equality. An alternative's improvement in selected absolute population disparities does not establish that equal proportions violates the constitutional population command when the change worsens other legitimate measures of equality; the Court neither requires equal proportions uniquely nor approves every population-related formula.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine participating Justices adopt this proposition at its stated scope; it controls by an express majority, with no judgment-only or narrower-ground vote.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The House’s size of 435 is statutory, not constitutional. Wesberry and Karcher’s intrastate equality and justification rules remain; no percentage tolerance, new burden sequence, uniquely required method, or approval of every population-based method is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse Count I’s judgment, remove its declaration of invalidity and dissolve its permanent injunction, and direct judgment for the Government on Count I only. Remand Count II without deciding it. The allocation remains operative subject to further lawful order; no seat transfer, new enactment or presidential command is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wesberry, 376 U.S. 1 — preserves its intrastate equal-vote holding while distinguishing allocation of indivisible seats among States; its historical explanation does not select Montana's interstate metric.
- Karcher v. Daggett, 462 U.S. 725 (1983) — distinguishes its intrastate line-drawing problem; its good-faith and specific-justification requirements remain unchanged, with no numerical safe harbor imported here.

#### Voinovich v. Quilter, 507 U.S. 146 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1618.

**Decided:** March 2, 1993.

##### Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts

**Holding:** Section 2 does not prohibit a State from choosing majority-minority districts merely because no federal violation compels their creation; challengers bear at least the initial burden of showing, under the totality of circumstances, unequal electoral opportunity caused by the challenged arrangement. A federal court may order such districts only when their creation is necessary to remedy an established federal-law violation, but that limit does not become a categorical restriction on state redistricting authority; independently applicable federal constitutional and statutory requirements remain enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II.B, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt the complete proposition; it directly controls the rejection of the lower court's statutory prohibition and burden allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): followed for the challengers' burden and the totality-of-circumstances inquiry; no district form is automatically unlawful under Section 2.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: applied to distinguish the State's primary districting authority from federal judicial remedial authority; its actual majority holdings remain controlling.

##### The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting

**Holding:** Assuming that Section 2 reaches the asserted loss of minority influence districts, the claim fails because challengers did not establish sufficiently cohesive white majority voting usually to defeat the minority's preferred candidates. The Gingles prerequisites apply with attention to the claim's nature: an influence claim would require modification or elimination of the first prerequisite that the minority could constitute a district majority, but the Court need not select that adaptation or decide whether influence claims are cognizable because the independently necessary bloc-voting showing is absent.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts II.A and II.C, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this independently dispositive rejection of the presented Section 2 claim and each stated reservation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): applied to the necessity of majority bloc voting and the causal mechanism of dilution; its reservations about influence claims remain unresolved.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: followed for application of the threshold framework to single-member district challenges, with its influence-claim reservation preserved rather than silently decided.

##### The particular finding of intentional discrimination is clearly erroneous

**Holding:** Assuming that the Fifteenth Amendment reaches the asserted vote-dilution claim, the finding that Ohio intentionally injured black voters cannot stand on this record: attention to perceived federal-law requirements and possession of an opponent's speculative documents, without evidence of reliance, do not establish the prohibited purpose the District Court inferred. The Court reverses that finding as clearly erroneous without deciding the Amendment's coverage of dilution or its relationship to race-conscious districting generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this record-specific constitutional disposition; none adopts a general immunity for political objectives or racial awareness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130 (1976): followed in leaving unresolved whether the Fifteenth Amendment reaches vote-dilution claims; the assumption made here creates no new coverage holding.
- Mobile v. Bolden, 446 U.S. 55 (1980): its plurality and separate writings are background to the alleged intentional-discrimination claim, not treated as a single majority rationale or a new categorical rule governing every race-conscious map.

##### Population deviations above ten percent require a genuine justification inquiry

**Holding:** A state legislative plan whose maximum total population deviation exceeds ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring the State to justify the disparity; crossing that threshold does not make preservation of county boundaries legally unavailable as a justification. The District Court must determine whether the actual plan reasonably advances that rational state policy, including its substantiated connection to the deviations and consistency of implementation, and whether the resulting disparities nevertheless exceed constitutional limits; the Court does not decide that Ohio has met its burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this standard and the population-only remand; neither the State's success nor a particular remedial map receives a controlling vote.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Thomson, 462 U.S. 835 (1983): followed for the prima facie threshold, the required connection to a rational state policy, and the separate constitutional limit; departures below ten percent remain generally minor, not categorically immune.
- Mahan v. Howell, 410 U.S. 315 (1973): followed in allowing a substantiated political-subdivision policy to justify departures above ten percent; it does not automatically validate Ohio's particular departures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State remains subject to independent federal constitutional and statutory limits. Influence-claim cognizability and adaptation of the first Gingles prerequisite, Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage and the distinct unpresented racial-classification claim remain unresolved. Neither political aims nor racial awareness receives categorical immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand only the population-equality claim for substantiated county-preservation justification and independent constitutional limits. The statutory and intent grounds do not return for retrial; no map or election date is ordered.

#### Strate v. A-1 Contractors

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1872.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

##### This authorized public highway falls within Montana's nonmember framework

**Holding:** For ordinary public motorists using this lawfully open, state-maintained federal right-of-way, the Tribe lacks the exclusion power that would otherwise govern access; Montana's nonmember framework therefore applies to this road-use tort. That consequence lasts within the authorized public use and neither conveys fee title nor removes every compatible tribal power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The May 8, 1970 grant applies to trust lands only, excludes non-trust property, and acknowledges $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. Tenure is unlimited only while used for the specified highway purpose and remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct a breach of grant terms or applicable regulations, two consecutive years of nonuse for the purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the completion affidavit when construction ends. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. The stated conditions bind and benefit the grantee's successors. No termination trigger is established on this record. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres do not identify the collision parcel; missing incorporated maps and a separate consent instrument establish no grant defect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Montana supplies the nonmember framework; Bourland supplies a particular public-use/exclusion consequence with residual authority preserved. Sac and Fox's Indian-country classification does not settle governing power over this claim.

##### A tribal forum requires an adequate source of governing power

**Holding:** Subject to controlling treaties or statutes and Montana's two exceptions, tribal adjudicatory authority over nonmembers does not exceed the corresponding substantive governing authority. A tribal court cannot compel these defendants to litigate this highway claim solely by proposing to apply another sovereign's law; completed exhaustion supplies no independent jurisdictional grant.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual supply review and initial tribal examination, not unlimited ultimate authority. Williams's resident-member trading relationship differs. Merrion preserves sovereign taxation distinct from private exclusion and rejects surrender by silence.

##### The subcontract does not bring this ordinary highway claim within the consensual exception

**Holding:** Montana's first exception requires a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or its members. Even assuming Stockert's trip served A-1's tribal community-center subcontract and Fredericks resided on the reservation, this non-Indian motorist's ordinary highway-negligence claim lacks that relation; contracting with a tribal enterprise does not submit a contractor to every reservation claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Express forum consent and contractual privity are not universally necessary for the exception. Injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a participant in the consensual activity is reserved; the asserted extra-record forum clause supplies no ground.

##### The supported interests do not satisfy the self-government exception

**Holding:** Montana's second exception preserves authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The serious individual injury, family relationships, assumed residence and ordinary road-safety interests shown here do not establish the threat or direct tribal effect necessary to bring this nonmember highway claim within that exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not deny the injury's real familial or community consequences, but they do not establish the required direct tribal effect. A possible state forum is not the independent reason tribal power is absent; no negligence or limitations result is decided.

##### The jurisdictional judgment does not itself establish every predicate for an injunction

**Holding:** Affirmance requires federal relief consistent with the absence of tribal jurisdiction over the reviewed claim, but does not itself issue an injunction or establish entitlement to every coercive remedy. On remand the federal court must respect the actual claims and lawful parties, immunity limits and ordinary equitable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The jurisdiction-only declaratory consent and resolution do not waive immunity for every claim or remedy. The sons' consortium claims, dismissed insurer, personal jurisdiction, tribal/internal roads, member conduct, express consent, superior federal authorization and distinct police/criminal powers remain outside decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of jurisdiction over Fredericks's reviewed claim unanimously, without further exhaustion or factfinding on the assumed work-trip and residence facts. Eight Associates require relief fitted to lawful parties, immunity and equity; no injunction automatically issues.

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. No new trial, hearing, release, sentencing proceeding, or determination of every admissibility ground follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ornelas*'s review allocation is applied unchanged. *Kimberlin* permits circumstantial proof when motive is an underlying element without adding one here. *Armstrong* remains confined to federal charging and discovery; its requirements and unresolved discrimination remedy do not become police-stop rules.

#### Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-501.

**Decided:** 1998-05-04.

##### Probable cause does not automatically justify additional station-house custody

**Holding:** Probable cause and state or local authority to enforce a fine-only civil licensing ordinance do not, by themselves, establish that transporting an identified operator to a station and confining him is reasonable. The added restraint must reasonably serve the actual enforcement function; nonexclusive possible justifications include securing identification or appearance, ending an actual continuing violation, protecting persons or evidence, or necessary processing in the circumstances. The rule imposes neither least restrictive means nor a duty to issue a citation whenever one is conceivable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer. Those five, including the author, directly adopt this rule and the targeted reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II’s affirmative reservation of actual reasonableness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Fine-only status creates no categorical arrest immunity, and the listed justifications are possibilities rather than findings. The continuing operation, possible daily fines, processing papers, one-hour custody and recognizance release remain relevant supported circumstances. A municipal policy cannot supply its own constitutional justification; the separate warrant theory and settled or abandoned claims remain outside review.

##### No controlling conclusion resolves the actual custody’s reasonableness

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices support targeted reversal and remand, but only Stone-Zsela concludes the actual custody was unreasonable. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer leave that application open; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the supported short enforcement processing reasonable and would affirm.

Part I’s rule controls by direct majority agreement. Part II’s application reservation has four joins, Stone-Zsela’s contrary application has one, and the dissent’s favorable application has four. These positions cannot be combined into a majority holding that this custody was unlawful or lawful, and no Marks synthesis is adopted. The lower courts must proceed under the controlling rule without a Supreme Court finding of ultimate violation, liability or immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim is set aside by targeted reversal and remand, 5–4, with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer supporting. The settled search claim and unappealed probable-cause dismissal remain undisturbed. Policy causation, individual responsibility, conduct-time immunity and relief retain their own predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Whren v. United States:** Preserved for ordinary initial probable-cause stops and applied at its independent execution limit; it does not establish unlimited custody for every civil violation.
- **Ohio v. Robinette:** Its separate later-restraint inquiry informs the new application without a mandatory citation or warning script.
- **Ayeni v. Mottola:** Its purpose-bound execution principle informs the added-restraint inquiry; the home setting and separate immunity decision retain their limits.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas:** History remains relevant to reasonableness, and reversal for a deficient framework need not establish a completed violation. No state arrest rule is automatically constitutionalized.
- **Soldal v. Cook County:** Civil enforcement remains within Fourth Amendment coverage, while reasonableness and liability stay distinct; its summary-judgment remedy does not establish those predicates automatically.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their lawful-arrest premise is preserved; search consequences cannot establish antecedent arrest lawfulness by assumption.
- **County of Riverside v. McLaughlin:** Its timing rule for post-arrest probable-cause review does not itself authorize this custody.
- **United States v. Lanier:** Conduct-time legal clarity remains separate from a present constitutional ruling; no newly stated rule retroactively defeats personal immunity.

#### Knowles v. Iowa, 525 U.S. 113

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-7597; 569 N.W.2d 601 (Iowa 1997)
**Decided:** 1998-12-08.

##### A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception

**Holding:** Issuing a traffic citation without a custodial arrest does not authorize the full search incident to arrest; unexercised authority to make an arrest cannot substitute for the custody that triggers that exception. A full search requires an independently applicable recognized basis, and the citation, general roadside risk and observed speeding supply none for this search; supported exit orders, protective measures, consent searches and searches based on independent probable cause retain their own conditions and scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support. Part II's ordinary-remedial-law return has the eight Associates' support; Stone-Zsela would require the specific suppression stated in his concurrence.

##### The dependent judgment returns under the corrected constitutional rule

**Holding:** The citation-search theory cannot sustain this search or the dependent judgment, which is reversed and remanded for proceedings under the corrected constitutional rule and ordinary, properly available remedial law. No alternative justification is established here, and the Court orders no acquittal, release, replacement sentence, civil relief or universal exclusion rule for traffic encounters.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but would add the express suppression direction stated in his separate opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional rule is unanimous. The eight-Associate remedial return does not adopt Stone-Zsela's separate automatic suppression direction. No alternative search ground is established; independent protective measures, consent and probable-cause grounds retain their own predicates. No acquittal, release, replacement sentence or civil liability is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chimel v. California: applies the officer-safety and evidence-preservation purposes of arrest-incident authority; actual custody matters to the exception's reach.
- United States v. Robinson: preserves the categorical full-search rule after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest; no individualized necessity requirement is added to that setting.
- New York v. Belton: distinguishes the vehicle search incident to arrest from a full search after citation alone.
- Whren v. United States (June 10, 1996): preserves objective justification for the ordinary initial stop and independent limits on later search, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette (November 18, 1996): preserves the distinction between traffic business, subsequent restraint, voluntary consent and any prior illegality.
- Maryland v. Wilson (February 19, 1997): preserves the limited passenger-exit authority without creating automatic frisk or search power.
- Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights (May 4, 1998): applies the distinction between state enforcement authority and the reasonableness of an added intrusion; no actual-custody or immunity question from that case is decided here.
- Terry v. Ohio: preserves the limited protective frisk on its own supported danger conditions.
- Michigan v. Long: preserves a limited protective vehicle search on its distinct danger, weapons-access and scope predicates.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether APA review is available for the §1536 claims and these interests

**Holding:** The consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels supply no other adequate court remedy for the administrative claims. Alleged water restrictions unsupported by the best available scientific and commercial data implicate interests at least arguably protected by the particular §1536(a)(2) data requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous; both APA holdings have nine direct joins.

**Material application:** The interest inquiry concerns the provision sued upon; sound scientific decisionmaking also protects affected persons against unsupported restrictions. The §1540(g)(5) savings clause preserves available rights without itself satisfying every APA condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 702 concerns relief other than money damages and retains other legal or equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. A mandatory or injunctive decree must identify responsible officers and successors by name or title. Section 704 retains final-action and no-other-adequate-remedy conditions; preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal postpones finality only when an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. No decree or unproved exhaustion prerequisite is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dalton’s integrated base-closure scheme supported its confined preclusion holding; no comparable scheme is shown here. Its four-Justice recommendation-finality rationale creates no general bar.

##### Whether the Biological Opinion completes agency action with legal consequences

**Holding:** This Biological Opinion is final because it completes the Service’s consultation decision and its incidental-take statement changes legal exposure under §1536(o)(2) when the statement’s conditions are met. The Bureau’s retained operating discretion does not reduce that completed, legally consequential action to tentative advice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** Practical influence and the Bureau’s announced compliance support causation; the conditional change in taking liability separately establishes legal consequences. Under §1536(a)(2), agencies must use the best available scientific and commercial data to avoid likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat unless the Committee grants an (h) exemption; none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must find no (a)(2) violation or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed to avoid it, and conclude incidental taking will not violate that subsection. Marine-mammal taking additionally requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The statement specifies taking impact, reasonable and prudent minimization, necessary marine-mammal measures and implementing conditions including reporting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section (o)(2) protects only taking compliant with those conditions, not every ESA violation; the separate (o)(1) exemption is not the source of this consequence. No marine-mammal finding is required for these fish. Not every report or prediction is final, and the biological merits, triggered duties, merits application of review standards and final relief remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed dismissal is reversed and remanded, leaving the separate NEPA dismissal undisturbed and ordering no substantive operational relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin permits review of a completed subordinate-agency determination despite a later governmental step; its reserved presidential-status question is not decided.

For Bennett v. Spear, No. 95-813, decided March 19, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Standing to challenge a biological consultation constraint,” and Environmental Law, “Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels,” for the separate constitutional and citizen-suit holdings.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

#### Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, 512 U.S. 504

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-120.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The redistribution provision reasonably limits newly shifted educational costs

**Holding:** Under the governing rule giving an administering agency's interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with that regulation, the Secretary reasonably construes 42 C.F.R. §413.85(c) to bar increased reimbursement resulting from transferring previously medical-college-borne educational costs to the hospital's patient-care account, even when the underlying clinical education is generally eligible. That construction sustains the challenged denial for the reporting year ending June 30, 1985; it does not establish a universal historic-dollar ceiling, make every newly measured expense ineligible, or decide the separate community-support ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. Five participating Justices directly adopt this regulatory construction, its application and its limits.

**Material application:** Section 413.85(a)(1) provides that allowable cost may include the net cost of approved educational activities under paragraph (g), apportioned for Medicare utilization under §413.50. Permission to include those costs is not an unconditional payment command. Paragraph (g) deducts tuition revenue and includes trainee stipends, teacher compensation and other direct and indirect educational-activity costs under Medicare cost-finding principles. The recodified paragraph (a)(2) directs payment for approved residency programs in medicine, osteopathy, dentistry and podiatry to §413.86 for cost reporting periods beginning on or after July 1, 1985. That later-period exception does not itself disallow the reporting year ending June 30, 1985, and the Court fixes no later-period payment or base-year amount.

Section 413.17(a), subject to the separate exception in paragraph (d), includes the cost of services, facilities and supplies furnished by organizations related through common ownership or control at the related organization's cost, capped at the price of comparable items that could be purchased elsewhere. It does not independently make an otherwise excluded educational cost allowable. No party's entitlement under paragraph (d) is decided, and its separate exception is not a premise of this holding. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)'s reasonable-cost system takes direct and indirect costs into account within authorized methods and included-item rules, excludes unnecessary costs in efficient delivery, and bars shifting Medicare costs to non-Medicare patients or the reverse. It does not guarantee payment of every asserted actual expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co., 325 U.S. 410 (1945): applies controlling weight to a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation; the plainly-erroneous-or-inconsistent limit remains operative.
- Udall v. Tallman, 380 U.S. 1 (1965): applies the same own-regulation standard; an alternative reasonable reading does not by itself displace the Secretary's permissible construction.
- Gardebring v. Jenkins, 485 U.S. 415 (1988): preserves the limit where an alternative reading is compelled by the regulation's plain language or other indications of the Secretary's intent at promulgation; no such compulsion is established here.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144 (1991): applies its account of reasonable own-regulation interpretation and administrative expertise; its allocation of interpretive authority under the Occupational Safety and Health Act remains specific to that statutory structure.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves reimbursement within lawful cost methods and limitations, including authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods; actual expenditure alone does not establish entitlement here.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., June 17, 1994: preserves the requirement to respect operative statutory limits; its mandatory-tariff holding does not invalidate this textually supported regulatory restriction or replace the own-regulation standard.
- Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: preserves the distinction between delegated authority and its actual exercise; its treatment of a general reasonable-doubt policy does not render every statement of regulatory intent nonoperative or create a Medicare proof burden.
- Ohio State University v. Secretary, Department of Health and Human Services, 996 F.2d 122 (6th Cir. 1993): rejects its contrary construction insofar as it excludes this transfer of otherwise eligible clinical-education costs from the redistribution provision; its separate community-support analysis is not resolved.

##### The actual agency ground survives the asserted inconsistency

**Holding:** The Administrator's expressed redistribution ground and the established history of college-borne costs support this denial; a memorandum's silence about redistribution and an intermediary's prior allowance do not, on this record, establish an authoritative contrary interpretation that defeats it. A considered, otherwise permissible interpretive change is not automatically invalid, but contrary text, genuine inconsistency and the required agency explanation must be examined, and a reviewing court may not substitute a new ground or missing finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this answer to the consistency and actual-ground objections; it supports application of the redistribution holding rather than furnishing an alternative reimbursement bar.

**Material application:** Letter 78-7 requires that faculty-service costs would be allowable if incurred directly, be allocated consistently among recipient components, comply with related-organization and comparable-price limits, and satisfy the applicable patient-care relation, documentation, reasonable-allocation and reasonable-amount requirements. Its identified additional nonfaculty costs are limited to direct and space costs. It does not permit duplicative additions where the hospital maintains its own corresponding library, administrative structure, office or clerical support. Those conditions survive; the Court does not read silence about redistribution as an unconditional promise of all overhead. The record does not explain the failure to claim reimbursement before 1974, and the Court does not turn that silence into a finding of community support or an estoppel rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court affirms on redistribution alone. It neither endorses nor rejects the separate construction treating all non-Medicare funding as community support; decides no merits of the Administrator's additional university general-and-administrative-cost ruling under §§413.9, 413.85 and Letter 78-7; and orders no reimbursement, penalty, recoupment, reopening of the settled faculty-compensation amounts, or relief on the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim. The original $2,861,247 disputed difference is not adjudicated as an amount remaining after settlement. Materially different retrospective applications, later §413.86 calculations and entitlement under §413.17(d) remain unresolved. No independently sufficient alternative holding is adopted, and no fractured-rationale synthesis is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: applies its rule that considered interpretive change is not automatically disqualifying while preserving scrutiny of text, delegation and reasons; unsupported litigation positions receive no automatic controlling weight.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: applies the requirement of a discernible actual agency rationale; the express redistribution ground satisfies it without a new judicial explanation.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943): preserves review on the agency's own ground rather than a substitute invented on review.
- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): preserves required reasoned agency decisionmaking and the prohibition on appellate rationalizations supplying missing findings.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): preserves the rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; this decision rests on the Administrator's stated rationale and decides no new retroactive-rulemaking question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The challenged reimbursement denial is affirmed, 5–4, on redistribution alone. No additional reimbursement or remand is ordered. The separate community-support construction and general-and-administrative-cost ruling are neither independently sustained nor rejected; settled faculty amounts and the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim remain untouched.
