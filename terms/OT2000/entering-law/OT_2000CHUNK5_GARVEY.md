## Standards and Tests selection: Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.

**Authority by component:**

- Antecedent assent and delegation: Antecedent assent and delegation, 1995-05-22. Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer on the antecedent-assent rule and application; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer alone on the additional conditional actual-delegation review rule (Stone-Zsela reserves that proposition) directly join this proposition.
- Ordinary appellate review: Ordinary appellate review, 1995-05-22. Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-22; the component authorities identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Antecedent assent and delegation; Ordinary appellate review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Clear collective waiver of a statutory discrimination forum

### Clear collective waiver of a statutory discrimination forum

**Governing question:** Whether a collective agreement surrenders an employee’s judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim.

**Current rule:** A union-negotiated surrender of that judicial forum must be clear and unmistakable: the agreement must identify the statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights. Submission of contractual disputes, employment coverage, final grievance decisions or a clause preserving contractual legality does not by itself submit every independent statutory cause. Separate instruments require separate examination. The same employment event can present contractual and statutory questions without merging their sources.

**Authority by component:**

- Clear-and-unmistakable statutory identification and instrument-specific assent: Canonical Decision Record `wright_v_universal_maritime_service_corp_merits_1998-11-16.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum, 1998-11-16; unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-11-16.

**Open question:** Enforceability of a sufficiently explicit collective waiver and general FAA coverage remain undecided.

**Holdings navigation:** [Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp., 525 U.S. 70 — Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum](HOLDINGS.md#neither-instrument-clearly-waives-the-statutory-judicial-forum-1).

## Opening authority: First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan

Source: `state/holdings/business-and-commercial-law.md`.

#### First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-560.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Antecedent assent and delegation

**Holding:** Courts independently determine agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Presenting an objection while denying panel jurisdiction does not itself establish delegation; company submission does not itself establish an owner's personal assent. Where parties actually delegate arbitrability, limited award review governs that decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer on the antecedent-assent rule and application; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer alone on the additional conditional actual-delegation review rule (Stone-Zsela reserves that proposition) directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Broad clauses, incorporated rules and independent nonsignatory-binding theories are not resolved. The antecedent-assent rule has nine joins; the conditional limited-review rule for actual delegation has eight, excluding Stone-Zsela. No judgment-only or fractured-decision aggregation supplies either coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, Inc. v. Communications Workers, 475 U.S. 643: applies clear-and-unmistakable delegation.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees, 489 U.S. 468: applies arbitration's contractual foundation.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: federal coverage supplies no missing assent.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc., March 6, 1995: actual integrated contract terms preserve the antecedent-consent inquiry.

##### Ordinary appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review factual findings for clear error. Arbitration supplies no additional appellate deference to the district judge's independent arbitrability determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, 475 U.S. 643: preserves decisionmaker/merits distinctions.
- Volt, 489 U.S. 468: actual agreement explains arbitral deference; it insulates no district-court legal conclusion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Third Circuit on arbitrability and appellate review. Vacatur of the award against the Kaplans personally remains effective; MKI's separate corporate submission remains distinct. No new FAA merits-vacatur ground or rehearing of agreed arbitration merits is authorized.

## Opening authority: Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp., 525 U.S. 70

Source: `state/holdings/labor-and-employment.md`.

#### Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp., 525 U.S. 70

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-889; Fourth Circuit No. 96-2850
**Decided:** 1998-11-16.

##### Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum

**Holding:** A union-negotiated surrender of an employee's judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim must be clear and unmistakable: the agreement must identify that statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights rather than merely submit contractual disputes. Neither this CBA nor the separate Seniority Plan does so, and their grievance provisions cannot support dismissal of Wright's ADA action for nonexhaustion; whether a sufficiently explicit collective waiver would be enforceable remains undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment vacates the exhaustion dismissal and returns the ADA claim for ordinary proceedings unless another independently lawful ground disposes of it. Neither instrument supports compelled arbitration. Explicit union-waiver enforceability, general FAA coverage, the ADA merits, qualifications and independent defenses remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alexander v. Gardner-Denver Co.: applies the distinction between collective contractual rights and the individual's statutory discrimination claim; no categorical decision on explicit waiver enforceability is added.
- Metropolitan Edison Co. v. NLRB: applies the requirement of a clear waiver of protected statutory rights to the forum question presented.
- Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp.: distinguishes its actual individual statutory-claim agreement; enforceability there does not create assent here.
- AT&T Technologies v. Communications Workers: preserves judicial determination of the submission actually made before arbitration may be compelled.
- First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan (May 22, 1995): applies antecedent assent and independent judicial determination; its arbitrability-delegation rule is not expanded into an already-decided universal collective-waiver rule.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton: applies integrated, instrument-specific construction; no contractual presumption supplies missing statutory-forum language.

## Current-term public authority: Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America — 2000-11-28

Source: `terms/OT2000/workspace/neutral-projection.md`. Each matching law and published-position section is copied below.

### Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America — 2000-11-28

Breyer's unanimous opinion enforces the actual conditional award. A reviewing court must identify an explicit, well-defined, dominant public policy grounded in law and legal precedent and a conflict with the performance the award requires. The transportation regime permits rehabilitation and qualified return; a general safety objection to repeated prior drug use cannot justify vacatur when the award preserves the regime's safeguards. Misco and W. R. Grace supply the law-grounded-policy and conflict inquiry; Enterprise Wheel preserves the unchallenged contractual just-cause assignment. First Options does not replace section 301 labor-award doctrine.

The first award imposed thirty days without pay, substance-abuse-program participation and testing at the employer's or approved professional's discretion over five years. After four negative tests and a second positive test, the second award imposed more than three months without pay, reimbursement of both parties' costs of both arbitrations, continued treatment and random testing, and an undated resignation triggered by another positive test within five years. No future trigger is found. Before safety-sensitive work, independent law requires professional evaluation, certification of compliance with any prescribed rehabilitation, a negative return-to-duty test and at least six random tests during the first year after return. Treatment depends on professional assessment and prescription; the award's program condition remains operative. Employment reinstatement does not establish completed compliance or an entitlement to drive now. A positive test is not a finding of driving under the influence.

The full objection to this award is resolved without determining whether every public-policy objection must rest on performance directly forbidden by positive law. That outer question remains open because the actual policy conflict fails on either view. Different awards contrary to governing legal policy remain reviewable. No general right to reinstatement or new public-policy exception is created.

Authority: [controlling holdings and qualifications](../records/Eastern_Associated_Coal_v_United_Mine_Workers_merits_2000-11-28.md#holdings), [precedent treatment](../records/Eastern_Associated_Coal_v_United_Mine_Workers_merits_2000-11-28.md#precedent-treatment), and [operative procedure](../records/Eastern_Associated_Coal_v_United_Mine_Workers_merits_2000-11-28.md#procedure-after-action).

## Current-term public authority: Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams — 2001-03-21

Source: `terms/OT2000/workspace/neutral-projection.md`. Each matching law and published-position section is copied below.

### Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams — 2001-03-21

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Circuit_City_Stores_v_Adams_merits_2001-03-21.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Circuit_City_Stores_v_Adams_merits_2001-03-21.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Employment contracts fall within the section 1 exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1 of the Federal Arbitration Act excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the Act; its residual employment category is not confined to transportation occupations. Adams's retail employment agreement is excluded, so the FAA supplies no authority to compel arbitration of his claims or to enjoin his state action.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt the statutory construction, its general employment-contract reach, its application and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the employment exclusion must be read with the coverage provision in the same enactment. Section 1 identifies employment contracts of seamen and railroad employees and then adds other classes of workers engaged in interstate or foreign commerce. The shared category can be workers in commerce. The residual words do not expressly restrict the exclusion to transporting persons or goods.

The employer's associated-words argument is substantial, but the inference drawn from a statutory list must remain consistent with the text's contemporary commercial meaning and setting. The named occupations placed important federal employment arrangements beyond doubt; the residual language protects the wider employment category. Reading section 2 to follow the full reach of commerce authority while confining the accompanying labor exclusion to occupations most clearly within federal authority in 1925 would disconnect the provisions. The contemporaneous labor setting confirms the broader boundary where the competing linguistic readings leave uncertainty. Concern about employee choice supports that reading but cannot itself create an exemption.

Allied-Bruce establishes section 2's actual-commerce reach and preserves its operation in state and federal court, but expressly leaves section 1 unresolved. Gilmer concerns statutory claims under a securities-registration agreement and reserves the employment-contract question. Neither supplies a contrary controlling answer. Ordinary assent and revocation principles under First Options and Casarotto govern when their predicates are present; they cannot substitute for the threshold category Congress excluded.

Adams was employed as a retail sales counselor under the employment application containing this arbitration clause. His agreement therefore falls within the exclusion without a finding that its particular procedure denied an effective remedy. The Ninth Circuit correctly removed the federal compulsion and injunction order. No existing Supreme Court holding is overruled.

**Limits and matters not reached:** Otherwise covered commercial agreements remain subject to section 2. An independent state-law basis for arbitration, discrimination liability, and classification of an independent contractor lie outside this employee's presented FAA exclusion question. The Court decides no ordinary contract defense or constitutional jury-waiver question because the statutory exclusion disposes of the requested FAA relief. The state proceeding remains subject to independently applicable law and properly preserved defenses.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson:** Preserved and distinguished. Its actual-commerce construction and section 2 state-court rule continue for otherwise covered agreements; its express employment reservation is resolved here under section 1.
- **Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp.:** Distinguished because the agreement there arose from securities registration. Arbitration of statutory employment claims through such an agreement does not settle the separate employment-contract exclusion.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Preserved for antecedent assent, actual delegation and review in its proper setting. It does not enlarge the FAA's statutory coverage, and no new delegation or award-review rule is adopted.
- **Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto:** Preserved for genuinely general contract defenses and the rule against arbitration-specific disabilities in covered agreements. Those enforcement rules do not replace section 1's antecedent exclusion.
- **Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph:** Its finality, statutory-access and record-specific cost-development holdings remain effective. They supply neither a section 1 answer nor a requirement that this excluded employee first prove prohibitive arbitral costs.
- **Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America:** Its section 301 labor-award and public-policy analysis remains in its distinct statutory setting; it supplies no contrary construction of FAA section 1.

**Resulting law:** Section 1's previously unresolved employment-contract category now excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the FAA, including retail employment. The Act therefore cannot supply compulsion or an injunction for this excluded agreement. Its section 2 rules remain operative for otherwise covered commercial agreements. The exclusion does not declare employment arbitration agreements categorically unenforceable under independent state law, decide the underlying claims, or impose a costs inquiry as a condition of exclusion.

### Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams — 2001-03-21

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** These Justices would reverse the categorical employment exclusion and remand. They read the residual class by the transportation character shared by seamen and railroad employees, giving independent work to both the enumeration and section 1's narrower commerce formulation. The labor context cannot erase that enacted limitation; section 2's broader coverage language does not require an equally broad section 1 exclusion. Gilmer reserved the precise question and thus does not decide it, but neither does that reservation establish the broader exclusion.

Their disagreement concerns coverage, not automatic enforcement. They would preserve actual formation, assent, delegation, clause scope, genuinely general revocation defenses, federal jurisdiction and lawful injunction authority. No new cost hearing follows merely because a defense is conceivable, and Adams's state-law claims do not automatically acquire Green Tree's federal-statutory-access premise. The original federal compulsion suit is distinct from the state-court command challenged by Scalia and Thomas in Allied-Bruce; their join withdraws no such state-court position. Coverage would remove the Ninth Circuit's exclusion ground without automatically reinstating every part of the district court's order or deciding discrimination liability.
