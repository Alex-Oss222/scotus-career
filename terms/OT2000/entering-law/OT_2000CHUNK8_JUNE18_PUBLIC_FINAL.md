## Event

Duncan v. Walker, No. 00-121. Argued March 26, 2001; decided June 18, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, which reinstated Walker's federal habeas application because it treated the pendency of his earlier federal application as other collateral review under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2). The question is whether that subsection excludes federal habeas pendency from the one-year limitation period.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Second Circuit is reversed and the case remanded, 7-2.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower courts must calculate time without the claimed federal-pendency exclusion, address independently preserved and legally available alternatives, and dismiss as untimely if none applies. No writ or automatic extension issues.

## Holdings

#### 1. Federal habeas pendency is outside section 2244(d)(2)

**Holding and operative rule:** The word State in 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review. A federal habeas application's pendency therefore is not excluded from the limitation period by that subsection, even when the application was timely and dismissed without prejudice because exhaustion was unestablished; a civil-rights pleading accompanying it does not change that classification.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and Holding 2. The seven share the textual and structural ground; the opinion does not adopt a categorical objection to independent equitable relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2244(d)(2) excludes time spent in a properly filed application for State post-conviction or other collateral review concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. In this coordinated phrase, the sovereign modifier identifies both forms of review. Reading other collateral review to include every federal application would leave State with little independent work. The broader category remains useful because state procedures vary and state custody may arise outside an ordinary criminal conviction. Congress's express distinction between state and federal proceedings elsewhere reinforces that reading.

Federal habeas is collateral review in ordinary usage, as Walker argues. But membership in that broad category does not answer which sovereign's proceedings Congress selected. State collateral litigation can complete exhaustion; federal habeas invokes the forum subject to the filing period. Rose v. Lundy supplies that exhaustion background without changing the enacted exclusion. A delayed federal dismissal can create serious practical difficulty, but this statutory construction does not depend on a promise that equity saves every applicant or on a conclusion that all equitable protection would frustrate the statute.

Lindh v. Murphy preserves provision-specific temporal treatment of the identified pre-enactment application; it does not exempt every later application about the conviction. Slack v. McDaniel preserves the first-application classification of its exhaustion return while expressly retaining independent timing requirements. Artuz v. Bennett distinguishes filing conditions from claim-relief bars; it does not turn a federal application into state review. Thus neither an initial timely filing nor non-successive status permanently satisfies the limitation for a distinct later application. Proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency remain independent requirements even for state proceedings.

#### 2. The statutory error requires reversal, with actual independent alternatives preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's reinstatement cannot rest on excluding Walker's first federal proceeding under §2244(d)(2). On remand the courts must calculate timeliness without that exclusion and adjudicate any independently preserved, legally available commencement or equitable ground under the applicable law and actual record; if none makes the application timely, it must be dismissed as untimely.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices directly join the remedial holding; agreement by Ginsburg and Breyer that independent alternatives remain available if their statutory view fails is not needed to establish its controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Walker's first application was dismissed without prejudice on July 9, 1996 because its allegations did not establish exhaustion. His May 20, 1997 application came after the appellate court's April 24, 1997 transitional deadline. That deadline is the premise of this review, not a ruling on every commencement dispute. The first dismissal left roughly nine months under that calculation, the later claims differed, and Walker did not return to state court. Those facts do not establish that court delay necessarily caused the late refiling. They also do not adjudicate every equitable contention.

The Second Circuit did not reach the equitable argument actually raised. Rejection of a statutory exclusion cannot substitute for adjudication of that independent argument on its own legal and factual terms. No equitable entitlement, finding of diligence, impediment, identical claims or automatic extension is supplied. There is no still-pending first application or stay request for this Court to administer. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted application on the merits; it does not require such denial. The Court orders no universal warning, election, dismissal deadline, or stay-and-abeyance procedure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general availability and conditions of equitable tolling, including court misdirection or extraordinary external impediments, are outside the statutory question reviewed and lack supporting findings for a ruling here. The requirements for staying a mixed petition likewise lie outside that question. The underlying constitutional claims, ultimate entitlement to habeas relief, and comprehensive classification of state filing rules are not decided. Adjudicating a preserved alternative on remand is remaining legal and factual work under its own applicable law, not an award of relief. The first dismissal remains nonmerits; no writ, release, resentencing or automatic extension issues.

## Precedent Treatment

- Rose v. Lundy supplies the exhaustion distinction between state review and federal habeas; its actual exhaustion rule remains unchanged and creates no compulsory stay regime here.
- Lindh v. Murphy retains its provision-specific protection of the identified pre-enactment application; it is distinguished from a permanent exemption for later applications concerning the same conviction.
- Slack v. McDaniel is reaffirmed within its exhaustion-return, non-successive-application scope. That classification supplies no limitations reset and leaves independent timing, exhaustion and default requirements intact.
- Artuz v. Bennett remains controlling on application-filing conditions as distinct from claim-relief bars. It does not decide the sovereign category or excuse a genuine filing defect.
- Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs and United States v. Brockamp do not decide this AEDPA statutory exclusion; their provision-sensitive treatment of equity supplies no ruling here either granting Walker tolling or foreclosing every lawful equitable ground.

## Law After Decision

Section 2244(d)(2) excludes qualifying state collateral pendency, not federal habeas pendency. A timely first federal application and a later application's non-successive classification do not by themselves satisfy the later application's limitation period. The Court resolves the sovereign-category question while retaining proper-filing, pertinent-judgment-or-claim and pendency conditions. Independent commencement rules and properly preserved equitable contentions require their own legal and factual grounds; the decision neither grants relief through them nor extinguishes them. Lindh, Slack and Artuz retain their distinct force.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Souter, concurs. They join the Court's textual construction and remand, but emphasize that this subsection's omission of federal pendency does not itself extinguish otherwise lawful equitable protection or permissible retention of a federal application while exhaustion is completed. Rose's exhaustion requirement should not be mistaken for a categorical prohibition on lawful protection against loss of a timely federal opportunity. Irwin's treatment of equitable tolling and Brockamp's attention to a particular statutory scheme require examination of the actual source and conditions, not a universal equation between every equitable accommodation and amendment of §2244(d)(2). They establish no entitlement for Walker, compulsory stay rule, warning duty or automatic suspension. Their affirmative safeguard explanation is separate from, and broader than, the Court's preservation of the actual alternative without deciding its general availability.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Ginsburg, dissents. Federal habeas is other collateral review concerning a state judgment, and the competing grammatical and neighboring-statutory arguments do not uniquely require State to govern that separate category. They would read the subsection to exclude qualifying federal pendency so that ordinary exhaustion administration does not randomly consume a timely first opportunity through the duration of a nonmerits federal proceeding. The rule would still require proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim and actual pendency; it would not make one timely filing permanently sufficient. They would affirm reinstatement and remand for the application's independent exhaustion and other remaining requirements, awarding no writ. If the Court's construction governs, they agree that the actual equitable alternative must remain open for lawful adjudication.

## Procedure After Action

The Second Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the statutory construction and preserved-alternative instructions. The federal habeas proceeding returns for timeliness adjudication; no merits determination or release is ordered. The April 24, 1997 deadline remains the review premise rather than a universal transition holding. If no independently available basis establishes timeliness after the required calculation, dismissal as untimely follows.

## Event

Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education, 185 F.3d 337; Fifth Circuit Nos. 97-30879 and 98-30132. Decided June 18, 2001. On merits review of the Fifth Circuit judgment affirming an Eastern District of Louisiana injunction against the board's mandatory evolution disclaimer. The question is whether the Establishment Clause permits the complete required statement, which references biblical creation, disclaims an intention to influence or dissuade that account or any other concept, and recognizes a student's choice to form an opinion or retain parental beliefs. The district court found no secular purpose; the appellate court accepted the secular objectives of disclaiming inferred orthodoxy and reducing offense but found an impermissible effect. The constitutional question, not fee calculation, is under review.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining the injunction against the mandatory disclaimer is affirmed, 6-3.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The existing injunction against requiring the complete disclaimer remains in force. The judgment requires no replacement statement and prohibits neither objective religious study nor an otherwise lawful general statement protecting against compelled professions. No damages or additional curricular directive issues.

## Holdings

#### The board may protect personal conscience but may not make preservation of a religious account part of its instruction

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may distinguish academic instruction from compulsory personal belief, but it may not require teachers, through a targeted curricular statement, to assure students that instruction will preserve a specified religious account. The board's mandatory evolution disclaimer, considered as a whole with its parental-belief and critical-thinking language, performs that prohibited official religious function; the injunction against requiring that statement is affirmed.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six direct rationale votes control the constitutional application and the unchanged whole-statement remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon as one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that inquiry, not independent prongs. The governmental act here is the board's own message, required of teachers immediately before evolution instruction. Its official attribution establishes who speaks; its text and actual use establish the prohibited function.

The board identifies biblical creation, assures students that the instruction is not intended to influence or dissuade that account or any other concept, and couples that assurance with their right to form an opinion or retain parental beliefs. The actual word or preserves choice. Evolution remains taught, students need not recite anything, and no prayer, profession, punishment or conversion is shown. Those points support the board's claim of conscience protection, but do not eliminate its selective undertaking to shelter a religious account through science instruction. The references to other concepts and critical thinking do not change that official task. The adoption history, including retention of the biblical reference after the creation-science controversy, confirms the function apparent from the complete policy rather than substituting a motive test for it.

Epperson v. Arkansas and Edwards v. Aguillard distinguish curricular authority from tailoring science instruction to religious doctrine. Settle preserves genuine academic study and legitimate pedagogical limits. Good News Club protects genuinely private religious access; it does not make this mandatory teacher message private. Marshfield requires examination of continuing public conduct, and Children's Healthcare permits a limited nondevotional accommodation rather than every religious preference. Lee and Santa Fe preserve prohibitions on official school religious activity; absence of prayer removes one possible violation but does not resolve this different use of public authority. Once that prohibited function is established, proof of successful persuasion or a completed penalty is unnecessary. Sincere secular objectives cannot justify religious means.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the complete adopted disclaimer. Its introductory paragraph directs teachers; its next two paragraphs are the statement delivered to pupils. Neither the first spoken paragraph by itself nor a severed replacement policy is adjudicated, because that distinct policy is outside the reviewed whole-disclaimer judgment. Mention of the Bible, religious motivation, offense or official attribution alone is not the rule. Objective religious study, academic criticism, private expression and otherwise lawful evenhanded protection against compelled professions remain permissible. A materially different general conscience notice is outside the presented question. No independent fee-calculation question is presented; the existing fee judgment is undisturbed, with no new fee rule. The remedy requires no evidence of conversion, new factual proceeding, damages or replacement curriculum.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton applies unchanged: attribution and actual function, with purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence, determine this official religious activity. No independent Lemon prong is restored.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing only as modified by Agostini; the lower court's independent effect/endorsement method is not adopted.
- Epperson v. Arkansas supplies the restriction against tailoring public science instruction to religious doctrine; its curricular boundary applies within the governing functional inquiry.
- Edwards v. Aguillard supplies the distinction between legitimate instruction and religiously directed curricular action; the present decision does not revive a freestanding purpose test or treat academic discussion of creation beliefs as forbidden.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board preserves genuine academic religious study and legitimate pedagogical requirements, while withholding immunity from religious curricular exclusions.
- Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe retain their official religious activity and school-created pressure protections. This statement is not prayer, and no new coercion finding is inferred from those decisions.
- Good News Club v. Milford Central School is distinguished: its protected access is genuinely private, while this required teacher statement is the board's own curricular act.
- Freedom From Religion Foundation v. City of Marshfield applies the need to examine actual continuing governmental religious conduct rather than rely on labels or possible observer offense.
- Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty v. Min De Parle retains the permission for its bounded secular-care accommodation and actual-function review; it supplies no general authorization to protect favored doctrine through official instruction.

## Law After Decision

The existing unified establishment rule applies to mandatory curricular assurances as well as other official religious acts. Schools remain free to teach science, examine religious accounts academically and protect personal conscience, but may not undertake official preservation of a selected religious account through the targeted statement held unlawful here. The holding rests on the complete policy's function, not its mere mention of religion or proof of student reaction. The former independent Lemon tests remain displaced. The existing injunction and dependent fee judgment remain undisturbed; no severed substitute text is adopted.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. They apply Agostini's governing attribution-and-function inquiry but read the complete statement as an assurance that learning the scientific account does not compel personal belief. The actual or permits students either to form their own views or to maintain parental beliefs; the notice refers to other concepts and calls for critical examination while leaving evolution instruction in place. Official speech is conceded, but they find neither adoption of religious truth, religious instruction nor solicitation of adherence in those operative directions.

The retained biblical example and adoption history are substantial contrary evidence. In their view those features do not transform the text's reassurance into religious governance. Scalia's Edwards analysis distinguishes actual curricular commands from inferred motives; Kennedy's official-school-religion concern in Lee and Santa Fe remains, but the representative religious-choice mechanism at issue in Santa Fe is absent here. Their common ground does not turn absence of legal compulsion into universal immunity, allow a parental curriculum veto, prescribe creation teaching, or authorize religious grading. They would reverse the federal constitutional ruling and remand for conforming lawful relief, leaving dependent fee consequences to ordinary proceedings without choosing an amount. The disagreement concerns this policy's function, not revival of the lower court's independent tests.

The Fifth Circuit's judgment sustaining the injunction is affirmed. The bar against requiring the complete April 19, 1994 disclaimer continues in force; no replacement curriculum, new evidentiary proceeding or separate fee calculation is ordered. Lawful academic instruction, private expression and a materially different general conscience notice are not prohibited by this decree.

## Event

Idaho v. United States, No. 00-189. Argued April 23, 2001; decided June 18, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed the district court's quiet-title decree after a nine-day trial. The United States sued as trustee, and the Coeur d'Alene Tribe intervened. The question is whether Congress's prestatehood treatment of the reservation, including authorization of partial-cession negotiations, clearly retained the disputed submerged lands against Idaho's otherwise presumptive title. Idaho concedes executive inclusion and congressional notice and does not challenge the historical findings.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment sustaining the geographically bounded trust-title decree is affirmed, 6-3.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6-3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The existing decree remains in force: United States trustee title; the Tribe's beneficial interest and exclusive use, occupancy and quiet enjoyment; the injunction against Idaho's adverse claim; and denial of the State's counterclaim. The decree applies only within the boundaries described by the March 3, 1891 and August 15, 1894 Acts and excludes the waters and submerged lands claimed by Idaho within Heyburn State Park. Its existing costs provisions remain undisturbed. No new geographic line or general regulatory decree issues.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Holdings

#### Congress's timely treatment of the identified reservation retained its unceded submerged estate

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal retention against future state ownership requires both clear inclusion of the submerged lands and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose. With inclusion and congressional knowledge conceded here, Congress's enacted prestatehood treatment of the defined Coeur d'Alene Reservation as an estate protected pending consensual, compensated acquisition clearly retained its unceded beds and banks; Idaho did not acquire the disputed estate at admission on July 3, 1890.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the rule, its application and affirmance of the actual decree.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Alaska requires separate clarity about inclusion and the intention to defeat state title; geography or purpose alone is insufficient. Shively v. Bowlby recognizes Congress's territorial power to retain beds for an appropriate public purpose. The conceded executive inclusion and congressional notice establish the identified estate, not the second requirement.

Congress's enacted treatment supplies that additional showing. The 1886 authorization concerned cession outside the existing reservation and required ratification. The 1888 railroad measure recognized the reservation, withheld rights until actual survey plats were filed and approved by the Secretary and compensation was fixed and paid, required tribal consent before rights accrued, and protected existing tribal tenure. The 1889 enactment authorized purchase only of nonagricultural portions chiefly valuable for minerals and timber that the Tribe consented to sell, on just and equitable terms and subject to congressional ratification. Read together, those measures preserved a known estate while Congress obtained particular portions through a limited consensual process. The lower lake and river belonged to the unceded remainder. The accepted admission disclaimer corroborates that specific retention; it is not independently sufficient for every Indian reservation.

Idaho correctly distinguishes protected use from title and notes that the agreements were unratified at admission. But the Court relies on the earlier statutes' own operative treatment, not on prematurely binding agreements. The agricultural and resource description defines the authorized purchase; it does not erase Congress's protection of the remainder. Transferring that remainder silently at statehood would defeat the arrangement Congress maintained. Tribal water uses explain the understood object and purpose, but cannot substitute for enacted authority. The Senate's June 1890 vote was not law. The March 1891 ratification confirms the earlier arrangement and does not newly divest a title that vested in Idaho. Alaska's legally effective interim segregation supports examining present legal effect; it does not equate every negotiation with a completed reservation or demand a particular verbal formula.

**Operative reach and remedy:** The affirmed decree quiets title in the United States as trustee, recognizes the Tribe's beneficial interest and exclusive use, occupancy and quiet enjoyment, enjoins Idaho's adverse claim to the covered beds and banks, and denies its counterclaim. It covers the reservation boundaries described by the March 3, 1891 and August 15, 1894 Acts, including the relevant Lake Coeur d'Alene and St. Joe River portions, but expressly excludes waters and submerged lands claimed by Idaho within Heyburn State Park. The existing decree's costs to the Tribe and agreement that the United States and Idaho bear their own costs are undisturbed; no separate new costs disposition is made.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not retain every navigable bed enclosed by an executive boundary, make a Senate vote law, dispense with a ratification condition, or use a general Indian-land disclaimer as an automatic title exception. The prior Coeur d'Alene immunity case did not decide this ownership or bar the United States' own suit. No title to the ceded northern lake, other aboriginal territory or the excluded Heyburn estate is adjudicated, and no new coordinates are supplied. The independent aboriginal-title theory was not decided below or developed on its merits here and may require first-instance factual consideration; affirmance on the reservation ground does not decide it. The suggested ratification relation-back theory supplies no alternative holding because the timely enacted ground suffices; no retroactive divestiture is authorized. Fishing restrictions, taxes, environmental regulation and jurisdiction over nonmembers are outside the title question. Recognition of title adds no damages, forced conveyance or general regulatory injunction.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Alaska is applied without weakening either clear-inclusion or clear-title-defeat requirement. Its legally operative reservation and interim-segregation holdings support examination of this different enacted record; its later implementing decree did not change the test or authorize retroactive confiscation.
- Shively v. Bowlby supplies Congress's territorial power to retain submerged property for an appropriate public purpose, subject to the distinct clear-intent inquiry.
- Montana v. United States supplies the strong presumption of state title and the need to distinguish reservation description from intent to withhold navigable beds. The present specifically identified estate and enacted treatment satisfy what mere inclusion would not.
- Utah Division of State Lands v. United States supplies the separate inquiry into timely intention to defeat state title; its insufficient reservoir-control showing does not make a particular verbal formula indispensable here.
- Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho retains its prospective-officer and immunity distinctions. It adjudicated neither ownership nor this separate United States trust-title suit; no title holding is inferred from it.
- Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley remains a rule about the presented nonmember fee-land taxation. It does not resolve title or convert this ownership judgment into general tribal regulatory power.

## Law After Decision

Alaska's two independent clear-showing requirements remain unchanged. Congress can manifest the necessary retention through the timely legal treatment of a specifically identified estate; the 1888 and 1889 enactments, read in their full prestatehood setting, do so here. Executive geography, cultural importance, unratified negotiations or a general disclaimer alone remain insufficient. The presently adjudicated beds and banks remain in federal trust for the Tribe under the actual decree, with Heyburn excluded. Ownership and regulatory authority remain distinct; neither an alternative aboriginal-title rule nor a ratification relation-back rule is added.

## Separate Writings

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissents. They accept the distinct inquiries into clear inclusion and timely defeat of state title, but conclude that these enactments do not satisfy the second. Congress could require consent, compensation and preservation of present tribal tenure while leaving the future State's ownership of navigable beds intact. The 1889 enactment authorized a contingent purchase of designated resources; neither that negotiation process nor the unratified agreements clearly resolved the separate statehood consequence.

Alaska involved operative statehood provisions with identified title-preserving effect. Its protection of a legally effective temporary segregation does not make every contemplated acquisition a comparable withdrawal. The dissent does not require express words or a special formula. It recognizes Congress's knowledge and the Tribe's water uses but considers those insufficient to bridge use and ownership. Postadmission ratification cannot provide a timely consequence absent from the earlier enactments, and the general disclaimer cannot independently cure that deficiency.

They would vacate the reservation-based judgment and remand for lawful consideration of the preserved independent aboriginal-title ground, which was not adjudicated below and was not developed on its merits in this Court. They do not resolve that alternative, decide relation back, direct unconditional quiet title in Idaho, expand the controversy into Heyburn State Park or add an automatic interim injunction. Scalia and Thomas's distinct Alaska position concerning the post-1953 Submerged Lands Act is not a ground governing Idaho's 1890 admission and is not attributed to Kennedy.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed, preserving the district court's August 14, 1998 quiet-title decree at its actual boundaries and with its express Heyburn exception. The decree's title, possession, fitted adverse-claim injunction and counterclaim provisions remain operative. The existing award of costs to the Tribe and the United States' and Idaho's agreement to bear their own costs are undisturbed. No evidentiary remand, new survey, enlargement to ceded land, damages award or general regulatory injunction is ordered.

Alaska involved operative statehood provisions with identified title-preserving effect. Its protection of a legally effective temporary segregation does not make every contemplated acquisition a comparable withdrawal. The dissent does not require express words or a special formula. It recognizes Congress's knowledge and the Tribe's water uses but considers those insufficient to bridge use and ownership. Postadmission ratification cannot provide a timely consequence absent from the earlier enactments, and the general disclaimer cannot independently cure that deficiency.

They would vacate the reservation-based judgment and remand for lawful consideration of the preserved independent aboriginal-title ground, which was not adjudicated below and was not developed on its merits in this Court. They do not resolve that alternative, decide relation back, direct unconditional quiet title in Idaho, expand the controversy into Heyburn State Park or add an automatic interim injunction. Scalia and Thomas's distinct Alaska position concerning the post-1953 Submerged Lands Act is not a ground governing Idaho's 1890 admission and is not attributed to Kennedy.

The dissent's insufficiency conclusion and proposed alternative-ground remand are noncontrolling. No separately published concurrence or new regulatory position is attributed to another Justice; no external consequence is admitted.
