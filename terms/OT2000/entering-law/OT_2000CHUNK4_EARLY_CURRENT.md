# Current-term law and published continuity for March 20-21, 2001

### City of Indianapolis v. Edmond — 2000-11-28

Souter's seven-Justice opinion (Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer joining) holds that a checkpoint stop is a seizure before exterior canine examination. A program principally directed to gathering evidence of ordinary narcotics offenses requires an independently lawful justification for dispensing with individualized suspicion; brief systematic stops and ancillary license or sobriety checks do not supply it. The admitted primary objective resolves this program's purpose without a fact-finding remand. Program instructions, official statements and actual practices may establish purpose; no statistical study, universal efficacy threshold, catastrophe requirement or exhaustion of every less intrusive alternative is required.

Whren's probable-cause rule does not establish authority for stopping everyone without suspicion. Chandler requires an actual adequate need; Sitz's highway-safety and Martinez-Fuerte's border settings remain distinct. Prouse supplies no general authorization from systematic selection; Bond preserves the distinction between lawful presence and separate intrusion without adding a checkpoint-purpose test. Comparable ordinary crime-detection roadblocks fall within the rule, but genuinely justified programs and lawful action on evidence encountered during them retain their own requirements. The Court reserves expansion of a program principally justified by license or sobriety checking to narcotics detection. Urgent threats, emergency roadblocks, airport or government-building security, other border settings, individualized-suspicion searches and the particular canine examination remain outside the challenge. The antecedent seizure makes the dog-sniff classification unnecessary to decide. The 7-2 affirmance and remand require preliminary prospective restraint while litigation continues; no replacement program, suppression or damages follows.

### Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America — 2000-11-28

Breyer's unanimous opinion enforces the actual conditional award. A reviewing court must identify an explicit, well-defined, dominant public policy grounded in law and legal precedent and a conflict with the performance the award requires. The transportation regime permits rehabilitation and qualified return; a general safety objection to repeated prior drug use cannot justify vacatur when the award preserves the regime's safeguards. Misco and W. R. Grace supply the law-grounded-policy and conflict inquiry; Enterprise Wheel preserves the unchallenged contractual just-cause assignment. First Options does not replace section 301 labor-award doctrine.

The first award imposed thirty days without pay, substance-abuse-program participation and testing at the employer's or approved professional's discretion over five years. After four negative tests and a second positive test, the second award imposed more than three months without pay, reimbursement of both parties' costs of both arbitrations, continued treatment and random testing, and an undated resignation triggered by another positive test within five years. No future trigger is found. Before safety-sensitive work, independent law requires professional evaluation, certification of compliance with any prescribed rehabilitation, a negative return-to-duty test and at least six random tests during the first year after return. Treatment depends on professional assessment and prescription; the award's program condition remains operative. Employment reinstatement does not establish completed compliance or an entitlement to drive now. A positive test is not a finding of driving under the influence.

The full objection to this award is resolved without determining whether every public-policy objection must rest on performance directly forbidden by positive law. That outer question remains open because the actual policy conflict fails on either view. Different awards contrary to governing legal policy remain reviewable. No general right to reinstatement or new public-policy exception is created.

Authority: [controlling holdings and qualifications](../records/Eastern_Associated_Coal_v_United_Mine_Workers_merits_2000-11-28.md#holdings), [precedent treatment](../records/Eastern_Associated_Coal_v_United_Mine_Workers_merits_2000-11-28.md#precedent-treatment), and [operative procedure](../records/Eastern_Associated_Coal_v_United_Mine_Workers_merits_2000-11-28.md#procedure-after-action).

### Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph — 2000-12-11

Part I of Stevens's opinion has all nine joins: compelling arbitration and dismissing every claim ends the action and is final and appealable under 9 U.S.C. §16(a)(3). Catlin's ordinary finality rule applies; a possible later award proceeding is separate. Digital Equipment's restrictions on premature collateral appeals do not bar this final appeal. A stay leaving claims pending is different, and appealability does not approve dismissal instead of a stay.

Part II-A also has all nine joins: contractual silence about arbitral costs alone proves neither unenforceability nor affordability, and genuinely prohibitive required costs may prevent effective assertion of federal statutory rights. Part II-B has five direct joins—Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—and requires focused development of the likely forum, mandatory access costs, allocation and the claimant's financial access on this record. Randolph's assertion of inability and the contract's silence establish no likely fee or allocation, but the absence of those facts does not establish an accessible forum. Substantiation of an actual obstacle is required during the appropriate proceedings. The categorical cost ruling is vacated 5-4; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse it on the deficient showing without the required new cost opportunity.

Mitsubishi and Gilmer preserve effective substantive statutory protection while allowing arbitration; Allied-Bruce preserves actual enforceability defenses after coverage. First Options preserves the relevant antecedent independent inquiry within the agreement's actual consent and delegation limits, without deciding a coverage or consent dispute here. The access inquiry concerns likely mandatory charges and this claimant's ability to pursue these claims, not a general right to costless dispute resolution or a universal lender-payment duty. A binding allocation or undertaking may remove an obstacle; a nonbinding appellate assurance does not amend the agreement. No finding that discovery was requested, denied or exhausted supports or is needed for this remand.

No majority adopts a complete general initial-proof rule for the trigger, consequences of nonproduction and unavailable cost information beyond the supported-obstacle requirement and this focused opportunity. Stone-Zsela's concrete-likelihood formulation includes ordinary-procedure disclosure of material information controlled by the party seeking compulsion and this opportunity to develop the objection. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas use their likelihood requirement to reject this showing without that required opportunity and do not adopt his disclosure qualification. Their shared terminology supplies no identical operative majority rule. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject deriving individual-access allocation merely from categorical congressional exclusion of statutory claims, without supplying a comprehensive replacement; Breyer joins the inquiry but reserves that broader critique. No narrowest-rationale aggregation resolves the division, and the current remand is complete despite the unresolved general allocation.

If the obstacle is unproved or lawfully removed, the cost objection does not prevent arbitration. If established, enforcement must be withheld to the necessary extent, subject to a lawful binding solution. Properly presented independent grounds remain, including the class-action contention not decided below. The Court decides neither TILA or ECOA merits, class relief, unrelated terms, nor whether the district court originally should have stayed the case. No unconditional arbitration mandate follows.

Authority: [controlling holdings and qualifications](../records/Green_Tree_Financial_v_Randolph_merits_2000-12-11.md#holdings), [precedent treatment](../records/Green_Tree_Financial_v_Randolph_merits_2000-12-11.md#precedent-treatment), and [operative procedure](../records/Green_Tree_Financial_v_Randolph_merits_2000-12-11.md#procedure-after-action).

### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The asserted bird-habitat criterion does not supply section 404 authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 404 of the Clean Water Act does not authorize federal jurisdiction over the isolated, nonnavigable intrastate ponds presented solely because migratory birds use them. The Corps must identify authority within the enacted water program; an asserted interstate environmental benefit alone does not supply the delegated jurisdiction, and the challenged jurisdiction and permit-denial ground must be set aside to the extent they depend exclusively on that criterion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Five Justices directly adopt this statutory ground, its limits and reversal with remand. The Court does not reach the contingent Commerce Clause question.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 404(a) authorizes permits for discharge of dredged or fill material into navigable waters. Section 502(7) defines those waters to include waters of the United States and the territorial seas. That expansion reaches beyond classical navigation without making the enacted water limitation meaningless. Riverside Bayview sustained adjacent wetlands because of their statutory and ecological relationship to covered waters and expressly left nonadjacent waters open. These isolated ponds are not wetlands under the Corps' vegetation definition and are not alleged to adjoin navigable waters. Bird use does not itself supply that missing statutory relationship.

Section 404(g)(1) allows state administration outside the waters reserved to federal administration, confirming coverage beyond traditional navigation without defining every remaining pond as covered. Section 101(a)'s biological-integrity and wildlife objectives operate consistently with the Act's provisions and alongside section 101(b)'s protection of state responsibilities. Neither purpose alone settles the disputed jurisdiction. MCI and FDA v. Brown & Williamson require authority in the connected statutory scheme before deference; Sweet Home construed a different enacted habitat-injury definition and supplies no substitute delegation here.

The Corps' change of position is not automatically disqualifying, and the preamble's form is not an independent reason to invalidate it. No universal rule governing Chevron, Skidmore or interpretation of an agency's own regulation is necessary: the asserted reach exceeds the statute on its terms. Christensen's limited advisory-letter holding is not enlarged. Because the statutory answer disposes of this assertion, Congress's possible broader power is not decided. The Court neither imposes present navigability as a universal prerequisite nor determines all isolated waters or materially different supported statutory connections.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes: preserves its adjacent-wetland construction and ecological reasoning, distinguishing the expressly reserved isolated-water application rather than treating adjacency as a universal statutory test.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: applies the requirement that an agency act within the actual statutory grant; an administrative criterion cannot supply omitted authority.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.: applies analysis of the connected statutory scheme before deference; the decision supplies no general rule against consequential agency regulation.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon: distinguishes its habitat-injury construction under a different statutory definition and preserves its actual-injury and causation limits.
- Christensen v. Harris County: leaves intact its specific advisory-letter and own-regulation holdings; it does not establish a categorical rule against every preamble or informal interpretation.
- United States v. Lopez, National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, and United States v. Morrison: leave their actual Commerce rules unchanged because the statutory ground makes the contingent constitutional question unnecessary.

## Earlier published noncontrolling positions

### City of Indianapolis v. Edmond — 2000-11-28

**Thomas, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia.** The dissent would reverse because this program genuinely performs systematic license and sobriety checks that provide an independent roadway justification. Under the dissent's reading of Whren and the checkpoint precedents, the added narcotics objective and exterior dog use do not invalidate a stop whose actual operation stays within that legitimate scope. The dissent answers the concern about nominal checks by requiring real, independently supportable roadway functions and lawful execution; it does not approve a drug-only checkpoint merely because it efficiently detects crime. It would return the case for proceedings under that objective mixed-function rule rather than approve every execution detail.

**Thomas, dissenting, Part II, joined by no other Justice.** Thomas would apply the restrained scope and objective reasonableness analysis of the checkpoint cases to these brief systematic automobile seizures. He rejects a primary-purpose threshold transferred from suspicionless searches of bodies, homes, or businesses. He also questions whether indiscriminate stops accord with the Fourth Amendment's original understanding, but does not seek to overrule Sitz or Martinez-Fuerte here because that issue was not developed for decision. Scalia joins neither this additional critique nor that reservation.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/City_of_Indianapolis_v_Edmond_merits_2000-11-28.md#separate-writings).

### Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph — 2000-12-11

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone-Zsela joins the entire Court opinion and would require Randolph to identify a concrete likelihood of required costs preventing effective statutory vindication. Her sworn resources, applicable schedules, contracts, forum correspondence, and other competent evidence may substantiate that objection; neither statistical research nor prior payment is necessary. Through ordinary focused procedure, the party seeking compulsion must disclose materially relevant cost and allocation information within its control. Contractual silence cannot become an impossible demand for concealed facts. He would compel arbitration if the obstacle is unproved or lawfully removed, otherwise withhold enforcement to the necessary extent, while preserving unresolved independent grounds. His initial likelihood and disclosure formulation is not attributed to the other remand Justices or treated as a complete majority rule.

**Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Stevens and Souter.** These Justices distinguish whether an arbitral forum is capable of deciding a category of federal claims from whether this claimant can afford to use it. A burden imposed on a party claiming that Congress excluded a statutory category from arbitration does not itself establish the same burden for financial access. They would resolve the concrete forum, allocation, lender practice, and affordability questions through the focused opportunity, with substantiation required, without announcing a comprehensive substitute burden rule. Contract silence proves neither prohibitive cost nor accessibility, and a nonbinding assurance is no enforceable allocation. Breyer joins the Court's inquiry and access ground but not this broader burden critique.

**O'Connor, dissenting from the cost disposition, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.** These Justices join finality and reject both silence-based invalidity and indifference to a genuine statutory-access barrier. They would require the resisting party to demonstrate a likelihood of prohibitive expense and reverse the cost ruling because no likely forum, charges, or amount chargeable to Randolph was established. Exact advance billing is unnecessary, but a conjectural risk should not mandate cost-development litigation. They would preserve ordinary properly supported procedural grounds and the unresolved class-action alternative on remand, without ordering unconditional arbitration or deciding statutory merits. Their disagreement is with the required new cost opportunity on this showing, not the possibility that a demonstrated cost barrier can defeat enforcement.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/Green_Tree_Financial_v_Randolph_merits_2000-12-11.md#separate-writings).

### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Souter dissents, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. They read the defined waters term, the aquatic-protection objective and section 404(g)(1)'s state-administration structure to authorize this supported habitat-water application. Riverside Bayview leaves isolated waters open; its adjacency facts do not establish an exhaustive limit. Sweet Home and PUD No. 1 support applying broad protective language according to its actual conditions. The agency's interpretation reinforces their textual conclusion without turning every preamble into a source of binding authority or disregarding section 101(b)'s state role.

They would also sustain this application under Home Builders' complete independent externality route. The regulated subject is discharge of dredged or fill material into the claimed waters. Filling removes aquatic habitat used by a shared mobile population; loss in one State can diminish the resource available elsewhere, while another State cannot restore that out-of-state habitat through ordinary territorial authority. The regional production and conversion evidence supports substantial class-wide importance, and the agency's aquatic-use and interstate-bird findings, together with the breeding-colony material, connect the site functionally to that mechanism. Direct permit review of habitat filling reasonably fits the resulting spillover. These are supported inferences and bounded predictive judgments, not new measurements of population loss.

Their strongest contrary concern is that the regional report does not describe every pond or quantify this fill's effect. They find the combined functional evidence sufficient without requiring a separate substantial effect for each discharge, a site percentage, a specified scientific method or proof of failed interstate agreements. The estimate of up to 50 percent of United States migratory-waterfowl production is not a percentage attributable to this site or to Illinois. Neither bird presence nor conservation's national importance alone is sufficient. They identify no independent necessary-regime predicate, and Holland's treaty holding is not a Commerce holding. Their preserved view that actual habitat-altering construction may itself be economic conduct does not replace their full join to this externality ground. They would affirm jurisdiction without reopening the abandoned permit-merits challenge or awarding a permit.

Authority: [published separate writings](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#separate-writings); [opinion topology](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#opinion-topology). These positions do not enlarge the controlling holdings.
