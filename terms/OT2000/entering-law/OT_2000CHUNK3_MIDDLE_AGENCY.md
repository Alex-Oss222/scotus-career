#### MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether APA review is available for the §1536 claims and these interests

**Holding:** The consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels supply no other adequate court remedy for the administrative claims. Alleged water restrictions unsupported by the best available scientific and commercial data implicate interests at least arguably protected by the particular §1536(a)(2) data requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous; both APA holdings have nine direct joins.

**Material application:** The interest inquiry concerns the provision sued upon; sound scientific decisionmaking also protects affected persons against unsupported restrictions. The §1540(g)(5) savings clause preserves available rights without itself satisfying every APA condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 702 concerns relief other than money damages and retains other legal or equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. A mandatory or injunctive decree must identify responsible officers and successors by name or title. Section 704 retains final-action and no-other-adequate-remedy conditions; preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal postpones finality only when an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. No decree or unproved exhaustion prerequisite is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dalton’s integrated base-closure scheme supported its confined preclusion holding; no comparable scheme is shown here. Its four-Justice recommendation-finality rationale creates no general bar.

##### Whether the Biological Opinion completes agency action with legal consequences

**Holding:** This Biological Opinion is final because it completes the Service’s consultation decision and its incidental-take statement changes legal exposure under §1536(o)(2) when the statement’s conditions are met. The Bureau’s retained operating discretion does not reduce that completed, legally consequential action to tentative advice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** Practical influence and the Bureau’s announced compliance support causation; the conditional change in taking liability separately establishes legal consequences. Under §1536(a)(2), agencies must use the best available scientific and commercial data to avoid likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat unless the Committee grants an (h) exemption; none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must find no (a)(2) violation or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed to avoid it, and conclude incidental taking will not violate that subsection. Marine-mammal taking additionally requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The statement specifies taking impact, reasonable and prudent minimization, necessary marine-mammal measures and implementing conditions including reporting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section (o)(2) protects only taking compliant with those conditions, not every ESA violation; the separate (o)(1) exemption is not the source of this consequence. No marine-mammal finding is required for these fish. Not every report or prediction is final, and the biological merits, triggered duties, merits application of review standards and final relief remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed dismissal is reversed and remanded, leaving the separate NEPA dismissal undisturbed and ordering no substantive operational relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin permits review of a completed subordinate-agency determination despite a later governmental step; its reserved presidential-status question is not decided.

For Bennett v. Spear, No. 95-813, decided March 19, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Standing to challenge a biological consultation constraint,” and Environmental Law, “Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels,” for the separate constitutional and citizen-suit holdings.

#### FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., 529 U.S. 120 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1152.

**Decided:** March 21, 2000.

##### Which FDCA version governs any tobacco authority after the 1997 Act

**Holding:** Public Law 105-115, section 422, leaves the question of tobacco authority unchanged and requires any such authority to be exercised under the FDCA as it stood on November 20, 1997. The Act's new mission and regulatory procedures therefore supply neither a new tobacco delegation nor a means to avoid a restriction of that earlier regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 501's generally applicable ninety-day effective date after November 21, 1997 yields to express provisions and excludes sections 111, 121, 125 and 307. It does not override section 422. Section 406's new mission and later classification, review and evidentiary procedures do not enter the earlier tobacco regime; preexisting qualified scientific-evidence and reclassification flexibility remains.

##### Whether the connected statutory scheme authorizes the asserted customary-tobacco drug/device regime

**Holding:** Read with its operative safety and marketing requirements and Congress's distinct tobacco enactments, the FDCA does not authorize the asserted drug/device regime for tobacco as customarily marketed. The FDA cannot obtain that power by construing broad coverage terms to include a continuing-market accommodation that the connected statutory requirements do not permit; the challenged rule is invalid on that ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct votes.

**Material application:** Intended-use evidence is assumed for this inquiry. The chemical action of nicotine does not decide the delivery component's classification, and therapeutic advertising is not the sole possible intended use. Combination-product assignment of responsibility under section 353(g) does not waive independent drug, misbranding, safety, approval or cessation commands. The Court rejects the claimed continuing-market accommodation under this particular scheme; it adopts no magnitude-based major-questions rule or categorical bar to reasoned agency reinterpretation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The following distinctions limit the statutory reasoning; they are not new enforcement findings:

- **Directions and warnings:** Section 352(f)(1) requires an exemption by regulation when adequate directions are unnecessary for public-health protection. That directions exemption does not waive section 352(f)(2)'s separate necessary warnings concerning pathological conditions, childhood use, unsafe dosage, method or duration. Section 352(j) concerns danger under labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration; existing tobacco warnings do not automatically establish FDCA compliance.
- **Classification and approval:** Device classes depend on their statutory control and information criteria, including the qualified low-risk route; risk alone does not compel an immediate judicial ban. The earlier scientific-evidence and reclassification procedures remain. No particular classification, new-drug application, grandfather status or approval is adjudicated. Section 321(p)'s pre-June 25, 1938 exception requires the drug's prior-Act status and unchanged labeling representations; the Court does not find those conditions satisfied or unsatisfied here.
- **Restricted devices:** Section 360j(e) permits practitioner-authorization or other sale, distribution or use conditions upon the required determination that harmful potential or necessary collateral measures prevent reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness otherwise. Training or facility limits require their own safe-use determination; lack of specified board eligibility or certification alone cannot exclude a user. Required label statements remain. None of those permissions is a general exemption from another command.
- **Ban proceedings:** Section 360f permits rulemaking on findings of substantial deception or unreasonable and substantial risk. When the Secretary has determined that labeling can correct the problem and provided written notice specifying the change and time allowed, failure to make that change is separately required. Immediate effect additionally requires unreasonable, direct and substantial danger and advance manufacturer notice, followed by prompt notice, an informal-hearing opportunity and expeditious affirmance, modification or revocation.
- **Cessation and recall:** Section 360h(e)(1) requires immediate cessation of distribution and notification and cessation-of-use instructions to health professionals and user facilities after a reasonable-probability finding that a human-use device would cause serious adverse health consequences or death. An informal-hearing opportunity must be provided within ten days of the order, and inadequate grounds require vacatur. Recall requires a further determination, timetable and progress reports; it cannot reach devices held by individuals, and the greater-risk exception concerns recall from user facilities. Those recall limits do not make the initial cessation command elective. Individual risk-notice duties remain. The Court makes none of the required enforcement determinations itself.
- **Other enactments and State law:** The cigarette statutes' labeling and advertising limits and the smokeless-tobacco statute's distinct package/advertisement rules retain their own scopes, including the latter's outdoor-billboard exception. Section 360k requires a relevant federal device requirement and a different or additional State duty in the specified subject relationship. Its exemption procedure requires an application, regulation, notice and oral-hearing opportunity; greater stringency is one route, compelling local conditions without violating federal requirements another. Medtronic therefore prevents treating coverage alone as universal displacement of State youth-access laws. No particular preemption or exemption is decided.

The Court decides no advertising merits, constitutional objection, therapeutic-claim product, biological-license question, particular new-drug refusal ground, international coordination requirement or later classification procedure. It does not pronounce every risky device unmarketable, require therapeutic claims for every drug, create a general prohibition on agency change, or require Congress to use particular words in every delegation. Congress may confer tobacco authority expressly.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of the August 28, 1996 rule for want of the asserted authority. Only genuinely necessary implementation or severability proceedings remain permissible. No replacement rule, tobacco ban, recall, damages or retrospective liability is ordered; the separately vacated advertising construction remains unadopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applies statutory construction before deference; agency reasonableness cannot supply a delegation absent from the enacted scheme.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.:** Preserves the distinction between genuine delegated ambiguity, regulatory validity and application; formal rulemaking alone does not establish authority.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.:** Applies its requirement that a bounded agency power preserve an operative statutory obligation. It creates no universal magnitude-based exception to delegation.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon:** Preserves broad statutory coverage within actual delegated language and concrete limits; it does not authorize disregard of a connected command.
- **AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board:** Preserves separate analysis of general implementing power, particular statutory constraints and unresolved applications.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala:** Preserves considered interpretive change within the statute; the FDA's former position is not an automatic legal bar.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.:** Preserves recognition of the agency's actual discernible ground without supplying missing findings or a judicial replacement policy.
- **Adams Fruit Co. v. Barrett:** Preserves the requirement of an actual congressional delegation; an agency cannot create its own authority.
- **United States v. Bacto-Unidisk:** Its broad protective reading of the FDCA remains, subject to the entire enacted scheme rather than isolated definitions.
- **United States v. Rutherford:** Preserves statutory safety and effectiveness requirements against a judicially created exemption; it is not a present tobacco ban or an adjudication of an application.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Preserves the actual federal/state counterpart and subject conditions for device preemption; federal coverage does not automatically eliminate every State rule.
- **United States v. Locke:** Its particular national-vessel and local-waterway allocation remains distinct; overlap alone supplies no general preemption rule here.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

For the compensatory-time use holding in Christensen, May 1, 2000, No. 98-1167, see Labor and Employment — Public compensatory time and compulsory paid use.

#### National Mining Association v. United States Department of the Interior

**Docket or dockets:** No separate Supreme Court docket supplied; D.C. Circuit No. 98-5248; review of 177 F.3d 1.
**Decided:** May 8, 2000.

##### I. The ordinary permit rule requires current control; former control has separate statutory conditions

**Holding:** Section 510(c), 30 U.S.C. §1260(c), ordinarily bars issuance when the schedule or other information shows a currently violating surface coal mining operation currently owned or controlled by the applicant, including actual downstream control through intermediary entities. After ordinary control ends, the separate former-control provision requires a demonstrated pattern of willful violations of the chapter, of such nature and duration, and with such resulting irreparable environmental damage, as to indicate an intent not to comply; mere former ownership or control cannot substitute for those cumulative conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices. Nine Justices adopt this holding and its stated limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The required three-year schedule lists notices, but the separate permit command reaches a current violation shown by that schedule or other information. The schedule period therefore does not supply a general age limit for violations considered. The block continues until the applicant supplies proof that the violation has been corrected or is in the process of being corrected to the satisfaction of the authority having jurisdiction. Completed correction is not invariably required.

##### II. Antecedent dates alone do not bar present eligibility rules addressing current control and a current violation

**Holding:** An otherwise lawful current-control permit condition is not categorically inapplicable merely because both the underlying violation and acquisition of control predate November 2, 1988, when the actual operative predicates are current control, a current violation and present eligibility for prospective operations. This does not authorize a genuinely new disability resting solely on completed conduct, backdate the rule, determine a displaced-clear-guidance claim or resolve the validity of a particular permit application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. The common ground is the particular current-condition distinction and Smiley's qualified interpretation rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not adopt Landgraf's entire general method anew. Stone-Zsela dissents on this component.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The present application date alone cannot validate a past-conduct disability. The older-violation/post-rule-acquisition branch remains permissible at this temporal level. No displaced clear guidance or actual reliance finding is made; existing Smiley qualifications remain.

##### III. Express correction authority remains subject to lawful predicates and required procedures

**Holding:** Section 201(c)(1), 30 U.S.C. §1211(c), supplies authority to suspend, revoke or withhold a permit for failure to comply with the chapter or valid regulations, including correction of an improperly issued permit that failed a lawful eligibility requirement. Section 510(c)'s issuance language does not eliminate that express power, but the correction must rest on valid substantive and temporal predicates and comply with the applicable procedures; no unlimited implied reopening authority or reopening of a particular final adjudication is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state-primacy requirements retain their independent force. For ordinary non-imminent violations, the applicable sequence includes notice, an abatement opportunity, an opportunity for public hearing, a written finding after the abatement period that the violation remains unabated, and a cessation order stating the steps necessary for expeditious abatement. The separate imminent-danger provisions remain distinct. General correction authority cannot bypass the required state-program procedures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not newly decide the officer/director, equity, asset-control, general-partner, operating or coal-right presumptions; additional schedule-information demands; section 2462 or whether permit blocking is punitive or remedial; the separate due-process or APA persuasion-burden challenge; a particular permit's factual or procedural validity; or a new replacement rule. Their separate existing dispositions or unresolved status are preserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the ordinary former-control limit and express correction authority; reverse the categorical two-pre-rule-date bar only for actual current control and a current violation. The existing rule-correction remand must reflect these limits. No particular permit is denied, withdrawn or reopened, and no new liability or evidentiary program is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products supplies the distinction between actual new legal consequences and antecedent facts. Its controlling general framework and the different relevant-activity concurrence retain their respective force; the common current-condition holding does not merge them.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204, retains the prohibition on unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking. Present regulation of continuing statutory conditions and Smiley's qualified interpretive use do not confer general retrospective power.
- Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A. supplies the qualified use of current authoritative interpretation for antecedent transactions without clear prior official guidance. Its reservation for displacement of clear guidance, protection of legitimate reliance in reasoned change, and prohibition on backdating remain intact.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. requires agency authority to fit the connected enacted scheme. Its tobacco-specific result supplies no universal major-questions rule or new mining exemption.
- Christensen v. Harris County preserves statutory authority, interpretive weight and textual fit as distinct inquiries. It does not convert every current agency interpretation into a binding or temporally valid rule.
- Greenwich Collieries and Allentown Mack retain their particular adjudicatory-burden and review rules. The Court does not import them into the ungranted procedural-rule litigation or decide a new control presumption here.

#### Loving v. United States, 517 U.S. 748

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1966.
**Decided:** June 3, 1996.

##### Did Congress authorize the challenged presidential limits on military capital murder punishment?

**Holding:** UCMJ Articles 18 and 56 authorize the President to restrict the availability of the death punishment Congress selected for Article 118(1) and (4); Article 36 supplies additional statutory confirmation and procedural authority. Together they authorize the challenged RCM 1004 restrictions, but not new capital offenses, death under Article 118(2) or (3), or punishment exceeding or contradicting Congress's choice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices). Thomas concurs in the judgment only and supplies no join to this or the following three holdings.

**Material application:** Withholding death from part of an already capital class is a punishment limit under Articles 18 and 56 even though it is not a numerical maximum. Congress defined the capital murders and death-or-life punishment; these factors neither add an offense nor enlarge its penalty. Article 36 supports implementing procedures: generally recognized federal criminal-trial principles apply so far as the President considers practicable; regulations may neither contradict nor be inconsistent with the Code, must be uniform insofar as practicable, and must be reported to Congress. Calling a factor procedural does not independently establish substantive authority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether Article 36 alone would suffice remains undecided. The assignment permits neither creation of civilian crimes nor vague or otherwise unconstitutional capital factors. The decision uses the governing pre-1990 Article 36 and pre-1991 RCM 1004; it determines no actual transmission or reporting violation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Neal v. United States*, 516 U.S. 284, retains the boundary between statutory punishment and valid delegated sentencing rules: these restrictions implement rather than replace Congress's punishment choice.

##### Must Congress additionally name capital aggravating factors expressly to authorize these restrictions?

**Holding:** The enacted punishment-limiting authority suffices without an additional formula expressly naming capital aggravating factors. Article 106a's later express espionage provisions neither withdraw that authority nor make it inapplicable to these murder offenses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding II; the same eight Justices directly join.

**Material application:** Articles 18 and 56 expressly assign punishment limitations. More explicit treatment of a distinct offense in Article 106a, including its additional presidential factor under Article 36, does not repeal that assignment. Existing authorization also did not expire when additional narrowing became constitutionally necessary on the assumption used in this case; an otherwise authorized use remains within the grant when a later constitutional requirement makes it necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual enacted authority remains essential. Executive practice, congressional silence and usefulness cannot substitute for it. The Court resolves no general criminal-law clear-statement question beyond the challenged assignment.

##### Is this military capital-sentencing assignment constitutionally nondelegable or inadequately bounded?

**Holding:** Congress may assign the President identification of narrowing factors for military capital sentencing when Congress defines the capital offenses and punishment, supplies intelligible legal boundaries, and confines implementation to the President's assigned military-administration role. Congress's offense selection, death-or-life alternatives, express punishment-limiting assignment and consistency restraints adequately bound this delegation even assuming that criminal and capital delegations require greater specificity than the ordinary standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding III; the same eight Justices directly join.

**Material application:** For this authority question, the Court assumes that these military murders require additional eligibility narrowing and that the three factors would adequately narrow if Congress enacted them. Neither Article I's military rulemaking clause nor the assumed capital requirement categorically requires Congress personally to prescribe each limiting factor. The President's executive and Commander-in-Chief responsibilities reinforce the assigned military role without enlarging statutory authority. The enacted limits and that actual role suffice without an additional express factor-selection principle; executive safeguards or panel unanimity could not cure absent congressional authority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether criminal delegations generally require enhanced specificity, whether additional eligibility narrowing is indispensable throughout military capital proceedings, whether Article 118 alone supplies all necessary narrowing, or whether independent Article II authority could support factors without congressional delegation. No rule for newly created actors, unrelated executive functions, wholesale civilian capital-doctrine incorporation, or military immunity from structural review follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Mistretta v. United States*, 488 U.S. 361, supplies guided sentencing implementation with Congress retaining legislative responsibility; its conditional capital discussion did not decide these factors. *Touby v. United States*, 500 U.S. 160, supplies review of an actual criminal delegation without resolving its open general specificity question; its drug-scheduling criteria are not a universal checklist. *Weiss v. United States*, 510 U.S. 163, and *Ryder v. United States*, 515 U.S. 177, retain constitutional review of military adjudicator arrangements and their respective appointment, tenure and defect-specific remedial limits.

##### What relief follows from the rejection of the presented authority challenges?

**Holding:** The statutory-authorization, additional-clear-authorization, categorical-nondelegability and inadequate-guidance objections establish no error and furnish no ground to disturb the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces' judgment. The affirmance rests on that absence of error, without harmless-error review or adjudication of unpresented individual-factor and collateral claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding IV; the same eight Justices directly join. Thomas separately supports the judgment alone.

**Material application:** The panel found premeditated murder during robbery, another Article 118 murder in the same case, and actual perpetration of the felony-murder killing under the authorized rule. Sustaining the rule's source does not independently adjudicate every factor's validity, every sentencing safeguard, or every underlying conviction claim. Congress's offense boundaries constrain delegation without necessarily satisfying the separate assumed eligibility-narrowing requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional need for a service connection in capital military offenses remains open; no new prerequisite is imposed. Survival of an aggravator does not replace correction of a separately established injury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The convictions and sentence remain undisturbed against these authority challenges; no resentencing, replacement life sentence, new guilt trial, acquittal, release or execution is ordered. Article 71 independently requires a final judgment as to legality through applicable military and Supreme Court review routes where review has not been waived or withdrawn. Death cannot be executed without presidential approval; the President may commute or remit it but may not suspend it. Article 71(c)(2)'s separate waiver-or-withdrawal route concerns dismissal or punitive discharge, and subsection (d)'s suspension power excludes death. The Court supplies no presidential approval, execution date or determination that every execution prerequisite is complete.

**Material judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support the judgment. The common rationale has eight direct supporters; Thomas's judgment vote is not a ninth rationale join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Arave v. Creech* retains genuine eligibility narrowing and the distinction between factor formulation and application. *Tuilaepa and Proctor* retain bounded individualized selection after unchallenged lawful eligibility, without enlarging partial joins. *Graham v. Collins* and *Johnson v. Texas* retain effective consideration of supported independent culpability mitigation. *Stringer v. Black*, *Sochor v. Florida* and *Espinosa v. Florida* retain their distinct established-weighing-error and adequate-cure requirements. *Tuggle v. Netherland* retains the independent-assistance-claim rule and unresolved error-effect standard. None establishes a delegation defect or harmlessness shortcut here. *Solorio v. United States*, 483 U.S. 435, remains a noncapital jurisdiction decision and does not resolve capital service connection.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether irrigators plead personal injury, traceability and redressability

**Holding:** Irrigators who allege loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow sufficiently plead concrete injury, causation and likely redress through removal of that constraint. Standing must later be supported by the evidence appropriate to each litigation stage; a citizen-suit authorization does not replace Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** The Bureau’s announced compliance connects the completed Service action to petitioners’ own alleged loss despite retained operational discretion. The possibility of other eventual choices does not require certainty of a changed allocation or defeat pleading-stage redress.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No biological error or final factual standing determination is made; the statutory review routes and relief remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed ESA/APA dismissal is reversed and remanded for claim-specific proceedings; the unchallenged NEPA dismissal is undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lujan’s personal-interest and stage-of-proof rules control, not its noncontrolling absent-agency application. Humane Society’s missing link to an already completed transfer is distinguished. Tracy permits cognizable economic injury without dispensing with other requirements, and Arizonans requires a continuing controversy without establishing a loss of this alleged injury.

#### Ohio Forestry Association, Inc. v. Sierra Club

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-16.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### The present substantive planning challenge is premature

**Holding:** A substantive challenge to a programmatic forest plan is unripe when the plan authorizes no immediate logging, establishes no sufficient present legal hardship, and leaves further project decisions and meaningful review before cutting. On this record that rule requires dismissal without prejudice of the plan/clearcutting, public-trust sales and timber-suitability-regulation claims, without deciding their substantive validity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices, supplies direct controlling authority for this rule and the limits below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither standing, APA finality, statutory or public-trust merits, nor an independent fee entitlement. It does not adopt the lower court's exceptional-circumstances formulation for even-aged management or equate an optimum-method inquiry with a resource-protection condition. A site-specific authorization, an immediately binding provision or regulation, or a properly presented completed procedural violation may receive review when its independent requirements are satisfied. Lujan's protection of a procedural right tied to a threatened concrete interest remains intact. No optional-appeal exhaustion requirement or future limitations ruling is adopted.

Assertions in later briefing concerning motorcycles, heavy machinery, road closures and hiking trails supply no established basis for resolving the reviewed substantive dispute differently. The Court does not find those activities or injuries proved, find intentional waiver, announce a general forfeiture rule, or adjudicate a distinct present-recreation claim on either ripeness or merits.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both lower merits adjudications are displaced, and all three substantive theories are dismissed without prejudice as unripe. No plan approval or invalidation, logging injunction, revision or fee award follows. Later review must satisfy its own requirements and need not await physical injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner and Texas v. United States: applied to fitness and present hardship; neither requires an advisory answer about an insufficiently concrete implementation.
- Toilet Goods Association v. Gardner: applied to distinguish completed agency action from ripeness for judicial review.
- Bennett v. Spear: distinguished at its supported present-consequences premise; later implementation discretion alone does not defeat review.
- New York v. United States and Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno: distinguished because their challenged rules imposed established present burdens; pre-enforcement review remains available on that basis.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: its concrete-interest procedural protection is preserved; this decision supplies no separate standing determination.

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded modification and distinct notice authority: MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co — The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing, 1994-06-17. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co, 1994-06-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Limited deferral or waiver is not categorically foreclosed, but no particular waiver is approved. Failure of the special-circumstances-or-conditions predicate is a four-Justice alternative, not a separate controlling holding. No universal major-questions doctrine is created; the statute’s permissible flexibility remains available within the actual delegation.

**Holdings navigation:** The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing.

## Standards and Tests selection: APA review of completed species-consultation determinations

### APA review of completed species-consultation determinations

**Governing question:** APA review and finality of completed §1536 consultation determinations.

**Current rule:** The ESA does not itself preclude APA review of administrative consultation claims for which its citizen-suit channels supply no other adequate judicial remedy. Claimed injury from restrictions unsupported by the best scientific and commercial data is at least arguably within §1536(a)(2)'s protected interests. A Biological Opinion is final agency action when it completes the Service's consultation determination and an incidental-take statement carries legal consequences by conditioning protection from taking liability on compliance. The operating agency's retained discretion does not by itself turn that completed, consequential determination into tentative advice.

**Authority by component:**

- Bennett v. Spear — The consultation claims have an APA route and a provision-specific interest, 1997-03-19; unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies the APA route and provision-specific interests
- Bennett v. Spear — The Biological Opinion is completed action with legal consequences, 1997-03-19; the same all-nine coalition supplies consultation finality and conditional legal consequences

**Present operation effective:** Bennett, 1997-03-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 702 addresses relief other than money damages and preserves other legal and equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. Any mandatory or injunctive decree must identify by name or title the responsible federal officers and their successors. Section 704 requires final action and no other adequate court remedy.

**Controlling construction:** Section 1536(a)(2) requires avoidance of likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat using the best scientific and commercial data available, unless the Committee has granted an exemption under (h). Under (b)(4), the Secretary must conclude that the action will not violate (a)(2), or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed not to violate it, and conclude that incidental taking will not violate that subsection. For a marine mammal, taking also requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The resulting statement specifies the taking’s impact, reasonable and prudent minimization measures, any required marine-mammal measures, and implementing terms including reporting. Section (o)(2) protects only taking that complies with those terms; it is not unconditional immunity from every ESA duty. The separate exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not the source of this statement’s effect.

**Open question:** The statutory and scientific merits, actual designation, triggered duty and ultimate relief remain for adjudication.

**Related current rules:** APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals states the independent §704 finality and required/inoperative-agency-appeal rule; Equitable restraint of officers acting beyond statutory authority addresses the separate non-APA officer remedy.

**Holdings navigation:** Bennett v. Spear (1997-03-19) — Administrative Law / Review of completed biological consultation; Bennett v. Spear (1997-03-19) — Whether the Biological Opinion completes agency action with legal consequences.

## Standards and Tests selection: FDCA authority over tobacco as customarily marketed

### FDCA authority over tobacco as customarily marketed

**Governing question:** Whether the FDCA authorizes the asserted drug-and-device regulatory regime for customary tobacco products.

**Current rule:** The FDCA’s connected safety and marketing commands, read with Congress’s distinct tobacco enactments, do not authorize the asserted customary-tobacco drug/device regime. Broad coverage definitions do not permit a continuing-market accommodation that the statute’s independent obligations do not authorize. Public Law 105-115 §422 leaves the tobacco-authority question unchanged and requires any tobacco authority to operate under the FDCA as it stood on November 20, 1997; the later mission and procedures supply neither new authority nor an escape from that earlier regime.

**Authority by component:**

- Earlier statutory regime and reserved authority question: FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. — The 1997 Act preserves the authority question and specifies the earlier tobacco regime, March 21, 2000.
- Connected statutory construction excluding the asserted customary-tobacco regime: Brown & Williamson — The asserted customary-tobacco regime exceeds the delegated authority, March 21, 2000. Both holdings: Stone-Zsela’s opinion joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** Brown & Williamson, March 21, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** This construction establishes no general major-questions doctrine.

The following distinctions limit the statutory reasoning; they are not new enforcement findings:

- **Directions and warnings:** Section 352(f)(1) requires an exemption by regulation when adequate directions are unnecessary for public-health protection. That directions exemption does not waive section 352(f)(2)'s separate necessary warnings concerning pathological conditions, childhood use, unsafe dosage, method or duration. Section 352(j) concerns danger under labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration; existing tobacco warnings do not automatically establish FDCA compliance.
- **Classification and approval:** Device classes depend on their statutory control and information criteria, including the qualified low-risk route; risk alone does not compel an immediate judicial ban. The earlier scientific-evidence and reclassification procedures remain. No particular classification, new-drug application, grandfather status or approval is adjudicated. Section 321(p)'s pre-June 25, 1938 exception requires the drug's prior-Act status and unchanged labeling representations; the Court does not find those conditions satisfied or unsatisfied here.
- **Restricted devices:** Section 360j(e) permits practitioner-authorization or other sale, distribution or use conditions upon the required determination that harmful potential or necessary collateral measures prevent reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness otherwise. Training or facility limits require their own safe-use determination; lack of specified board eligibility or certification alone cannot exclude a user. Required label statements remain. None of those permissions is a general exemption from another command.
- **Ban proceedings:** Section 360f permits rulemaking on findings of substantial deception or unreasonable and substantial risk. When the Secretary has determined that labeling can correct the problem and provided written notice specifying the change and time allowed, failure to make that change is separately required. Immediate effect additionally requires unreasonable, direct and substantial danger and advance manufacturer notice, followed by prompt notice, an informal-hearing opportunity and expeditious affirmance, modification or revocation.
- **Cessation and recall:** Section 360h(e)(1) requires immediate cessation of distribution and notification and cessation-of-use instructions to health professionals and user facilities after a reasonable-probability finding that a human-use device would cause serious adverse health consequences or death. An informal-hearing opportunity must be provided within ten days of the order, and inadequate grounds require vacatur. Recall requires a further determination, timetable and progress reports; it cannot reach devices held by individuals, and the greater-risk exception concerns recall from user facilities. Those recall limits do not make the initial cessation command elective. Individual risk-notice duties remain. The Court makes none of the required enforcement determinations itself.
- **Other enactments and State law:** The cigarette statutes' labeling and advertising limits and the smokeless-tobacco statute's distinct package/advertisement rules retain their own scopes, including the latter's outdoor-billboard exception. Section 360k requires a relevant federal device requirement and a different or additional State duty in the specified subject relationship. Its exemption procedure requires an application, regulation, notice and oral-hearing opportunity; greater stringency is one route, compelling local conditions without violating federal requirements another. Medtronic therefore prevents treating coverage alone as universal displacement of State youth-access laws. No particular preemption or exemption is decided.

The Court decides no advertising merits, constitutional objection, therapeutic-claim product, biological-license question, particular new-drug refusal ground, international coordination requirement or later classification procedure. It does not pronounce every risky device unmarketable, require therapeutic claims for every drug, create a general prohibition on agency change, or require Congress to use particular words in every delegation. Congress may confer tobacco authority expressly.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Surface-mining permit eligibility and correction

### Surface-mining permit eligibility and correction

**Governing question:** Current-control, former-control, timing and correction requirements under SMCRA §§510(c) and 201(c)(1).

**Current rule:** The ordinary §510(c) permit bar requires an applicant’s current ownership or control of a currently violating surface coal mining operation, including actual downstream control through intermediaries. The required three-year notice schedule does not limit the age of a current violation established by other information. The block ends when the applicant proves correction or correction in progress satisfactory to the authority with jurisdiction; completed correction is not invariably required. Former control is a separate route requiring a demonstrated pattern of willful chapter violations whose nature, duration and resulting irreparable environmental harm indicate an intent not to comply; former control alone is insufficient.

A lawful present eligibility condition based on actual continuing control and violation is not categorically inapplicable merely because both began before November 2, 1988. A present application date alone does not validate a new disability based solely on completed conduct. Section 201(c)(1) expressly permits suspension, revocation or withholding for noncompliance with the chapter or valid regulations, including correction of an improperly issued permit, but only on valid substantive and temporal predicates and through required procedures.

**Authority by component:**

- Current and former control, correction in progress and schedule scope: National Mining Association v. Department of the Interior — I. The ordinary permit rule requires current control; former control has separate statutory conditions, May 8, 2000; Stevens’s Part I, unanimous.
- Continuing-condition timing: National Mining Association — II. Antecedent dates alone do not bar present eligibility rules addressing current control and a current violation, May 8, 2000; eight Associates.
- Express corrective authority and procedural limits: National Mining Association — III. Express correction authority remains subject to lawful predicates and required procedures, May 8, 2000; unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** National Mining Association, May 8, 2000.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** State-primacy requirements remain. For ordinary non-imminent violations, the applicable sequence includes notice, an abatement opportunity, an opportunity for public hearing, a written post-abatement finding of continuing noncompliance, and a cessation order specifying steps for prompt abatement. Separate imminent-danger procedures are not merged with that sequence.

**Exceptions and limits:** An upstream relationship or ownership label alone does not prove actual control. The decision creates no general retroactive rulemaking or implied reopening power and decides no displacement of clear prior guidance, particular final adjudication, permit validity, new evidentiary presumption, or §2462 characterization.

## Standards and Tests selection: Bounded presidential narrowing of military capital punishment

### Bounded presidential narrowing of military capital punishment

**Governing question:** Presidential authority to identify narrowing factors within Congress’s military capital scheme.

**Current rule:** UCMJ Articles 18 and 56 authorize presidential restrictions on the death punishment Congress selected for Article 118(1) and (4); Article 36 supplies additional confirmation and authority for pretrial, trial and post-trial procedures, including modes of proof, for courts-martial, military commissions and other military tribunals, and courts-of-inquiry procedure. Generally recognized federal criminal-trial principles of law and evidence apply so far as the President considers practicable. Regulations may not contradict or be inconsistent with the Code, must be uniform insofar as practicable and must be reported to Congress. That authority supports RCM 1004’s narrowing restrictions without a further formula naming capital aggravators. It does not authorize new capital offenses, death under Article 118(2) or (3), or punishment contradicting or exceeding Congress’s choice. Congress may assign narrowing-factor implementation where it defines the offenses and punishment, supplies intelligible legal boundaries and leaves implementation within the President’s military-administration function. The offense selections, death-or-life alternatives, express limiting assignment and consistency restraints adequately guide this task even assuming greater criminal/capital specificity is required. Article 106a’s later express espionage provisions do not withdraw the murder authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory limiting power and clear-authorization question: I. Congress authorized these limitations on its capital murder punishment; II. No additional capital-specific wording is required for this authorization; constitutional boundaries: III. This military implementation assignment has adequate constitutional boundaries, 1996-06-03. Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Loving, 1996-06-03.

**Open question:** Article 36 alone, independent Article II power, general criminal-delegation specificity and a capital service-connection requirement remain undecided. No universal transfer of civilian capital doctrine or exemption of military institutions from structural review follows. Article 71 finality and presidential-approval requirements remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Loving v. United States, 517 U.S. 748.
