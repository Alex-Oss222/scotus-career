### Christensen v. Harris County, No. 98-1167

Merits decision, May 1, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 23, 2000. On certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 158 F.3d 241, whose decision in No. 97-20796 was captioned Moreau v. Harris County. The questions are whether the Fair Labor Standards Act requires a specific agreement before a public employer may direct use of accrued compensatory time, and what weight the Labor Department's contrary opinion letter receives under the statute and regulations.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Fifth Circuit's final judgment for the County and all other defendants is affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The dissenting Justices would set aside that judgment and remand for actual agreement coverage, terms and validity under a consent requirement.

The employees' categorical declaration and dependent $21,360 fee award remain displaced. The Court orders no agreement-specific proceedings, negotiations, trial, damages or new fee award. The policy remains subject to express statutory protections, binding agreements and other applicable law. Abandoned wrongful-refusal and retaliation claims are not revived.

Part II: advisory-letter statutory weight, five. Part III: regulatory textual fit, six. |

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

##### I. The statute does not make employee requests the exclusive means of using accrued time

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 207(o) does not categorically forbid a public employer from directing paid use of lawfully accrued compensatory time merely because no agreement specifically authorizes compulsory use. The employer remains bound by lawful agreement restrictions, protected employee requests and the statute's accrual, value and overtime requirements; compelled paid use is not permission to confiscate credits or evade overtime.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, six Justices in all.

**Controlling explanation:** Paragraph (5) protects an employee who requests accrued time: use must be allowed within a reasonable period unless it would unduly disrupt operations. It does not say that a request is the only permissible trigger for use. Paragraph (2)'s agreement requirement governs substituting time for cash overtime, not every later scheduling choice. Moreau preserves its actual agreement routes; neither it nor the statute establishes the terms of this class's agreements.

The accrual cap limits how much time may be accumulated and requires cash for additional overtime at the cap; it does not promise employees the right to reach that cap. The permitted cashout likewise contradicts an absolute right to retain every credit, although cash payment and paid absence remain different transactions. Paid time away from work consumes credits in their defined form: the employee receives the regular rate for hours not worked, which are excluded from hours worked when computing overtime. These statutory reasons suffice without a general presumption that employers control every unregulated workplace question.

The competing consent construction treats the substitute for cash as exclusively employee-controlled. It does not follow from the original agreement requirement or protected request right. An actual lawful term can restrict compulsory use, but none identified here defeats the categorical statutory judgment. That absence does not prove no agreement exists or every employee consented. It supplies no automatic right to new agreement proceedings. The decision leaves constitutional property questions, other labor law and actual wrongful-refusal claims outside this review.

**Operative qualifications:** For the covered state, local and interstate public agencies, a wholly time-based substitute must accrue at least one-and-one-half hours for each overtime hour under the applicable representative agreement or prior individual understanding. For employees outside the applicable representative-agreement route who were hired before April 15, 1986, regular practice on that date itself supplies the agreement; no additional individual agreement is required. Moreau preserves the later-hire individual route where the chosen representative cannot lawfully enter a binding agreement. Unwritten understandings require a record; an express employment condition requires knowing, voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object may evidence assent, but coercion or pressure may not substitute for it. Compatible agreements may mix cash and time or set use terms while preserving the statutory premium; inconsistent terms yield to the Act.

The cap is 480 hours for the specified public-safety, emergency-response or seasonal work, and 240 for other covered work. Additional overtime at the applicable cap must be compensated as overtime. Cashout may occur at any time using the regular rate when paid. Termination requires at least the higher of the final regular rate or the average over the last three years. For termination-rate averaging, employment after a break is treated as a new period when the break was intended to be permanent and post-April 14, 1986 credits were cashed out at the initial separation; if the final period is shorter than three years, averaging uses that period. No termination or rate calculation is decided here.

For employee requests, customary schedules, peak demands, emergency staffing, substitutes and compatible agreement terms inform a reasonable period. An employer must reasonably and in good faith anticipate an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service to establish undue disruption; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and the statute does not guarantee the employee's exact chosen date. Section 553.25 forbids using time to evade overtime or coercing acceptance beyond what can realistically and in good faith be made available within a reasonable request period. Its request provisions exclude separately defined other compensatory time, including pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. The separate court-reporter transcript provision is not presented in this Sheriff's Department case. No violation of these distinct protections is found.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding and operative rule:** The advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual implementing grant permits administration and regulation. It does not make every later advisory response an exercise of authority to bind courts or employers. NationsBank and Smiley involved identified authoritative exercises of delegated responsibility; they do not settle this letter's status merely by identifying its agency. Skidmore preserves its potential persuasive significance rather than discarding it by label. Its construction adds a specific-consent restriction absent from the statutory request trigger and prior-agreement condition. The record establishes no policy change, internal authorization instrument or Government endorsement on which to rest the result. Boston & Maine forbids inventing an agency ground. The Court decides this letter's advisory status on the record, not the validity of every informal agency interpretation.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Auer concerns a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. It does not impose a universal rulemaking-format prerequisite on interpretation, but requires actual textual fit. Section 553.23 permits additional consistent terms; section 553.25 protects requests and practical availability. Neither makes employee requests exclusive or requires an agreement covering every scheduling choice. The letter's interpretation therefore adds a condition rather than resolves pertinent ambiguity. The separate inquiry into statutory authority does not replace this own-regulation analysis. No conclusion here forecloses whatever authority the Secretary may possess to adopt a valid new regulation limiting compelled use.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Moreau retains its distinct earlier-hire practice and later-hire legally capable representative rules; it supplies no finding about this class's actual scheduling terms or general bargaining right.
- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

#### Law After Decision

Section 207(o) supplies no categorical employee veto over compulsory paid use of valid accrued time. Actual binding agreements and every identified statutory protection retain their force. This advisory letter supplies no controlling statutory restriction and cannot add a condition inconsistent with the regulation. The Court decides no future rule's validity, blanket employer-control principle, confiscation power or constitutional property question.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia joins the statutory construction and regulatory textual-fit ruling but withholds the Court's no-Chevron ground. Advisory form alone should not exclude authoritative consideration of a responsible agency's construction within a genuine implementing delegation. Even assuming the claimed authority here, however, the separate accrual-agreement and request provisions cannot reasonably bear the letter's additional restriction. His judgment does not depend on finding an unproved endorsement or internal delegation.

Justice Souter joins the Court in full and emphasizes that the decision does not foreclose the Secretary's authority to issue a valid future regulation restricting compelled use. The present statute-and-letter dispute does not decide that different exercise of power.

Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, would remand for agreement-specific proceedings. The compensatory-time scheme is a conditioned, consensual substitute for ordinary cash overtime. Prior agreement, protected availability and an express cash-redemption power support requiring an agreement covering compulsory consumption. Cash payment and forced leisure are different transactions; authorization of one does not itself authorize the other. The agency's experienced reading reinforces that construction. The writing does not require negotiation of every optional term, identical class agreements or a new bargaining right. Actual earlier-hire practice and later-hire agreement routes govern the remand, and neither the categorical declaration nor its fee award would automatically revive. Breyer adopts these statutory reasons as persuasive support, without declaring this the only permissible judicial reading.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Ginsburg, additionally explains that Skidmore remains independently available. The agency's experience and the statutory reasons supporting consent make this construction persuasive; if authoritative Chevron treatment applies, the construction is reasonable within the actual implementing delegation. This bounded alternative does not require a final finding that the letter conclusively has authoritative status or a universal rule about informal agency forms. The own-regulation argument remains distinct and subject to Auer's responsibility and textual-fit predicates. These grounds support agreement-specific remand without presuming the letter's full contents or any particular agreement terms.

The September 14, 1992 Wage and Hour Division letter is known through its published recitation. That recitation does not establish its full contents, any additional qualifications or its internal authorization. No further contents or actual agreement terms are established. The policy concerns paid absence charged to credits, not uncompensated leave or simple forfeiture.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Review ends with the defense judgment in force against the categorical FLSA claim. The Court directs no negotiation, agreement hearing, enforcement proceeding or new agency rule, and awards no replacement fees or damages. Independent valid agreement obligations and other applicable law retain their ordinary operation.

**End of entry: Christensen v. Harris County, merits decision, May 1, 2000.**

### Holder v. Hall, No. 91-2012

Merits decision, June 30, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 4, 1993; decided June 30, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, Hall v. Holder, 955 F.2d 1563 (1992), which reversed rejection of the Section 2 claim and remanded for formulation of a remedy. The District Court's decision is reported at 757 F. Supp. 1560 (M.D. Ga. 1991).

The question is whether the size of this county governing body may be challenged as dilutive under Section 2 and whether a reasonable alternative supplies a measure of unequal opportunity for its sole-commissioner structure. Review does not reopen the Court of Appeals' findings of political cohesion and sufficient white bloc voting. The county officials seek reversal; the voters and local NAACP defend liability and a lawful remedy.

The sole commissioner exercises both legislative and executive powers. Black residents constituted approximately 19% of the voting-age population in the census figures used at trial. Georgia's 1985 local act authorized five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chairman, conditioned on a referendum; voters rejected the proposal in 1986. The school board already uses five districts, and the District Court found that a local district arrangement could contain a compact Black majority. The school-board comparison does not establish identical governmental functions. The Court of Appeals found the remaining Gingles predicates and unequal opportunity under the totality of circumstances, suggested the school-board districts as a possible model, and directed formulation of a remedy. It did not impose a final map or number of commissioners and did not reach the separate constitutional claim.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Eleventh Circuit's Section 2 liability judgment and its existing remand for formulation of a remedy, 5–4.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section 2 liability judgment | Affirmed, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Liability stands on the limited statutory question; no renewed trial of statutory liability |
| Existing statutory remedy remand | Preserved, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Formulate lawful, workable relief with the responsible state institutions; no exact board size, final map, quota or candidate victory ordered |

The four dissenting Justices would reverse Section 2 liability and return the unresolved constitutional claim to the Court of Appeals. The Court's affirmance makes that constitutional alternative unnecessary; no Justice decides its merits here. The six votes for threshold statutory coverage are proposition votes, not six votes to affirm liability.

| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would reverse statutory liability and remand for consideration of the constitutional claim | Section 2 protects ballot access and proper voting/counting, not dilution of electoral weight; incompatible dilution precedents should be rejected |

Kennedy joins only Part II of O'Connor's writing and reserves affirmative threshold coverage. Thomas and Scalia do not join that part: recognizing the size-comparison difficulty does not make their broader construction a join in a rationale that preserves ordinary dilution law. Stone-Zsela joins Blackmun throughout and no separate writing. No other joins are implied.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The county's governing-body size is within Section 2's coverage

**Controlling proposition:** The number of members of this elected county governing authority is a standard, practice or procedure within Section 2, 42 U.S.C. §1973; its combination of legislative and executive functions and its present single-member form do not categorically exempt it from the statute. Coverage alone establishes neither a usable measure of dilution nor unequal electoral opportunity, and it does not prescribe a remedy.

**Authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this threshold rule. O'Connor joins this part while opposing statutory liability; Kennedy reserves the affirmative coverage question, and Scalia and Thomas reject coverage of dilution.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute reaches the electoral structure of this county governing authority. Section 2 addresses standards, practices and procedures that impair protected citizens' opportunity to participate and elect their chosen representatives. Chisom v. Roemer rejects an artificial exclusion of elected officials from that protection, while Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas distinguishes statutory coverage from the later consideration of legitimate state interests. The commissioner exercises legislative as well as executive powers; electing one official does not remove that electoral arrangement from the statutory words. Whether a different number of offices supplies a reasonable comparison is a separate question. Neither broad coverage nor the results amendment dispenses with that inquiry or the required proof of unequal opportunity. Section 5's comparison of a proposed change with an existing practice also cannot establish Section 2 liability. The Court therefore rejects a categorical coverage exemption without making every choice about county government unlawful or ordering any particular structure.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Chisom v. Roemer, 501 U.S. 380 (1991): applies its rejection of artificial exclusions from Section 2's electoral coverage; the decision does not itself settle the body-size comparison.
- Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas, 501 U.S. 419 (1991): follows its separation of coverage, legitimate state interests, liability and remedy; extends no automatic right to additional offices.
- Presley v. Etowah County Commission, January 27, 1992: preserves its Section 5 voting/governing boundary and its reservation of independent Section 2 claims; it supplies no Section 2 size benchmark.
- City of Rome v. United States, 446 U.S. 156 (1980): distinguishes Section 5 review of changes against an existing practice from the proof required for this Section 2 results claim.

##### 2. A reasonable comparison must have legal and practical roots independent of a desired racial tally

**Controlling proposition:** A Section 2 challenge to this sole-commissioner structure may use a reasonable, workable alternative grounded in the actual legal and institutional setting, suitable to the governmental task, and justified independently of the desired number of minority-preferred winners; a uniquely ideal size is not required. The county-specific authorization of five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chair, the existing school-board districts and the compact potential majority satisfy that comparison requirement here, without making population share, a possible seat, or any proposed enlargement sufficient by itself.

**Authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the complete comparison rule and its application. O'Connor and Kennedy reject that comparison; Thomas and Scalia reject dilution coverage on a different, broader ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a comparison need not identify the only permissible legislature. Gingles requires a meaningful account of the opportunity the challenged arrangement withholds; it does not make an existing electoral structure its own conclusive measure. Georgia authorized an alternative for this very county: five commissioners elected by district and a chairman elected at large. The school board's existing districts give the geographic comparison additional practical grounding, and the District Court found a compact potential Black majority. The school board does not perform identical functions, and the referendum rejected the commission proposal. Those facts deserve consideration, but they do not erase the county-specific evidence of feasibility or immunize a proved federal violation. Houston Lawyers requires attention to genuine institutional interests, and Growe preserves state responsibility for districting. The plaintiffs must establish suitability and unequal opportunity, not merely exhibit a map producing a seat. Multiple reasoned alternatives can exist without arbitrariness. Enlargement of an existing multimember body or fragmentation of an indivisible executive office solely to manufacture seats is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extends its comparison inquiry to this locally grounded sole-commissioner challenge while retaining its causal predicates and totality inquiry.
- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: applies meaningful consideration of the State's institutional interests; those interests are neither ignored nor converted into categorical structural immunity.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves the State's districting responsibility and causal proof requirements; it neither decided nor barred this size comparison.

##### 3. The accepted comparison does not replace the required proof of unequal opportunity

**Controlling proposition:** This size-dilution claim requires a sufficiently large and geographically compact minority to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate, together with unequal opportunity established under the totality of circumstances; the comparison alone is insufficient and discriminatory purpose is not required. The extent of protected-class electoral success may be considered but creates no right to election in proportion to population, and the preserved appellate findings establish statutory liability here without reopening cohesion or bloc voting outside the limited review question.

**Authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the stated application and preservation of the existing proof requirements. There is no separate holding that the Gingles predicates alone establish liability or shift the ultimate statutory burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the voters have established more than an opportunity to draw a favorable district. Gingles supplies the causal predicates, and Growe extends their use beyond multimember submergence while keeping the totality inquiry distinct. The District Court found the potential compact majority. The Court of Appeals corrected its treatment of the evidence of cohesion and majority bloc voting, then considered the totality and found unequal electoral opportunity. Challenges to those appellate cohesion and bloc-voting findings fall outside the question under review. Voinovich requires a proved statutory injury rather than a preference for one districting form; that requirement remains. The statutory results standard does not require a discriminatory-purpose finding, and the District Court's rejection of intentional discrimination therefore does not defeat this claim. Nor does nearly one-fifth of the voting-age population establish an entitlement to office. Electoral success is a circumstance the statute permits consideration of, with its actual context preserved. The Court affirms the existing liability determination, not an automatic rule based on demographics or a hypothetical winning district.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the causal prerequisites and independently necessary totality inquiry; no universal statistical method or proportional representation rule is adopted.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves the need for local cohesion and bloc-voting proof, including the stated special-circumstances qualification; coalition cognizability, influence claims and the population denominator remain unresolved.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the distinction between unequal-opportunity proof and preference for a district form; no change to its initial burden rule or its influence-claim reservations.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent elements of Section 2; its rejection of the design-only constitutional theory adds no constitutional-injury prerequisite to this statutory claim.

##### 4. The existing remand must provide a lawful remedy for the established violation

**Controlling proposition:** Because Section 2 liability is affirmed, the existing remand for formulation of a workable remedy continues, with genuine county-government interests and the responsible state institutions receiving consideration and relief confined to curing the established violation. The local authorization and district pattern inform that inquiry but compel neither their exact adoption, a particular number of officers, a racial quota nor any candidate's victory; no new liability trial is ordered and the constitutional alternative need not be decided.

**Authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; the five-Justice judgment coalition adopts these remedial limits and the affirmance of the Eleventh Circuit's existing remedy remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying a useful comparison does not enact a replacement government. Houston Lawyers treats the State's genuine institutional interests as relevant to relief as well as liability. Growe recognizes state responsibility for districting, and Voinovich permits federal structural relief only as necessary to remedy an established violation. Here the Court of Appeals found that violation and directed formulation of a remedy; it suggested a local district pattern rather than imposing a final map. Affirmance preserves that procedural distinction. The rejected referendum means that the alternative commission was not adopted, but local rejection cannot bar all relief for the proved federal wrong. The responsible institutions and the courts must address a workable remedy within the statutory limits. This Court supplies no division of governmental duties or mandatory board size that the record does not establish. The constitutional-purpose claim remains unnecessary to the judgment. Neither that unresolved alternative nor the remaining remedial work warrants another trial of the statutory liability already adjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: follows its instruction to consider genuine state structural interests in fitting relief to a proved violation.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves state responsibility and the lawful federal remedial role; its case-specific dismissal direction is not a universal bar to retaining remedial proceedings.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the distinction between state districting choice and a federal remedy justified by an established violation; no mandate to maximize districts follows.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

The holding-specific treatments above govern: Gingles's comparison inquiry is extended to this locally grounded sole-commissioner claim while its predicates and totality remain; Chisom and Houston Lawyers support coverage with separate proof and remedy; Growe and Voinovich retain state responsibility and the requirement of an established violation for federal relief. Presley and City of Rome do not make Section 5's baseline a Section 2 measure. Shaw retains the independent statutory inquiry. No precedent is overruled. Thomas and Scalia's proposal to reject contrary dilution precedents is noncontrolling and does not alter their present force.

Effective June 30, 1994, Section 2 permits this sole-commissioner size challenge when the claimant supplies a reasonable, workable comparison rooted in the actual legal and institutional setting and proves the required causal conditions and unequal opportunity under the totality. The decision keeps four inquiries distinct: statutory coverage, a suitable comparison, proof of the statutory injury, and lawful relief. This decision answers the previously open comparison question on this record; it does not treat a demographic fraction, inability to elect a preferred official, or a map producing one seat as sufficient.

Gingles's ordinary predicates and totality remain; a protected class's electoral success may be considered, and proportional election of protected-class members is not guaranteed. Section 2's protection against racial or color discrimination and its language-minority cross-reference remain unchanged. No discriminatory-purpose requirement is added to this results claim, and no question under the language-minority cross-reference is separately decided. No ruling eliminates ordinary dilution claims or categorically immunizes government size.

The State retains its districting role, and federal structural relief must cure an established violation. Neither the local comparison nor today's judgment requires maximization of opportunity districts, adoption of the school-board map, six officers, or any racial quota. The referendum did not adopt the proposed commission, and the Court does not enact it. Enlargement of an existing multimember body and division of an indivisible executive office solely to manufacture seats remain unresolved. Other counties must establish suitable comparisons and prove their own claims; later applications are not predetermined. Coalition and influence claims and the population denominator remain open as before.

Shaw's actual design-only constitutional boundary remains intact and supplies no extra injury element for Section 2. Section 5's change-based inquiry remains distinct. The constitutional claim in this case is not decided. The four stated holdings rest on direct majorities; there is no need to identify a narrower rationale under Marks, and the incompatible grounds of the two dissents are not combined into Court law.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

##### Stevens's concurrence, joined by Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg

Stevens joins the Court throughout and gives an additional reason for rejecting the proposal to remove vote dilution from Section 2. The statute has been repeatedly considered and reenacted against the background of decisions protecting effective electoral opportunity. Congress's 1982 amendment deliberately retained protection against discriminatory results while withholding any guarantee of proportional officeholding. Gingles implements that compromise; Chisom and Houston Lawyers preserve broad coverage while requiring the appropriate liability and remedial inquiries. Growe and Voinovich continue those distinctions. Difficulty applying the compromise to electoral structures does not authorize the Court to replace it with a statute confined to registration and ballot administration. The constitutional concerns associated with racial districting do not dispense with the enacted statutory inquiry or establish that every remedy is lawful. The concurrence accepts the Court's concrete comparison and the separate consideration of legitimate county interests. It supplies no right to enlarge any government until a minority can win, no additional remedy, and no disposition of the unreviewed constitutional claim. Its additional reenactment and stare decisis rationale has four named adherents and is not an independently controlling alternative holding.

##### O'Connor's concurrence in part and dissent in part; Kennedy joins Part II only

O'Connor agrees in Part I that governing-body size is a covered standard, practice or procedure. Chisom and Houston Lawyers support that threshold conclusion, and statutory stare decisis weighs against Thomas's proposal to eliminate dilution doctrine. She accordingly joins Part I of the Court's opinion. But coverage does not answer whether electoral opportunity can be measured against an objective alternative. Kennedy reserves that affirmative coverage conclusion and does not join this part of her separate writing.

In Part II, joined by Kennedy, O'Connor would reject this size-dilution claim. Gingles requires an undiluted norm against which impairment can be measured. A different method of electing existing offices may supply such a norm; the statute offers no principled choice of how many offices the government must have. Georgia's authorization, the school-board arrangement and the potential majority district show that an alternative might work. They do not explain why that size supplies the legally relevant baseline for a county government with different responsibilities. The rejected referendum underscores the existence of a governmental choice without establishing immunity from otherwise applicable law. This objection concerns size as the comparison variable, not an invitation simply to propose another feasible number. Section 5's review of change uses an existing practice as its baseline; its coverage cannot cure the different Section 2 comparison problem. The two Justices would reverse statutory liability and remand for appellate consideration of the unresolved constitutional claim, without finding discriminatory purpose, imposing an intent requirement on ordinary results liability, or abolishing other dilution claims.

##### Thomas's dissent, joined by Scalia

Thomas and Scalia would reverse on a broader statutory ground. They read Section 2's qualifications, prerequisites, standards, practices and procedures in the context of the Act's protection of registration, access to the ballot, casting a vote and proper counting. In their view, that language does not reach the allocation of electoral weight among groups through governing-body size or districting. Subsection (b) identifies discriminatory results within the practices covered by subsection (a); it does not enlarge that subject matter into a vote-dilution cause of action. The results test would therefore remain for covered ballot-access practices, without a new requirement to prove purpose.

They regard the lack of a principled body-size benchmark and the difficulty of choosing a theory of representation as further reasons to reject dilution doctrine. The proportional-representation proviso reinforces their objection to making racial electoral success the measure of the statutory right. They would reject Gingles's contrary statutory construction and the incompatible Section 2 propositions in Growe and Voinovich, despite their participation in those decisions. They do not claim that those decisions already adopted their proposed construction. Their agreement that size presents a comparison problem does not join O'Connor and Kennedy's sufficient rationale, which leaves ordinary dilution protection intact.

The dissent preserves its authors' constitutional objection to racial separation expressed in their Shaw dissent, while recognizing that it is not the Court's governing rule. It decides no constitutional-purpose claim, overrules no Section 5 precedent and supplies no alternative map or board size. Its proposed disposition is reversal of statutory liability followed by consideration of the constitutional claim below. Neither its broader statutory theory nor the distinct size-comparison dissent changes the Court's controlling rule.

### Strate v. A-1 Contractors, No. 95-1872

Merits decision, April 28, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 7, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 76 F.3d 930 (1996) (en banc). The federal §1331 action challenges the outer federal limit on the Tribal Court of the Three Affiliated Tribes' subject-matter jurisdiction over Gisela Fredericks's personal-injury claim against A-1 Contractors and Lyle Stockert, arising from a November 9, 1990 collision on State Highway 8 within the Fort Berthold Reservation. The en banc court reversed the District Court's judgment sustaining jurisdiction; the earlier panel judgment was vacated. Tribal exhaustion is complete. Personal jurisdiction, the adult sons' unresolved consortium claims and the dismissed insurer are outside this appeal.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This authorized public highway falls within Montana's nonmember framework

**Holding and operative rule:** For ordinary public motorists using this lawfully open, state-maintained federal right-of-way, the Tribe lacks the exclusion power that would otherwise govern access; Montana's nonmember framework therefore applies to this road-use tort. That consequence lasts within the authorized public use and neither conveys fee title nor removes every compatible tribal power.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins in Parts I, III and IV. Parts II and V are joined by the eight Associates.

**Controlling explanation:** Montana distinguishes retained internal and land-based authority from authority over nonmembers on land opened to outside use, preserving two exceptions and superior federal authorization. Bourland applies an exclusion consequence to particular acquired lands while requiring a separate residual-authority inquiry. The authorized public highway has the pertinent characteristic here: ordinary motorists do not obtain the Tribe's discretionary permission to pass. The federal instrument is a conditional easement, not a fee conveyance or an express surrender of every governmental power. Its continuing trust status and compatible crossings remain effective but do not establish a present gatekeeping right over authorized motorists. Sac and Fox's classification of qualifying rights-of-way as Indian country remains intact; territorial classification does not resolve every nonmember governing power. No exact collision-parcel owner, invalid issuance or termination event is found. The Court decides this highway use, not every reservation road, easement or source of tribal authority.

**Grant qualifications:** The May 8, 1970 grant applies to trust lands only, excludes non-trust property, and acknowledges $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. Tenure is unlimited only while used for the specified highway purpose and remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct a breach of grant terms or applicable regulations, two consecutive years of nonuse for the purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the completion affidavit when construction ends. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. The stated conditions bind and benefit the grantee's successors. No termination trigger is established on this record. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres do not identify the collision parcel; missing incorporated maps and a separate consent instrument establish no grant defect.

##### II. A tribal forum requires an adequate source of governing power

**Holding and operative rule:** Subject to controlling treaties or statutes and Montana's two exceptions, tribal adjudicatory authority over nonmembers does not exceed the corresponding substantive governing authority. A tribal court cannot compel these defendants to litigate this highway claim solely by proposing to apply another sovereign's law; completed exhaustion supplies no independent jurisdictional grant.

**Authority:** Scalia, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the narrower case-specific grounds, not this general ceiling.

**Controlling explanation:** A binding tort judgment exercises sovereign authority over the defendants' conduct even though forum and choice of law are analytically distinct. National Farmers Union supplies federal review after tribal examination; Iowa Mutual protects that initial institutional opportunity, not ultimate power over every reservation occurrence. Williams involved a trader's claim against resident members about their transaction, a concrete internal relationship missing here. Merrion rejects equating sovereign taxing power with a private owner's right to exclude and rejects surrender by contractual silence. The Court infers no general waiver here: taxation of mineral lessees' reservation business and governmental-services relationship differs from the ordinary highway claim. Montana's external limit, the authorized public use and separately tested exceptions supply the rule. Congress remains able to enlarge tribal-court jurisdiction; no criminal-jurisdiction analogy displaces the distinct civil inquiry.

##### III. The subcontract does not bring this ordinary highway claim within the consensual exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Montana's first exception requires a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or its members. Even assuming Stockert's trip served A-1's tribal community-center subcontract and Fredericks resided on the reservation, this non-Indian motorist's ordinary highway-negligence claim lacks that relation; contracting with a tribal enterprise does not submit a contractor to every reservation claim.

**Controlling explanation:** A-1 voluntarily made the $12,490 subcontract with the tribally owned LCM corporation. Its existence deserves real consideration; neither express forum consent nor contractual privity is a universal prerequisite to every use of the exception. But Fredericks asserts the duty one public-road driver owes another non-Indian user, not a tribal contract entitlement, an obligation to a participant in the work, or control of project performance. The truck uses a public right of passage rather than permission available only through the bargain. The contract can explain the trip without making this claim part of governing that relationship. The work purpose and residence are assumed favorably, not resolved against petitioners, so further factfinding on those disputes cannot change this result. No weight is given to a purported Utah forum or law clause outside the appellate record, and reported building or employment obligations establish no blanket consent. A different injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a participant in the consensual activity presents a different question.

##### IV. The supported interests do not satisfy the self-government exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Montana's second exception preserves authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The serious individual injury, family relationships, assumed residence and ordinary road-safety interests shown here do not establish the threat or direct tribal effect necessary to bring this nonmember highway claim within that exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Montana protects necessary tribal self-government, not general territorial jurisdiction over every local civil wrong. Fredericks's twenty-four-day hospitalization, ties to her tribal-member sons, and the community's safety interests are real; the Court does not find an absence of consequences. Their ordinary individual, familial and community character nevertheless does not supply the distinct governmental threat or direct effect required here. Treating those effects as sufficient for every road accident would undo the external limit the exception qualifies. No unproved fiscal or service burden supplies a missing premise. Her sons' membership does not change her status or bring their separate claims into this appeal. The protective state action indicates another possible forum, but availability of a state forum is not the independent reason tribal power is absent. No negligence or limitations result in that forum is decided.

##### V. The jurisdictional judgment does not itself establish every predicate for an injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmance requires federal relief consistent with the absence of tribal jurisdiction over the reviewed claim, but does not itself issue an injunction or establish entitlement to every coercive remedy. On remand the federal court must respect the actual claims and lawful parties, immunity limits and ordinary equitable requirements.

**Authority:** The same eight-Associate coalition as Part II directly supports this remedial holding; Stone-Zsela favors affirmative prospective relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The amended answer consents to defending declaratory relief on tribal civil jurisdiction while withholding consent to other claims, issues, relief and liability. Resolution 92-025-JJR is likewise confined to protecting and asserting jurisdiction in this case. Descriptions of the defense in the lower opinions do not enlarge those instruments into an unrestricted waiver. The outer federal jurisdictional ruling permits appropriate conforming declaratory relief; any injunction still requires its actual lawful basis and equitable predicates. Neither the sons' unresolved claims nor the dismissed insurer can be disposed of through this appeal. No additional exhaustion or factual inquiry into the assumed trip and residence is necessary to decide the reviewed jurisdictional question.

The Court does not decide tribal or closed internal roads, member conduct, explicit forum consent, qualifying superior federal authorization, separate criminal authority, police patrols or detention for handoff, or every third-party tort. The contested late treaty theory and unratified agreement supply no independent ground; no holding of inevitable forfeiture, ratification or exclusive state jurisdiction follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Montana v. United States supplies the nonmember framework and both retained exceptions, each independently applied to this claim.
- South Dakota v. Bourland is applied by its public-use and exclusion reasoning while preserving residual authority; its different acquired-land setting is not turned into a universal easement rule.
- National Farmers Union supplies the §1331 review channel and initial tribal examination, without creating ultimate jurisdiction.
- Iowa Mutual retains its exhaustion function and civil-jurisdiction distinction; exhaustion here is already complete.
- Williams v. Lee is distinguished by its resident-member trading relationship, absent from the ordinary highway claim.
- Merrion v. Jicarilla Apache Tribe retains its distinction between sovereign taxation and private exclusion; no blanket surrender by silence is inferred here.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation retains Indian-country classification, which is distinct from authority over this nonmember claim.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation retains its source-, membership-, residence- and relationship-specific treatment; it supplies no general reservation tort power.

#### Law After Decision

This public-highway claim lies outside tribal adjudicatory power even on the favorable work-trip and residence assumptions. The general adjudicatory ceiling has eight direct joins, with superior federal law and the Montana exceptions preserved. The decision neither extinguishes all tribal authority over the land nor creates a universal third-party-tort or privity bar. Appropriate federal relief must still fit the claims, parties, immunity and equitable requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the jurisdictional judgment and dissents in part as to relief. He applies Montana source by source to this ordinary nonmember public-highway claim: location within the reservation does not alone establish power, the subcontract does not govern every legally unrelated road-duty claim, and family ties do not convert the injury into the necessary self-government threat. He joins Parts I, III and IV and does not supply the broader ceiling in Part II. He favors prospective declaratory and injunctive relief protecting A-1 and Stockert from further tribal adjudication of the reviewed claim, leaving Fredericks to a forum with jurisdiction. The Court instead preserves the predicates for coercive relief on remand. His position does not decide the unreviewed sons' claims, tribal internal roads, member conduct, explicit forum consent or a claim directly arising from a qualifying consensual transaction.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit's en banc judgment rejecting jurisdiction is affirmed. The federal action returns for appropriate conforming relief within the stated boundaries, without reopening the jurisdictional merits for work-purpose or residence factfinding. No injunction, negligence determination, damages award, state limitations ruling, costs or fees are entered by this decision.
