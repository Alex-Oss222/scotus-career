### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

### Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

**Governing question:** Intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination of objectively intimate information entrusted for a limited criminal investigation.

**Current rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects this confined intimate marital and family interest against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination without sufficient governmental justification. An assurance of confidentiality informs the objective expectation but does not alone create a federal right; consent to investigative use is not consent to general publication. Ownership or authorship is not indispensable to a person's own intimate interest. Public inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not automatically establish prior dissemination, voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of distinct official facilitation of publication. The protection concerns intimate personal information, not reputation alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined protected interest and justification: I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty, 1996-04-29; Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Sheets, 1996-04-29.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure retain their separate rules. There is no universal strict-scrutiny or least-intrusive-means test for personal, financial or business information, general deletion right, inherited privacy claim, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author’s speech. Independent municipal attribution and conduct-date qualified immunity remain. The unobjected trial intent instruction does not establish a universal new mental-state rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County.

### Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

**Current rule:** An employer acting on a genuine, reasonable account of speech that would be disciplinable under the governing public-employee-speech rules is protected even if that factual account is mistaken. Conversely, an unreasonable and incorrect account does not defeat a claim when the employee actually spoke protected speech and that speech caused the adverse action. The employee must show protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may establish by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech. These limited propositions do not impose liability for every investigation defect or adopt the plurality’s complete investigative procedure.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.

#### Sheets v. Salt Lake County

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit Nos. 93-4128 and 93-4134; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied. 45 F.3d 1383 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** April 29, 1996.

##### Official disclosure of intimate information entrusted for a limited investigation

**Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects objectively intimate marital and family information against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination when the material was entrusted for a limited criminal investigation and the disclosure lacks sufficient governmental justification on this record. A confidentiality assurance informs the objective expectation but does not itself create the federal right; consent to investigative use does not surrender the material to general publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Gary Sheets asserts his own intimate injury from publication of his deceased wife's diary, rather than an inherited personality or ownership right. Public archival inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not itself prove previous public dissemination, voluntary exposure, or the lawfulness of the official's distinct facilitation of publication. General interest in the crime does not establish a sufficient governmental connection to these passages. Legitimate investigation, lawful retention, public accountability, and legally required disclosure remain distinct; none justifies this transfer on the supported record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No archive regime is invalidated, and the private author's speech is not regulated. No general deletion entitlement, inherited privacy claim, copyright right, universal scrutiny or balancing formula, least-intrusive-means requirement, or liability for every state confidentiality breach is established. Promise, official possession, or subjective preference alone is insufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Whalen*'s confidentiality foundation is developed into this actual-disclosure protection while preserving safeguarded collection and acknowledging its reservation; its separate compelling-interest concurrence is not adopted universally. *Nixon* supports personal privacy independent of ownership while preserving protected archival screening. *Paul*'s reputation/arrest-record rule does not foreclose intimate injury or require tangible economic loss. *Collins* preserves separate federal-injury and municipal-attribution inquiries. *Albright* supplies no unified displacement, state-tort cure, or tangible-loss rule. *Hodge*'s reserved disclosure merits are decided only at this confined scope, leaving its retention-immunity and mootness holdings intact.

##### Legal coverage, supported findings, and intent in this action

**Holding:** Constitutional coverage and legal sufficiency receive independent judicial determination, while denial of Rule 50 relief is reviewed favorably to the prevailing party's supported evidence and reasonable inferences. The objective instructions and supported findings establish this confined right's application; the unobjected instruction requires intentional acts causing deprivation, not specific intent to the author's eventual publication or knowledge of constitutional illegality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** Instructions required objectively intimate home, marriage, and family information, a reasonable expectation in Sheets's position, and proof by a preponderance. Supported circumstantial evidence establishes intentional official disclosure and resulting publication without direct admission or eyewitness testimony. Competing testimony concerning the assurance and notice is not replaced with appellate findings. No necessary criterion of the Court's confined rule lacks factual support; declining the circuit's broader compelling-interest/least-intrusive-means formula therefore requires no new trial.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the reviewed constitutional determination, 7–2. The distinct $650,000 compensatory judgment and $77,896.93 fee judgment remain effective. No punitive award, new damages or fee standard, retrial, immunity proceeding, or reassessment is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Limitations, evidence, municipal policy and causation, remittitur, fees, and personal immunity are not independently reopened. Actually preserved defenses retain their own law and posture; none is presumed unresolved. Recognition of the right supplies no automatic conduct-date notice, and personal immunity remains distinct from County liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* requires all relevant conduct-date authority when immunity is properly presented. *Markman*'s patent-specific allocation creates no general power to retry historical facts in this action.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** A public employer does not violate the employee's substantive speech right by disciplining her because of a genuinely held, objectively reasonable account of speech that the Pickering–Connick balance permits it to discipline, even if that account is later shown mistaken. The protection requires actual reliance on the permissible ground; it does not protect a reasonable investigation used to conceal retaliation for different protected speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968): applies the public-employee speech balance, adding this limited factual-error permission.

##### An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Discipline because of an unreasonable and incorrect report of speech that was actually protected violates the substantive speech right when the protected speech bears the required causal relation to the action, subject to distinct causation defenses and remedial immunity. This agreement does not establish liability for every investigative imperfection, for an unreasonable report of actually unprotected speech, or without proof of the underlying protected conduct and causal action.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated, but defendants' summary judgment remains set aside because genuine reliance and retaliation for other protected criticism are materially disputed. **Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. **Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Givhan, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): apply the employee's causal showing and the employer's preponderance same-decision defense.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982): preserves individual qualified immunity as distinct from the substantive speech rule.

#### United States v. National Treasury Employees Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1170.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Scope of public-employee speech relief (The existing ban is unenforceable throughout the certified class; The injunction does not adjudicate the claims of unrepresented senior employees; Crane retains his individual relief without a grade-only remand).

##### A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden

**Holding:** When the Government broadly restricts future compensated expression by its employees acting as citizens, Pickering requires a justification commensurate with the restriction's actual reach and burden on employees and listeners. Section 501(b)'s honoraria ban is unconstitutional as applied to the represented lower-level employees' protected expression unrelated to their employment because the record does not adequately connect that burden to actual or apparent misuse of office; narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563, supplies the employment balance, applied to the breadth of a prospective restriction. Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138, supplies public-concern principles without limiting protected subjects to politics or workplace criticism. Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994, retains its distinct particular-speech and reasonable-account rules. Simon & Schuster v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, supplies the relevance of financial burdens. Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC, June 27, 1994, preserves substantial but reviewable legislative predictive judgments; it does not decide this employment case.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 501(b) prohibits receipt of an honorarium by a Member, officer or employee; §505(2) excludes special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202. Section 505(3) covers money or anything of value for an appearance, speech or article; a series falls within the definition if its subject directly relates to official duties or payment is because of governmental status. That parenthesis does not already restrict every single honorarium. Actual and necessary travel expenses for the individual and one relative are excluded to the extent another person pays or reimburses them, and otherwise deducted to the extent they are not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses mean transportation, lodging and meals away from residence or principal employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received, subject to a $2,000 ceiling on each payment and no financial benefit to the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child or dependent relative. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and a civil penalty up to the greater of $10,000 or compensation received. Under §504(b), a written opinion from an entity identified by §503 protects its recipient and another covered person whose facts are materially indistinguishable from subsection (a)'s sanction only for conduct after issuance, in good faith and in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings. These are qualified provisions, not general exemptions. The separate §501(a) high-level outside-income ceiling is not adjudicated.

Nonenforcement of the existing ban throughout the certified below-GS–16 class is preserved by five votes; Crane retains individual relief by six; relief for unrepresented senior employees is removed. ### Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College — 2000-11-27

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The dissent would recognize an employee's direct claim when attributed expression protected by the public-employment rules substantially motivates an adverse employment action, subject to the employer's proof that it would have acted for an independent lawful reason. The First Amendment constrains the punitive use of expression against the person officials target; mistaken identity does not remove that person's injury or justify suppressing protected criticism. Actual authorship would therefore be unnecessary. Pickering–Connick would determine protection and legitimate employment interests, and Mt. Healthy would govern causation and the same-decision defense. Erroneous attribution or an imperfect investigation alone would prove neither liability nor the other elements. Waters's limited protection for genuine reliance on a reasonable account of disciplinable speech would remain; it would not protect hostility to protected criticism. Letters, notices, chronology, testimony, and competent circumstantial evidence could establish the actual ground under ordinary Crawford-El procedure, without a statistical requirement.

The dissent would also replace Harlow's defense insofar as lack of sufficiently similar precedent defeats damages after an underlying violation is established. An official would instead have to prove actual and objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly permitting the challenged conduct. Legal silence, custom, or general supervisory power would not suffice. In the dissent's view, §1983's remedy and the protections for reasonable factual mistakes within the substantive inquiry make that narrower reliance defense adequate. Elder's independent consideration of relevant law and ordinary procedural protections would survive, as would independent absolute immunity. This would change existing immunity doctrine, including positions Stone-Zsela previously accepted. He would reverse and remand to determine protected status, adverse action, motive, causation, any supported reliance defense, and compensation for proved injury. No damages judgment would follow now, and prospective relief would require an actual continuing threat rather than a presumed revival of the expired notice.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/Wasson_v_Sonoma_County_Junior_College_merits_2000-11-27.md#separate-writings).
