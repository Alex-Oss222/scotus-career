### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The asserted bird-habitat criterion does not supply section 404 authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 404 of the Clean Water Act does not authorize federal jurisdiction over the isolated, nonnavigable intrastate ponds presented solely because migratory birds use them. The Corps must identify authority within the enacted water program; an asserted interstate environmental benefit alone does not supply the delegated jurisdiction, and the challenged jurisdiction and permit-denial ground must be set aside to the extent they depend exclusively on that criterion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Five Justices directly adopt this statutory ground, its limits and reversal with remand. The Court does not reach the contingent Commerce Clause question.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 404(a) authorizes permits for discharge of dredged or fill material into navigable waters. Section 502(7) defines those waters to include waters of the United States and the territorial seas. That expansion reaches beyond classical navigation without making the enacted water limitation meaningless. Riverside Bayview sustained adjacent wetlands because of their statutory and ecological relationship to covered waters and expressly left nonadjacent waters open. These isolated ponds are not wetlands under the Corps' vegetation definition and are not alleged to adjoin navigable waters. Bird use does not itself supply that missing statutory relationship.

Section 404(g)(1) allows state administration outside the waters reserved to federal administration, confirming coverage beyond traditional navigation without defining every remaining pond as covered. Section 101(a)'s biological-integrity and wildlife objectives operate consistently with the Act's provisions and alongside section 101(b)'s protection of state responsibilities. Neither purpose alone settles the disputed jurisdiction. MCI and FDA v. Brown & Williamson require authority in the connected statutory scheme before deference; Sweet Home construed a different enacted habitat-injury definition and supplies no substitute delegation here.

The Corps' change of position is not automatically disqualifying, and the preamble's form is not an independent reason to invalidate it. No universal rule governing Chevron, Skidmore or interpretation of an agency's own regulation is necessary: the asserted reach exceeds the statute on its terms. Christensen's limited advisory-letter holding is not enlarged. Because the statutory answer disposes of this assertion, Congress's possible broader power is not decided. The Court neither imposes present navigability as a universal prerequisite nor determines all isolated waters or materially different supported statutory connections.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes: preserves its adjacent-wetland construction and ecological reasoning, distinguishing the expressly reserved isolated-water application rather than treating adjacency as a universal statutory test.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: applies the requirement that an agency act within the actual statutory grant; an administrative criterion cannot supply omitted authority.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.: applies analysis of the connected statutory scheme before deference; the decision supplies no general rule against consequential agency regulation.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon: distinguishes its habitat-injury construction under a different statutory definition and preserves its actual-injury and causation limits.
- Christensen v. Harris County: leaves intact its specific advisory-letter and own-regulation holdings; it does not establish a categorical rule against every preamble or informal interpretation.
- United States v. Lopez, National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, and United States v. Morrison: leave their actual Commerce rules unchanged because the statutory ground makes the contingent constitutional question unnecessary.



### Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc. / American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Whitman — 2001-02-27

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Implementation costs do not set primary or secondary national standards

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109(b) excludes economic implementation costs from national primary and secondary ambient air-quality standard setting. Primary standards must provide the protection requisite for public health under section 108's scientific pollutant-effects criteria and with an adequate margin of safety; secondary standards must protect public welfare from known or anticipated adverse effects associated with the pollutant's presence under those criteria, without importing the primary safety-margin requirement.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's operative rule and standard/implementation distinction have nine direct primary-standard supporters and eight secondary-standard supporters, as identified in the topology. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the secondary judgment. The additional text-and-structure explanation has eight primary and seven secondary supporters; Breyer joins the operative rules and common distinction through the qualifications stated in his concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory criteria concern the pollutant's effects on health and welfare, while other provisions govern the means of attaining the standards. Describing compliance expense as an economic effect on health or welfare does not convert it into an effect associated with the pollutant's presence. Section 109(d)(2)(C)(iv) separately requires the scientific committee to advise about adverse health, welfare, social, economic and energy effects of attainment and maintenance strategies; it does not direct EPA to reduce either national standard by a cost balance. Periodic review returns to sections 108 and 109(b).

Union Electric preserves the division between standards and implementation. MCI and Brown & Williamson require respect for connected statutory commands; they supply no generic presumption against consequential regulation. The argument that all rational public protection must weigh compliance expense asks the Court to add a criterion Congress assigned elsewhere. Constitutional avoidance cannot create a cost construction unavailable under the enacted commands.

The common rule is not a zero-risk command and does not prohibit costs where other provisions authorize them. No concentration, scientific-method finding or indirect health-effects claim is resolved here. Breyer's additional history-based method and risk observations do not become part of the other Justices' explanation merely because the cost outcomes are unanimous.

#### II. The primary-standard delegation supplies adequate congressional guidance

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109(b)(1) constitutionally guides EPA by identifying the pollutant subject, published scientific criteria, requisite public-health protection and adequate safety margin. Requisite protection is sufficient without exceeding what protection requires; Congress need not choose a unique numerical concentration to confine the technical judgment.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Kennedy's adequate-guidance rule and common explanation. Kennedy, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join the bounded-execution characterization; Stevens and Souter accept a constitutional delegation of legislative rulemaking instead.

**Controlling explanation:** Hampton, Mistretta and Touby require an intelligible legislative principle. Congress here selected the protected interest, subject, degree of protection and scientific framework. The remaining judgment applies those choices to evidence; it is not permission to select any social objective. Loving reinforces attention to actual congressional limits, while its military and criminal setting retains its qualifications.

The absence of one inevitable numerical answer does not establish standardless power. Scientific uncertainty and the absence of a risk-free concentration require reasoned judgment within the statutory limits, subject to ordinary review. The seven Justices accepting the bounded-execution account regard that judgment as implementation of Congress's policy rather than transfer of the policy choice itself. Stevens and Souter agree that Congress has supplied sufficient limits while describing the authorized rulemaking as legislative. Neither characterization difference changes the adequacy holding.

Thomas joins under existing precedent while reserving its ultimate constitutional sufficiency; no reconsideration of that doctrine is presented. The Court reverses the delegation ruling and reaches no separately presented constitutional challenge to section 109(b)(2). Statutory context concerning that subsection's welfare objective supplies no additional constitutional adjudication.

#### III. An agency cannot cure a genuinely standardless delegation

**Holding and operative rule:** If Congress has supplied no constitutionally sufficient governing principle, an agency cannot cure the defect by selecting its own limit. EPA may choose and explain a lawful scientific methodology within section 109(b)(1)'s existing boundaries, but may not supply a missing legislative policy.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Kennedy's no-agency-cure holding; it independently rejects the court of appeals' proposed cure mechanism.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing delegation cases require Congress to establish the boundary. An agency's promise to exercise less power would itself make the policy choice the legislature failed to make. The court of appeals therefore could not remedy a genuinely standardless statute by returning it for EPA to devise a limiting principle.

Here Congress has already supplied adequate guidance, so the constitutional remand fails on that ground as well. The Court removes that remand without approving every methodology or insulating scientific reasoning from administrative review. Selecting and defending a permissible method inside the statutory assignment differs from creating a new limit to save a statute that has none. Economic balancing is neither required nor authorized as a cure for this asserted constitutional defect.

#### IV. The completed implementation framework is independently reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** EPA's settled choice of the statutory implementation framework accompanying the final ozone standard is final action reviewable under section 307(b), and the present legal question is ripe. The determination selects the governing implementation obligations and has present legal consequences; tentative guidance, influence or planning expense alone does not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's independent finality and ripeness holding has all nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Bennett requires both completion of the agency's decisionmaking and legal consequences. The 1996 proposal was preliminary and nonbinding. After comment, EPA settled which statutory framework would govern the revised standard, retained Subpart 2 only until old-standard attainment, and thereafter refused to reconsider that determination. The consequence is the governing legal regime for States' required implementation work, not merely persuasive advice or an uncompleted future choice. No sanction, approved plan or current violation is necessary.

The issue is legal and needs no further site-specific facts; review does not interrupt an unresolved agency choice on that framework. States must undertake implementation under the selected regime. Section 307 supplies a specific preenforcement channel and does not invariably require the enforcement effects that might govern a different APA challenge. Ohio Forestry's deferred logging-plan dispute preserved later project review and did not decide APA finality; its future-project premise is absent here.

This Court's authorized questions include reviewability and implementation, and the necessary statutory subsidiary question falls within Rule 14.1(a). That scope conclusion is separate from appellate jurisdiction and finality. No plain-error enlargement is required. The Court sustains review independently of the final-standard route in Holding V.

#### V. Review of the final ozone standard independently includes its disputed implementation premise

**Holding and operative rule:** A court reviewing the concededly final ozone standard under section 307(b) may decide the implementation relationship actually placed in issue by industry's claim that Subpart 2 displaced the general implementation regime and therefore barred revision. That necessary statutory premise supplies an independently sufficient review route; it does not bring every future plan or implementation dispute into final-standard review.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this alternative holding. Stone-Zsela's reviewability support rests on Holding IV and does not join this additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Industry challenged EPA's authority to revise the standard on the premise that revision would invoke a general implementation regime displaced by Subpart 2. EPA answered that the subparts could operate together under its construction. A reviewing court cannot decide the actual challenge while treating the opposing statutory premise as immune from review. Section 307(b)'s review of the final standard therefore encompasses this necessary legal question even apart from finality of the implementation interpretation as distinct action.

The route is bounded by the challenge actually made. It neither approves later plans nor supplies review of hypothetical future agency choices. The authorized implementation and reviewability questions encompass the necessary subsidiary issue under Rule 14.1(a); that Court-rule relationship does not itself create section 307 jurisdiction. No separate plain-error route is invoked.

#### VI. EPA must reconcile the general and ozone-specific provisions without erasing applicable commands

**Holding and operative rule:** EPA may resolve genuine statutory fit questions concerning revised ozone standards, but attainment of the old one-hour standard alone does not authorize elimination of otherwise applicable Subpart 2 classifications, dates and associated requirements. EPA's conditional transition is unlawful to that extent, and the agency must supply a construction that gives the applicable general and ozone-specific provisions their enacted work.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Kennedy's implementation holding, qualifications and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 172(a)(1)(C) separately preserves specifically provided classifications, and section 172(a)(2)(D) preserves specifically provided attainment dates. Section 181(b)(1) reaches later designations, defeating the inference that a 1989 heading confines the specific subpart to old classifications alone. EPA cannot retain those constraints temporarily and then discard them solely because an area attains the old standard. Its actual position is conditional termination, not immediate universal repeal.

The old one-hour design-value structure and revised eight-hour standard nonetheless create real fit questions, including areas below the old threshold but above the new one and dates already passed. Section 181(b)(1) extends fixed dates for later classifications. Preserving applicable commands therefore does not mechanically transplant every original metric and date or eliminate every role for Subpart 1.

MCI, Brown & Williamson and National Mining require each applicable statutory command to retain force. Chevron permits reasonable resolution of genuine delegated ambiguity; it does not permit an agency to make a connected specific provision inoperative. Christensen preserves the separate inquiry into interpretive legal force, and no amount of deference supplies authority missing from the statute.

EPA receives the first opportunity to reconcile the provisions lawfully. The Court selects no substitute regime, concentration or State plan and decides no further section 182 application. The following existing qualifications govern the remand at their own terms; they do not establish that a particular area qualifies for an adjustment, extension or different classification.

**Precedent treatment:**

### Easley v. Cromartie / Smallwood v. Cromartie — 2001-04-18

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Easley_v_Cromartie_Smallwood_v_Cromartie_merits_2001-04-18.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Easley_v_Cromartie_Smallwood_v_Cromartie_merits_2001-04-18.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Racial predominance cannot cure the absence of a cognizable districting injury

**Holding and operative rule:** A common territorial district cannot be invalidated solely because racial considerations predominated, even when ordinary districting criteria were subordinated. The court must identify an actually presented and preserved exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior civic membership or other independently cognizable deprivation, apply that claim's own elements, burdens and justification rules, and otherwise enter judgment for defendants on the insufficient design-only theory.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, with the author counted once within those five. The four other Justices support only immediate vacatur, not this rule or remand. Five direct joins control; no Marks synthesis is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw v. Reno distinguishes the racial composition of a common electorate from independently unequal treatment. Johnson v. Miller applies that distinction even when predominance and subordination of ordinary criteria are established. Hunt v. Cromartie made the same distinction the mandate in this case. A fuller finding about a legally insufficient premise cannot supply the missing constitutional wrong. Rule 52's deference to factual findings does not determine their legal consequence.

District shape, race-politics correlation, drafting communications, testimony, maps and electoral experience may be relevant to a cognizable claim. They are neither an automatic injury nor evidence that must be disregarded. No statistical model or particular alternative map is a universal entry requirement; any evidence required by the actual claim remains necessary. A partisan explanation does not defeat a separately proved wrong. Likewise, unequal treatment itself can complete an injury without a second governmental act, electoral defeat or financial loss.

The record establishes that the district court relied on predominance, but it does not establish whether a separate claim was actually preserved and proved under Hunt's mandate. The proper consequence is legal vacatur with confined review of any such claim, using existing findings to the extent material and ordinary procedural rules. It is neither a new claim nor a source-based finding of waiver.

**Limits and remand work:** Existence, preservation, proof and appropriate relief for an independent claim require the actual record. No final plan-validity ruling, replacement-plan statutory condition, new map or election remedy is decided. An unpleaded theory is outside the preserved case; no presumption establishes that all independent claims were omitted. Ordinary application of the controlling rule remains distinct from a legal question left unanswered.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Shaw v. Reno is reaffirmed: territorial racial composition alone is insufficient, while independently unequal treatment remains actionable under its own rules.
- Johnson v. Miller is reaffirmed and applied to the legal consequence of a completed predominance finding; its ordinary evidentiary rules and no-second-loss qualification remain.
- Hunt v. Cromartie is enforced: its remand did not direct a predominance-only trial and cannot be displaced by completing one.
- Bush v. Vera retains the same territorial-design boundary and protection of independently cognizable claims.
- Adarand Constructors v. Peña remains the justification framework for an actual covered racial allocation; it does not supply the missing cause of action from territorial design.

**Resulting law:** Predominance alone remains insufficient in territorial districting challenges after trial as well as at pleading or summary judgment. Courts must connect purpose findings to a cognizable claim and provide relief only for a proved wrong under that claim's elements. Ordinary factual deference cannot convert an insufficient theory into a constitutional deprivation. The decision preserves actual exclusion, dilution, unequal participation and civic-membership claims without requiring a later additional loss.



### Atwater v. City of Lago Vista — 2001-04-24

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Atwater_v_City_of_Lago_Vista_merits_2001-04-24.md#holdings).

#### Additional custody for a known fine-only criminal offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor does not, by itself, justify additional station-house transport and confinement. That added custody must reasonably serve an actual enforcement function, considering supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence-protection or necessary-processing needs; the examples are nonexclusive, are not presumed, and require neither the least intrusive imaginable alternative nor citation whenever conceivable.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to this rule, the rejection of categorical summary judgment and the common mandate. The rule extends Ricci's civil-licensing holding to the fine-only criminal enforcement presented here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority to stop a person and the reasonable extent of custody are different questions. Ricci requires an actual enforcement connection for added station custody after a fine-only civil violation. The criminal label does not, by itself, justify the same added restraint where imprisonment is unavailable as punishment. The State's penalty choice reinforces the need to assess the scale of the seizure, without granting immunity from arrest.

Whren preserves an ordinary initial stop on objective probable cause while retaining independent limits on greater restraint. Knowles keeps search consequences separate from the antecedent lawful-arrest question. Robinson and Gustafson presuppose a qualifying lawful arrest; their incident-search rules cannot supply the missing justification for disputed custody. Wilson v. Arkansas treats relevant history as part of reasonableness without constitutionalizing every state authorization. A categorical historical breach-of-peace rule is unnecessary to decide this actual-function question.

Clear administration is a legitimate interest. But optional booking paperwork cannot conclusively establish the need for the custody that creates it. The inquiry permits practical judgments without hindsight perfection or proof that every alternative would fail. The lower court's probable-cause rule omitted that inquiry.

The record must include both the missing physical documents and the supplied license number, address and known local residence. The earlier warning followed recognition that the child was belted; it does not establish another seatbelt offense. The friend took the children. These facts cannot be replaced by assumptions of failed verification, flight risk, continuing danger or compulsory station processing. Neither berating nor a routine booking preference alone supplies an enforcement need. The correct rule defeats defendants' categorical entitlement to judgment, while the Justices differ on the ultimate application and the remaining record determinations.

#### The common judgment does not establish a controlling application conclusion

**Judgment and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit judgment sustaining the categorical custody summary judgment is vacated, and the custody claim returns for proceedings under the actual-enforcement rule. The return permits genuinely supported disputes and preserved defenses, requires no new evidentiary hearing, invents no enforcement justification and reopens no established fact by speculation; it does not invalidate the uncontested seatbelt convictions or award damages.

**Authority and application limits:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer expressly support this vacatur and remand. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer also conclude that the supported plaintiff-favorable account does not establish a sufficient custody function and would reverse on that ground. Stone-Zsela does not join that application: he requires actual verification and custody-need determinations under the rule. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the arrest reasonable on their stated grounds and would affirm. There is no majority holding that this custody was constitutional or unconstitutional; the four-Justice application and the distinct concurrence cannot be combined through Marks into one.

Personal qualified immunity remains a separate conduct-date inquiry using March 1997 law; Ricci, Knowles, McArthur and this decision supply no retrospective notice. Municipal liability separately requires an attributable policy or custom, applicable fault, causation and injury. No final finding resolves those predicates. Other punishment classes are outside the presented question. Ordinary application of the rule and liability work under it are distinct from the Court's lack of a controlling answer on this custody's ultimate reasonableness.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights, May 4, 1998:** Its actual-enforcement requirement is extended from fine-only civil licensing custody to the added custody for a known fine-only criminal offense. Its rejection of categorical arrest immunity, least restrictive means and a universal citation duty remains. Its record-specific application fracture supplies no automatic application answer here.
- **Whren v. United States, June 10, 1996:** Its objective initial-stop rule is preserved and its independent limits on greater restraint applied. Hostility does not replace the objective enforcement inquiry.
- **Knowles v. Iowa, December 8, 1998:** Its distinction between actual lawful custody and search consequences is applied; it supplies no authority to justify an arrest by its ensuing search or booking effects.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their incident-search rules remain conditioned on a qualifying lawful arrest. Neither decides the contested antecedent custody authority here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas, May 22, 1995:** Relevant history remains part of constitutional reasonableness, without turning state arrest authorization into a complete federal justification.
- **Illinois v. McArthur, February 20, 2001:** Its particularized residential-preservation holding remains at that scope. Concrete need and fitted execution inform the comparison; its home setting and approximate duration create no traffic-booking permission or safe harbor.
- **Wilson v. Layne; Wallis v. Spencer:** Conduct-date immunity and independent municipal responsibility remain distinct from constitutional merits. Neither supplies a present finding of immunity, City policy, fault or causation here.

**Resulting law:** Ricci's actual-enforcement rule now governs added station transport and confinement for known fine-only criminal offenses as well as its prior civil setting. Probable cause and local arrest permission alone do not establish reasonableness; genuinely supported enforcement needs and the scale of restraint must be assessed. Neither a universal citation requirement nor categorical arrest immunity follows.

The Court supplies no controlling application conclusion about this custody, immunity or municipal liability. The four-Justice conclusion that the accepted account is constitutionally insufficient remains noncontrolling; the actual-function rule and bounded corrective mandate control further proceedings. Ordinary fact, defense and remedial work must respect the supported record rather than manufacture new justification.



### C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma — 2001-04-30

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/C_L_Enterprises_v_Citizen_Band_Potawatomi_Indian_Tribe_merits_2001-04-30.md#holdings).

#### The integrated agreement clearly consents to confirmation

**Holding and operative rule:** A tribe clearly waives immunity from the contemplated state-court confirmation proceeding when its integrated agreement requires binding arbitration, expressly provides for judicial entry of the award, incorporates rules providing for that enforcement, and selects an applicable regime under which a competent state court may enter judgment. This agreement supplies that limited consent without a prescribed formula referring to sovereign immunity; commercial activity, a generic choice-of-law clause, or arbitration standing alone does not establish the same waiver.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices, controls the rule, its application and the limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Kiowa preserves tribal immunity for commercial and governmental contracts both on and off reservation land, while recognizing clear waiver. Santa Clara Pueblo requires unequivocal consent, and Potawatomi separates the underlying obligation from amenability to suit. The Court applies those principles to what this agreement promises. Its final-arbitration undertaking expressly ends in judicial entry under the selected Oklahoma enforcement law. Treating the competent-court qualification as requiring an entirely separate immunity waiver would deny that express judicial undertaking its operation. The qualification preserves ordinary court competence; it does not undo the consent the agreement itself conveys.

First Options requires actual agreement and attention to its scope. The Tribe tendered the form, but that conduct supports assent to the words rather than independently waiving immunity. Clarity concerns the undertaking's legal content, not special immunity vocabulary. The agreement links the arbitral decision and judicial confirmation under an identified regime. Enforcing that bounded forum choice respects both sovereign immunity and the Tribe's power to consent.

The commercial location and perceived fairness of requiring payment cannot independently supply waiver. The Tribe's refusal to participate in arbitration establishes neither default nor the correctness of the award. Ordinary confirmation defenses remain for their own adjudication after removal of the immunity bar.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding creates no otherwise absent federal subject-matter jurisdiction. Execution against particular assets and unrelated claims are outside the confirmation question. Signatory authority and former 25 U.S.C. §81 were not aired below and fall outside the accepted issue. The Court need not decide whether tribal and state immunity-waiver doctrines are parallel. Incorporated arbitration rules supply no new holding about delegation of arbitrability.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc.:** Applies the clear-waiver exception without changing immunity for governmental or commercial contracts, on or off reservation land.
- **Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez:** Applies unequivocal relinquishment without adding a prescribed verbal formula.
- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe:** Preserves the distinction between substantive obligation and amenability; the obligation alone remains insufficient.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Applies actual assent and instrument-specific scope without expanding its delegation rule.

**Resulting law:** The combined undertaking here clearly authorizes the contemplated confirmation action. The general tribal-immunity rule remains intact. Other contracts must be construed on their own terms under the same clear-waiver requirement; the Court adopts no commercial-activity exception or automatic waiver from arbitration alone. Remaining award and enforcement issues require their independent legal predicates.



### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Souter dissents, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. They read the defined waters term, the aquatic-protection objective and section 404(g)(1)'s state-administration structure to authorize this supported habitat-water application. Riverside Bayview leaves isolated waters open; its adjacency facts do not establish an exhaustive limit. Sweet Home and PUD No. 1 support applying broad protective language according to its actual conditions. The agency's interpretation reinforces their textual conclusion without turning every preamble into a source of binding authority or disregarding section 101(b)'s state role.

They would also sustain this application under Home Builders' complete independent externality route. The regulated subject is discharge of dredged or fill material into the claimed waters. Filling removes aquatic habitat used by a shared mobile population; loss in one State can diminish the resource available elsewhere, while another State cannot restore that out-of-state habitat through ordinary territorial authority. The regional production and conversion evidence supports substantial class-wide importance, and the agency's aquatic-use and interstate-bird findings, together with the breeding-colony material, connect the site functionally to that mechanism. Direct permit review of habitat filling reasonably fits the resulting spillover. These are supported inferences and bounded predictive judgments, not new measurements of population loss.

Their strongest contrary concern is that the regional report does not describe every pond or quantify this fill's effect. They find the combined functional evidence sufficient without requiring a separate substantial effect for each discharge, a site percentage, a specified scientific method or proof of failed interstate agreements. The estimate of up to 50 percent of United States migratory-waterfowl production is not a percentage attributable to this site or to Illinois. Neither bird presence nor conservation's national importance alone is sufficient. They identify no independent necessary-regime predicate, and Holland's treaty holding is not a Commerce holding. Their preserved view that actual habitat-altering construction may itself be economic conduct does not replace their full join to this externality ground. They would affirm jurisdiction without reopening the abandoned permit-merits challenge or awarding a permit.

### Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc. / American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Whitman — 2001-02-27

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Breyer joins the operative cost rules and separation of standard setting from implementation, but reaches them through the whole structure and the reported 1970 sponsor and Senate materials and later 1977 and 1989 materials showing a technology-forcing choice. He rejects deriving the result from isolated section 109 words or a general presumption that rational cost consideration always needs unusually clear authorization. Ordinarily he would construe genuine ambiguity to allow attention to seriously disproportionate adverse consequences; Congress displaced that choice here. Scientific discretion can consider severity, numbers affected, distribution, uncertainty, contextually trivial risks and comparative health consequences. That discussion supplies no implementation-cost exception, zero-risk mandate or finding that a particular technology will emerge. He retains his direct joins to the other holdings and the bounded-execution characterization.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The statutory limits and scientific criteria sufficiently confine EPA, and an agency could not cure an absence of legislative policy by choosing one itself. They join both propositions and every judgment component. Their disagreement is with describing authorized rulemaking as inherently nonlegislative: in their view it is legislative rulemaking that Congress may constitutionally delegate when sufficiently bounded. Their characterization does not enlarge the agency's authority or validate a standardless assignment.

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** Thomas joins the Court's holdings under existing delegation precedent but reserves whether an intelligible principle always satisfies Article I. His prior military-specific position in Loving supplies no general civilian-delegation theory. No party has presented reconsideration of the governing standard, so he does not demand it here or withhold the present joins.

### Easley v. Cromartie / Smallwood v. Cromartie — 2001-04-18

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, concurs in immediate vacatur but dissents from the constitutional rule and remand limitation. Consistent with his separate position in Hunt, he would reconsider the rule that rejects predominant racial assignment as a cognizable injury and permit a claim when race predominates over ordinary districting criteria. The objection is governmental racial assignment, not unusual shape, racial awareness or a percentage alone. The common electorate does not, in this view, answer the unequal-treatment concern.

The four would return the case for adjudication under that proposed rule instead of foreclosing the predominance claim. They do not conclusively affirm every trial finding, decide the full justification question or certify compliance with Hunt's different mandate. O'Connor's join does not adopt a finding of predominance as her own final factual conclusion. The writing does not establish an independently preserved claim or its waiver and supplies no controlling replacement for the Court's five-Justice rule.

### Atwater v. City of Lago Vista — 2001-04-24

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, in Part II.** These four conclude that the supported plaintiff-favorable account fails the governing constitutional standard. Atwater supplied identifying information, was known locally, acknowledged the offense and arranged for a friend to take the children. General child-safety and appearance purposes do not establish why handcuffing, transport, booking and cell confinement served a sufficient added function. Missing physical papers remain relevant but do not establish inability to identify her, and the earlier warning supplies no recidivism premise. Booking cannot prove its own antecedent necessity merely by generating paperwork.

Their conclusion applies Ricci's functional rule to this record; it does not require citation in every case or impossibility of every alternative. Their Ricci application reservation was record-specific, and they do not repeat it here. They prefer reversal on this constitutional ground but expressly support the common vacatur because it removes the categorical judgment and returns actual disputes and preserved defenses under their rule. They do not join a reservation that the same established facts might suffice. Actual liability, March 1997 immunity, City responsibility and lawful relief remain distinct, with no speculative factual reopening or obligatory hearing.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment.** He joins the actual-function extension and categorical-rule correction but does not decide this custody's ultimate reasonableness. Missing papers may justify verification; they do not conclusively justify each later restraint, particularly in light of the supplied number, address and known residence. Berating and a routine booking preference alone cannot justify confinement. He would require the lower courts to determine whether supported identification or other practical enforcement needs justified the additional restraint, without inventing a verification failure or demanding a perfect alternative. His remand separately preserves conduct-date immunity and municipal policy and causation. He does not join Part II's present constitutional-insufficiency conclusion, award damages or disturb the conviction.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter.** Ordinary processing of an observed criminal charge, with the actual bounded execution here, supplies a sufficient enforcement relationship. The restraint involved handcuffing, transportation, booking and approximately an hour of confinement pending bond, not an unrelated investigation or a claimed permission for any duration or method. Their Ricci dissent treated ordinary same-offense processing as legally meaningful without proof that citation was impossible. That earlier case's documents and continuing-business problem are not transferred as facts here. This is a legal appraisal of criminal processing, not a finding of a special identity, appearance or safety need or compulsory station procedure. Supplied identification and local residence weaken those distinct factual rationales without eliminating ordinary criminal enforcement. The four would affirm on constitutional merits, leaving immunity and municipal predicates unadjudicated. Excessive force, unrelated additional detention and unjustified duration retain their own limits; no fixed permissible interval follows.

**Scalia, additionally dissenting, joined by Souter.** They would also sustain an ordinary criminal-arrest rule under which probable cause for an observed criminal offense and ordinary enforcement processing suffice, subject to independent execution, duration and enforcement-relationship limits. They distinguish Ricci's civil violation and give administrable criminal-arrest authority independent weight. The rule does not depend on predictions that an identified local driver will evade court, on subjective motive or on search consequences. They reject an added individualized showing merely because punishment is fine-only. Kennedy and Thomas do not join this broader categorical proposition; their dissent rests on the narrower actual processing and execution ground.
