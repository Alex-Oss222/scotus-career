# Buford v. United States, No. 99-9073; 532 U.S. 59. — 2001-03-20

Source Record: Buford_v_United_States_merits_2001-03-20.md

## Event

Buford v. United States, No. 99-9073. Argued January 8, 2001; decided March 20, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 201 F.3d 937. The question is the appellate standard for a district court's determination that the prior drug conviction was not functionally consolidated with four related robbery convictions for career-offender treatment. Buford seeks independent appellate reconsideration; the United States seeks affirmance.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

Affirm the Seventh Circuit, 9–0, on the functional-consolidation review question.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirmed, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None. The supported determination and the challenged 188-month sentence remain intact. No new sentencing proceeding or regrouping is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Complete rule, explanation, application and affirmance |

## Holdings

### Deference follows the task performed

**Holding and operative rule:** Appellate courts independently review the meaning of the related-case provisions and authoritative commentary, but defer to a district court's supported, record-specific determination of functional consolidation when it depends on the procedural history and practical sentencing context. Concurrent sentences and a common hearing are relevant but not conclusive, and absence of a formal consolidation order is not itself dispositive; under that allocation, the determination here is sustained.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the district court performed a practical sentencing task, rather than defining a general constitutional boundary. Section 3742(e) requires due deference to application of the Guidelines to facts. Koon and Powell supply the necessary separation: legal misinterpretations and unsupported grounds remain correctable, while a supported assessment sensitive to the particular proceedings receives deference. Stinson makes consistent commentary authoritative; it does not require appellate redetermination of every application.

The four robbery convictions were concededly related. The disputed drug conviction shared a sentencing judge, hearing, presentence report and concurrent terms, and counsel agreed to simultaneous sentencing. Those facts favor consolidation and must be considered. Separate filings, prosecutors, pleas, judgments and sentences nevertheless supported the district court's contrary assessment. Familiarity with local sentencing arrangements helps identify what those proceedings actually accomplished. Neither one hearing nor the absence of a formal order supplies an automatic answer.

The increased sentencing range makes correct legal criteria important, but does not make this fact-bound application especially useful as a general appellate rule. Independent legal review, correction of unsupported inferences and the Sentencing Commission's supervisory role preserve uniformity. Ornelas and Fields require independent review of ultimate constitutional search thresholds for their distinct institutional reasons; they do not select one standard for every mixed question. No actual legal misinterpretation requiring another sentencing proceeding is identified here.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The complete substantive definition of functional consolidation and a constitutional jury-allocation claim lie outside the granted review question. The commentary's intervening-arrest exclusion remains distinct from the alternatives of offenses on the same occasion, part of a single common scheme or plan, or consolidated for trial or sentencing. No finding under another alternative is made. This decision creates no general constitutional recidivism exception and changes neither Apprendi's reserved Guidelines questions nor Almendarez-Torres's limited controlling judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Koon v. United States / Powell v. United States:** Applied to separate independent legal correction from deference to supported sentencing application; neither is made a blanket rule for every Guidelines question.
- **Stinson v. United States:** Applied for the authority of consistent commentary; its superior-law limits remain unchanged.
- **Ornelas v. United States; United States v. Fields / Crawley:** Distinguished by the recurring constitutional thresholds requiring independent review; their review rules remain fully effective.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Its division of appellate responsibility supports attention to the task rather than a universal mixed-question label.
- **Apprendi v. New Jersey; Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Their actual holdings and reservations are left intact; this appellate-review decision supplies no new jury or recidivism rule.

## Law After Decision

The related-case functional-consolidation application receives task-sensitive deference, with legal criteria independently reviewed and record support still required. The decision specifies this review allocation without changing the substantive commentary or constitutional search-review rules. It does not establish that simultaneous or concurrent sentencing always combines, or always fails to combine, prior convictions.

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment stands on the question reviewed; the challenged sentence remains in force. No further nonroutine Court action or sentencing remand is ordered.

The review question assumes the functional-consolidation doctrine presented below; it does not decide its complete substantive definition.

# TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc., No. 99-1571; 532 U.S. 23. — 2001-03-20

Source Record: TrafFix_Devices_v_Marketing_Displays_merits_2001-03-20.md

## Event

TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc., No. 99-1571. Argued November 29, 2000; decided March 20, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 200 F.3d 929. The Court reviews the effect of an expired utility patent and alternative designs on the functionality of the asserted sign-stand trade dress. The Sixth Circuit reversed summary judgment on that issue; TrafFix seeks restoration of the judgment, while MDI seeks further trade-dress proceedings.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

Reverse the reviewed functionality ruling and remand to restore judgment for TrafFix on the asserted functional dual-spring trade-dress claim, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None. Restore judgment for TrafFix on the asserted functional trade dress and implement the consequences for relief dependent on that ground. Preserve the independent WindMaster/WindBuster word-mark judgment. No antitrust recovery or revival of the sham-litigation counterclaim is ordered, and unrelated independent relief is not disturbed.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Complete functionality rule, application and confined reversal/remand |

## Holdings

### Useful features remain available to competitors

**Holding and operative rule:** An expired utility patent is strong evidence that the feature it identifies as useful is functional. When the claimed feature is essential to the article's use or purpose or affects its cost or quality, source recognition and alternative workable designs do not make it protectable trade dress; a genuinely distinct ornamental, incidental or arbitrary appearance must be evaluated on its own rather than presumed functional merely because it appears in a patented product.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, directly controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that trade-dress law does not continue exclusive control of useful engineering merely because buyers recognize its source. Inwood supplies the essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality inquiry. Qualitex retains that inquiry while also addressing competitive disadvantage unrelated to reputation. Its competitive-necessity discussion does not require another inquiry after traditional utilitarian functionality is established. Wal-Mart separately requires source significance for product design; recognition cannot replace nonfunctionality.

The patents describe the dual springs' contribution to wind resistance and stability. That operation, supported by the record, establishes the functional character of the feature claimed here. MDI's argument concerns the overall stand, including legs, uprights and spring placement. Considering that combination does not reveal a distinct nonfunctional ornament in this record. Requiring a competitor to conceal, cover or rearrange the working springs would restrict use of the useful configuration itself. The availability of another workable arrangement therefore does not defeat the functionality ground.

The patent creates a substantial evidentiary obstacle, not a conclusive presumption covering every appearance detail. A claimant remains able to identify and support a genuinely separate nonfunctional feature. None has been established in the claim before the Court. The undisputed functional evidence is sufficient without deciding the temporal application of the 1999 burden amendment. Functionality disposes of this trade-dress theory, so secondary meaning supplies no further ground that must be reached.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The decision establishes no automatic constitutional bar to all trade dress associated with expired patents and announces no new aesthetic-functionality test. A distinct unasserted ornamental design lies outside this claim. Those questions are unnecessary or outside the granted scope, respectively; the functional dual-spring claim itself is resolved, not returned for another alternatives inquiry.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc.:** Applied for the traditional essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality rule.
- **Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.:** Applied and clarified: its competitive-disadvantage discussion complements, rather than displaces, the traditional utilitarian bar. No additional competitive-necessity showing is required once that bar is met.
- **Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Samara Brothers, Inc.:** Applied to keep source significance for product design separate from nonfunctionality; its actual distinctiveness rule remains unchanged.
- **Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc.:** Its protection of otherwise qualifying trade dress does not remove the independent functionality requirement; its existing scope remains intact.

## Law After Decision

A utility patent supplies strong, feature-specific evidence of functionality. Proven useful function independently defeats trade-dress protection despite source recognition or available alternative designs. The rule leaves protection of genuinely distinct nonfunctional appearance to its own requirements and leaves the 1999 amendment's temporal reach undecided.

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for ordinary implementation of the restored functionality judgment and its dependent remedial consequences. The lower courts may not reopen the resolved dual-spring claim merely to compare alternative designs. Independent word-mark relief remains effective, and no new ornamental claim or antitrust disposition is created.

# Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams, No. 99-1379; 532 U.S. 105. — 2001-03-21

Source Record: Circuit_City_Stores_v_Adams_merits_2001-03-21.md

## Event

Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams, No. 99-1379. Argued November 6, 2000; decided March 21, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 194 F.3d 1070. The question is whether FAA section 1 excludes employment contracts generally within the Act's asserted commerce reach or only the employment contracts of transportation workers. The Ninth Circuit reversed an order compelling arbitration and enjoining Adams's state discrimination and tort action; Circuit City seeks reversal, and Adams seeks affirmance.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

Affirm the Ninth Circuit, 5–4, on the section 1 employment-contract exclusion.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirmed, 5–4. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The reversal of the federal order compelling arbitration and enjoining the state action remains effective. Adams may pursue the state proceeding subject to independently applicable law and properly preserved defenses. No discrimination liability or independent state-law arbitration ground is decided.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Complete section 1 rule, explanation, application and affirmance |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Transportation-worker construction; would reverse and remand while preserving actual enforcement predicates |

## Holdings

### Employment contracts fall within the section 1 exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1 of the Federal Arbitration Act excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the Act; its residual employment category is not confined to transportation occupations. Adams's retail employment agreement is excluded, so the FAA supplies no authority to compel arbitration of his claims or to enjoin his state action.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt the statutory construction, its general employment-contract reach, its application and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the employment exclusion must be read with the coverage provision in the same enactment. Section 1 identifies employment contracts of seamen and railroad employees and then adds other classes of workers engaged in interstate or foreign commerce. The shared category can be workers in commerce. The residual words do not expressly restrict the exclusion to transporting persons or goods.

The employer's associated-words argument is substantial, but the inference drawn from a statutory list must remain consistent with the text's contemporary commercial meaning and setting. The named occupations placed important federal employment arrangements beyond doubt; the residual language protects the wider employment category. Reading section 2 to follow the full reach of commerce authority while confining the accompanying labor exclusion to occupations most clearly within federal authority in 1925 would disconnect the provisions. The contemporaneous labor setting confirms the broader boundary where the competing linguistic readings leave uncertainty. Concern about employee choice supports that reading but cannot itself create an exemption.

Allied-Bruce establishes section 2's actual-commerce reach and preserves its operation in state and federal court, but expressly leaves section 1 unresolved. Gilmer concerns statutory claims under a securities-registration agreement and reserves the employment-contract question. Neither supplies a contrary controlling answer. Ordinary assent and revocation principles under First Options and Casarotto govern when their predicates are present; they cannot substitute for the threshold category Congress excluded.

Adams was employed as a retail sales counselor under the employment application containing this arbitration clause. His agreement therefore falls within the exclusion without a finding that its particular procedure denied an effective remedy. The Ninth Circuit correctly removed the federal compulsion and injunction order. No existing Supreme Court holding is overruled.

**Limits and matters not reached:** Otherwise covered commercial agreements remain subject to section 2. An independent state-law basis for arbitration, discrimination liability, and classification of an independent contractor lie outside this employee's presented FAA exclusion question. The Court decides no ordinary contract defense or constitutional jury-waiver question because the statutory exclusion disposes of the requested FAA relief. The state proceeding remains subject to independently applicable law and properly preserved defenses.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson:** Preserved and distinguished. Its actual-commerce construction and section 2 state-court rule continue for otherwise covered agreements; its express employment reservation is resolved here under section 1.
- **Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp.:** Distinguished because the agreement there arose from securities registration. Arbitration of statutory employment claims through such an agreement does not settle the separate employment-contract exclusion.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Preserved for antecedent assent, actual delegation and review in its proper setting. It does not enlarge the FAA's statutory coverage, and no new delegation or award-review rule is adopted.
- **Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto:** Preserved for genuinely general contract defenses and the rule against arbitration-specific disabilities in covered agreements. Those enforcement rules do not replace section 1's antecedent exclusion.
- **Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph:** Its finality, statutory-access and record-specific cost-development holdings remain effective. They supply neither a section 1 answer nor a requirement that this excluded employee first prove prohibitive arbitral costs.
- **Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America:** Its section 301 labor-award and public-policy analysis remains in its distinct statutory setting; it supplies no contrary construction of FAA section 1.

## Law After Decision

Section 1's previously unresolved employment-contract category now excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the FAA, including retail employment. The Act therefore cannot supply compulsion or an injunction for this excluded agreement. Its section 2 rules remain operative for otherwise covered commercial agreements. The exclusion does not declare employment arbitration agreements categorically unenforceable under independent state law, decide the underlying claims, or impose a costs inquiry as a condition of exclusion.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** These Justices would reverse the categorical employment exclusion and remand. They read the residual class by the transportation character shared by seamen and railroad employees, giving independent work to both the enumeration and section 1's narrower commerce formulation. The labor context cannot erase that enacted limitation; section 2's broader coverage language does not require an equally broad section 1 exclusion. Gilmer reserved the precise question and thus does not decide it, but neither does that reservation establish the broader exclusion.

Their disagreement concerns coverage, not automatic enforcement. They would preserve actual formation, assent, delegation, clause scope, genuinely general revocation defenses, federal jurisdiction and lawful injunction authority. No new cost hearing follows merely because a defense is conceivable, and Adams's state-law claims do not automatically acquire Green Tree's federal-statutory-access premise. The original federal compulsion suit is distinct from the state-court command challenged by Scalia and Thomas in Allied-Bruce; their join withdraws no such state-court position. Coverage would remove the Ninth Circuit's exclusion ground without automatically reinstating every part of the district court's order or deciding discrimination liability.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgment stands, and the district court's FAA compulsion and injunction order remains reversed. The state case may proceed under its applicable law and properly preserved defenses. No further nonroutine action by this Court or new evidentiary proceeding is ordered.

# Egelhoff v. Egelhoff, No. 99-1529; 532 U.S. 141. — 2001-03-21

Source Record: Egelhoff_v_Egelhoff_merits_2001-03-21.md

## Event

Egelhoff v. Egelhoff, No. 99-1529. Argued November 8, 2000; decided March 21, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Washington Supreme Court, 139 Wash. 2d 557, 989 P.2d 80. The question is whether ERISA preempts Washington's automatic divorce-revocation rule as applied to the identified life-insurance and pension benefits. The Washington Supreme Court upheld reversal of summary judgments for the designated former spouse; she seeks reversal, while the children seek the benefits under state revocation and intestacy law.

David and Donna Egelhoff divorced in April 1994. He died intestate just over two months later without changing either beneficiary designation. Donna had received $46,000 in life-insurance proceeds before the children's recovery action; pension distribution is not established. The investment plan pays a surviving spouse, or children equally if there is no surviving spouse, when a designation is invalid. The insurance plan is silent about the consequence of invalidity. Neither term establishes that divorce invalidated this designation or that the relationship description itself made continued marriage a condition.

**The state provision's terms:** RCW 11.07.010 reaches specified nonprobate assets held when a Washington superior court enters a dissolution or invalidity decree. It revokes predecree death-transfer provisions favoring the former spouse and treats that spouse as having died when the decree was entered. The revocation does not apply to the extent the governing instrument expressly provides otherwise; the decree requires an asset payable at death for the former spouse or children of the marriage and no other nonprobate assets fulfill that requirement at death; or the decedent could not have revoked unilaterally immediately after the decree because of its terms or another reason.

Payors are protected for payment or other reliance before defined actual knowledge and face liability for action after actual knowledge of revocation. If there is a known dispute or uncertainty about entitlement, they may notify claimants and withhold payment until all interested claimants consent in writing or a competent court directs or authorizes payment. With knowledge of a dispute, a payor may require a satisfactory secured bond or similar indemnity; the bond is twice the asset's death-date fair market value or the adverse claim, whichever is less. These are permissions, not an unconditional duty to withhold.

Payor knowledge requires postdeath written notice identifying the asset with reasonable specificity and informing the payor of revocation or the dispute, received with a reasonable opportunity to act. Separate recipient protection covers purchasers for value without knowledge and recipients without knowledge in partial or full satisfaction of a legally enforceable obligation. A recipient outside that protection who is not entitled under the statute must return the asset or its value. Recipient knowledge is personal knowledge or possession of relevant documents, obtained after death and sufficiently before receipt or purchase to permit action. In each definition, possession or receipt for more than thirty days presumptively allows a reasonable opportunity; fewer than five business days presumptively does not. Only clear and convincing contrary evidence rebuts those presumptions.

Covered assets pass at death outside a will through the specified written insurance, employee-benefit, annuity, similar-contract or IRA provisions; payable-on-death, trust or survivorship bank accounts; qualifying grantor trusts effective or irrevocable only at death; and authorized transfer-on-death or pay-on-death securities designations. The section applies as of July 25, 1993 to decrees after July 24, 1993, and as of the enactment's effective date to earlier decrees. Section 2 of the 1994 enactment took effect January 1, 1995. No applicable exception, notice event or separate temporal dispute is found here.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

Reverse and remand the rejection of preemption for both identified benefit demands, 7–2.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas | Entire specific substitution rule, both benefit applications and reversal/remand |

## Holdings

### Automatic substitution of the beneficiary is preempted for both benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** ERISA section 514 displaces this state divorce-revocation rule to the extent it automatically substitutes the recipient of covered benefits for the plan's operative beneficiary designation. That rule defeats both identified revocation-based claims, including recovery of the already paid insurance from its recipient; the payment does not create a legally independent source of title, and pension anti-alienation is not the ground for the insurance holding.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Seven Justices directly adopt the rule, its application to both benefit demands, its limits and the confined reversal/remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that choosing the beneficiary is a central act of plan administration. Section 514, read with the plan-document duties, prevents the State from replacing the operative designation through this automatic divorce rule. Its application to nonplan property does not remove the instruction it gives when applied to covered benefits. An opt-out still requires affirmative plan drafting and attention to differing state rules to preserve the otherwise operative designation.

The children's strongest answer is that the statute supplies background validity law where the plans leave a gap. The investment plan's invalid-designation fallback makes that question real, but does not authorize every state rule declaring a designation invalid. The actual documents establish no continuing-marriage condition or adoption of this revocation rule. Insurance-plan silence likewise does not establish such adoption. The challenged rule changes entitlement because of a subsequent divorce, rather than identifying a demonstrated defect in execution, capacity or expression of the designation.

Travelers, Dillingham and De Buono distinguish ordinary costs and background effects from direct benefit or administrative commands. UNUM preserves that distinction even when an administrative reassignment uses ordinary-law terminology. The statute's defeasibility, notice protections and permission to withhold payment limit liability but do not eliminate its substitute-beneficiary operation. The result rests on that direct conflict, not disfavor toward family law or conjectured administrative inconvenience.

The insurance payment changes possession, not the legal source of the children's asserted entitlement. Their claim still depends on statutory revocation rather than an independent later promise or transaction. Boggs directs attention to that source, but its pension anti-alienation rule cannot be transferred to welfare insurance. Section 514 independently supplies the present insurance result. Unknown pension-payment status therefore does not prevent resolution of either legal claim.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The decision preempts these revocation-based demands, not every state rule governing capacity, execution, interpretation, marriage, inheritance or distributed money. Unlawful-killing disqualifications, simultaneous death, actual fraud, a valid domestic-relations order and independently arising postdistribution obligations lie outside the presented claim. No alternative beneficiary, actual redesignation, applicable exception, federal survivor entitlement, notice liability, benefit waiver or award is found. Ordinary implementation must follow the actual documents and governing federal protections, with properly preserved independent grounds retained.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Travelers; Dillingham; De Buono:** Their distinction between ordinary background or economic effects and direct benefit or administrative commands is applied, not displaced. General applicability is no automatic exemption, and family-law subject matter is no automatic ground for preemption.
- **UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward:** Its concrete administrative-connection rule is applied to beneficiary substitution. Ordinary interpretive principles are not categorically preempted, just as UNUM did not establish a universal federal agency code.
- **Boggs v. Boggs:** Its attention to the asserted source of benefit entitlement is applied; its pension and survivor holdings retain their actual scope. The present paid-insurance conclusion rests independently on section 514 and does not extend pension anti-alienation to welfare insurance.
- **Mackey v. Lanier Collection Agency & Service, Inc.:** The distinction between pension restrictions and welfare-benefit treatment remains effective. Ordinary recipient obligations are not categorically displaced merely because property once came from a plan.

## Law After Decision

Automatic divorce revocation cannot substitute the beneficiary of these covered pension or life-insurance benefits, including through a recipient recovery resting on that same statutory source. The decision adds this concrete application of section 514 without expanding remote-effects preemption or insulating all distributed assets. The actual plan documents, applicable federal protections and genuinely independent preserved grounds govern implementation; payment status does not change the source of the claims decided here.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, dissenting; Stevens joins the background-validity ground but not the separate recipient-only insurance ground.** These Justices would affirm rejection of preemption for both demands. The investment plan expressly contemplates invalid designations and supplies its own surviving-spouse-then-children fallback. The insurance plan supplies no contrary invalidity term. A defeasible state rule determining continued validity can therefore fill a documentary gap rather than contradict a settled plan instruction. Traditional family and property law does not automatically prevail, but Travelers and Dillingham require an actual federal conflict; uniform administration alone does not identify which background validity rules federal law excludes.

They distinguish Boggs's succession-based transfer of an existing interest in then-undistributed pension property from a rule determining whether the participant's own designation remains valid. The statute's opt-out, entitlement exceptions and payor protections matter to its operation. Neither the word wife nor the fallback proves automatic cancellation, incorporation of state law, or the children's entitlement. Their proposed affirmance would leave actual exceptions, validity and ordinary state-law application for the lower courts, without awarding benefits or adjudicating separate payor penalties.

**Breyer's additional insurance ground, not joined by Stevens:** The insurance has already been paid. A recovery confined to the recipient need not redirect future plan payments or impose a second payment on the plan, and welfare insurance does not carry pension anti-alienation protection. Breyer would also permit that confined recipient claim for the reasons of his Boggs separate position. He recognizes that the asserted right derives from divorce revocation rather than a newly discovered bargain; his disagreement is with treating that source as preemptive after payment. This alternative alone does not resolve the pension demand, whose distribution status is unknown.

## Procedure After Action

The Washington Supreme Court's nonpreemption judgment is reversed for both demands. The case returns to implement the plan-document and federal-protection rules with properly preserved independent grounds. No additional evidentiary proceeding, administrator review, second payment or particular monetary recovery is commanded.

Traditional family and property law does not automatically prevail, but Travelers and Dillingham require an actual federal conflict; uniform administration alone does not identify which background validity rules federal law excludes.

They distinguish Boggs's succession-based transfer of an existing interest in then-undistributed pension property from a rule determining whether the participant's own designation remains valid. The statute's opt-out, entitlement exceptions and payor protections matter to its operation. Neither the word wife nor the fallback proves automatic cancellation, incorporation of state law, or the children's entitlement. Their proposed affirmance would leave actual exceptions, validity and ordinary state-law application for the lower courts, without awarding benefits or adjudicating separate payor penalties.

**Breyer's additional insurance ground, not joined by Stevens:** The insurance has already been paid. A recovery confined to the recipient need not redirect future plan payments or impose a second payment on the plan, and welfare insurance does not carry pension anti-alienation protection. Breyer would also permit that confined recipient claim for the reasons of his Boggs separate position. He recognizes that the asserted right derives from divorce revocation rather than a newly discovered bargain; his disagreement is with treating that source as preemptive after payment. This alternative alone does not resolve the pension demand, whose distribution status is unknown.

## Event

Ferguson v. City of Charleston, No. 99-936. Argued October 4, 2000; decided March 21, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 186 F.3d 469. The question is whether the public hospital's nonconsensual drug-testing program, coordinated with police to obtain prosecution evidence, is justified by special needs. Ten patients challenge the appellate special-needs ruling; hospital, municipal and law-enforcement defendants seek affirmance.

The district court found a genuine maternal and infant health goal in deciding the later-abandoned discrimination claim, but separately rejected a special-needs justification for the coordinated police operation. The jury found for defendants under an instruction placing on them a preponderance burden to establish consent to collection, testing and possible police disclosure. ## Holdings

### The coordinated prosecution program lacks an adequate special-needs justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming legally insufficient consent, the public hospital's systematic collection and testing of patient urine under the arranged disclosure, evidence-preservation and arrest program is not justified by the special-needs exception. Genuine clinical work and a treatment objective do not themselves supply an independently adequate justification for this program's designed prosecution operation; the special-needs affirmance must be reversed, with the preserved consent issue and remaining claim-specific consequences returned for ordinary adjudication.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All seven directly adopt this confined rule, its application and the reversal/remand. No general immediate-versus-ultimate-purpose rule is adopted.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual operation must have an adequate justification for dispensing with ordinary Fourth Amendment protection. Chandler requires a substantial operational need, and Edmond prevents an ordinary narcotics-evidence program from obtaining an exception merely through additional legitimate functions. Beneficial ends alone cannot distinguish this evidence program from ordinary prosecution intended to prevent harm.

## Event

Gibbs v. Babbitt. Decided March 21, 2001, after full merits submission. On review of the Fourth Circuit, 214 F.3d 483, which affirmed summary judgment for federal officials on June 6, 2000. The question is whether Congress may apply the experimental red-wolf taking prohibition to intrastate, noncommercial taking on private property. Landowners Charles Gibbs and Richard Mann and Hyde and Washington Counties seek declaratory and injunctive relief against the challenged private-land applications; the officials seek preservation of the judgment.

The Service reintroduced captive-bred wolves onto federal refuges. ### Part I. The enacted taking class and the required connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The regulated class is the taking of protected wildlife without a commercial element. **Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires identification of the enacted class and its actual interstate mechanism before aggregate magnitude can establish power. Home Builders applies that requirement to wildlife taking; an owner's commercial project does not rewrite the class Congress regulated. Morrison preserves the requirement despite extensive evidence of downstream economic consequences.

The present rule prohibits taking experimental red wolves on private land without making commercial activity an element. # Texas v. Cobb, No. 99-1702; 532 U.S. 162. — 2001-04-02

## Event

Texas v. Cobb, No. 99-1702. Argued January 16, 2001; decided April 2, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals. The questions concern whether appointed counsel on a burglary charge protects questioning about murders inseparable from that occurrence and whether a custodial waiver permits the police-initiated interrogation after the attached counsel protection was asserted. Texas seeks reinstatement of its capital-murder judgment; Cobb seeks preservation of the new-trial judgment.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment setting aside the capital-murder conviction and remanding for a new trial.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirmed, 5–4. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The challenged police-elicited confession is excluded from the new murder trial under the attached Sixth Amendment protection. The State may retry Cobb using independent admissible evidence and pursue lawful investigation; this judgment is neither an acquittal nor a bar to prosecution.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg | Complete connected-episode rule, application of the existing waiver protection, and affirmance |
| Dissent | Scalia | O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas | Elements-based attachment rule; would reverse and remand without deciding the alternative waiver question |

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. All five directly adopt both holdings and their operative limits.

### 1. Representation protects interrogation about an inseparable part of the charged occurrence

**Holding and operative rule:** Once prosecution and representation begin, the Sixth Amendment counsel protection extends to questioning about an additional offense so integrated with the charged occurrence that the questioning necessarily reconstructs the same represented criminal episode. Different statutory elements do not alone remove that protection; shared participants, a similar method or a general criminal association, without that inseparable connection, do not establish it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the State cannot divide this represented occurrence into separate charging labels and then treat interrogation of the same event as outside counsel's protection. McNeil makes the right offense-specific and leaves genuinely unrelated investigations open. It does not settle how closely integrated additional charges should be classified. Moulton protects the accused against deliberate circumvention of counsel while another offense is investigated. Today's rule develops that protection for an additional prosecution inseparable from the represented episode; Moulton did not already decide this precise boundary.

Blockburger and Brown identify legally identical and greater or lesser included offenses for double jeopardy. Their elements inquiry serves the separate task of determining legislatively distinct crimes. It is insufficient as the exclusive boundary of assistance in defending an actual prosecution. The State's proposed rule would allow the order in which integrated charges are filed to separate the counsel safeguard from the investigation it protects. A prohibition on use only in the initially charged case would leave that practical circumvention intact.

Cobb had been indicted for the burglary and was represented by appointed counsel. The murders occurred during that burglary, and the confession reconstructed the same occurrence. The capital count charged multiple murders in one criminal transaction; it was not a burglary-felony-murder count. The statutory differences therefore are real, but do not answer the counsel question. Earlier requests for counsel's permission to interview Cobb confirm that the relationship was apparent; those permissions did not authorize the later interrogation. The intimate connection of this investigation supplies an objective boundary without making every represented person immune from questioning about separate crimes.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The rule does not extend to every factually associated crime, rest on an officer's unexpressed motive, or bar speaking to represented persons as witnesses. Ordinary attachment requirements remain. Unrelated crimes and a materially different investigative connection are outside this holding.

### 2. A custodial waiver does not replace the applicable attached-right waiver protection

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid Miranda waiver does not, by itself, authorize police-initiated deliberate elicitation about the protected episode after the accused has asserted the attached counsel right within Michigan v. Jackson. On the state court's established assertion and prejudice determinations, this confession must be excluded and its new-trial judgment stands.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda protects a suspect during custodial interrogation. The Sixth Amendment separately protects assistance after prosecution begins. McNeil preserves that distinction; satisfying one safeguard does not establish a waiver under the other. Jackson governs police-initiated questioning after assertion of the attached right. The state court treated Cobb's acceptance of appointed counsel as that assertion and rejected unilateral waiver in the later encounter. The State cannot evade that protection merely by calling the closely integrated murders a different offense.

Prior counsel permission related to earlier interviews, and no permission for this interrogation is established. Nor is this a defendant-initiated encounter. Applying the existing Jackson protection answers the State's alternative custodial-waiver contention without redefining every form of Sixth Amendment waiver. The state court found the confession's admission prejudicial. The Court does not replace that determination with a new factual assessment or presume that independent evidence cannot support a lawful retrial.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The Court does not hold that appointment universally invokes Fifth Amendment counsel protection, overrule Jackson, or decide a defendant-initiated encounter or a genuinely valid Sixth Amendment waiver. No independent coercion claim is adjudicated. Davis's requirement to clarify a reasonable possible present request remains effective in its own custodial setting; no such new request is found here.

## Precedent Treatment

- **McNeil v. Wisconsin:** Preserved for offense specificity and the distinction between Fifth and Sixth Amendment protections; its unresolved offense boundary now includes the inseparable represented occurrence described here, while unrelated investigations remain outside attachment.
- **Maine v. Moulton:** Extended from protection against circumvention in the charged prosecution to an additional prosecution inseparable from that represented occurrence. The original charged-case protection remains effective.
- **Brewer v. Williams:** Preserved for the protection against deliberate elicitation after attachment; it supplies no finding of a new waiver here.
- **Blockburger v. United States:** Retained as an elements test in its double-jeopardy setting, but rejected as the exclusive definition of counsel scope.
- **Brown v. Ohio:** Its greater and lesser included offense treatment remains intact; that legal identity does not exhaust the represented-episode protection.
- **Michigan v. Jackson:** Applied to the state court's established assertion and police-initiated encounter; a valid custodial waiver does not displace its independent protection.
- **Miranda v. Arizona:** Preserved as an independent custodial safeguard. The valid waiver here does not settle the attached Sixth Amendment question.
- **Davis v. United States:** Preserved, including mandatory clarification of a reasonable possible present request; no new request or clarification violation is found.
- **Dickerson v. United States:** Preserved for Miranda's constitutional status; it does not merge the distinct counsel protections or establish a Sixth Amendment waiver.

## Law After Decision

The offense-specific counsel right now includes interrogation necessarily reconstructing an inseparable part of the represented criminal occurrence, although the additional offense has different elements. The rule does not cover every factual association or unrelated offense. Existing attachment, assertion and waiver requirements remain distinct, and Miranda compliance alone does not displace Jackson. The decision supplies no general immunity from investigation and leaves double-jeopardy elements doctrine unchanged.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.** These Justices would reverse and remand because the Sixth Amendment right had not attached to the separately defined murders. McNeil makes the right offense-specific. Blockburger and Brown provide a legal boundary that reaches the same offense and its greater or lesser included counterpart without depending on the prosecutor's first charging label. The burglary and the actual multiple-murder capital count each require an element the other lacks.

They accept that Moulton forbids circumventing counsel to obtain evidence for the charged prosecution. But that protection does not, in their view, attach a right before proceedings begin on every inseparable additional offense. The Court's episode inquiry replaces a legal boundary with uncertain factual judgments and extends the safeguard beyond the pending prosecution. Independent Miranda protection and continued exclusion of unlawfully elicited charged-offense evidence remain meaningful.

Because no right attached to the murder charge on their ground, they would leave the alternative Jackson waiver issue undecided. # Wallis v. Spencer; Ninth Circuit No. 97-55579, 202 F.3d 1126; no Supreme Court docket supplied. — 2001-04-02

## Event

Wallis v. Spencer. Decided April 2, 2001, on merits review of the Ninth Circuit's amended decision, 202 F.3d 1126, No. 97-55579, reversing summary judgment for the City of Escondido. A Supreme Court docket number and oral-argument date are not reported. Review encompasses two questions: whether the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments permit removal from parental custody without judicial authorization absent specific, articulable evidence of imminent serious bodily harm and a response reasonably confined to averting it; and whether protective custody permits invasive examinations for sexual-abuse evidence without parental consent or prior judicial authorization when neither urgent medical need nor imminent evidence loss requires immediate examination.

Bill and Becky Wallis's children, Lauren, five, and Jessie, nearly three, were removed during September 21–22, 1991. An allegation originating with an aunt receiving psychiatric care concerned their father's planned killing of Jessie at the equinox. The referral disclosed serious reliability concerns, and there had been an earlier unfounded report. Officers asserted that they had been told a pickup order existed; none had been issued. CPS communications, the request to remove the children and the officers' knowledge are disputed. Evidence described a departmental practice of accepting reported orders without verification.

About three days later, invasive anal and vaginal examinations and photographs were performed for abuse evidence without parental consent, notice or judicial authorization reported in the record. The City's institutional connection and the independent policy theory's presentation are disputed. A later juvenile proceeding rejected the sacrifice explanation but relied on a physician's report to continue separation. Subsequent specialist review undermined that report, and the children returned after approximately two and one-half months. The City seeks restoration of summary judgment; the family seeks adjudication of its federal and related California claims. Only the City remains in this appeal.

## Holdings

### I. Emergency removal requires an immediate danger and a response confined to it

**Holding and operative rule:** Unilateral, nonconsensual removal of children from parental custody without prior judicial authorization requires specific, articulable information giving objectively reasonable grounds to believe that imminent serious bodily harm requires action before authorization can reasonably be obtained; the removal's scope and duration must be reasonably directed to that danger. Genuine disputes about the information, urgency and scope here prevent summary judgment for the City on the preserved removal claim, without establishing a constitutional violation or municipal liability.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II.A and III, and concurs only in the judgment on Parts II.B and IV. All nine directly adopt this removal holding and support the complete partial affirmance, partial vacatur and remand. The removal rule states necessary protections for the intervention presented, not an exhaustive test for every parental-right claim; no combination of separate rationales supplies its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The children's Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable seizure and the parents' Fourteenth Amendment family interests are distinct. Troxel protects parental direction against displacement by ordinary governmental preference but supplies no complete custody test. McArthur requires attention to actual need, execution, duration and available judicial authorization; its limited residential restraint does not itself authorize this greater bodily and familial intrusion.

The reported threat to kill Jessie at the equinox was grave. Transmission through a therapist and protective-services personnel could support prompt protective action without certainty of abuse. Neither the report's later disproof nor the aunt's psychiatric treatment alone defeats that possibility. But the disclosed reliability problems, earlier unfounded report, disputed therapist communications, investigation actually performed and children's observed condition cannot be resolved against the family on summary judgment. No judge issued a removal order. Whether CPS reported one or requested removal, and what the officers knew, remain disputed.

The allegation did not implicate Becky or threaten Lauren. Those differences and practical protective alternatives bear on the intrusion's scope; they do not establish that another placement was actually available. A date-specific threat cannot by itself justify indefinite separation. Permission to enter the home does not establish consent to removal or examinations.

Courts determine the governing law and may decide sufficiency on undisputed facts. The Court does not assign every reasonable-cause question to a jury, impose a fixed corroboration category, require exhaustion of every imaginable alternative, or determine credibility here. Nor does it establish a universal unfitness prerequisite, strict-scrutiny framework or equivalence of the two Amendments. The legal consequence of mistaken reliance on a reported order remains unresolved; the described appellate omission establishes no forfeiture at every stage.

**Precedent treatment:** Troxel's protected parental direction is applied at its controlling scope; its separate broader frameworks remain noncontrolling. McArthur's attention to actual need, scope and duration is applied without transferring its particular residential authorization to child removal.

### II.A. Municipal responsibility requires the City's own actionable policy, fault and causal connection

**Holding and operative rule:** A section 1983 municipal claim requires a federal deprivation caused by the City's own attributable policy or legally effective custom and the fault required by the theory actually presented; a training, supervision or safeguards omission requires deliberate indifference, supported notice and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing the injury. The testimony connecting an asserted settled removal practice to this intervention defeats the categorical conclusion that no policy evidence exists; the remaining elements are unproved.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices directly join this holding and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Collins separates a federal deprivation from municipal responsibility. Monell rejects employer-based federal liability. Carter applies Canton to deliberate municipal omissions: notice may arise from an established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances. Prior identical incidents are not invariably required. Negligence, employee error and hindsight that further training might have helped do not suffice.

The City correctly argues that an officer's mistake or outside misinformation alone cannot establish its responsibility. Here, however, a supervisor and the designated knowledgeable officer described a settled practice of accepting reported orders without verification. Evidence connects reliance on the supposed order to removal and the absence of further investigation. That permits the policy claim to proceed; it does not establish that the practice was unconstitutional, that every witness possessed final policymaking authority, or that the City caused every later injury.

An affirmative policy and an omission remain different theories. The latter requires proof of deliberate indifference, notice, the particular deficiency and actual causation; the duration of a practice alone proves none of those elements. Final authority depends on the legally effective allocation of power for the challenged function, not operational discretion alone. Interagency cooperation does not make county personnel or a physician City employees. No universal paper-order inspection requirement or prohibition of telephone communication follows.

Ordinary pleading and proof controls continue, without a special requirement to plead inaccessible internal operations in heightened detail.

**Precedent treatment:** Collins and Monell preserve separate deprivation and attribution requirements. Carter and Canton govern omission fault, notice and causation; McMillian governs function-specific final authority. Leatherman preserves ordinary municipal pleading.

### II.B. The City cannot borrow personal or state immunity against section 1983

**Holding and operative rule:** The City cannot defeat its own federal municipal liability by borrowing an individual's personal qualified or absolute immunity or by invoking state immunity against section 1983. Rejection of those categorical defenses is affirmed without deciding any absent individual's immunity or liability.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in this disposition without adding grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Carter separates municipal responsibility from the personal immunity an officer may possess. A defense protecting an individual's performance of a function does not exempt the City's own actionable policy from federal law. State immunity likewise does not displace the federal municipal claim.

The City is the only remaining defendant in this appeal. Its inability to borrow these defenses does not adjudicate an officer's immunity or restore a personal claim. Denius preserves particularized objective notice from the relevant law when the individual acted; an identical earlier case is unnecessary, but Ferguson in 2001 cannot supply September 1991 notice. The Court resolves only the City's categorical defenses, leaving the constitutional deprivation, institutional fault, causation and injury requirements intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Carter's separation of municipal responsibility and personal immunity is applied. Denius's conduct-date notice rule remains intact and supplies no retrospective notice or municipal immunity.

### III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible

**Holding and operative rule:** The independent City examination-policy adjudication and its associated constitutional rules are vacated insofar as they conclusively govern the remand; the lower court must first determine that theory's actual presentation and scope under ordinary procedural rules before adjudicating its independent merits. Examination injuries caused by a proved removal-policy violation do not invariably require a second examination policy, but the underlying wrong, actual causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must still be established separately for each claimed consequence.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this threshold and causation holding. No independent examination merits rule is decided. Stone-Zsela's concurrence states his own conditional bodily-integrity rule and receives no other join.

**Controlling explanation:** The examinations were substantial bodily intrusions. The district court addressed them, the report identified the City as requesting agency, and testimony concerned the detective and hospital contract. Both questions were granted. These circumstances support arguments about presentation and attribution; they do not resolve the specific objection that an independent City examination-policy claim was never properly presented. Review does not amend a complaint. Neither a lower dissent nor the absence of original filings here establishes forfeiture.

The proper course is to determine that threshold without conclusively imposing the lower court's independent examination, notice, opportunity-to-object or parental-presence rules. Even its presence rule permitted exclusion for a valid reason with nearby presence instead. Today's judgment finds neither preservation nor waiver and neither constitutional nor unconstitutional examinations.

Ferguson requires an adequate justification for the particular prosecution operation it addressed, while preserving genuinely different clinical functions and consent issues. It supplies no universal mixed-purpose prohibition or three-permission formula. Denius's public-employment information ruling supplies no bodily-examination protocol. The present record establishes no urgent medical need, imminent evidence loss, valid substitute consent or waiver; protective custody alone cannot establish those missing predicates. The Court nonetheless leaves the independent merits question for a properly presented claim. The 1998 California examination enactment does not authorize the 1991 acts.

The removal claim's possible consequential-injury route remains distinct, as the lower opinion's note 15 recognized. Failure of a separate examination-policy theory would not automatically extinguish that route; sequence alone does not prove it. Initial removal, prehearing examinations and posthearing separation require their own causal analysis. A later medical report and judicial intervention neither automatically sever nor establish City responsibility for the entire two-and-one-half-month separation. Ordinary proceedings supply this inquiry, not a mandated new evidentiary hearing or permission to manufacture a new claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Ferguson and Denius are preserved within their distinct operational and informational scopes; neither decides this unadjudicated independent claim. Monell and Carter continue to require an actual municipal causal connection even when a second policy is unnecessary to recover consequential injury.

### IV. The state claims return under their own liability and immunity rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The surviving California claims return within their existing scope for ordinary application of state liability and immunity law; federal restrictions on municipal respondeat-superior liability and borrowed personal immunity do not replace that separate state inquiry. Disputed CPS enlistment and the character of the challenged acts prevent disposition here under Government Code section 820.2, while the lower court's rejection of the reply-only section 821.6 theory is not reopened.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this limited state-law ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in the state-law remand without adding grounds; no independent constitutional examination holding is supplied through it.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 815.2(a)'s employee-based state liability and section 815.2(b)'s derivative immunity operate under their own terms. Section 820.2 protects an act or omission resulting from vested discretion even if that discretion is abused. Newton and Alicia T., as applied below, protect child-abuse investigation and public employees reasonably enlisted to assist, including the decision to investigate and actions necessary for a meaningful investigation. Protection does not extend to nondiscretionary acts or at least some intentional torts, including battery and false imprisonment under Newton. Whether CPS enlisted these officers, whether particular conduct involved discretion, and which pleaded tort claims fall outside protection remain unresolved. A claimed mandatory policy does not become discretionary merely because the setting involves child protection.

The lower court left the tort-specific limits for initial determination below. This Court neither extends that immunity nor decides battery, abduction, assault or intentional-infliction liability. Its rejection of the section 821.6 contention rested on reply-only presentation and the City's disclaimer of participation in the dependency prosecution; neither is reopened. Preserved state examination issues remain governed by their actual scope and state law, without importing the vacated independent federal examination rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Newton v. County of Napa and Alicia T. v. County of Los Angeles retain their stated state-law roles in the remand; neither becomes a new federal immunity rule. Monell's federal rejection of respondeat superior does not displace the distinct California statutory route.

# Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc., No. 00-152; 532 U.S. 189. — 2001-04-17

## Event

Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc., No. 00-152. Argued February 26, 2001; decided April 17, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which reinstated its judgment sustaining a declaration of unconstitutionality and an injunction against California's public-works withholding scheme. The question is whether ordinary judicial contract remedies can supply due process for this disputed payment without a separate administrative hearing.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses and remands for proceedings consistent with the adequate-contract-process holding.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The constitutional declaration and dependent injunction are set aside. No federal payment award follows; the contract dispute, wage allegations and any supported, preserved remedial barrier remain for their proper adjudication.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Complete adequate-contract-process holding, qualifications and remedy |

## Holdings

### Effective judicial contract proceedings supply the process required for this disputed debt

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State contests a public-contract payment, an actually available judicial contract proceeding capable of determining and enforcing the claimant's entitlement can supply due process without a duplicate administrative hearing. Assuming G & G has a protected contract interest, the supported ordinary action against its prime contractors defeats the categorical no-process challenge here; an inaccessible action, a conclusive unreviewable assessment or destruction of the claim would not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the rule, its explanation, limits and remedy; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an opportunity to obtain a judicial decision on this disputed contractual debt supplies the process due. G & G challenges state officials' direction to withhold payment for alleged prevailing-wage violations. American Manufacturers distinguishes an enforceable claim from an unconditional right to advance payment. Its separate private-carrier holding does not immunize the State's own command, and its protection of fair adjudication remains intact.

The relevant interest is payment allegedly owed for completed work, not money already possessed, an occupational license or an established benefit award. Good protects existing possessory control. Mathews requires process suited to the actual interest, while Logan prevents the State from destroying a protected claim through its own deficient machinery. Those principles do not require a second administrative forum where an ordinary court can decide and enforce this debt.

The statutory action by the contractor or its assignee, a person to whom it transfers the claim, against the awarding body is distinct from an ordinary contract action against the prime contractor. The first has a ninety-day period after completion and acceptance and its own exclusive-remedy provision. No assignment or timely filing is presumed. J & K Painting supports the separate ordinary route against the prime; lack of an assignment does not by itself establish that the subcontractor has no judicial remedy. California courts retain final authority to construe that state law.

G & G's strongest objection is that dependence on another party's assignment or a conclusive official assessment could make judicial relief illusory. Such a supported obstacle would matter. But no established bar forecloses the separate ordinary action on this record. Normal litigation delay does not alone destroy this debt claim; neither does the Court declare every cost or delay immaterial for all interests. The ruling addresses adequacy of process, not a requirement to exhaust state remedies before a federal civil-rights action under section 1983. Future administrative amendments supply no part of today's ground.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The Court assumes the asserted property interest and decides adequate process; it establishes no universal absence of contract property and no general private-state-action rule. Wage violations, penalties, the amount owed, any actual assignment, timely filing and ultimate recovery are not decided. An actually preserved, supported obstacle to all effective adjudication must be addressed if established. The procedure taking effect July 1, 2001 does not govern this judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- **American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan:** Applied to distinguish a protected adjudicable claim from unconditional advance payment. Its private-carrier attribution ruling does not resolve actual state-directed withholding; its limits preserving fair adjudication, prior awards and established continuation interests remain effective.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge:** Applied through attention to the disputed contractual interest and the actual judicial forum, without a categorical requirement of duplicate administrative procedure.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property:** Distinguished because seizure of existing possessory control differs from this disputed unpaid debt; its protections remain effective.
- **Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co.:** Preserved against destruction or effective foreclosure of a protected claim. A judicial action that cannot decide the entitlement would not provide the process on which this judgment rests.
- **J & K Painting Co. v. Bradshaw:** Its explanation of an ordinary subcontractor action supports availability independently of the statutory assignee route. It establishes neither an actual assignment nor successful recovery in this case, and California courts retain final authority over state-law construction.

## Law After Decision

Due process for this disputed public-contract debt can be supplied by meaningful ordinary judicial contract adjudication. Absence of a hearing in the withholding statute alone does not establish a constitutional violation. The remedy must actually permit determination and enforcement of entitlement; the ruling does not validate seizure of possessed property, termination of established awards, conclusive unreviewable wage assessments or foreclosure of all remedies. No general section 1983 exhaustion rule follows.

# United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co., No. 00-203; 532 U.S. 200. — 2001-04-17

Source Record: United_States_v_Cleveland_Indians_Baseball_merits_2001-04-17.md

## Event

United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co., No. 00-203. Argued February 27, 2001; decided April 17, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which sustained an employer tax refund based on allocation of back wages to earlier earning years. The question concerns both the rates and annual wage bases governing employer taxes under the Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) and Federal Unemployment Tax Act (FUTA) on wages actually paid in 1994 for salary losses in 1986 and 1987. The United States seeks reversal; the Company seeks to retain the refund.

The settlement paid awards corresponding to eight players' 1986 salary losses and fourteen players' 1987 losses. The non-interest awards are stipulated to be wages. All but one recipient had reached the relevant earlier wage maxima; the Company paid these recipients no other wages in 1994. The reported employer payments of $99,382 in FICA and $1,008 in FUTA taxes differ from the $97,202 refund judgment. No final account for the exceptional recipient is supplied by those aggregate figures.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the refund judgment and remands for application of the 1994 employer-tax rules.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The requested refund is rejected insofar as it rests on allocation to 1986 or 1987. Any computational implementation must apply the stated 1994 component rules without reopening the settled payment facts. No fixed-dollar award, new evidentiary hearing or individual benefit recalculation is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Both holdings, regulatory rationale, all rate and annual-base components, and remedy |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | None | Independent statutory payment-year ground; does not join the Court's regulatory rationale |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Scalia | None | Reasonable agency resolution of the damages-allocation gap; does not join either other rationale |

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion is joined throughout by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven Justices directly adopt both holdings and the common regulatory explanation. Stone-Zsela and Scalia agree with the judgment and resulting payment-year applications on the distinct grounds described below; neither joins the Court's complete rationale.

### 1. The established interpretation lawfully places these back wages in the payment-year tax account

**Holding and operative rule:** Treasury's fair and considered interpretation of the applicable rate and separate annual-base regulations requires these taxable salary awards to enter the employer's payment-year FICA and FUTA calculations. The interpretation fits the statutes and regulations and receives controlling weight under Auer; wage status alone does not settle timing, and the Court does not hold that the statutes unambiguously compel this treatment independently of the lawful administrative construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual, established interpretation governs this unresolved allocation question. The regulations distinguish remuneration paid or received in a calendar year from services performed in that year. The rate provisions select the payment-year percentage, while separate annual-base provisions identify the year's wage account. They do not expressly address backpay. Revenue Rulings 78-336 and 89-35 evidence a preexisting construction placing back wages in the payment year, rather than a position newly invented for this litigation.

Auer gives a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with that rule. Thomas Jefferson University requires the agency's actual ground, not a judicial substitute. The identified construction fits each payment and annual-base provision and remains within the statutes. Christensen prevents interpretation from amending clear regulatory text and distinguishes an advisory statutory view from an own-regulation construction; publication of a revenue ruling alone does not confer independent controlling statutory force.

Nierotko gives the Company's contrary argument real force. It classified backpay as wages and allocated it to earlier benefit periods despite paid-quarter language. But restoring a worker's service-based benefit account does not determine employer taxes under the later annual rate and base provisions. Common wage status does not require every statutory account to use identical timing. Payment-year taxation can disadvantage either the taxpayer or the Treasury. In Nierotko's benefit setting, inflexible payment-year attribution instead could only disadvantage the employee. That structural difference permits the differing allocation despite real tax anomalies; it does not eliminate them. The tax scheme permits the existing payment-based construction, while avoiding repeated reconstruction of prior payroll accounts supports its reasonableness. Administrative convenience neither creates authority nor overrides contrary text.

Gitlitz enforces an express statutory classification and sequence. It does not make this wage stipulation a concession about attribution or remove genuine interpretive room for a salary-damages substitute. The Company's preferred tax saving is not itself an enacted entitlement. The Court therefore sustains the actual regulatory construction without changing the governing deference rules or disturbing Nierotko's benefit holdings.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The applicable payment rule recognizes actual or constructive payment; no earlier constructive payment is established, and an earlier due date alone does not establish it. The separate nonqualified-deferred-compensation statutes use the later of performance of services and lapse of substantial forfeiture risk; no such plan is established here. The rule concerns these stipulated non-interest salary awards and does not decide the separate minor-cash deemed-payment category, other unpresented payment categories or individual benefit accounts. The independent binding force of revenue rulings as a general class is unnecessary and remains undecided.

### 2. Rates and annual bases must each be applied under their 1994 provisions

**Holding and operative rule:** These 1994 payments use each tax's 1994 statutory rate and its separate 1994 annual wage-base rule; an unchanged percentage or ceiling does not permit combining the payment with another year's wage account. The operative component applications are:

| Employer-tax component | Governing 1994 treatment | Distinct consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security rate, section 3111(a) | 6.2 percent | The 1986 and 1987 rate of 5.7 percent does not govern these payments. |
| Social Security annual base, section 3121(a)(1) | $60,600 | The earlier $42,000 and $43,800 bases do not carry forward with the salary losses. |
| Medicare rate, section 3111(b) | 1.45 percent | The same percentage in the earlier years does not resolve the base question. |
| Medicare wage base | No former wage ceiling | The separate Social Security ceiling does not limit this Medicare component. |
| FUTA rate, section 3301 | 6.2 percent | The earlier 6.0 percent rate does not govern. |
| FUTA annual base, section 3306(b)(1) | $7,000 per recipient in this employer's annual account | Its unchanged amount does not merge different years; the obsolete $3,000 printed regulatory figure cannot supersede the statute. |

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the rate and base rules independently. Sections 31.3111-3 and 31.3111-2(c) address FICA attachment and rate, while section 31.3121(a)(1)-1(a)(2) supplies the separate annual-base framework. Sections 31.3301-2 and 31.3301-3(b), together with section 31.3306(b)(1)-1(a)(2), perform the corresponding FUTA work. Their established backpay interpretation places the payments in 1994 for each inquiry. The governing statute controls the current dollar ceiling when an older regulatory print states an obsolete amount.

The earlier wage maxima explain why the allocation matters for all but one recipient. They do not establish the exceptional recipient's final liability, convert Medicare's unchanged percentage into an unchanged tax, or fix a net tax from the displayed statutory percentages alone. The lower court must implement these legal rules on the settled payment record, rather than award a refund on the rejected earlier-year theory.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The judgment fixes the applicable allocation, rates and bases, not an unsupported final dollar computation. Employee credits, benefits classification, any employee election and unpresented interest payments are outside this employer refund action. No speculative factual hearing is required merely to repeat the agreed payment dates.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Social Security Board v. Nierotko:** Preserved for wage classification and allocation to earlier benefit periods, including its consideration of paid-quarter language. Its benefit judgment does not compel the same allocation under the later employer-tax provisions, and its restriction of agencies to lawful statutory authority remains effective.
- **Auer v. Robbins:** Applied to the responsible agency's fair, considered and textually permissible construction of its own regulations; no new general deference rule is adopted.
- **Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala:** Applied to the agency's actual established ground, without supplying a fictional interpretation or overlooking the statutory boundary.
- **Christensen v. Harris County:** Preserved for separate treatment of advisory statutory views and regulatory interpretation, and for the prohibition on altering clear regulatory text by interpretation. A revenue ruling's publication alone does not settle its independent force.
- **Gitlitz v. Commissioner:** Its express classification and sequencing commands remain effective. A stipulation of wage status does not resolve this distinct temporal-attribution question or authorize a policy override of clear tax law.

## Law After Decision

The existing lawful Treasury construction governs payment-year employer taxation of these back wages under the applicable separate rate and annual-base provisions. The controlling rationale depends on a fair, established regulatory interpretation within statutory bounds, and does not adopt independent statutory compulsion as its sufficient ground. Benefit attribution remains governed by its own law. The judgment preserves actual-or-constructive-payment qualifications and does not establish an earlier payment, deferred-compensation plan or employee election.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment; no other Justice joins.** He would derive the payment-year rule independently from the tax provisions' payment and annual-receipt structure. The regulations and settled administration confirm that reading; they do not supply the rule in place of enacted text. Nierotko's benefits account serves a different statutory function, so its treatment of similar words does not require identical tax attribution. Administrative consistency permits predictable payroll calculation but cannot cure contrary text. He preserves Auer and Thomas Jefferson University without relying on deference to decide an ambiguity in this case. He agrees that the employer refund based on earlier-year allocation fails and that any remaining work is computational implementation. His independent statutory rationale is not the Court's controlling explanation.

**Scalia, concurring in the judgment; no other Justice joins.** He views temporal allocation of this damages substitute as a statutory gap. Neither treating the payments as wages nor observing that money changed hands in 1994 makes ordinary wage-payment language itself decide that gap. Nierotko's corresponding paid-period language makes direct textual compulsion particularly doubtful. He rejects the Court's explanation that different tax and benefit inequities themselves justify different meanings, but finds payment-year taxation a reasonable administrative resolution through the actual regulations. He therefore agrees with the operative payment-year applications and reversal without joining the Court's complete explanation or Stone-Zsela's direct statutory construction. His approach preserves the agency's lawful bounds and leaves individual benefits outside this case.

## Procedure After Action

The Sixth Circuit must return the employer refund action for implementation of the 1994 rates and annual bases and rejection of the contrary allocation premise. The agreed payment dates and wage characterization are not reopened. No individual benefit award, new election, speculative hearing or fixed tax amount is ordered.

# United States v. Weston; D.C. Circuit No. 99-3119; judgment below, 206 F.3d 9. — 2001-04-17

Source Record: United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md

## Event

United States v. Weston; D.C. Circuit No. 99-3119. Merits decision, April 17, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 206 F.3d 9, decided March 24, 2000. Submitted on full merits materials. The question is whether a court authorizing unwanted antipsychotic medication of an incompetent pretrial defendant must address likely Sixth Amendment trial effects before treatment when competency restoration is an actual governmental purpose. The Government seeks reinstatement of the authorization; Weston seeks to preserve its reversal.

Weston was indicted following the July 24, 1998 Capitol shootings and was found incompetent on April 22, 1999. After an initial administrative hearing lacked required notice, a second received the defense psychiatrist's report and counsel's suggestions, although counsel did not attend. The district court's September 9, 1999 order accepted a medical-and-safety justification and deferred trial effects. The medical evidence identifies possible danger in staff encounters but also acknowledges that present isolation and continuous observation controlled immediate danger. The court of appeals set the authorization aside and remanded.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the appellate judgment setting aside the medication authorization and remands for proceedings under the stated liberty and prospective trial-effects requirements.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The existing medication authorization remains set aside. A renewed order requires supported medical appropriateness, overriding need and consideration of reasonable less intrusive means. At the scope of Part II, the judge must also assess likely defense effects before treatment and reassess actual effects before any trial. No release, dismissal, competency finding, guilt determination, fixed regimen or prohibition of voluntary treatment follows.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirms and remands | Part I: liberty justification; all nine |
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Additional ground for affirmance | Part II: bounded advance trial-effects inquiry; seven |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins both Court parts | Broader timing protection for every restoration-purpose order, including mixed purposes |
| Concurrence | Kennedy | No other Justice | Joins both Court parts | Stronger proposed ultimate restoration showing |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Thomas | Scalia | Joins Part I and judgment, not Part II | Direct liberty ground without the added advance trial-effects requirement |

## Holdings

### I. The present safety justification does not support unwanted medication

**Holding and operative rule:** When a pretrial defendant objects to antipsychotic medication, the Government must establish medical appropriateness and an overriding need after considering reasonable less intrusive means. A safety label does not satisfy that duty where the evidence establishes present containment of immediate danger and the order does not adequately address that alternative; the present authorization therefore cannot stand.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I is directly joined by all nine Justices. It independently supports affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Riggins v. Nevada requires a supported justification for overriding bodily refusal, rather than medical benefit alone. Washington v. Harper recognizes a substantial refusal interest while permitting justified safety treatment; it does not make diagnosis or potential dangerousness conclusive. Here the treating psychiatrist acknowledged that isolation and continuous observation controlled immediate danger. The risk during staff contact and the medical and practical costs of prolonged isolation are substantial competing considerations, but the order must assess them against the actual alternatives. The Court does not find indefinite isolation harmless, permanently preferable or constitutionally required. Nor does prosecutorial good faith supply the missing safety justification when restoration is an acknowledged purpose. The second administrative hearing received defense material; the defect of the first hearing does not establish that the second lacked every opportunity for input. A future order supported by materially changed circumstances or an adequate independent safety showing remains possible. This order lacks the required justification.

### II. The restoration purpose requires a preliminary assessment of likely defense effects on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** When competency restoration is an actual purpose of proposed unwanted medication and the record supplies no independently sufficient safety justification, the judge must assess the proposed regimen's reasonably foreseeable effects on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and presentation of the defense before authorizing it. The Government must support that preliminary assessment through available clinical evidence, reasonable alternatives and workable safeguards; perfect prediction is unnecessary, but a promise to examine effects after treatment does not suffice, and actual effects require renewed review before trial.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II is directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas do not join it. Seven direct joins establish the stated rule without a Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** Riggins recognizes that medication can impair the substance of consultation and testimony in ways a completed trial record cannot reconstruct. That protection would be ineffective if all defense effects could be postponed until after the intervention intended to permit prosecution. The Government correctly observes that exact effects cannot always be known beforehand. The required inquiry therefore considers the proposed treatment, clinical observations and supported inferences, not a guarantee against every side effect. An insanity defense does not invariably entitle a defendant to display untreated symptoms. Recordings and psychiatric explanation may preserve evidence of earlier symptoms, and dose changes may help; their adequacy must be assessed, not presumed. They cannot necessarily restore impaired communication or preparation. Harper's safety setting does not answer the deficient mixed-purpose order before the Court. The judge remains responsible for the legal trial-rights determination. This necessary preliminary inquiry is not a comprehensive sufficient standard for ultimate restoration authorization.

**Limits and work on remand:** The clinical findings for any renewed regimen remain factual work under these rules. Whether prosecution alone can ultimately justify involuntary treatment is outside the granted timing question. Immediate emergency treatment, treatment solely for execution, capital medical ethics, guilt, discharge and dismissal are not decided. No majority rule is adopted here for a materially different order resting on an independently sufficient safety basis; Stone-Zsela's broader position and Kennedy's stronger ultimate standard remain separate.

## Precedent Treatment

- Riggins v. Nevada is applied to the present refusal-interest defect and extended, at the seven-Justice scope, to the timing of a preliminary defense-effects inquiry before restoration treatment. Its complete ultimate-restoration question remains open.
- Washington v. Harper retains its medically justified confinement-safety rule; its setting and procedure do not automatically answer pretrial restoration and defense protection. No categorical safety-treatment prohibition is adopted.

## Law After Decision

An objecting pretrial defendant retains the Riggins liberty protection. For a restoration-purpose order without an independently sufficient safety justification, the judge must also assess likely defense effects before treatment, using supported clinical prediction and safeguards and preserving later reassessment. The two inquiries are distinct. These requirements govern renewed authorization, not merely review after a conviction. They neither establish a right to remain incompetent nor decide a complete substantive restoration standard.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court and would require the advance defense inquiry whenever restoration is an actual purpose, even if an additional safety rationale is independently supported. Bodily justification cannot extinguish the distinct protection of consultation, preparation and trial participation. Riggins supplies the practical reason for addressing those effects before the intrusion; Harper's convicted-prisoner safety setting does not dispense with that inquiry. He requires the Government to support the proposed regimen, alternatives and safeguards as compatible with an effective defense, without perfect prediction, and requires later review of actual effects. The proposed rule does not decide prosecution-only ultimate authority, genuine emergencies or treatment solely for execution.

Justice Kennedy joins the Court's necessary minimum and preserves his Riggins position that ultimate restoration authorization requires an extraordinary showing against significant impairment of the accused's reactions, participation or assistance to counsel. Omission of the preliminary inquiry necessarily fails that position; performing it is not by itself enough. His stronger standard is not the Court's rule.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, joins Part I and concurs in the judgment. The established involuntary treatment and preserved direct liberty objection distinguish this prospective case from their objections to reversing a completed conviction on speculative trial prejudice in Riggins. The deficient present justification warrants relief without adopting Part II's advance Sixth Amendment requirement. A properly supported independent safety authorization may leave trial protection for its appropriate later proceeding; they do not authorize an incompetent or unfair trial.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court merits review is complete. The case returns through the court of appeals for supported findings before any renewed authorization. The present order supplies no authority to medicate. Any later lawful treatment and trial require the distinct continuing protections stated above; no trial date or later treatment outcome is fixed.
