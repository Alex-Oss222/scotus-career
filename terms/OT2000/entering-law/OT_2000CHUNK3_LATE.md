#### United States Department of Justice v. Landano, 508 U.S. 165 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2054.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Confidentiality requires an assurance or a supported inference, not an FBI label

**Holding:** Under Exemption 7(D), a source is confidential when the information was furnished under an express assurance or circumstances supporting a reasonable inference of confidentiality; an assurance of absolute secrecy is unnecessary. The Government bears the burden and cannot presume every FBI criminal-investigation source confidential, but may rely on a supported, rebuttable inference tied to particular circumstances or a properly defined category, including the crime and the source’s relationship to it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, 489 U.S. 749: distinguished; privacy categories do not establish every source’s confidentiality.
- John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S. 146: compilation analysis preserved; it does not eliminate the confidentiality requirement.
- Department of State v. Ray, December 16, 1991: distinct Exemption 6 privacy holding preserved.

##### Source identity and criminal-source information receive distinct statutory protection

**Holding:** Exemption 7(D) protects identity when law-enforcement disclosure could reasonably be expected to reveal a confidential source, including an agency, authority or private institution furnishing information confidentially; it also protects information furnished by a confidential source in a criminal investigation conducted by a criminal-law-enforcement authority. The statute separately covers a lawful national-security intelligence investigation; reasonably segregable nonexempt material remains subject to release, but redacting identity alone does not remove protection for covered source information.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public-testimony waiver and other exemptions remain unresolved. In-camera affidavits and protective procedures may be used when justified, but are not universally required. The separate national-security clause is not construed beyond its stated statutory coverage.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for confidentiality determinations with the Government retaining its burden and the requester able to rebut supported inferences using nonprotected evidence. Release reasonably segregable nonexempt material, not protected source information merely because names are redacted; no blanket disclosure or criminal relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S. 146: statutory compilation requirement applied.
- Department of Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352: nonexempt segregable disclosure preserved, without overriding protected source information.

#### Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1223.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration

**Holding:** Section 7114(b)(4)'s duty to supply qualifying bargaining data operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law; when the asserted exception to the Privacy Act is §552a(b)(2), the question is whether FOIA itself requires disclosure. Retained Reporters Committee precedent governs the ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry, so collective-bargaining utility alone does not enlarge the incorporated FOIA balance; the Court does not decide that this precedent's restriction was inevitably compelled by FOIA's original text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labor duty concerns requested data normally maintained by the agency in its regular course of business, reasonably available and necessary for full and proper discussion, understanding and negotiation of subjects within collective bargaining, and not guidance, advice, counsel or training provided for management officials or supervisors relating to collective bargaining. These conditions are not independently disputed or newly adjudicated here. For a record contained in a system of records, §552a(b) bars agency disclosure absent a written request by, or prior written consent of, the individual to whom it pertains, or an applicable enumerated exception. A union request is not the subject individual's request or consent. Routine use under §552a(b)(3) and other exceptions are distinct and undecided; their applicability is not guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: retained and applied as statutory precedent for ordinary third-party public-interest analysis and requester neutrality; its original-text justification is not newly endorsed as inevitable, and requester identity can still matter when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground and the requester is the person protected by that privilege, a situation absent here.
- Department of the Treasury, IRS v. FLRA: applied for respecting enacted limits on labor-agency authority; the uncontested bargaining-necessity determination is not reconsidered.
- Department of State v. Ray: preserved; the incorporated Exemption 6 inquiry remains a concrete balance, not a rule that another statute's useful purpose automatically establishes FOIA-required disclosure.

##### The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record

**Holding:** On these requests, official disclosure of employee home addresses would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy under Exemption 6 because the real interest in controlling dissemination of home locations outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated addition to public understanding after names and work stations were supplied. Exemption 6 permits withholding and FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the resulting prohibition against compelling release through the invoked §552a(b)(2) route, without deciding a different demonstrated transparency use, routine use, subject consent or another exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exemption 6 applies to personnel, medical and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy; the requested employee-address records fall within that category. Exemption 7(C) instead concerns information compiled for law-enforcement purposes and disclosures that could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; that different standard is not used here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of State v. Ray: applied for concrete privacy injury and demonstrated incremental public understanding; its confidentiality assurance is not a prerequisite or automatic exemption, and no general bar to derivative benefits or prior-wrongdoing requirement is added.
- Department of the Air Force v. Rose: applied; disclosure remains the general rule, with the enacted Exemption 6 privacy balance preserved.
- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: applied to the relevant public interest and continued control over officially disseminated personal information despite scattered public availability; its rap-sheet sensitivity and Exemption 7(C) threshold do not replace the different record or Exemption 6 standard here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Enforcement of both the Gulfport and Lowry disclosure orders is reversed on the asserted §552a(b)(2) ground. No new necessity determination, constitutional privacy remedy or approval of a routine use is ordered; other disclosure routes must meet their own requirements.

#### Weiss v. United States, 510 U.S. 163

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1482, including Hernandez under Supreme Court Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

For the other questions in Weiss v. United States, 1994-01-19, No. 92-1482, including Hernandez under Supreme Court Rule 12.2, see Criminal Procedure — Fixed tenure and impartial military adjudication.

##### Commissioned officers assigned germane military judicial duties

**Holding:** The Appointments Clause applies to military judges, but the already commissioned officers serving as trial and appellate judges in these cases require no second appointment: their judicial duties are germane to the military offices conferred by presidential appointment and Senate confirmation. Assignment by a Judge Advocate General does not itself confer a new, unrelated federal office; the holding does not authorize that result or decide civilian appellate appointments or assignment to principal offices.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices support the confined existing-commission and germane-duty proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Buckley v. Valeo: applied to the officer character of the military judges' duties; military office is not exempt from the Appointments Clause.
- Shoemaker v. United States: applied to germane additional duties within an existing commissioned office; no general authority to fill unrelated offices is recognized.
- Freytag v. Commissioner: its insistence on constitutional appointment of officers is preserved; the present judges already hold constitutionally sufficient commissions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civilian appellate appointments, unrelated new offices and assignment to principal offices are not decided. The tenure holding depends on this system's protections, including limits on command evaluation, improper censure and influence, judicial review and disqualification; supported retaliation, reassignment and structural-bias challenges remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Court of Military Appeals judgments, including Hernandez under Rule 12.2, are affirmed on the appointment and fixed-tenure objections. No second appointment, fixed term or retrial is ordered.

#### Ryder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-431.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Ryder v. United States, decided June 12, 1995, No. 94-431. see Criminal Procedure — Military judicial appointments and impartiality (The higher court did not supply the missing Article 66 review).

##### A timely direct appointment challenger receives lawful adjudication

**Holding:** A litigant who timely challenges a substantial constitutional defect in the appointments of officers adjudicating his own case and pursues that objection on direct review is entitled to a fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed officers; the de facto officer doctrine cannot validate the defective panel’s judgment against him. Ryder must receive new Article 66 review by a properly appointed military appellate panel, without automatic dismissal or reversal of his trial conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this bounded rule and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns a timely direct challenge to substantial constitutional appointment defects. It does not prescribe collateral reopening, invalidate ministerial acts, decide defaults in an available challenge channel, or settle every officer-and-department classification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley v. Valeo is distinguished as a universal de facto remedial defense. Glidden Co. v. Zdanok and Freytag are applied to substantial constitutional adjudicator qualifications. Ball v. United States, McDowell v. United States and Ex parte Ward are distinguished by collateral or technical-assignment settings. The completed Weiss decision retains its commissioned-officer/germane-assignment scope and leaves civilian appointments open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for fresh Article 66 review by a lawfully appointed military appellate panel. No acquittal, dismissal, trial-conviction reversal or decision of the underlying drug, evidence or sentence merits is ordered.

#### Edmond v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-262.
**Decided:** 1997-05-19.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable are included under Rule 12.4; all six judgments receive the same disposition.

##### Congress authorized these appointments by the Secretary

**Holding:** Section 323(a), read with Article 66's provision for civilian military appellate judges, authorizes the Secretary of Transportation as department head to appoint the civilian officers serving on the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals. Article 66 assignment is distinct from appointment, but no additional statute repeating the office's name in an appointment clause is required for these existing statutory offices.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Secretary's formal appointment, not the General Counsel's prior assignment, supplies the lawful act. No power to create unrelated offices, appoint principal officers or use every general personnel statute is recognized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Weiss's commissioned-officer/germane-assignment rule left civilians open; Article 66 and §323(a) now supply the separate appointment basis. Freytag preserves proper appointment; Ryder does not cure an earlier defect through continued service.

##### The combined statutory hierarchy establishes inferior status here

**Holding:** These civilian military appellate judges are inferior officers because the combined structure subjects their assignments and administration to the Judge Advocate General, permits removal from judicial assignments and prescription of rules, and places their decisions under superior review by presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed judges; they lack final authority free of that system of direction and review. Their significant duties and protected adjudicative independence do not make them principal officers, and neither appellate review nor removal alone is held sufficient for every office.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Souter concurs on a distinct fuller classification rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article 67 review is required for affirmed death sentences and cases the Judge Advocate General sends, and otherwise requires the accused's petition and good cause. Under Article 67(c), it may act only on the findings and sentence approved by the convening authority and affirmed or set aside as incorrect in law by the Court of Criminal Appeals, and takes action only on matters of law. In a Judge Advocate General referral, it need take action only on the issues he raises; on the accused's petition, it need take action only on issues specified in the grant. Those minimum issue obligations do not convert legal review into guaranteed plenary factual rehearing. Neither review nor removal alone is held sufficient for every office; removal does not authorize unlawful decisional interference. Article III status and different offices with unreviewable final authority remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the military appellate judgments for Edmond, Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable, 9–0 each. No defect requiring fresh Article 66 review is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley preserves officer status for significant adjudicative duties. Morrison and Freytag require the actual office inquiry without a universal single-factor rule. Ryder's fresh Article 66 fact-and-sentence adjudication remedy remains for qualifying timely substantial defects; Article 67 review is no substitute, but the defect premise is absent here.

#### Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9059.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

##### Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue in a capital sentencing proceeding and the only legally available noncapital sentence is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity for the defendant to inform the jury of that ineligibility through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. A direction to disregard parole and give life imprisonment its ordinary meaning does not satisfy that requirement on this record; the rule neither requires a particular script nor promises immunity from clemency or future legislation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The operative alternative was life without ordinary parole under §24-21-640's second-or-subsequent violent-conviction rule following separate sentencing for the prior conviction; offenses within one day or one continuous course count as one. The eligibility premise is uncontested here, not a rule that every life sentence excludes parole. The State may still prove danger to inmates or guards and offer truthful information about commutation or pardon.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977): its opportunity-to-answer principle supplies the due-process foundation for responding to material sentencing information.
- Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986): its future-danger rebuttal principle is applied to a fixed legal sentencing alternative; its independent mitigation rule remains intact.
- California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983): distinguished; permission to give truthful commutation information does not authorize withholding this responsive fact about ordinary parole.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their effective-mitigation rules remain controlling within their scope; neither is treated as having already decided this separate legal-information question.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O'Dell v. Netherland, June 19, 1997, No. 96-6867: the confined claim is available against Teague under separate majority old-rule and conditional watershed holdings; eligibility, effective opportunity, error consequence and genuine habeas conditions remain.

##### The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued

**Holding:** Under Chapman's direct-review standard, the State cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt that barring the parole-ineligibility explanation did not contribute to this jury's selection of death, given the State's future-danger argument, the complete exclusion of the legal answer and the jury's express parole inquiry. The death-sustaining judgment is reversed and the case remanded for a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; a lawful noncapital disposition remains available, and the convictions and separate sentences are undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no independently sufficient Eighth Amendment instruction rule. It does not determine an entitlement to parole information where the State has not raised future danger, settle a disputed parole-eligibility question, require a fixed form of words, guarantee against clemency or future legislation, or announce automatic reversal for every omission. The State may pursue a lawful noncapital disposition instead of another capital hearing. No guilt retrial, acquittal, release, invalidation of the separate sentences or general prohibition of capital punishment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied; the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, and fails on this record.
- Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994: distinguished as an affirmative disclosure of another jury's death decision; this decision neither supplies a missing majority for its further-remedy positions nor changes that case's mandate.
- Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: preserves the distinction between correcting a capital sentencing defect and disturbing guilt; no automatic noncapital sentence follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sustaining judgment is reversed, 7–2, and the case remanded. Five Justices require a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; the State may instead pursue a lawful noncapital disposition. The existing death sentence cannot be retained by a further harmlessness finding on this omission. Guilt and the separate life and thirty-day sentences remain undisturbed.

#### Mitchell v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-7541.
**Decided:** 1999-04-05.

##### The plea did not waive privilege concerning reserved quantity

**Holding:** A guilty plea does not itself waive the privilege against compelled self-incrimination concerning disputed sentencing facts while testimony creates a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment. This accepted plea, its limited admissions and its trial-silence warning did not waive protection concerning expressly reserved quantity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle's sentencing protection and Brown's scope-based testimonial-waiver rule are applied without making every plea statement inadmissible or establishing a permanent privilege.

##### Protected silence cannot prove the crime's circumstances at sentencing

**Holding:** A sentencing court may not draw an adverse factual inference from a defendant's protected silence in determining the circumstances and details of the crime. The Government must establish disputed quantity through lawful affirmative evidence; permission for judicial sentencing findings does not authorize this source of proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six Justices).

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Griffin's no-inference protection is applied to proof of crime facts at criminal sentencing; Estelle supplies continuing protection. Baxter and Woodard are distinguished by their civil settings. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan retain their stated lawful operations.

##### Other potentially sufficient evidence does not establish harmlessness here

**Holding:** The Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that this constitutional error did not contribute to the affected sentencing determination. Because the judge expressly relied on silence in crediting disputed quantity testimony, the finding cannot be preserved merely because the remaining evidence could support it; the sentence must be vacated and quantity reconsidered without that inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six Justices).

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman supplies the contribution-based constitutional harmlessness standard. Wright's existing lawful-basis requirement is preserved and applied at its actual sentencing scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The privilege exists while testimony presents a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment; no permanent privilege or ban on lawful voluntary-statement use follows. No quantity-element classification, universal jury rule, mandatory-minimum rule or entitlement to acceptance credit is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the sentence and remand for quantity determination from lawful affirmative evidence without the adverse silence inference. No fixed term, automatic minimum, plea withdrawal, acquittal or jury hearing is ordered; consequential components follow their own governing law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Estelle and Brown govern continuing privilege and actual waiver. Griffin now forbids adverse use of protected silence to determine crime facts at sentencing; its established trial operation remains. Baxter and Woodard do not authorize the present criminal inference. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan preserve lawful sentencing findings and consequences without removing independent constitutional safeguards. Chapman governs harmlessness; Wright retains its separate sentencing-basis requirement. Jones and Monge supply no quantity classification or universal mandatory-minimum rule here. No precedent is overruled.

#### Ramdass v. Angelone, 530 U.S. 156 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-7000.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### II. Absence of entry does not conclusively defeat the truthful-response claim

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue, the absence of formal entry of judgment on a separate guilty verdict does not by itself defeat a claim that due process required a truthful account of that verdict's conditional parole consequence. A case in which substantive adjudication was complete and only entry remained may require that account; mere expectation of conviction, confidence in guilt or a favorable release prediction does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record does not establish that substantive adjudication was complete. Rule 3A:15 preserved judicial power to set aside the verdict for trial error or insufficient evidence; that power proves neither an actual pending defect nor that every substantive question had ended. Later entry proves neither proposition retrospectively. A verdict facing a genuine unresolved merits issue is outside this rule. Any required account must state the conditional consequence of entry; it may not falsely state that Ramdass was already ineligible during the jury's deliberations. The Court decides no separate arbitrary-manipulation claim or separate claim concerning the April 2 judicial sentencing.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment does not reinstate the District Court's sentencing order or vacate the sentence. It orders no present writ, new penalty proceeding, evidentiary hearing or other evidentiary command, deadline, release, stay or execution-related relief. The conviction and unrelated claims remain untouched. Denial on a properly supported independent ground remains available on remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Simmons v. South Carolina:** Applies its effective-opportunity protection and clarifies that entry alone does not conclusively foreclose a truthful account of a completed adjudication's conditional consequence. Actual entitlement and effect remain unresolved; no false statement of present ineligibility is authorized.
- **Gardner v. Florida:** Applies the right to explain material capital-sentencing information; it supplies the opportunity-to-answer foundation, not a present writ.
- **Skipper v. South Carolina:** Applies the protection for rebutting a State future-danger case; its distinct mitigation holding remains intact.
- **California v. Ramos:** Preserves truthful release-related information and distinguishes it from a misleading account of the lawful alternative; no guarantee against clemency or future legislation is required.
- **Allridge v. Scott:** Preserves the distinction between fixed legal consequences and release predictions. It does not settle whether the Domino's substantive adjudication was complete and does not supply a constitutional holding dated before November 1994.

For state-law classification, the independent federal habeas gateways, and the bounded remand, see Ramdass v. Angelone, No. 99-7000, June 12, 2000, under Federal Courts, federal habeas review of capital parole information.

#### United States v. Hubbell, 530 U.S. 27 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-166.
**Decided:** June 5, 2000.

##### Compelled testimonial selection cannot become an immunized witness's investigative roadmap

**Holding:** When a broad subpoena compels an immunized individual to use personal knowledge to identify, locate and assemble previously unidentified incriminating documents, and that production communicates information beyond a foregone conclusion, sections 6002–6003 bar direct criminal use of the compelled testimony and evidence derived from it. The Government bears the affirmative burden of proving legitimate sources wholly independent of that testimony; declining to introduce either the production act or the papers at trial does not satisfy that burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the full rule, its exceptions and the dismissal consequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This protection is not immunity for every transaction described in a paper. Genuine foregone conclusions, third-party production, collective-entity doctrine and genuinely independent evidence retain their distinct operation; Balsys leaves domestic immunity intact. Section 6002 also retains its express exception for prosecution for perjury, giving a false statement or otherwise failing to comply with the order. None supplies the prosecution here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general privilege for voluntary contents, transaction-wide immunity, new source hearing, disposition of other defendants or revival of the Independent Counsel authority issue follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mandate requires implementation of dismissal of this indictment under the immunity ruling and the conditional agreement. No new source inquiry is ordered. Other defendants' charges and the excluded Independent Counsel authority ruling remain outside the judgment; independent evidence retains its ordinary legal availability without any finding that such evidence exists here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Fisher v. United States:** Applies the act/contents distinction and foregone-conclusion boundary; voluntary preparation does not excuse derivative use of compelled testimony.
- **United States v. Doe (1984):** Applies protection for an incriminating individual act of production despite unprivileged contents.
- **Kastigar v. United States:** Applies direct and derivative immunity and the Government's affirmative wholly independent-source burden.
- **Doe v. United States (1988):** Distinguishes a noninformative hypothetical consent act from disclosure of the individual's knowledge.
- **Braswell v. United States:** Preserves collective-entity doctrine and its treatment of the individual production act; it does not resolve this immunized personal subpoena by collapsing act into contents.
- **United States v. Balsys:** Preserves domestic use-and-derivative-use immunity; its independent-foreign-risk rule does not narrow the protection here.

## Standards and Tests selection: Timely direct challenges to adjudicators

### Timely direct challenges to adjudicators’ appointments

**Governing question:** Relief for a litigant’s timely substantial constitutional appointment objection on direct review.

**Current rule:** A litigant who timely challenges a substantial constitutional defect in the appointment of officers adjudicating his own case and pursues it on direct review is entitled to fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed officers. The de facto officer doctrine cannot validate the defective panel’s judgment against him. A higher review that lacks the displaced proceeding’s fact weighing, credibility, contested-fact and sentence-review functions does not replace those lost opportunities.

## Standards and Tests selection: Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

### Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

**Governing question:** When a capital defendant must be allowed to explain the only lawful alternative to death.

**Current rule:** When the State puts future dangerousness in issue and the only lawful alternative to death is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. No single verbal formula is required. Speculative clemency does not make ordinary parole available. On direct review, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that denying the explanation did not contribute to the jury’s selection of death; where it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it continues to seek death. The error is not classified as structural.

For federal collateral review, Gardner and Skipper dictated this confined effective-opportunity protection before October 3, 1988; Teague does not bar that claim as new. Independently, even if classified as new, it falls within the watershed exception when future dangerousness is an operative basis for death, the sole lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and no accurate defense presentation or adequate instruction affords an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise. Neither retroactivity ground establishes the predicates of a violation, its error consequence or other genuinely applicable habeas conditions. Prison dangerousness remains a permissible State argument, without an automatic exception to the information protection.

When future dangerousness is at issue, the lack of formal entry on a separate guilty verdict does not conclusively defeat a claim to an accurate explanation of its conditional parole consequence if substantive adjudication is complete and only entry remains. A genuinely unresolved merits issue, anticipated conviction or prediction of release is different. Any required account must state the condition of entry truthfully rather than claim existing ineligibility. State law continues to define qualifying conviction; disagreement with that legal definition is neither an unreasonable factual determination nor by itself a federal custody violation. On habeas review the completed-adjudication predicate, actual informational opportunity, section 2254(d)(1) eligibility at the state-decision cutoff, any material Teague issue and the applicable error-effect inquiry remain distinct; none follows automatically from removal of a categorical bar.

## Standards and Tests selection: Immunized testimonial document selection and derivative use

### Immunized testimonial document selection and derivative use

**Governing question:** When identifying and assembling documents under an immunity order supplies prohibited investigative leads.

**Current rule:** When an immunized individual must use personal knowledge to identify, locate and assemble previously unidentified incriminating documents in response to a broad subpoena, and production communicates more than a foregone conclusion, sections 6002–6003 prohibit both direct criminal use of that testimony and derivative evidence. The Government must affirmatively establish legitimate sources wholly independent of the compelled testimony. Keeping the production act and papers out of the trial evidence does not prove investigative independence.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Hubbell; No. 99-166. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Compelled testimonial selection cannot become an immunized witness's investigative roadmap, 2000-06-05; Kennedy with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-05; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Voluntarily created document contents are not generally privileged, but that does not excuse derivative exploitation of compelled testimonial identification. Foregone conclusions, third-party production, collective-entity rules and truly independent evidence remain distinct. Section 6002 retains its exception for perjury, false statements and otherwise failing to comply with the order; no transaction-wide immunity follows.

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice.
