## Standards and Tests selection: An appellate brief functioning as a notice of appeal

### An appellate brief functioning as a notice of appeal

**Governing question:** Whether a document styled as a brief can satisfy the notice-of-appeal requirement.

**Current rule:** A document filed within the applicable appeal period may serve as the notice if its objective content identifies the appealing party, judgment or order appealed, and appellate court, as Rule 3(c) requires. Informality of title or form cannot alone require dismissal; every required item remains necessary. Under Rule 4's mistaken-filing provision, a notice mistakenly filed in the court of appeals is treated as filed in the district court upon the appellate court's receipt.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective content, timely functional compliance, and mistaken appellate filing: Smith v. Barry — Law of the decision, Holding and operative rule, January 14, 1992; O'Connor's opinion joined by eight Justices. Scalia's judgment concurrence is not counted as joining the full rationale.

**Present operation effective:** *Smith v. Barry*, January 14, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** Smith v. Barry, 502 U.S. 244 (1992) — Appellate jurisdiction and notices of appeal.

## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial exhaustion and damages-only federal prison medical-care claims

### Judicial exhaustion and damages-only federal prison medical-care claims

**Governing question:** Whether exhaustion of an administrative process must precede an existing federal constitutional damages action when Congress has imposed no exhaustion command.

**Current rule:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement governs according to its terms. In the non-APA judicial-exhaustion setting addressed here, absent such a command the court exercises sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt judicial access against the actual interests in agency authority, expertise, correction of error, useful fact development, and judicial efficiency. Under the law governing McCarthy, neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-effective §1997e imposed an exhaustion requirement on the existing *Carlson* federal medical-care damages channel. For claims governed by that former statutory baseline, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for a completed medical-care wrong need not exhaust the general BOP procedure considered in *McCarthy*: it cannot award compensation; its initial 15-day filing period and successive 20- and 30-day appeal periods, even with valid-reason extensions, risk forfeiture; and investigation and written responses without a hearing or formal evidentiary findings provide insufficient adjudicative benefit to outweigh access to court. Speculative FTCA conversion or settlement does not supply an available monetary remedy in that process.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory primacy, judicial discretion, and actual congressional scope: McCarthy v. Madigan — Statutory design governs the exhaustion inquiry, March 4, 1992.
- Damages-only BOP exception and its operative procedural premises: McCarthy — This damages-only claim need not pass through the general BOP process, March 4, 1992. Blackmun's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter controls both components; Scalia and Thomas join only the result on their no-compensation ground.

**Present operation effective:** McCarthy, March 4, 1992, within its surviving scope; PLRA, April 26, 1996, changes the covered statutory premise.

**Open question:** Prospective relief, a procedure able to award the requested compensation, and materially different administrative adjudication are not decided. The rule supplies no exception to a congressional command and no extension of *Bivens* to a new setting.

**Holdings navigation:** McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140 (1992) — Administrative exhaustion.

**Related current rules:** APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals states Darby's separate §704 restriction; Prior administrative exhaustion of an FTCA action states McNeil's express statutory prerequisite. Those decisions do not change McCarthy's former-law damages-only BOP holding; the later express PLRA command operates at its own scope.

**Effective transition:** PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), April 26, 1996, replaces §1997e with a command to exhaust available remedies before a covered confined-prisoner conditions action under §1983 or other federal law. See Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions. McCarthy's absence-of-command premise no longer supplies a blanket exemption from that text; disputed pending-case operation is not resolved by admission. The general judicial-exhaustion rule survives outside a controlling statutory command.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions

### Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions

**Governing question:** The original §1997e prefiling condition and distinct screening commands.

**Current rule:** A confined prisoner may not bring a prison-conditions action under §1983 or any other federal law until available administrative remedies are exhausted. On its own motion or a party's motion, the court shall dismiss such an action if satisfied that it is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim or seeks money from an immune defendant. A claim that on its face has one of those four defects may be dismissed without first requiring exhaustion. State failure to adopt or adhere to an administrative grievance procedure alone supplies no action under CRIPA §3 or §5. “Prisoner” in §1997e means a person incarcerated or detained in any facility who is accused of, convicted of, sentenced for, or adjudicated delinquent for violations of criminal law or terms or conditions of parole, probation, pretrial release or a diversionary program.

**Authority by component:**

- Available-remedies command, screening and its prior-exhaustion exception: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(a)–(c), (h), April 26, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** PLRA, April 26, 1996.

**Effective transition:** The statute was enacted April 26, 1996. Section 802's express all-prospective-relief transition is not generalized to §1997e or §1915; disputed application to pending matters remains unresolved unless an applicable authority separately resolves it.

**Unresolved interaction:** McCarthy's former damages-only BOP conclusion rested on absence of a statutory command. It supplies no exception to this new text. The admission establishes no litigant's exhaustion or availability finding and adopts no later construction of what makes a remedy available. §1915's fee-screening mechanisms remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prisoner mental-injury suits and litigation procedure

### Prisoner mental-injury suits and litigation procedure

**Governing question:** The original §1997e physical-injury, participation and reply requirements.

**Current rule:** A confined prisoner may bring no federal civil action for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without a prior showing of physical injury. To the extent practicable, pretrial proceedings in federal prison-conditions cases in which the prisoner must or may participate are conducted by telephone, video conference or other telecommunications without removal from the facility. With the custodian's agreement a hearing may be held there; to the extent practicable counsel must be allowed remote participation.

A defendant may waive reply to a confined prisoner's §1983 or other federal-law action without admitting its allegations. No relief may be granted unless a reply is filed. The court may require a reply if the plaintiff has a reasonable opportunity to prevail on the merits. “Prisoner” in §1997e means a person incarcerated or detained in any facility who is accused of, convicted of, sentenced for, or adjudicated delinquent for violations of criminal law or terms or conditions of parole, probation, pretrial release or a diversionary program.

**Authority by component:**

- Injury condition, hearings and reply: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), original §1997e(e)–(h), April 26, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** PLRA, April 26, 1996.

**Effective transition:** The statute was enacted April 26, 1996. Section 802's express all-prospective-relief transition is not generalized to §1997e or §1915; disputed application to pending matters remains unresolved unless an applicable authority separately resolves it.

**Exceptions and limits:** The statute does not establish an actual injury, alter the substantive elements of an Eighth Amendment violation or supply a default admission. No later interpretation of the physical-injury condition or its remedial reach is imported.

## Standards and Tests selection: Professional direction and genuine NLRA supervisory authority

### Professional direction and genuine NLRA supervisory authority

**Governing question:** How §2(11) distinguishes skilled patient-care direction from supervisory status.

**Current rule:** The Board may distinguish a professional employee’s skilled direction of care from genuine authority in the employer’s interest to perform any of §2(11)’s listed supervisory functions: hire, transfer, suspend, lay off, recall, promote, discharge, assign, reward, discipline, responsibly direct, adjust grievances or effectively recommend such action. The authority must involve independent judgment and not merely routine or clerical action. No hiring-or-firing power is indispensable, and genuinely held authority may qualify despite infrequent exercise. Professional judgment may be independent judgment, but skilled care alone does not establish the required supervisory authority. This is a permissible distinction, not a mandatory professional exemption or a holding that patient care never serves the employer’s interest.

**Authority by component:**

- Permissible functional distinction and all statutory predicates: NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America — The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority, 1994-05-23. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. All five directly adopt the permissible-construction ground; Stone-Zsela's proposed mandatory construction appears only in his concurrence.

**Present operation effective:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, 1994-05-23.

**Holdings navigation:** The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees

### Nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees

**Governing question:** What instructions govern nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees?

**Current rule:** An enforceable nominal-damages judgment directly benefiting the plaintiff by changing the fee defendant's legal obligation establishes prevailing-party eligibility under §1988. Magnitude bears on amount, not that eligibility. Reasonable fees require an explained Hensley assessment of actual success and unrelated unsuccessful work, permitting reduction to zero without mechanical proportionality to damages or a presumption of substantial fees. Correcting an appellate eligibility error does not itself fix the amount or automatically reinstate a prior award.

**Authority by component:**

- Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility: Farrar v. Hobby — Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility, 1992-12-14. Part I of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; all nine Justices support this controlling proposition.
- Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review: Farrar v. Hobby — Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review, 1992-12-14. Part II of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices control the remedy and stated fee principles.

**Present operation effective:** 1992-12-14; Farrar v. Hobby, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Farrar v. Hobby, 506 U.S. 103 (1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Prior administrative exhaustion of an FTCA action

### Prior administrative exhaustion of an FTCA action

**Governing question:** What instructions govern prior administrative exhaustion of an FTCA action?

**Current rule:** Before instituting an FTCA action the claimant must present the claim to the proper agency and receive final written denial sent by certified or registered mail; after six months without final disposition, the claimant may elect to treat inaction as denial. Later exhaustion does not cure the original premature filing even if little litigation occurred. Section 2675(a) excepts Federal Rules third-party complaints, crossclaims and counterclaims. New-action and limitations questions remain open.

**Authority by component:**

- Administrative exhaustion must precede the original FTCA action: McNeil v. United States — Administrative exhaustion must precede the original FTCA action, 1993-05-17. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-05-17; McNeil v. United States, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** McNeil v. United States, 508 U.S. 106 (1993).

## Holdings selection from federal-courts

#### McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6861.
**Decided:** March 4, 1992.

##### Whether Congress requires exhaustion of the general BOP grievance procedure for this federal medical-care damages action

**Holding:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement controls according to its terms. Where Congress has not required exhaustion, courts must exercise sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt access to court against the actual interests in agency authority and judicial efficiency; neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-current §1997e imposes a mandatory gate for this federal prisoner’s *Bivens* medical-care claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the governing inquiry and statutory application.

**Current-force modification:** The statutory-primacy rule and the construction of former §1997e remain controlling. PLRA §803(d) replaces that text: a prisoner confined in a jail, prison or other correctional facility may not bring a prison-conditions action under §1983 or any other federal law until available administrative remedies are exhausted. For this provision, prisoner includes a person incarcerated or detained for an accusation, conviction, sentence or delinquency adjudication under criminal law or the terms of parole, probation, pretrial release or diversion. The former absence of a congressional mandate does not establish an exemption from this new command. The admission decides neither the amended terms' disputed scope nor their temporal application to a particular pending action; it does not reopen McCarthy's judgment.

**Material application:** General prison-administration authority under 18 U.S.C. §4042 contains no exhaustion command. The then-current 42 U.S.C. §1997e addresses specified state-prisoner §1983 actions through limited discretionary stays, not this federal action. The Bureau’s general grievance procedure is not a comprehensive congressional replacement for the existing medical-care damages channel.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exception to an express congressional requirement or new *Bivens* action is created. Liability, immunity, causation and damages are not determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Patsy v. Board of Regents of Florida*, 457 U.S. 496, makes congressional design central. *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185, supplies the agency-authority, expertise, error-correction and efficiency purposes. *McGee v. United States*, 402 U.S. 479, supports sound judicial discretion absent congressional mandate. *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561, distinguishes statutory exhaustion and the adequacy of the administrative route. *Carlson v. Green*, 446 U.S. 14, remains the established medical-care damages channel without extension. *Bush v. Lucas*, 462 U.S. 367, and *Schweiker v. Chilicky*, 487 U.S. 412, are distinguished because there is no comparable comprehensive congressional remedial replacement here.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §803(d), replacement §1997e(a), (h), and the former absence of a mandatory exhaustion command. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### Whether the actual BOP procedure justifies requiring exhaustion of this damages-only claim absent a statutory mandate

**Holding:** Absent a statutory mandate, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for the completed medical-care wrong alleged here need not exhaust the described BOP process: it cannot award compensation, its short filing periods risk forfeiture, and its limited adjudicative benefit does not outweigh access to court. The result does not decide a request for prospective relief, a procedure able to grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices join this particular balancing and application. Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment solely because monetary relief is unavailable; their votes do not support the deadline or broader balancing rationale.

**Current-force modification:** The balancing and damages-only result remain controlling where no statute requires exhaustion. For an action governed by amended §1997e(a), the former-law absence of a mandate cannot itself excuse compliance with the new available-remedies requirement described in the preceding question. No construction of availability, compensation-incapable procedures or pending-case application under the amended text is established by source admission.

**Material application:** The process provides investigation and written responses but no hearing, formal evidentiary findings or monetary award. It requires an initial complaint within 15 days and appeals within 20 and 30 days, with extensions for valid reasons. The rejected regional appeal illustrates forfeiture risk without establishing official misconduct. Speculative FTCA conversion or settlement does not establish an effective damages remedy under these regulations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The deadline analysis does not invalidate short filing periods categorically. No finding of bias, deliberate indifference, damages entitlement or loss of a defense is made. The holding does not convert negligence into a constitutional violation or expand the established damages action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the exhaustion dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings on the damages complaint without completion of this grievance process as a prerequisite. Liability, defenses and damages remain for adjudication.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *McKart*, 395 U.S. 185, supports comparing actual individual and institutional interests. *Coit*, 489 U.S. 561, informs practical adequacy without categorically invalidating deadlines. *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78, preserves judicial cognizance of valid prisoner claims without deciding this claim’s validity. *Hillsborough Township v. Cromwell*, 326 U.S. 620, and *Union Pacific Railroad Co. v. Board of County Commissioners*, 247 U.S. 282, address uncertainty in an administrative route to the requested relief.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §803(d), the independent statutory exhaustion condition rather than former discretionary balancing. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

## Holdings selection from federal-courts

#### Smith v. Barry, 502 U.S. 244 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-7477.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether a timely appellate brief may function as a notice of appeal

**Holding:** A timely document styled as an appellate brief may serve as a notice of appeal if it objectively identifies the appealing party, the judgment or order appealed, and the appellate court as Rule 3(c) requires. Rule 4 treats a notice mistakenly filed in the court of appeals as filed in the district court upon receipt; informal form or title alone cannot require dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; these eight Justices supply the controlling rationale. Scalia concurs in the judgment and agrees with the express form-or-title ground.

**Material application:** Smith returned the clerk's informal-brief form within the valid appeal period. Use of that form did not negate the notice the document objectively might convey, and a requirement of separate notice and brief documents could not add a jurisdictional condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Each item required by Rule 3 remains necessary. The first notice remains ineffective under the then-current posttrial-motion rule. The Court does not decide whether the May 4 document actually supplies each required item or which judgment or orders it fairly identifies.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for the Fourth Circuit to examine the informal brief's actual contents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Torres makes Rule 3 compliance mandatory while allowing functional compliance with its required information. Foman supports contextual reading where intent to seek review is manifest. Griggs is applied. Ibarra remains confined to timely criminal reconsideration motions.

## Holdings selection from civil-rights

#### Farrar v. Hobby, 506 U.S. 103 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-990.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility

**Holding:** A civil-rights plaintiff who obtains an enforceable nominal-damages judgment against the defendant from whom fees are sought is a prevailing party under §1988. The judgment must directly benefit the plaintiff by changing the defendant’s legal obligation; its nominal amount does not defeat eligibility.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices support this controlling proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 — applied; nominal relief vindicates the established procedural violation without presumed compensatory injury.
- Hewitt v. Helms, 482 U.S. 755 — distinguished; favorable language without enforceable relief differs from a payment judgment.
- Rhodes v. Stewart, 488 U.S. 1 — distinguished; ineffective declaratory relief differs from a judgment directly benefiting the estate.
- Texas State Teachers Assn. v. Garland Independent School District, 489 U.S. 782 — applied and clarified; magnitude affects amount, not eligibility where nominal relief is enforceable.

##### Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review

**Holding:** When the court of appeals denies fees solely because a nominal-damages plaintiff is ineligible, correction of that error does not itself determine a reasonable amount. On this record, the fee judgment is vacated and remanded for preserved reasonableness objections and an explained Hensley assessment of actual success, allowing reduction to zero without automatically reinstating the prior award or requiring proportionality to damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices control the remedy and stated fee principles.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424 — applied; actual success, unrelated unsuccessful work and a reasoned discretionary assessment govern amount.
- City of Riverside v. Rivera, 477 U.S. 561 — applied within its authority; no mechanical proportionality requirement, without guaranteed fees for nominal relief.
- Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87 — applied; the court must determine a reasonable statutory fee in the circumstances.
- City of Burlington v. Dague, June 24, 1992 — distinguished; contingency enhancement under the two environmental statutes does not decide this eligibility or remand question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Eligibility does not guarantee a positive fee. Actual success, exclusion of unrelated unsuccessful work and an explained Hensley assessment govern; no damages-to-fees ratio or invented public benefit is permitted. The decision does not categorically forbid affirmance on a sufficient alternative fee ground in another case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the fee judgment and remand for preserved reasonableness objections and lower-court review as necessary. The prior $280,000 award is not automatically restored; the result may be a reduced award or zero.

## Holdings selection from labor-and-employment

#### NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, 511 U.S. 571

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1964.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority

**Holding:** The Board permissibly interprets NLRA §2(11), in the context of the Act's protection of skilled and professional employees, to distinguish direction incidental to skilled patient care from genuine supervisory authority exercised for the employer. On this record, its functional construction and supported determination that the four licensed practical nurses are employees must be sustained; no occupational exemption or independent judicial substitute for the agency's rationale is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. All five directly adopt the permissible-construction ground; Stone-Zsela's proposed mandatory construction appears only in his concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fall River Dyeing & Finishing Corp. v. NLRB, 482 U.S. 27 (1987): applies review for rational interpretation consistent with the Act and substantial evidentiary support.
- NLRB v. Yeshiva University, 444 U.S. 672 (1980): uses its distinction between ordinary professional duties and management, while preserving professional/institutional overlap and the separate managerial question.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves binding statutory boundaries; it does not settle this classification tension.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943), and National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: permit reliance on the agency's discernible actual rationale, not an invented substitute.
- Packard Motor Car Co. v. NLRB, 330 U.S. 485 (1947): distinguishes its pre-amendment employer-interest discussion from the later statutory definition.
- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, January 24, 1994: preserves Board judgment within an enacted delegation, without authorizing disregard of statutory text.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2(11)'s alternative authorities are hiring, transfer, suspension, layoff, recall, promotion, discharge, assignment, reward, discipline, responsible direction, grievance adjustment and effective recommendation of such action. Any one may suffice, even if rarely exercised, but exercise must serve the employer and require independent judgment beyond routine or clerical action. Hiring or firing is not indispensable. Professional judgment can be independent judgment exercised through genuine supervisory authority; it does not itself establish that authority. The sustained construction is permissible, not compulsory for all future Boards.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit's supervisory-status ruling is reversed and remanded, 5–4. Give effect to the supported Board employee-status determination and decide independently preserved remaining issues. No backpay award or finding of every unfair-labor-practice element is supplied.

### Artuz v. Bennett — 2000-11-07

Lindh's provision-specific temporal rule survives; it neither exempts this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA nor answers proper filing. ## Current neutral projection copy

### Lopez v. Davis — 2001-01-10

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The Bureau may apply this reasoned firearm-conduct category

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3621(e)(2)(B) permits the Bureau of Prisons to withhold a discretionary early-release reduction categorically from otherwise eligible prisoners whose current felony conduct involved carrying, possessing or using a firearm or other dangerous weapon, under the challenged 1997 regulation. A nonviolent conviction and successful completion of qualifying treatment are necessary conditions for a reduction, not an entitlement to individual selection or an award; any reduction remains limited to one year, and the category must remain within the statute and rationally applied to the actual qualifying conduct.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six adopt the entire statutory ground, its public-safety application and affirmance. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Kennedy dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress distinguished treatment qualification, threshold eligibility for a reduction and the Bureau's discretionary choice whether to grant one. The permission to reduce custody does not require the Bureau to decide each recurring safety issue anew. Heckler v. Campbell permits valid rules within a statutory delegation; it supplies no authority to disregard that delegation. Here firearm involvement in felony conduct rationally bears on safe early release. Congress's nonviolent-conviction threshold does not direct the Bureau to disregard that conduct at the separate selection stage. The 1997 rule does not reclassify the conviction as violent.

Yang preserves statutory limits, reasoned administration and the distinction between eligibility and relief. Its immigration-waiver rule forbids using qualifying fraud automatically to defeat every eligible applicant; this subset does not extinguish the release incentive for all eligible prisoners. Koray likewise requires the statutory categories to do their work and supplies no independent deference shortcut. The Court's conclusion rests on this delegation and reasoned safety criterion, not administrative convenience alone.

The Bureau's separate duty to make appropriate treatment available to prisoners it determines have a treatable addiction or abuse condition remains. Residential treatment and aftercare are required for eligible willing prisoners with a substance-abuse problem, subject to available appropriations and release-date priority. After the Director finds successful residential treatment, the prisoner remains in custody under appropriate conditions; if those conditions differ because of completion, periodic testing is required and the altered conditions must end when abuse recurs. None of those commands makes early release mandatory.

Lopez's firearm enhancement presents the challenged category. The record establishes treatment admission eligibility, not successful completion or entitlement to a reduction. Ordinary objections to a misapplied category, disregard of binding rules or supported arbitrary administration remain distinct. The Court does not validate every possible exclusion or automatic denial to all eligible prisoners. The amici rulemaking-procedure objection was not raised or decided below or presented in the petition and is not reached; no constitutional liberty-interest question is decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Heckler v. Campbell: applies the permission to resolve recurring issues by rules within the actual delegation; it does not make every categorical restriction lawful.
- INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang: preserves eligibility/discretion separation and reasoned, consistent administration; distinguishes its prohibition of automatically defeating every eligible waiver application from this reasoned subset under a different statute.
- Reno v. Koray: preserves contextual statutory construction and its detention-credit holding; it supplies neither the present release rule nor an independently sufficient administrative-deference ground.

## Current neutral projection copy

### Lopez v. Davis — 2001-01-10

Stone-Zsela dissents, joined by Stevens and Kennedy. Congress's nonviolent-conviction criterion and successful-treatment condition make a genuine opportunity for consideration part of the rehabilitative scheme. Permission to deny a reduction does not make that adjacent eligibility language idle. A firearm sentencing circumstance may strongly indicate danger, but it is not the conviction Congress specified and does not establish that treatment and individual circumstances can never matter.

The dissent distinguishes Campbell's rules resolving recurring subsidiary issues from a rule foreclosing the selection Congress made available. Yang likewise permits relevant discretionary judgment within the statute, rather than revision of statutory eligibility. The dissent accepts structured consideration, including strong or nearly decisive weight for preconviction firearm conduct and relevant postconviction circumstances. It rejects neither every category nor every consideration of offense conduct.

The dissent would set aside this categorical bar, require the Bureau to establish whether Lopez successfully completed the qualifying treatment, and, if so, consider a reduction through a reasoned decision addressing safety and rehabilitation. The record establishes treatment admission eligibility, not completion. The Bureau could still deny a reduction on lawful individual grounds. No particular reduction, immediate release, universal constitutional hearing right or liberty entitlement would follow.

### Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. — 2001-02-27

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Semtek_International_v_Lockheed_Martin_merits_2001-02-27.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Semtek_International_v_Lockheed_Martin_merits_2001-02-27.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Federal law ordinarily adopts rendering-state preclusion for diversity judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal common law governs the claim-preclusive effect of a federal diversity judgment on state-law claims and ordinarily incorporates the preclusion law applied by the courts of the State in which the rendering federal court sits, except when that law conflicts with a specifically demonstrated federal interest. No such conflict exists in this California limitations dismissal, so Maryland must apply California's claim-preclusion law rather than an automatic nationwide rule.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both holdings and the stated remand.

**Controlling explanation:** A federal judicial act requires a federal rule governing its effect. That source does not require a uniform rule extinguishing the underlying state claim everywhere. Erie and Hanna explain why removal into diversity should not change substantive consequences in a way that encourages forum selection and unequal treatment. Incorporating the rendering State's preclusion law avoids giving the same limitations judgment greater reach solely because the defendant removed the action.

O'Melveny and Atherton reject general uniformity as sufficient reason to displace state-created rights while preserving exceptional federal fields. The effect of a federal judgment supplies the federal field here; a particular conflict remains necessary to justify different substantive content. A state's rule might conflict with a federal interest in protecting federal judicial processes, but this judgment rests only on the State's limitations period. Lockheed's general appeal to finality does not establish such a conflict. The final judgment retains its lawful effect without receiving every additional consequence Lockheed requests.

Section 1738 and the Full Faith and Credit Clause concern state judgments and do not directly dictate the effect of this federal judgment. Baker illustrates the distinction between lawful adjudicative effect and an additional forum consequence, rather than supplying a direct statutory command here. California's actual preclusion rule was not determined below and remains for application on remand; the Court does not promise a second opportunity to litigate the tort merits. Federal-question judgments and materially different process-sanction dismissals fall outside the presented diversity limitations dispute.

#### II. Rule 41(b)'s merits designation does not supply universal claim preclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 41(b)'s default merits operation governs the dismissal's rendering-court effect and does not itself dictate the claim-preclusive consequences in every other forum. The default yields when the dismissal order specifies otherwise and excludes dismissals for lack of jurisdiction, improper venue or failure to join a party under Rule 19; a merits designation under the default does not replace the federal rule stated in Holding I.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly adopts this interpretation and rejects Maryland's contrary automatic-preclusion rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 41(b) allows dismissal for failure to prosecute or comply with the Rules or a court order and describes the default operation of covered dismissals and dismissals not otherwise provided for. Its language need not decide every later court's treatment to perform that procedural work. Rule 41(a)'s distinction between dismissals with and without prejudice reinforces the rendering-court function; neither subdivision purports to supply a complete national preclusion code for state claims.

Reading a procedural merits designation as worldwide extinction of the state claim would also create a serious Rules Enabling Act problem: section 2072 forbids procedural rules from abridging, enlarging or modifying substantive rights. The narrower reading gives the Rule effective work without that unsupported consequence. The label therefore does not establish that Maryland must dismiss regardless of California law.

The California judgment is valid and final, and this external-effect decision grants no permission to return to that court. The separate Rules Enabling Act question whether a federal court may bar refiling in circumstances where a state court would dismiss without prejudice is not decided; a direct challenge to that rendering-court consequence is outside this review. The rule governing other forums is resolved without adjudicating that distinct issue.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins: applies the allocation protecting state-created rights from a removal-driven change in substantive consequences.
- Hanna v. Plumer: applies the concerns with forum selection and unequal administration while respecting the actual scope of the federal procedural rule.
- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: preserves exceptional federal fields but rejects general uniformity as sufficient reason for displacement; the federal judgment supplies the source here.
- Atherton v. FDIC: applies the need for an identified federal conflict before substituting uniform substantive content.
- Baker v. General Motors Corp.: distinguishes lawful judgment effect from additional forum consequences; its section 1738 holding concerns state judgments and is not extended as a direct command for federal judgments.

### Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc. / American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Whitman — 2001-02-27

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Implementation costs do not set primary or secondary national standards

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109(b) excludes economic implementation costs from national primary and secondary ambient air-quality standard setting. Primary standards must provide the protection requisite for public health under section 108's scientific pollutant-effects criteria and with an adequate margin of safety; secondary standards must protect public welfare from known or anticipated adverse effects associated with the pollutant's presence under those criteria, without importing the primary safety-margin requirement.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's operative rule and standard/implementation distinction have nine direct primary-standard supporters and eight secondary-standard supporters, as identified in the topology. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the secondary judgment. The additional text-and-structure explanation has eight primary and seven secondary supporters; Breyer joins the operative rules and common distinction through the qualifications stated in his concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory criteria concern the pollutant's effects on health and welfare, while other provisions govern the means of attaining the standards. Describing compliance expense as an economic effect on health or welfare does not convert it into an effect associated with the pollutant's presence. Section 109(d)(2)(C)(iv) separately requires the scientific committee to advise about adverse health, welfare, social, economic and energy effects of attainment and maintenance strategies; it does not direct EPA to reduce either national standard by a cost balance. Periodic review returns to sections 108 and 109(b).

Union Electric preserves the division between standards and implementation. MCI and Brown & Williamson require respect for connected statutory commands; they supply no generic presumption against consequential regulation. The argument that all rational public protection must weigh compliance expense asks the Court to add a criterion Congress assigned elsewhere. Constitutional avoidance cannot create a cost construction unavailable under the enacted commands.

The common rule is not a zero-risk command and does not prohibit costs where other provisions authorize them. No concentration, scientific-method finding or indirect health-effects claim is resolved here. Breyer's additional history-based method and risk observations do not become part of the other Justices' explanation merely because the cost outcomes are unanimous.

#### II. The primary-standard delegation supplies adequate congressional guidance

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109(b)(1) constitutionally guides EPA by identifying the pollutant subject, published scientific criteria, requisite public-health protection and adequate safety margin. Requisite protection is sufficient without exceeding what protection requires; Congress need not choose a unique numerical concentration to confine the technical judgment.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Kennedy's adequate-guidance rule and common explanation. Kennedy, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join the bounded-execution characterization; Stevens and Souter accept a constitutional delegation of legislative rulemaking instead.

**Controlling explanation:** Hampton, Mistretta and Touby require an intelligible legislative principle. Congress here selected the protected interest, subject, degree of protection and scientific framework. The remaining judgment applies those choices to evidence; it is not permission to select any social objective. Loving reinforces attention to actual congressional limits, while its military and criminal setting retains its qualifications.

The absence of one inevitable numerical answer does not establish standardless power. Scientific uncertainty and the absence of a risk-free concentration require reasoned judgment within the statutory limits, subject to ordinary review. The seven Justices accepting the bounded-execution account regard that judgment as implementation of Congress's policy rather than transfer of the policy choice itself. Stevens and Souter agree that Congress has supplied sufficient limits while describing the authorized rulemaking as legislative. Neither characterization difference changes the adequacy holding.

Thomas joins under existing precedent while reserving its ultimate constitutional sufficiency; no reconsideration of that doctrine is presented. The Court reverses the delegation ruling and reaches no separately presented constitutional challenge to section 109(b)(2). Statutory context concerning that subsection's welfare objective supplies no additional constitutional adjudication.

#### III. An agency cannot cure a genuinely standardless delegation

**Holding and operative rule:** If Congress has supplied no constitutionally sufficient governing principle, an agency cannot cure the defect by selecting its own limit. EPA may choose and explain a lawful scientific methodology within section 109(b)(1)'s existing boundaries, but may not supply a missing legislative policy.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Kennedy's no-agency-cure holding; it independently rejects the court of appeals' proposed cure mechanism.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing delegation cases require Congress to establish the boundary. An agency's promise to exercise less power would itself make the policy choice the legislature failed to make. The court of appeals therefore could not remedy a genuinely standardless statute by returning it for EPA to devise a limiting principle.

Here Congress has already supplied adequate guidance, so the constitutional remand fails on that ground as well. The Court removes that remand without approving every methodology or insulating scientific reasoning from administrative review. Selecting and defending a permissible method inside the statutory assignment differs from creating a new limit to save a statute that has none. Economic balancing is neither required nor authorized as a cure for this asserted constitutional defect.

#### IV. The completed implementation framework is independently reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** EPA's settled choice of the statutory implementation framework accompanying the final ozone standard is final action reviewable under section 307(b), and the present legal question is ripe. The determination selects the governing implementation obligations and has present legal consequences; tentative guidance, influence or planning expense alone does not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's independent finality and ripeness holding has all nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Bennett requires both completion of the agency's decisionmaking and legal consequences. The 1996 proposal was preliminary and nonbinding. After comment, EPA settled which statutory framework would govern the revised standard, retained Subpart 2 only until old-standard attainment, and thereafter refused to reconsider that determination. The consequence is the governing legal regime for States' required implementation work, not merely persuasive advice or an uncompleted future choice. No sanction, approved plan or current violation is necessary.

The issue is legal and needs no further site-specific facts; review does not interrupt an unresolved agency choice on that framework. States must undertake implementation under the selected regime. Section 307 supplies a specific preenforcement channel and does not invariably require the enforcement effects that might govern a different APA challenge. Ohio Forestry's deferred logging-plan dispute preserved later project review and did not decide APA finality; its future-project premise is absent here.

This Court's authorized questions include reviewability and implementation, and the necessary statutory subsidiary question falls within Rule 14.1(a). That scope conclusion is separate from appellate jurisdiction and finality. No plain-error enlargement is required. The Court sustains review independently of the final-standard route in Holding V.

#### V. Review of the final ozone standard independently includes its disputed implementation premise

**Holding and operative rule:** A court reviewing the concededly final ozone standard under section 307(b) may decide the implementation relationship actually placed in issue by industry's claim that Subpart 2 displaced the general implementation regime and therefore barred revision. That necessary statutory premise supplies an independently sufficient review route; it does not bring every future plan or implementation dispute into final-standard review.

**Authority:** Kennedy, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this alternative holding. Stone-Zsela's reviewability support rests on Holding IV and does not join this additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Industry challenged EPA's authority to revise the standard on the premise that revision would invoke a general implementation regime displaced by Subpart 2. EPA answered that the subparts could operate together under its construction. A reviewing court cannot decide the actual challenge while treating the opposing statutory premise as immune from review. Section 307(b)'s review of the final standard therefore encompasses this necessary legal question even apart from finality of the implementation interpretation as distinct action.

The route is bounded by the challenge actually made. It neither approves later plans nor supplies review of hypothetical future agency choices. The authorized implementation and reviewability questions encompass the necessary subsidiary issue under Rule 14.1(a); that Court-rule relationship does not itself create section 307 jurisdiction. No separate plain-error route is invoked.

#### VI. EPA must reconcile the general and ozone-specific provisions without erasing applicable commands

**Holding and operative rule:** EPA may resolve genuine statutory fit questions concerning revised ozone standards, but attainment of the old one-hour standard alone does not authorize elimination of otherwise applicable Subpart 2 classifications, dates and associated requirements. EPA's conditional transition is unlawful to that extent, and the agency must supply a construction that gives the applicable general and ozone-specific provisions their enacted work.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Kennedy's implementation holding, qualifications and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 172(a)(1)(C) separately preserves specifically provided classifications, and section 172(a)(2)(D) preserves specifically provided attainment dates. Section 181(b)(1) reaches later designations, defeating the inference that a 1989 heading confines the specific subpart to old classifications alone. EPA cannot retain those constraints temporarily and then discard them solely because an area attains the old standard. Its actual position is conditional termination, not immediate universal repeal.

The old one-hour design-value structure and revised eight-hour standard nonetheless create real fit questions, including areas below the old threshold but above the new one and dates already passed. Section 181(b)(1) extends fixed dates for later classifications. Preserving applicable commands therefore does not mechanically transplant every original metric and date or eliminate every role for Subpart 1.

MCI, Brown & Williamson and National Mining require each applicable statutory command to retain force. Chevron permits reasonable resolution of genuine delegated ambiguity; it does not permit an agency to make a connected specific provision inoperative. Christensen preserves the separate inquiry into interpretive legal force, and no amount of deference supplies authority missing from the statute.

EPA receives the first opportunity to reconcile the provisions lawfully. The Court selects no substitute regime, concentration or State plan and decides no further section 182 application. #### Employment contracts fall within the section 1 exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1 of the Federal Arbitration Act excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the Act; its residual employment category is not confined to transportation occupations. Adams's retail employment agreement is excluded, so the FAA supplies no authority to compel arbitration of his claims or to enjoin his state action.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt the statutory construction, its general employment-contract reach, its application and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the employment exclusion must be read with the coverage provision in the same enactment. Section 1 identifies employment contracts of seamen and railroad employees and then adds other classes of workers engaged in interstate or foreign commerce. The shared category can be workers in commerce. The residual words do not expressly restrict the exclusion to transporting persons or goods.

The employer's associated-words argument is substantial, but the inference drawn from a statutory list must remain consistent with the text's contemporary commercial meaning and setting. The named occupations placed important federal employment arrangements beyond doubt; the residual language protects the wider employment category. Reading section 2 to follow the full reach of commerce authority while confining the accompanying labor exclusion to occupations most clearly within federal authority in 1925 would disconnect the provisions. The contemporaneous labor setting confirms the broader boundary where the competing linguistic readings leave uncertainty. Concern about employee choice supports that reading but cannot itself create an exemption.

Allied-Bruce establishes section 2's actual-commerce reach and preserves its operation in state and federal court, but expressly leaves section 1 unresolved. Gilmer concerns statutory claims under a securities-registration agreement and reserves the employment-contract question. Neither supplies a contrary controlling answer. Adams was employed as a retail sales counselor under the employment application containing this arbitration clause. His agreement therefore falls within the exclusion without a finding that its particular procedure denied an effective remedy. The Ninth Circuit correctly removed the federal compulsion and injunction order. No existing Supreme Court holding is overruled.

**Limits and matters not reached:** Otherwise covered commercial agreements remain subject to section 2. The state proceeding remains subject to independently applicable law and properly preserved defenses.

- **Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp.:** Distinguished because the agreement there arose from securities registration. Arbitration of statutory employment claims through such an agreement does not settle the separate employment-contract exclusion.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Preserved for antecedent assent, actual delegation and review in its proper setting. It does not enlarge the FAA's statutory coverage, and no new delegation or award-review rule is adopted.

The Act therefore cannot supply compulsion or an injunction for this excluded agreement. Its section 2 rules remain operative for otherwise covered commercial agreements. The exclusion does not declare employment arbitration agreements categorically unenforceable under independent state law, decide the underlying claims, or impose a costs inquiry as a condition of exclusion.

### Alexander v. Sandoval — 2001-04-24

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Alexander_v_Sandoval_merits_2001-04-24.md#holdings).

#### Prospective enforcement of Title VI's valid implementing requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VI's established private action permits statutory beneficiaries to obtain prospective relief requiring a covered recipient to comply with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 regulations implementing the statutory protection against discrimination, including the effects regulation assumed valid here. Congress supplies the action through Title VI read as a whole; a regulation cannot independently create an action under an otherwise remediless statute, and plaintiffs must prove the regulation's actual elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation rather than rely on statistical imbalance alone.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this rule, its explanation, the application and remedy, and the treatment of Powell below. These are five direct joins at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's protection and its authorized implementation operate together. Section 601 identifies the persons protected from discrimination in federally assisted programs; section 602 expressly directs implementation of that protection. Cannon supplies the established private-action setting and a contextual inquiry into congressional authorization. Congress's 1986 recognition of legal and equitable remedies in Title VI suits and the Civil Rights Restoration Act's preservation of the enforcement structure provide affirmative statutory context, not an inference from silence alone. Read together, these provisions support prospective enforcement of valid, definite administration requirements protecting those beneficiaries.

Petitioners correctly distinguish an agency's authority to impose a duty from Congress's authorization of a judicial action. But section 602's agency-facing language does not sever its implementing standards from the private action accepted within this particular statutory scheme. Administrative funding enforcement is not made the exclusive means of securing compliance. The additional effects obligation does not become an intentional-discrimination claim under section 601; prospective enforcement rests on the integrated statute. Guardians and Choate explain that substantive distinction without themselves conclusively deciding this action.

McClellan requires contextual authorization and rejects an unsupported Cable Act damages action; it does not impose an express-action-clause prerequisite or erase Title VI's established enforcement background. Central Bank preserves a confined received securities action and confirms the need to examine the particular scheme, without deciding this Title VI question. Franklin separates an action's existence from available relief; an injunction request does not supply its own authority. The practical loss of an effective individual remedy confirms the consequences of the competing readings, but does not create the action.

The Department receives federal assistance. The regulations' validity and the finding of unjustified discriminatory effects are accepted premises of this review. Sandoval may maintain this prospective action, and the reviewed injunction remains supported by that route.

#### Powell's direct-action restriction is overruled to the stated extent

**Holding and operative rule:** Powell v. Ridge is overruled insofar as its direct-action holding forecloses the prospective Title VI enforcement just described. Its distinct section 1983 holding is not decided or abrogated, and today's judgment neither authorizes retrospective damages for unintentional discrimination nor disturbs a completed final judgment.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join this express partial overruling and its complete statutory stare-decisis explanation in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court does not distinguish Powell on the ground that Sandoval seeks an injunction. Powell itself rejected prospective enforcement of the additional effects obligation. Nor do a different state policy or a finding instead of an allegation of effects change its action-creation rule. Reconsideration is necessary.

Statutory stare decisis deserves substantial weight because Congress can correct an interpretation and courts ordinarily preserve settled constructions. Powell nevertheless gave insufficient weight to the integrated source of Title VI's accepted beneficiary action. It treated the difference between statutory intentional-discrimination liability and valid preventive implementation as dispositive of prospective enforcement, although that difference does not answer the statute-specific authorization question. The Court corrects that identified error in the enforcement arrangement, rather than making disagreement alone a reason to overrule precedent.

Powell is recent, and no concrete reliance arrangement has been identified here. Neither consideration independently permits overruling a statutory decision. Their significance is that covered recipients already owed the regulatory duties whose prospective enforcement was barred. Restoring that enforcement channel does not prescribe a new substantive language policy, impose retrospective damages, or reopen a final judgment. Reliance on the absence of a beneficiary's action does not outweigh the better-supported statutory arrangement in these circumstances. The Chief Justice acknowledges that he joined Powell's contrary holding.

The Court preserves Powell's distinction between regulatory validity and the source of a private remedy, while rejecting its conclusion about this prospective Title VI action. Powell's separate section 1983 analysis concerns a distinct enforcement vehicle and individual statutory-right inquiry. That question is outside this grant and remains untouched.

#### Application, remedy and limits

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed insofar as it recognizes this private prospective action and sustains the injunction requiring accommodation of non-English speakers in driver's-license examinations. The injunction remains operative on the accepted findings and within the scope reviewed. No damages are awarded and no regulation is invalidated.

Regulatory validity, the correctness of the disparate-impact merits determination, damages availability for unintentional discrimination, and a separate section 1983 action are outside the granted question. The accepted section 601 action for intentional discrimination remains, with its own intent requirement; this judgment finds no intentional discrimination. Ordinary application of the announced rule requires proof of the actual regulatory elements, coverage and fitted relief. Those applications are work under the governing rule, not a reservation about whether this category of prospective enforcement exists. The Court creates no new claim, funding cutoff, private administrative-exhaustion requirement or statistical presumption.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Powell v. Ridge, April 3, 2000:** Its direct-action prohibition is overruled only to the extent it bars prospective enforcement of valid, sufficiently definite Title VI implementing requirements protecting statutory beneficiaries. Its separate section 1983 holding, the distinction between regulatory validity and remedy authorization, and its completed judgment remain undisturbed.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** Its contextual inquiry and established Title VI/Title IX private-action setting supply the foundation for the integrated statutory interpretation; Cannon is not treated as having already decided this regulatory action.
- **Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission:** Its distinction between intentional-discrimination liability and regulatory effects protection is preserved. Its separate positions and remedial discussion are not converted into an earlier controlling resolution of the present action.
- **Alexander v. Choate:** Its account of the statutory/regulatory distinction remains; that distinction does not by itself answer prospective enforcement under Title VI as a whole.
- **McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc., January 7, 1999:** Its requirement of contextual congressional authorization is applied. Its rejection of the particular Cable Act damages action remains, distinguished by that statute's different remedial background rather than by a rule that requests for injunctions create actions.
- **Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., April 19, 1994:** Its confined received securities action remains intact; its contextual received-action rationale is consistent with, but does not itself establish, the Title VI rule adopted here.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992:** Its separation of an existing action from appropriate relief is applied. Its intentional-discrimination damages holding does not decide damages for unintentional effects here.
- **Blessing v. Freestone, April 21, 1997:** Its separate individual-right and section 1983 enforcement inquiries remain. The present Title VI direct-action holding supplies no new section 1983 ruling.

**Resulting law:** Effective April 24, 2001, Title VI's established private action permits statutory beneficiaries to enforce prospectively valid, sufficiently definite section 602 requirements implementing their protection against discrimination. This includes valid effects regulations of the kind presented here. Plaintiffs must establish the regulation's actual elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation; statistical imbalance alone is not automatically enough.

Powell's contrary direct-action restriction no longer governs that category of prospective claims. Its separate section 1983 holding remains, as do section 601's intentional-discrimination requirement, valid administrative enforcement and the distinction between a regulatory duty and the congressional source of a private action. This decision neither creates private actions under otherwise remediless statutes nor resolves damages for unintentional effects. Its rule governs covered claims; it does not reopen completed final judgments.

### Randell v. Johnson — 2001-04-25

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Randell_v_Johnson_merits_2001-04-25.md#holdings).

#### I. The precise damages claim must necessarily contradict an operative determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Favorable invalidation is not required for a section 1983 claim whose success is compatible with the valid sentence or custody determination, including a claim that officials executed a valid sentence contrary to its terms. Before imposing the prerequisite, the court must identify the operative judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and determine whether success on the actual civil theory necessarily establishes its invalidity; a sentence-administration label neither establishes nor avoids that consequence.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by all eight Associate Justices. All nine directly adopt this classification rule, rejection of the categorical screening ground, and vacatur and remand for the necessary comparison.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the legal consequence of the claim matters more than its label or its connection to custody. Heck distinguishes a necessarily conviction-negating claim from an independent injury compatible with the criminal judgment. Edwards applies that distinction to earned-credit proceedings: calling a claim procedural does not save it if success necessarily defeats an outstanding adjudication, but a genuinely independent injury requires its own analysis. Wolff preserves compatible civil relief, and Preiser's release channel does not turn every damages complaint concerning confinement into habeas.

Randell alleges that officials failed to credit parole-warrant confinement and required him to serve the same time again. That allegation could seek enforcement of a valid sentence, or it could necessarily contradict an operative credit determination. The screening record does not sufficiently identify the determination or establish that relationship. A court cannot assume every alleged excess day negates a judgment, nor assume every administrative-execution claim leaves the controlling custody determination untouched.

The lower court also treated Heck as already deciding a universal former-prisoner bar. The actual rule reserved that population and persons without realistic custody-review opportunity; Spencer preserved those reservations without adopting an affirmative civil exception. Existing precedent therefore did not supply the categorical ground used below. This error requires vacatur, not a finding that Randell was overconfined or already possesses a successful damages claim.

#### II. Genuine loss of any realistic custody-review opportunity permits the civil claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When a former prisoner's damages claim necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination, prior favorable invalidation is not required if the claimant never had a realistic lawful opportunity to contest that confinement before custody review became unavailable, or lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. A claimant who lost a genuinely usable opportunity through an unexcused failure remains subject to the prerequisite; release or present federal habeas unavailability alone is neither the exception nor qualifying invalidation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Five directly adopt this new bounded rule within Heck's reservation, its statutory explanation and its unexcused-loss limit. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule coordinating available statutory remedies cannot require a route the claimant never realistically could use. Heck's controlling statutory explanation reconciles section 1983 with a lawful custody-review channel. Preiser supports that coordination where the remedies intersect. Patsy rejects a general requirement to exhaust state procedures before seeking section 1983 relief. The common-law favorable-termination analogy helps implement the statutory arrangement; it does not create an absolute civil disability whenever confinement once occurred.

The defendants correctly object that release cannot reward strategic delay or authorize a person to bypass a real review opportunity. The rule therefore turns on realistic availability and the circumstances of its loss, not custody status alone. An unexcused failure to use a genuine opportunity leaves the prerequisite in place. The exception concerns meaningful first review, not an entitlement to repeat unsuccessful review, and expiration does not invalidate the underlying determination.

The inquiry must use supported facts about custody, notice, court access, timing and the ability to pursue the particular challenge. Neither absence of an earlier successful writ nor the theoretical existence of another procedure proves what opportunity actually existed. An authorized qualifying invalidation route may matter where the prerequisite applies, but requiring proof that every conceivable state process is unavailable would recreate the general exhaustion rule Patsy rejects.

Spencer did not already grant this exception; it decided the live controversy in a habeas case and expressly preserved the civil question. This decision resolves the legal consequences of genuine unavailability and unexcused loss. Randell's actual circumstances remain for ordinary adjudication under that rule, without an assumed finding of diligence, abandonment or deliberate delay.

#### III. The remand applies the rule without deciding overconfinement or damages

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit's screening affirmance is vacated and the case remanded to compare the pleaded credit theory with the actual custody determination and, if necessary invalidity is established, decide realistic review opportunity and legally excused loss from supported facts. A compatible claim or one within the no-realistic-review exception may proceed under ordinary civil requirements; an otherwise covered claim lacking qualifying invalidation must be dismissed without prejudice for lack of present cognizability, not as an adjudication that the alleged misconduct did not occur.

**Authority:** All nine support vacatur and the classification return in Part I. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer directly join Part III's complete conditional directions, including application of Part II's exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal habeas concerning the disputed confinement was unavailable when Randell filed on June 18, 1999. That fact does not resolve earlier opportunity, a previously adjudicated credit issue, a usable qualifying invalidation route or the legality of the confinement. The court must permit ordinary consideration of pertinent allegations and supported facts, without assuming an unexcused bypass or imposing general state-remedy exhaustion.

Where the invalidation prerequisite continues to apply, Heck recognizes reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal or a federal habeas writ calling the determination into question. Failed review and mere expiration do not qualify. The covered claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation, so the proper remedy for present noncognizability is dismissal without prejudice, not conversion to habeas, an exhaustion stay or an invented tolling regime. Accrual and limitations for a claim outside that prerequisite remain for their independently applicable law.

Section 1915(e)(2) still requires dismissal at any time, notwithstanding any fee paid, for an untrue poverty allegation or an action or appeal that is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim or seeks money from an immune defendant. Correcting the legal failure-to-state-a-claim ground does not decide any other actual screening ground. Nor does failure to satisfy a legal prerequisite establish factual frivolousness.

Individual responsibility, constitutional violation, causation, injury, immunity and properly applicable preclusion remain separate. The requested $1,000 per day establishes no measure or amount of damages. The Court orders no custody recalculation, release, state reopening, new evidentiary hearing, stay, appointed counsel or present award.

#### Scope of the decision

The governing no-realistic-opportunity standard is resolved for the former-prisoner category presented; its factual application is not postponed as an unresolved legal question. The Court adopts neither a categorical bar against all former prisoners nor an unconditional exception whenever federal habeas is presently unavailable. Ordinary proof, pleading, defenses and limitations retain their force. No finding establishes actual overconfinement, deliberate delay, a prior adverse credit adjudication or an adequate remaining state procedure.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Heck v. Humphrey, June 24, 1994:** Its necessary-invalidity comparison, qualifying invalidation routes, deferred accrual for covered claims and without-prejudice dismissal are applied. The Court now resolves part of its express reservation by adopting the bounded former-prisoner no-realistic-review exception and retaining the prerequisite after unexcused loss of a usable opportunity. Its original in-custody holding is not overruled.
- **Edwards v. Balisok, May 19, 1997:** Its legal-consequence analysis governs sentence-credit claims; procedural wording does not avoid necessary invalidity, and genuine independent injuries remain distinct. Its invalidation and accrual rules remain for claims still covered.
- **Spencer v. Kemna, March 3, 1998:** Its habeas mootness holding and preservation of the civil-remedy reservations remain. Its discussion did not itself create today's affirmative exception or a universal former-prisoner bar.
- **Preiser v. Rodriguez:** Its special channel for release from custody supplies the statutory coordination premise; this damages action is neither automatically converted into habeas nor denied solely because it concerns custody.
- **Patsy v. Board of Regents:** Its rejection of general section 1983 state-remedy exhaustion remains controlling. The realistic-opportunity inquiry cannot be converted into exhaustion of every conceivable state procedure.
- **Wolff v. McDonnell:** Its preservation of compatible civil relief informs the independent-claim boundary; factual overlap with custody does not itself require invalidation.
- **Carey v. Piphus:** Its common-law remedial guidance remains subordinate to the federal statutory remedy, and actual compensable injury remains necessary; no automatic per-day award follows.

**Resulting law:** A civil claim compatible with the valid custody determination does not require favorable invalidation. If success necessarily contradicts that determination, a former prisoner may proceed without invalidation when meaningful lawful custody review never realistically existed or was lost through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. Unexcused loss of an actually usable opportunity leaves the prerequisite in force; release and present federal habeas unavailability alone do not satisfy it.

Courts must identify the actual custody determination and review opportunity. This rule resolves the legal consequences of availability and unexcused loss, while leaving Randell's facts and ordinary civil requirements for adjudication. For a claim remaining subject to invalidation, Heck's qualifying routes, deferred accrual and without-prejudice dismissal continue. No general state-remedy exhaustion, automatic damages right or compulsory reopening follows.

### United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative — 2001-05-14

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/United_States_v_Oakland_Cannabis_Buyers_Cooperative_merits_2001-05-14.md#holdings).

#### I. The distributor has no statutory medical-necessity exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** The Controlled Substances Act forecloses the Cooperative's medical-necessity defense to its prohibited manufacture, distribution and associated possession with intent to distribute marijuana. Medical recommendations and state permission do not supply federal authorization, and even satisfaction of the lower court's medical criteria does not create this distributor's exception.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those eight directly adopt both bounded holdings and their remedy; Breyer does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 841(a)(1) prohibits the identified conduct except as authorized by the subchapter. Schedule I treatment and the specified research route give operative content to that authorization structure; the Cooperative claims no research authorization. The conclusion rests on Congress's enacted classification, not a judicial finding that marijuana can never benefit a patient. Rutherford supports adherence to the chosen medical-regulatory scheme. Bailey supplies necessity's ordinary background and attention to lawful alternatives, but does not permit a court to override the particular medical-distribution policy Congress prescribed.

The Cooperative's strongest argument is the need of seriously ill patients after other treatment fails. The district modification required four conjunctive conditions: a serious medical condition; imminent harm without cannabis; need for cannabis to treat or alleviate the condition or associated symptoms; and no reasonable legal effective alternative because all legal alternatives tried were ineffective or caused side effects the patient could not reasonably tolerate. Those conditions must be accurately understood, and the order does not establish that every recipient satisfied them. Even assuming satisfaction, however, medical need does not furnish the statutory authorization for this distributor. Neither the absence of express necessity-defense words nor sympathy decides the issue; the prohibition, classification and actual authorization channels together do so.

#### II. Equitable discretion does not supply the same substantive exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 882(a) preserves lawful discretion whether and how to enjoin violations, but a court may not grant the same prohibited medical-distribution exemption by recharacterizing its own balance of medical benefits and statutory policy as injunction modification. The court may tailor authorized enforcement means consistently with the Act; it may not displace the substantive distribution rule.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Opinion of the Court directly controls the independent equitable ground and its preserved discretion.

**Controlling explanation:** Hecht and Romero-Barcelo preserve traditional choice and tailoring in equitable enforcement. Meghrig requires that this discretion operate within the enacted remedial scheme. Grupo Mexicano likewise distinguishes an actual source of remedial authority from a desired result; its disputed general-equity question does not decide every statutory injunction. Section 882(a) authorizes relief without commanding an injunction in every case or its broadest possible form. A court may therefore consider whether relief is needed, whether its wording exceeds a violation, and how lawful compliance should be ordered. It may not authorize continued prohibited distribution because it gives the medical advantages of nonenforcement greater weight than Congress did. That would supply the statutory exception rejected above through the remedy alone. These limits resolve the independently offered equitable ground for affirmance without deciding all injunctions affecting individual patients.

**Limits:** No ambiguity justifies inserting this distribution exception through constitutional avoidance. That conclusion does not resolve the underlying Commerce Clause, substantive due-process or patient-liberty objections left unadjudicated below; their ordinary preservation and merits requirements remain. Individual possession without distribution, individual-patient necessity and injunction questions, and necessity under materially different federal statutes are outside this case. No general doubt about necessity doctrine or automatic maximal injunction follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **United States v. Rutherford:** Applies respect for the medical-regulatory scheme against a judicial treatment exemption.
- **United States v. Bailey:** Preserves necessity's ordinary framework and lawful-alternative limits without recognizing or rejecting a universal defense under all federal statutes.
- **Hecht Co. v. Bowles; Weinberger v. Romero-Barcelo:** Preserve choice and tailoring of lawful equitable enforcement, not authority to contradict a substantive statutory restriction.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc.:** Applies source-specific remedial limits without creating a universal ban on monetary equity or injunction modification.
- **Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc.:** Applies the distinction between actual remedial authority and a desired result at its proper scope; its unsecured-creditor rule does not decide all statutory injunctions.

**Resulting law:** The Act permits no medical-distribution exemption of the kind claimed by the Cooperative through statutory necessity or the same equitable balance. This construction reaches its manufacture, distribution and associated possession with that intent. Lawful enforcement discretion and independently established constitutional limits retain their distinct predicates. No rule about individual possession, individual-patient necessity or injunctions, or necessity under materially different federal statutes is announced; no medical finding that marijuana can never benefit a patient follows.

#### Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 512 U.S. 267

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Maher Terminals is included under Rule 12.2, covering lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims; Greenwich concerns black-lung benefits.

##### The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order

**Holding:** When APA §556(d) governs and no applicable statutory exception provides otherwise, the proponent of a rule or order bears the burden of persuasion, not merely the obligation to produce evidence. With the preponderance standard governing these benefits proceedings, a claimant cannot prevail on a necessary element left in genuine evidentiary equipoise after complete weighing and application of operative statutory presumptions and lawful defense burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves its preponderance standard and uses its explanation of the APA framework; its proof-quantum holding is not recast as an earlier decision squarely settling allocation.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393, 404 n.7 (1983): disapproves the production-only construction of APA §7(c), now §556(d), to the extent inconsistent with the persuasion allocation; preserves the Wright Line holding requiring the General Counsel to prove unlawful motivation and permitting an employer same-decision affirmative defense.

##### The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation

**Holding:** Longshore Act §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), requires APA adjudication notwithstanding other provisions of the chapter, and §23(a), 33 U.S.C. §923(a), does not exempt these claims from §556(d)'s persuasion allocation by relaxing evidentiary and technical procedural rules. Section 923(a) separately requires reception of a deceased employee's declarations concerning the injury under investigation and makes them sufficient to establish the injury when corroborated by other evidence; that preserved rule does not establish Santoro's disputed aggravation and hastened death on the claim presented here, and the vacatur and remand for a lawful preponderance determination are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

**Material application:** Section 923(a) frees the deputy commissioner or Board from common-law or statutory evidence rules and technical or formal procedure except as provided by the chapter; it permits, rather than commands, a chosen investigative or hearing method best suited to ascertain the parties' rights. Its deceased-declaration reception and corroborated-sufficiency commands are separate. The §20(a) presumption that a claim comes within the Act operates in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; the ALJ found it rebutted here, and it is no longer evidence supporting ultimate persuasion. The causal questions concern aggravation and hastened death, not an established finding that the workplace incident originally caused the cancer. Incomplete weighing remains a question for administrative reconsideration and ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): applies the preponderance quantum in APA adjudication while retaining the distinction between agency factfinding and appellate review.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393 (1983): retains genuine affirmative-defense burdens; it does not authorize transfer of claimant persuasion merely because a benefits scheme is remedial.

##### The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA

**Holding:** The Black Lung Benefits Act's §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore adjudicatory provisions subject to its statutory and regulatory qualifications; even assuming it authorizes an appropriate regulatory departure from APA §556(d), §718.3(c)'s statement of a congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish that departure. The APA persuasion rule therefore governs the disputed pneumoconiosis and total-disability determinations here, and the Greenwich vacatur and remand are affirmed without deciding the full scope of §932(a)'s regulatory power or adopting an independent ground under §718.403 or Mullins.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Material application:** Section 932(a) applies after December 31, 1973 during periods when the State's compensation law is not on the Secretary's §931(b) list, to coal-mine operators respecting death or total disability due to pneumoconiosis arising from mine employment, or entitlements under §921(c)(5). It excludes Longshore Act §§1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 37, 38, 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 and 51. Incorporation is except as otherwise provided in §932(a) or by the Secretary's regulations, and references to the employer refer to fund trustees as the Secretary considers appropriate consistently with 26 U.S.C. §9501(d). Additional provisions the Secretary prescribes in the Federal Register to provide payment to entitled persons may not be inconsistent with the provisions specifically excluded. The cross-reference to §28(h)(1) of the 1984 Longshore amendments excludes that Act's §7 medical-services amendments from black-lung claims. None of those excluded-provision, fund-substitution or medical-services matters is adjudicated here.

Section 718.3(c) expresses the policy of giving claimants the benefit of all reasonable doubt about total or partial disability or death due to pneumoconiosis; it does not expressly prescribe an ultimate equipoise award. Section 718.403 places proof of an alleged fact on the party alleging it except as provided in the subchapter, but its independent effect is not decided. Ondecko's more than ten years of coal-mine employment bears on whether established pneumoconiosis arose from that employment; it does not supply the separate disease and total-disability findings disputed here. An ALJ must not stop weighing at an apparent tie or rename a tie a slight preponderance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no final entitlement, medical fact, benefit amount or award of relief to either employer. It preserves statutory exceptions and presumptions according to their own triggers and effects, does not settle the full regulatory-exception power under §932(a), and does not decide the independent §718.403/Mullins ground. There is no separate alternative holding and no fractured rationale requiring a Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mullins Coal Co. v. Director, OWCP, 484 U.S. 135 (1987): preserves its requirement of actual weighing under its interim-presumption regulation and its reservation of the APA question; the Court does not adopt the Third Circuit's separate use of Mullins and §718.403 as an independent bar.
- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves the preponderance standard while the incorporated APA allocation determines the consequence of genuine equipoise.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Third Circuit judgments are affirmed: the Greenwich award and Maher lifetime-disability and death-benefit orders remain vacated for administrative reconsideration. #### Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-795.

**Decided:** 1998-01-26.

##### The announced reasonable-doubt polling threshold is permissible

**Holding:** The Board may require an employer to possess a good-faith reasonable doubt, supported by objective considerations, of an incumbent union's majority support before conducting a safeguarded poll. Using the same prerequisite for polling, withdrawing recognition and seeking a Board election is not, by itself, irrational or inconsistent with the Act; objective doubt is uncertainty about majority support, not proof that the union has lost it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This threshold is permissible, not obligatory for every future Board; §§7 and 8(c) supply no unconditional right to poll. Existing safeguards require a majority-claim inquiry purpose, its communication, assurances against reprisal, secret ballot, no unfair practices or coercive atmosphere, and advance time-and-place notice. Certification, contract and representation protections retain their distinct predicates.

##### The presented challenge reaches the standard actually applied

**Holding:** A challenge expressly directed to the amount of evidence demanded before polling fairly includes whether the Board applied its announced reasonable-doubt standard to the order under review. That inquiry reaches the associated whole-record application here; it creates no general permission to add an independent factual question merely because merits briefs discuss it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

##### An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof

**Holding:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the Board's finding must be supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole under the substantive standard actually governing the case. When the announced predicate is objective reasonable doubt, the agency cannot sustain its order by effectively demanding proof approaching actual loss of majority support, or silently convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts III–IV have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, providing five direct votes for this framework and its application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency’s §10(c) preponderance burden differs from whole-record substantial-evidence review. Lawful expressly adopted presumptions remain possible; expertise cannot silently alter the governing standard, and no systematic-manipulation finding or automatic credit for unverified reports follows.

##### This record requires denial of enforcement

**Holding:** The finding that Allentown lacked objective reasonable doubt when it announced the poll is unsupported by substantial evidence on the whole record. The enforcement judgment must therefore be reversed with instructions to deny enforcement of the challenged Board order; the record does not call for another agency weighing under the same or a newly demanding threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the application and complete mandate.

**Material application:** The individually accepted reports together with Marsh, Bloch and steward Mohr establish objective uncertainty without a headcount of actual minority support. Their cumulative relation to the continuing workforce controls; the later 19–13 poll provides no retroactive justification.

##### The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority

No precedent is overruled.

Section 2(11) identifies authority to hire, transfer, suspend, lay off, recall, promote, discharge, assign, reward or discipline other employees, responsibly direct them, adjust their grievances, or effectively recommend such action. The functions are alternatives: possession of any one qualifying authority may suffice, even if rarely exercised, but its exercise must be in the employer's interest and require independent judgment rather than merely routine or clerical action. Hiring or firing is not indispensable. Professional judgment can supply independent judgment when it is exercised through genuine supervisory authority; skilled judgment does not itself establish that authority.

The Board's functional construction is permissible, not compulsory for all future Boards. A charge nurse's reallocation of aides may involve both clinical and supervisory judgment, so the actual delegation matters. Professional judgment can satisfy independent judgment when genuine supervisory authority exists; it cannot establish that authority by itself. The four employees are licensed practical nurses, and their protection does not depend on classifying them as registered professionals. The agency's actual limited-role findings support its result. His mandatory construction has no other joins and does not bind later Boards; the Court's adopted rule permits the Board's present interpretation.

Kennedy dissents, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. The statute separately requires a listed supervisory function and independent judgment, which can preserve employee coverage without an additional exclusion from employer interest. They would affirm the Sixth Circuit's refusal of enforcement on the legal ground presented. They do not hold that every nurse is a supervisor or decide an alternative classification ground the Board did not present. In their view, the statutory objection cannot be answered by describing the same direction as incidental professional work when it otherwise satisfies the enacted definition.

##### Statutory design governs the exhaustion inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement controls according to its terms. Where Congress has not required exhaustion, courts must exercise sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt access to court against the actual interests in agency authority and judicial efficiency; neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-current §1997e imposes a mandatory gate for this federal prisoner’s medical-care claim for damages under Bivens.

**Authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the governing inquiry and statutory application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a judicial exhaustion rule must fit Congress’s design and the purposes served by the actual administrative process. Patsy makes congressional intent central, while McKart and McGee explain why administrative expertise, correction of error and useful fact development matter when courts retain discretion. Coit distinguishes that discretion from a statutory command. Congress’s general assignment of prison administration to the Bureau says nothing requiring this prisoner to exhaust before seeking damages. Section 1997e’s limited treatment of certain state-prisoner actions does not enact a broader federal rule by implication. Carlson supplies the established medical-care damages channel; Bush and Schweiker concern comprehensive remedial choices Congress made in different programs. The Bureau’s own grievance procedure is not an equivalent congressional substitute. Respondents’ appeal to administrative autonomy therefore requires examination of what this procedure can accomplish, rather than automatic dismissal. The Court does not displace an express statutory requirement, announce a new Bivens action or determine the medical claim’s merits.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Patsy v. Board of Regents of Florida*, 457 U.S. 496 — applied for congressional primacy and the actual limited scope of contemporaneous §1997e.
- *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185 — applied for the purposes of agency authority, expertise, correction and efficiency.
- *McGee v. United States*, 402 U.S. 479 — applied for sound judicial discretion where Congress has not clearly required exhaustion.
- *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561 — applied to distinguish statutory exhaustion and the adequacy of an administrative route.
- *Carlson v. Green*, 446 U.S. 14 — preserved as the existing medical-care damages channel, without extending it to a new context.
- *Bush v. Lucas*, 462 U.S. 367 — distinguished: no comparable comprehensive congressional remedial scheme displaces this existing channel.
- *Schweiker v. Chilicky*, 487 U.S. 412 — distinguished on the same absence of a congressional replacement scheme.

##### This damages-only claim need not pass through the general BOP process

**Controlling proposition:** Absent a statutory mandate, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for the completed medical-care wrong alleged here need not exhaust the described BOP process: it cannot award compensation, its short filing periods risk forfeiture, and its limited adjudicative benefit does not outweigh access to court. The result does not decide a request for prospective relief, a procedure able to grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative adjudication.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join Blackmun’s particular balancing and application. Scalia and Thomas agree with the result solely because no money remedy is available; their judgment concurrence does not join the deadline or broader balancing rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this grievance process provides too little countervailing benefit to justify barring the damages complaint. McKart requires attention to the process’s real function, not merely its administrative label. Investigation and written responses may help officials improve care, but they neither compensate this completed injury nor create formal findings on which a court can conclusively dispose of the claim. The successive filing periods also risk forfeiture; extensions exist, but their valid-reason condition does not remove that practical burden, illustrated by the rejected regional appeal. Coit’s concern for an adequate route and Turner’s protection of valid prisoner claims inform the balance. The proposed conversion to an FTCA claim and possible settlement do not establish an effective damages remedy in these regulations or this record; Hillsborough cautions against requiring an uncertain route to relief. Ordinary litigation remains available for developing facts and asserting defenses. The Court decides no medical violation, improper grievance handling or exhaustion rule for prospective relief.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185 — applied through a claim-specific comparison of individual and institutional interests.
- *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561 — its adequacy principle informs the practical evaluation; the filing-period application is not a categorical invalidation of short deadlines.
- *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78 — applied for continued judicial cognizance of valid prisoners’ constitutional claims, without deciding this claim’s validity.
- *Hillsborough Township v. Cromwell*, 326 U.S. 620 — applied against forcing resort to an uncertain administrative avenue for the requested relief.
- *Union Pacific Railroad Co. v. Board of County Commissioners*, 247 U.S. 282 — applied for the significance of uncertainty in the available administrative remedy.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

No general exception to a congressional exhaustion command is created. Prospective relief, an administrative monetary remedy and materially different adjudicative procedures remain open. The Court makes no finding of bias, deliberate indifference, damages entitlement or loss of a defense. It does not transform negligence into a constitutional violation or extend Bivens to a new context.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Effective March 4, courts may not dismiss this damages-only federal medical-care action for failure to complete the described general BOP grievance process. Seven Justices apply the inquiry that begins with the statute and considers the procedure’s purposes, including the deadline burden and limited factual record; the two Justices concurring in the judgment share only the narrower no-compensation ground. Congress’s express requirements retain their own force, and Carlson’s established channel is preserved without expansion. Future procedures and prospective claims remain distinct.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Scalia, joined by Thomas, agrees with reversal solely because the Bureau’s process offers no monetary relief to a claimant seeking only damages. McNeese v. Board of Education, 373 U.S. 668, and Montana National Bank v. Yellowstone County, 276 U.S. 499, support that effective-remedy ground. They withhold the broader general-exhaustion discussion and its unmodified application to Bivens, and reject treating these reasonable filing periods, with extensions, as a reason to excuse exhaustion.

Dates and procedural background: [503 U.S. 140 (1992), pp. 140–158](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-503/pdf/USREPORTS-503-140.pdf).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court review ends with reversal and remand. The federal damages complaint may proceed without exhaustion of this general grievance process, subject to ordinary adjudication and defenses. No damages amount, deliberate-indifference finding, immunity decision or retained Supreme Court supervision is entered.

**End of entry: McCarthy v. Madigan, merits decision, March 4, 1992.**

## Additional Holdings selection: Holly Farms

#### Holly Farms Corp. v. NLRB, 517 U.S. 392

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-210.
**Decided:** April 23, 1996.

##### Preservation of the primary-agriculture objection

**Holding:** Section 160(e) bars judicial consideration of an objection not urged before the Board, its member, agent, or agency unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. Raising secondary agriculture did not preserve the distinct primary-harvesting theory, and no extraordinary circumstance excuses the omission here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, joined by all seven other Associates; Stone-Zsela joins only the disposition declining consideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether poultry catching can be primary agriculture remains undecided. The preserved secondary-agriculture claim remains reviewable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Woelke & Romero* supplies the agency-preservation requirement and its extraordinary-circumstances qualification.

##### Secondary agriculture and these live-haul occupations

**Holding:** Secondary agriculture requires both a practice performed by a farmer or on a farm and a relationship incident to or in conjunction with the relevant farming operations. A nonfarmer's employees may qualify through the on-farm alternative, but location alone is insufficient; preparation for market and delivery to storage, market, or carriers remain subject to both requirements. Courts independently identify those statutory boundaries before reviewing an unresolved agency construction for permissibility.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court opinion, unanimous.

**Material application:** Ownership of birds, employer identity, and integration within one business are not conclusive. Work may serve farming and processing simultaneously; exclusive farming purpose is not an additional requirement.

##### Classification of these live-haul drivers

**Holding:** The Board lawfully classifies these substantially off-farm live-haul drivers as covered employees. Transport serving Holly's separate processing operation does not become agriculture because the birds were raised on farms, Holly continuously owned them, or the trip begins there.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion, unanimous.

##### Classification of these catchers and forklift operators

**Holding:** The statute does not unambiguously exempt these on-farm catchers and forklift operators. The Board permissibly treats their catching, cooping, and loading as a separately organized removal operation serving processing rather than as incident to or in conjunction with the growers' farming, on its actual, substantially supported integration rationale.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six Justices. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject this application while joining the shared statutory predicates and driver holding.

**Material application:** Independent growers raise the birds to marketable size. Holly separately organizes and supervises removal according to processing shifts, with handling continuing until plant receivers take over. Those facts support the Board's actual rationale; the Labor Department's poultry-preparation examples retain the statutory conditions and do not compel exemption. Substantial evidentiary support remains distinct from judicial statutory interpretation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision mandates no classification for every poultry arrangement, farmer's own employees, other contractors, or mixed-duty drivers, and does not hold that the opposite driver classification on identical facts would also be permissible. It neither makes employer identity conclusive nor declares all post-growing work nonagricultural or a processing plant incapable of being a market. No new proof burden, general remand rule, or *Gissel* standard is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm enforcement within the classification question; the Board's existing order remains effective. No unit reconsideration, bargaining-order remand, election, hearing, or monetary award is ordered. Tyson's separate successorship issue and unrelated unfair-labor-practice issues are not reopened; genuinely remaining matters retain their ordinary lawful channels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Farmers Reservoir* distinguishes farming from a separately organized enterprise serving it while preserving both statutory gateways. *Bayside* rejects attribution of independent growers' farming through processor ownership and supports the driver holding; its on-farm reservation is resolved only for this catcher/loader arrangement. *Chevron* requires independent boundary review before permissible interpretation. *Town & Country* preserves each express exclusion within bounded employee classification; *Health Care* supports functional construction on the agency's actual rationale without importing its supervisory test. *MCI* forbids eliminating a statutory condition; the on-farm alternative remains effective. No precedent is overruled.
