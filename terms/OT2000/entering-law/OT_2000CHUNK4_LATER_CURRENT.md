### Gitlitz v. Commissioner — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Gitlitz_v_Commissioner_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Gitlitz_v_Commissioner_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Gitlitz_v_Commissioner_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. Excluded discharge income passes through and increases basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the Code version governing the 1991 returns, an insolvent S corporation's debt-discharge amount excluded from gross income by section 108(a) remains an item of income under section 1366(a)(1)(A), passes through in each shareholder's pro rata share and increases stock basis under section 1367(a)(1)(A). Losses and deductions are then limited by section 1366(d)(1), using that adjusted stock basis and the shareholder's qualifying debt basis; exclusion remains capped by the corporation's insolvency immediately before discharge and supplies no free-standing deduction entitlement.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those eight directly adopt both holdings and the statutory remand. Breyer dissents from this item's pass-through and resulting basis increase.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 61 identifies discharge of indebtedness as income. Section 108 excludes the qualifying amount from gross income without declaring that the item ceases to be income. Section 1366(a)(1)(A) expressly encompasses tax-exempt income, and section 1367(a)(1)(A) links the basis increase to that category. The Code therefore supplies both the income classification and the adjustment.

Section 108(d)(7)(A), as applicable in 1991, directs that subsections (a), (b) and (g) operate at the corporate level. That direction determines where exclusion and attribute reduction occur; it does not erase the income item or suspend the shareholder provisions. Section 1367(b)(1)'s return-inclusion restriction concerns amounts required to be included in gross income, and does not prohibit this increase for an excluded item that section 1366 recognizes. Bufferd preserves the different statutory functions of corporate and shareholder tax consequences. Hill likewise recognizes distinct statutory accounts, without a general prohibition against multiple tax benefits.

The Commissioner's objection that shareholders receive a benefit without fresh economic outlay cannot add a condition absent from these coordinated provisions. The result is confined to the presented statutory mechanism and version. It neither makes every excluded receipt a basis item nor exempts a claimed deduction from other applicable limits. Here the reported $2,021,296 discharge is less than the corporation's reported $2,181,748 insolvency; the holding determines the legal treatment rather than entering an unsupported final tax amount.

#### II. The discharge-year calculation precedes the prescribed attribute reduction

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 108(b)(4)(A) requires the corporate tax-attribute reductions described in section 108(b)(2) after determination of the tax for the discharge year. For these S corporation returns, that determination includes the pass-through income, basis increase and otherwise allowable shareholder losses; only losses still disallowed under section 1366(d)(1) are treated by section 108(d)(7)(B) as a corporate net operating loss for this attribute-reduction calculation.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Court opinion directly adopts the sequence. Breyer's conditional acceptance of the timing command for an item that properly enters shareholder tax is not a join to this actual item's pass-through, basis or sequence holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Determining discharge-year shareholder tax requires calculating basis and allowable losses. Section 1366(d)(1) expressly uses basis determined with the section 1367(a)(1) income adjustments. Only then can the court identify the losses still disallowed and apply section 108(d)(7)(B). That paragraph retains work when losses exceed properly adjusted basis; it need not suppress the income adjustment to operate.

The Tenth Circuit's reduction-first approach reverses the enacted sequence. Breyer's competing construction instead disputes whether the special corporate exclusion-and-offset mechanism permits this shareholder item at all. The Court rejects that antecedent restriction for the textual reasons in Part I. Once the item enters the calculation, the express after-tax command governs. A perceived double benefit cannot authorize a court to rearrange that command. Atlantic Mutual permits administrative construction within genuine interpretive room, but supplies no power to contradict an express statutory direction or a general revenue-protection override; no applicable authorized regulation identified here independently changes the sequence.

The judgment is reversed and remanded for recomputation of the deficiencies and permitted losses under these rules, followed by the applicable corporate attribute reduction. The reported half shares and claimed losses of $1,010,648 per shareholder are not automatic awards. Ordinary amount, basis and deduction limits remain for application. Later statutory changes, other entities and other tax years lie outside the question presented; no new evidentiary hearing or reopening of settled tax facts is directed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bufferd v. Commissioner: applies the distinction between S corporation and shareholder statutory tax functions; its return-and-limitations holding is not converted into an answer it had already given to debt discharge.
- United States v. Hill: applies its respect for distinct statutory tax accounts and retains its rejection of a general ban on multiple tax benefits; its mineral-basis rule is unchanged.
- Atlantic Mutual Insurance Co. v. Commissioner: preserves deference within actual statutory interpretive room while distinguishing a construction that would contradict the express sequence; no general revenue-protection exception is adopted.
- Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert: preserves the need to give operative statutory and regulatory qualifications independent work; it supplies no authority to replace this Code sequence with a categorical economic-outlay condition.


### Denius v. Dunlap — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling law](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following public holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### Independent legal consultation cannot be burdened by an unjustified employment condition

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may not condition continued employment on a blanket authorization to disclose confidential communications used to obtain independent legal advice without an adequate employment-related justification for that burden. The rule protects access to legal assistance; it does not make every evidentiary privilege a constitutional right or bar lawful investigation and genuinely pertinent, confined inquiries.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the rule and affirm restoration of the equitable claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Button and Trainmen protect the ability to obtain legal assistance. Shelton's employment-disclosure principle and Pickering's employment balance prevent use of a public job to burden protected independent activity without an adequate reason. The Court applies those protections to the actual demand: the attorney clause reaches independent consultation unrelated to Denius's teaching and burdens his future ability to seek advice. No employment reason for that additional access has been established. Loss of employment for refusing the condition supplies a concrete injury even without proof that officials already obtained a confidential communication.

The employer answers that the earlier cases concerned litigation and association, rather than a general civil privilege. The Court does not turn the privilege into an absolute constitutional shield. Confidential consultation enables people to identify and assert their legal rights; a blanket employment condition reaches that protected activity directly. Ordinary privilege rules and independently lawful investigation continue to operate. A privilege exception does not itself justify an unrelated employment demand.

The form's actual terms govern the condition imposed. An uncommunicated intention to conduct only screening already authorized does not confine those terms. The equitable claim therefore returns for adjudication under this rule. This is First Amendment protection of independent legal assistance, not a Sixth Amendment right without an attached criminal proceeding. No final equitable entitlement or compensable injury is established by recognition of the right alone.

#### An employment demand for intimate medical information requires actual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. The court must identify the actual covered information, its intimacy, the governmental need, and the scope, use and safeguards of the demand; the rule creates no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or mandatory-factor test.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this acquisition protection and conditional equitable remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects objectively intimate personal information against unjustified official dissemination. The personal interest can also be burdened when the government demands access as the price of retaining employment. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection. Doe sustained a confined benefits-related inquiry while assuming the asserted right; it did not authorize access detached from that function. The Court now recognizes the bounded acquisition protection necessary to decide this case.

Relevant fitness verification can serve a legitimate employment need. That possibility does not itself justify unlimited access to treatment, family affairs or other unrelated intimate information. The actual connection and protections must support the demand imposed, and undisclosed benign intentions cannot replace them. Conversely, an administrative imperfection does not alone prove a constitutional violation, and every datum need not be indispensable.

The authorization's one-year duration limits time, not the breadth of its subject matter. Its opening language is broad, but medical records are not expressly enumerated. The district court must determine whether the authorization actually reaches them. If it does, the court applies the rule to covered intimate material and the supported employment justification. Coverage, personal responsibility, causation and appropriate relief remain factual or remedial work under the announced law; they do not make recognition of the right provisional.

#### Financial records are protected only to the extent they reveal objectively intimate matters

**Holding and operative rule:** Financial information falls within the protected personal interest only to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. A court cannot establish constitutional coverage from the financial label, the breadth of an authorization, or the owner's preference for secrecy alone; once protected content is identified, the employer's demand is governed by the same actual-need, scope, use and safeguards rule as other intimate information.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices vacate the categorical financial-privacy determination while preserving adjudication of the equitable claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets grounds protection in objectively intimate life, rather than a general right to keep every record secret. That principle supplies the relevant boundary here. An ordinary loan, credit rating or business record is not constitutionally protected merely because it is financial or confidential. The same kind of record may reveal intimate personal or family matters in a particular instance, and its content then requires the justification and protection the Court has described.

Denius has identified no particular protected financial item. The loss of employment makes his challenge concrete, but does not establish coverage of every financial record. The Court of Appeals therefore could not infer a categorical constitutional interest from the authorization's breadth alone. Its determination is vacated at that point, and the equitable claim returns for identification of the information and application of the governing rule. This is an identified content boundary, not a reservation whether objectively intimate financial material can ever be protected. Other undeveloped informational categories and the separate release-of-liability theories were not preserved for this review and are not decided.

#### Conduct-date qualified immunity bars the attorney and financial damages claims

**Holding and operative rule:** An official remains protected from personal damages when all relevant law at the time of the challenged conduct did not give sufficiently particularized objective notice that the conduct violated the asserted right; an identical prior case is unnecessary, but later recognition of a right supplies no retrospective notice. The 1996 law did not sufficiently establish these legal-consultation and intimate-financial acquisition extensions on this authorization, so the judgments granting personal damages immunity on those two claims are affirmed.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all eight Associate Justices adopt the rule and both immunity judgments. Stone-Zsela dissents on these components.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve the objective, particularized notice inquiry. Elder requires independent consideration of all relevant preconduct authority rather than only the cases a party happened to cite below. Crawford-El preserves that legal defense alongside ordinary proof, and Wasson confirms that an unsettled constitutional extension does not become actionable for damages merely because a present court could adopt it.

Button's access-to-counsel cases did not sufficiently establish this general employment-condition application in 1996. The financial precedents likewise did not give adequate notice of the protected reach now identified. Sheets's intimate-publication holding did not automatically decide acquisition or every financial record. Neither the 1997 Doe and Crawford-El decisions nor the present decision can supply 1996 notice.

The Court rejects a requirement that officials prove actual reliance on binding authority directly permitting their conduct. That would replace the objective immunity defense, not apply it. Constitutional violation, contemporaneous notice and injury remain distinct. The retained defense protects these two damages components; it does not justify dismissal of a separately available equitable claim, convert the demand into lawful conduct, or supply immunity to an entity or claim outside its personal-damages scope.

#### The medical damages claim presents a different notice question

**Holding and operative rule:** If the authorization reaches objectively intimate medical information, the existing summary-judgment record does not support personal damages immunity merely because no previous decision addressed this exact form. The cumulative law before the 1996 demand gave notice that an unlimited civilian-medical access condition, unsupported by an articulated countervailing employment purpose, could not be justified solely by public-employer status; medical coverage and genuinely supported factual or independent defenses remain for adjudication.

**Authority:** Part IV of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt this conduct-date notice ground. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh vote for the conditional medical damages remand but does not join this rationale. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** Elder requires consideration of the cumulative conduct-date law. Pesce recognized the constitutional significance of employment-conditioned confidential disclosure while sustaining justified child-abuse reporting. Schaill recognized substantial medical confidentiality while sustaining limited disclosure to an appropriate official for a concrete program need. Anderson v. Romero distinguished the recognized civilian medical interest from the unsettled prisoner claim on which it granted immunity. Considered with Whalen and the then-effective Sheets holding, those decisions gave notice of the no-justification boundary relevant here.

The contrary argument correctly observes that those cases involved different circumstances, often upheld disclosure, and did not decide this unexecuted authorization. Those limits do not erase the distinction between justified, confined medical access and an unlimited demand supported only by the existence of public employment. The Court does not treat the circuit cases as controlling Supreme Court holdings or assert that they adjudicated this form.

Actual medical coverage is disputed, and no personal liability follows before it is established. The undisclosed narrow screening intention cannot supply justification for the broader written condition. A genuinely supported material employment justification, personal causation, injury and other proper defenses remain subject to ordinary proceedings. The judgment restores the medical damages claim for that work; it neither finds a violation after trial nor awards compensation. The rule announced today is not itself the source of historical notice.

#### Precedent treatment

- NAACP v. Button and Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen v. Virginia: extend protection of obtaining legal assistance to this unjustified compulsory employment authorization; ordinary privilege and investigation rules remain distinct.
- Shelton v. Tucker: applies the principle that an employment disclosure condition requires justification for its actual burden on protected independent activity.
- Pickering v. Board of Education: applies the relationship between the employee's protected activity and the employer's actual functional justification.
- Connick v. Myers: preserves contextual public-employment analysis; supplies no categorical permission to demand all independent legal confidences.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applies its requirement that a public employer justify the actual unrelated burden; the present rule does not import a universal higher test for every advance employment policy.
- Waters v. Churchill: preserves its genuine-ground distinction and separate causation and immunity inquiries; an undisclosed intention does not alter the written condition at issue.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County: preserves its intimate-publication holding and objectively intimate-content boundary; develops protection against an unjustified employment demand for access without converting Sheets itself into an earlier acquisition holding.
- Whalen v. Roe: preserves legitimate safeguarded collection; its reservation of possible abuses does not foreclose the bounded acquisition right recognized here.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority: preserves its assumed-right disposition of confined benefits administration; distinguishes that supported function from the unrestricted additional access demanded here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the Court declines to replace it with an actual-authoritative-reliance defense.
- Elder v. Holloway: applies independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date authority, without requiring an identical case or importing later notice.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserves ordinary proof, actual causation and lawful procedural screening alongside immunity; its later date supplies no 1996 notice.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves distinct present constitutional merits and contemporaneous notice; its unresolved attributed-speech question does not decide the different medical-law notice record here.
- Pesce v. J. Sterling Morton High School District 201, Schaill v. Tippecanoe County School Corp., and Anderson v. Romero: considered as preconduct circuit authority for the cumulative civilian-medical notice inference, with their justified-disclosure and prisoner-specific limitations retained; none becomes a controlling Supreme Court precedent by that use.

#### Law after decision

Public employment cannot be conditioned on an unjustified blanket surrender of independent legal consultation. Compulsory access to objectively intimate medical information and comparably intimate content in financial records requires an actual legitimate employment connection and reasonably fitted scope, use and protection. General financial secrecy, a disclosure label and a confidentiality preference do not suffice. Actual coverage and content must be identified.

This decision recognizes the bounded acquisition protection left undecided in Doe and distinguishes it from Sheets's public-dissemination holding. The ordinary conduct-date qualified-immunity defense remains in force. Attorney and financial damages immunity is affirmed, while the medical damages claim returns on the distinct cumulative notice ground and actual-coverage condition. Personal immunity does not eliminate live equitable claims. No fractured-decision question or new mandatory scrutiny formula is created.


### Director of Revenue of Missouri v. CoBank ACB — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Director_of_Revenue_of_Missouri_v_CoBank_ACB_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Director_of_Revenue_of_Missouri_v_CoBank_ACB_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Director_of_Revenue_of_Missouri_v_CoBank_ACB_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The amended cooperative-bank provision preserves state income taxation

**Holding and operative rule:** Banks for cooperatives remain subject to otherwise applicable state income taxation under the Farm Credit Act's entity-specific arrangement after the 1985 removal of the federal-stock-linked exemption. The amendment deleted both the broad institutional exemption and its ownership condition; it did not create a permanent immunity for the bank's operating income, and CoBank cannot obtain the disputed 1991–1994 refunds on that federal-exemption theory.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the complete statutory ground and limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** First Agricultural National Bank requires respect for Congress's particular bank-tax allocation. Section 2134 designates banks for cooperatives and their obligations as federal instrumentalities, but its retained exemption protects issued notes, debentures and other obligations, principal and interest. It excepts surtaxes, estate, inheritance and gift taxes, and expressly subjects interest to federal income tax in the holder's hands. It does not exempt the institution's operating income.

The 1971 law separately exempted the bank, its property, franchises, capital, reserves, surplus, other funds and income only during federal stock ownership. That exemption retained taxation of real and tangible personal property on the same basis as similar property and federal income taxation of obligation interest in the holder's hands. Federal investment was repaid by 1968. In 1985 Congress removed both sentences containing the institutional exemption and ownership condition while replacing the relevant federal stock-purchase mechanism. Reading the whole enacted change, Congress removed a conditional exemption that no longer operated; it did not enlarge that exemption by deleting it. The 1988 holder-interest amendment confirms the retained obligation-specific structure.

Sections 2023 and 2098 expressly exempt the distinct Farm Credit Banks and federal land bank associations, their capital, reserves, surplus and derived income, retaining comparable-value taxation of real estate and federal holder-income taxation for their protected mortgages and obligations. Those differentiated provisions cannot be transferred to this bank category.

CoBank's strongest argument treats present silence about consent as restoring default instrumentality immunity. The amended allocation answers that contention without a universal rule about constitutional immunity, statutory silence or every deletion. Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services distinguished federal identity, substantive tax privileges and the federal forum bar; it left tax merits open. This case properly reaches those merits through the state review system. Independent state tax and refund requirements remain for remand; the Court calculates no liability or refund.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas: preserves its federal-forum holding and express reservation of substantive tax privileges; distinguishes this state-court review of the merits. Instrumentality status, substantive exemption and procedural privilege remain separate.
- First Agricultural National Bank of Berkshire County v. State Tax Commission, 392 U.S. 339: applies its respect for the particular congressional tax allocation; does not extend one category's immunity to every federal bank or decide the broader constitutional question it left open.


### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The particular preservation need and limited execution justify this restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers may temporarily restrict unaccompanied entry into a home while obtaining a warrant when particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual risk of evidence destruction, a restraint reasonably confined to preservation and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization together justify that intrusion. The approximately two-hour restriction here satisfies those conditions in combination; probable cause, refusal of consent, awareness of an investigation, disposable drugs, jailability or duration alone creates no automatic exception or safe harbor.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Stone-Zsela dissents; Stevens would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted and, because the Court reaches the merits, would affirm suppression. Souter's additional concurrence does not limit his full join.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton preserves the home's special warrant protection. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not authorize a greater residential intrusion, and Bond requires an independent basis for an added intrusion despite lawful presence. Edmond addresses suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints; its program-purpose threshold supplies no rule for this individualized preservation measure.

Probable cause was uncontested. McArthur's wife reported seeing him slide drugs under the couch; he knew officers were investigating and had ready access to the identified disposable evidence. These particular circumstances reasonably supported a destruction concern without requiring police to wait for an observed attempt. Refusal to consent supplies neither guilt nor an independent exigency. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another restrained unaccompanied reentry. McArthur made two or three entries for cigarettes and telephone use with observation just inside the doorway. The officers conducted no evidentiary search before the warrant. Their approximately two-hour effort was diligent and the actual restriction reasonably preserved the evidence pending a magistrate's decision.

Place gives duration and diligence independent importance; no fixed two-hour allowance follows. Welsh requires attention to offense gravity. These offenses were modest but jailable: up to thirty days for the marijuana count and one year for paraphernalia. That distinguishes Welsh's civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest setting without making jailability sufficient. The substantial home interest and modest offenses weigh against restraint, but the actual preservation need and limited warrant-directed execution make this measure reasonable.

Segura's independent-source result does not validate every earlier seizure, and its broader Part IV discussion had only two votes. The Court relies on the justification before restraint, not the later discoveries. Limited observational consent is not general search consent or abandonment of the home-access objection. No general power to occupy or search homes, police-created unlawful exigency, requirement to exhaust every imaginable alternative or universal warrant-exception checklist is decided.

#### II. The presented suppression ground cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Illinois appellate judgment is reversed insofar as suppression rests on this temporary restriction of residential access, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings. Any actually preserved independent warrant, admissibility or exclusionary-rule issue remains governed by its own law; none is found established or newly created here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court treated the temporary restraint as an unlawful seizure and suppressed the later warrant evidence on that ground. Because the actual restraint was justified, that ground fails. The Court does not give the later warrant retroactive force, independently resolve every feature of that warrant or determine guilt. Correction of this suppression ground requires further proceedings, not an automatic conviction, dismissal of charges, release, new trial or new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: preserves the home's warrant protection; no general residential-entry authority is created.
- Whren v. United States: preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis and independent limits on greater intrusion, scope and duration; traffic probable cause is not residential authority.
- Bond v. United States: applies the requirement of independent justification for an added intrusion; lawful presence and limited observation do not imply consent to search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond: distinguishes suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints from this individualized preservation measure; its program-purpose holding remains intact.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: applies attention to actual duration and diligence in temporary preservation; no fixed permissible interval is transferred from luggage to a home.
- Welsh v. Wisconsin, 466 U.S. 740: preserves the importance of offense gravity and home protection; distinguishes its civil, nonjailable nighttime arrest without making every jailable offense sufficient for restraint.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796: preserves its independent-source holding while declining to treat it as approval of every initial seizure; the broader securing-premises discussion in Part IV remains a two-Justice position, not a majority holding.


### Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield — 2001-02-27

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Freedom_From_Religion_Foundation_v_City_of_Marshfield_merits_2001-02-27.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Freedom_From_Religion_Foundation_v_City_of_Marshfield_merits_2001-02-27.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. A genuine transfer must end the City's continuing religious function

**Holding and operative rule:** A bona fide sale ends public ownership but does not cure an establishment violation if the municipality continues acts that present the religious message as its own. The City's continued operation of its surrounding park as the undifferentiated setting for its historical religious centerpiece, together with the retained public-park dedication and ineffective separation, preserves that municipal presentation here despite genuine private ownership, maintenance and lighting.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six directly adopt both this application and the remedy in Holding II.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and its function. It retains Lemon through a unified inquiry: governing specific prohibitions and distinct doctrines apply first, established permissible relationships retain their scope, and residual questions turn on actual attribution and function. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that inquiry; independent former prongs do not supply a different test.

The act here is the City's continuing presentation of its own park as an integrated setting for this religious centerpiece. The parties' stipulation establishes that the Fund, not the City, maintains and lights its parcel. The fair-price, lawful sale is real; the public-use covenant has not been shown to have been enforced. Those facts materially favor private attribution. They do not resolve the separate significance of the City's continued surrounding presentation, retained park dedication and lack of intelligible differentiation. No single feature, including the covenant, establishes the violation by itself.

Pinette and Chabad protect genuinely private expression and reject attribution from religious content or location alone. This centered municipal setting has not been established as their generally available forum. Robinson distinguishes the City's own institutional presentation from private expression; Brentwood likewise demands actual governmental responsibility rather than a label. The Court identifies continuing City conduct, not an observer's offense, dislike or possible mistake. The dissent gives the private-operation facts dispositive force; that overlooks the public function that the City retains around the transferred parcel. The genuine transfer therefore does not moot the challenge to that remaining function.

#### II. Corrective relief must end municipal sponsorship while preserving private expression

**Holding and operative rule:** The remand must secure effective cessation of the identified municipal religious presentation through operational and intelligible separation, using clarification or boundary measures on land the City controls or another lawful means. Relief must preserve the genuine conveyance and the Fund's private expression and property, and cannot require destruction, relocation or alteration of private speech merely to prevent possible misunderstanding.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly join the Court's remedial holding and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini ties the cure to the governmental function that violates the Constitution; Pinette protects the private religious expression that remains outside that function. The existing disclaimer is on Fund property and the record establishes no completed separation. The lower court may require accurate boundaries and clear attribution on municipal land, but cannot assume power to install a replacement sign on the Fund's parcel or compel the Fund to change its message. A disclaimer succeeds only when the City's actual presentation supports it.

The final design, placement and lawful treatment of the retained-use arrangement require site and property details for implementation. Those are remedial tasks under the announced rule, not a reopening of the stipulated private maintenance and lighting. No particular fence, cancellation of the covenant, physical removal, relocation or destruction is necessary as a categorical condition. An equally effective lawful separation may suffice. The Court affirms corrective proceedings under that standard rather than endorsing the lower court's independent endorsement prong or a universal two-condition rule for all divestitures.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Agostini v. Felton: applies the unified governmental-act and function inquiry to an unresolved monument divestiture; its distinct doctrines and established permissible relationships retain their actual scope.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: remains governing as modified by Agostini; its former independent prongs are not revived.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: preserves genuine private religious access and limits the cure; neither categorical forum immunity nor attribution from proximity follows.
- Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller: preserves contextual private-expression protection; interior or adjacent public location alone is insufficient.
- Robinson v. City of Edmond: applies the distinction between official institutional presentation and private expression without converting its seal holding into a categorical monument rule.
- Brentwood Academy: reinforces examination of actual governmental responsibility; the athletic-association application supplies no automatic rule for adjoining private land.

### Gitlitz v. Commissioner — 2001-01-09

Breyer dissents. He reads section 108(d)(7)(A)'s corporate-level direction together with paragraph (B)'s capture of disallowed shareholder losses as a specific exclusion-and-offset mechanism. In his view, excluded corporate discharge should not first become shareholder income that releases the very losses designated for corporate reduction. His Hubert dissent emphasizes the coordinated scheme's economic operation rather than a bookkeeping label, and his O'Gilvie and Atlantic Mutual positions require attention to the receipt's actual statutory treatment.

He accepts that discharge excluded from gross income can remain income in a general sense. He also recognizes the after-tax timing command for an item that validly enters shareholder tax. The taxpayers' broad income language and sequencing argument therefore do not, in his view, answer the antecedent question whether this particular corporate exclusion permits the shareholder item. He would deny the basis increase and affirm on that statutory ground. The resulting benefit to a solvent shareholder reinforces his construction; it is not an independent policy veto, a universal reduction-first rule, an allegation of misconduct or a general prohibition of multiple tax benefits.

### Denius v. Dunlap — 2001-01-17

[Published writings](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#separate-writings); noncontrolling except for expressly identified joins to the Court's opinion.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part, concurring in the judgment in part, and dissenting in part.** He joins the legal-consultation and intimate-information holdings and their equitable remedies. He agrees that Button and Trainmen protect independent legal assistance, that the actual written condition governs, and that objectively intimate medical and financial content requires a justified and suitably confined governmental demand. These grounds do not constitutionalize ordinary financial secrecy or dispense with proof of coverage and injury.

He would replace the broader clearly-established-law defense with a narrow defense requiring defendants to prove genuine, objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the demand. Legal silence and routine supervisory power would not suffice. Justified factual judgment belongs in the constitutional merits analysis; uncertainty in precedent should not independently defeat compensation for a proved deprivation. Authoritative legal permission would protect actual, reasonable reliance without immunizing conduct merely because no earlier decision used the same authorization form. This changes the defense preserved by Elder, Crawford-El and Wasson; it does not claim the new acquisition rule was established in 1996.

He would therefore reverse attorney damages immunity, affirm the medical damages remand on his distinct defense rule, and vacate financial damages immunity insofar as it forecloses a qualifying intimate-information claim solely for lack of a previously specific decision. The record identifies no binding permission for the unrestricted demand. Remand would permit a properly supported reliance defense and adjudication of actual scope, participation, justification, protected content, causation and injury. His position awards no damages or reinstatement and does not affect independently required live-equitable-relief conditions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the attorney and financial damages-immunity holding. They reject the First Amendment extension because Button and Trainmen protect litigation and expressive association rather than a general constitutional guarantee covering individual civil legal confidences. The absence of an identified constitutional source is not cured by the demand's poor justification or by treating every evidentiary privilege as protected activity. They accept the employment injury and the distinction between immunity and equitable relief.

They also reject extending substantive due process from Sheets's actual public-dissemination holding to compulsory access or authorization. Their position, reflected in the Sheets dissent and Doe concurrence, requires a demonstrated constitutional source for the new acquisition guarantee; intimate subject matter and state confidentiality expectations do not alone supply one. They accept Sheets as binding within its decided scope and do not treat information held by a third party as necessarily public.

Independently, they would restore medical damages immunity. Pesce and Schaill upheld justified disclosures in different settings, and Anderson v. Romero distinguished other interests while granting immunity. Those sources did not sufficiently establish the asserted application to an unexecuted authorization with disputed medical coverage. They would reject the three constitutional equitable claims on the grounds stated and retain damages immunity for each category. Their position makes no finding that the demand was wise or that a state-law remedy displaces a proved federal right.


### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

**Souter concurs and joins the Court opinion in full.** Exigency and permissible scope depend on the risk at the time of the proposed action. In his assessment, while McArthur was inside with immediate access to the hidden drugs, the actual destruction risk could have justified prompt entry and search. Once McArthur stepped outside and police could control access, the same immediate necessity for a greater warrantless evidentiary intrusion was absent. Temporary control of reentry while pursuing a warrant respects the preference for a magistrate's authorization and affords a reasonable opportunity to secure it. Police need not let an occupant return simply to recreate the emergency that would support a greater intrusion. This analysis distinguishes the risk and available response at each point; it does not find that a greater search occurred, infer an unreported occupant or turn the later discoveries into the earlier justification. The conditional greater-intrusion view belongs to his concurrence and is not an additional Court holding.

**Stone-Zsela dissents.** Control of access to a home is a seizure requiring an adequate warrant exception. The wife's contemporaneous observation of concealment strengthens probable cause and bears on risk, but hiding an existing stash does not itself show imminent destruction. McArthur could see his wife confer with police and leave with an officer; those facts support anticipation of a search, but add no particularized urgency beyond that awareness and the disposable character of drugs. Treating that combination as sufficient would authorize a similar restraint in nearly every residential drug investigation. An actual destruction attempt is not invariably required, but the inference must concern imminent loss rather than probable cause alone.

The two-hour duration and accompanied entries reduce the burden but cannot supply the missing exception. Segura does not validate every initial seizure because a later warrant produces evidence. Welsh gives the modest offenses real weight without misclassifying them as nonjailable. Contextual reasonableness evaluates a demonstrated exception; it cannot replace the warrant boundary. Stone-Zsela would affirm suppression for the search resulting from the unsupported restraint, subject to any independently preserved exclusionary-rule issue actually left unresolved. He would not invalidate the later warrant on an unpresented ground or dismiss independently supported charges.

**Stevens dissents.** He first would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because these slight possession offenses make an unsuitable occasion to define the limits of restraints on residential possession. Because the Court decides the merits, he would affirm. Welsh requires serious attention to offense gravity, and his understanding of Segura gives deprivation of home possession independent significance despite an eventual warrant. Even accepting probable cause, diligent warrant pursuit, accompanied entries and the comparatively limited restraint, he concludes that the substantial interest in private use of the home outweighs this modest preservation interest. His position concerns these facts; it neither denies that the offenses permit imprisonment nor categorically prohibits every misdemeanor preservation measure. He does not demand an observed destruction attempt in every case.

### Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield — 2001-02-27

**Justice Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The genuine conveyance, actual private maintenance and lighting, and absence of asserted covenant enforcement establish private operation on this stipulated record. Pinette and Chabad protect that expression; Agostini and Brentwood require a governmental act, not an association drawn from visual continuity or ordinary public access. Robinson concerned an actual official emblem, which this privately operated parcel is not. The dissent regards the surrounding public park's continued operation as insufficient to turn the Fund's message into the City's. It would reverse and restore defense summary judgment without a new factual hearing or disclaimer prerequisite. Its conclusion does not protect a future sham transfer, actual governmental adoption or enforcement directing religious operation.

### Seling v. Young — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. Civil designation does not foreclose proof of actual punishment

**Holding and operative rule:** A court first identifies whether the legislature intended a civil or criminal commitment scheme; civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that the challenged commitment's purpose or effect is punitive. Actual design and operation must be assessed cumulatively, including contrary civil evidence: a civil label is not conclusive, but confinement, connection to prior crime, indeterminate duration, criminal-style safeguards, deficient treatment or an individual treatment failure alone establishes neither punishment nor a right to release.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt both holdings, the specified limits and the scoped affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Hendricks permits proof of punitive operation in a nominally civil commitment and invalidated the demonstrated retrospective application there. Hudson preserves design-and-operation review and supplies no universal rule confining the inquiry to statutory wording. The Court must therefore distinguish an inadequately administered but genuinely civil program from custody that actually imposes punishment.

The superintendent's concern that day-to-day conditions should not transform a scheme's classification supports a demanding burden and careful remedial distinctions. It cannot make proved punitive operation legally irrelevant. Clinical testimony, treatment records and plans, staff and patient accounts, confinement practices, program design and other reliable direct or circumstantial evidence can bear on the cumulative question. No statistical study or successful cure is required, and officials cannot defeat strong combined proof merely by isolating every circumstance. Conversely, employee misconduct or poor care alone does not establish that the custody is punishment.

Young's allegations concerning security, confinement, abuse, staffing, therapy and practical release remain allegations. The separate Turay injunction and reported progress are evidence within their actual scope, not conclusive proof of punishment, compliance or cure of an earlier unlawful application. Statutory examination, review and release mechanisms are relevant civil evidence, but their existence does not prove their operation for Young. The Court neither finds punishment nor rewrites those mechanisms. The supported claim requires adjudication under the governing burden, rather than categorical foreclosure by the civil label.

#### II. The existing punishment remand stands, with distinct constitutional and remedial predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's reviewed punishment-remand portion is affirmed, preserving the opportunity to establish Young's supported claim under the former chapter 153 habeas framework applicable to his 1994 application. If the clearest proof establishes punishment and the challenged custody retroactively adds it for conduct predating enactment, custody under that unlawful application must end through appropriate habeas relief, while independent lawful custody is preserved; a conditions violation that leaves custody lawfully civil instead requires relief through its proper conditions channel.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion adopts the remand, temporal rule and remedy distinction. The separate due-process, equal-protection and commitment-procedure rulings Young did not seek to review remain undisturbed.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh's application-date rule leaves this pre-April 24, 1996 application under former chapter 153 law; amended section 2254(d) does not govern its merits. Separately directed provisions and ordinary habeas requirements retain their operation. The Ninth Circuit already allowed an opportunity to prove punitive operation by the clearest proof. That disposition is proper under Hendricks and Hudson, so correcting the account of governing precedent requires instructions rather than vacatur of the opportunity itself. The remand creates no universal entitlement to an evidentiary hearing and no present writ or release order.

Punishment and retrospectivity require separate findings. The existence of a conditions remedy cannot defeat a proved unlawful-custody claim; conversely, conditions defects do not automatically require release. The court must consider the supported record and material genuine factual disputes under the former habeas rules. No replacement confinement authority is supplied.

Double Jeopardy remains distinct. A punitive character alone proves neither the relevant prior jeopardy nor that the challenged confinement is a second punishment for the same offense. Those matters require separate determination under the governing attachment and offense-identity law if the preserved claim remains necessary after the punishment and retrospective-custody findings. Hendricks did not decide them. The Court establishes no independent double-jeopardy entitlement here and need not add a second ground if Ex Post Facto relief fully resolves the custody claim. The remaining punishment, chronology, same-offense and custody determinations are adjudicative work under the stated rules, not permission to treat a civil label or shared past misconduct as conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kansas v. Hendricks: applies its rebuttable civil-intent and clearest-proof framework, cumulative operational inquiry and retrospective-punishment consequence; its particular Kansas facts are not transferred to Young.
- Hudson v. United States: applies its preserved design-and-operation inquiry; it did not replace actual-operation review with a statutory-face-only rule or overrule Halper in its personal-monetary-penalty field.
- United States v. Ursery: preserves the demanding rebuttal of civil classification without transferring its forfeiture outcome to personal confinement.
- United States v. Ward and Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez: preserve the intent-and-effect inquiry and cumulative use of punitive indicators, without a conclusive single factor or new exhaustive checklist.
- Lindh v. Murphy: applies the pre-enactment application rule, preserving separately directed provisions rather than exempting every habeas issue from every amendment.
