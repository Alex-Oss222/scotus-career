## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under §242

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Lanier — Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted, 1997-03-31; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies conduct-time notice and the limit on reliance on bad purpose
- United States v. Lanier — Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition supplies the permissible legal landscape
- United States v. Lanier — Obvious applications need no factual twin, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition rejects a factual-twin requirement while preserving unsettled boundaries
- United States v. Lanier — The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition identifies the shared clarity protection without merging criminal liability

**Present operation effective:** Lanier, 1997-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Willful bad purpose cannot cure an unknowable constitutional prohibition. The Government must still establish constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness and every other element under the criminal burden. Due-process rights and direct official assaults outside custody are not categorically excluded, but no particular count's merits or notice is predetermined.

**Related current rules:** Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review retains its civil review, timing and preservation mechanics.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lanier (1997-03-31) — Criminal Procedure / Criminal civil-rights liability and fair warning.

## Standards and Tests selection: Retrospective reduction of the legally required quantum of proof

### Retrospective reduction of the legally required quantum of proof

**Governing question:** Whether an offense-date evidentiary sufficiency requirement may be removed for earlier conduct.

**Current rule:** A State may not retrospectively remove a legal requirement of additional evidence where already admissible testimony was insufficient to convict without that evidence unless an age or timely-report condition applied. Continuing to instruct on reasonable doubt does not cure this reduction in the legally required quantum of proof. Ordinary witness-competence, admissibility and procedural reforms remain permissible when they leave legal sufficiency unchanged; the rule creates no freestanding constitutional corroboration requirement.

**Authority by component:**

- Binding sufficiency requirement distinguished from competence and admissibility: Carmell v. Texas — A State may not retrospectively reduce the evidence legally required for conviction, May 1, 2000; Stevens joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Carmell, May 1, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** The prohibition does not depend on proof of innocence or prediction of a different verdict. Under the former Texas Article 38.07 rule applied in Carmell, the existing trial record must satisfy the restored sufficiency requirement; if it does not, the affected count requires acquittal rather than a further opportunity to supply missing prosecution evidence. That consequence follows the sufficiency ground, not every change in an evidentiary rule.

## Matter and effective-law boundary

Rogers v. Tennessee; decision May 14, 2001; notice measured May 6, 1994. Direct review, not an AEDPA petition.

## Earlier published decision: United States v. Lanier

### United States v. Lanier, No. 95-1717

Merits decision, March 31, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 7, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which vacated convictions under 18 U.S.C. § 242 using a rule requiring a Supreme Court precedent involving fundamentally similar facts. The question is the fair-warning standard governing the charged official deprivations, not the ultimate validity of every count.

#### Judgment

**Vacate the en banc Sixth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court does not automatically reinstate the convictions or sentence. Conduct-time notice and any remaining properly presented issues require count-specific appellate review under the governing law.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The unanimous opinion controls every holding and qualification below.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

##### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under § 242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

##### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

##### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

##### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from § 242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved § 242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

#### Law After Decision

Section 242 has no Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite. Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.

#### Source Notes

The granted standard question and en banc disposition support the limited return. No unreviewed trial account is adopted as a finding on an individual count. [Case and procedural source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-1717.ZS.html).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit must apply the proper conduct-time fair-warning rule and consider remaining properly presented matters count by count. Convictions and sentence are not automatically reinstated; no acquittal, retrial or new evidentiary hearing is ordered by this mandate.

**End of entry: United States v. Lanier, merits decision, March 31, 1997.**

### Carmell v. Texas, No. 98-7540

Plenary merits decision, May 1, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 30, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Texas Court of Appeals, Second District. The question is whether applying the 1993 amendment to Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Article 38.07 to earlier offenses violates the Ex Post Facto Clause by reducing the evidence legally sufficient to convict.

The indictment contained fifteen sexual-offense counts involving conduct from February 1991 through March 1995. Counts 7–10 concern conduct from June 1992 through July 1993, when the complainant was fourteen or fifteen. Before September 1, 1993, uncorroborated testimony permitted conviction if the complainant informed a person other than the defendant within six months; the reporting requirement did not apply when the complainant was younger than fourteen at the offense. The amendment extended that exception to complainants younger than eighteen and the reporting period to one year. The March 1995 report met neither period for the challenged counts.

The state appellate court sustained retrospective application while assuming absence of corroboration for its legal ruling. Texas preserves its contention that the trial record contained corroboration. The six counts involving conduct while the complainant was under fourteen and five post-amendment counts, including the life-sentence counts, are outside this challenge.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded as to counts 7–10, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg.

The State's preserved contention that corroborating evidence existed remains for determination under the former law. If the existing proof fails that law's sufficiency requirement on an affected count, the consequence is acquittal on that count, not a new opportunity to supply deficient prosecution proof. The Court does not order immediate acquittal on an assumed absence of corroboration. The other eleven counts remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, including the distinction between proof sufficiency and admissibility and the existing-record remedy. O'Connor dissented, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg. The dissent would affirm rejection of the federal challenge on its witness-specific credibility classification, without a categorical exemption for procedural rules.

#### Holdings

##### A State may not retrospectively reduce the evidence legally required for conviction

**Holding and operative rule:** When the offense-date law makes already admissible testimony legally insufficient to convict without additional evidence unless its stated age or timely-report condition is satisfied, a later law may not retrospectively remove that additional-proof requirement. Ordinary changes in witness competence, admissibility or trial procedure remain permissible when they do not reduce the legal sufficiency required for conviction.

**Authority:** Stevens's six-Justice Opinion of the Court, with the full joins stated above, controls both holdings and their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this amendment changes the proof the law requires, not merely the means of presenting proof. Calder v. Bull identifies retrospective reduction of the evidence required to convict as a distinct forbidden category. Collins v. Youngblood preserves that category while rejecting a general rule against every loss of a substantial procedural protection. Hopt v. Utah permits removal of a witness's incompetency because the amount or degree of proof required remains unchanged. Thompson v. Missouri permits additional handwriting-comparison evidence without directing that a formerly deficient evidentiary basis is sufficient.

Here the complainant could testify under either law. For counts 7–10, neither the former under-fourteen exception nor the six-month reporting condition applied; the March 1995 report also fell outside the amended one-year period. The expanded under-eighteen exception therefore permits her testimony alone to support conviction where the former law required corroboration. The continued reasonable-doubt instruction does not answer that distinct reduction in legally required evidence. A witness-specific reliability purpose likewise does not eliminate the former condition's binding sufficiency operation. The Court does not require proof of innocence, prediction of a different jury verdict or distrust of this complainant. Nor does it constitutionalize corroboration independently of the offense-date law or restore the broader procedural theories rejected by Collins.

##### The preserved corroboration question must be decided under the former law

**Holding and operative rule:** The constitutional ruling sustaining retrospective application to counts 7–10 must be reversed, and Texas's preserved corroboration contention must be determined on the existing trial record under former Article 38.07. Failure of that record to satisfy the former sufficiency requirement requires acquittal on the affected count; the remand does not authorize a new opportunity to supply missing prosecution evidence.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court assumed an absence of corroboration to decide the constitutional issue; it did not finally resolve Texas's contention that corroboration existed. The Court cannot turn that assumption into a finding for either side. Its constitutional holding restores the former legal condition, leaving the actual record to be measured against it. The accepted state-law consequence of noncompliance is evidentiary insufficiency and acquittal, rather than a mere admission error permitting the prosecution to repair its proof. The Court therefore neither orders immediate acquittal nor directs an unconditional new trial. The eleven unchallenged counts, including those supporting life sentences, supply no reason to deny review of these four convictions and receive no relief from this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Calder v. Bull, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 386, supplies the evidence-sufficiency category as retained and bounded by the later controlling cases; it does not freeze every evidentiary rule.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37, preserves that category and the rejection of a free-standing substantial-procedural-protection theory. Its overruling of Kring and Thompson v. Utah remains intact.
- Hopt v. Utah, 110 U.S. 574, is distinguished because removal of witness incompetency did not lessen the amount or degree of proof essential to conviction; its competence holding remains in force.
- Thompson v. Missouri, 171 U.S. 380, is distinguished because admission of handwriting exemplars added information for the jury without reducing the proof legally required. Its admissibility holding remains in force, subject to Collins's treatment of the older procedural formulations it cited.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, California Department of Corrections v. Morales and Garner v. Jones retain their separate rules governing meaningful parole-consideration opportunities. They supply neither a trial-proof probability test nor a new burden allocation here.

#### Law After Decision

The Ex Post Facto Clause protects the offense-date legal quantum of proof as well as the other retained Calder categories. Retrospective removal of this corroboration requirement crosses that boundary when the former age and reporting conditions are unsatisfied. The decision leaves ordinary competence and admissibility reforms available, creates no universal corroboration requirement and alters no parole doctrine. No precedent is overruled.

#### Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg, dissents. She would affirm because the age and reporting conditions regulate the credibility of a particular class of testimony, leaving the charged acts, lawful punishment and ultimate burden of persuasion unchanged. Hopt permits a formerly disqualified witness to supply evidence for an earlier offense; Thompson permits formerly excluded comparison evidence. For the dissent, removal of this special credibility disability has the same constitutional function. The State's sufficiency terminology and acquittal remedy show the rule's importance but do not alone determine its federal character: permissible evidence restrictions can also decide whether a prosecution succeeds.

The dissent does not exempt every rule called procedural or abolish Calder's evidence category. A true reduction in the required proof or burden remains distinct. It disagrees with the Court's classification of this age-and-report rule, makes no finding that corroboration actually existed, and does not treat the unchallenged sentences as eliminating the controversy. Its position would defeat the presented federal challenge without deciding unpresented state-law claims.

#### Source Notes

The [official reported record, 529 U.S. 513, 516–521 and notes 1–4](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-513.pdf), and its [published text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/98-7540.ZO.html), support the count, age, reporting and disputed-corroboration history. The state appellate court's assumption is not a final finding that corroboration was absent. The four-count review does not affect the eleven other convictions.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Texas Court of Appeals for determination of the preserved corroboration contention from the existing record under the former rule. If an affected count lacks the legally required proof, the state sufficiency consequence applies. No release from the untouched sentences, automatic retrial or new evidence-gathering order is directed. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

**End of entry: Carmell v. Texas, plenary merits decision, May 1, 2000.**

### Fiore v. White — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The existing missing element defeats both challenged convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not sustain a conviction when its authoritative account of the law in force at the relevant time identifies a necessary element and the State concedes it produced no evidence of that element. Pennsylvania's clarification that actual absence of the required permit was always an element of the owner/operator offense therefore establishes a due-process violation in both of Fiore's challenged no-permit convictions; deviation from a permit's terms cannot substitute for its absence.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both holdings and the bounded reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** In re Winship requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every element. Jackson v. Virginia enforces that protection against a conviction unsupported on a necessary element. State law identifies the offense; the federal Constitution requires its elements to be proved. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's August 21, 2000 answer establishes that Commonwealth v. Scarpone clarified the statute's existing meaning, including its meaning when Fiore's convictions became final. The Commonwealth does not dispute that Fiore possessed a permit. The asserted departure from its conditions thus cannot establish the different no-permit element.

The Third Circuit's premise that a new state rule was unavailable on collateral review cannot survive the authoritative answer. The Court decides no general obligation to apply genuinely new state constructions retroactively: there is no new construction here. Nor can the seriousness of other environmental misconduct supply the missing element. The distinct authorization, transportation-license and permit-compliance provisions do not become substitute offenses in this dispute. The ruling covers the no-permit conviction from each trial, not the other convictions among Fiore's sixty convictions.

#### II. The constitutional ruling and the conditions for the writ remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's rejection of the constitutional claim is reversed and the case remanded to resolve any properly preserved, actually undecided statutory prerequisite to habeas relief under the applicable post-April 24, 1996 regime. If those prerequisites permit relief, the habeas order must remove both invalid no-permit convictions and end custody attributable to them, preserving independent convictions and lawful custody.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion directly adopts this remand and the following statutory conditions; the Court makes no finding that an unresolved gate is satisfied or violated.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh v. Murphy distinguishes applications filed before enactment from later applications; Fiore's later filing is not exempt from the applicable amendments. Under section 2254(b), relief requires exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Merits denial despite nonexhaustion is permitted, not required; the State waives exhaustion only through counsel's express waiver. The appellate court expressly left exhaustion and state merits adjudication undecided.

If the relevant claim was actually adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, section 2254(d) requires the statutory inquiry. Williams v. Taylor preserves the independent contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses, the objective legal standard and the restriction to this Court's holdings at the actual state merits decision. Winship and Jackson are preexisting rules, not rules originating in today's decision. The separate statutory factual-review provisions retain their own triggers and force. A finding of constitutional error cannot silently resolve those questions; conversely, remand supplies no permission to reopen the conceded permit fact, the state court's authoritative answer or the decided constitutional issue. The court below must resolve a real preserved prerequisite and implement the resulting conviction-specific relief, rather than infer an unconditional all-count writ.

**Precedent treatment:**

- In re Winship: applies its proof-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt requirement to an existing state-law element; the seriousness of other conduct cannot supply the omitted proof.
- Jackson v. Virginia: applies its protection against conviction without evidence sufficient to establish each necessary element.
- Fiore v. White, November 30, 1999: completes the reserved federal inquiry after the authoritative state answer; the earlier certification itself supplied neither state-law meaning nor a federal merits judgment.
- Lindh v. Murphy: preserves its filing-date distinction; this post-enactment application falls outside its pre-enactment exemption.
- Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams): applies its independent statutory clauses and objective, date-bounded Supreme Court-law inquiry if an actual state merits adjudication triggers section 2254(d); no exclusive allocation between the clauses is created.

### Fiore v. White — 2001-01-09

Stone-Zsela concurs in the entire opinion and judgment. The acknowledged permit, authoritative answer and Commonwealth's concession establish the decisive constitutional facts without another round of proof. Once the lower court resolves an actually preserved habeas prerequisite, relief should promptly end custody attributable to the two invalid no-permit convictions while preserving independent lawful custody. He would not permit a retrial that merely relabels the same permit deviation as absence of the permit the Commonwealth concedes existed. That position concerns this conceded missing-permit charge; it does not decide unrelated environmental offenses or a general question of retrial law.

Authority: [published separate writings](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#separate-writings); [opinion topology](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#opinion-topology). These positions do not enlarge the controlling holdings.
