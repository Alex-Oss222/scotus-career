#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

#### Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-6322.
**Decided:** April 26, 2000.

##### I. The judicial certificate denial is reviewable

**Holding:** A court of appeals' judicial denial of appellate certification is a case reviewable by certiorari under section 1254(1), whether the certificate was correctly or incorrectly required. That review does not itself issue a certificate or authorize the underlying merits appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial certificate review is distinct from review of a circuit authorization determination concerning a successive district application. The latter restriction is unchanged.

##### II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate

**Holding:** Amended section 2253(c) governs an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even when the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier habeas application. Slack's April 29, 1998 notice therefore invokes the certificate-of-appealability requirement; the prior CPC label and the State's earlier contrary legal position do not dispense with that required authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with its author, Ginsburg: five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with applying that regime but joins no Part II rationale. Stevens, Souter and Breyer would retain former CPC treatment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The appellate-date rule does not apply amended section 2254(d) to the pre-enactment district application or establish a universal claim-by-claim transition rule. A mislabeled CPC request or notice may be treated as a request under Rule 22; neither is an issued certificate. The State's legal concession cannot supply the required authorization.

##### III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings

**Holding:** When a district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate of appealability requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and about whether the procedural ruling was correct. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent component first, and any certificate must identify the qualifying issue or issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has nine direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens, Souter and Breyer adopt this interpretation conditionally while maintaining that former CPC governs this appeal; their Part III agreement does not enlarge the Part II coalition.

**Material application:** Reasonable debate requires more than a nonfrivolous assertion but not final proof of success. Where the district court has actually rejected constitutional merits, reasonable jurists must be able to debate that assessment or find it wrong. A plain correctly applied procedural bar defeats certification without an unnecessary constitutional inquiry; the constitutional component may instead be considered first when more apparent.

##### IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds

**Holding:** When an initial federal application is dismissed without prejudice solely to permit completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows that filing or adds grounds. Rule 9(b)'s new-ground abuse branch does not supply the missing second-or-successive predicate in this exhaustion-return setting; the contrary classification here is rejected, while actual independent default, exhaustion, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, making seven direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal with remand. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this classification and disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9(a)'s distinct delay provision permits dismissal where filing delay prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. No such prejudice or diligence finding is made here. Rule 9(b)'s dismissal permission is not an automatic command, and former section 2244(b)'s exception to its prior-merits permission requires both an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; that prior-merits predicate is absent here. No free-floating equitable sanction replaces an applicable source's conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not cover every nonmerits dismissal, immunize deliberate withholding under an actually applicable rule, authorize unlimited amendment or excuse independent state default, exhaustion or timing requirements. Amended section 2244 retains its own applicability and classification conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit certificate denial is reversed for certification review with the successive classification rejected. The abuse dismissals of grounds 2, 4(c), 5, 6 and 7 cannot stand on that classification; their separate exhaustion objections remain unresolved. Ground 3's whole-action nonexhaustion dismissal and the merits of grounds 1, 4(a) and 4(b) are not adjudicated. No certificate, writ, hearing, automatic reopening or limitations reset issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hohn is applied to judicial review of the certificate denial and the distinction between review, certification and relief. Its reserved transition issue is resolved here only at the scope of Part II.
- Felker retains its bar on review of actual successive-application authorization and its distinct original-habeas rules; this judicial certificate denial is outside that authorization determination.
- Lindh retains pre-enactment treatment of its identified district applications. The present appellate requirement does not apply amended section 2254(d) merely because an appeal is later.
- Carriger retains its specified capital district-law extension; it establishes no universal timing rule for every habeas provision.
- Barefoot supplies the substantial-showing and reasonable-debate background; amended section 2253 requires the constitutional component and issue identification stated here.
- Peguero is distinguished because an authorized judge had actually issued a certificate there; neither its existing-authorization rule nor this judgment creates one here.
- Rose is applied to the first federal merits opportunity following complete-exhaustion procedure; exhaustion itself remains required on its actual terms.
- Stewart is used as a limited first-review analogy, preserving its presented, premature and expressly reserved Ford-claim boundary. The additional-claim exhaustion-return rule is adopted here, not attributed to that earlier holding.
- Lonchar is applied to require actual statutory or Rule predicates for dismissal; no general equitable disapproval or automatic entitlement to a hearing replaces them.
- Sanders and McCleskey retain their structured abuse inquiries. Their pleading and excuse principles do not supply the missing successive category for this exhaustion return.
- Weeks retains independent statutory eligibility, constitutional claim and relief inquiries; a successful procedural challenge alone grants no writ.
- Michael Wayne Williams retains applicant-attributed failure and actual hearing prerequisites; an undeveloped record here does not itself trigger a hearing bar or compel a hearing.
- Terry Williams retains only its adopted statutory propositions, separate constitutional entitlement and remedy. Neither four-Justice exclusive clause construction is imported, and its post-enactment application does not change Slack's district-law temporal premise.
- Edwards retains the actual adequate, independent and properly applied state-default condition for an assistance claim used as cause, with its recognized excuses. It establishes no Nevada default and does not equate federal successive classification with state default.

#### Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1474.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

##### A disputed federal tribal title question does not create a sovereign-lands exception to prospective officer relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a claim for prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officials actually connected to an alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights merely because adjudicating that violation requires resolving a disputed federal title question or affects important sovereign interests. The decree must address proved unlawful future official conduct and may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or require the State itself to convey title.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Material application:** The pleaded land-regulating officials have a concrete connection to the alleged interference; a general law-obedience duty would not suffice. The actual request for exclusive use and exclusion of regulation is acknowledged, but the Ninth Circuit withheld a decree binding Idaho’s title and the case remains at the pleading stage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither generalized state-interest balancing nor a special sovereign-lands exception bars the route. The Tribe must establish the federal right, ongoing violation and lawful remedy. No ownership, navigability, prestatehood disposition or separate United States action is adjudicated; no general Article I abrogation rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm permission for the prospective-officer claims, 5–4; leave the State, agency and direct quiet-title or retrospective-relief dismissals undisturbed, 9–0. Further merits and tailored-remedy proceedings are permitted, without automatic injunction or property transfer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young supplies the connected-officer route; Tindal and Treasure Salvors distinguish officer conduct relief from sovereign title. Oneida supplies federal-question character. Edelman, Green and Pennhurst retain retrospective and state-law limits. Seminole’s actual IGRA officer holding is consistent but supplies neither a universal statutory route nor an abrogation rule, and no IGRA sequence is imported.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### Settle v. Dickson County School Board

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 93-6207; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 25, 1996.

##### What genuine curricular grounds may limit a religious research topic

**Holding:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The teacher-only submission is not presumed publicly attributable to the school or an open forum. Objective inquiry may be distinguished from devotion or personal testimony. Genuine factual or evaluative mistakes can remain pedagogical judgments, while a legally impermissible categorical religious ban does not become valid as an honest academic mistake. The full policy permits objective, respectful, balanced academic religious treatment without promoting tenets or demeaning belief and accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief; neither provision guarantees approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal curricular viewpoint neutrality, unrestricted topic choice, religion-based exemption, perfect-accuracy duty or independent Free Exercise, Establishment or equal-protection holding.

##### Whether the summary-judgment record establishes lawful cause or the same-decision defense

**Holding:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Failure to secure permission was supported but its independent causal effect was disputed by the next-day no-penalty alternative, mixed reasons and original-choice answer, which also covered Buddha and Muhammed. Faith alone does not prove inability to research. Familiarity does not prove absence of new inquiry. Source requirements must reflect permitted secondary and outside sources, not a new Bible-only or school-library-only premise. Comparator descriptions are relevant without proving identical circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No pretext, hostility, final causation, liability or automatic defeat of a proved same-decision defense is found. Waters supplies its shared genuine-ground distinction, not an imported employment investigation code. Graduation proves no universal mootness; actual capacities, municipal attribution, immunity and remedies remain open. Conduct-date immunity notice concerns March–April 1991, not later decisions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0, for ordinary proceedings on the free-speech claim. No compulsory approval, reading, grading, credit, removal of the zero, damages, new discovery procedure or trial; another supported lawful disposition remains possible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, 530 U.S. 290 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-62.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment restrains the challenged official representative invocation mechanism. It preserves genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access, and requires neither a new forum nor censorship of otherwise lawful private religious viewpoints. It does not decide graduation, damages or later incidents outside this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues in the form Agostini adopted; the Court revives neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Its official-organization rule applies to this school-created invocation mechanism. Its independently sufficient substantial-pressure rule remains, without a compulsory-attendance prerequisite; no new pressure holding is made here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Preserved for its established official devotional program and its distinction between independent wording and private access; the Court does not transplant every feature of university commencement to football.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its actual generally available private forum is distinguished from this selective representative event component; religious content and government location remain insufficient by themselves to establish attribution.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its independently organized private use remains protected; the school's own representative ceremony presents a different governmental function.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Its protection of religious viewpoints within genuine comparable private access remains intact; no schoolwide private forum is inferred here.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its independently directed private-expression relationship remains intact; it does not authorize government-organized worship.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio v. City of Cincinnati:** Its contextual attribution inquiry remains; neither a forum label nor location alone supplies the answer.
- **Strout v. Albanese:** Its distinction between genuine private expression and governmental performance or purchase of a religious function is preserved; no football-specific rule is imported from its tuition setting.

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### United States v. Alaska

**Docket or dockets:** No. 84, Original.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The normal baseline governs without a proved general ten-mile island-fringe rule

**Holding:** Alaska's statutory three-mile submerged-land grant is measured from the Convention's normal low-water baseline, including applicable river and bay closing rules and separate belts of qualifying islands. The evidence establishes no firm and continuing general federal rule converting all waters behind fringing islands with entrances under ten miles into inland waters; the earlier Mississippi Sound judgment does not preclude that conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article 4 permits but does not require straight baselines; the United States made no selection here. The international twelve-mile territorial sea does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant. Alaska asserted general practice rather than specific historic title to Stefansson Sound; no different proved inland-water claim or general power to contract existing state territory is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California (1965) supplies Convention definitions. The 1985 Alabama/Mississippi decision established particular historic title, not a universal fringe rule; preclusion fails even assuming its availability, without expanding Mendoza or creating an original-action exception. Nome’s artificial-extension rule and the later Alabama/Mississippi and Massachusetts decrees retain their actual geographic scopes.

##### Dinkum Sands’ frequent seasonal submergence defeats island status

**Holding:** A naturally formed feature must ordinarily remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum to qualify as an island under Convention Article 10(1). Dinkum Sands' frequent seasonal submergence below that datum defeats island status and a separate three-mile grant; isolated emergence does not create alternating island entitlement, and abnormal temporary inundation of an otherwise enduring island remains distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No percentage-of-days test is adopted. Natural accretion or erosion of an established island is distinct. Article 11 supplies no low-tide-elevation entitlement at the reported distances exceeding three miles from mainland and nearby islands.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Borax and the California decree support the approximately nineteen-year mean-high-water datum, not an absolute physical-permanence condition. Measurements and the survey support the formation’s frequent submergence despite occasional emergence or maps derived from earlier observations.

##### The Petroleum Reserve instruments satisfy separate clear-inclusion and title-defeat requirements

**Holding:** Federal retention requires clear inclusion of the submerged lands and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose; geography or general purpose alone does not suffice. The 1923 Petroleum Reserve order's boundary and petroleum function, together with Congress's specific Statehood Act treatment of that reserve, satisfy both requirements and retain the disputed submerged estate, under the same demanding inquiry for equal-footing and statutory territorial-sea lands.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts III–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The 1923 order’s ocean-side barrier boundary, small-lagoon closures and express three-mile limit identify the estate. Ownership of the submerged petroleum deposits serves the national-defense purpose. Statehood Act §11(b)’s specific recognition of the reserve, read with that estate and function, confirms retained ownership; legislative jurisdiction alone would not establish title.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1313(a) is not diluted by a general federal-grant presumption. Congressional confirmation makes it unnecessary to decide independent Pickett Act authority; no broad Executive retention power is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shively preserves congressional retention for appropriate public purposes. Utah Division and Montana require distinct clear inclusion and title-defeating intent; their insufficient reservoir-control and unnecessary-riverbed showings differ from this defined petroleum estate.

##### The legally operative prestatehood wildlife segregation retained the described submerged lands

**Holding:** The 1957 application and legally operative temporary-segregation regulation set the specifically described submerged lands apart for the proposed wildlife refuge before statehood. Statehood Act §6(e)'s exclusion of lands withdrawn or otherwise set apart as wildlife refuges therefore retained those lands, although the application was not a completed final reservation; lands outside its boundaries or lacking comparable legal effect are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts III–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The 1957 application’s extreme-low-water boundary, coastal features and identified aquatic habitat establish inclusion. The regulation immediately barred settlement, location, sale, selection, entry, lease and other disposals, including mining and mineral leasing, only insofar as the proposed reservation would prevent them; administrative jurisdiction remained unchanged. Those conditions nevertheless gave the specific segregation present legal effect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 6(e) supplies title-defeating intent, separately from §6(m)’s incorporation of the Submerged Lands Act. Neither general ownership nor wildlife purpose alone suffices. The 1960 reservation receives no retroactive effect; the 1980 enlargement does not set the original boundary. Scalia and Thomas do not join the geographic determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Alaska’s baseline, Dinkum and Petroleum Reserve exceptions are overruled; the United States’ original Range exception is sustained. The parties must propose a conforming implementing decree; jurisdiction is retained and each bears its own costs. No final coordinate schedule, new survey, damages or oil-accounting award is entered.

**Current-force modification:** The June 29, 2000 implementing decree completes the earlier direction to propose a decree. Its fixed offshore line, qualified reciprocal injunctions, lease-administration terms and 180-day Section 7 escrow-distribution obligation now govern implementation. The reservation coastlines remain ambulatory, and jurisdiction is retained to effectuate and supplement the decree and the parties' rights. The 1997 title and baseline holdings remain unchanged.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Alaska, No. 84, Original, June 29, 2000: implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A; fixed offshore boundary, qualified resource rights, lease and escrow transitions, and retained jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Utah Division and Montana’s distinct inclusion and retention requirements govern. The statutory wildlife exclusion reaches this legally effective temporary segregation without treating every pending application as a completed reservation.

#### United States v. Alaska, 530 U.S. 1021 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 84, Original.

**Decided:** June 29, 2000.

##### 1. Entry implements the settled judgment and formally permits the counterclaim

**Holding:** The jointly submitted decree is entered because its terms implement the Court's completed title and boundary judgment without enlarging or reopening it. Alaska receives leave to file its counterclaim as part of the existing original action, with no new merits hearing or alteration of the settled title classifications.

**Proposition-level authority:** The Court's implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A. No individual participation lineup or tally is publicly announced; entry supplies no additional join in the 1997 title rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decree implements the June 19, 1997 judgment and the supported continuous-line agreement through Point Hope. It makes no new historic-title finding concerning Harrison Bay or Arco and supplies no title determination outside the described estates.

##### 2. The offshore line is fixed and the reciprocal rights retain their qualifications

**Holding:** Paragraph B fixes the federal-state offshore boundary at the original Exhibit A coordinates, with NAD 83/WGS 84 controlling and NAD 27 values serving only as conversion aids; under 43 U.S.C. section 1301(b), that line does not migrate with later coastal change. Against the other sovereign and persons claiming under it, the United States has exclusive exploration and natural-resource rights seaward, subject to section 1337(g) and Paragraph C, and Alaska has corresponding shoreward rights except within the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge and subject to section 1313 and Paragraph C; the reciprocal noninterference injunctions bind each sovereign, its privies, assigns, lessees and other persons claiming under it at that qualified scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** The Court's implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A. No individual participation lineup or tally is publicly announced; entry supplies no additional join in the 1997 title rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1313 preserves lawfully and expressly acquired federal parcels and their accretions, resources and improvements; lands lawfully held under state law; lands expressly retained or ceded at statehood, apart from a merely general marginal-sea retention or cession; proprietary acquisitions by eminent domain, purchase, cession, gift or otherwise; federal-use filled, built-up or reclaimed lands; existing federal rights in lands presently and actually occupied under claim of right; lands or interests held for tribes, bands, groups or individual Indians; and structures and improvements constructed under the federal navigational servitude. No new parcel-specific application is decided.

Section 1337(g)'s coastal-state revenue rights remain in force without transferring seaward title. Its ordinary account covers specified bonuses, rents, royalties and other bidding revenues from post-September 18, 1978 federal leases in the three-nautical-mile band, excluding federal income and windfall-profits taxes. Partial tracts ordinarily use the percentage of surface acreage within that band. Alaska's special treatment includes a partially covered tract's revenues until seven years after settlement of a boundary dispute under a section 1336 agreement made before January 1, 1986; for other Alaska tracts that special period ended April 15, 1993. Subject to the disputed-boundary provisions, the State receives 27 percent of the covered revenues with accrued interest by the last business day of the month after Treasury deposit, with the remainder sent simultaneously to federal miscellaneous receipts.

For a section 1336 boundary dispute, section 1337(g)(5) credits the agreement account with qualifying post-1978 revenues from tracts wholly or partly within the State-asserted band if not already deposited. Its interim 27-percent payment with interest concerns tracts wholly within the federally asserted band and falls due on the later of thirty days after December 1, 1987 or the last business day of the month after deposit. At settlement, additional sums due are paid from escrow; an insufficiency is met from federal lease revenues under the statutory formula. The statute's information and confidentiality rules, common-deposit notification and optional sharing agreements, account investment, October 1, 1985 accounting-effective date, and equal division of the state share where a tract falls within two or more States' bands remain intact. The decree calculates no statutory award or disputed account balance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The original Exhibit A coordinate schedule in the entered decree controls, including its NAD 83/WGS 84 datum. The [original schedule](https://www.supremecourt.gov/pdfs/recordsandbriefs/1000370914/1000370914_001.pdf) is incorporated without numerical retranscription; conversions and illustrative maps cannot replace it.

##### 3. Three lease categories govern administration and escrow without altering third-party rights

**Holding:** Paragraph C governs accounting and administration by the three distinct categories below, with the Section 7 escrow deadline running no later than 180 days after entry on June 29, 2000. It preserves the sovereigns' rights and obligations toward lessees and third parties, whether arising under Section 7 agreements or otherwise, and all rights and obligations under present or future unitization, operating, enhanced-recovery, commingling or similar agreements between the parties or with others.

**Proposition-level authority:** The Court's implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A. No individual participation lineup or tally is publicly announced; entry supplies no additional join in the 1997 title rationales.

**Material application:**

| Lease category | Administration, distribution and transition |
|---|---|
| Existing and former leases subject to Section 7 agreements | Distribute escrow according to the agreements within 180 days after entry. Carry out all applicable agreement provisions and administer the leases under their terms. |
| Existing nonagreement leases intersected by the fixed line | The original lessor continues administration and has the exclusive right to all past and future lease revenue. Upon expiration, relinquishment or termination, Paragraph B alone governs exploration and resource-exploitation rights in the formerly leased area. Fixing the line does not change distributions from former nonagreement leases that expired before entry and would have been intersected. |
| Existing Section 7 agreement leases also intersected by the fixed line | The agreement governs escrow distribution and administration. Upon expiration, relinquishment or termination, Paragraph B alone governs the area's exploration and resource-exploitation rights. Fixing the line does not change distributions from former agreement leases that expired before entry and would have been intersected. |

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decree determines no account amount, present delinquency, cancellation or new third-party obligation. Filing the proposal did not start the 180-day escrow period.

##### 4. The two reservation coastal descriptions preserve their different measures

**Holding:** Paragraph D identifies the Petroleum Reserve's coastal boundary as the continuous line beginning at the western bank of the Colville River and following the highest highwater mark westward, closing small-lagoon entrances including Peard Bay, Wainwright Inlet, the Kuk River, Kugrua Bay and River, and other small bays and river estuaries; it follows the ocean side of barrier islands and sandspits within three miles of shore and the ocean side of the Plover Islands to Icy Cape's northwestern extremity, approximately 70 degrees 21 minutes N., 161 degrees 46 minutes W. Paragraph E identifies the Arctic Refuge's coastal boundary from the international boundary between Alaska and Yukon Territory at extreme low water near Monument 1, westward along extreme low water and across lagoon entrances so that all offshore bars, reefs, islands and lagoons separating them from the mainland are within the Refuge, ending at Brownlow Point, approximately 70 degrees 10 minutes N., 145 degrees 51 minutes W.

**Proposition-level authority:** The Court's implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A. No individual participation lineup or tally is publicly announced; entry supplies no additional join in the 1997 title rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Highest highwater for the Petroleum Reserve is not substituted for extreme low water for the Refuge. Paragraph D preserves the submitted geographic description of the named reserve; it makes no new finding resolving the executive-order citation discrepancy. Paragraph E's reference to Public Land Order 2214, 25 Fed. Reg. 12598 (1960), identifies the descriptive coastline without making that order retroactively defeat state title. The legally operative 1957 segregation remains the earlier judgment's ground, and the 1980 enlargement does not supply the original statehood boundary. Neither paragraph decides title outside the described estate or replaces the original offshore coordinate schedule.

##### 5. Reservation coasts may move, and jurisdiction remains for implementation

**Holding:** Under Paragraph F the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge coastal boundaries migrate with changes in the relevant physical features, and the parties may address resulting disputes through negotiation, alternative dispute resolution or invocation of the Court's retained jurisdiction; they may jointly submit a boundary-location agreement for entry as a supplemental decree. Under Paragraph G the Court retains jurisdiction to entertain further proceedings, enter orders and issue writs necessary or advisable to effectuate and supplement this decree and the parties' rights; in all other respects the decree is final.

**Proposition-level authority:** The Court's implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A. No individual participation lineup or tally is publicly announced; entry supplies no additional join in the 1997 title rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ambulatory reservation coasts differ from the fixed offshore line. Retained jurisdiction implements the decree; it does not reopen title, require a mandatory dispute-resolution sequence or find that a current boundary dispute exists.
