#### O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC, 512 U.S. 79

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-489.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### These acquired California claims carry California imputation law

**Holding:** On the pre-FIRREA framework, California law governs imputation and related defenses to the institution's California negligence and fiduciary-duty claims acquired by the federal receiver: fund protection, greater recovery and general administrative uniformity do not establish the particular significant conflict with federal policy required for the proposed federal exception. The Ninth Circuit's federal-common-law ruling is reversed and the case remanded to determine the preserved state-law questions, without directing judgment for either party.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same pre-FIRREA ground, independently sufficient to reject the proposed rule without deciding FIRREA's temporal reach.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Erie Railroad v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64: applies the absence of general federal common law to privately generated state causes; exceptional federal fields are not abolished.
- Wallis v. Pan American Petroleum Corp., 384 U.S. 63: applies its requirement of an identified significant conflict with federal policy; ordinary federal financial interests are insufficient here.
- Boyle v. United Technologies Corp., 487 U.S. 500: preserves federal-interest and conflict prerequisites for exceptional displacement; the necessary conflict is absent in these acquired claims.
- United States v. Kimbell Foods, Inc., 440 U.S. 715: distinguishes federally generated program rights and preserves its rejection of automatic nationwide uniformity even there.
- United States v. Texas, April 5, 1993: distinguishes the established federal delay-compensation obligation on an assumed federal debt; it supplies no new receiver exemption from state tort defenses.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: preserves the separation of sovereign consent, federal-party status and substantive cause of action; this action remains state created.

##### FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule

**Holding:** If FIRREA governs these claims, §1821(d)(2)(A)(i) transfers the institution's rights, titles, powers and privileges to the FDIC subject to the legal incidents of those rights and the federal exceptions Congress actually enacted; it does not authorize adding the proposed federal exemption from California imputation law. The Court leaves FIRREA's temporal reach unresolved because the independently sufficient pre-FIRREA analysis produces the same state-law remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this conditional statutory construction and its temporal reservation. It is not a finding that FIRREA applies retrospectively.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide California imputation, adverse-interest exceptions, estoppel, engagement scope, duty, breach, causation, recoverable client losses, assigned investor claims, a statute-of-frauds defense, or FIRREA's temporal reach. Reversal does not reinstate the unexplained district-court summary judgment. The complaint does not seek reimbursement of the investors' rescission payments.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Erie Railroad v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64: preserves state substantive law where no enacted or otherwise valid federal displacement is established.
- Boyle v. United Technologies Corp., 487 U.S. 500: remains available within its actual exceptional-federal-law requirements; this construction does not occupy every federal common-law field.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, April 26, 1994: preserves its temporal-direction and new-consequences framework; FIRREA's temporal application is unnecessary to decide and is not presumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for preserved California-law questions. The unexplained original summary judgment is not reinstated automatically. No liability, damages or refund is ordered; FIRREA's temporal reach remains unresolved.

#### Baker v. General Motors Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-653.

**Decided:** 1998-01-13.

##### Judgment recognition begins with lawful rendering-state effect and has no general local-policy exception

**Holding:** Section 1738 applies to state consent judgments in this federal forum. Consent, disagreement with a local evidentiary policy or a decree's mere modifiability does not permit withholding its lawful adjudicative effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation or another constitutionally sufficient relationship may bind an absent person; mere shared interests or opportunity to intervene does not suffice.

##### This injunction does not itself require the separate forum to exclude Elwell's testimony

**Holding:** Giving the GM–Elwell decree its lawful adjudicative effect does not require the Missouri federal court to execute its broad personal testimony restriction as an evidentiary rule in the Bakers' distinct action. The decree adjudicated relations between GM and Elwell, not the Bakers' wrongful-death claims or their entitlement to invoke ordinary evidence process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy, O'Connor and Thomas agree in judgment on a distinct Michigan-law rationale that does not control this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general nonparty, public-policy or injunction exception to §1738 is adopted. The decree may retain lawful personal enforcement against Elwell; another forum acquires no authority to modify it. No universal witness-access right or rule governing every land, antisuit or evidence decree follows.

##### Neither the instruments nor orderly-review principles compel the Michigan modification proceeding asserted here

**Holding:** The Bakers' request for evidence in their own case is not, on the asserted basis, a request to modify Michigan's injunction. Celotex retains its requirements of actual jurisdictional relationship, sufficient authority, covered restraint and lawful avenues of relief and review; it supplies no missing basis to bind these outsiders or turn their evidence request into disobedience.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, unanimous in the bounded conclusion and preserved limits through the identified writings.

**Material application:** The injunction’s exception concerns the then-pending Georgia case. The separate settlement protects court-ordered testimony elsewhere against a breach action under the agreement or injunction. The instruments and exceptions remain distinct.

##### The remand preserves ordinary evidentiary rules and other appellate grounds

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit must reconsider the case without treating §1738 as requiring this exclusion. Actual privilege, trade-secret, work-product, admissibility and legally supported preclusion questions retain their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III mandate, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported absence of a particular privilege objection establishes neither blanket admissibility nor waiver of every possible objection. Other appellate grounds remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded unanimously without reinstating the entire $11.3 million verdict, mandating a new trial or Michigan modification proceeding, dissolving the decree or granting Elwell personal-enforcement immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Matsushita is applied to lawful rendering-state effect, consent judgments and the distinction between recognition and additional coercive control; its collateral-review reservation remains open. Richards retains the constitutional prerequisites for binding absent persons without creating a universal nonparty exception. Celotex is distinguished by its actual jurisdictional connection, authority, covered restraint and qualified relief/review mechanism; its orderly-review rule remains intact, including the preserved United Mine Workers and Walker qualifications. No precedent is overruled.

#### U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton / Bryant v. Hill

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-1456; 93-1828.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton / Bryant v. Hill. The dockets share the holdings at their stated component scopes.

##### States cannot add congressional qualifications through the ordinary ballot

**Holding:** A State may not deny an otherwise constitutionally qualified congressional candidate ordinary ballot access because of prior congressional service when that restriction adds a substantive personal qualification. The exclusive Article I qualifications cannot be supplemented indirectly through the ballot; genuine time, place and manner administration remains distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes, in both consolidated dockets.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Tenth Amendment supplies no reserved state power to alter qualifications for national offices created by Article I. A remaining write-in opportunity does not save this substantive service-based exclusion. Neutral filing, petition, support and ballot-management requirements and separate state-office provisions remain outside the holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 395 U.S. 486: applies fixed congressional qualifications and elector choice to the State's service-based restriction; Congress's own exclusion was its original setting.
- Storer, 415 U.S. 724: distinguishes genuine electoral administration from added substantive qualifications; ordinary neutral election rules are preserved.
- Gregory, 501 U.S. 452: distinguishes state control of its own officers from qualification of federal representatives; its actual state-officer protection remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Arkansas Supreme Court in Nos. 93-1456 and 93-1828. Section 3 of Amendment 73's ordinary-ballot exclusions after three House terms or two Senate terms remain unenforceable; no state-office term limit or separate redistricting remedy is adjudicated.

#### Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour

**Docket or dockets:** 97-25.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### Genuine nomination procedures do not add a personal congressional qualification

**Holding:** These genuine support and nomination requirements regulate access procedure rather than adding a personal qualification for service in Congress, so the distinct Qualifications Clause challenge fails. Article I's exclusive qualifications remain binding; a substantive personal exclusion cannot escape that rule merely by being labeled ballot administration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter). All eight Associates directly join Part III.B's genuine-procedure conclusion. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally join Part III.A's application of the exclusive-qualifications premise, providing five direct votes for that explanation. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not join III.A. Stone-Zsela supports the claim's rejection without adding a ground; his vote is not counted for either rationale.

**Material application:** U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton distinguishes substantive personal eligibility from genuine regulation of the time, place and manner of elections. Its rule prevents a State from withholding ordinary ballot access from an otherwise qualified congressional candidate because of a prohibited additional personal qualification. The requirements here concern demonstrated electoral support and nomination, not a candidate's personal eligibility to serve. That procedural classification answers this challenge, while the association analysis separately examines whether the actual burden is constitutionally justified. Calling a restriction procedural does not immunize severe access barriers or disguised personal exclusions. The five Justices joining III.A apply Thornton's controlling exclusivity premise; the three joining III.B alone need not abandon their disagreement with that premise to reject a challenge to genuine procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Thornton is applied within its substantive-versus-procedural boundary; its existing six-Justice authority is not reconstructed from today's distinct votes.

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices control the complete rule. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent also accept the calibrated framework without making their contrary application controlling.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, preserves actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements, including its protection against cross-component exclusion. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate state interests. Hawaii's party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the rationale and affirmance. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy disagree with this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the complete access system reviewed and its reasonable burdens.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions support the limited-burden finding. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every later preference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently validate every surrounding access rule or decide a prohibition within materially more restrictive access rules. Candidate qualifications and reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere remain open; no absolute write-in right or guarantee of competitive races is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit; its reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction or replacement election-administration scheme issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, permits primary winnowing and preventive regulation without proof of a particular past disruption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and restrictions against circumvention. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, governs this limited-burden application; the decision creates no exception to its inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, controls. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent expressly agree on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual access analysis separately stated above. Political expression outside the ballot and every restriction on eligible candidates in a different system are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief from the prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.

#### Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour

**Docket or dockets:** 97-25.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### The actual congressional access system survives this limited challenge

**Holding:** Under Anderson and Burdick, Illinois may require meaningful support in the relevant congressional electorate and use different primary and general-election nomination routes where the complete actual system leaves a reasonable available route to the general ballot. On this record, trustee-based statewide status does not compel district-established status, and the combined requirements do not establish the severe or unjustified burden asserted; the holding creates no categorical safe harbor for percentages, slates or cumulative deadlines.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter). Souter's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** Anderson v. Celebrezze requires assessment of the injury's character and magnitude, the State's precise interests and their need for the burden. Burdick v. Takushi requires narrow fit to a compelling interest for severe burdens and ordinarily permits reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens justified by important regulatory interests. The inquiry considers the whole system, not a nominal alternative alone.

Norman v. Reed permits distinct-electorate support requirements while forbidding party-name barriers and loss of independently qualified components because another electorate failed. Statewide trustee support does not itself show congressional-district support. The different denominators and stages make the petition comparison more than a simple tenfold disparity. Jenness v. Fortson permits meaningful support requirements in an actual workable system; Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party likewise examines the available nomination and advocacy alternatives. Here the capped general-ballot petition route supplies an available alternative without an automatic constitutional right to a public party primary.

The substantial numerical difference and combined conditions deserve examination. The 1994 successes are relevant but not conclusive; disputed 1996 acceptances cannot prove full compliance, and the exceptional 1992 route cannot establish ordinary-year accessibility. No supported genuine dispute of the asserted impossible cumulative burden requires trial. Nor does the record establish Norman's disqualification of an independently qualified component through another electorate's failure. The strongest contrary argument requires a demonstrated heavier cumulative burden or automatic transfer of statewide status that this record does not support. Literal impossibility is not a universal prerequisite to a constitutional claim. Incumbent preservation is no independent justification, and a properly supported future burden challenge remains available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson applies the actual-burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry.
- Burdick preserves complete-system review and the severe-burden boundary; its write-in presumption is not generalized.
- Norman preserves electorate-specific support and independently qualified components; it creates no automatic statewide-to-district status.
- Jenness permits a meaningful demonstration of support, not universal approval of five-percent petitions.
- Timmons applies proportionate regulation within actual alternatives, not an incumbent-protection interest or categorical approval of every nomination rule.

##### The judgment supplies no replacement election system

**Holding:** The access judgment is affirmed and the distinct Qualifications Clause claim rejected without reopening the primary, declaring automatic district-established status, substituting a signature number or granting a new statewide qualification. Independently qualified components retain Norman's protection, and supported challenges to different actual cumulative burdens remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter). Souter's Part IV has all eight Associates on the affirmance and its grounds; Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of the distinct Qualifications Clause claim.

**Material application:** The supported record permits disposition of the actual claims without inventing a genuine factual dispute or designing an alternative nomination scheme. The Court's conclusion does not validate every complete-slate condition, deadline combination or future application. Nor does it decide the separate equal-protection claim that was not pleaded. A later claim must establish its actual injury, applicable interests and remedy rather than treating this bounded affirmance or isolated successful qualification as conclusive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Norman retains its independently qualified-component rule and bounded election remedy; neither is displaced by this affirmance.

#### Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-928.

**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

For the other questions in Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, No. 95-928, decided January 14, 1997, see Federal Courts - Preservation and question-presented limits (Review of the source-of-law argument).

##### State law supplies the ordinary corporate duty

**Holding:** State law supplies the ordinary standard of care for these acquired corporate-governance claims against officers and directors of a federally chartered savings association unless enacted federal law or a specifically demonstrated significant conflict with an identified federal policy displaces it. Federal chartering, insurance, receivership recovery and general administrative uniformity do not establish that conflict here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** New Jersey's precise duty and business-judgment rules, breach, causation, defenses and liability remain undecided. Specific enacted duties or valid federal regulation may displace state law; distinct federal enforcement obligations are not adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** O'Melveny's acquired-claim and specific-conflict rules are applied to these corporate duties; Erie and Wallis retain their general-law and conflict limits; Boyle's exceptional-displacement requirements remain available; Kimbell Foods supplies no automatic uniform federal rule.

##### Section 1821(k) establishes a federal minimum and preserves other rights

**Holding:** Section 1821(k), when applicable, permits personal monetary liability of a director or officer of an insured depository institution, whether federally or state chartered, for gross negligence, similar conduct or conduct demonstrating a greater disregard of duty, including intentional tortious conduct, with those culpability terms defined under applicable state law; the action must be by, on behalf of, or at the request or direction of the Corporation, prosecuted wholly or partly for its benefit, with the Corporation acting as receiver or conservator, on a claim purchased from, assigned by or otherwise conveyed by such a receiver or conservator, or on a claim purchased from, assigned by or otherwise conveyed wholly or partly by an insured institution or its affiliate in connection with §1823 assistance. That authorization defeats a more protective state liability shield but preserves rights actually available under other applicable law, including a state duty permitting recovery for less culpable conduct: gross negligence is a federal floor, not a ceiling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The savings sentence does not create general federal common law or restore withdrawn claims. The section's temporal application remains open; the independently erroneous common-law premise permits disposition without deciding it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** O'Melveny's conditional FIRREA analysis is preserved without converting it into a retroactivity decision; its source-of-law rule supplies no additional federal action. Landgraf's provision-specific temporal inquiry remains available when application to earlier conduct must actually be decided.

##### Vacatur requires further proceedings under the correct sources

**Holding:** The Third Circuit judgment is vacated because it restored the asserted general federal-common-law theories and excluded federally chartered institutions from §1821(k). On remand the lower courts must apply the state-law starting point and the federal provision at its lawful scope, resolve properly available claims and defenses, and determine any liability and relief without treating this decision as reinstatement of withdrawn state claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Vacatur awards no damages and supplies no reinstatement of withdrawn state theories. Procedural availability, amendment and genuinely remaining claims and defenses belong to ordinary remand proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** O'Melveny's separation of the governing-law correction from final relief is applied; no liability or temporal rule is added by the mandate.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-law incidents of claims acquired by the FDIC

### State-law incidents of claims acquired by the FDIC

**Governing question:** Whether FDIC receivership creates a federal rule displacing state imputation defenses to acquired state tort claims.

**Current rule:** When the FDIC acquires an institution’s state-law tort claims, state law ordinarily supplies the associated imputation rules and defenses. Federal financial interests or a preference for uniformity alone do not justify new federal common law; a significant conflict with an identified federal policy is required. For pre-FIRREA receivership claims the ordinary rule applies independently of that Act. If FIRREA applies, §1821’s succession to the institution’s rights carries their legal incidents subject to Congress’s enacted exceptions; it supplies no additional categorical anti-imputation rule. State law likewise supplies the ordinary officer/director standard of care for acquired corporate-governance claims involving a federally chartered savings association unless federal enactment or a specifically established significant conflict displaces it. Federal chartering, deposit insurance, receivership recovery and general administrative uniformity alone do not establish such a conflict.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law rule and federal-common-law limit: O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — These acquired California claims carry California imputation law, 1994-06-13. Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same pre-FIRREA ground, independently sufficient to reject the proposed rule without deciding FIRREA's temporal reach.
- Independent conditional FIRREA ground: O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule, 1994-06-13. Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this conditional statutory construction and its temporal reservation. It is not a finding that FIRREA applies retrospectively. 
- State source of ordinary corporate duty, including federally chartered associations: Atherton v. FDIC — Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation — II. State law supplies the ordinary corporate duty, January 14, 1997; Scalia for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Atherton, January 14, 1997, extends the state-law source rule to ordinary corporate-governance duties of federally chartered associations; O’Melveny’s imputation rules remain.

**Open question:** The decision leaves FIRREA’s temporal applicability and the eventual operation of the particular state-law defense for the lower courts.

**Holdings navigation:** These acquired California claims carry California imputation law; FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule. Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (1997-01-14) — State law supplies the ordinary corporate duty.

## Standards and Tests selection: Recognition of injunctions and another forum’s evidence process

### Recognition of injunctions and another forum’s evidence process

**Governing question:** Whether §1738 compels another forum to execute a personal testimony restriction as its own evidentiary rule.

**Current rule:** A state consent judgment receives its lawful rendering-state adjudicative effect without a general local-public-policy exception; consent or mere modifiability does not eliminate recognition. But recognition of a personal injunction does not by itself require a separate forum to enforce a broad testimony restriction as its evidence rule in strangers’ distinct action, where their claims or entitlement to ordinary process were not adjudicated. Recognition, preclusion, personal enforcement and the requested evidentiary act must be distinguished.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful recognition without a public-policy exception; bounded recognition/execution distinction: Baker v. General Motors Corp., 1998-01-13; Stevens Part I for all nine; Part II with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Injunctions are not categorically excluded from §1738; nonparty status alone supplies no universal exception, and sufficient binding relationships remain possible. No authority to modify the rendering court’s decree, general witness-access right or immunity from lawful personal enforcement follows. Privilege, work product, trade secrets, admissibility and legally supported preclusion retain their own predicates.

**Related current rules:** Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments supplies rendering-state effect; Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies its independent limits.

**Holdings navigation:** Baker v. General Motors Corp. — Recognition of state-court judgments.

## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. When the complete access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate interests, including primary finality and prevention of evasive candidacies or party raiding. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**
- Governing inherited framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden”, June 8, 1992; White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas (five), applying Anderson and the earlier simulated Norman rule.
- Limited-burden write-in presumption: Burdick, “2. Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid in its reasonably accessible system,” June 8, 1992; same five Justices.
- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the write-in presumption and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute validation of all write-in bans follows. Materially more restrictive access systems may impose severe burdens. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

**Holdings navigation:** Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.
