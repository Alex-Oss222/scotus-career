### Artuz v. Bennett — 2000-11-07

Ginsburg's unanimous opinion affirms: under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a state collateral application is properly filed when delivery and acceptance comply with the rules governing filing the application. Claim-specific mandatory relief bars do not defeat otherwise proper filing. New York Criminal Procedure Law §440.10(2)(a) requires denial after merits appellate adjudication unless a subsequent retroactively effective change in controlling law occurred; paragraph (c) requires record facts sufficient for adequate appellate review and omitted review caused by an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or raise the issue on a perfected appeal. Both govern claim relief, not delivery and acceptance. Neither provision is found actually applicable to a particular Bennett claim.

Clerk acceptance does not cure a genuine filing defect. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, fees and filing preconditions remain distinct; the Court does not classify every rule without its terms. Whether deadline exceptions can preserve proper filing of a late application remains open. The Court fixes no pending period or tolling duration and makes no finding about receipt or absence of the written order; absence was an assumption below based on Bennett's allegation. Transitional timing, exhaustion, default and habeas entitlement remain outside the grant. Lindh's provision-specific temporal rule survives; it neither exempts this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA nor answers proper filing. The one-year framework and other habeas defenses remain effective; the remand awards no writ or release.
