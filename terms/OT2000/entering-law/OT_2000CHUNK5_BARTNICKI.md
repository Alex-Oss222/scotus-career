## Standards and Tests selection: Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions

### Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions

**Governing question:** How the First Amendment reviews content-neutral cable-carriage requirements.

**Current rule:** Cable operators’ editorial choices receive ordinary First Amendment protection, without the broadcast-scarcity rationale. Content classification examines what the law selects and why. The general full-power must-carry provisions at issue are content neutral and receive intermediate scrutiny: they must advance substantial interests unrelated to suppressing expression and burden no substantially greater amount of speech than necessary. The least restrictive alternative is not required. Preserving broadcast availability, diverse information sources and fair competition are substantial interests, but identifying them does not prove the law satisfies the test. Legislative predictive judgments receive substantial deference while courts independently determine whether reasonable inferences rest on substantial evidence and support the claimed harms and response.

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposeful published assistance to completed crimes

### Purposeful published assistance to completed crimes

**Governing question:** When does public distribution fail to supply categorical First Amendment immunity for assistance to a crime?

**Current rule:** Public distribution creates no categorical immunity from civil liability where a publisher intends to facilitate murder through detailed operational assistance, intends criminal use and materially assists the resulting crime, subject to the tort’s substantive and causal requirements. Knowledge of the particular plot is not indispensable to that stipulated combination. Offensiveness, publication, abstract advocacy, foreseeability, knowledge of possible misuse or a reader’s independent imitation alone does not establish purposeful participation.

**Authority by component:**

- Intentional-participation boundary and actual-assistance predicate: Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc — Purposeful, materially effective assistance to murder has no categorical publisher immunity, 1998-05-14.

**Present operation effective:** Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, 1998-05-14.

**Exceptions and limits:** The claimant must establish the necessary facts through operative admissions or admissible proof; motion-only stipulations do not establish them for trial. Brandenburg’s advocacy requirements remain unchanged. No lesser-mental-state rule, general imminence exception, prior restraint or rule for news, fiction, historical works or ordinary technical information is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. — Purposeful operational assistance to crime.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

### Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

**Governing question:** Intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination of objectively intimate information entrusted for a limited criminal investigation.

**Current rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects this confined intimate marital and family interest against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination without sufficient governmental justification. An assurance of confidentiality informs the objective expectation but does not alone create a federal right; consent to investigative use is not consent to general publication. Ownership or authorship is not indispensable to a person's own intimate interest. Public inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not automatically establish prior dissemination, voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of distinct official facilitation of publication. The protection concerns intimate personal information, not reputation alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined protected interest and justification: I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty, 1996-04-29; Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Sheets, 1996-04-29.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure retain their separate rules. There is no universal strict-scrutiny or least-intrusive-means test for personal, financial or business information, general deletion right, inherited privacy claim, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author’s speech. Independent municipal attribution and conduct-date qualified immunity remain. The unobjected trial intent instruction does not establish a universal new mental-state rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County.

## Opening authority: Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622

#### Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-44.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Cable editorial expression receives ordinary First Amendment protection

**Holding:** Cable operators' editorial selection and cable programmers' expression are protected speech, and compulsory carriage burdens those interests. Cable's physical transmission capacity and market power do not justify importing broadcasting's spectrum-scarcity standard or reducing review of this expressive burden to rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-A and II-B as relevant to this proposition, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens recognizes protected cable expression but does not join these complete portions and is not counted for their formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock, 499 U.S. 439: applies its recognition of cable speech and press; its approval of a broad content-neutral tax does not resolve compulsory carriage.
- City of Los Angeles v. Preferred Communications, Inc., 476 U.S. 488: applies its protection of editorial selection and attention to cable's characteristics; it had left the precise standard open.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC, 395 U.S. 367: confines its scarcity rationale to the broadcasting setting; it supplies no diminished cable standard here.

##### Content classification depends on what the law selects and why

**Holding:** A law burdening protected expression because of its content ordinarily must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest; a burden justified without reference to content is subject to the appropriate intermediate review. A benign purpose or absence of hostility does not neutralize a content-based operation, while a distinction among speakers or media does not by itself establish content discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens does not join that portion; his agreement with the case-specific classification below is distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991: applies its actual content-based escrow holding; neutral collection remedies remain outside that holding.
- Arkansas Writers' Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: applies its scrutiny of differential burdens dependent on a publication's content.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content as the classification inquiry.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: applies its rejection of benign-purpose neutrality for an unexplained content distinction; its commercial-speech fit holding remains bounded.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its distinction between permissible neutral differential treatment and censorial or content-based burdens.

##### The general full-power carriage requirements are content neutral

**Holding:** The general commercial and noncommercial full-power must-carry obligations regulate cable's distribution bottleneck and preservation of independently available broadcast service without selecting the stations' individual messages; intermediate scrutiny therefore governs those obligations. This classification does not decide the distinct low-power local-programming criteria, the market-adjustment provision, or a materially different content-based administration, and creates no general right to compel private editorial publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-C and II-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this application.

**Material application:** The classification addresses the general full-power core of §§4–5, whose commercial and noncommercial eligibility, capacity, duplication, signal-quality and channel-position provisions remain distinct. It does not adjudicate the separately reserved low-power programming criteria or market adjustments, authorize an editorial-balance mandate for another medium, or confer both must-carry and retransmission-consent entitlement at once.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241: distinguishes compelled reply and interference with newspaper editorial judgment; its protection is not displaced.
- Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. Public Utilities Commission, 475 U.S. 1: distinguishes its fractured compelled-access reasoning concerning speech-responsive access and attribution; no unanimous categorical rule is attributed to it.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its allowance for justified neutral distinctions between media; the cable-specific burden still requires constitutional scrutiny.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC: supplies no basis for the classification; broadcast scarcity is not extended.

##### Intermediate scrutiny requires advancement and a proportionate speech burden

**Holding:** A qualifying content-neutral carriage requirement must further an important or substantial governmental interest unrelated to suppressing expression and must not burden substantially more speech than necessary to further that interest. The Government need not establish that the law is the least restrictive conceivable alternative, but the actual expressive burden and relevant alternatives bear on whether its fit is sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. This common standard does not establish common agreement about whether the existing record satisfies it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367: applies the important-interest, nonsuppression and incidental-burden requirements to this content-neutral carriage burden.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism: applies its requirement against substantially excessive burdens while preserving its rejection of least-restrictive-means review.

##### Preserving broadcast availability, diverse sources and fair competition are substantial interests

**Holding:** Preserving free local broadcast service, maintaining diverse sources of information and promoting fair competition in television distribution are important content-neutral interests supporting consideration of the general full-power carriage scheme under intermediate scrutiny. Their importance does not itself prove threatened harm, advancement, adequate fit or the scheme's final constitutionality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. The separate opinions disputing the neutrality of the Act's localism and programming preferences do not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock: applies the distinction between a medium's economic characteristics and selection of protected ideas.
- United States v. O'Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism: apply the requirement of a substantial nonsuppressive interest without treating that element as proof of advancement or tailoring.

##### Predictive judgments receive substantial deference and remain subject to review

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** In reviewing Congress's predictive judgments relevant to the carriage scheme, a court must give substantial deference while independently assuring that Congress drew reasonable inferences from substantial evidence. Congress need not compile the formal evidentiary record required of a court or agency; this general principle neither decides whether the evidence here suffices nor joins the four-Justice application requiring additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, supported by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Souter, together with Stevens's express agreement in his concurrence at this limited level of generality: five Justices. Stevens does not join Part III-B, its real-harm formulation as a complete passage, or its record-insufficiency application. This is direct agreement on the stated proposition across writings, not a Marks inference or a fifth join in the plurality portion.

**Current force:** Controlling at this express five-Justice level of agreement only; no majority adopts Part III-B's present-record insufficiency application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landmark Communications, Inc. v. Virginia, 435 U.S. 829: applies the judiciary's continuing responsibility to review an expressive burden despite legislative findings.
- Sable Communications of California, Inc. v. FCC, 492 U.S. 115: applies the refusal to make congressional findings conclusive; its strict-scrutiny setting does not impose that standard here.

##### Authority of the remand on evidentiary sufficiency

**Authority of the judgment:** The summary judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter. No controlling rationale exists under Marks for the present-record insufficiency conclusion: only Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter adopt it; Stevens agrees in remand without adopting it. The separate grounds do not establish a logical subset. The six controlling propositions above retain only their separately stated support and scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final constitutionality of the general scheme remains open. The district court must consider the separate low-power criteria in the first instance and may address the market-adjustment provision; neither is independently upheld or judicially severed. The initial home-shopping transition clause does not establish current FCC treatment. The remand orders no replacement quota, damages, blanket injunction against the Act or particular interim relief. Any interim relief requires its own properly presented legal basis. The legal standards and the judgment govern; the four-Justice explanation of why further proof is needed remains identified as such.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The summary judgment sustaining the challenged carriage scheme is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, under the controlling intermediate framework. Final constitutionality remains open. The district court must consider low-power eligibility in the first instance and may consider market adjustments; any interim relief needs its own properly presented basis. No new channel quota, damages or blanket injunction against the Act is ordered.

## Opening authority: Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission

#### Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-992.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

For the separate classification questions excluded from review, see *Turner*, March 31, 1997, No. 95-992 (Federal Courts — Preservation and question-presented limits).

##### Whether the developed record establishes material advancement of preservation and diversity

**Holding:** On the developed record, the general full-power must-carry scheme materially advances the substantial interests in preserving free local broadcasts and independently available information sources. Supported preventive judgments do not require proof of impending industry collapse or individual failure of every carried station, but remain subject to independent judicial review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Breyer; five direct votes support the shared preservation-and-diversity rationale.

**Material application:** Evidence connects bottleneck control and incentives favoring affiliated programming or advertising with carriage refusals, drops, repositioning, lost audience and revenue, and threatened local service. Contrary profitability and growth evidence does not refute every supported local threat. Audience options may be reduced without predatory intent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no independently sufficient economic-predation or fair-competition application. Legislative prediction is not conclusive, and a desirable broadcast policy alone is insufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Turner* (1994) supplies the full-power content-neutral classification, intermediate standard, and limited predictive-deference proposition. Its earlier four-Justice account of record insufficiency does not become majority law. *Denver Area* preserves ordinary cable speech protection and separate component review; spectrum-scarcity doctrine is not extended to cable.

##### Whether the existing duties adequately fit the accepted interests on this facial record

**Holding:** The reviewed full-power duties burden no substantially greater amount of cable editorial discretion than necessary to protect the accepted interests on this developed facial record. Intermediate scrutiny requires an adequate fit, not the least restrictive imaginable arrangement; the supported predictive inquiry permits final affirmance without further trial here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Breyer; five Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, and Ginsburg dissent from the merits affirmance.

**Material application:** Capacity categories, qualified-station and nonduplication rules, and the distinct educational obligations limit the duties. Voluntary carriage counts toward them without eliminating the burden of losing the freedom to change programming. The 1.2 percent figure measures added must-carry channels, not all reserved capacity, displaced programs, or foregone choices. Existing editorial space and expansion matter alongside burdens on operators, programmers, and viewers. The record supports the lower court's conclusion that leased access, A/B switches, narrower former FCC-style rules, and subsidies would not protect the accepted interests as effectively.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under 47 U.S.C.§534, systems with 12 or fewer usable activated channels must carry at least three local commercial signals; systems also having 300 or fewer subscribers are exempt from that section only while they delete no broadcast signal. Systems with more than 12 channels carry eligible local commercial signals up to one-third of usable activated capacity. This is not a combined commercial-and-educational ceiling. Substantially duplicative commercial signals already represented in carriage and additional same-network affiliates need not be carried; voluntary duplicates and multiple affiliates count toward the obligation. When eligible stations exceed the required number, the operator selects among them, but cannot substitute low power for full power and must choose the elected network's affiliate with the closest city-of-license reference point to the principal headend.

Section535 adds separate educational duties. Systems with 12 or fewer channels carry one qualified local educational signal and may carry more. Systems with 13–36 carry at least one and need not carry more than three, subject to continued carriage; they may carry more. If no qualified local station is present, the former category must import one qualified educational signal and the latter at least one. For that importation, the smallest systems need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 but must use the first available channel. All qualified local educational stations carried on that date retain carriage unless operator and station give written waiver consent. Systems above36 channels, including those expanding beyond36, carry requesting qualified local educational stations; a system required to carry three need not add stations substantially duplicating another qualified local educational station requesting carriage. This is not a general three-station cap. A13–36-channel system already carrying a State-network educational affiliate need not add another same-network affiliate whose programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate.

Commercial distant signals require indemnification of increased copyright liability. A commercial station below the specified headend levels (−45dBm UHF; −49dBm VHF) is excluded if it does not agree to bear the costs of delivering a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational carriage is not required without delivery of a good-quality or baseband signal; its distant-signal indemnity exception concerns adding a signal and expressly does not erase the continued-carriage rule. These are actual programming obligations, including primary video, audio and captions. Commercial carriage includes technically feasible program-related blanking-interval or subcarrier material; educational carriage includes technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language purposes. Other ancillary material remains discretionary. Positioning and availability duties also constrain operation; the law does not merely reserve empty channels. No new dispute over ancillary material, positioning, payment, notice or enforcement is decided.

No blanket exemption of legislative facts from summary-judgment rules follows. More burdensome future legislation and supported system-specific displacement challenges remain open. The commercial one-third cap does not combine commercial and educational duties; the educational scheme creates no universal three-station ceiling. No dispute over ancillary programming, positioning, payment, notice, or enforcement is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the reviewed full-power facial judgment, leaving its duties enforceable. No replacement quota, Act-wide injunction, damages, takings disposition, or religious-claim disposition issues. The separate low-power and market-modification classifications remain outside the judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *O'Brien* requires a substantial nonsuppressive interest and fitted burden. *Ward* requires adequate tailoring while preserving concrete alternatives as relevant evidence, without a least-restrictive-means command.

## Earlier published decision: Rice v. Paladin Enterprises

Source: `terms/OT1997/output/OT_1997CHUNK8.md`.

### Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc., Fourth Circuit No. 96-2412, 128 F.3d 233

Decision, May 14, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided May 14, 1998, on review of the judgment reversing defendants' summary judgment in the civil action arising from three murders. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is recorded.

The agreed issue for the Rule 56 motion was whether the First Amendment supplied a complete legal defense. For that motion only, defendants stipulated a purpose to assist criminal readers in murder for hire and actual operational assistance to the perpetrator through the publications involved. They did not concede specific knowledge of the plot, victims or conspiracy. Plaintiffs also stipulated intended legitimate readership, including writers, law-enforcement personnel, entertainment readers and researchers. Both the identified murder manual and the separate silencer manual were involved. All other issues of law and fact remained reserved.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The absolute constitutional defense fails on the assumed facts. No tort liability, damages, injunction against a publication or final factual determination is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Purposeful, materially effective assistance to murder has no categorical publisher immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment does not categorically bar civil liability where a publisher intentionally supplies detailed operational assistance to facilitate murder, intends criminal use, and the communication materially assists the resulting crime, subject to the applicable tort's substantive and causal requirements. This conclusion rests on the motion-only stipulated combination; publication, offensiveness, abstract advocacy, knowledge of possible misuse, foreseeability or a reader's independent imitation does not alone establish it.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Giboney distinguishes expression that participates in independently unlawful conduct from protected advocacy. Claiborne Hardware bars civil responsibility based merely on association, advocacy or another person's violence; Brandenburg preserves its own imminent-lawless-action requirements for advocacy. Those protections cannot be bypassed simply by calling a book dangerous or a tort generally applicable.

The stipulated facts supply the different combination at issue here: a purpose to furnish operational help for murder for hire and actual material assistance to these completed crimes. The constitutional boundary therefore rests on intentional participation and its real assisting function, not persuasion, a disliked viewpoint or a judicial assessment of literary value. Dawson and Wisconsin v. Mitchell require a legally relevant connection rather than punishment of belief. Madsen and Schenck likewise preserve responsibility and actual-concert limits; sympathy or shared ideas do not establish participation.

The absence of knowledge of this particular plot does not create absolute immunity where purpose to assist criminal use and actual assistance are assumed. Nor does the existence of legitimate readers erase the stipulated assisting conduct. The Court rejects neither fact. It rejects only the proposition that public distribution necessarily immunizes purposeful participation regardless of those predicates.

The stipulations remain confined to the motion. Plaintiffs must establish the legally necessary facts through operative admissions or admissible proof in the reserved proceedings; defendants retain their factual and legal defenses. The Court decides no lesser-mental-state standard, general exception to Brandenburg's imminence requirement, prior restraint, or liability rule for news, fiction, historical works, ordinary technical information or generalized dangerous ideas. It does not adopt the lower opinion's broader statements about the publications' value or establish exclusive criminal readership.

**Precedent treatment:** Giboney's participation distinction and Claiborne Hardware's responsibility limits apply; Brandenburg's advocacy rule remains unchanged. Dawson, Mitchell, Madsen and Schenck preserve the required connection to actual unlawful conduct.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Brandenburg:** Retained for advocacy; no general immediacy exception is created.
- **NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware:** Applied to preserve protected advocacy, association and responsibility limits against liability for others' violence.
- **Giboney and Brown v. Hartlage:** Supply the distinction between expression and its function as participation in independently unlawful conduct, without a category of unprotected dangerous ideas.
- **Dawson and Wisconsin v. Mitchell:** Preserve the requirement that expression have a legally relevant connection to misconduct rather than serve as punishment for belief.
- **Madsen and Schenck:** Their responsibility and actual-concert limits remain; the decision does not turn shared viewpoint into participation.
- **Cohen v. Cowles Media:** No categorical press exemption follows, but the general-law label alone does not establish constitutional validity.

#### Law After Decision

Purposeful, materially effective operational assistance to murder may be subject to otherwise valid tort liability. The absolute defense fails only on the stipulated motion facts and stated constitutional predicates. Advocacy protections, ordinary tort elements, causal proof, defenses and the distinction between motion assumptions and final findings remain intact; no publication is banned and no liability is adjudged.

#### Source Notes

The Fourth Circuit's opinion, 128 F.3d 233, and its complete note 2 stipulation supply the motion-only concessions, lawful intended readership and lack of specific knowledge of the plot. Original filings are not reproduced. The stipulations neither settle later trial facts nor authorize a finding that only criminals were intended readers.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fourth Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the reserved law and facts. The defendants' summary judgment on the complete First Amendment defense remains reversed. The Court directs no particular trial finding, damages award, evidentiary hearing or further Supreme Court event.

## Earlier published decision: Sheets v. Salt Lake

### Sheets v. Salt Lake County

Merits decision, April 29, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

In October Term 1995, the Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the Tenth Circuit's January 10, 1995 judgment, 45 F.3d 1383, in appellate Nos. 93-4128 and 93-4134. The question is whether officials violated Gary Sheets's own constitutional confidentiality interest by giving a private author access to intimate material entrusted for a murder investigation.

After Kathy Sheets died in a 1985 bombing, Gary supplied her diary to investigators. Farnsworth testified to a confidentiality assurance and its communication to investigators; Bell could not recall an assurance, and George's notice was disputed. The diary described Gary's intimate marital and personal life. After the investigation ended in 1987, the investigative file, including the diary, was archived and publicly inspectable.

Investigator Michael George allowed his friend, author Lindsey, to examine his file box; other authors received answers without comparable access. George had copied intimate entries and kept detailed notes, which he destroyed before Lindsey's book appeared in August 1988. The jury inferred that George disclosed the material and caused its publication. The County's unwritten practice permitted discussion of closed cases with the press subject to evenhanded treatment. Gary seeks redress for his own injury, not his wife's surviving personality, copyright or ownership alone. All nine Justices participated at consideration and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2, within the reviewed constitutional-confidentiality question.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Justices Scalia and Thomas.

The constitutional ruling does not independently settle or reopen limitations, evidence, municipal policy and causation, remittitur, the amount of fees or personal immunity. Any actually preserved independent defense retains its governing law and procedural posture; none is presumed unresolved. Recognition of this right today supplies no automatic notice at the earlier conduct date, and personal immunity is distinct from County liability.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivers the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All seven directly adopt both controlling propositions, their explanations and the bounded affirmance. Justice Scalia dissents, joined throughout by Justice Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty

**Controlling proposition:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects a person's objectively intimate marital and family information against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination when the information was entrusted for a limited criminal investigation and the disclosure lacks sufficient governmental justification on this record. A confidentiality assurance informs the objective expectation but does not alone create the federal right; consent to investigative use does not surrender the information to general publication.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Whalen distinguishes avoiding disclosure of personal matters from autonomy in important decisions. It upheld safeguarded collection and reserved unwarranted dissemination. Nixon considered legitimate personal privacy independently of ownership while sustaining protected archival screening. Those decisions supply the foundation, but neither resolved this disclosure. The Court now protects this confined intimate interest; it does not attribute today's application to an already complete confidentiality test.

Gary asserts exposure of his own marriage and personal life. Kathy's authorship does not make her the only person affected. Paul rejected reputation injury from an official arrest record; this claim concerns intimate information, not unfavorable statements alone. Collins's refusal to turn state duties into a federal workplace guarantee likewise does not decide this affirmative disclosure. Neither a promise nor a breach of Utah's confidentiality rule would suffice without the independently protected interest.

The file became publicly inspectable after 1987. That fact weighs against an expectation of secrecy, but does not itself establish prior public dissemination of these passages, Gary's voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of George's distinct facilitation of their publication. The Court does not invalidate the archive-access regime. Access by legitimate investigators also differs from transferring intimate material for a private author's use.

Trial counsel invoked public interest in the sensational crime and its subjects, although no compelling disclosure interest was advanced on appeal. General interest in the crime does not establish a sufficient governmental connection to publishing these intimate passages. Legitimate investigation, lawful retention, public accountability and legally required disclosure remain distinct; none supplies the missing justification for this transfer on the supported record. The decision regulates the official conduct at issue, not the private author's speech. It creates no general right to deletion or inherited claim, and imposes no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or fixed balancing test for every personal, business or financial record.

##### II. Independent legal review and supported trial findings establish this application

**Controlling proposition:** The Court independently determines constitutional coverage and legal sufficiency while reviewing the denial of Rule 50 relief under the ordinary standard favorable to the prevailing party's supported evidence and reasonable inferences. The objective instructions and supported findings establish the confined right's application here; the unobjected instruction requires intentional acts causing deprivation, not specific intent to the author's ultimate publication or knowledge that the acts violate the Constitution.

**Authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Constitutional protection does not arise whenever a plaintiff wishes to keep something private. The jury was instructed on intimate home, marriage and family information, the expectation of a reasonable person in Sheets's position and his preponderance burden. Those objective criteria and the supported intimate subject permit the Court to decide legal coverage without adopting a wholly subjective preference. Markman's patent-construction allocation does not transfer these historical facts and credibility choices to appellate judges, or surrender the governing constitutional rule to the jury.

Farnsworth's testimony supports an assurance and its communication; Bell's failure to recall it and disputed notice to George do not become contrary appellate findings. Lindsey identified possible official sources. Farnsworth denied disclosure; Cannon lacked the diary and detailed notes; George acknowledged detailed excerpts and access to his files. Only Lindsey's book quoted the diary. Taken favorably to the verdict, that circumstantial evidence supports intentional disclosure by George and resulting publication. A direct admission or eyewitness account was unnecessary. Excluded writings unconnected to these passages do not prove that the diary's intimate contents were already public.

The defendants did not object to the intent instruction. The Court neither adds a purpose to violate a known right nor requires a newly found specific desire for Lindsey's eventual publication. It also does not treat lawful investigative sharing itself as the deprivation. The protected interest and supported causal disclosure distinguish this case from confidential retention.

No additional necessary criterion adopted here lacks factual support. A new trial is therefore unnecessary merely because the Court declines to adopt the circuit's comprehensive compelling-interest and least-intrusive-disclosure formulation. The judgment stands within the reviewed question; independent municipal requirements, conduct-date immunity and unreviewed award issues remain distinct.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Whalen v. Roe, 429 U.S. 589: develops its confidentiality foundation into the present actual-disclosure protection while preserving its approval of safeguarded collection and acknowledging its express reservation. Brennan's compelling-interest concurrence is not adopted as a universal rule.
- Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, 433 U.S. 425: applies the significance of legitimate personal privacy independently of ownership and distinguishes safeguarded archival screening from this unjustified dissemination; its governmental-custody holding remains intact.
- Paul v. Davis, 424 U.S. 693: preserves its reputation/arrest-record holding and distinguishes the exposure of intimate marital information; no universal tangible-economic-loss prerequisite follows.
- Collins v. Harker Heights, February 26, 1992: preserves the distinction between state obligations and a federal injury, and between that injury and municipal attribution. Its workplace-omission holding does not foreclose the present affirmative-disclosure claim.
- Albright v. Oliver, January 24, 1994: its separate constitutional-source, prosecution/remedy and additional-injury rationales remain distinct; no universal displacement, state-tort cure or tangible-loss rule is derived from a judgment without a controlling rationale.
- Hodge v. Jones, October 23, 1995: its confidential-retention immunity and mootness holdings remain intact. The broader disclosure merits it reserved are decided only at the present confined scope.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date precedent on a properly preserved immunity defense; today's recognition neither manufactures earlier notice nor opens an unpreserved defense.
- Markman v. Westview Instruments, April 23, 1996: its patent-construction allocation remains at its actual scope and supplies no general power to retry historical facts in this constitutional action.

No precedent is overruled. The Court adopts neither a general informational-privacy framework nor a new compensatory-damages or fee standard.

Effective April 29, 1996, the confined protection recognized here distinguishes supported official acts causing publication from ordinary investigative use or public archival availability. Official possession, a state assurance or subjective preference alone does not establish the right, and public inspectability does not automatically extinguish the claim or prove prior dissemination. Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure remain subject to their own rules.

The decision establishes no deletion entitlement, inherited privacy claim, copyright right, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author's speech. It imposes no universal scrutiny formula, heightened civil proof burden or new publication-purpose requirement. Independent municipal attribution, historical individual notice and ordinary judgment and fee requirements remain intact.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents. He accepts for purposes of review the supported intentional disclosure, causation and Gary's own intimate injury, but finds no established constitutional guarantee of the asserted kind. Whalen deliberately reserved unwarranted dissemination, and Nixon sustained protected governmental screening rather than recognizing this damages entitlement. Decisional-autonomy precedents do not themselves establish general control over information held by government. Paul's refusal to constitutionalize the arrest-record claim reinforces the need to identify the actual federal guarantee, without making every intimate disclosure identical to reputation harm.

The dissent therefore finds the majority's extension unsupported even though the conduct may violate a promise, state duties or ordinary privacy expectations. Scalia's Albright source objection and Thomas's distinct insistence on a demonstrated substantive guarantee support that conclusion; they do not establish a general rule that state tort remedies cure an otherwise recognized substantive violation. Hodge left this merits question open. The dissent does not infer prior publication from archive availability, deny the accepted intimate injury, make Kathy's authorship dispositive or require tangible economic loss.

The two would reverse the reviewed constitutional ruling, vacate the federal judgment dependent on this right and remand for disposition consistent with rejection of the §1983 theory. Consequential prevailing-party fee treatment would follow under governing law. They direct no new factual trial, state-tort adjudication, refund amount or independent fee rule, and make no new ruling on personal immunity or the reasonableness of the awards.

### Denius v. Dunlap — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling law](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following public holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### Independent legal consultation cannot be burdened by an unjustified employment condition

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may not condition continued employment on a blanket authorization to disclose confidential communications used to obtain independent legal advice without an adequate employment-related justification for that burden. The rule protects access to legal assistance; it does not make every evidentiary privilege a constitutional right or bar lawful investigation and genuinely pertinent, confined inquiries.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the rule and affirm restoration of the equitable claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Button and Trainmen protect the ability to obtain legal assistance. Shelton's employment-disclosure principle and Pickering's employment balance prevent use of a public job to burden protected independent activity without an adequate reason. The Court applies those protections to the actual demand: the attorney clause reaches independent consultation unrelated to Denius's teaching and burdens his future ability to seek advice. No employment reason for that additional access has been established. Loss of employment for refusing the condition supplies a concrete injury even without proof that officials already obtained a confidential communication.

The employer answers that the earlier cases concerned litigation and association, rather than a general civil privilege. The Court does not turn the privilege into an absolute constitutional shield. Confidential consultation enables people to identify and assert their legal rights; a blanket employment condition reaches that protected activity directly. Ordinary privilege rules and independently lawful investigation continue to operate. A privilege exception does not itself justify an unrelated employment demand.

The form's actual terms govern the condition imposed. An uncommunicated intention to conduct only screening already authorized does not confine those terms. The equitable claim therefore returns for adjudication under this rule. This is First Amendment protection of independent legal assistance, not a Sixth Amendment right without an attached criminal proceeding. No final equitable entitlement or compensable injury is established by recognition of the right alone.

#### An employment demand for intimate medical information requires actual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. The court must identify the actual covered information, its intimacy, the governmental need, and the scope, use and safeguards of the demand; the rule creates no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or mandatory-factor test.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this acquisition protection and conditional equitable remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects objectively intimate personal information against unjustified official dissemination. The personal interest can also be burdened when the government demands access as the price of retaining employment. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection. Doe sustained a confined benefits-related inquiry while assuming the asserted right; it did not authorize access detached from that function. The Court now recognizes the bounded acquisition protection necessary to decide this case.

Relevant fitness verification can serve a legitimate employment need. That possibility does not itself justify unlimited access to treatment, family affairs or other unrelated intimate information. The actual connection and protections must support the demand imposed, and undisclosed benign intentions cannot replace them. Conversely, an administrative imperfection does not alone prove a constitutional violation, and every datum need not be indispensable.

The authorization's one-year duration limits time, not the breadth of its subject matter. Its opening language is broad, but medical records are not expressly enumerated. The district court must determine whether the authorization actually reaches them. If it does, the court applies the rule to covered intimate material and the supported employment justification. Coverage, personal responsibility, causation and appropriate relief remain factual or remedial work under the announced law; they do not make recognition of the right provisional.

#### Financial records are protected only to the extent they reveal objectively intimate matters

**Holding and operative rule:** Financial information falls within the protected personal interest only to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. A court cannot establish constitutional coverage from the financial label, the breadth of an authorization, or the owner's preference for secrecy alone; once protected content is identified, the employer's demand is governed by the same actual-need, scope, use and safeguards rule as other intimate information.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices vacate the categorical financial-privacy determination while preserving adjudication of the equitable claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets grounds protection in objectively intimate life, rather than a general right to keep every record secret. That principle supplies the relevant boundary here. An ordinary loan, credit rating or business record is not constitutionally protected merely because it is financial or confidential. The same kind of record may reveal intimate personal or family matters in a particular instance, and its content then requires the justification and protection the Court has described.

Denius has identified no particular protected financial item. The loss of employment makes his challenge concrete, but does not establish coverage of every financial record. The Court of Appeals therefore could not infer a categorical constitutional interest from the authorization's breadth alone. Its determination is vacated at that point, and the equitable claim returns for identification of the information and application of the governing rule. This is an identified content boundary, not a reservation whether objectively intimate financial material can ever be protected. Other undeveloped informational categories and the separate release-of-liability theories were not preserved for this review and are not decided.

#### Conduct-date qualified immunity bars the attorney and financial damages claims

**Holding and operative rule:** An official remains protected from personal damages when all relevant law at the time of the challenged conduct did not give sufficiently particularized objective notice that the conduct violated the asserted right; an identical prior case is unnecessary, but later recognition of a right supplies no retrospective notice. The 1996 law did not sufficiently establish these legal-consultation and intimate-financial acquisition extensions on this authorization, so the judgments granting personal damages immunity on those two claims are affirmed.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all eight Associate Justices adopt the rule and both immunity judgments. Stone-Zsela dissents on these components.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve the objective, particularized notice inquiry. Elder requires independent consideration of all relevant preconduct authority rather than only the cases a party happened to cite below. Crawford-El preserves that legal defense alongside ordinary proof, and Wasson confirms that an unsettled constitutional extension does not become actionable for damages merely because a present court could adopt it.

Button's access-to-counsel cases did not sufficiently establish this general employment-condition application in 1996. The financial precedents likewise did not give adequate notice of the protected reach now identified. Sheets's intimate-publication holding did not automatically decide acquisition or every financial record. Neither the 1997 Doe and Crawford-El decisions nor the present decision can supply 1996 notice.

The Court rejects a requirement that officials prove actual reliance on binding authority directly permitting their conduct. That would replace the objective immunity defense, not apply it. Constitutional violation, contemporaneous notice and injury remain distinct. The retained defense protects these two damages components; it does not justify dismissal of a separately available equitable claim, convert the demand into lawful conduct, or supply immunity to an entity or claim outside its personal-damages scope.

#### The medical damages claim presents a different notice question

**Holding and operative rule:** If the authorization reaches objectively intimate medical information, the existing summary-judgment record does not support personal damages immunity merely because no previous decision addressed this exact form. The cumulative law before the 1996 demand gave notice that an unlimited civilian-medical access condition, unsupported by an articulated countervailing employment purpose, could not be justified solely by public-employer status; medical coverage and genuinely supported factual or independent defenses remain for adjudication.

**Authority:** Part IV of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt this conduct-date notice ground. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh vote for the conditional medical damages remand but does not join this rationale. Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** Elder requires consideration of the cumulative conduct-date law. Pesce recognized the constitutional significance of employment-conditioned confidential disclosure while sustaining justified child-abuse reporting. Schaill recognized substantial medical confidentiality while sustaining limited disclosure to an appropriate official for a concrete program need. Anderson v. Romero distinguished the recognized civilian medical interest from the unsettled prisoner claim on which it granted immunity. Considered with Whalen and the then-effective Sheets holding, those decisions gave notice of the no-justification boundary relevant here.

The contrary argument correctly observes that those cases involved different circumstances, often upheld disclosure, and did not decide this unexecuted authorization. Those limits do not erase the distinction between justified, confined medical access and an unlimited demand supported only by the existence of public employment. The Court does not treat the circuit cases as controlling Supreme Court holdings or assert that they adjudicated this form.

Actual medical coverage is disputed, and no personal liability follows before it is established. The undisclosed narrow screening intention cannot supply justification for the broader written condition. A genuinely supported material employment justification, personal causation, injury and other proper defenses remain subject to ordinary proceedings. The judgment restores the medical damages claim for that work; it neither finds a violation after trial nor awards compensation. The rule announced today is not itself the source of historical notice.

#### Precedent treatment

- NAACP v. Button and Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen v. Virginia: extend protection of obtaining legal assistance to this unjustified compulsory employment authorization; ordinary privilege and investigation rules remain distinct.
- Shelton v. Tucker: applies the principle that an employment disclosure condition requires justification for its actual burden on protected independent activity.
- Pickering v. Board of Education: applies the relationship between the employee's protected activity and the employer's actual functional justification.
- Connick v. Myers: preserves contextual public-employment analysis; supplies no categorical permission to demand all independent legal confidences.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applies its requirement that a public employer justify the actual unrelated burden; the present rule does not import a universal higher test for every advance employment policy.
- Waters v. Churchill: preserves its genuine-ground distinction and separate causation and immunity inquiries; an undisclosed intention does not alter the written condition at issue.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County: preserves its intimate-publication holding and objectively intimate-content boundary; develops protection against an unjustified employment demand for access without converting Sheets itself into an earlier acquisition holding.
- Whalen v. Roe: preserves legitimate safeguarded collection; its reservation of possible abuses does not foreclose the bounded acquisition right recognized here.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority: preserves its assumed-right disposition of confined benefits administration; distinguishes that supported function from the unrestricted additional access demanded here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the Court declines to replace it with an actual-authoritative-reliance defense.
- Elder v. Holloway: applies independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date authority, without requiring an identical case or importing later notice.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserves ordinary proof, actual causation and lawful procedural screening alongside immunity; its later date supplies no 1996 notice.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves distinct present constitutional merits and contemporaneous notice; its unresolved attributed-speech question does not decide the different medical-law notice record here.
- Pesce v. J. Sterling Morton High School District 201, Schaill v. Tippecanoe County School Corp., and Anderson v. Romero: considered as preconduct circuit authority for the cumulative civilian-medical notice inference, with their justified-disclosure and prisoner-specific limitations retained; none becomes a controlling Supreme Court precedent by that use.

#### Law after decision

Public employment cannot be conditioned on an unjustified blanket surrender of independent legal consultation. Compulsory access to objectively intimate medical information and comparably intimate content in financial records requires an actual legitimate employment connection and reasonably fitted scope, use and protection. General financial secrecy, a disclosure label and a confidentiality preference do not suffice. Actual coverage and content must be identified.

This decision recognizes the bounded acquisition protection left undecided in Doe and distinguishes it from Sheets's public-dissemination holding. The ordinary conduct-date qualified-immunity defense remains in force. Attorney and financial damages immunity is affirmed, while the medical damages claim returns on the distinct cumulative notice ground and actual-coverage condition. Personal immunity does not eliminate live equitable claims. No fractured-decision question or new mandatory scrutiny formula is created.

### Denius v. Dunlap — 2001-01-17

[Published writings](../records/Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md#separate-writings); noncontrolling except for expressly identified joins to the Court's opinion.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part, concurring in the judgment in part, and dissenting in part.** He joins the legal-consultation and intimate-information holdings and their equitable remedies. He agrees that Button and Trainmen protect independent legal assistance, that the actual written condition governs, and that objectively intimate medical and financial content requires a justified and suitably confined governmental demand. These grounds do not constitutionalize ordinary financial secrecy or dispense with proof of coverage and injury.

He would replace the broader clearly-established-law defense with a narrow defense requiring defendants to prove genuine, objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the demand. Legal silence and routine supervisory power would not suffice. Justified factual judgment belongs in the constitutional merits analysis; uncertainty in precedent should not independently defeat compensation for a proved deprivation. Authoritative legal permission would protect actual, reasonable reliance without immunizing conduct merely because no earlier decision used the same authorization form. This changes the defense preserved by Elder, Crawford-El and Wasson; it does not claim the new acquisition rule was established in 1996.

He would therefore reverse attorney damages immunity, affirm the medical damages remand on his distinct defense rule, and vacate financial damages immunity insofar as it forecloses a qualifying intimate-information claim solely for lack of a previously specific decision. The record identifies no binding permission for the unrestricted demand. Remand would permit a properly supported reliance defense and adjudication of actual scope, participation, justification, protected content, causation and injury. His position awards no damages or reinstatement and does not affect independently required live-equitable-relief conditions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the attorney and financial damages-immunity holding. They reject the First Amendment extension because Button and Trainmen protect litigation and expressive association rather than a general constitutional guarantee covering individual civil legal confidences. The absence of an identified constitutional source is not cured by the demand's poor justification or by treating every evidentiary privilege as protected activity. They accept the employment injury and the distinction between immunity and equitable relief.

They also reject extending substantive due process from Sheets's actual public-dissemination holding to compulsory access or authorization. Their position, reflected in the Sheets dissent and Doe concurrence, requires a demonstrated constitutional source for the new acquisition guarantee; intimate subject matter and state confidentiality expectations do not alone supply one. They accept Sheets as binding within its decided scope and do not treat information held by a third party as necessarily public.

Independently, they would restore medical damages immunity. Pesce and Schaill upheld justified disclosures in different settings, and Anderson v. Romero distinguished other interests while granting immunity. Those sources did not sufficiently establish the asserted application to an unexecuted authorization with disputed medical coverage. They would reject the three constitutional equitable claims on the grounds stated and retain damages immunity for each category. Their position makes no finding that the demand was wise or that a state-law remedy displaces a proved federal right.
