## Standards and Tests selection: Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation

### Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation

**Governing question:** When a prisoner’s §1983 damages claim must await invalidation of the conviction or sentence.

**Current rule:** For an in-custody claimant with a real lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, a §1983 damages claim whose success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that conviction or sentence cannot proceed before favorable invalidation. Qualifying invalidation is reversal on direct appeal, executive expungement, a declaration of invalidity by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling it into question. The court compares the actual civil theory and criminal basis, not labels. Independent injuries whose success is compatible with the criminal judgment remain separately cognizable. The barred claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation; the rule is not a direction to file now and seek a stay or an invented tolling regime. Within the in-custody category, damages or retrospective declarations alleging dishonest concealment of exculpatory evidence or disabling adjudicator bias in an outstanding earned-credit deprivation likewise require invalidation when success necessarily defeats that adjudication. Classify each alleged procedural injury by its legal consequence: a genuinely independent failure to state supporting facts and compatible prospective relief may proceed under ordinary requirements. An invalidity-dependent claim is dismissed without prejudice, not stayed for exhaustion; failed review or loss of a review opportunity is not qualifying invalidation.

Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. ## Standards and Tests selection: Expired parole-revocation sentences and continuing habeas injury

### Expired parole-revocation sentences and continuing habeas injury

**Governing question:** Continuing Article III injury after custody under a challenged parole revocation ends.

**Current rule:** Custody under the challenged revocation at filing satisfies §2254’s statutory threshold, but Article III independently requires a live personal injury throughout review. After the revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed from revocation as it may be from a conviction; the petitioner must establish a concrete continuing consequence redressable in the habeas action. Speculative future uses and delay alone do not supply it.

**Authority by component:**

- Custody at filing distinct from continuing injury; no presumed revocation consequences: Spencer v. Kemna, 1998-03-03; Breyer Part I with O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; Part II continuing-injury ground additionally joined by Stone-Zsela.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** Capable-of-repetition review requires both an inherently too-short duration and reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Litigation delay or fault creates no jurisdiction by estoppel. No categorical exclusion of reputational injury is adopted. Heck and Edwards’s former-prisoner and realistic-review-opportunity reservations remain open; possible difficulty with a separate civil remedy supplies no damages claim or continuing habeas injury here.

**Related current rules:** Habeas custody in a continuous consecutive sentence series concerns a different unexpired sentence aggregate.

**Holdings navigation:** Spencer v. Kemna — Mootness and completed sentences.

## Standards and Tests selection: Dismissal and counsel requests under amended §1915

### Dismissal and counsel requests under amended §1915

**Governing question:** Screening despite partial or full fee payment and assistance for persons unable to afford counsel.

**Current rule:** Under original amended §1915(e)(2), the court shall dismiss at any time, notwithstanding any filing fee or portion paid, when it determines that the poverty allegation is untrue or the action or appeal is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim or seeks monetary relief from an immune defendant. Section 1915(e)(1) permits the court to request an attorney for any person unable to afford counsel; it does not itself compel an appointment or establish eligibility.

**Authority by component:**

- New screening commands and separate counsel discretion: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §804(a)(5), original §1915(e), April 26, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** PLRA, April 26, 1996.

**Effective transition:** The statute was enacted April 26, 1996. Section 802's express all-prospective-relief transition is not generalized to §1997e or §1915; disputed application to pending matters remains unresolved unless an applicable authority separately resolves it.

**Unresolved interaction:** Denton construes former discretionary §1915(d). Its irrational-or-wholly-incredible factual standard and nonmerits distinctions are not a holding construing every new mandatory ground or the appellate standard under amended §1915(e). The new enactment does not convert every pleading failure into factual frivolousness.

### Heck v. Humphrey, No. 93-6188

Merits decision, June 24, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

512 U.S. 477. Argued April 18, 1994; decided June 24, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 997 F.2d 355, affirming dismissal without prejudice. The question is whether a state prisoner may pursue §1983 damages claims necessarily negating an outstanding conviction and how that action relates to habeas. Heck seeks reinstatement; the officials seek affirmance.

Heck was serving a fifteen-year voluntary-manslaughter sentence when he alleged unlawful investigation, destruction of exculpatory evidence and an unlawful voice-identification procedure by prosecutors James Humphrey and Robert Ewbank and investigator Michael Krinoph. He sought compensatory and punitive damages, not release or an injunction. The allegations are not findings. His conviction was upheld on direct review; one federal habeas petition failed for lack of exhaustion and another was denied. The lower courts characterized the action as necessarily challenging the conviction; Heck did not dispute that characterization in his petition or principal merits brief, and the late reply does not establish a separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participated at argument and decision. No Justice is recused.

#### Judgment

**Dismissal without prejudice affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

Dismissal rests on lack of present cognizability, rejecting conversion into habeas and a general exhaustion rationale.

No present damages action, stay or remand; a later claim may proceed after qualifying invalidation, subject to remaining elements and defenses.

No release, misconduct finding, damages award, immunity determination or state-law preclusion ruling is ordered. The claim barred by this prerequisite has not accrued; its limitations period is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet. Independently cognizable injuries are not barred merely because a conviction exists.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter throughout; Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg in Parts I, III and IV | Unanimous affirmance | I: bounded cognizability rule; II: five-Justice statutory coordination; III: invalidation and accrual; IV: independent injuries and present application |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg | Joins Parts I, III and IV and affirmance; does not join Part II | Common-law-informed favorable-invalidation ground, including the view that release from custody alone does not remove the prerequisite |
| Concurrence | Souter | Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor | Joins the Court's opinion and affirmance | Statutory reason for protecting people genuinely outside the habeas intersection; no join by Stone-Zsela in that affirmative extension |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record

**Controlling proposition:** A state prisoner in Heck's position, in custody with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, has no presently cognizable §1983 damages claim when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that outstanding conviction or sentence, unless the judgment has undergone qualifying invalidation. The rule decides that setting; it does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide plaintiffs who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity or whose custody has ended.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the necessary effect of the civil claim, not its connection with a criminal prosecution, determines the present bar. Preiser identifies the special statutory channel for obtaining release from unlawful custody. Wolff preserves civil claims whose success does not determine an entitlement to release, and Patsy rejects a general exhaustion condition for civil-rights actions. None answers this damages claim simply by identifying the requested form of relief. Heck seeks money, but the accepted characterization of his action makes invalidity of the operative conviction indispensable to success. The absence of a release demand therefore does not establish present cognizability. Conversely, a mere possibility that civil liability might cast doubt on a conviction is insufficient. The Court imposes no jurisdictional immunity for officials and decides no constitutional misconduct claim. The question here concerns a prisoner who had a lawful avenue to contest custody; the broader consequences of lost or unavailable custody review do not receive a controlling answer.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): its release-channel distinction informs the new, bounded damages prerequisite; its holding is not misdescribed as already deciding damages-only claims.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974): preserves civil relief compatible with the operative conviction and custody judgment.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): remains the rule against general §1983 exhaustion; this decision addresses present cognizability instead.

##### II. Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite

**Controlling proposition:** For the in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim described in Part I, coordination of §1983 with the available habeas channel supports favorable invalidation as a prerequisite, with the common-law malicious-prosecution analogy implementing that coordination. This is a new, limited restriction on §1983, not conversion of the damages complaint into habeas, a general exhaustion rule, or adoption of every malicious-prosecution element.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court recognizes the cost of withholding an otherwise available form of compensation. Section 1983 does not expressly require favorable termination, and habeas cannot award the damages Heck requests. Those objections prevent treating the prerequisite as an obvious consequence of the damages label or as a cost-free procedural arrangement. Preiser nevertheless requires respect for the specific route by which this prisoner can contest custody. Wolff and Patsy preserve the civil remedy without answering whether it may establish the opposite of an outstanding criminal judgment. Carey permits common-law principles to inform the remedy; the favorable-termination analogy supplies a way to coordinate these statutes without importing malice or want of probable cause into every constitutional claim. Deferred accrual preserves a later compensation action if lawful invalidation occurs. This reasoning suffices for Heck because his civil theory necessarily negates the judgment and he has had a lawful custody-review channel. It does not resolve the materially different position of a person lacking that opportunity or no longer in custody.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: extends statutory coordination to the particular damages claim while preserving the distinct relief and conditions of habeas.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves its permissible civil-remedy category; factual overlap with prosecution does not remove a claim from that category.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguishes general exhaustion from the newly adopted prerequisite; exhaustion alone cannot satisfy it.
- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978): applies common-law remedial guidance as an aid to construing the federal remedy, not a wholesale importation of a state tort.

##### III. Qualifying invalidation and deferred accrual

**Controlling proposition:** The claim barred under Part I becomes cognizable only when the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. That claim accrues only upon such invalidation; exhaustion, unsuccessful review, or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation, and neither immediate accrual with equitable tolling nor a preservation stay is required for this unaccrued claim.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt the four invalidation routes and the accrual consequence for the barred claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court makes nonaccrual integral to the prerequisite. A rule withholding the cause of action while allowing its limitations period to expire would frustrate the later remedy the rule preserves. Carey's use of appropriate common-law remedial principles supports considering the completion of the claim rather than pretending that a presently barred action must already be filed. The Seventh Circuit's suggested equitable protection for a diligent litigant addresses a different premise: accrual before the invalidity prerequisite is met. The Court does not adopt that premise. Heck's conviction was sustained on direct review; one federal petition failed for lack of exhaustion and another was denied. Neither result meets any qualifying invalidation route. Patsy's protection against general exhaustion remains intact, but completing review cannot itself establish the opposite of the judgment reviewed. Dismissal without prejudice therefore preserves the possibility of a later action following invalidation, subject to its remaining elements and defenses. Accrual of an independently cognizable injury is not decided by this rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Carey v. Piphus: applies its common-law-informed approach to the federal remedy without imposing additional constitutional-liability elements.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: preserves the distinction between exhaustion and existence of a cognizable damages claim.

###### Limits and questions not reached

Recognition of a conviction's invalidation by an authorized state tribunal identifies one circumstance in which the present §1983 prerequisite is satisfied. It does not itself create jurisdiction, confer authority to reopen the underlying conviction, or prescribe grounds upon which a state tribunal must grant collateral relief. Those questions remain governed by the independently applicable law authorizing that tribunal's action.

##### IV. Independent injuries, claim-specific analysis and this dismissal

**Controlling proposition:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity falls outside this prerequisite even if it concerns the same investigation or trial; courts must compare the civil claim's elements with the basis of the conviction, and compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury. On the accepted characterization of Heck's presented action, dismissal without prejudice stands on lack of present cognizability, without deciding misconduct, immunity, personal responsibility, preclusion, damages, or entitlement to release.

**Authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt this boundary, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court does not equate every investigative wrong with an invalid conviction. Wolff preserves compatible civil claims, while Cavanaugh permits an opportunity-only parole claim whose success establishes neither invalidity nor shorter custody. An unlawful-search claim may likewise coexist with a valid conviction because independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error can preserve the criminal judgment. That possibility neither saves every Fourth Amendment claim nor defeats every evidence-related claim. Carey still requires a compensable injury; conviction or imprisonment injury cannot be recovered while its necessary invalidity remains unestablished. Ordinary causation, immunity and preclusion questions remain separate. Here the lower courts characterized the action as necessarily attacking the conviction, and Heck did not dispute that characterization in his petition or principal merits brief. His late reply does not establish a separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand. The Court accepts that review posture without making findings that officials committed the alleged wrongs or that every conceivable severable theory would fail.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves the distinction between compatible civil relief and relief dependent on invalidating custody.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: distinguishes its opportunity-only claim; the existing §1983 holding remains intact.
- Carey v. Piphus: applies the requirement of actual compensable injury without adjudicating the amount or merits here.

#### Authority limits and questions not reached

The nine-vote propositions in Parts I, III and IV and the five-vote statutory explanation in Part II have direct support. No Marks aggregation is necessary or used. The common-law rationale extending beyond continued custody has four votes, as does the affirmative statutory protection for people genuinely outside the habeas intersection. Neither becomes a Court holding. No universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action, automatic state-law preclusion, automatic conversion into habeas, or abstention rule for pending criminal proceedings is adopted. The application of this prerequisite to former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity remains unresolved by the Court. Custis's separate no-meaningful-review reservation for the ACCA sentencing channel remains intact.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

The holding-specific treatments above govern. Cavanaugh's opportunity-only §1983 rule remains intact. Albright supplies no general constitutional malicious-prosecution holding, and this decision creates none. Custis's ACCA sentencing rule and its no-meaningful-review reservation remain confined to that channel. Reed's distinction between federal habeas jurisdiction and collateral entitlement remains intact; denial of review is not qualifying invalidation. No precedent is overruled.

Effective June 24, 1994, the particular in-custody necessary-invalidity damages claim is subject to favorable invalidation and deferred accrual. Courts must examine the civil claim and the basis of the conviction, preserving claims whose success can coexist with the criminal judgment. Parts I, III and IV have nine votes; Part II's limited statutory explanation has five. The Court adopts neither a universal custody-independent bar nor an affirmative outside-habeas exception.

Section 2254's custody requirement, exhaustion command and merits inquiry remain distinct. The exhaustion exceptions for absence of an available state corrective process or circumstances rendering that process ineffective to protect the prisoner's rights remain in force. Neither exception invalidates a conviction by itself. Ordinary immunity, personal responsibility, causation, damages and applicable preclusion defenses remain for a cognizable action. Pending-criminal-case abstention is not decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg, agrees with the bounded rules and dismissal but would rest the prerequisite on the common-law-informed character of a claim necessarily contradicting an outstanding criminal judgment. Carey permits that remedial guidance, and the favorable-termination analogy addresses the contradiction without creating a freestanding constitutional malicious-prosecution tort. These Justices do not make continued custody or present habeas jurisdiction an element: release alone does not permit the contradictory damages judgment. They reject generic exhaustion, automatic habeas conversion and blanket preclusion. Independent injuries remain available under their own elements and defenses, and invalidity-dependent damages accrue only upon qualifying invalidation. Their broader sufficient ground has four votes; it does not become the Court's rule for persons outside custody or displace Custis's distinct sentencing reservation.

Justice Souter, joined by Justices Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor, joins the Court's opinion but explains an affirmative boundary beyond the case decided. Preiser justifies reconciliation where the habeas and civil-rights statutes intersect; Patsy and Wolff resist turning that reconciliation into an indiscriminate civil-remedy prohibition. Common-law analogy may implement the statutory accommodation but cannot itself eliminate redress for people genuinely outside habeas's reach. These Justices therefore would not impose the same prerequisite on such plaintiffs merely because favorable termination would be demanded by a common-law analogy. For Heck, who could contest custody, they accept all four invalidation routes, delayed accrual and dismissal without prejudice. They reject conversion and premature merits or immunity rulings. Their affirmative outside-habeas conclusion has four votes; the Court leaves that question unresolved.

The [Seventh Circuit opinion, 997 F.2d 355](https://static.case.law/f2d/997/html/0355-01.html), supplies the lower judgment and its conversion, exhaustion and tolling approach. The reported voluntary-manslaughter conviction controls the offense description. The full underlying record and every brief were not independently available; the question is stated in substance, and no unreported preserved count, misconduct finding or verbatim grant wording is asserted.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review is complete. The dismissal without prejudice stands. The Court orders no release, stay, automatic conversion or further merits proceedings. If a qualifying invalidation occurs, Heck may pursue an otherwise proper action subject to its remaining requirements; invalidation alone does not establish liability or a damages amount. No precise mandate-issuance date is stated.

### Spencer v. Kemna, No. 96-7171

Decision after plenary review, March 3, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Eighth Circuit, which affirmed dismissal of the §2254 petition. Spencer challenges the September 1992 parole revocation, not the original burglary and stealing convictions. He filed on April 1, 1993 while imprisoned under the revocation; the challenged sentence expired October 16, 1993. Review concerns statutory custody, continuing Article III injury, mootness exceptions and the asserted effect of possible civil-remedy limits.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Affirm dismissal as moot, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens.

The dismissal rests on absence of a continuing Article III controversy, not failure of custody when the petition was filed or a decision on the revocation’s merits. Stevens would reverse and remand the live petition for its remaining lawful adjudication.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Affirms mootness dismissal | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join all parts; Stone-Zsela joins Part II’s actual-continuing-injury ground and mootness result, but agrees only with Part II’s capable-of-repetition and litigation-delay dispositions and Parts I and III’s ancillary dispositions, without those additional rationales |
| Dissent | Stevens | — | Would reverse and remand | Recognizes the particular official adverse adjudication’s continuing reputational consequence; does not decide the revocation merits or an affirmative section 1983 exception |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without an additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Controlling explanation:** Spencer was imprisoned under this revocation on April 1, 1993. The District Court’s alternative suggestion that expiration removed statutory custody is incorrect. The later seven-year imprisonment arises from an unrelated conviction, rather than the unexpired consecutive aggregate addressed in Garlotte. A timely filing therefore satisfies the statutory entry condition without itself supplying an enduring constitutional controversy. Lujan and Arizonans require an actual personal interest throughout adjudication, not simply an interest that existed when suit began.

##### II. The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding and operative rule:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Authority:** Part II’s continuing-injury rule and mootness result have eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela and the seven Associates joining the Court opinion. The seven Associates also directly adopt the specific exception and delay applications.

**Controlling explanation:** An attack on a conviction is distinct from this challenge to a revocation of an otherwise valid sentence. Lane supports examining the actual surviving interest, although Spencer, unlike Lane’s respondents, challenges the adverse revocation finding itself. Future parole use depends on discretionary treatment, sentencing use on later legal developments, impeachment on future testimony and permissible use, and other-crimes evidence on additional contingencies. No present mandatory disability or demonstrated imminent adverse use is established. MacArthur’s actual statutory information interest and existing damages action do not make every retained adverse record a live habeas controversy; the Court adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both a duration too short for full review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Neither the asserted delay nor possible injury to others establishes those conditions. State extensions and delayed judicial action cannot create Article III power through fault or estoppel. Expiration occurred by the sentence’s terms, not a newly established strategic cessation. The Court decides no revocation claim, criminal guilt or exoneration.

##### III. An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition of this ancillary contention without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Heck’s decided prerequisite concerns the specified in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim; Edwards preserves that limit while requiring genuinely independent injuries to be classified by their consequences. Neither decision supplies the universal former-prisoner bar assumed by Spencer’s argument. A claim whose success does not necessarily invalidate the revocation would also require its own analysis. The Court need not choose an affirmative former-prisoner exception to reject the asserted inevitable bar. No damages claim is pleaded here, and possible difficulty obtaining another remedy does not empower the Court to decide an otherwise moot habeas petition. The existing reservations remain open rather than becoming either a categorical bar or a newly adjudicated exception.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

#### Law After Decision

A timely custody-based habeas filing does not survive expiration of the challenged sentence without continuing Article III injury or a valid exception. This record establishes neither. The revocation’s merits remain unadjudicated, and the separate civil-remedy questions expressly reserved by Heck and Edwards remain open.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens distinguishes Lane because Spencer challenges the official adverse revocation finding itself, not merely completed custody while leaving that finding untouched. He considers the particular government adjudication and the State’s continuing reliance on the incorporated serious-misconduct report a present personal reputational consequence that invalidation could meaningfully remove. He does not presume every revocation carries statutory disabilities or depend on a future crime, audience reaction or invented damages claim. The written order found condition violations and incorporated a report; it was not an express rape conviction, and the State’s characterization remains distinct. MacArthur’s requirement of a supported personal interest does not, in his view, erase this already imposed official determination. He would remand for unresolved lawful adjudication, without granting a writ, finding procedural wrongdoing or deciding the civil-remedy question.

#### Source Notes

The revocation order, incorporated report, custody dates, claimed procedural defects and later separate sentence are reported in [523 U.S. 1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-1.pdf). The woman’s allegations, Spencer’s admitted cocaine use and denial of force, the written condition-violation order and the State’s description remain distinct. The underlying reports and complete record are described through published recitations; they establish no criminal conviction for the alleged conduct. No reputation-only submission or damages demand by Spencer is attributed to him. [Lane, 455 U.S. 624](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-455/pdf/USREPORTS-455-624.pdf), supplies the earlier posture and continuing-interest analysis.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The habeas action remains dismissed as moot, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new revocation hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered. This Supreme Court review is complete, and no continuing return condition is created.

**End of entry: Spencer v. Kemna, decision after plenary review, March 3, 1998.**
