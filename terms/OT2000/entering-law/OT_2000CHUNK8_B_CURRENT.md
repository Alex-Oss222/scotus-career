# June 25 current-term public law and positions

### Artuz v. Bennett — 2000-11-07

Ginsburg's unanimous opinion affirms: under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a state collateral application is properly filed when delivery and acceptance comply with the rules governing filing the application. Claim-specific mandatory relief bars do not defeat otherwise proper filing. New York Criminal Procedure Law §440.10(2)(a) requires denial after merits appellate adjudication unless a subsequent retroactively effective change in controlling law occurred; paragraph (c) requires record facts sufficient for adequate appellate review and omitted review caused by an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or raise the issue on a perfected appeal. Both govern claim relief, not delivery and acceptance. Neither provision is found actually applicable to a particular Bennett claim.

Clerk acceptance does not cure a genuine filing defect. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, fees and filing preconditions remain distinct; the Court does not classify every rule without its terms. Whether deadline exceptions can preserve proper filing of a late application remains open. The Court fixes no pending period or tolling duration and makes no finding about receipt or absence of the written order; absence was an assumption below based on Bennett's allegation. Transitional timing, exhaustion, default and habeas entitlement remain outside the grant. Lindh's provision-specific temporal rule survives; it neither exempts this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA nor answers proper filing. The one-year framework and other habeas defenses remain effective; the remand awards no writ or release.

### Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices — 2000-12-11

Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, sustains Maine's $250 per-election legislative contribution ceiling, 6-3. The State retains the burden to demonstrate that a direct candidate-contribution ceiling is closely drawn to an objectively supported, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest while preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy in the actual campaign setting. Russell and Shrink Missouri supply that standard, not a fixed dollar floor, general $250 safe harbor or requirement that challengers prove campaigning impossible. Maine's evidence of contributor pressure for official accommodation, campaign scale, actual fundraising, per-election operation and usable private channels meets its burden on the combined record. Completed bribery and statistical proof are unnecessary. Static receipt projections establish a serious risk, not future causal loss; special-election experience is corroborative rather than conclusive, and donor percentages are not dollar-loss measures. Referendum support cannot settle constitutional rights, and public financing cannot cure an independently invalid private restriction.

Part II has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Both distinct triggers survive 5-4: responsive grants measured by private candidate financing, and grants measured by qualifying independent advocacy. A capped, support-qualified mechanism may respond to either where its demonstrated programmatic connection and actual operation establish neither an independent legal restraint nor substantial practical suppression of opposing lawful activity. The private speaker retains its funds, message, lawful fundraising and spending, without endorsement, attribution or an expenditure ceiling. The qualified recipient accepts restrictions in an actual contest; supplements are finite and adjusted for specified favorable independent spending. The record establishes competitive effects rather than substantial suppression. This is neither unlimited permission to offset speech nor a categorical claim that subsidies cannot burden it. The State cannot avoid examining the consequence by calling enrollment voluntary.

Buckley preserves the contribution/expenditure distinction and voluntary public funding with participant conditions; independent expenditures do not become coordinated contributions because a grant formula measures them. Reform Party rejects an automatic entitlement to parity or insulation from an opponent's public resources while preserving actual suppression review. Day's invalidating judgment and actual vote-supported propositions remain binding for its materially different mechanism. Neither its four-Justice expressive-burden rationale nor Stone-Zsela's separate rationale becomes controlling by aggregation. Daggett's five direct Part II joins require no narrowest-rationale inference.

Part III has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. It rejects an additional whole-system coercion claim because the combined program and sustained ceiling leave a meaningful private campaigning alternative on this record. Lawful components can combine into an unconstitutional burden; nominal voluntariness, attractive incentives or a better-funded opponent do not alone resolve that inquiry. Actual contribution capacity, the grant cap, participant duties and conditional shortage rule support the result. No fixed funding percentage, mandatory study or proof of a disappeared candidate is required. O'Connor's Part III join preserves her matching objection; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not reach this additional theory after their proposed targeted invalidations and supply no sustaining votes. All three rules preserve review of materially different suppressive applications. No component is invalidated and no severability remedy becomes operative.

**Operative statutory qualifications.** The legislative ceiling is per election; the individual candidate's own and spouse's contributions retain §1015(1)'s exception. That exception does not remove separate restrictions accepted by a certified public participant. A legislative participant must file the declaration, obtain ballot qualification and compliant seed funding, and collect qualifying $5 contributions from at least 150 registered district voters for a Senate race or 50 for a House race. Contributions go to the fund by check or money order during the qualifying period with the candidate's knowledge and approval, an identifying receipt, and no exchanged value. Seed money is limited to $100 per individual, including candidate or family funds, and aggregate legislative amounts of $1,500 for Senate or $500 for House, subject to Commission rule authority. Unspent seed money transfers to the fund at certification. Later private contributions require specific Commission authorization.

Contested-election initial grants use the corresponding office's average expenditures in contested races in the prior two applicable elections, with the initial 2000 amount reduced by 25 percent; inadequate data permits use of the most recent applicable elections. An uncontested primary uses the corresponding uncontested average or the contested average if lower. No grant issues for an uncontested general election.

The matching measure is the greater of expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, alone or with qualifying independent expenditures reported under §1019. Once that measure exceeds the applicable initial distribution, the Commission pays the opposing certified candidate the reported excess. Supplements stop at twice the relevant contested-primary or contested-general initial distribution, making the ordinary combined maximum three times that distribution. The regulatory deduction reduces matching payments for independent express advocacy favoring the participant or opposing the private opponent after that opponent exceeds the initial distribution; it never reduces the initial grant. No unpresented multiple-opponent formula is decided.

Section 1125(13) forbids spending beyond available fund revenue. If revenue cannot meet the initial or matching distributions, the Commission may authorize certified candidates to accept and spend private contributions up to the applicable funding amounts, reduced by seed money and subject to the legislative $250 per-donor-per-election ceiling and Commission rules. This is conditional permission, not a mandatory release or unlimited financing right. No actual shortage is found.

**Scope.** These qualifications describe the mechanism adjudicated; unchallenged administrative details receive no independent constitutional holding. The separate §1019 disclosure challenge, governor ceiling, unsupported party-specific theory, and unpursued primary-allocation and §1017 theories are outside this grant. Statutory participant terminology establishes no government command to stigmatize nonparticipants.

### Mariani v. United States — 2000-12-12

O'Connor's Part I has eight reasoned joins, all Associates; Stone-Zsela expressly agrees only to reach the questions and supplies no threshold ground. Section 437h's eligible-plaintiff requirement, Article III injury and each question's substantiality are distinct. It authorizes the Commission, a national party committee or a presidential-eligible voter to bring the appropriate constitutional action in district court, with qualifying questions immediately certified to the court of appeals en banc. Mariani's uncontested voter eligibility, concrete prosecution stake and developed constitutional challenge satisfy the separate requirements. The actual Reform Party tribunal rule requires independent substantiality; judicial economy or acquiescence is insufficient. Certiorari lies under §1254(1), not the direct Supreme Court appeal repealed in 1988. Its transition for then-pending cases and judgments predating effectiveness does not apply. The Fund Act's different procedure supplies no substitute, and the threshold establishes neither guilt nor standing over every hypothetical recipient application.

Parts II and III each have the same six direct joins—O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—and each affirm 6-3. Part II rejects categorical invalidity of the presented viewpoint-neutral prohibition on direct business-corporation treasury contributions to federal candidates under the sufficiently important anticorruption-interest and close-fit standard. Part III independently rejects invalidity based on the developed soft-money comparison: materially different party-financing channels do not require direct treasury transfers to candidates where the prohibition retains actual close fit to that direct-transfer risk. Candidate participation in party fundraising, party support, access and some coordinated advertising require examination but establish neither every transaction's legal identity nor its legality.

Russell, Shrink Missouri and the actual Daggett decision preserve the government's affirmative burden to justify the full source exclusion and meaningful association; no dollar safe harbor, impossibility requirement or automatic corporate-form exclusion results. Buckley's contribution distinction permits attention to candidate control over transferred funds. National Right to Work Committee's solicitation holding, commercial-source distinction and protection for economic payers support voluntary political financing separate from commercial receipts. Candidate-directed obligation and circumvention risk, not concentrated wealth or ordinary access alone, supports this restriction. Corporate administration and lawful solicitation cannot turn treasury money into the fund's contribution money. Available statutory channels establish no actual fund, adequate donor pool, resources or success for these companies. A materially different organization or supported operational burden remains separately reviewable.

Austin's independent-expenditure holding remains undisturbed and supplies neither automatic approval nor a direct-contribution exception. Akins retains actual candidate connection, definitions, thresholds, exclusions and separate reporting provisos; ideological agreement proves no coordination and reported coordination does not validate all party arrangements. Day's supported pooling, meaningful-association and nonprofit independent-spending protections survive, as does Daggett's cumulative-burden rule; public funding or a different association's resources cannot automatically cure an invalid private restraint. The corporate answers validate no unchallenged soft-money practice, independent expenditure, press activity or every corporate application.

Part IV has all nine joins. Truthful source attribution and evasion prevention independently justify rejecting the presented constitutional objections to covered candidate contributions in another person's name, knowing permission to use one's name, and knowing acceptance, including the alleged knowing reimbursement scheme. Different regulation elsewhere supplies no false-donor entitlement even for the corporate dissenters. Buckley supplies the source-information/enforcement interests; McIntyre's truthful anonymous individual advocacy remains distinct from candidate-finance attribution and tailored impersonation control. No general identification duty for all political association follows. A supported reasonable probability of threats, harassment or reprisals retains disclosure protection and a possible nondisclosure remedy, not permission to name a false donor; no such showing is established here.

The first §441f clause has no express knowledge word; the second and third do. The holding establishes neither strict criminal liability, statutory enforcement elements nor the validity of unpresented innocent-error applications. Prosecution must prove corporate source where relevant, attribution, knowledge applicable to the alleged knowing conduct and all other elements. Alleged reimbursements, legislative interest and soft-money practices prove neither transactions, a completed exchange, knowledge, actual influence nor guilt. The constitutional answers return the matter to ordinary criminal adjudication without finding any count sufficient or that every clause was charged. No acquittal, new penalty, severability remedy, replacement ceiling, damages, refund or order governing unchallenged party practices follows.

#### Statutory scope and operative qualifications

The direct-treasury holding concerns the presented business-corporation-to-federal-candidate class. Section 441b(a) covers national-bank and congressionally organized corporate contributions or expenditures in elections for any political office, including candidate-selection primaries, conventions, and caucuses. For corporations generally and labor organizations, it covers elections for presidential and vice-presidential electors, Senators, Representatives, Delegates, and Resident Commissioners, and the corresponding nomination processes. It separately prohibits knowing acceptance or receipt by candidates, political committees, or other persons, and consent to prohibited organizational contributions or expenditures by corporate or bank officers or directors and labor-organization officers. A labor organization includes an organization, agency, employee committee, or plan with employee participation and at least a partial purpose of dealing with employers over grievances, labor disputes, wages, pay rates, hours, or working conditions. These distinct classes receive no new independent holdings merely because they share the section; independent expenditures and press activity remain outside this decision.

Section 441b(b)(2) reaches direct or indirect payments, distributions, loans, advances, deposits, gifts, services, and value to candidates, campaign committees, political parties, or organizations in connection with covered elections. It excepts ordinary-course national or State bank loans complying with applicable banking law and regulations. Three separate exclusions protect corporate communications on any subject to stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families, or labor communications to members and families; nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote activity directed to the corresponding groups; and establishment, administration, and solicitation for separate segregated political funds by corporations, labor organizations, membership organizations, cooperatives, and nonstock corporations. The distinct membership-communication exclusion and reporting proviso in §431, preserved in Akins, are not substituted for these provisions.

The political fund cannot use value obtained by physical force, job discrimination, financial reprisal or threats; payments required for labor-organization membership or employment; or commercial transactions. Any person soliciting an employee must disclose, at the time of solicitation, the fund's political purpose and the employee's right to refuse without reprisal. Corporate support for establishment, administration, and solicitation does not authorize treasury money as the fund's candidate-contribution money.

Ordinary corporate solicitation reaches stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families; ordinary labor solicitation reaches members and their families. Corporations, labor organizations, and their funds may also make two written solicitations per calendar year to corporate stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, employees, and their families, mailed to residences and designed to conceal from the solicitor who gives $50 or less and who gives nothing. Additional routes preserve member solicitation by membership organizations, cooperatives, nonstock corporations, and their funds, and trade-association or trade-association-fund solicitation with a member corporation's separate, specific approval, limited to one association per corporation per calendar year. That trade-association route reaches the approving member corporation's stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families.

Lawful methods of soliciting or facilitating voluntary fund contributions available to corporations for stockholders and executive or administrative personnel must also be available to labor organizations for members. A corporation using those methods, including its subsidiaries, branches, divisions, and affiliates, must provide them on written request to a labor organization representing members working for those entities at reimbursement of expenses only. Executive or administrative personnel must be salaried rather than hourly and have policymaking, managerial, professional, or supervisory responsibility. These statutory routes establish no finding that the particular companies possess a successful fund, sufficient donor pool, or practical resources.

Section 441f contains no internal blanket exception. The actual contribution definition, true source, attributable reporting, knowledge and enforcement requirements, and supported associational protections retain their separate work. None of the indictment allegations supplies a completed exchange, corrupt intent, knowledge, or actual influence finding.

### Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle — 2001-04-23

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Childrens_Healthcare_Is_a_Legal_Duty_v_Min_De_Parle_merits_2001-04-23.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Childrens_Healthcare_Is_a_Legal_Duty_v_Min_De_Parle_merits_2001-04-23.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. This limited secular-care accommodation is permissible in its terms

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may accommodate religious refusal of medical treatment by reimbursing the independently identifiable nonmedical component of an existing health benefit for patients whose physical condition otherwise qualifies, subject to the enacted coverage limits and effective public control. The Constitution does not require this limited accommodation to include every comparable secular objection; the permission does not authorize devotional services, a subsidy entitlement or every current application.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I has five direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, including the author. The four other Justices dissent from this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos permits accommodation beyond constitutional compulsion. Kiryas Joel preserves that permission while prohibiting denominational allocation of governmental authority and an anomalous denominational franchise. This program removes a religious obstacle to receiving only the secular component of otherwise qualifying care; it does not confer the governmental franchise condemned there. The religious condition is a serious distinction, and removing a denomination's name cannot alone justify it. Its support here is the limited relationship between the physical need, the existing benefit and the refusal of the medical component. Agostini requires examination of the governmental function, not a presumption that religious motivation makes physical care devotional. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual financed use from institutional identity or possible diversion. These principles permit the stated accommodation while requiring separate review of the care actually purchased and the control actually exercised. The holding does not make all religion-specific benefits valid or establish that the present program operates lawfully in every application.

#### II. Initial institutional review does not necessarily transfer final public power

**Holding and operative rule:** An institution's initial coverage recommendation does not unconstitutionally delegate final governmental authority when public officials retain independent responsibility to determine statutory eligibility, may require sufficient objective information and may deny payment on an inadequate showing. Exemption from compelled religiously objectionable medical procedures does not eliminate the condition standard, all usable nonmedical evidence or review of billed services; formal retention of authority does not establish its meaningful operation in every application.

**Authority:** All nine directly join Breyer's Part II. This statutory-facial conclusion is independent of the eligibility and operational disagreements.

**Controlling explanation:** Larkin v. Grendel's Den prohibits the transfer of a final public licensing veto to religious bodies. Kiryas Joel prohibits denominational allocation of civil governmental authority. Neither equates an initial provider recommendation with final public power. Here utilization review, a requirement to supply sufficient condition information, review of covered services and final governmental responsibility remain distinct. Nonmedical observations, records and other reliable evidence may establish the physical condition without a compelled medical examination. A claim lacking sufficient information may be denied; the exemption does not make institutional religious judgment conclusive. The separate protection against religiously contrary medical supervision also preserves review for noncovered, excessive or fraudulent billing. The objection that public review could become nominal concerns operation and remains legally material. It does not establish inevitable delegation from the enacted terms alone.

#### III. The operational judgment requires assessment under the actual-function rules

**Holding and operative rule:** Summary judgment on a supported challenge to actual public financing or religious control must evaluate the evidence and reasonable supported inferences under the financed-function and public-control rules, rather than infer compliance from a secular description or formally retained authority. The operational judgment is vacated for that bounded assessment; religious affiliation, private prayer, possible diversion and a source gap alone establish neither a violation nor a duty to reopen evidence.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III is directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author. Stone-Zsela supplies an additional judgment vote only. The five direct joins, not an aggregation of separate grounds, control.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires identification of what government purchases and who controls it. Mitchell distinguishes enforceable safeguards from their observance, demands an actual financed-use connection and preserves independently lawful applications. Marshfield confirms that a genuine formal change does not end the inquiry into continuing public conduct, although its particular monument facts supply no benefits rule. The Cook affidavit and earlier manual supply concrete evidence to assess for linkage and materiality; the lower court's independent Lemon-prong analysis and generalized characterizations do not complete that inquiry. The contrary objection is substantial: prayer in a religious institution does not itself show that public money purchased it, and the manual retains final public responsibility. The remand respects those limits. It finds neither financed devotion nor a surrendered public decision, shifts no ordinary burden and requires no new evidence where the existing materials suffice. The courts must decide the supported operational claims under the correct rule and fit any eventual relief to a proved function while preserving lawful applications.

#### Statutory conditions preserved by these holdings

Medicare payment requires an effective election and a condition that would qualify for the corresponding inpatient-hospital or extended-care benefit at an ordinary hospital or skilled nursing facility. The signed election states conscientious opposition to nonexcepted medical treatment and inconsistency with sincere religious belief. Excepted treatment means treatment received involuntarily or required by federal, state or local law. An election ordinarily takes effect upon execution and continues until revoked. Written voluntary notice revokes it; nonexcepted treatment reimbursed under Medicare produces statutory deemed revocation. The form separately warns that receipt of nonexcepted medical services revokes the election and may restrict future services. After two elections and revocations, the next election waits one year from the most recent revocation and later elections wait five years. No individual compliance or revocation fact is found.

Qualifying institutions must be tax exempt, lawfully operated, supply the defined physical care exclusively through experienced nonmedical personnel on a 24-hour inpatient basis, and satisfy the religious-service and medical-provider ownership and affiliation restrictions. Ownership interests below five percent are disregarded. The separate affiliation exceptions cover uncompensated governing-body service, a director, trustee, officer, employee or staff member of the nonmedical institution having a family relationship with an individual affiliated with, or holding an ownership interest in, a medical-treatment provider, and vendors serving both types of providers. Accreditation may substitute only for conditions for which the Secretary finds reasonable assurance of compliance. Utilization committees review admissions, duration, extended stays and services, keep records and satisfy additional necessary review requirements. Institutions must supply required coverage and quality information and meet necessary health-and-safety requirements. The patient examination exemption preserves the sufficient-condition-information requirement; the distinct institutional supervision exemption preserves billing review for noncoverage, excess and fraud.

The Secretary must estimate expenditures before each fiscal year beginning with fiscal 2000 and monitor them beginning with fiscal 1999. If the estimate exceeds the statutory trigger, proportional payment reduction is required, subject to the Secretary's option to impose alternative coverage conditions or limitations sufficient to meet the trigger. The unadjusted trigger starts at $20 million for fiscal 1998 and increases by the prescribed annual consumer-price index; the next year's trigger is reduced or increased by the previous excess or deficit, with a $50 million ceiling on an increase. Permitted patient charges increase by the reduction amount. The review restriction concerns the identified expenditure estimates and reductions, not every coverage determination. After three consecutive excessive-expenditure years beginning no earlier than fiscal 2002, payment from the next January 1 is limited to patients with elections then effective and only for those elections' duration. No expenditure or sunset trigger is established here. Medicaid's statutory substitutions and supervision exemptions require their own application; Medicare's election mechanism is not silently made the entire Medicaid regime.

**Limits and remaining work:** The enactment applies to services furnished from August 5, 1997; its July 1, 1998 deadline for initial regulations and recognition of good-faith elections before those regulations took effect do not establish actual compliance. The actual financed-service and review connection remains for adjudication, not an unresolved constitutional standard. The Court neither decides individual sincerity or coverage claims nor orders severability relief against the upheld facial criterion.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos supplies permission for noncompulsory accommodation at the limited secular-benefit scope; it is not a universal subsidy rule.
- Kiryas Joel retains its civil-authority, denominational-neutrality and accommodation boundaries; the program's statutory recommendation is distinguished from its general governmental franchise.
- Larkin v. Grendel's Den retains its prohibition on a religious final civil veto and is distinguished from initial recommendations subject to public decisions.
- Agostini v. Felton governs through the modified unified Lemon inquiry, preserving distinct doctrines and operational secular-service controls. Former independent Lemon prongs are not revived.
- Mitchell v. Helms supplies actual financed-use proof, the difference between statutory controls and compliance, and fitted relief preserving lawful applications.
- Freedom From Religion Foundation v. City of Marshfield supplies examination of actual public conduct despite a genuine formal change; its monument-specific outcome is not transferred to benefits.

**Resulting law:** The enacted religious nonmedical-care accommodation and initial recommendation structure survive the facial challenges at the stated conditions. Public payment must still purchase independently identifiable secular work and remain under meaningful public authority. Statutory qualification, beneficiary eligibility, a covered service and an actual payment are distinct. The courts must reassess the supported operational claims under those rules without presumed diversion or presumed compliance. Stone-Zsela's equal-consideration extension and the three Associates' financial-preference theory do not become governing law.

### Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley — 2001-05-29

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Atkinson_Trading_Co_v_Shirley_merits_2001-05-29.md#holdings).

#### 1. Tribal tax power over these nonmember fee-land transactions requires an applicable source or exception

The Court holds that Montana governs this occupancy tax on nonmember guest transactions at a nonmember hotel on non-Indian fee land. In the absence of superior federal authorization, the Tribe must establish a qualifying consensual relationship connected to the tax or the conduct's qualifying threat or direct effect on tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare; neither exception is established here.

**Authority for both holdings:** The opinion of Stone-Zsela, joined in full by all eight other participating Justices. The separate three-Justice view about other land settings is not part of this holding.

Merrion recognizes sovereign taxing power rather than merely a private owner's exclusion right, but its tribal-land lease setting does not supply unlimited authority over these fee-land transactions. Strate requires the actual source, meaningful relationship and separate tribal-effect inquiries; territorial balancing cannot replace them. The reservation boundary remains intact and does not decide every question of governing power.

The first exception requires a meaningful connection between the tax and commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or its members. General police, fire and medical benefits do not supply consent for all nonmember transactions. A federal trader license is not a tribal agreement authorizing this levy. Even considering the operator's business contacts, a connection in one area does not authorize an unrelated tax on guests. Those contacts fail here, so the Court need not decide whether operator contacts could ever justify a tax whose incidence falls on guests. Express tax consent or privity is not universally required, and no properly grounded fee for a particular service is adjudicated.

Under the second exception, ordinary revenue needs, service provision and business presence alone do not establish the required threat or direct tribal effect. The Court denies neither the value of services nor their real consequences; it adds no quantified threshold and does not reduce every limb of the exception to one formula. Taxes on members, qualifying tribal leases or transactions, and authority supplied by federal law are not invalidated.

#### 2. The absence of tax authority controls relief without creating a waiver or refund action

The Court reverses approval of the challenged tax and returns the case for conforming lawful relief. Declaratory and prospective relief must fit the actual claims, lawful parties, immunity and ordinary equitable requirements; reversal does not itself create a refund cause of action or satisfy every injunction predicate.

Strate preserves the difference between governing power and amenability to suit. C & L enforces a clear, integrated contractual waiver involving arbitration and judicial confirmation; no such undertaking appears here. Commerce, location or receipt of services supplies neither unlimited taxing power nor an implied universal waiver. The returned court must give the no-authority ruling its proper effect under the applicable relief requirements, without inventing a monetary claim or a new tax arrangement.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Montana v. United States: applied to this nonmember activity on non-Indian fee land, including both exceptions and their distinct conditions.
- Merrion v. Jicarilla Apache Tribe: its sovereign-taxing principle is preserved; its tribal-land lease relationship does not establish authority over the challenged fee-land guest transactions.
- Strate v. A-1 Contractors: applied to source, meaningful nexus, tribal effect and separate relief requirements; title is not converted into disestablishment or elimination of sovereign taxation.
- C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma: its specific integrated contractual waiver is preserved; location, commerce and services do not establish that waiver here.

**Resulting law:** The challenged occupancy tax exceeds tribal authority under the applicable fee-land rule. A relationship must actually connect to the levy, and the second exception requires its own qualifying tribal effect. Relief remains subject to its independent legal prerequisites. The Court announces no controlling all-land Montana rule.

### Good News Club v. Milford Central School — 2001-06-11

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/Good_News_Club_v_Milford_Central_School_merits_2001-06-11.md#holdings).

#### I. Devotional expression does not remove this moral instruction from the admitted subject

**Holding and operative rule:** When a public school opens its community forum to comparable private moral and character instruction, it may not exclude an otherwise eligible program because that instruction uses prayer, Bible lessons, worship or invitation to religious adherence. Genuine reasonable and viewpoint-neutral subject, activity and operational limits remain available, but religious form cannot itself reclassify comparable admitted instruction as an excluded subject.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt Part I of the Opinion of the Court at this level of generality and in its application. Breyer delivers that opinion, and the other seven members of this coalition join. Eight direct votes control; Stevens dissents from the application.

**Controlling explanation:** Milford permits educational, social, civic, recreational and community uses. Its superintendent permits general moral instruction through Aesop’s stories but excludes this Club’s instruction promoting the Christian gospel. The Club’s integrated prayer, biblical lessons, songs, games and invitation to Christian adherence are real features of its teaching, not facts the Court disregards. They do not remove its otherwise comparable moral instruction from the admitted subject.

Lamb’s Chapel supplies the rule against excluding a religious perspective on an admitted subject. Ladue applies that rule to after-school moral instruction that includes prayer and Bible lessons. Bronx Household confirms both the religious-instruction rule and the prohibition on excluding otherwise comparable private worship solely for devotional form in a broad community forum. Milford’s distinction between religious discussion and gospel instruction cannot preserve a religious exclusion where comparable secular instruction is permitted. The Aesop testimony identifies policy scope; the holding does not depend on finding an Aesop club actually met.

Perry Education and Cornelius preserve genuine forum boundaries. They do not permit a viewpoint boundary to be renamed an activity limit merely because the speaker intends to persuade. The Court need not classify every integrated worship service as instruction or decide a request divorced from this forum’s admitted subjects. This Club need not remove its prayer or invitation to adherence to receive the protection of the actual instructional comparison. Its establishment consequences require the separate inquiry in Part II.

**Precedent treatment:** Lamb’s Chapel and Ladue are applied to the admitted instructional subject. Bronx Household’s instruction and bounded devotional-access holdings remain controlling. Perry Education and Cornelius retain genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral forum limits. Rosenberger reinforces viewpoint neutrality within the actual private-expression program without creating a financing entitlement.

#### II. These private-use arrangements defeat the asserted establishment defense and establish equal-access entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Independent adult leadership, parental authorization and after-school operation in this community forum constitute private access where the asserted school arrangements amount to administration of that access rather than governmental religious instruction, adoption, solicitation or substantial school-created religious pressure. On these circumstances the asserted establishment defense fails, and the Club is entitled to equal access under the same genuine neutral rules as comparable groups, with appropriate declaratory and access relief determined below.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court, delivered by Breyer, is joined by the other members of the six-Justice coalition comprising Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Those six directly control the establishment conclusion and remedy. Souter and Ginsburg join neither; Stevens dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini retains Lemon through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform that inquiry; they do not survive as independent fallback prongs. Bronx Household distinguishes a private religious meeting in school space from the school’s own religious program. Lee and Santa Fe independently prohibit official worship and preserve protection against substantial governmental religious pressure.

The Club has private adult leadership and requires parental authorization. The four-minute interval between dismissal and the proposed meeting, elementary ages, permission forms, assigned resource room, asserted continuing school activities and occasional audiovisual use warrant contextual attention. Even considered in their adverse supported forms, they identify administration and convenience of private access, not a governmental devotional function or substantial school-created pressure. A possible child’s misunderstanding and the program’s religious intensity cannot alone supply that missing governmental act. This is a legal conclusion about the asserted arrangements, not a universal finding that every conceivable official act is absent. Marshfield requires attention to an actual continuing governmental religious presentation; school location alone supplies no equivalent here.

Parental permission and neutral availability are relevant together with actual operation, not automatic constitutional immunities. Official recruitment, teacher-led devotion or actual compelled participation would present materially different conduct. No such conduct is established by relabeling the private meeting an extension of the school day. Nor must parents prove that private religious expression can never influence a child.

The two summary-judgment motions receive separate treatment. Failure of Milford’s motion does not itself establish the Club’s entitlement; the governing forum rule and the distinct legal insufficiency of this establishment defense establish it. The remand implements access and appropriate declaratory relief rather than reopening the rejected defense. Remaining monetary issues require their own lawful determinations.

**Precedent treatment:** Agostini’s modified Lemon inquiry is applied unchanged. Bronx Household supplies the private-access attribution rule. Lee and Santa Fe retain their independent official-worship and pressure protections. Marshfield is distinguished because it addresses an actual continuing governmental presentation; its functional inquiry remains operative.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lamb’s Chapel and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue are applied to private religious moral instruction within an admitted subject.
- Bronx Household’s instruction and comparable devotional-access holdings govern; its separate establishment holding preserves inquiry into actual governmental adoption.
- Perry Education and Cornelius retain reasonable, viewpoint-neutral genuine forum boundaries.
- Rosenberger supplies program-specific viewpoint neutrality; its printing benefit creates no general devotional-financing entitlement.
- Agostini’s modified Lemon inquiry governs attribution and function. Independent former prongs are not restored.
- Lee and Santa Fe retain official-worship prohibitions and the independent protection against substantial school-created religious pressure.
- Marshfield’s actual continuing governmental presentation is distinguished; its requirement to identify governmental religious function remains in force.

**Resulting law:** Schools admitting comparable private moral instruction must treat its religious and devotional forms on equal terms. Under the actual private-use arrangements here, the asserted establishment defense does not defeat the Club’s access entitlement. Administration of a private forum remains distinct from official worship, adoption, recruitment and substantial school-created religious pressure. Neutrality, private labels and parental permission do not automatically settle materially different conduct. Genuine neutral forum management remains lawful, and monetary relief remains a separate question.

### Saucier v. Katz — 2001-06-18

Authority: [binding mandate and law](../records/Saucier_v_Katz_merits_2001-06-18.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Saucier_v_Katz_merits_2001-06-18.md#precedent-treatment).

#### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Operative rule:** The Court of Appeals must reconsider Saucier's claimed personal immunity under the existing objective qualified-immunity inquiry, separately from the Fourth Amendment force merits and on the facts properly assumed on interlocutory review. The judgment leaves both a grant and a denial of immunity available under that inquiry; it decides neither outcome.

**Authority:** Souter announces the judgment. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join that exact mandate with him, supplying five votes. They do not join his complete rationale. This is a binding instruction in this case, not adoption by those five Justices of a general second reasonableness screen or a mandatory merits-first sequence.

**Controlling explanation and application:** The Court returns the claim for the specified legal inquiry without resolving the disputed historical account. Hunter v. Bryant supplies judicial immunity determination on supported summary-judgment assumptions. Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier exclude appellate reassessment of factual sufficiency; Sterling v. Borough of Minersville applies that boundary specifically to disputed personal participation. Those existing constraints prevent treating the petition's Parker-only account as an appellate finding. The lower courts may decide a separable legal question on the proper assumptions and leave genuinely material factual disputes to the proper forum. The competing demand for immediate immunity does not carry the judgment, and the mandate does not establish the contrary immunity result. No common further justification for the five votes is attributed to the Court.

#### Existing law, separate rationales and questions not decided

Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton remain in force at their existing scope, as carried through Hunter, Elder v. Holloway, Wilson v. Layne and Denius v. Dunlap. Graham v. Connor continues to govern force merits. The Court adopts neither Stone-Zsela's replacement defense nor the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection as a new rule of law. Souter's ordinary merits-first proposal likewise lacks a controlling judgment-majority rationale here.

No controlling rationale can be derived under Marks v. United States: retaining a distinct objective defense, replacing it with actual authoritative reliance, and rejecting an overlapping force-specific reasonableness screen are not nested rules yielding the same results. Agreement on this remand does not make one a logical subset of the others. The judgment governs the parties; no broader unified rule is inferred from the separate writings or from agreement between Souter and the dissenters.

The Court makes no ultimate finding of constitutional violation, reasonable force, participation, immunity, causation or compensable loss. The First Amendment, false-arrest/probable-cause, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are outside the granted inquiry and are not reopened. The existing federal-officer damages channel is assumed for this review; no new cause of action, institutional liability or compensation scheme is established. These limits leave factual and remedial work under existing law, not an entitlement to either party's preferred result.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Graham v. Connor:** Its complete, objective, on-scene force inquiry remains the merits rule, including the circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions confronting the officer. No physical-injury prerequisite, least-dangerous-tactic command or categorical security exception is added.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap:** Its retention of the existing objective defense remains; its distinct disclosure claims do not settle this force application, and its 2001 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Conn v. Gabbert:** Its reservation of mandatory immunity sequencing is not displaced by a controlling sequence holding here.
- **Marks v. United States:** No common narrower rationale is identified among the materially different judgment-supporting writings; it supplies no basis to combine them into a new rule.

**Resulting law:** Existing objective qualified immunity and Graham's force standard remain operative. This case must return for the distinct inquiry specified in the mandate, with the legal-notice inquiry fixed to September 24, 1994 and the proper interlocutory factual assumptions preserved. No general mandatory merits-first rule or authoritative-reliance replacement is adopted. No precedent is overruled. The absence of a common new immunity rationale does not unsettle the existing factual-review limits or the binding remand instruction. Personal immunity remains distinct from constitutional legality, actual participation, institutional responsibility and remedy.

### United States v. Mead Corp. — 2001-06-18

Authority: [controlling holdings and authority limits](../records/United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md#holdings).

#### I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding and operative rule:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Authority for Holdings I and II:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion have eight direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author. Scalia does not join either proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to administer and classify does not make every administrative interpretation controlling law for a reviewing court. Haggar requires actual delegated authority and statutory fit before acceptance of a reasonable choice within genuine ambiguity. Its formally promulgated Customs regulation does not establish the same authority for the different mechanism here. Christensen distinguishes controlling authority from an advisory interpretation's useful reasoning without deciding that all informal actions have the same status.

Customs letters guide particular transactions, may be modified, issue from numerous offices and do not bind unrelated importers as a generally applicable regulation would. Their place in the protest and judicial-review scheme confirms that administrative operation is distinct from delegated interpretive lawmaking. Formal rulemaking or adjudication can be important evidence of delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite. Independent judicial factfinding likewise does not itself defeat lawful deference; Haggar remains intact.

The Solicitor General's brief, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, represents this as Customs' official position. The Court accepts that representation. It identifies whose interpretation is defended, but does not establish that Congress gave this ruling mechanism controlling legal effect. Headquarters' developed reasons and the responsible institution's endorsement strengthen the claim to consideration, not the statutory delegation. The 1993 action remains governed by its own legal regime; later procedures for changing rulings do not supply retrospective authority. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations are outside this grant.

#### II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding and operative rule:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an agency can offer useful reasons without possessing authority to bind the court's answer. Skidmore provides that separate inquiry, and Christensen preserves it when controlling statutory deference is unavailable. Customs addressed dictionary meaning, commercial usage and whether a ring mechanism binds pages. Those reasons bear on the actual tariff language and cannot be discarded by attaching a nonregulatory label to the letter.

Mead correctly points to the change from earlier duty-free treatment. That change may weaken a consistency claim; it does not eliminate every reason the agency offers. Expertise, national responsibility and official endorsement likewise do not automatically make the position persuasive. The court evaluates the explanation on its merits within the enacted tariff boundaries. Neither the fact that Customs changed course nor the fact that it administers a complex scheme decides the tariff question in advance. The distinction preserves meaningful independent judicial interpretation rather than converting persuasion into a lesser form of mandatory deference.

#### III. The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with the persuasive consideration required by Holding II. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appellate judgment cannot stand on an inquiry that wholly excluded the permissible persuasive contribution of Customs' interpretation. The goods are described as ring-bound planners containing calendar and memorandum pages; no missing product fact supplies the reason for return. Nor is there a general entitlement to have a lower court decide every interpretive question first. The complete tariff question is presented, but neither proposed definitive construction commands a majority: independent construction sustaining the category and controlling acceptance of Customs' reasonable construction rest on distinct noncontrolling grounds. The seven-Justice disposition instead requires the corrected judicial inquiry without predetermining its answer.

The arguments remain concrete. Mead contends that removable pages and prospective scheduling are outside bound diaries. Customs answers that rings bind pages and that diaries include dated prospective entries. The reviewing court must decide the statutory meaning, consider the actual agency reasons and implement the resulting classification through ordinary tariff procedures. The disputed tariff meaning is genuinely undecided here for want of a majority construction; assessment calculations following the eventual lawful classification are ordinary implementation, not a second unresolved deference rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

**Resulting law:** Materially equivalent Customs ruling letters do not command controlling Chevron treatment through this mechanism. Courts must independently interpret the tariff while giving the agency's actual reasons the persuasive consideration they earn. Other genuine delegations, including valid regulations governed by Haggar, retain their own authority. Official endorsement does not collapse the delegation and persuasion questions. Neither ultimate classification of these planners commands a majority; the governing judicial inquiry is settled, and the tariff answer returns for decision under it.

### Saucier v. Katz — 2001-06-18

Published positions: [writings and scope](../records/Saucier_v_Katz_merits_2001-06-18.md#separate-writings). Each position below is noncontrolling; the exact five-Justice mandate governs the return.

**Souter, announcing the judgment.** Souter considers the merits and immunity inquiries legally distinct: an officer may perceive the facts correctly yet reasonably misunderstand the particular constitutional limit. Harlow and Anderson preserve that legal-error protection; Graham's allowance for reasonable factual perceptions does not itself decide it. General prohibition of excessive force is insufficient by itself, but a materially identical precedent is not indispensable. Hunter and Elder require the proper adverse factual assumptions and the full relevant conduct-date law. Souter would have ordinary lower-court immunity adjudication first determine whether those assumed facts establish a constitutional violation and, if so, whether the particular right was sufficiently established, so that the constitutional rule can develop. He recognizes the limits of the granted review here and assumes a possible violation; he does not require decision of a constitutional issue outside review or an advisory ruling. That sequencing view is his own, not a rule adopted by the judgment majority. Unlike the dissenters, he leaves the first distinct application to the lower court and directs neither immunity nor its denial. Unlike the other concurring Justices, he retains the distinct legal-error rationale in this force setting.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice accepts the exact existing-law remand while proposing a different personal defense. He would displace Harlow and Anderson only insofar as they protect an official merely because the violated right was insufficiently clearly established, preserving their compatible particularized analysis and lawful summary-judgment requirements. First, the complete constitutional rule would account for urgency, reasonably perceived danger, reasonable factual mistakes and operational discretion. If a violation were established, the official would bear the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance on it and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately conveying that authority could implement reliance. Legal silence, lack of a materially identical case, department custom, an unsupported supervisor's assurance and general job authority would not suffice.

In his view, legitimate protection against unexpected legal change concerns affirmative authoritative permission. The existing defense's predictability and litigation-control benefits do not justify withholding an otherwise available remedy from the first claimant establishing a particular application of settled protection. Graham already accounts for the officer's practical circumstances; those same factual allowances should not excuse wrongful force again. On Katz's supported account, an unnecessary shove of an already controlled, unresisting demonstrator violates the force standard without requiring physical injury, while necessary proportionate security removal remains lawful. Actual necessity, resistance, perceptions and participation remain disputed. The record identifies no binding precedent or enactment directly authorizing the alleged unnecessary shove; general security authority is insufficient.

His proposed defense would govern this pending case, with a fair opportunity for bounded submissions on genuinely new reliance elements. An expectation of immunity from unclear law would not preserve the displaced defense; final judgments and ordinary preservation and preclusion rules would remain. The proposal concerns this personal legal-uncertainty defense and materially equivalent claims, not function-specific absolute immunity, sovereign immunity, proper defendants, causation, injury or the availability of a cause of action. Failure of the proposed defense would establish neither a constitutional violation, damages, institutional liability nor a new cause of action; each independent requirement remains. These are separate views: the Court's mandate imposes no reliance burden, transition rule or additional submissions requirement.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens and Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** These Justices join the exact vacatur and open application remand while maintaining that Graham already tests the practical choices and reasonable mistakes available to an officer using force. They reject a second overlapping reasonableness shelter for force found objectively unreasonable under that complete standard. That objection is confined to this excessive-force setting; they retain ordinary Harlow protection elsewhere and do not endorse the Chief Justice's general actual-reliance replacement.

The supported unnecessary shared shove of a controlled, unresisting person cannot be dismissed by treating every degree of force as justified by the initial need for security removal. Johnson and Sterling prevent reappraisal of the participation evidence on this appeal. Plakas permits early merits judgment on a properly supported no-violation account, but its immediate armed attack without a contrary material account is absent here. The continuous movement into the van is relevant context, not a factual answer to the alleged unnecessary force. Their separate analysis does not alter their vote for the mandate: neither immunity nor denial, automatic trial, liability or damages is directed.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** These Justices would reverse and direct personal damages immunity while assuming a possible constitutional violation. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal error independently of Graham's merits inquiry. Accepting the plaintiff-favorable two-officer account, they regard the carrying and van entry as a continuous urgent removal from a protected event; the particular boundary of this brief nondeadly force was not sufficiently apparent in September 1994. They recognize the strongest contrary point: a controlled, unresisting person's forceful van entry may lack additional demonstrated need. They nevertheless treat this as uncertainty over the degree of force during removal, not a legally unmistakable separate assault after the seizure ended.

Their position does not depend on deciding that Parker acted alone, finding resistance, declaring the force lawful or making injury a prerequisite. Hunter and Sterling preserve the adverse assumed facts; their disagreement concerns the legal conclusion drawn from them. They support an ordinary merits-first approach for the reasons of legal development Souter describes, subject to the actual scope of review. Their agreement with him on that position is not a join in the judgment-announcing opinion and supplies no controlling sequence rule. They reject an open application remand because they would decide the legal defense now.
