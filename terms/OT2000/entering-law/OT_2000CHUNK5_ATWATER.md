## Standards and Tests selection: Additional station-house custody for a civil licensing offense

### Additional station-house custody for a civil licensing offense

**Governing question:** Does probable cause for a fine-only civil licensing violation justify transport and confinement?

**Current rule:** Probable cause and state or local enforcement authority do not alone justify transporting an identified operator to a station and confining him. Added restraint must reasonably serve its actual enforcement function. Possible justifications include necessary identification or appearance, ending a continuing violation, protection of persons or evidence and necessary processing; these examples are nonexclusive and not presumed. Municipal policy cannot itself establish constitutional reasonableness.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent assessment of added restraint and nonexclusive functional justifications; five direct joins: Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights — Probable cause does not automatically justify additional station-house custody, 1998-05-04.

**Present operation effective:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights, 1998-05-04.

**Exceptions and limits:** Fine-only status supplies no categorical arrest immunity, least-restrictive-means rule or automatic citation duty. The Court’s rule does not establish that the custody in Ricci was ultimately lawful or unlawful; neither application view has a majority.

**Holdings navigation:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights — Additional custody for civil licensing violations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Traffic citations and the custodial-arrest search exception

### Traffic citations and the custodial-arrest search exception

**Governing question:** Whether issuing a traffic citation authorizes a full search incident to arrest.

**Current rule:** Issuing a citation without custodial arrest does not trigger the full search-incident-to-arrest exception. Unexercised arrest authority, general roadside risk and the observed traffic offense do not substitute for its custody predicate. A further search requires its own recognized basis. Terry requires supported suspicion that the person is armed and dangerous; a Long vehicle search requires specific facts supporting danger and possible immediate control of weapons and remains confined to weapon-hiding areas. Exit directions alone authorize neither frisk nor full search. Consent and the automobile exception retain their independent conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Custody predicate and distinct protective-search bases: Canonical Decision Record `knowles_v_iowa_merits_1998-12-08.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception, 1998-12-08; unanimous Part I.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-12-08.

**Exceptions and limits:** Robinson’s categorical rule remains for an actual qualifying lawful custodial arrest. No later arrest may be invented to validate an earlier intrusion retrospectively, and no universal traffic-evidence exclusion rule follows.

**Holdings navigation:** [Knowles v. Iowa, 525 U.S. 113 — A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception](HOLDINGS.md#a-citation-does-not-trigger-the-full-custodial-arrest-search-exception-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

### Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

**Governing question:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause despite another investigative purpose.

**Current rule:** Objective probable cause of a traffic violation supports an ordinary initial stop notwithstanding an unrelated investigative aim. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce that violation absent that aim, or on compliance with customary assignment and enforcement policies. Manner, scope and duration remain independently constrained: a materially greater detention or search requires independent grounds. This rule does not remove purpose-specific conditions from inventory and administrative exceptions; inventories retain standardized criteria or established routine and genuine inventory purposes rather than investigatory rummaging. Purposeful racial selection remains independently prohibited by Equal Protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective initial-stop rule: Objective traffic probable cause supports this ordinary initial stop; independent execution and discrimination limits: Policy, execution and discrimination limits, 1996-06-10; Thomas’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Whren, 1996-06-10.

**Open question:** No discovery entitlement, immunity, suppression consequence or damages remedy for discriminatory selection is decided. Armstrong’s federal-charging threshold is not transferred to stop claims; traffic probable cause does not authorize every later intrusion.

**Holdings navigation:** Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806.

## Opening authority: Knowles v. Iowa, 525 U.S. 113

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`.

#### Knowles v. Iowa, 525 U.S. 113

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-7597; 569 N.W.2d 601 (Iowa 1997)
**Decided:** 1998-12-08.

##### A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception

**Holding:** Issuing a traffic citation without a custodial arrest does not authorize the full search incident to arrest; unexercised authority to make an arrest cannot substitute for the custody that triggers that exception. A full search requires an independently applicable recognized basis, and the citation, general roadside risk and observed speeding supply none for this search; supported exit orders, protective measures, consent searches and searches based on independent probable cause retain their own conditions and scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support. Part II's ordinary-remedial-law return has the eight Associates' support; Stone-Zsela would require the specific suppression stated in his concurrence.

##### The dependent judgment returns under the corrected constitutional rule

**Holding:** The citation-search theory cannot sustain this search or the dependent judgment, which is reversed and remanded for proceedings under the corrected constitutional rule and ordinary, properly available remedial law. No alternative justification is established here, and the Court orders no acquittal, release, replacement sentence, civil relief or universal exclusion rule for traffic encounters.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but would add the express suppression direction stated in his separate opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional rule is unanimous. The eight-Associate remedial return does not adopt Stone-Zsela's separate automatic suppression direction. No alternative search ground is established; independent protective measures, consent and probable-cause grounds retain their own predicates. No acquittal, release, replacement sentence or civil liability is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chimel v. California: applies the officer-safety and evidence-preservation purposes of arrest-incident authority; actual custody matters to the exception's reach.
- United States v. Robinson: preserves the categorical full-search rule after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest; no individualized necessity requirement is added to that setting.
- New York v. Belton: distinguishes the vehicle search incident to arrest from a full search after citation alone.
- Whren v. United States (June 10, 1996): preserves objective justification for the ordinary initial stop and independent limits on later search, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette (November 18, 1996): preserves the distinction between traffic business, subsequent restraint, voluntary consent and any prior illegality.
- Maryland v. Wilson (February 19, 1997): preserves the limited passenger-exit authority without creating automatic frisk or search power.
- Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights (May 4, 1998): applies the distinction between state enforcement authority and the reasonableness of an added intrusion; no actual-custody or immunity question from that case is decided here.
- Terry v. Ohio: preserves the limited protective frisk on its own supported danger conditions.
- Michigan v. Long: preserves a limited protective vehicle search on its distinct danger, weapons-access and scope predicates.

## Opening authority: Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`.

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. No new trial, hearing, release, sentencing proceeding, or determination of every admissibility ground follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ornelas*'s review allocation is applied unchanged. *Kimberlin* permits circumstantial proof when motive is an underlying element without adding one here. *Armstrong* remains confined to federal charging and discovery; its requirements and unresolved discrimination remedy do not become police-stop rules.

#### Wilson v. Arkansas

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-5707.

##### Announcement and reasonable home entry

**Holding:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of Fourth Amendment reasonableness when officers enter a dwelling. - Ker v. California, 374 U.S. 23: applies state-entry reasonableness with circumstance-dependent excuses.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the state court to evaluate the actual entry, record-supported excuses and remedy. No violation, automatic suppression or final vacation of the conviction is established.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Richards v. Wisconsin, April 28, 1997, resolves the reserved quantum for the identified warrant-execution exceptions as particularized reasonable suspicion and rejects the categorical felony-drug exception; constitutional exclusion after a violation remains open.

## Earlier published decision: Ricci v. Village

Source: `terms/OT1997/output/OT_1997CHUNK7.md`.

### Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights, No. 97-501

Merits decision, May 4, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 21, 1998. On review of the Seventh Circuit’s affirmance of summary judgment, 116 F.3d 288. The question concerns the reasonableness of automatic station-house custody for operating a business without the required license, accepting probable cause and state and local arrest authority.

Officers at Ricci’s telemarketing business asked about his license, and he acknowledged having none. Under Village policy they took him to the station, locked him in an interview room for approximately an hour while preparing enforcement documents, and released him on recognizance. His wife obtained the license, and the charge was dismissed. The civil licensing ordinance carried a $5–$500 fine for each offense, with each day a separate offense; no accumulated fine is adjudicated here.

The separate premises-search claim was settled, and dismissal of the no-probable-cause claim was not appealed. A distinct warrant-clause argument first raised at appellate oral argument was held waived; Ricci conceded an arrest under a warrant would have been reasonable. The preserved question concerns the objective extent of this custody, not a revived warrant, search or probable-cause claim.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in the stated respect and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

The adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim is set aside. The case returns for further proceedings under the additional-restraint rule. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer leave the actual custody application for the lower courts; Stone-Zsela would decide that custody unreasonable and limit remand to remaining policy causation, defenses and relief. No application conclusion commands five votes, and no present damages or immunity judgment issues. The settled search claim and unappealed probable-cause dismissal remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Breyer, Stone-Zsela | Targeted reversal and remand | Additional-restraint rule; rejection of categorical probable-cause/local-authority ground; setting aside adverse custody summary judgment |
| Opinion announcing the judgment, Part II | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Breyer | Same reversal and remand; leaves actual application for the lower courts | Four-Justice explanation of the unresolved enforcement-connection inquiry and remand scope |
| Concurrence in Part I and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Supports reversal and remand; would decide actual custody unreasonable | Objective mismatch on this record; remand limited to remaining liability, defenses and relief; does not join Part II’s application reservation |

#### Holdings

##### Probable cause does not automatically justify additional station-house custody

**Controlling proposition:** Probable cause and state or local authority to enforce a fine-only civil licensing ordinance do not, by themselves, establish that transporting an identified operator to a station and confining him is reasonable. The added restraint must reasonably serve the actual enforcement function; nonexclusive possible justifications include securing identification or appearance, ending an actual continuing violation, protecting persons or evidence, or necessary processing in the circumstances. The rule imposes neither least restrictive means nor a duty to issue a citation whenever one is conceivable.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer. Those five, including the author, directly adopt this rule and the targeted reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II’s affirmative reservation of actual reasonableness.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an established violation supplies authority to intervene, but the permissible extent of the seizure remains a constitutional question. Whren validates an ordinary initial probable-cause traffic stop while preserving independent manner, scope and duration limits. Robinette separates initial authority from justification for later restraint without requiring an indispensable release script. Ayeni likewise confines an authorized intrusion to its lawful function; its home-search setting is not automatically transferred to this business case. Wilson makes relevant history part of reasonableness without constitutionalizing every state rule, and Soldal prevents a civil label from excluding Fourth Amendment protection.

The lower court’s categorical two-predicate premise cannot settle this added custody merely because municipal policy requires it. A policy cannot supply its own constitutional justification. The lower opinion did discuss the continuing operation, potential daily fines, processing documents, one-hour duration and recognizance release; those supported circumstances must not be ignored or described as missing. The governing inquiry concerns their actual relationship to the added restraint.

Fine-only status matters to the reasonable extent of custody but creates no categorical immunity from arrest. The identified examples are possibilities, not findings that a particular justification occurred here. Robinson and Gustafson address consequences of a lawful arrest and cannot establish the disputed antecedent lawfulness by assumption. Hidden dislike of the business is not the ground; Whren’s objective approach remains. The separate warrant theory and the settled or abandoned claims stay outside review. The reversal rejects the automatic formula and sets aside its adverse custody judgment without a five-Justice holding that this custody was unconstitutional.

##### No controlling conclusion resolves the actual custody’s reasonableness

**Judgment without controlling application rationale:** Five Justices support targeted reversal and remand, but only Stone-Zsela concludes the actual custody was unreasonable. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer leave that application open; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the supported short enforcement processing reasonable and would affirm.

**Authority:** Part I’s rule controls by direct majority agreement. Part II’s application reservation has four joins, Stone-Zsela’s contrary application has one, and the dissent’s favorable application has four. These positions cannot be combined into a majority holding that this custody was unlawful or lawful, and no Marks synthesis is adopted. The lower courts must proceed under the controlling rule without a Supreme Court finding of ultimate violation, liability or immunity.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Whren v. United States:** Preserved for ordinary initial probable-cause stops and applied at its independent execution limit; it does not establish unlimited custody for every civil violation.
- **Ohio v. Robinette:** Its separate later-restraint inquiry informs the new application without a mandatory citation or warning script.
- **Ayeni v. Mottola:** Its purpose-bound execution principle informs the added-restraint inquiry; the home setting and separate immunity decision retain their limits.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas:** History remains relevant to reasonableness, and reversal for a deficient framework need not establish a completed violation. No state arrest rule is automatically constitutionalized.
- **Soldal v. Cook County:** Civil enforcement remains within Fourth Amendment coverage, while reasonableness and liability stay distinct; its summary-judgment remedy does not establish those predicates automatically.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their lawful-arrest premise is preserved; search consequences cannot establish antecedent arrest lawfulness by assumption.
- **County of Riverside v. McLaughlin:** Its timing rule for post-arrest probable-cause review does not itself authorize this custody.
- **United States v. Lanier:** Conduct-time legal clarity remains separate from a present constitutional ruling; no newly stated rule retroactively defeats personal immunity.

#### Law After Decision

Automatic station-house custody for this fine-only civil licensing offense requires an objective enforcement connection beyond probable cause and local permission alone. The rule permits reasonable practical enforcement judgments and supplies no universal citation requirement or categorical fine-only arrest prohibition. The adverse custody judgment is set aside, but no controlling holding determines whether this actual processing satisfies the rule. Municipal policy causation, individual responsibility, conduct-time immunity and relief retain their independent predicates.

#### Separate Positions

**Ginsburg, joined by the other members of the four-Justice Part II coalition, on the unresolved application.** Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that the categorical premise foreclosed the required assessment of added restraint even though the lower court discussed continuing operation, possible daily fines, processing and duration. They would evaluate those existing facts under the actual-enforcement rule rather than decide now that custody was unlawful. A favorable judgment for defendants remains legally possible after that inquiry. No new hearing is required simply because further facts might exist, and practical processing may suffice when reasonably connected to enforcement. Wilson and Soldal permit correction of a legal framework and adverse summary judgment without a present violation finding.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment.** Probable cause identifies the licensing violation but does not answer whether taking this identified operator from his business served a sufficient enforcement purpose. The record identifies an immediately obtainable license and a fine-only regulatory obligation, without failure to identify himself, flight, danger or another reason for removal. Stone-Zsela finds the automatic policy’s custody objectively disconnected from securing compliance or appearance and would decide it unreasonable on this record. His ground is not hidden official hostility toward the fundraising business, which Whren does not make decisive. He would remand only for municipal policy causation, preserved defenses and relief. Individual immunity must use the legal notice available in 1994; this present clarification does not retroactively defeat it. He neither creates a rule for every minor offense nor revives the settled search or abandoned probable-cause claim, and he does not join leaving the constitutional application open.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, dissenting.** These Justices reject unlimited authority from local permission and probable cause but find the necessary enforcement connection established here. Ricci admitted an actual continuing violation; custody lasted about an hour while officers prepared the arrest sheet, local-ordinance complaint and bond receipt and arranged recognizance release. Those tasks concerned enforcement of the same offense, unlike an unrelated additional intrusion under Ayeni. Neither the civil label nor the availability of a possible on-site citation requires proof that citation was impossible before ordinary processing. Whren, Wilson and Robinette preserve review of atypical force, unjustified duration, unrelated later detention or a method lacking a reasonable enforcement relationship. None of those broader permissions is asserted as a categorical rule here. They would affirm this actual application without deciding individual immunity.

#### Source Notes

The custody, continuing-operation and preservation account appears in [Ricci v. Arlington Heights, 116 F.3d 288](https://openjurist.org/116/f3d/288/ricci-v-arlington-heights-illinois), including its four footnotes. That opinion does not adjudicate individual immunity, and its silence does not establish forfeiture. The record concerns approximately one hour of interview-room confinement and processing; hypothetical shackling, strip searches, deadly force and home entries are not facts of this case.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Seventh Circuit with adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody claim set aside. The controlling additional-restraint rule governs further proceedings; the Court supplies no majority determination of actual unreasonableness, damages, municipal causation, officer participation or immunity. No particular evidentiary hearing, new licensing policy or revival of other claims is ordered.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The particular preservation need and limited execution justify this restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers may temporarily restrict unaccompanied entry into a home while obtaining a warrant when particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual risk of evidence destruction, a restraint reasonably confined to preservation and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization together justify that intrusion. The approximately two-hour restriction here satisfies those conditions in combination; probable cause, refusal of consent, awareness of an investigation, disposable drugs, jailability or duration alone creates no automatic exception or safe harbor.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Stone-Zsela dissents; Stevens would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted and, because the Court reaches the merits, would affirm suppression. Souter's additional concurrence does not limit his full join.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton preserves the home's special warrant protection. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not authorize a greater residential intrusion, and Bond requires an independent basis for an added intrusion despite lawful presence. Edmond addresses suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints; its program-purpose threshold supplies no rule for this individualized preservation measure.

Probable cause was uncontested. McArthur's wife reported seeing him slide drugs under the couch; he knew officers were investigating and had ready access to the identified disposable evidence. These particular circumstances reasonably supported a destruction concern without requiring police to wait for an observed attempt. Refusal to consent supplies neither guilt nor an independent exigency. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another restrained unaccompanied reentry. McArthur made two or three entries for cigarettes and telephone use with observation just inside the doorway. The officers conducted no evidentiary search before the warrant. Their approximately two-hour effort was diligent and the actual restriction reasonably preserved the evidence pending a magistrate's decision.

Place gives duration and diligence independent importance; no fixed two-hour allowance follows. Welsh requires attention to offense gravity. These offenses were modest but jailable: up to thirty days for the marijuana count and one year for paraphernalia. That distinguishes Welsh's civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest setting without making jailability sufficient. The substantial home interest and modest offenses weigh against restraint, but the actual preservation need and limited warrant-directed execution make this measure reasonable.

Segura's independent-source result does not validate every earlier seizure, and its broader Part IV discussion had only two votes. The Court relies on the justification before restraint, not the later discoveries. Limited observational consent is not general search consent or abandonment of the home-access objection. No general power to occupy or search homes, police-created unlawful exigency, requirement to exhaust every imaginable alternative or universal warrant-exception checklist is decided.

#### II. The presented suppression ground cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Illinois appellate judgment is reversed insofar as suppression rests on this temporary restriction of residential access, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings. Any actually preserved independent warrant, admissibility or exclusionary-rule issue remains governed by its own law; none is found established or newly created here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court treated the temporary restraint as an unlawful seizure and suppressed the later warrant evidence on that ground. Because the actual restraint was justified, that ground fails. The Court does not give the later warrant retroactive force, independently resolve every feature of that warrant or determine guilt. Correction of this suppression ground requires further proceedings, not an automatic conviction, dismissal of charges, release, new trial or new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: preserves the home's warrant protection; no general residential-entry authority is created.
- Whren v. United States: preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis and independent limits on greater intrusion, scope and duration; traffic probable cause is not residential authority.
- Bond v. United States: applies the requirement of independent justification for an added intrusion; lawful presence and limited observation do not imply consent to search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond: distinguishes suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints from this individualized preservation measure; its program-purpose holding remains intact.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: applies attention to actual duration and diligence in temporary preservation; no fixed permissible interval is transferred from luggage to a home.
- Welsh v. Wisconsin, 466 U.S. 740: preserves the importance of offense gravity and home protection; distinguishes its civil, nonjailable nighttime arrest without making every jailable offense sufficient for restraint.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796: preserves its independent-source holding while declining to treat it as approval of every initial seizure; the broader securing-premises discussion in Part IV remains a two-Justice position, not a majority holding.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

**Souter concurs and joins the Court opinion in full.** Exigency and permissible scope depend on the risk at the time of the proposed action. In his assessment, while McArthur was inside with immediate access to the hidden drugs, the actual destruction risk could have justified prompt entry and search. Once McArthur stepped outside and police could control access, the same immediate necessity for a greater warrantless evidentiary intrusion was absent. Temporary control of reentry while pursuing a warrant respects the preference for a magistrate's authorization and affords a reasonable opportunity to secure it. Police need not let an occupant return simply to recreate the emergency that would support a greater intrusion. This analysis distinguishes the risk and available response at each point; it does not find that a greater search occurred, infer an unreported occupant or turn the later discoveries into the earlier justification. The conditional greater-intrusion view belongs to his concurrence and is not an additional Court holding.

**Stone-Zsela dissents.** Control of access to a home is a seizure requiring an adequate warrant exception. The wife's contemporaneous observation of concealment strengthens probable cause and bears on risk, but hiding an existing stash does not itself show imminent destruction. McArthur could see his wife confer with police and leave with an officer; those facts support anticipation of a search, but add no particularized urgency beyond that awareness and the disposable character of drugs. Treating that combination as sufficient would authorize a similar restraint in nearly every residential drug investigation. An actual destruction attempt is not invariably required, but the inference must concern imminent loss rather than probable cause alone.

The two-hour duration and accompanied entries reduce the burden but cannot supply the missing exception. Segura does not validate every initial seizure because a later warrant produces evidence. Welsh gives the modest offenses real weight without misclassifying them as nonjailable. Contextual reasonableness evaluates a demonstrated exception; it cannot replace the warrant boundary. Stone-Zsela would affirm suppression for the search resulting from the unsupported restraint, subject to any independently preserved exclusionary-rule issue actually left unresolved. He would not invalidate the later warrant on an unpresented ground or dismiss independently supported charges.

**Stevens dissents.** He first would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because these slight possession offenses make an unsuitable occasion to define the limits of restraints on residential possession. Because the Court decides the merits, he would affirm. Welsh requires serious attention to offense gravity, and his understanding of Segura gives deprivation of home possession independent significance despite an eventual warrant. Even accepting probable cause, diligent warrant pursuit, accompanied entries and the comparatively limited restraint, he concludes that the substantial interest in private use of the home outweighs this modest preservation interest. His position concerns these facts; it neither denies that the offenses permit imprisonment nor categorically prohibits every misdemeanor preservation measure. He does not demand an observed destruction attempt in every case.

### Wallis v. Spencer — 2001-04-02

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md#precedent-treatment).

#### I. Emergency removal requires an immediate danger and a response confined to it

**Holding and operative rule:** Unilateral, nonconsensual removal of children from parental custody without prior judicial authorization requires specific, articulable information giving objectively reasonable grounds to believe that imminent serious bodily harm requires action before authorization can reasonably be obtained; the removal's scope and duration must be reasonably directed to that danger. Genuine disputes about the information, urgency and scope here prevent summary judgment for the City on the preserved removal claim, without establishing a constitutional violation or municipal liability.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II.A and III, and concurs only in the judgment on Parts II.B and IV. All nine directly adopt this removal holding and support the complete partial affirmance, partial vacatur and remand. The removal rule states necessary protections for the intervention presented, not an exhaustive test for every parental-right claim; no combination of separate rationales supplies its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The children's Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable seizure and the parents' Fourteenth Amendment family interests are distinct. Troxel protects parental direction against displacement by ordinary governmental preference but supplies no complete custody test. McArthur requires attention to actual need, execution, duration and available judicial authorization; its limited residential restraint does not itself authorize this greater bodily and familial intrusion.

The reported threat to kill Jessie at the equinox was grave. Transmission through a therapist and protective-services personnel could support prompt protective action without certainty of abuse. Neither the report's later disproof nor the aunt's psychiatric treatment alone defeats that possibility. But the disclosed reliability problems, earlier unfounded report, disputed therapist communications, investigation actually performed and children's observed condition cannot be resolved against the family on summary judgment. No judge issued a removal order. Whether CPS reported one or requested removal, and what the officers knew, remain disputed.

The allegation did not implicate Becky or threaten Lauren. Those differences and practical protective alternatives bear on the intrusion's scope; they do not establish that another placement was actually available. A date-specific threat cannot by itself justify indefinite separation. Permission to enter the home does not establish consent to removal or examinations.

Courts determine the governing law and may decide sufficiency on undisputed facts. The Court does not assign every reasonable-cause question to a jury, impose a fixed corroboration category, require exhaustion of every imaginable alternative, or determine credibility here. Nor does it establish a universal unfitness prerequisite, strict-scrutiny framework or equivalence of the two Amendments. The legal consequence of mistaken reliance on a reported order remains unresolved; the described appellate omission establishes no forfeiture at every stage.

**Precedent treatment:** Troxel's protected parental direction is applied at its controlling scope; its separate broader frameworks remain noncontrolling. McArthur's attention to actual need, scope and duration is applied without transferring its particular residential authorization to child removal.

#### II.A. Municipal responsibility requires the City's own actionable policy, fault and causal connection

**Holding and operative rule:** A section 1983 municipal claim requires a federal deprivation caused by the City's own attributable policy or legally effective custom and the fault required by the theory actually presented; a training, supervision or safeguards omission requires deliberate indifference, supported notice and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing the injury. The testimony connecting an asserted settled removal practice to this intervention defeats the categorical conclusion that no policy evidence exists; the remaining elements are unproved.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices directly join this holding and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Collins separates a federal deprivation from municipal responsibility. Monell rejects employer-based federal liability. Carter applies Canton to deliberate municipal omissions: notice may arise from an established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances. Prior identical incidents are not invariably required. Negligence, employee error and hindsight that further training might have helped do not suffice.

The City correctly argues that an officer's mistake or outside misinformation alone cannot establish its responsibility. Here, however, a supervisor and the designated knowledgeable officer described a settled practice of accepting reported orders without verification. Evidence connects reliance on the supposed order to removal and the absence of further investigation. That permits the policy claim to proceed; it does not establish that the practice was unconstitutional, that every witness possessed final policymaking authority, or that the City caused every later injury.

An affirmative policy and an omission remain different theories. The latter requires proof of deliberate indifference, notice, the particular deficiency and actual causation; the duration of a practice alone proves none of those elements. Final authority depends on the legally effective allocation of power for the challenged function, not operational discretion alone. Interagency cooperation does not make county personnel or a physician City employees. No universal paper-order inspection requirement or prohibition of telephone communication follows.

Ordinary pleading and proof controls continue, without a special requirement to plead inaccessible internal operations in heightened detail.

**Precedent treatment:** Collins and Monell preserve separate deprivation and attribution requirements. Carter and Canton govern omission fault, notice and causation; McMillian governs function-specific final authority. Leatherman preserves ordinary municipal pleading.

#### II.B. The City cannot borrow personal or state immunity against section 1983

**Holding and operative rule:** The City cannot defeat its own federal municipal liability by borrowing an individual's personal qualified or absolute immunity or by invoking state immunity against section 1983. Rejection of those categorical defenses is affirmed without deciding any absent individual's immunity or liability.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in this disposition without adding grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Carter separates municipal responsibility from the personal immunity an officer may possess. A defense protecting an individual's performance of a function does not exempt the City's own actionable policy from federal law. State immunity likewise does not displace the federal municipal claim.

The City is the only remaining defendant in this appeal. Its inability to borrow these defenses does not adjudicate an officer's immunity or restore a personal claim. Denius preserves particularized objective notice from the relevant law when the individual acted; an identical earlier case is unnecessary, but Ferguson in 2001 cannot supply September 1991 notice. The Court resolves only the City's categorical defenses, leaving the constitutional deprivation, institutional fault, causation and injury requirements intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Carter's separation of municipal responsibility and personal immunity is applied. Denius's conduct-date notice rule remains intact and supplies no retrospective notice or municipal immunity.

#### III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible

**Holding and operative rule:** The independent City examination-policy adjudication and its associated constitutional rules are vacated insofar as they conclusively govern the remand; the lower court must first determine that theory's actual presentation and scope under ordinary procedural rules before adjudicating its independent merits. Examination injuries caused by a proved removal-policy violation do not invariably require a second examination policy, but the underlying wrong, actual causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must still be established separately for each claimed consequence.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this threshold and causation holding. No independent examination merits rule is decided. Stone-Zsela's concurrence states his own conditional bodily-integrity rule and receives no other join.

**Controlling explanation:** The examinations were substantial bodily intrusions. The district court addressed them, the report identified the City as requesting agency, and testimony concerned the detective and hospital contract. Both questions were granted. These circumstances support arguments about presentation and attribution; they do not resolve the specific objection that an independent City examination-policy claim was never properly presented. Review does not amend a complaint. Neither a lower dissent nor the absence of original filings here establishes forfeiture.

The proper course is to determine that threshold without conclusively imposing the lower court's independent examination, notice, opportunity-to-object or parental-presence rules. Even its presence rule permitted exclusion for a valid reason with nearby presence instead. Today's judgment finds neither preservation nor waiver and neither constitutional nor unconstitutional examinations.

Ferguson requires an adequate justification for the particular prosecution operation it addressed, while preserving genuinely different clinical functions and consent issues. It supplies no universal mixed-purpose prohibition or three-permission formula. Denius's public-employment information ruling supplies no bodily-examination protocol. The present record establishes no urgent medical need, imminent evidence loss, valid substitute consent or waiver; protective custody alone cannot establish those missing predicates. The Court nonetheless leaves the independent merits question for a properly presented claim. The 1998 California examination enactment does not authorize the 1991 acts.

The removal claim's possible consequential-injury route remains distinct, as the lower opinion's note 15 recognized. Failure of a separate examination-policy theory would not automatically extinguish that route; sequence alone does not prove it. Initial removal, prehearing examinations and posthearing separation require their own causal analysis. A later medical report and judicial intervention neither automatically sever nor establish City responsibility for the entire two-and-one-half-month separation. Ordinary proceedings supply this inquiry, not a mandated new evidentiary hearing or permission to manufacture a new claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Ferguson and Denius are preserved within their distinct operational and informational scopes; neither decides this unadjudicated independent claim. Monell and Carter continue to require an actual municipal causal connection even when a second policy is unnecessary to recover consequential injury.

#### IV. The state claims return under their own liability and immunity rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The surviving California claims return within their existing scope for ordinary application of state liability and immunity law; federal restrictions on municipal respondeat-superior liability and borrowed personal immunity do not replace that separate state inquiry. Disputed CPS enlistment and the character of the challenged acts prevent disposition here under Government Code section 820.2, while the lower court's rejection of the reply-only section 821.6 theory is not reopened.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this limited state-law ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in the state-law remand without adding grounds; no independent constitutional examination holding is supplied through it.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 815.2(a)'s employee-based state liability and section 815.2(b)'s derivative immunity operate under their own terms. Section 820.2 protects an act or omission resulting from vested discretion even if that discretion is abused. Newton and Alicia T., as applied below, protect child-abuse investigation and public employees reasonably enlisted to assist, including the decision to investigate and actions necessary for a meaningful investigation. Protection does not extend to nondiscretionary acts or at least some intentional torts, including battery and false imprisonment under Newton. Whether CPS enlisted these officers, whether particular conduct involved discretion, and which pleaded tort claims fall outside protection remain unresolved. A claimed mandatory policy does not become discretionary merely because the setting involves child protection.

The lower court left the tort-specific limits for initial determination below. This Court neither extends that immunity nor decides battery, abduction, assault or intentional-infliction liability. Its rejection of the section 821.6 contention rested on reply-only presentation and the City's disclaimer of participation in the dependency prosecution; neither is reopened. Preserved state examination issues remain governed by their actual scope and state law, without importing the vacated independent federal examination rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Newton v. County of Napa and Alicia T. v. County of Los Angeles retain their stated state-law roles in the remand; neither becomes a new federal immunity rule. Monell's federal rejection of respondeat superior does not displace the distinct California statutory route.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Troxel v. Granville, 530 U.S. 57:** Applies protected parental direction against displacement by ordinary governmental preference. No complete custody framework, unfitness prerequisite, general harm formula or scrutiny level is imported from its separate positions.
- **Illinois v. McArthur, decided February 20, 2001:** Applies attention to actual justification, scope, duration and judicial authorization; its confined residential restraint supplies no automatic authority for removing children or examining their bodies.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115:** Applies the distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility; institutional fault cannot substitute for a constitutional injury.
- **Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658:** Applies the municipality's own policy or legally effective custom and causal responsibility; section 1983 supplies no respondeat-superior liability.
- **Carter v. City of Philadelphia, decided March 13, 2000:** Applies policy, omission fault, notice, identified deficiency and causation separately; a municipality cannot borrow personal immunity, and institutional cooperation alone creates no employment relationship.
- **City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378:** Preserves deliberate indifference and actual causation for training or supervision omissions; neither negligence nor hindsight suffices, and identical prior incidents are not universally necessary.
- **McMillian v. Monroe County, decided June 2, 1997:** Preserves the inquiry into legally effective final authority for the challenged function; operational discretion alone does not establish it.
- **Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163:** Preserves ordinary municipal pleading without heightened internal-policy particulars; ordinary discovery and proof requirements remain.
- **Denius v. Dunlap, decided January 17, 2001:** Preserves particularized conduct-date personal-immunity notice and the distinction between justified intimate-information acquisition and an unrestricted employment demand. Neither supplies a bodily-examination protocol or municipal immunity.
- **Ferguson v. City of Charleston, decided March 21, 2001:** Preserves the confined actual-operation ruling and unresolved consent inquiry; it establishes no categorical mixed-purpose prohibition, universal three-permission rule or independent holding on these examinations.
- **Newton v. County of Napa, 266 Cal. Rptr. 682; Alicia T. v. County of Los Angeles, 271 Cal. Rptr. 513:** Retain their state-law treatment of discretionary child-protection investigation and enlisted assistance, with the nondiscretionary and tort-specific limits reserved below. Neither alters federal municipal liability.

**Resulting law:** Unilateral emergency removal must rest on particularized grounds of imminent serious bodily harm requiring action before judicial authorization can reasonably be obtained, with scope and duration fitted to that danger. This necessary protection does not establish a comprehensive family-law scrutiny standard. The City's own actionable policy or omission, fault and actual causal responsibility remain separate from both a constitutional deprivation and an individual's immunity.

No Supreme Court merits rule governs the independent examination claim from this disposition. Its presentation must be determined before adjudication. A separate examination policy is not invariably needed for examination injury actually caused by a proved removal-policy violation; neither chronology nor judicial intervention alone resolves causation. The California claims retain their separate statutory liability and immunity rules. Stone-Zsela's conditional examination standard remains a concurrence.

### Wallis v. Spencer — 2001-04-02

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins Parts I, II.A and III: the minimum emergency rule, municipal policy, fault and causation requirements, and preservation-first remand. He supports the City's federal-immunity disposition and the state-law remand without adding grounds. He also states the bodily-integrity rule he would apply if the independent examination claim is properly presented: absent genuine urgent medical need or imminent loss of evidence, an invasive investigative examination of these children requires valid parental consent or prior judicial authorization through an appropriate process. Lawful protective removal alone supplies neither authorization. Reasonable arrangements for parental participation may reflect a genuine examination need; a categorical exclusion is insufficient.

The child's own bodily rights matter independently of parental authority. The three-day interval and evidentiary purpose require an actual explanation of urgency. An additional bodily intrusion cannot be justified solely by the legality of the initial custody. This applies the ordinary distinction between judicial authorization and genuine exigency to the examination; it creates no universal strict-scrutiny framework for family administration. Institutional responsibility remains essential: a preserved City policy or omission must be connected to the examination, and moral responsibility for the overall episode cannot replace that proof.

He reserves whether the City-specific examination theory was preserved and supported, joins vacatur of its conclusive adjudication and does not find these examinations unlawful or award damages. His conditional standard is not a holding of the Court.
