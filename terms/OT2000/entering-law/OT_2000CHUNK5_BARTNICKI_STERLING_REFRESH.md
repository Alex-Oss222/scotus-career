## Public Projection

## Event

Sterling v. Borough of Minersville; Third Circuit No. 99-1768, judgment reported at 232 F.3d 190. Merits decision, April 24, 2001. On review of the Third Circuit judgment sustaining the reviewed denial of personal immunity. The questions concern an adult arrestee's protection against an operative threat of intimate family disclosure and whether that application was sufficiently clear in April 1997. Hoban separately disputes his participation.

On April 17, 1997, police arrested eighteen-year-old Marcus Wayman and a seventeen-year-old companion for underage drinking. At the station, Wilinsky allegedly required Wayman to tell his grandfather his suspected sexual orientation or face disclosure by the officer, accompanied by a biblical admonition. Whether the youths admitted homosexual activity is disputed. Wayman died by suicide after release. The estate asserts constitutional and state claims; the unlawful-arrest claim was eliminated below, and this review does not restore it.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in merits consideration and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms denial of Wilinsky's legal immunity defense on the supported privacy theory and dismisses Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention from immediate appellate review.

## Holdings

### I. An operative official ultimatum can invade intimate confidentiality

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer violates the Fourteenth Amendment protection of intimate confidentiality by imposing a credible official ultimatum requiring an adult to disclose suspected sexual orientation to a family member or face disclosure by the officer, when no legitimate governmental purpose supports either course. The present infringement is operative compelled surrender of an objectively intimate confidence; completed officer disclosure is unnecessary, but an idle insult or nonoperative threat does not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the right, application on the supported assumptions and stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an officer cannot evade protected confidentiality by making the citizen perform the disclosure. Sheets protects objectively intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius recognizes that an official disclosure condition can itself burden intimate information before publication occurs. Today's bounded extension concerns an arrest-backed command to reveal an adult's intimate life to family, rather than every threat or a general right to keep all information secret.

The supported account describes an operative choice imposed through official arrest authority: Wayman must disclose his suspected orientation or the officer will disclose it. The alleged demand therefore does more than predict possible future misconduct. Its coercion presently targets the protected confidence. No supported drinking-enforcement purpose connects disclosure to his grandfather with this adult's arrest. Religious admonition supplies no policing justification. Disputed admissions do not establish that the information was already public.

Doe preserves the distinction between confined, justified benefits administration and an unrelated use; it neither requires public dissemination for every claim nor itself decides this extension. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection, and Nixon recognizes personal privacy without making ownership decisive. Their foundations and the actual holdings of Sheets and Denius support this confined coercion rule, not an all-purpose informational guarantee.

The officers' strongest objection distinguishes an unexecuted threat from a completed deprivation. That distinction remains valid for an idle remark or a demand without operative effect. It does not answer a credible, arrest-backed command to surrender the confidence itself. The plaintiff must still prove the actual demand, responsible actor, coercive effect, causation and compensable injury. This legal ruling finds none of those disputed historical facts after trial.

### II. The supported no-purpose ultimatum defeats legal immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** On the supported plaintiff-favorable account, the law effective on April 17, 1997 gave sufficiently particularized objective notice that using arrest authority to compel an adult's intimate family disclosure for no official purpose was unconstitutional; Wilinsky is not entitled to qualified immunity on that legal ground. An identical orientation-and-ultimatum case is unnecessary, but operative coercion and the complete absence of an official disclosure purpose are material limits, and subjective bad faith alone does not defeat immunity.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to the notice ground and affirmance. O'Connor and Kennedy join the present merits rule but not this conduct-date conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson retain an objective, particularized notice test. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law, not merely an identical earlier case or the authorities cited below. Sheets had taken effect in April 1996. Its completed-publication setting differs from the present ultimatum, but it established protection for objectively intimate information against exposure unrelated to the official purpose for access. The officer cannot avoid that boundary by coercing the person to perform the surrender.

The earlier Third Circuit authorities reinforce the distinction between confidential information and a genuine official need. Westinghouse addressed intimate medical material; Fraternal Order of Police addressed justified employment inquiry and disclosure safeguards; the 1995 circuit Doe opinion evaluated an actual health-plan monitoring interest. Those cases do not themselves decide this ultimatum, and their full balancing formulations are not adopted. Their justified-use boundaries, together with Sheets, make this no-purpose demand sufficiently apparent.

Walls is substantial contrary authority, but its employment-screening inquiry differs from an arrest-based command to disclose to family without a policing purpose. Decisions distinguishing ordinary threats from completed injuries likewise do not authorize operative compelled self-disclosure. The Court does not derive notice simply from the demand's offensiveness or an officer's subjective understanding.

Denius supplies present merits law, not April 1997 notice. The Supreme Court's May 1997 Doe decision, subsequent Shahar and Wilson v. Layne decisions, and still later authorities supply no retrospective notice either. The relevant cumulative law already existed when the alleged act occurred. Denial of the legal defense permits ordinary proceedings; it does not prove participation, causation of death or damages, and it orders no automatic trial.

### III. Hoban's factual participation challenge is outside immediate review

**Holding and operative rule:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal cannot determine whether pretrial evidence sufficiently establishes an officer's participation in the alleged conduct. Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention is dismissed from immediate appellate review; a genuinely separable legal defense may be decided only on the proper supported assumptions, without choosing between disputed accounts.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson distinguishes immediate review of a separable legal immunity question from review of evidentiary sufficiency concerning participation. Behrens preserves that boundary within an otherwise authorized appeal; legal phrasing cannot bring the factual dispute within the appellate court's power. Hunter leaves legal immunity for judicial decision under ordinary summary-judgment constraints, and Elder requires consideration of relevant law without authorizing factual retrial. Hoban's denial of actual participation therefore does not permit appellate selection of the true account. His role remains for the proper trial-level proceedings, while any properly separable assumed-fact legal issue retains its own lawful footing.

### Remaining limits and ordinary proceedings

The case concerns an adult and an operative no-purpose ultimatum. It decides neither an emergency protective disclosure on facts absent here nor a general right to conceal evidence of criminal conduct. No universal privacy test, strict scrutiny, least restrictive means or automatic threat liability is adopted. Whether the ultimatum occurred, each officer's responsibility, coercive effect, causation of Wayman's death, compensable injury and lawful damages remain for proof. Municipal responsibility, other constitutional claims and state-law claims retain their own predicates and actual procedural posture. Recognition of the legal rule and denial of immunity establish no damages amount or liability for every defendant.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Sheets v. Salt Lake County, April 29, 1996:** Its objectively intimate-information protection is extended from unjustified official dissemination to the operative coerced family disclosure presented here. Its actual-publication holding remains; it did not itself decide threats. Its date makes its confined principle relevant to April 1997 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap, January 17, 2001:** Its recognition that a compulsory disclosure condition can impose a present burden supports the extension, with actual intimacy and official justification required. Its differentiated immunity analysis preserves conduct-date notice; its present holding supplies no earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority, May 5, 1997:** Its assumed-right disposition of confined, justified benefits administration is preserved, distinguished from the unrelated ultimatum. It supplies no April 1997 notice. The distinct 1995 circuit opinion is relevant conduct-date authority at its own scope.
- **Whalen v. Roe; Nixon v. Administrator of General Services:** Their confidentiality foundations and approval of legitimate safeguarded governmental uses remain. Neither is treated as having decided this coercive demand.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald; Anderson v. Creighton:** Objective, particularized qualified immunity remains; it is neither replaced by a reliance-only defense nor defeated by subjective malice alone.
- **Elder v. Holloway; Hunter v. Bryant:** Independent consideration of relevant contemporaneous law and judicial immunity review under proper factual assumptions are applied. Neither permits appellate fact selection or retrospective notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its particular earlier notice determination and rejection of a universal identical-case requirement remain. Its 1999 decision is not itself April 1997 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones; Behrens v. Pelletier:** Their factual-sufficiency exclusion and distinct assumed-fact legal-review channel govern Hoban's contention.
- **Westinghouse; Fraternal Order of Police; the 1995 circuit Doe opinion:** Considered as preconduct circuit confidentiality authorities concerning actual official functions and safeguards; they are not elevated into Supreme Court holdings or transplanted wholesale.
- **Walls v. City of Petersburg:** Its employment-inquiry setting and contrary notice significance are considered and distinguished from this no-purpose arrest-backed family disclosure demand; its existence is not ignored.

## Law After Decision

Protected intimate confidentiality reaches the credible official ultimatum compelling an adult's family disclosure without legitimate governmental purpose. Present coercion can supply the infringement even without completed officer publication; an idle insult or nonoperative threat cannot. The bounded rule governs claims meeting those conditions and requires proof of actual responsibility, effect, causation and injury.

The ordinary objective qualified-immunity test remains. On this supported no-purpose ultimatum, the April 1997 authorities defeat the asserted legal defense without making all later privacy extensions retrospectively clear. Hoban's factual participation challenge remains outside immediate appellate review. No municipal liability, causal death finding, damages amount or general informational-secrecy right follows.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy.** They join the bounded present right and the exclusion of Hoban's factual appeal, but would grant Wilinsky personal immunity on this damages theory. Sheets's completed dissemination of entrusted marital material differs from an unexecuted orientation ultimatum. Denius properly recognizes present protection but cannot fill the earlier gap. Walls's employment-questionnaire ruling and the distinction between threats and completed injury reinforced objective uncertainty in April 1997. A demand may have no legitimate policing purpose yet remain an insufficiently clear constitutional application for personal damages. They require particularized notice, not an identical case in every setting. Their proposed reversal would protect the reviewed individual damages theory, without denying all compelled-disclosure protection, resolving other claims or deciding municipal responsibility.

**Scalia, dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the factual-appellate boundary but reject the additional merits extension. Sheets and Denius bind at their actual holdings; Whalen's reservation and intimate subject matter do not independently establish every further substantive-due-process entitlement. The moral wrong or absence of a sound purpose does not replace identification of a constitutional source. They accept the supported alleged ultimatum for legal review and do not rely on factual disbelief, harmlessness or a supposed state remedy curing a federal violation. Independently, they would grant immunity because the preconduct law did not sufficiently establish this orientation-and-unexecuted-ultimatum application. Their reversal reaches the reviewed individual theory, not every defendant or remaining claim.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed legal immunity denial stands, and ordinary proceedings may continue on actual participation, coercive effect, causation, injury and lawful relief. Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention is dismissed from interlocutory review; any properly separable legal issue must use the required assumptions. The Court orders no automatic trial, new hearing or award, finds no officer responsible for Wayman's death and resolves no independent municipal or state-law claim. No Supreme Court return condition is retained.
