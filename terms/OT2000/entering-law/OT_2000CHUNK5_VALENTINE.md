## Standards and Tests selection: Reliability of anonymous gun allegations before a stop and frisk

### Reliability of anonymous gun allegations before a stop and frisk

**Governing question:** Whether an anonymous gun allegation and identification of its target justify a forcible stop and protective frisk.

**Current rule:** An anonymous assertion that a described person carries a concealed gun is insufficient when officers confirm only publicly observable identity and location and lack additional circumstances adequately supporting the accusation’s reliability. Reliability and basis of knowledge are assessed together under the totality of the circumstances. Corroborated predictions are one way, not an invariably required way, to support reliability. Ordinary gun danger supplies no exception, and a gun discovered only after the intrusion cannot retroactively justify it.

**Authority by component:**

- Accusation reliability distinct from identification; no ordinary gun exception or retrospective justification: Florida v. J. L. — An accurately identified target does not make this gun accusation reliable; The gun allegation and its later confirmation do not supply the missing authority, March 28, 2000; Stevens’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** J. L., March 28, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sufficiently reliable information can justify action without an officer first seeing the gun. Concrete nonpredictive assurances remain possible. The ruling creates no universal equation of firearm possession with illegality or dangerousness and leaves independently lawful protective searches intact.

**Open question:** Materially different imminent threats, accountable eyewitness reports, school searches, airport screening and other special settings require their own analysis.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion, nonwarrant probable cause and warrantless-home-entry exigency.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Appellate courts likewise independently decide whether established circumstances supply constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry, with the same protection for historical and genuinely factual subsidiary findings and genuine due weight for grounded experience. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Review allocation, objective totality and due weight: II. Ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently reviewed, with factual protection and genuine due weight, 1996-05-28; O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

- Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review: United States v. Fields, 1998-02-09; O’Connor Part III-A with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Fields, 1998-02-09, for home-entry exigency; Ornelas, 1996-05-28, for reasonable suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

**Holdings navigation:** Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690. United States v. Fields / Crawley — Residential privacy, warrantless entry and suppression.

## Standards and Tests selection: Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition

### Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition

**Governing question:** Who may invoke the Second Amendment against federal and state action and what acquisition interest it protects.

**Current rule:** The Second Amendment protects an individual’s keeping and bearing of arms within its constitutional scope; formal militia enrollment, current service or preparation for organized military activity is not a prerequisite. Through the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, the same constitutional standards apply against the States. Cruikshank and Presser cease to control only insofar as they bar that application; Presser’s unauthorized armed-organization disposition remains. Acquisition necessary to constitutionally protected keeping falls within that protection, so a State cannot categorically exclude a claim because it obstructed purchase rather than seized an arm. Coverage still depends on the actual weapon and conduct and is distinct from the validity of a restriction. Military usefulness alone neither excludes an arm nor supplies immunity from manufacture, transfer, possession, taxation or registration controls. In particular, personal protection and military utility alone supply no exemption from NFA identification and registration requirements administering lawful making and transfer.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal holder, state incorporation and necessary acquisition: The arms guarantee is personally held and does not require formal militia enrollment; The Fourteenth Amendment applies the personal arms guarantee to the States; Necessary acquisition cannot be excluded merely because the State obstructed purchase, 1995-12-11. O’Connor Parts III-A–C with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (six).
- Military-utility boundary: The right is personally held, and military usefulness supplies no automatic immunity, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part III-A, all nine.
- Separate NFA registration obligation: Military usefulness supplies no exemption from the separate registration duty, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part IV, all Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Love, 1995-12-11, and Hale, 1996-02-12, for their respective components.

**Open question:** No comprehensive weapons classification, closed regulatory list or universal defensive-purpose rule is established. Purchase from every seller is not guaranteed; licensing and investigation are not categorically prohibited. State-law illegality alone does not establish infringement. Registration that cannot lawfully be approved remains a different unresolved application.

**Related current rules:** Core-Preservation Review supplies the justification standard after constitutional coverage is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Love v. Pepersack; United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016.

## Standards and Tests selection: Core-Preservation Review

### Core-Preservation Review

**Governing question:** Whether a restriction burdens constitutionally protected arms activity and what justification its practical operation requires.

**Current rule:** Stage 1 asks whether the law interferes with protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation of arms, with constitutional coverage determined from text, history and the Court's precedents. If protected activity is burdened, Stage 2 requires justification corresponding to the severity of the restriction's practical operation, subject to the independent categorical rule that government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home.

A core deprivation receives strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation receives intermediate scrutiny: an important governmental objective, a substantial relationship between the regulation and that objective, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate the protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right remain unconstitutional. Routine equipment specifications, administrative procedures and minor requirements qualify for that last category only when they do not materially obstruct the right. Actual operation controls: calling an effective prohibition a safety measure or administrative rule cannot reduce its level of review.

**Exceptions and limits:** The home boundary protects a meaningful core; it does not grant immunity to every weapon, person or use. Necessary ammunition and transportation receive protection as incidents of protected keeping or bearing, not through coverage of every item or transport method. That boundary, the graduated standard and the application to particular facts perform different functions.

**Application to particular persons:** The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation of particular persons under Wright's Core-Preservation Review and satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use challenge. Keeping and bearing remain covered at Stage 1; at Stage 2, preventing armed recurrence is an important objective, the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate and conviction safeguards substantially connect the restriction to that objective, and the defined eligibility and relief limits constrain its burden; the personal right does not compel an official-use exception.

*Love* recognizes personal protection, *Hale* distinguishes coverage from regulation, and *Rybar* rejects a setting-based denial of the right. Keeping or bearing does not lose protection merely because it occurs at work. There is, however, no independent fundamental entitlement to an armed public job. *Wright* measures justification by practical burden. This serious disability, confined by its adjudicated conduct predicate and statutory safeguards, is a substantial regulation of particular persons rather than incidental paperwork or a general abolition of ordinary defensive arms. The developed claim concerns access through the governmental channel for armed service; no individual's ordinary home-possession application is presented.

The contrary argument confuses an individual right with immunity from a justified conduct-based restriction. A service weapon remains accessible to its possessor; official employment does not neutralize the risk established by qualifying conduct. Intermediate scrutiny is satisfied on the presented record, independently of ordinary equality review. This does not authorize disarmament for any misdemeanor, a mere allegation or an unrelated offense. An improperly classified conviction, an unavailable statutory safeguard or a genuinely different individual private-possession record remains open. Wright's prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession remains intact; no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question is presented.

**Statutory scope of the official-use application:** The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

**Authority by component:**

- Stage 1 coverage, all three Stage 2 standards, practical operation and the independent categorical home rule: United States v. Wright, 1998-10-05, Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (five direct rationale votes).
- Both-stage remand direction and incidental registration application: Wright, Parts II-C and III; the same five. All nine support the bounded machinegun vacatur and registration affirmance. Stevens's concurrence, Part A, joined by all seven other Associates, independently sustains the tax-administration and registration ground; Stone-Zsela adds no independent tax ground.
- Substantial regulation of qualifying persons, intermediate scrutiny and official-use limits: Fraternal Order of Police v. United States, 1998-10-07, Core-Preservation Review permits this conduct-based disability and official-use exclusion; Statutory scope retained throughout. Souter's opinion is joined in full by all nine Justices.
- Preserved-claim boundary: Hiley v. Barrett, 1998-10-09, Part II, Personal arms protection does not revive abandoned claims; O'Connor, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight); Stone-Zsela agrees in judgment only.

**Present operation effective:** Wright, 1998-10-05; Fraternal Order of Police, 1998-10-07, for the application to qualifying persons in the defined official-use category.

**Open question:** Prohibiting ordinary functional handguns in the home and effectively eliminating all lawful public carry illustrate core burdens; no particular public-carry law is before the Court. Public-carry licensing, safety instruction, examinations, demonstrated proficiency, refresher training and recertification, including their classification and justification, are expressly reserved. No universal numerical rule, militia exclusion, military-weapon immunity or comprehensive prohibited-arms list is adopted.

The tier and ultimate arms validity of Wright's machinegun restriction remain undecided. Hiley does not adjudicate the abandoned arms claim or its private-possession application; Wright and Fraternal Order of Police govern preserved arms challenges.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Wright — Core-Preservation Review](HOLDINGS.md#core-preservation-review-governs-restrictions-on-the-personal-arms-right); [Wright — Both-stage remand](HOLDINGS.md#the-machinegun-count-affirmance-requires-both-stages-in-the-first-instance); [Fraternal Order of Police — Conduct-based disability and official-use exclusion](HOLDINGS.md#core-preservation-review-permits-this-conduct-based-disability-and-official-use-exclusion); [Hiley — Preserved claims](HOLDINGS.md#personal-arms-protection-does-not-revive-abandoned-claims).

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.

### Illinois v. Wardlow, No. 98-1036

Merits decision, January 12, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 2, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois. The question is whether the actual pre-stop record supplied particularized reasonable suspicion after Wardlow looked toward officers and fled in an area associated with narcotics trafficking. The subsequent frisk and bag search are outside the granted question.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, who would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand for any properly remaining issues.

The Illinois Supreme Court's judgment sustaining suppression is affirmed because the initial stop lacked reasonable suspicion. The existing suppression judgment remains in force; the Court does not separately decide the justification or scope of the frisk.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas as to Part I only | Five-Justice affirmance | All nine join the governing rule in Part I; five join application and remedy in Part II |
| Dissent in part | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand | Disagrees with Part II; joins Part I's common rule |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Suspicion must remain individualized when flight and location are considered together.

Terry requires objective, particularized reasonable suspicion under the whole circumstances. Flight may contribute to that assessment, and a demonstrated connection between the location and crime may supply context, but location alone is insufficient and neither any flight nor every flight in a broadly described high-crime area creates a universal permission to stop.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support as the governing law applied here.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry permits grounded inferences rather than proof of an offense, while Brown v. Texas rejects substituting a neighborhood label for an individualized basis. A refusal to cooperate or departure from police is not automatically criminally suspicious. Ornelas requires independent review of ultimate constitutional sufficiency with respect for historical findings and due weight to supported local inferences; it permits no supplementation of testimony. The later discovery of a firearm cannot justify the earlier stop. This common rule leaves the Justices' disagreement about its application intact.

##### II. This record does not establish the required suspicion.

Wardlow's observed departure and route, considered with the credited narcotics setting and a permissible police-recognition inference, do not supply a sufficiently particularized basis for suspecting him of wrongdoing on this record. The initial stop was therefore unlawful, and the Illinois judgment sustaining suppression is affirmed.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has direct support from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court credits the state high court's finding about narcotics activity and does not exclude flight from consideration. Nolan saw Wardlow look toward the officers and run through a gangway and alley, but observed no law violation or transaction. His description of customers and lookouts concerned what normally occurred in such areas; it did not place a crowd or such activity beside Wardlow on this occasion. Nolan's uniform is established, while vehicle markings and Wardlow's observation of the whole caravan are not. Even allowing an inference that he recognized police, the flight's significance and the generalized setting remained too ambiguous to connect this person adequately to suspected criminal activity. The contrary reading gives those circumstances more particularizing force than this testimony supports. The Court finds neither an innocent motive nor actual guilt, requires no observed completed offense, and adds no categorical third factor.

**Questions not reached:** The separate safety basis and scope of the frisk and bag search are not adjudicated. A stronger flight-related record remains governed by Terry's existing totality inquiry.

#### Precedent Treatment

Terry v. Ohio is applied without a new categorical flight or corroboration rule. Brown v. Texas preserves the insufficiency of location alone. Ornelas v. United States supplies independent ultimate review and due weight for grounded factual inferences; it is not authority to invent additional observations. Flippo v. West Virginia's separate-authorization principle is consistent with keeping the stop and later search distinct but does not decide the stop's factual sufficiency.

#### Law After Decision

The existing totality standard remains unchanged. This pre-stop combination is insufficient on the supported record; flight remains relevant in other factually supported settings. The decision creates neither a right-to-flee exclusion of all evidentiary weight nor a mandatory third circumstance beyond flight and location.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, agrees with the governing totality rule but finds reasonable suspicion here. Wardlow looked toward the officers and immediately ran through a gangway and alley in the credited narcotics setting. For the dissent, that temporal sequence and evasive route provide the individualized conduct missing from a location-only stop. Police recognition can reasonably be inferred without proving that he noticed every vehicle, and an innocent explanation need not defeat every grounded investigative inference. The dissent assumes neither marked cars nor an actual crowd, transaction, pre-stop firearm or guilty motive; the trial court's racial observation supplies no suspicion. It applies Ornelas's controlling independent review while giving weight to supported local inferences; Scalia retains his prior objection to that review allocation. The four would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand for any properly remaining issue, without automatically reinstating the conviction or deciding the separate frisk and bag search.

#### Source Notes

The petition, Joint Appendix and party briefs supply the suppression testimony and the state decisions. Testimony establishing Nolan's uniform is distinct from an inferred recognition of police, and the general expectation of customers or lookouts is distinct from an observed crowd. The state high court accepted the narcotics-area finding. The firearm was discovered after the stop and cannot supply its predicate.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Illinois Supreme Court's suppression judgment stands. No further ruling on the bag search or frisk is directed by this disposition.

**End of entry: Illinois v. Wardlow, merits decision, January 12, 2000.**

## Earlier published decision: Florida v. J. L.

Source: `terms/OT1999/output/OT_1999CHUNK4.md`.

### Florida v. J. L., No. 98-1993

Merits decision, March 28, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 29, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Florida, reviewing 727 So. 2d 204, which quashed the reversal of the order suppressing the gun. The question is whether the anonymous gun report and its actual corroboration supplied reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and frisk.

An unknown caller identified several young Black males at a bus stop and reported that the one in a plaid-looking shirt carried a gun. Officers arrived about six minutes after dispatch, found the described group and clothing, and saw no suspicious conduct. The officer directed J.L. to place his hands on the bus stop and began a frisk; she saw the gun butt only after the frisk began. Race served to identify the reported person and supplied no inference of illegality. The case concerns suppression in delinquency proceedings, not review of a conviction or sentence.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Suppression remains in place. The Court directs no additional dismissal, acquittal, release, new trial, evidentiary hearing or remedy for either companion.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join both holdings, including contextual reliability, rejection of an ordinary-firearm exception, the requirement of justification before the intrusion, and every stated limit.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### An accurately identified target does not make this gun accusation reliable

**Holding:** An anonymous assertion that the described person carries a concealed gun does not supply reasonable suspicion for a forcible stop and protective frisk when, as here, police promptly confirm only publicly observable identity and location and have no further circumstance adequately supporting the accusation's reliability. The inquiry remains the totality of the circumstances, and corroborated predictions are one possible means of supporting reliability rather than a requirement for every anonymous report.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying the person accused and supplying adequate grounds to credit the accusation are different tasks. Terry requires objective grounds before police make the seizure and protective search, although reasonable suspicion is less demanding than probable cause. Gates and White require reliability and basis of knowledge to be assessed together. White's corroborated future conduct showed special familiarity that the present description of an openly visible scene does not establish. The prompt response and accurate description matter, but together they still provide no adequate assurance concerning the concealed gun. Adams v. Williams permitted action without an officer's own view of the weapon because the personally known, present informant supplied assurances missing here. Police need not invariably wait to see a gun; sufficiently reliable information may authorize action. The Court does not require predictions, exclude innocent facts, find this caller dishonest, or require a particular questioning protocol.

##### The gun allegation and its later confirmation do not supply the missing authority

**Holding:** The ordinary safety concern raised by this gun allegation creates no exception to the requirement of sufficiently reliable grounds for the initial stop and frisk. A gun first observed after that intrusion began cannot justify commencing it.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry already permits a limited protective search on less than probable cause when its predicates exist. The danger that would follow if an allegation is true does not establish sufficient reason to credit the allegation. Otherwise, an unknown person could direct a search of any accurately described target merely by adding an untested gun accusation. Dickerson requires lawful protective access before a discovery may justify seizure, and Wardlow rejects retrospective reliance on the weapon ultimately found. Neither decision is treated as having already decided the anonymous-tip question. Here the officer's view of the gun followed the start of the frisk and supplies no pre-stop observation. The Court leaves different imminent threats, accountable eyewitness reports, school searches, airport screening and other special settings unresolved. Other concrete assurances, including nonpredictive ones, may establish reliability in another case. This decision adds no restriction to an independently lawful protective search after a valid stop and adopts no universal equation between firearm possession, illegality and dangerousness.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Terry v. Ohio supplies the antecedent objective grounds and bounded protective-search authority; its lower reasonable-suspicion threshold remains.
- Adams v. Williams is distinguished by its personally known, present informant; officers still need not personally observe a weapon when sufficiently reliable information supports action.
- Illinois v. Gates and Alabama v. White supply contextual reliability analysis; White's predictive corroboration is a sufficient example, not an invariant checklist.
- Ornelas applies to independent review of constitutional sufficiency with protection for historical facts and genuine due weight for grounded experience; it establishes no additional observation based on experience.
- Minnesota v. Dickerson preserves authorized contact and lawful access; its manipulation holding remains distinct from the initial-stop defect here.
- Illinois v. Wardlow supplies objective totality and the rejection of retrospective gun justification; its actual observed-conduct dispute does not establish an anonymous-tip rule.

#### Law After Decision

An accurate anonymous description of a person in public does not alone make an otherwise unsupported concealed-gun accusation sufficient for this stop and frisk. Ordinary gun danger supplies no shortcut, while other concrete reliability circumstances and materially different settings remain subject to their own facts. The existing totality and protective-search standards continue in force.

#### Source Notes

The Florida judgments, suppression order and officer's reproduced testimony appear in the [record and briefs collection](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0284). The testimony places sight of the gun after the frisk began and establishes approximately six minutes between dispatch and arrival. The record establishes no caller identification, call tracing, recording, actual basis of knowledge, pre-stop gun sighting or particularized emergency. [Alabama v. White](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep496/usrep496325/usrep496325.pdf) and [Adams v. Williams](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep407/usrep407143/usrep407143.pdf) supply the distinct informant settings discussed above.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Florida Supreme Court judgment is affirmed and suppression remains effective. Further lawful state proceedings follow the ordinary effect of that judgment. No additional hearing or retained Supreme Court return condition is imposed.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

#### I. The particular preservation need and limited execution justify this restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers may temporarily restrict unaccompanied entry into a home while obtaining a warrant when particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual risk of evidence destruction, a restraint reasonably confined to preservation and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization together justify that intrusion. The approximately two-hour restriction here satisfies those conditions in combination; probable cause, refusal of consent, awareness of an investigation, disposable drugs, jailability or duration alone creates no automatic exception or safe harbor.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Stone-Zsela dissents; Stevens would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted and, because the Court reaches the merits, would affirm suppression. Souter's additional concurrence does not limit his full join.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton preserves the home's special warrant protection. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not authorize a greater residential intrusion, and Bond requires an independent basis for an added intrusion despite lawful presence. Edmond addresses suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints; its program-purpose threshold supplies no rule for this individualized preservation measure.

Probable cause was uncontested. McArthur's wife reported seeing him slide drugs under the couch; he knew officers were investigating and had ready access to the identified disposable evidence. These particular circumstances reasonably supported a destruction concern without requiring police to wait for an observed attempt. Refusal to consent supplies neither guilt nor an independent exigency. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another restrained unaccompanied reentry. McArthur made two or three entries for cigarettes and telephone use with observation just inside the doorway. The officers conducted no evidentiary search before the warrant. Their approximately two-hour effort was diligent and the actual restriction reasonably preserved the evidence pending a magistrate's decision.

Place gives duration and diligence independent importance; no fixed two-hour allowance follows. Welsh requires attention to offense gravity. These offenses were modest but jailable: up to thirty days for the marijuana count and one year for paraphernalia. That distinguishes Welsh's civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest setting without making jailability sufficient. The substantial home interest and modest offenses weigh against restraint, but the actual preservation need and limited warrant-directed execution make this measure reasonable.

Segura's independent-source result does not validate every earlier seizure, and its broader Part IV discussion had only two votes. The Court relies on the justification before restraint, not the later discoveries. Limited observational consent is not general search consent or abandonment of the home-access objection. No general power to occupy or search homes, police-created unlawful exigency, requirement to exhaust every imaginable alternative or universal warrant-exception checklist is decided.

#### II. The presented suppression ground cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Illinois appellate judgment is reversed insofar as suppression rests on this temporary restriction of residential access, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings. Any actually preserved independent warrant, admissibility or exclusionary-rule issue remains governed by its own law; none is found established or newly created here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court treated the temporary restraint as an unlawful seizure and suppressed the later warrant evidence on that ground. Because the actual restraint was justified, that ground fails. The Court does not give the later warrant retroactive force, independently resolve every feature of that warrant or determine guilt. Correction of this suppression ground requires further proceedings, not an automatic conviction, dismissal of charges, release, new trial or new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: preserves the home's warrant protection; no general residential-entry authority is created.
- Whren v. United States: preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis and independent limits on greater intrusion, scope and duration; traffic probable cause is not residential authority.
- Bond v. United States: applies the requirement of independent justification for an added intrusion; lawful presence and limited observation do not imply consent to search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond: distinguishes suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints from this individualized preservation measure; its program-purpose holding remains intact.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: applies attention to actual duration and diligence in temporary preservation; no fixed permissible interval is transferred from luggage to a home.
- Welsh v. Wisconsin, 466 U.S. 740: preserves the importance of offense gravity and home protection; distinguishes its civil, nonjailable nighttime arrest without making every jailable offense sufficient for restraint.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796: preserves its independent-source holding while declining to treat it as approval of every initial seizure; the broader securing-premises discussion in Part IV remains a two-Justice position, not a majority holding.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

**Souter concurs and joins the Court opinion in full.** Exigency and permissible scope depend on the risk at the time of the proposed action. In his assessment, while McArthur was inside with immediate access to the hidden drugs, the actual destruction risk could have justified prompt entry and search. Once McArthur stepped outside and police could control access, the same immediate necessity for a greater warrantless evidentiary intrusion was absent. Temporary control of reentry while pursuing a warrant respects the preference for a magistrate's authorization and affords a reasonable opportunity to secure it. Police need not let an occupant return simply to recreate the emergency that would support a greater intrusion. This analysis distinguishes the risk and available response at each point; it does not find that a greater search occurred, infer an unreported occupant or turn the later discoveries into the earlier justification. The conditional greater-intrusion view belongs to his concurrence and is not an additional Court holding.

**Stone-Zsela dissents.** Control of access to a home is a seizure requiring an adequate warrant exception. The wife's contemporaneous observation of concealment strengthens probable cause and bears on risk, but hiding an existing stash does not itself show imminent destruction. McArthur could see his wife confer with police and leave with an officer; those facts support anticipation of a search, but add no particularized urgency beyond that awareness and the disposable character of drugs. Treating that combination as sufficient would authorize a similar restraint in nearly every residential drug investigation. An actual destruction attempt is not invariably required, but the inference must concern imminent loss rather than probable cause alone.

The two-hour duration and accompanied entries reduce the burden but cannot supply the missing exception. Segura does not validate every initial seizure because a later warrant produces evidence. Welsh gives the modest offenses real weight without misclassifying them as nonjailable. Contextual reasonableness evaluates a demonstrated exception; it cannot replace the warrant boundary. Stone-Zsela would affirm suppression for the search resulting from the unsupported restraint, subject to any independently preserved exclusionary-rule issue actually left unresolved. He would not invalidate the later warrant on an unpresented ground or dismiss independently supported charges.

**Stevens dissents.** He first would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because these slight possession offenses make an unsuitable occasion to define the limits of restraints on residential possession. Because the Court decides the merits, he would affirm. Welsh requires serious attention to offense gravity, and his understanding of Segura gives deprivation of home possession independent significance despite an eventual warrant. Even accepting probable cause, diligent warrant pursuit, accompanied entries and the comparatively limited restraint, he concludes that the substantial interest in private use of the home outweighs this modest preservation interest. His position concerns these facts; it neither denies that the offenses permit imprisonment nor categorically prohibits every misdemeanor preservation measure. He does not demand an observed destruction attempt in every case.
