## Standards and Tests selection: Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

### Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

**Governing question:** Due process protection against arbitrary punitive amounts, including meaningful judicial review, substantive excessiveness and territorial limits.

**Current rule:** A State may not remove meaningful judicial review of the amount of a punitive-damage award without an adequate substitute protecting against arbitrary deprivation. Review limited to whether any evidence supports an award, or safeguards directed only to entitlement rather than amount, do not necessarily fill that gap. The inquiry concerns meaningful protection against arbitrary magnitude; it does not establish a fixed constitutional ratio, mandatory remittitur, a prescribed written-opinion format or a required number of reviewing courts. Independently of adequate procedure, due process forbids punitive awards grossly excessive in relation to legitimate punishment and deterrence; fair notice includes possible severity as well as punishable conduct. Review considers reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff, and authorized or imposed civil or criminal sanctions for comparable misconduct. Reprehensibility is especially important; the guideposts are neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a ratio or dollar ceiling. Small compensation, egregious wrongdoing, difficult detection or difficult valuation can support a larger ratio; speculative potential loss and wealth cannot replace the required relationship to the wrong. A State may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on the State or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions may nevertheless illuminate pattern, intent and reprehensibility.

**Authority by component:**

- Amount review and inadequate substitutes: Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg — Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process; Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap, 1994-06-24. Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this bounded procedural rule, without a substantive numerical rule. Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and limited review remedy.
- BMW — Parts I and III-A, substantive excessiveness, notice and qualified guideposts, 1996-05-20; Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (five).
- BMW — Part II, territorial punishment and evidence distinction, 1996-05-20; the same five plus Stone-Zsela (six).

**Present operation effective:** BMW, May 20, 1996; Oberg’s procedural protection remains operative.

**Open question:** The decision does not itself determine the permissible amount of the award or prescribe the only constitutionally sufficient system of review.

**Holdings navigation:** Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process; Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap; BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, 517 U.S. 559.

**Open question concerning territorial punishment:** Whether a State may seek to change unlawful conduct elsewhere remains unresolved. The rule imposes no universal exclusion of foreign evidence or rule governing repeated punitive awards for the same conduct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

### Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review?

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No fixed ratio, mandatory exhaustive factor list, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. The gross-disproportionality inquiry assesses the offense’s gravity without converting the case’s lawful-funds, limited-harm and sentencing findings into a mandatory checklist or its dollar amounts into constitutional ceilings. The distinct due-process defense for a separately held automobile interest is stated in ‘Reasonable prevention and a separately held automobile interest’; other constitutional innocent-owner questions remain unresolved. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy, which uses the independent civil-intent/clearest-proof inquiry. Alexander’s independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct. Punitive criminal forfeiture under §982(a)(1) is a fine and violates the Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the offense. Statutory connection alone does not answer that question, and instrumentality is no categorical prerequisite excluding all currency-reporting forfeitures. The appellate constitutional proportionality determination is reviewed independently; underlying supported factual findings receive clear-error review.

## Opening authority: TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources, 509 U.S. 443 (1993)

Source: `state/holdings/tort-law.md`.

#### TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources, 509 U.S. 443 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-479.
**Decided:** June 25, 1993.

##### The particular judicial review is constitutionally sufficient

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The jury process followed by the trial judge’s hearing and review and the state supreme court’s review of this punitive award satisfy procedural due process on this record. The trial judge’s failure to provide a separate written explanation does not by itself invalidate the award; this decision supplies neither a universal procedural checklist nor a substantive permissible ratio.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s plurality procedural discussion, expressly agreeing on this proposition with Scalia’s concurrence joined by Thomas, this proposition; Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Stevens, Blackmun and Kennedy adopt the procedural discussion; Scalia and Thomas expressly concur that the instructed jury and actual judicial reasonableness review provided here satisfy due process. This common case-specific proposition has five votes; no formal whole-opinion join or Marks substantive rule is inferred.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Five agree only that the particular process is sufficient. No ratio, universal procedural checklist, potential-harm formula, instruction-waiver rule or replacement substantive-excessiveness test commands a majority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the $10 million punitive award by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas (5–4). The $19,000 compensation, title ownership and underlying state tort liability remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip, 499 U.S. 1: actual procedural safeguards applied; no mandatory reproduction of every Alabama feature.
- Garnes v. Fleming Landfill, 186 W.Va. 656, 413 S.E.2d 897: state review framework examined as part of this record, not transformed into a federal checklist.

##### Whether the amount of this punitive award establishes a controlling substantive due-process rule

**Authority of the judgment:** The $10 million punitive award is affirmed by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, 5–4. No controlling substantive rationale exists under Marks. Stevens and Blackmun assess substantive reasonableness using potential harm; Kennedy instead relies on rational punishment for the misconduct shown and rejects the reconstructed potential-harm arithmetic; Scalia and Thomas reject substantive numerical review and rely on traditional procedure. The latter rationale is not a logical subset of either substantive rationale. Agreement on this judgment does not establish a ratio, a general substantive test or an instruction-waiver rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The independently majority-supported procedural proposition is stated above. Haslip’s existing force remains; the particular judgment supplies no general approval of another award with a similar ratio.

## Opening authority: Honda Motor Co., Ltd. v. Oberg, 512 U.S. 415

Source: `state/holdings/tort-law.md`.

#### Honda Motor Co., Ltd. v. Oberg, 512 U.S. 415

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-644.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process

**Holding:** Oregon may alter historical procedures, but it may not remove the traditional judicial safeguard against an arbitrary punitive-damages amount without an adequate substitute. Where the existing process leaves the amount without that protection, due process requires a meaningful opportunity for judicial examination and lawful correction of the amount; review confined to whether any evidence supports punitive entitlement does not perform that function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this bounded procedural rule, without a substantive numerical rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip, 499 U.S. 1: applied for the function of actual instructions and judicial review; no requirement to reproduce every Alabama procedure follows.
- TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources Corp., 509 U.S. 443: distinguished from a legal prohibition on amount review; its record-specific procedural sufficiency remains controlling, and its fractured substantive grounds supply no numerical or potential-harm formula.
- Hurtado v. California, 110 U.S. 516: applied to permit procedural innovation with adequate protection; it does not validate elimination of a safeguard without adequate replacement.

##### Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap

**Holding:** The instructions actually given, clear-and-convincing proof of punitive entitlement, prima-facie gate for wealth evidence, discretionary award, complaint-based ceiling and retained legal-error and no-evidence review, considered together, do not adequately replace judicial examination of the punitive amount. The judgment sustaining the $5 million punitive award is therefore reversed and remanded for constitutionally adequate review and further proceedings, without deciding that the sum is excessive, fixing a substitute, eliminating punitive entitlement or reopening compensation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and limited review remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip, 499 U.S. 1: applied to assess the function of safeguards together; Oregon's trial improvements do not by themselves replace the missing amount inquiry.
- TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources Corp., 509 U.S. 443: followed as to the difference between real review without a separate written trial explanation and a bar to review; its procedural holding is not converted into a substantive ceiling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permissible ratio, maximum award, precise constitutional review standard, federal checklist, compulsory remittitur, mandatory written opinion, or universal repeated appellate review is prescribed. Corporate status does not remove procedural protection. Neither the amount's substantive validity nor a new punitive-entitlement challenge is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the punitive award without adequate amount review is reversed and remanded for meaningful judicial examination, lawful correction and consistent further proceedings. No substantive excessiveness finding, substitute sum, automatic punitive retrial or new challenge to punitive entitlement is decided. The compensatory judgment remains undisturbed.

## Opening authority: United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`.

#### United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1487.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### A punitive criminal forfeiture must not be grossly disproportionate to its offense

**Holding:** Section 982(a)(1)’s forfeiture imposed on this convicted offender as punishment is a fine subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. A punitive forfeiture violates that Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the offense for which it is imposed; statutory property connection alone does not answer excessiveness, and instrumentality is not a categorical prerequisite barring every currency-reporting forfeiture.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial respect for legislative punishment judgments and the imprecision of gravity assessments make gross disproportionality, rather than strict equivalence, the governing boundary. Solem supplies that respect and proportionality analogy without deciding a new imprisonment question. The nature of the offense, culpability, harm, property’s relation to wrongdoing and authorized sanctions inform the comparison where relevant. They are not a compulsory exhaustive checklist, fixed ratio or government-cost formula. Congress may punish a reporting violation through property forfeiture, but its statutory authorization does not foreclose the constitutional inquiry. The Court rejects the circuit’s conclusion that such currency can never be forfeited for want of an instrumentality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Austin v. United States:** Partly punitive coverage and an excessiveness inquiry beyond statutory connection are applied; its holdings are not converted into an instrumentality-only test or a universal historical forfeiture theory.
- **Alexander v. United States:** Its independent offense-related Excessive Fines inquiry for criminal forfeiture is applied and given the gross-disproportionality formulation here.
- **Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal:** Its distinction between governmental punishment and private awards supports fine coverage.
- **Solem v. Helm:** Its legislative-deference and proportionality principles inform this fines rule without a new imprisonment holding.
- **Department of Revenue v. Kurth Ranch, United States v. Ursery and Hudson v. United States:** Their distinct Double Jeopardy classification rules remain intact and supply no automatic Excessive Fines result or universal cost ceiling.
- **One Lot Emerald Cut Stones and One Assortment of 89 Firearms:** Their distinct customs and forfeiture classifications establish no exemption or proportionality answer for this direct criminal reporting fine.

##### The legal proportionality judgment receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review the constitutional determination whether this punitive forfeiture is grossly disproportionate, while reviewing supported underlying factual findings for clear error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The facts found and the constitutional evaluation perform different work. Deference to the trial court’s supported findings does not transfer the legal boundary of the Excessive Fines Clause to unrestricted sentencing discretion. Independent review supplies no authority to invent an additional offense or substitute adverse facts for the findings. The Court announces no universal review standard for every mixed question.

##### Full forfeiture is grossly disproportionate on these findings

**Holding:** Forfeiture of the full $357,144 is grossly disproportionate to the willful reporting offense established here, considering the lawful funds and purpose, absence of another-crime connection, limited reporting harm and particular sentencing assessment together. The refusal of full forfeiture is affirmed without reviewing the existing $15,000 amount or setting a constitutional dollar ceiling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** The reporting duty is serious, and willful failure remains culpable even when transporting the funds would otherwise be lawful. But the proved offense withheld information; the findings establish neither criminal proceeds nor laundering, trafficking, fraud or a separate revenue loss against which the whole fund may be measured. Dismissal of the false-statement count does not establish that no false statement occurred.

The applicable six-month and $5,000 Guidelines maxima reflect the particular offense’s assessed severity. Congress’s broader five-year and $250,000 maxima and the forfeiture authorization deserve weight but do not conclusively make this full exaction proportionate. Treating them as conclusive would deprive independent excessiveness review of work. The lawful source, lawful debt and lack of another-crime connection are relevant together; lawful property is not automatically immune from a fine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting system’s preventive function and the large unreported transaction supply the strongest contrary argument. They do not overcome the extraordinary mismatch on these supported findings. The Court substitutes no new amount. Because Bajakajian did not cross-appeal, neither $15,000’s validity nor authority to select it is decided; no rule identifies $15,001 as excessive. Wealth, income, livelihood effects, other materially different crime-connected forfeitures and a comprehensive innocent-owner or in-rem rule remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment refusing the Government’s demand for full forfeiture remains effective. No new calculation or further forfeiture hearing is directed. The unappealed partial forfeiture and other sentence components remain undisturbed without affirmative adjudication of their independent validity.

## Opening authority: Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`.

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.

### BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, No. 94-896

Merits decision, May 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 11, 1995. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1257 to the Supreme Court of Alabama, 646 So. 2d 619. The questions are whether Alabama provided a meaningful remedy after identifying improper reliance on out-of-state transactions and whether the remitted $2 million punitive award is grossly excessive under the Fourteenth Amendment. The latter question includes, but is not confined to, the 500-to-1 ratio to compensation.

Gore paid $40,750.88 for a BMW sold as new. Acid-rain paint damage during transit had required $601 in refinishing, which was not specifically disclosed. BMW NA's policy withheld disclosure of repairs costing less than three percent of suggested retail price. A detailer discovered the refinishing after about nine months; no apparent finish problem during that use or physical injury was established. The signed damage/inspection acknowledgment did not defeat the adjudicated suppression claim. The jury awarded $4,000 for diminished value and $4 million in punitive damages. Alabama found sufficient clear-and-convincing evidence of the conscious or deliberate fraud required for punitive entitlement in this action.

The parties stipulated to 983 refinished vehicles; BMW estimated 11–14 Alabama transactions. Counsel proposed roughly 1,000 transactions multiplied by $4,000. Alabama found substantial jury reliance on foreign transactions but distinguished their permissible use to show pattern or intent from their use as a punitive multiplier. Because the record did not establish which foreign transactions were unlawful, the state court rejected that multiplier and expressly excluded foreign acts from its own amount analysis. The trial court had held an evidentiary postverdict hearing and found no bias, passion or prejudice. The Alabama Supreme Court applied its punitive-review criteria, compared awards and conditionally reduced punitive damages to $2 million.

The operative opinion is the August 19, 1994 substituted opinion, not the withdrawn October 1993 opinion. Gore accepted the remittitur on August 26. The September 9 certificate affirms a total $2,004,000 judgment, with interest and costs. The separate judgment against manufacturer BMW AG was reversed and rendered for that defendant. BMW NA's punitive challenge does not reopen that disposition or the $4,000 compensation.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision. No Justice is recused or otherwise nonparticipating.

**Supporting:** Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, who would affirm the remitted judgment against the presented federal challenge.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Grossly excessive punitive damages violate due process; fair notice includes severity

**Controlling proposition:** A punitive award grossly excessive in relation to the State's legitimate interests in punishment and deterrence violates due process. The inquiry begins with those interests. Fair notice encompasses both conduct subject to punishment and the possible severity of the penalty; wealth does not remove that constitutional constraint.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: five.

**Controlling explanation:** States may punish fraud and deter its repetition through damages exceeding compensation. The different functions of compensation and punishment preclude a rule requiring the two to move in exact proportion. They do not authorize every amount. Haslip sustained punitive adjudication bounded by reasonable constraints and a relationship to legitimate punitive purposes; it did not approve every later Alabama award.

The strongest objection is that traditional procedures and actual judicial review should leave the selected amount to state judgment. Those safeguards are indispensable, but the conclusion that review occurred does not dispose of a separately presented claim of gross excessiveness. TXO sustained its particular process without supplying a controlling substantive formula. Oberg requires meaningful examination and lawful correction of the amount, while reserving the substantive question answered here.

Notice of liability and notice of severity perform related but distinct work. Alabama's established disclosure duty remains valid, and the adjudicated suppression is not made lawful by disagreement among States or a later statute. Nonetheless, the punishment must remain within the constitutional protection against grossly excessive deprivation. Corporate wealth can bear on deterrence without replacing the required relationship to the wrong. The Court adopts neither all criminal procedural protections for civil actions nor a right to mathematical advance calculation of every permissible award.

**Precedent treatment:** Haslip's substantive and procedural protections are applied. TXO's record-specific procedural holding and Oberg's amount-review requirement remain intact; neither is recast as an earlier adoption of today's substantive framework.

##### II. Foreign evidence and punishment are distinct; Alabama's stated correction is accepted

**Controlling proposition:** Alabama may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and having no impact on Alabama or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Whether a State may seek to change unlawful conduct elsewhere remains open. Relevant foreign transactions may illuminate pattern, intent and reprehensibility; their evidentiary use does not itself impose punishment for those transactions.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six.

**Controlling explanation:** Alabama has a legitimate interest in protecting its purchasers from fraud. That interest does not give it authority to punish lawful transactions disconnected from the State simply because it would prefer a different disclosure policy elsewhere. Differences among state policies therefore matter to the permissible reach of deterrence. The holding is qualified by lawfulness and absence of Alabama impact; it supplies no universal immunity for conduct outside the State.

Evidence and punishment answer different questions. A recurring policy, including transactions elsewhere, can support knowledge, intent or the character of the Alabama wrong when governing law makes it relevant. The Court neither excludes all such evidence nor adjudicates the legality of every sale in the national sample. Nor does it decide whether another State could punish conduct unlawful within its own authority.

The original multiplier presented the identified problem. Alabama rejected it and expressly excluded foreign acts from its own amount determination. That statement is accepted; a separate state concurrence's description does not establish that the majority covertly retained the multiplier. The judgment under review is the accepted $2 million remittitur. Recognizing the correction neither reinstates the original verdict nor determines whether the remaining amount survives the independent substantive inquiry in Part III.

**Precedent treatment:** Shutts and Allstate v. Hague support attention to the State's connection with the transactions affected. Their choice-of-law principles are not transformed into an evidence exclusion or a numerical punitive ceiling.

##### III-A. Three qualified guideposts structure constitutional excessiveness review

**Controlling proposition:** Review considers the reprehensibility of the defendant's conduct; the relationship of punitive damages to actual or potential harm to the plaintiff; and civil or criminal sanctions authorized or imposed for comparable misconduct. These are operative guideposts, with reprehensibility especially important, but not an exhaustive mechanical checklist, fixed ratio or dollar ceiling.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III-A, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: five.

**Controlling explanation:** Reprehensibility connects severity to the wrong being punished. The nature of the injury, deliberate or affirmative misconduct, repeated behavior and vulnerability can distinguish more blameworthy conduct from less aggravated wrongdoing. Economic injury is not categorically immune from substantial punishment; intentional misconduct or exploitation can justify a substantial award without physical injury.

The harm comparison likewise informs rather than mechanically decides proportionality. Actual compensation need not measure the harm deliberately threatened. Supported potential harm belongs in the inquiry, while speculative loss cannot be invented to justify a chosen number. Small compensation, egregious conduct, injury difficult to detect or harm difficult to value can support larger ratios. The Court announces no bright line beyond which every award fails.

Comparable authorized or imposed sanctions inform the seriousness with which the State treats the misconduct and the notice of possible severity. They do not automatically become exclusive civil-punishment caps. State discretion, deterrence and differences among remedies remain relevant, but cannot make every comparison immaterial.

These guideposts supply reasons for constitutional judgment rather than an unexplained impression that an award seems large. Haslip's refusal to establish a universal mathematical boundary is preserved. TXO's divided potential-harm reasoning cannot be treated as binding arithmetic, and disparity among jury outcomes alone establishes no bias. The inquiry requires judgment applied to lawful purposes and supported facts.

**Precedent treatment:** Haslip's reasonableness inquiry is developed into the stated guideposts. TXO establishes no controlling substantive formula, and this decision adopts no fixed ratio.

##### III-B. The remitted $2 million is grossly excessive on this record

**Controlling proposition:** After accepting Alabama's removal of foreign transactions from its punitive calculation, the remaining $2 million is grossly excessive in light of the established wrong, supported harm and comparable sanctions. Neither adequate power to review nor actual reduction conclusively establishes a constitutionally permissible amount.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III-B, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: five.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court accepts the deliberate-suppression finding and entitlement to punitive damages. Recurrence and concealment support punishment beyond compensation. The injury nevertheless was economic, without an established health or safety danger, and the character of this nondisclosure does not equate it with the most aggravated fraud. A repair bill alone would understate Gore's loss; the jury's $4,000 diminution finding supplies the actual compensation.

No supported much larger injury to Gore supplies the missing scale. The estimated 11–14 Alabama transactions can illuminate pattern and culpability, but are not adjudicated individual claims or an automatic aggregate punishment base. Neither national loss nor a larger future injury is established in this record. The 500-to-1 disparity therefore raises a substantial concern on this record, though that number is not itself a universal rule.

Variable disclosure laws inform notice and blameworthiness without erasing Alabama's adjudicated duty. The identified $2,000 statutory sanction and the comparison awards provide no adequate notice or justification for this magnitude. The statutory figure is a comparator, not an exclusive cap, and BMW's proposed $56,000 calculation is not adopted.

The strongest argument for affirmance combines deliberate recurring conduct, deterrence and Alabama's actual hearing, review and halving. Those circumstances receive weight; they do not justify the extraordinary remaining amount under the guideposts. BMW's ability to pay cannot fill the gap. This is a substantive conclusion about the corrected award, not a finding that the reviewing judges were dishonest, that no review occurred or that the original multiplier remained secretly operative.

**Precedent treatment:** Haslip did not immunize every Alabama result. TXO's supported threatened harm is not presumed present here. Oberg's protection of amount review is preserved without treating its distinct procedural defect as established in this case.

##### IV. Alabama chooses the lawful means of redetermining punitive damages

**Controlling proposition:** Reverse only the judgment sustaining the $2 million punitive award and remand for a lawful punitive disposition. Alabama initially determines whether a new punitive trial or an independent appellate determination is appropriate. The Court selects no sum and does not require a new jury merely because the original verdict used an improper multiplier.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: five.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court has decided that the corrected amount is grossly excessive, not merely that Alabama omitted a hearing or failed to provide enough explanatory words. Its remedy therefore requires a disposition consistent with that substantive holding. Repeating the same $2 million on this record with fuller prose would not answer it.

The accepted remittitur establishes the operative judgment; it does not prevent further lawful correction after federal review. State procedures retain the first choice between a new punitive trial and an appropriate appellate redetermination. No constitutional maximum, mandatory reduction or universal rule governing every judicial alteration is selected.

The compensation and suppression liability supply no basis for reopening, and entitlement to a lawful punitive award survives. BMW AG's separate favorable disposition remains outside this challenge. Neither revival of the discarded $4 million nor judgment on other purchasers' claims follows. The limited remand respects Alabama's punitive authority while enforcing the constitutional constraint applied here.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

Haslip remains governing: States may employ punitive damages with reasonable constraints, actual review and a relationship to legitimate punishment and deterrence. Its rejection of a universal mathematical line survives. The present guideposts and application develop substantive excessiveness review without facially invalidating Alabama's punitive system.

TXO's five-Justice procedural sufficiency proposition remains intact. Its distinct substantive writings establish no common ratio or potential-harm formula. Oberg continues to require meaningful examination and lawful correction of punitive **amount**, or an adequate functional substitute; review of entitlement or legal error alone does not necessarily suffice. The Court does not overrule that safeguard or impose its Oregon-specific result on Alabama's different process.

Shutts and Allstate v. Hague support territorial limits within their stated roles; neither creates a general prohibition on relevant foreign evidence. No precedent is overruled. The three-Justice supplemental constraints explanation and the dissenters' competing federalism and source-of-right theories retain their separate authority.

Due process forbids grossly excessive punitive awards in relation to legitimate state punishment and deterrence. Fair notice includes possible severity. Reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or potential harm to the plaintiff, and comparable sanctions are controlling guideposts, with their stated qualifications. They are neither optional observations nor a fixed ratio, exclusive checklist or dollar cap.

The qualified territorial rule has six votes. It excludes punishment of conduct lawful elsewhere and having no Alabama impact, preserves relevant evidence, and reserves the question of efforts to change unlawful foreign conduct. Alabama's stated removal of foreign transactions is accepted. Five Justices nevertheless invalidate the surviving $2 million on substantive grounds and require a lawful punitive redetermination.

Meaningful amount review remains required. The existence of process does not immunize every result, and this result does not establish absence of process. Compensation, punitive entitlement, BMW AG's dismissal, the validity of every form of judicial alteration and questions of repeated punishment for the same conduct remain within the limits stated above. No new general corporate-rights catalogue is adopted.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Breyer, concurring, joined by O'Connor and Souter.** All three join the Court's opinion in full and supply an additional explanation. A procedurally proper award ordinarily deserves a strong presumption of validity. Here both the standards' failure significantly to constrain discretion as interpreted and applied and the gross disproportion to legitimate punitive objectives overcome that presumption. The existence of hearings, state criteria and actual appellate correction is acknowledged.

Clear-and-convincing proof of punitive entitlement does not distinguish every permissible amount. Harm, culpability and profit criteria must constrain the actual sum; wealth and litigation incentives do not supply a missing limit, and inapplicable mitigation factors do not do so. A supported economic theory can provide meaningful discipline, but a hypothetical model cannot replace the grounds and evidence before the state court. Historical comparisons illustrate the concern without creating a damages formula. The concurrence neither invalidates Alabama's system on its face nor requires written arithmetic, a compulsory economic model or an automatic new trial. Its conjunctive rationale is supported by three Justices, not an additional Court holding.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** They would affirm. Their substantive/process distinction preserves traditional adjudication and meaningful judicial review enforcing state-law limits while rejecting an independent federal constitutional entitlement to a different punitive sum. They regard the guideposts as lacking a sufficient constitutional source and workable measure for federal supervision of amounts. Disagreement with a number does not prove the absence of a safeguard.

Alabama held a postverdict hearing, applied its review criteria, expressly removed the foreign multiplier and reduced the judgment. Assuming the identified error required correction, the correction occurred; they need not adopt the Court's territorial rule. Their position approves neither the original $4 million nor every possible remittitur. Both retain Oberg's amount-review protection; Thomas's agreement rests on his own TXO and Oberg positions, without adopting a separate opinion he did not join.

**Ginsburg, dissenting.** She would affirm from institutional restraint, federalism and respect for actual state correction. Alabama's courts addressed deliberate recurring fraud, considered their substantive criteria and comparison awards, and replaced the jury's national measure with a reduced judgment. The difficulty of supplying a workable federal scale counsels against displacing that state judgment here. Her concern is not answered simply by identifying a high ratio.

She does not join Scalia's categorical rejection of every substantive due-process protection or affirm by applying the Court's new guideposts. Her earlier reservation of a properly presented gross-excessiveness challenge remains meaningful. The present federal challenge does not justify additional intervention in light of the safeguards and correction actually supplied. State reform may address punitive practice, but proposed reforms are not treated as operative law, and a later disclosure amendment does not erase this adjudicated wrong. Her conclusion rests on the established review and correction.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part II and otherwise dissenting.** He would affirm the remitted $2 million punitive judgment. His answer to the first question is yes: Alabama supplied a constitutionally sufficient remedy for the identified use of foreign transactions through meaningful review and its established, accepted remittitur procedure. His answer to the second is no: constitutionally adequate adjudication and meaningful review do not leave an additional federal substantive power to invalidate an amount merely because its magnitude or ratio appears excessive.

BMW's corporate form does not remove its property and procedural protections, but those protections do not themselves establish a substantive entitlement to the sum a federal judge prefers. Each claimed constitutional consequence requires its own source. TXO established no controlling ratio or general substantive formula; Oberg requires meaningful examination and lawful correction of the amount without supplying a maximum, mandatory remittitur or federal recalculation formula. That amount-review function cannot be reduced to checking punitive entitlement alone.

He agrees that Alabama cannot punish beyond its lawful authority and that relevant foreign evidence differs from foreign conduct made an independent object of punishment. Knowledge, intent, deliberateness, notice or a common policy may make such evidence relevant. Alabama expressly corrected its identified error; another potentially lawful remedy does not automatically become constitutionally necessary.

Compensation measures loss, while punitive damages serve punishment and deterrence. A large ratio alone cannot determine whether the latter rests on a lawful basis; nor does a large compensatory award justify any punishment. Reprehensibility, ratios and comparable sanctions may inform state-law or otherwise established review without becoming the Court's new constitutional elements. Courts retain meaningful amount review and correction of unlawful punitive availability, instructions, proof, considerations, procedural unfairness and statutory violations. Those examples are not an exhaustive checklist.

His conclusion neither makes punitive damages unreviewable nor authorizes punishment outside Alabama's lawful authority. It preserves traditional remittitur, restores no part of the original $4 million, and reserves whether every form of judicial alteration is permissible. He joins Part II's narrow territorial and evidentiary propositions, but not the Court's independent substantive framework, application or remand.

#### Record and Statutory Qualifications

Appendix A, 1a–26a, contains the substituted Alabama opinion and Houston concurrence; the postverdict order follows, and 31a contains the September 9 certificate recording Gore's August 26 acceptance. The trial material, stipulated national sample, estimated Alabama transactions and competing state opinions retain their distinct status. The reproduced papers do not include the full jury charge or establish an additional concession, quantified national loss or adjudicated aggregate claim. BMW's proposed $56,000 and the $2,000 sanction comparison are arguments, not adopted ceilings. Houston's separate account does not establish the majority's continued use of the forbidden multiplier. No determination about multiple punitive awards for the same conduct is made.

The later 1993 amendment to Alabama §8-19-5 defines material damage by repair cost exceeding three percent of suggested retail price or $500, whichever is greater. Its calculation excludes replacement of tires, glass, bumpers and in-dash audio equipment with identical manufacturer equipment; below-threshold nondisclosure supplies neither revocation nor material misrepresentation or omission under that amendment. Alabama declined to apply it retroactively to this sale. Its terms and other States' disclosure rules bear on the arguments about notice and culpability without displacing the adjudicated earlier duty. Proposed reforms are not operative rules.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Alabama must provide a lawful punitive disposition consistent with the substantive constitutional ruling. It determines in the first instance whether a new punitive trial or an independent appellate redetermination is appropriate. The Court sets no replacement sum, mandatory remittitur figure or constitutional ratio. Merely adding explanation for the same $2 million on the same record would not cure the holding that this amount is grossly excessive.

The $4,000 compensation, underlying suppression liability, entitlement to some lawful punitive award and BMW AG's separate dismissal remain undisturbed. The original $4 million is not restored. No nationwide recovery, adjudication of other purchasers' claims or ruling on multiple punitive awards for the same conduct is entered. Stone-Zsela's affirmative answer concerning Alabama's correction and his partial territorial join do not constitute a separate Court judgment affirming the punitive award.

The case returns to Alabama for a lawful punitive disposition, with the State initially selecting between a new punitive trial and an independent appellate redetermination. No replacement sum, automatic jury proceeding, special mandate date or retained Supreme Court merits proceeding is prescribed. The original $4 million remains displaced; compensation, underlying suppression liability, lawful punitive entitlement and BMW AG's separate dismissal are not reopened. Ordinary mandate procedures apply.

### Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., No. 95-719

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 16, 1996; decided June 24, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit's September 12, 1995 judgment, 66 F.3d 427, in a diversity action in the Southern District of New York.

The Center conceded liability for 300 lost original photographic transparencies. A June 1994 jury awarded Gasperini $450,000, reflecting $1,500 per transparency. The district judge denied the Rule 59 new-trial motion without explanation. Applying New York's material-deviation standard, the Second Circuit ordered a new trial unless Gasperini accepted $100,000. No acceptance is established.

The questions are whether the state compensation standard governs, how Rule 59 operates with it, and what appellate review the Seventh Amendment permits. The appellate order is conditional, not an unconditional replacement judgment. The separate ruling allowing industry-standard evidence with other proof does not make $1,500 per transparency conclusive and is not overturned here.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens would affirm the existing conditional new-trial/$100,000 order; Scalia and Thomas would reverse that order and restore the district judgment against the reviewed excessiveness challenge.

The district judge must apply the state standard through Rule 59; appellate review is for abuse of discretion within the limits below. The Court selects neither $450,000 nor $100,000, orders no automatic new trial, and makes no independent findings about uniqueness or earning prospects.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | State measure governs; Stevens does not join the remand | State compensation standard and coexistence with Rule 59 |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Souter | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacatur and remand for district reconsideration | Federal trial responsibility, bounded appellate review, vacatur and remand |
| Concurrence in Part I and dissent from Part II and the judgment | Stevens |  | Would affirm the conditional new-trial/$100,000 order | State measure accepted; existing appellate order would be affirmed on legal-boundary review |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would reverse the appellate order and restore the district judgment | Rule 59 and historical reexamination barriers; reversal and restoration of district judgment |

#### Holdings

##### I. New York's compensation standard applies through the federal new-trial function

**Operative rule:** In this diversity action governed by New York damages law, the federal district court must assess excessiveness under CPLR section 5501(c)'s material-deviation measure through Rule 59. The state compensation constraint applies without importing the State's appellate hierarchy, displacing federal new-trial procedure, creating a fixed damages cap or authorizing federal additur.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Stevens does not join Part II's constitutional allocation or remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Erie and Guaranty Trust require respect for the State's substantive recovery limits in diversity. Allowing materially different compensation merely because federal jurisdiction is available would invite forum selection for a larger award and unequal administration of the same state claim. This is more than a preference for a particular reviewing court.

Hanna first requires asking whether the Federal Rule actually governs the disputed point. Rule 59 permits new trials for reasons historically recognized in federal actions at law. Excessiveness is such a reason; that text does not prescribe an immutable substantive degree of excessiveness independent of the law governing compensation. Applying New York's measure therefore preserves the federal motion and trial function rather than overriding a conflicting Rule.

Section 5501(c) directs the Appellate Division, in its specified review of a money judgment following an itemized verdict, to find excessiveness or inadequacy when the award materially deviates from reasonable compensation; the context is a request for a new trial unless a different award is stipulated. Although the enacted direction names the appellate court, Inya, Cochetti and Shurgan establish trial application of the same measure under New York law. Those decisions preserve sparingly exercised trial discretion. They do not compel one remedy for every claimed deviation or establish a photographic price schedule.

The contrary view treats the measure as a standard of judicial reassessment already occupied by Rule 59. But the traditional federal ground and the State's compensation constraint can perform their separate work. Browning-Ferris recognizes state damages confines alongside federal new-trial procedure; its reserved appellate constitutional question requires the separate analysis in Part II. Byrd preserves federal allocation of functions. The Court does not transplant every outcome-affecting state practice, the State's reviewing hierarchy or its power of conditional increases.

##### II. The district judge applies that measure; appellate review must remain genuinely deferential

**Operative rule:** The district judge must exercise the federal new-trial function using the state measure, while the court of appeals reviews that determination for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment. The Seventh Amendment permits that review of trial-court discretion but does not permit the appellate court to substitute its own first-instance valuation of the jury's compensatory award; this judgment must be vacated for district reconsideration under the proper allocation.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Reexamination Clause protects jury-tried facts while preserving the traditional judicial power to grant a new trial. The district judge's trial experience and recognized new-trial responsibility place the state-standard assessment there. Byrd requires preserving the federal division of functions even when state procedure affects outcomes.

Appellate examination of whether that authority was lawfully exercised can coexist with the protected jury function when it genuinely reviews discretion. It must correct an erroneous legal measure, but may not use the abuse label to perform a fresh valuation from the cold record. This resolves, within that limit, the constitutional appellate question Browning-Ferris expressly reserved. The contrary historical objection properly demands more than a change of labels; the required restraint preserves the distinction between reviewing the district court's exercise of new-trial power and independently retrying compensation.

The district denial supplies no demonstrated application of New York's measure. That absence is not a finding that the judge actually used a particular wrong formula. The Second Circuit's first operative valuation cannot substitute for the assigned district assessment. Its use of favorable inferences and a conditional remittitur does not remove that allocation problem. Another district consideration therefore remains necessary.

On remand the judge must examine the record under the state standard, informed by pertinent New York decisions, and select lawful relief. Comparable cases are guidance, not fixed prices. Neither the jury's $450,000 nor the appellate $100,000 is declared correct. Donovan's accepted-remittitur bar does not apply because no acceptance is established. If the district court orders remittitur, Gasperini must have the choice of accepting the reduced award or a new jury trial; the court may not simply substitute a lower judgment. This differs from the additur barred by Dimick. No automatic retrial or imported state conditional increase follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64, and Guaranty Trust Co. v. York, 326 U.S. 99:** Apply respect for substantive state recovery limits in diversity without making every outcome-affecting procedural rule binding.
- **Hanna v. Plumer, 380 U.S. 460:** Applies the Federal Rule coverage inquiry before an unguided choice of law. Rule 59's traditional new-trial ground coexists here with the state compensation measure.
- **Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Electric Cooperative, Inc., 356 U.S. 525:** Preserves federal institutional allocation rather than requiring reproduction of the state reviewing hierarchy.
- **Browning-Ferris Industries of Vermont, Inc. v. Kelco Disposal, Inc., 492 U.S. 257:** Applies its distinction between state damages constraints and federal new-trial procedure. Its express reservation of the Seventh Amendment appellate question is resolved here only by the bounded abuse-of-discretion rule.
- **Donovan v. Penn Shipping Co., 429 U.S. 648:** Preserves federal procedural control and the bar on appealing an accepted remittitur. The absence of acceptance here distinguishes that bar.
- **Dimick v. Schiedt, 293 U.S. 474:** Preserves traditional remittitur and the prohibition on federal additur. State conditional increases do not alter that distinction.
- **Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, June 24, 1994, and BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, May 20, 1996:** Preserve their distinct punitive-damages protections. Neither supplies a compensatory formula, mandatory number of reviewing courts or answer to this federal reexamination question.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., April 23, 1996:** Preserves function-specific jury allocation and its reservation of subsidiary appellate review; patent construction does not dictate this compensation review.
- **Thompson v. Keohane, November 29, 1995; Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996; and Koon v. United States, June 13, 1996:** Retain their particular legal/factual review standards. No universal independent review of mixed issues or separate Seventh Amendment holding is inferred from them.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

New York's material-deviation measure governs compensation review in this diversity action through the district judge's Rule 59 authority. The court of appeals may correct legal error and review for abuse of discretion, but must respect the district judge's case-specific assessment and may not make its own first operative valuation.

The state substantive constraint and federal allocation both retain force. There is no universal rule importing state appellate procedure, no automatic damages cap, and no change to federal review of an independently presented constitutional limit on damages. The state measure does not itself decide which amount is reasonable here or compel a particular form of relief.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Stevens joins Part I but dissents from Part II and the judgment. He agrees that the New York compensation constraint applies and that Rule 59 does not displace it. He would affirm the Second Circuit's existing order because, in his view, it enforced a legal outer boundary while drawing reasonable factual inferences for Gasperini. A court can correct compensation beyond the legal limit without impermissibly retrying a fact.

Stevens rejects a Seventh Amendment requirement of the Court's particular appellate deference. His common-law account preserves the essentials of jury protection without fixing every historical institutional form; practical respect for the district judge does not create a constitutional bar to this legal correction. The district court already had an opportunity to review the award, and he identifies no permissible different result requiring another pass. His affirmance would retain Gasperini's choice between a new trial and accepting $100,000, not impose an unconditional replacement judgment. He does not convert Markman into a general appellate-review holding or BMW into compensatory-damages law.

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Thomas. Their first independent objection is that Rule 59 governs the federal degree of judicial excessiveness review through its reference to historical federal practice. Under Hanna, Erie policies cannot displace a Federal Rule that governs the point. They acknowledge the State's trial-court application of section 5501(c), but classify that measure as judicial reassessment rather than a substantive cap. Genuine state damages elements, categories and actual statutory ceilings remain binding; the dissent does not insist upon a single verbal federal excessiveness formula.

Their second independent objection is historical: appellate correction of legal error differs from reassessing compensation facts or evidentiary weight. Neither an abuse-of-discretion label nor a conditional new-trial option makes prohibited appellate factual reexamination permissible. Browning-Ferris expressly reserved that constitutional issue; Honda's punitive procedure and the other modern allocations do not decide it.

Scalia and Thomas retain traditional trial-court new-trial power and conditional remittitur, distinguish additur, and preserve ordinary correction of genuine legal insufficiency. They would reverse the appellate conditional-remittitur order and restore the district judgment against this excessiveness challenge, without computing a different award. No independent reversible industry-evidence error is established.

#### Source Notes

The [Second Circuit opinion, 66 F.3d 427](https://static.case.law/f3d/66/html/0427-01.html), supplies the verdict, unexplained district ruling, conditional appellate order, separate evidence ruling and the material-deviation language in the itemized-verdict context. Its assessments of uniqueness and earning prospects are the appellate analysis under review, not findings newly made by this Court.

[Inya v. Ide Hyundai, 209 A.D.2d 1015](https://static.case.law/ad2d/209/html/1015-01.html), [Cochetti v. Gralow, 192 A.D.2d 974](https://static.case.law/ad2d/192/html/0974-01.html), and [Shurgan v. Tedesco, 179 A.D.2d 805](https://static.case.law/ad2d/179/html/0805-02.html), establish state trial application of the measure with sparingly exercised discretion. Their personal-injury amounts are not valuation evidence for these transparencies, and their state remedies do not authorize federal additur.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Court of Appeals with directions for district reconsideration of the Rule 59 motion under New York's material-deviation standard. The district court must perform that assessment and select any legally available relief; a later appeal is subject to the bounded abuse-of-discretion rule.

The Court neither directs a new jury trial now nor reinstates the $450,000 as substantively correct. It does not approve the $100,000 limit, find how many transparencies were unique, or infer acceptance of remittitur. The distinct industry-evidence ruling remains undisturbed. No new liability issue, waiver, damages tariff or further Supreme Court event is prescribed.

### TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc. — 2001-03-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/TrafFix_Devices_v_Marketing_Displays_merits_2001-03-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/TrafFix_Devices_v_Marketing_Displays_merits_2001-03-20.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Useful features remain available to competitors

**Holding and operative rule:** An expired utility patent is strong evidence that the feature it identifies as useful is functional. When the claimed feature is essential to the article's use or purpose or affects its cost or quality, source recognition and alternative workable designs do not make it protectable trade dress; a genuinely distinct ornamental, incidental or arbitrary appearance must be evaluated on its own rather than presumed functional merely because it appears in a patented product.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's opinion, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, directly controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that trade-dress law does not continue exclusive control of useful engineering merely because buyers recognize its source. Inwood supplies the essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality inquiry. Qualitex retains that inquiry while also addressing competitive disadvantage unrelated to reputation. Its competitive-necessity discussion does not require another inquiry after traditional utilitarian functionality is established. Wal-Mart separately requires source significance for product design; recognition cannot replace nonfunctionality.

The patents describe the dual springs' contribution to wind resistance and stability. That operation, supported by the record, establishes the functional character of the feature claimed here. MDI's argument concerns the overall stand, including legs, uprights and spring placement. Considering that combination does not reveal a distinct nonfunctional ornament in this record. Requiring a competitor to conceal, cover or rearrange the working springs would restrict use of the useful configuration itself. The availability of another workable arrangement therefore does not defeat the functionality ground.

The patent creates a substantial evidentiary obstacle, not a conclusive presumption covering every appearance detail. A claimant remains able to identify and support a genuinely separate nonfunctional feature. None has been established in the claim before the Court. The undisputed functional evidence is sufficient without deciding the temporal application of the 1999 burden amendment. Functionality disposes of this trade-dress theory, so secondary meaning supplies no further ground that must be reached.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The decision establishes no automatic constitutional bar to all trade dress associated with expired patents and announces no new aesthetic-functionality test. A distinct unasserted ornamental design lies outside this claim. Those questions are unnecessary or outside the granted scope, respectively; the functional dual-spring claim itself is resolved, not returned for another alternatives inquiry.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc.:** Applied for the traditional essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality rule.
- **Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.:** Applied and clarified: its competitive-disadvantage discussion complements, rather than displaces, the traditional utilitarian bar. No additional competitive-necessity showing is required once that bar is met.
- **Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Samara Brothers, Inc.:** Applied to keep source significance for product design separate from nonfunctionality; its actual distinctiveness rule remains unchanged.
- **Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc.:** Its protection of otherwise qualifying trade dress does not remove the independent functionality requirement; its existing scope remains intact.

**Resulting law:** A utility patent supplies strong, feature-specific evidence of functionality. Proven useful function independently defeats trade-dress protection despite source recognition or available alternative designs. The rule leaves protection of genuinely distinct nonfunctional appearance to its own requirements and leaves the 1999 amendment's temporal reach undecided.

## Current-term public authority: Buford v. United States — 2001-03-20

Source: `terms/OT2000/workspace/neutral-projection.md`. Each matching law and published-position section is copied below.

### Buford v. United States — 2001-03-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Buford_v_United_States_merits_2001-03-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Buford_v_United_States_merits_2001-03-20.md#precedent-treatment).

#### Deference follows the task performed

**Holding and operative rule:** Appellate courts independently review the meaning of the related-case provisions and authoritative commentary, but defer to a district court's supported, record-specific determination of functional consolidation when it depends on the procedural history and practical sentencing context. Concurrent sentences and a common hearing are relevant but not conclusive, and absence of a formal consolidation order is not itself dispositive; under that allocation, the determination here is sustained.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the district court performed a practical sentencing task, rather than defining a general constitutional boundary. Section 3742(e) requires due deference to application of the Guidelines to facts. Koon and Powell supply the necessary separation: legal misinterpretations and unsupported grounds remain correctable, while a supported assessment sensitive to the particular proceedings receives deference. Stinson makes consistent commentary authoritative; it does not require appellate redetermination of every application.

The four robbery convictions were concededly related. The disputed drug conviction shared a sentencing judge, hearing, presentence report and concurrent terms, and counsel agreed to simultaneous sentencing. Those facts favor consolidation and must be considered. Separate filings, prosecutors, pleas, judgments and sentences nevertheless supported the district court's contrary assessment. Familiarity with local sentencing arrangements helps identify what those proceedings actually accomplished. Neither one hearing nor the absence of a formal order supplies an automatic answer.

The increased sentencing range makes correct legal criteria important, but does not make this fact-bound application especially useful as a general appellate rule. Independent legal review, correction of unsupported inferences and the Sentencing Commission's supervisory role preserve uniformity. Ornelas and Fields require independent review of ultimate constitutional search thresholds for their distinct institutional reasons; they do not select one standard for every mixed question. No actual legal misinterpretation requiring another sentencing proceeding is identified here.

**Limits and matters not reached:** The complete substantive definition of functional consolidation and a constitutional jury-allocation claim lie outside the granted review question. The commentary's intervening-arrest exclusion remains distinct from the alternatives of offenses on the same occasion, part of a single common scheme or plan, or consolidated for trial or sentencing. No finding under another alternative is made. This decision creates no general constitutional recidivism exception and changes neither Apprendi's reserved Guidelines questions nor Almendarez-Torres's limited controlling judgment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- **Koon v. United States / Powell v. United States:** Applied to separate independent legal correction from deference to supported sentencing application; neither is made a blanket rule for every Guidelines question.
- **Stinson v. United States:** Applied for the authority of consistent commentary; its superior-law limits remain unchanged.
- **Ornelas v. United States; United States v. Fields / Crawley:** Distinguished by the recurring constitutional thresholds requiring independent review; their review rules remain fully effective.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Its division of appellate responsibility supports attention to the task rather than a universal mixed-question label.
- **Apprendi v. New Jersey; Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Their actual holdings and reservations are left intact; this appellate-review decision supplies no new jury or recidivism rule.

**Resulting law:** The related-case functional-consolidation application receives task-sensitive deference, with legal criteria independently reviewed and record support still required. The decision specifies this review allocation without changing the substantive commentary or constitutional search-review rules. It does not establish that simultaneous or concurrent sentencing always combines, or always fails to combine, prior convictions.
