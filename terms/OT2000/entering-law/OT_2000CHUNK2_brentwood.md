### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground](HOLDINGS.md#a-court-may-choose-a-straightforward-nonmerits-jurisdictional-ground-1).

## Source: state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md — Special federal corporations as governmental actors

### Special federal corporations as governmental actors

**Governing question:** First Amendment responsibility of a corporation specially created and governed by the Federal Government.

**Current rule:** A corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes when the Federal Government creates it by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains power to appoint a majority of its governing directors. A statutory nonagency label cannot defeat that constitutional status, and ordinary managerial discretion does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act. Charter, subsidy, regulation, governmental property, public benefit, a single appointment, temporary investment or present share ownership alone does not satisfy the combined rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental: Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental, 1995-02-21. Justice Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight Justices directly support the same permanent-majority-appointment rule and its application; Justice O'Connor's conditional view of a question she declines to reach is not a join.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-02-21; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule decides constitutional actor status, not the underlying speech claim, forum classification or relief. It supplies no universal agency, immunity, Treasury-liability, tort-liability or charter-jurisdiction classification.

**Holdings navigation:** Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental.

### Private payment choices and governmental attribution

**Governing question:** Whether statutory permission and public review mechanisms make a private compensation carrier’s initial withholding state action.

**Current rule:** Private carriers’ independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because the State permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis of responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or an exclusively governmental function. Screening for proper invocation and assigning a reviewer do not by themselves substantively approve the medical objection or order the initial suspension.

**Authority by component:**

- Choice-specific attribution and limited significance of permission and review: Canonical Decision Record `american_manufacturers_v_sullivan_merits_1999-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier’s payment choice governmental, 1999-03-03; seven direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** The legislature’s own act, a public obligor, an official review decision or a distinct proved attribution basis requires its own inquiry. The ruling does not make all acts in the compensation system private.

**Holdings navigation:** [American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan — Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental](HOLDINGS.md#statutory-permission-does-not-itself-make-the-private-carriers-payment-choice-governmental-1).

#### American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-2000
**Decided:** 1999-03-03.

##### Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental

**Holding:** These private carriers' independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because state law permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis for responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or a function exclusively reserved to government; those predicates are not established for this initial withholding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven direct joins control.

##### General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service

**Holding:** General eligibility for workers' compensation does not by itself establish an unconditional constitutional right to constant payment of every disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. This rule neither extinguishes a protected claim to fair adjudication nor decides against a claimant an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient individual expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight direct joins control. Stevens supports the payment disposition on a different ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991; Rendell-Baker v. Kohn, 457 U.S. 830:** Applied to distinguish regulation or public support from governmental responsibility for the particular private act.
- **Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922:** Applied; a state-created procedure and sufficient governmental participation remain separate requirements.
- **Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614:** Distinguished for actual participation in the governmental jury function; its attribution inquiry remains effective.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Preserved for governmental institutional creation and permanent control and for the proper scope of an included question; it supplies no finding of such carrier status here.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium:** Preserved at its actual component holdings; it creates neither universal immunity for permissive legislation nor automatic attribution of every permitted private choice.
- **Tulsa Professional Collection Services v. Pope, 485 U.S. 478:** Preserved; a non-self-executing procedure does not eliminate the need to identify significant governmental involvement.
- **Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422; Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill, 470 U.S. 532:** Preserved for protected claims and the distinction between substantive entitlement and procedure; neither establishes unconditional payment of every disputed bill.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Its procedural inquiry is not replaced or applied to decide every preserved individual or public-party claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private initial withholding requires its own governmental attribution basis. General benefit eligibility alone supplies no unconditional advance-payment right. Actual governmental conduct, fair adjudication of protected claims, prior service entitlements and properly supported continuation interests remain distinct. The decision establishes no universal immunity or absence of property and designs no additional statewide procedure.

**Operative remedy or transition:**

| Private carriers' initial withholding | Attribution ruling reversed, 7-2 on reaching this ground | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Ginsburg and Stevens would not decide this additional ground; neither holds that the carriers are state actors. |
| Categorical advance-payment/severance requirement | Set aside, 9-0, on divided grounds | All nine | Eight join the limited entitlement rule; Stevens relies on fair governmental process. |
| Unchallenged notice/input and distinct preserved issues | Left undisturbed or returned for ordinary resolution as applicable | The mandate preserves their actual scope | No new merits ruling on matters outside review. |

On remand the supported private-carrier constitutional theory must be rejected absent a distinct established attribution basis. No immediate payment follows solely from general eligibility. The Court orders no wholesale dismissal of public-defendant claims, damages, class certification, new discovery or additional procedural design. Only actually pleaded, preserved and unresolved claims remain; the Court creates none.

#### Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1525.
**Decided:** February 21, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Preserved claims and disavowed legal arguments (The Court may consider this included legal argument despite its earlier disavowal); Federal Courts — Relief after reversal of governmental-actor status (Governmental status requires a remand, not an immediate display order).

##### Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental

**Holding:** When the Federal Government creates a corporation by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains authority to appoint a majority of its governing directors, the corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes notwithstanding a statutory declaration that it is not a governmental agency. Amtrak satisfies those combined conditions; ordinary operating discretion in its corporate officers does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- San Francisco Arts & Athletics, Inc. v. United States Olympic Committee, 483 U.S. 522 (1987): distinguishes federal charter and assistance to a private institution from special creation coupled with permanent governmental majority-appointment authority.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 (1991), and Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992): preserve attention to actual governmental legal machinery in their jury-selection settings; neither makes billboard advertising an exclusively governmental function or supplies a general rule for all publicly benefited corporations.
- Jackson v. Metropolitan Edison Co., 419 U.S. 345 (1974), and Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991 (1982): distinguish attribution of a private entity's particular act from identifying an institution that is itself governmental under the combined rule.
- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., decided November 14, 1994: keeps the distinct inquiry into an entity's state sovereign immunity separate; absence of immunity does not imply constitutional privacy.
- American National Red Cross v. S. G., 505 U.S. 247 (1992): preserves its express charter-jurisdiction rule, which supplies no constitutional actor classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal charter, subsidy, regulation, governmental property, public benefit, a single appointment, temporary investment, or merely the current ownership of shares is not independently sufficient under this rule. Permanent authority to appoint the governing majority, governmental objectives, and special governmental creation must be present together. The Court decides no universal agency status, sovereign immunity, Treasury obligation, federal tort-liability rule, or charter jurisdiction. The 1994 recodification from former 45 U.S.C. §541 to 49 U.S.C. §24301(a) does not itself adjudicate constitutional status. The statutory board includes the Secretary of Transportation; Amtrak's president; three Presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed directors representing the statutory labor, state-executive, and business categories; two Presidential selections from commuter-authority nominees under the statute; and two preferred-shareholder selections. The governmental preferred shareholder supplies those last two selections on this record. Neither the entire board's consideration of this particular refusal nor a new direct federal order to reject it is found.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit is reversed and remanded, 8–1, to adjudicate the unreviewed First Amendment and forum questions with Amtrak treated as governmental and reconsider associated state-law dispositions under their own rules. The display injunction is not automatically reinstated; no final display, damages, contract or TDI termination relief is awarded.
