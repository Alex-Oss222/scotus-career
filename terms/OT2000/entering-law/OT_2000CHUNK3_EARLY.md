#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc., 518 U.S. 470 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-754 and 95-886.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### What federal predicate is required and whether substantial-equivalence clearance supplies design approval

**Holding:** Section 360k(a) requires an applicable federal requirement and a state requirement respecting a human-use device that differs from or adds to it and concerns safety, effectiveness or another matter included in the federal requirement, subject to subsection (b). This lead's substantial-equivalence clearance does not establish substantive federal approval of its design and therefore does not preempt the restored negligent-design or design-based strict-liability claims on that asserted ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The 1982 marketing letter and §807.97 separate clearance from substantive safety approval. Class III status, grandfathering and manufacturing conformance to the original design do not establish PMA or a substantive FDA design choice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No design defect, breach, causation, actual PMA case or hypothetical device-specific standard is decided. Regulatory overlap alone supplies no independent actual conflict.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Design restoration affirmed in No. 95-754, 9–0; state-law elements remain necessary.

##### Whether a genuinely parallel state conduct duty becomes additional because it supplies damages

**Holding:** Section 360k(a) does not expressly preempt an independently available state duty that genuinely requires the same conduct as the applicable federal duty merely because state law adds damages. Requiring negligence or an unreasonable hazard in addition to violation of that same conduct obligation can narrow recovery without making the substantive duty different; a claim requiring different warnings or production conduct is not parallel merely because its pleading invokes federal law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Actual operation controls: equal conduct plus narrower recovery elements differs from liability despite federal compliance or a requirement of additional warning/production conduct. The appellate court reached the reply-stage contention and assumed, without deciding, Florida recognition and adequate pleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No private FDCA enforcement action is created. Florida recognition, pleading, violation, causation and relief remain undecided; independently established actual conflict remains a separate question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical parallel-duty barrier in No. 95-886 is reversed and remanded, 9–0; no exemption must first be sought for an otherwise nonpreempted equal duty.

##### Whether the asserted generic manufacturing and warning predicates preempt the presented duties

**Holding:** The general manufacturing and prescription-information requirements invoked here do not establish a federal counterpart that makes the presented ordinary manufacturing and warning duties different or additional within section 360k(a). The challenged preemption barriers are therefore reversed for negligent manufacture, failure to warn and corresponding strict-liability theories, without exempting all tort law or all claims involving generally applicable regulations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices.

**Material application:** Part 820 and §801.109 impose genuine obligations but their legal force and shared subject do not establish the specific substantive federal choice these duties would alter. Master records and original-design conformance do not substantively approve safety; label submission is not approval of precise contents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General framing does not mean no federal command, and no universal manufacturer- or model-specificity prerequisite applies. Ambiguous duties may be identified in ordinary proceedings; no mandatory preliminary hearing in all products cases.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Manufacturing/warning and corresponding strict-liability barriers reversed, 6–3. O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent as to genuinely divergent duties, while joining the equal-duty remand.

**Independent alternative holding:** Within the statutory comparison, the FDA reasonably interprets section 360k through section 808.1(d) to require specific counterpart regulations or other specific applicable requirements before divergent state requirements are displaced. Giving that formal construction substantial weight independently supports survival of these manufacturing and warning claims, while preserving its general-applicability, equal-duty and divergent substantive-application qualifications.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Breyer’s Part V, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela does not join. The FDA’s formal §808.1 interpretation receives weight within actual delegation and enacted ambiguity, not authority to contradict Congress, universal deference to litigating positions or free-standing preemptive power. Section 808.1(d)(6)(ii) still reaches a generally worded rule whose actual application imposes a divergent specific-device duty.

##### Whether common-law damages obligations can be requirements under §360k(a)

**Holding:** A state obligation enforced through common-law damages can constitute a requirement under section 360k(a); common law has no categorical exemption from the express clause. Inclusion alone does not establish preemption: the applicable federal obligation, actual difference or addition, subject connection and statutory exceptions must still be satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct votes. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg reserve this general question.

**Material application:** Judicial damages duties can change conduct as effectively as enacted commands. Inclusion does not itself preempt any surviving Lohr claim or supply a categorical preemption outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The following operative qualifications govern the statutory comparison and remand; they do not enlarge the separately stated proposition coalitions.

- **Separate state-requirement exemption:** Section 360k(b) permits the Secretary, on a State or political subdivision's application, after notice and opportunity for an oral hearing, to exempt a requirement by regulation on prescribed conditions if it is more stringent than the otherwise applicable federal requirement, or if compelling local conditions require it and compliance would not violate an applicable federal requirement. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second alternative. An exemption does not remove federal obligations. No pertinent exemption is established; an equal, nonpreempted duty does not first require one.
- **Section 808.1's complete scope:** Subsection (b) includes force-of-law court decisions. Subsection (d)(1) preserves general requirements whose purpose reaches other products as well as devices, including general electrical codes and the Uniform Commercial Code's fitness warranty, and unfair-trade rules not limited to devices. Subsection (d)(2) preserves equal or substantially identical requirements. Subsection (d)(6)(ii) preserves general adulteration/misbranding prohibitions but displaces an actual divergent substantive device requirement; interpretation and enforcement, rather than literal wording alone, control that comparison.
- **Other section 808.1 qualifications:** Professional-practice permits, licensing, registration, certification and related approval remain subject to conflicting federal restrictions under sections 520(e) or (g), which prevail. State procurement specifications and Medicaid or similar payment criteria are preserved. General enforcement, inspections, registration and licensing remain subject to conflicting federal records, reporting and good-manufacturing-practice rules under sections 519 and 520(f), which prevail. Administrative delegations and related matters, measures solely raising revenue or charging fees, and the identified atomic-energy and radiation measures remain outside preemption on their stated terms; the last category lasts until applicable specific FDA requirements intervene. Subsection (e) assigns the FDA a determination role through prescribed procedures subject to judicial review, without establishing an exhaustion bar here. Subsections (c) and (f) preserve applicable federal duties whether or not a state requirement is exempt or preempted. No unestablished factual exception is applied.
- **Original manufacturing regime:** Part 820 applies to finished human-use devices and defines a finished device to include an accessory suitable for use whether packaged or labeled. It is not generally directed at manufacturers of components or parts; blood and blood components have Part 606's separate treatment. The everyday description of this lead as a component does not settle that classification. Additional critical-device duties retain their own applicability: the definition addresses an implant or life-supporting device whose failure when properly used can reasonably be expected to cause significant injury, with identification by the Commissioner after advisory consultation. Class III status alone is not a substitute finding. Where simultaneous compliance with applicable regulations is impossible, the specific device regulation supersedes the general regulation. No exemption, variance or violation is found here.
- **Real manufacturing obligations:** The original provisions require appropriate quality assurance, trained personnel, suitable premises and equipment, controlled component acceptance and handling, written manufacturing specifications and processing controls, inspection and failure investigation, labeling integrity, protective packaging, distribution controls and records. Section 820.100 requires manufacturing conformance to the original design or approved changes, with controlled and documented specification and process changes; it does not itself approve the design's safety. Critical-device additions, including component sampling, operation verification, labeling, distribution and history records, depend on their actual applicability. The Court decides no breach of any particular provision.
- **Distinct manufacturing exemption or variance:** Section 360j(f)(2) permits a subject person to petition in the prescribed form with required information. An exemption requires a determination that compliance is unnecessary to assure safety, effectiveness and statutory compliance. A variance requires a determination that the proposed replacement methods, facilities and controls for manufacture, packing, storage and installation are sufficient to assure safety, effectiveness and compliance with the Act; an approval order must impose conditions necessary to assure those ends. Optional advisory referral requires recommendations within sixty days; the Secretary must approve or deny within sixty days after submission or, if referred, after expiration of the committee's sixty-day period, whichever is later. An informal hearing is available after the order. These provisions differ from a State's section 360k(b) exemption, and no granted exception is inferred.
- **Prescription information:** Section 801.109 conditionally exempts a device unsafe without licensed-practitioner supervision from consumer adequate-directions requirements. The device must be in the possession of a person, or that person's agents or employees, regularly and lawfully engaged in its manufacture, transportation, storage or wholesale or retail distribution, or in the possession of a practitioner licensed by law to use or order it; it must be sold only to or on the prescription or other order of such a practitioner for professional use. Subsection (b)'s prescription caution and method-of-use label requirements exclude surgical instruments. Subsection (c) requires dispensing-package information about indications, effects, routes, methods, frequency and duration, relevant hazards, contraindications, side effects and precautions for intended and advertised uses. That information may be omitted only when commonly known to licensed practitioners; on written request stating reasonable grounds, the Commissioner will offer an opinion on proposed omission. No omission qualification is found here.
- **Separate labeling exceptions:** Section 801.109(d) independently requires adequate use information on manufacturer, packer or distributor labeling that furnishes or purports to furnish use information, whether or not within the dispensing package. Its exception covers a reminder piece calling attention only to the device name without indications or other use information. Subsection (e)'s issuance-or-revision-date duty excludes labels and cartons. These exceptions do not merge with subsection (c)'s permissive omission or subsection (b)'s surgical-instrument exception. Section 807.87(e)'s submission of proposed labels, labeling and advertisements is not substantive approval of their contents.
- **Clearance, classification and approval:** Section 360(k)'s report ordinarily precedes intended interstate commercial introduction by at least ninety days. Classification, substantial equivalence and premarket approval remain distinct. Section 360e(a) requires approval for a class III device subject to a subsection (b) regulation or classified under section 360c(f), unless exempt under section 360j(g). Section 360e(b) supplies the rulemaking route for pre-1976 devices and their substantially equivalent types; no such approval is found for this lead. PMA's distinct evaluation and application requirements do not become clearance requirements by inference. The Court decides neither a different approval pathway nor a particular investigational exemption.
- **Temporal limits:** The 1994 Code and April 1, 1996 CFR are text sources, not findings that every amendment applied to the 1982 clearance, 1987 implantation or 1990 injury. The 1990 codification requires the same intended use and a Secretary's order finding either the same technological characteristics or, for different characteristics, submitted information—including clinical data if the Secretary deems it necessary—demonstrating that the device is as safe and effective as a legally marketed device and raises no different questions of safety and efficacy than the predicate. Different technological characteristics means a significant change in materials, design, energy source or other device features. A predicate removed from the market at the Secretary's initiative or judicially determined adulterated or misbranded cannot establish substantial equivalence. That later text is not backdated; the lower court assumed that it described earlier practice. Later submission amendments and design-validation language create no earlier approval or breach. The original Part 820 regime governs the comparison, and no later quality-system revision enters the decision. An actual conduct-date duty remains to be identified for liability.
- **Savings and federal enforcement:** Section 360h(d) preserves liability despite compliance with an order under that section; it is not an unrestricted saving clause for every device action. When damages for economic loss enforce such liability, the value of a remedy supplied under the order must be taken into account. No such order or offset is adjudicated. Section 337 reserves direct enforcement to the United States except for its State food-enforcement provision. A State may bring proceedings in its own name and jurisdiction to civilly enforce, or restrain violations of, sections 341 and 343(b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (k), (q) and (r), only for food located in the State. Proceedings may not begin until thirty days after notice to the Secretary, or until ninety days after notice if the Secretary begins an informal or formal enforcement action concerning the food within the first thirty days. Proceedings are barred while the Secretary diligently prosecutes a court proceeding concerning the food or after settlement of that proceeding or the related informal or formal enforcement action; the State may intervene as of right in the described court proceeding. This exception creates no private device action. No unpresented standalone enforcement defense or later implied-preemption rule is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ordinary proceedings retain the separate design, manufacture and warning strict-liability predicates and derivative consortium claim. The unappealed warranty dismissal remains. No compliance finding, liability, damages, mandatory pleading amendment, trial or agency referral.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992:** Its actual covered common-law warning proposition supports identifying the duty imposed by liability. Its distinct statutory clauses and category-specific surviving grounds remain intact; no whole amended-Act opinion or blanket tort exemption is imported. Part IV applies the inclusion principle to section 360k through its own text.
- **CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood, April 21, 1993:** Supplies the requirement of the actual applicable federal predicate and subject comparison. Its railroad-specific substantial-subsumption language, warning predicates, local-hazard qualification and speed holding are not substituted for the Medical Device Amendments.
- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick, April 18, 1995:** Applies the distinction between an operative federal requirement and regulatory absence or overlap, and preserves independently established actual-conflict analysis. Its reserved common-law question under another statute and its motor-vehicle saving clause do not decide this clause.
- **New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co., April 26, 1995:** Supports examining the connection required by the particular statute instead of unlimited displacement from indirect effects. Its ERISA holding becomes no general exemption for state health or tort law.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T, June 17, 1994:** Requires independent adherence to statutory limits before agency deference. The FDA may not dispense with Congress's comparison or create unlimited preemption authority.
- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837; Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Preserve reasonable agency interpretation within enacted ambiguity and actual delegated responsibility. Part V applies that bounded principle to the formal FDA construction without adopting every agency view or enlarging the statutory exemption power.
- **Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., June 3, 1996:** Preserves statutory construction before reasonable delegated interpretation, and distinguishes substantive agency responsibility from free-standing power to preempt. Its specific banking permission and qualified use of interpretation for antecedent transactions do not supply substantive approval or retroactive duties for this device.

#### Geier v. American Honda Motor Co., 529 U.S. 861 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1811.
**Decided:** May 22, 2000.

##### I. The express clause does not independently extinguish this saved common-law claim

**Holding:** Former §1392(d), read with §1397(k), does not expressly displace this common-law no-airbag claim merely because the car complied with FMVSS 208. The common-law saving rule defeats categorical compliance immunity without declaring that damages duties can never be state safety standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1392(d) permits enforcement of identical standards and higher performance requirements for vehicles or equipment procured for government use. Section 1397(k) separately saves common-law liability despite compliance; the procurement permission is not transplanted into that provision.

##### II. Preserved common-law claims remain subject to an independently established actual conflict

**Holding:** The presence of the express clause and compliance saving provision does not foreclose an independently demonstrated conflict between a state common-law duty and an operative federal command or supported regulatory objective. Federal compliance, overlapping regulation, agency preference or regulatory silence alone does not establish that conflict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

##### III. The general no-airbag duty defeats this deliberately chosen transition

**Holding:** The presented general duty to install a driver's airbag is preempted because it would displace FMVSS 208's deliberately retained restraint alternatives and phased transition, which were operative means of promoting safety through development and public acceptance. This conflict sustains dismissal only of that general theory; compliance does not immunize every federally permitted design, and nonconflicting federal-minimum, noncompliance, defective-installation, performance, risk-specific and genuinely model-specific duties remain available when otherwise supported.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins. The four partial joiners dissent from this holding and the judgment.

**Material application:** The operative alternatives, phase-in percentages and differentiated incentives supported the chosen development-and-acceptance transition. The conflict requires no fixed technology mix, minimum count of airbag-free vehicles, or command that this Accord lack an airbag. Later safety evidence cannot change the governing historical regulation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general motor-vehicle field immunity, fixed technology allocation, binding deference to litigation advocacy, universal all-tort exemption or impossibility-only test is adopted. No Honda-specific production, credit, attribution, due-care or testing fact, and no completed belt-law trigger, is found. The Court neither invalidates the standard nor decides whether an independently presented different duty would conflict.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal only of the general no-airbag theory. No factual remand, agency proceeding, new claim or determination of ordinary tort elements follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick:** Applies its independent actual-conflict inquiry and distinguishes its regulatory absence from this affirmative, operative transition. Its rejection of compliance immunity and unsupported federal purposes remains intact.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.:** Applies clause- and duty-specific construction. Its distinct cigarette provisions supply neither a universal tort exemption nor a rule excluding every independent conflict inquiry.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Preserves the rejection of automatic displacement from regulatory overlap. No separate physical-impossibility formulation becomes the Court's exclusive rule.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Applies the need to compare actual substantive duties and keep interpretive weight within statutory authority. Damages duties can be requirements without every such duty being preempted.
- **Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Shanklin:** Preserves its specific device-selection holding and its distinction from a universal minimum-standard ceiling; this case rests on its own regulatory method.
- **United States v. Locke / Intertanko v. Locke:** Preserves its subject-specific federal allocation and full enacted saving language. Its national vessel field does not establish a motor-vehicle field immunity.
- **National Mining Association v. Department of the Interior; Public Lands Council v. Babbitt:** Preserve actual delegated authority, distinct statutory conditions and temporal limits. Neither authorizes later advocacy to manufacture an earlier regulatory objective or to disregard common-law saving language.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress need not prove a violation in every jurisdiction, make formal findings, use a prescribed quantity or form of evidence, require individualized proof for every prohibited act, or enact geographic limits or sunsets. Such design features may inform substantive fit but are not universal conditions. Independent Fifteenth Amendment power is not further resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.

#### United States v. Locke / International Association of Independent Tanker Owners v. Locke, 529 U.S. 89

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 98-1701 and 98-1706, consolidated for decision.

**Decided:** March 6, 2000.

##### National vessel standards and local waterway measures require different inquiries

**Holding:** PWSA Title II occupies the assigned national subjects of general vessel design, construction, alteration, repair, maintenance, equipment, operation, manning and personnel qualifications; a State cannot add standards within that field. Genuinely local port and waterway measures outside that assignment require a separate Title I inquiry into operative federal law, actual conflict or an affirmative federal determination that additional regulation is inappropriate; overlap, federal silence and agency preference alone do not displace them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine directly adopt these holdings at their stated scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Classification turns on actual function, not a spill-prevention label. Federal rules may differ for domestic trade and exceed international standards; cargo types and grades and consultation with governmental and knowledgeable parties remain required. Those federal powers confer no independent state authority within the assigned field.

##### The Oil Pollution Act savings do not repeal the separate vessel regime

**Holding:** Section 2718 preserves its express additional liabilities, requirements, removal measures, funds and penalties against the specified preemptive effects of the Oil Pollution Act, the 1851 Act and 26 U.S.C. §9509, according to each subsection's own terms. It does not repeal the independent PWSA occupation of general vessel subjects or exempt every state regulation bearing an oil-pollution purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine directly adopt these holdings at their stated scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2718(a) preserves state/local oil-discharge and removal liabilities or requirements and Solid Waste Disposal Act/state-law obligations against the Oil Pollution Act and 1851 Act. Paragraph (b) protects state oil-pollution funds and contributions against the Oil Pollution Act and section 9509. Paragraph (c) protects additional governmental liabilities, requirements and discharge-or-threat penalties against all three named enactments. Paragraph (d) creates no individual federal-employee cause of action for official conduct. The savings are not limited to money liability.

##### Training, language and the general watch rules regulate assigned vessel subjects

**Holding:** Washington's comprehensive additional crew-training and drill requirement, WAC 317-21-230; general master/deck-officer language and communication requirements, §317-21-250; and general minimum-watch and fleet bridge-management component of §317-21-200 are displaced as national training, qualification, manning and operation standards. The separate restricted-visibility rule and other unresolved provisions must receive their own classification and any necessary conflict analysis on remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine directly adopt these holdings at their stated scope.

**Holding:** WAC 317-21-130's worldwide operating-history reporting requirement for operators for the five years before filing a prevention plan and thereafter imposes an additional general vessel operating-history condition within the national maritime arrangement and is displaced. The conclusion preserves the uniform State reporting, compilation and submission role expressly authorized by §6102 and does not invalidate every state casualty report or genuinely local incident measure.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

##### Limits and questions not reached

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither constitutional holding nor the judgment preapproves every compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. #### Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-102.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### The enacted Rules displace mandatory general acceptance

**Holding:** Frye’s mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern admission of scientific expert evidence in federal trials under the Federal Rules of Evidence. Acceptance remains potentially relevant under Rule 702, but its absence does not alone require exclusion and its presence does not dispense with the Rules’ other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Frye v. United States, 293 F.1013: displaced as a mandatory prerequisite in federal trials; not a Supreme Court Rule 702 holding being overruled.
- Beech Aircraft v. Rainey, 488 U.S. 153: statutory interpretation of enacted Rules applied.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45: compatible common-law guidance retained, incompatible prerequisite rejected.
- Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S. 171: Rules supersede incompatible common law.

##### Reliable scientific foundation and fit require flexible preliminary screening

**Holding:** Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance the preliminary foundation for scientifically reliable knowledge that fits the disputed factual question and will assist the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application using relevant nonexclusive considerations—testability and testing, peer review/publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance—without making any one consideration an invariant prerequisite; admissibility does not determine ultimate causation or sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The original Rules govern. No later Rule 702 text, universal technical-specialty rule, appellate-review standard or substantive causation finding is adopted. Cross-examination supplements screening for admissible evidence; Rules 403, 703, 706 and ordinary sufficiency rules remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Frye-based summary judgment and remand for evaluation of material proffers and reconsideration of summary judgment. Further fair foundation submissions may be received; no direction to admit every expert.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S. 171, 175–176: preponderance for preliminary admissibility applied, not a substantive causation burden.
- Rock v. Arkansas, 483 U.S. 44: adversarial testing remains appropriate for admissible evidence; no excuse for unsupported foundation.
- United States v. Downing, 753 F.2d 1224: helpful fit and flexible reliability discussion informs interpretation, not a binding Supreme Court multifactor checklist.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, No. 97-1709, March 23, 1999: extends original-Rule-702 reliability and fit screening to technical and other specialized knowledge, with case-specific flexible criteria and discretion over the reasonable screening method.

#### General Electric Co. v. Joiner

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-188.

**Decided:** 1997-12-15.

##### Admission and exclusion receive the same abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding:** Appellate courts review a trial court's admission or exclusion of scientific expert testimony for abuse of discretion. Legal error remains independently correctable, historical findings receive their proper protection, and permissible case-specific evidentiary judgments receive deference. The importance of an exclusion to summary judgment supplies no different or asymmetric standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, No. 97-1709, March 23, 1999: applies abuse-of-discretion review to both the reasonable reliability-screening method and the final technical-expert evidentiary decision.

##### Reliability includes the inferential connection to the offered proposition

**Holding:** Original Rules 104(a) and 702 permit the judge to examine whether the expert's reasoning reliably connects the scientific material to the conclusion actually offered. Credentials, accepted terminology, or an assertion of a weight-of-evidence method cannot substitute for an adequate connection; screening that connection does not itself decide ultimate causation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, unanimous at the stated legal level.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under the original Rules 104(a) and 702, the scientific foundation and fit must be established by a preponderance. Neither a study identical to the claimant’s circumstances nor independent proof from each study is required; animal work, nonsignificant associations, competing causes and individually inconclusive materials may support a reliable cumulative inference.

##### The PCB-only exclusion was permissible on this proffer

**Holding:** The described differences between the experimental materials and Joiner's exposure and disease, considered with the identified limitations of the human studies, permitted the District Court to find an insufficiently supported PCB-only inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela joins reversal without this application; Stevens would vacate for appellate reconsideration.

**Material application:** The experimental dose, subjects and tumor differences and limitations of the human studies permitted exclusion of this PCB-only promotion inference. Admission was not held necessarily forbidden; no finding that PCBs cannot promote lung cancer follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 8–1. The unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling survives, and causation admissibility on that assumed exposure remains open. The full defense summary judgment is not reinstated; no mandatory expert appointment or new hearing follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Daubert's flexible foundation, valid-application and preponderance rules are applied and supplemented by today's appellate-review rule; its distinction between admissibility and ultimate causation remains. Koon, Pierce and Cooter & Gell inform the allocation of legal correction and discretionary application. Ornelas is distinguished by its particular ultimate constitutional inquiry, not narrowed into a universal rule about mixed questions. Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite remains displaced. Rules 403, 703 and 706 and Rule 56 keep their separate functions. No precedent is overruled.

#### Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1709.
**Decided:** 1999-03-23.

##### Rule 702 requires reliable specialized knowledge, whatever the field

**Holding:** Original Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized expert testimony. Experience may supply relevant knowledge, but the expert's professional label does not excuse a reliable connection between the method used and the proposition offered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Daubert's reliability-and-fit duty is extended to the other categories of knowledge expressly covered by original Rule 702; its flexible character remains. Joiner's inferential-connection rule applies to this technical proffer, without converting admissibility into a decision on the underlying claim.

##### The reliability inquiry must fit the proffer and receives abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding:** A trial judge may use Daubert's nonexclusive reliability considerations when they help evaluate the particular expertise, method and application, but need not demand every consideration in every field. Appellate abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable choice of how to assess reliability and the ultimate admission or exclusion, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part II, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Daubert's considerations remain useful but nonmandatory tools. Joiner's uniform review rule is applied to technical evidence and to reasonable selection of the reliability inquiry; its protection against unsupported analytical gaps remains intact.

##### The District Court was entitled to reject the unsupported defect inference

**Holding:** Rule 702 permitted the District Court to reject Carlson's defect inference because he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause. This permissible-exclusion proposition does not itself settle the distinct instruction governing appellate review and summary judgment on remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (eight Justices). Stevens reserves application; this coalition does not establish Part III-B's mandate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Joiner's permission to reject an unsupported inference is applied to this technical proffer. Daubert's flexible foundation-and-fit requirement remains distinct from a finding of ultimate causation.

##### The remand must conform to the exclusion and resulting summary judgment

**Holding:** On the reconsidered proffer, the case is remanded for a disposition sustaining the exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment, because the record supplies no remaining evidence of defect. The mandate does not require a new hearing, appointed expert or another appellate determination whether to sustain this exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (seven Justices). Stone-Zsela and Stevens do not join the conforming mandate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Joiner's abuse-of-discretion allocation is applied to the reconsidered ruling and its supported procedural consequence. Daubert's adaptable inquiry is followed as reflected in reconsideration; neither decision requires rejection of every experience-based method.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Original Rule 702 governs. The Court adopts no later Rule amendment, universal technical checklist or substantive finding that misuse rather than defect caused the accident. The ruling establishes neither automatic admissibility of experiential evidence nor categorical unreliability of visual inspection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Original Rule 702 governs; no later amendment, universal technical checklist, finding that misuse caused the accident or categorical rejection of visual inspection is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The eight-Justice reversal and seven-Justice operative mandate require a disposition sustaining exclusion and the resulting defense summary judgment on this reconsidered proffer, not a new hearing, appointed expert or another appellate determination whether to sustain exclusion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Daubert supplies flexible foundation and fit and now reaches all knowledge covered by original Rule 702, while its nonexclusive considerations remain adaptable. Joiner supplies uniform abuse-of-discretion review and permission to examine an unsupported analytical connection; both apply to technical expertise. Neither decision becomes a mandatory-factor checklist or a merits causation rule. No precedent is overruled.

#### United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** February 12, 1996.

##### The accepted negative-record certifications require no separate unavailability showing

**Holding:** On the accepted Rule 803(10) foundation and Rule 902(1) authentication, the described certificates reporting a diligent search and the absence of a regularly maintained public record may be admitted without first producing the custodian or proving unavailability. This application of the received confrontation framework rests on the rooted exception and the certificates' limited search-and-nonentry function; it does not validate every formal affidavit or resolve a disputed search, wording or sufficiency question.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight rationale joins. Scalia and Thomas retain their White reservation concerning possible reconsideration of formalized testimonial materials. Stone-Zsela joins the admission judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The concession concerns the rooted exception and authentication, not registry infallibility, the constitutional issue or every offense element. Authentication alone is not constitutional reliability. The reported 'application by Hale' wording establishes neither a search of all relevant routes nor a search confined to personally filed applications; no defect is inferred. Competent rebuttal remains available, and no sufficiency or harmless-error determination is made. Scalia and Thomas adopt no new categorical testimonial test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Roberts and Wright distinguish rooted exceptions from residual reliability inquiries; Inadi rejects a universal production requirement; White requires attention to the exception and function without itself deciding these certificates. Coy and Craig's witness-presentation safeguards remain. The certificates' limited search-and-nonentry function does not reconstruct possession, classify arms or decide an element for the jury.

##### The reported Senate offer does not establish ordinary admission error

**Holding:** The exclusion of the reported 1979 Senate excerpts is sustained because the presented challenge identifies no passage, relevant nonhearsay purpose, applicable exception, foundation or demonstrated bearing on the searched registry that establishes an abuse of discretion. The ruling creates no categorical age bar and does not foreclose a properly founded challenge to registry reliability.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part VI-A, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight rationale joins. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bishop/Stokes distinguishes absent detail in an appellate report from an established failure to conduct evidentiary review. The ordinary-admission ground is independent of the distinct Rule 201 ground; no defense-presentation violation or harmlessness is found.

##### Official publication does not make disputed assertions judicially noticeable

**Holding:** Rule 201 does not permit judicial notice of the truth of reasonably disputed assertions merely because they appear in a congressional hearing transcript. Refusal to notice the Senate excerpts' disputed claims about registry reliability is therefore sustained; notice that the hearing occurred remains distinct from accepting its assertions as true or as establishing the registry's condition in 1991.

##### An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison

**Holding:** When an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission establishes the complete prior-conviction status element, that element is the Government's sole legitimate purpose for the evidence, and the name or nature of the conviction presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, Rule 403 requires exclusion of that name or nature. ## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. ## Standards and Tests selection: Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

### Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

**Governing question:** Reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702.

**Current rule:** Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern scientific expert evidence under the federal Rules. Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance a preliminary foundation of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the disputed factual question and assisting the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application flexibly. Relevant nonexclusive considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance; none is an invariant prerequisite. Acceptance alone does not establish admissibility, and its absence alone does not require exclusion. Admissibility does not determine causation or sufficiency. The judge may examine the reliable inferential connection from the scientific materials to the offered conclusion; credentials or the label weight of evidence cannot replace that connection. No identical-study condition, automatic exclusion of animal or nonsignificant studies, or requirement that each study independently prove the conclusion follows; supported cumulative inference remains possible. Admission and exclusion both receive abuse-of-discretion appellate review, with independent correction of legal error and proper protection for historical findings. An exclusion’s decisive effect on summary judgment does not alter that standard. No later Rule text is adopted. The reliability-and-fit duty applies to scientific, technical and other specialized knowledge under original Rule 702. Experience may supply knowledge, but a professional label does not replace a reliable connection between the method, its actual application and the proposition offered. Daubert’s nonexclusive considerations apply when useful to the particular expertise; a judge need not demand laboratory testing, publication or every consideration in every field. Abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable selection of the screening method and the ultimate evidentiary ruling, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prior-conviction status admissions in Rule 403 balancing

### Prior-conviction status admissions in Rule 403 balancing

**Governing question:** Whether the name or nature of a prior conviction should be admitted when an adequate status admission is available.

**Current rule:** Rule 403 requires consideration of an actually available, unconditional admission that legally establishes the complete prior-conviction status element. If status is the evidence’s only legitimate purpose and the conviction’s name or nature presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, exclude that name or nature. General prosecutorial preference for its own proof cannot replace this comparison. The conviction remains relevant and the status element must still be proved and submitted under adequate instructions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable. Alleged customer loss from a State competitor’s misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising. The statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which the asserted §5 remedy is fitted. Established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property. This limit concerns the missing constitutional connection, not a requirement that every prohibited act itself violate the Constitution. Patents are protected property, and Congress may prevent their unconstitutional deprivation through a congruent and proportional remedy subjecting States to private infringement actions: exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative State remedies support the judgment that immunity could leave unconstitutional deprivations without adequate redress. Ordinary infringement without a constitutional violation does not defeat that remedy, and its validity does not require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation; national uniformity and equal competition alone would not supply Section 5 power.

**Authority by component:**
- Enforcement boundary and method: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The asserted bird-habitat criterion does not supply section 404 authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 404 of the Clean Water Act does not authorize federal jurisdiction over the isolated, nonnavigable intrastate ponds presented solely because migratory birds use them. The Corps must identify authority within the enacted water program; an asserted interstate environmental benefit alone does not supply the delegated jurisdiction, and the challenged jurisdiction and permit-denial ground must be set aside to the extent they depend exclusively on that criterion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Five Justices directly adopt this statutory ground, its limits and reversal with remand. The Court does not reach the contingent Commerce Clause question.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 404(a) authorizes permits for discharge of dredged or fill material into navigable waters. Section 502(7) defines those waters to include waters of the United States and the territorial seas. That expansion reaches beyond classical navigation without making the enacted water limitation meaningless. Riverside Bayview sustained adjacent wetlands because of their statutory and ecological relationship to covered waters and expressly left nonadjacent waters open. These isolated ponds are not wetlands under the Corps' vegetation definition and are not alleged to adjoin navigable waters. Bird use does not itself supply that missing statutory relationship.

Section 404(g)(1) allows state administration outside the waters reserved to federal administration, confirming coverage beyond traditional navigation without defining every remaining pond as covered. Section 101(a)'s biological-integrity and wildlife objectives operate consistently with the Act's provisions and alongside section 101(b)'s protection of state responsibilities. Neither purpose alone settles the disputed jurisdiction. MCI and FDA v. Brown & Williamson require authority in the connected statutory scheme before deference; Sweet Home construed a different enacted habitat-injury definition and supplies no substitute delegation here.

The Corps' change of position is not automatically disqualifying, and the preamble's form is not an independent reason to invalidate it. No universal rule governing Chevron, Skidmore or interpretation of an agency's own regulation is necessary: the asserted reach exceeds the statute on its terms. Christensen's limited advisory-letter holding is not enlarged. Because the statutory answer disposes of this assertion, Congress's possible broader power is not decided. The Court neither imposes present navigability as a universal prerequisite nor determines all isolated waters or materially different supported statutory connections.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes: preserves its adjacent-wetland construction and ecological reasoning, distinguishing the expressly reserved isolated-water application rather than treating adjacency as a universal statutory test.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: applies the requirement that an agency act within the actual statutory grant; an administrative criterion cannot supply omitted authority.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.: applies analysis of the connected statutory scheme before deference; the decision supplies no general rule against consequential agency regulation.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon: distinguishes its habitat-injury construction under a different statutory definition and preserves its actual-injury and causation limits.
- Christensen v. Harris County: leaves intact its specific advisory-letter and own-regulation holdings; it does not establish a categorical rule against every preamble or informal interpretation.
- United States v. Lopez, National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, and United States v. Morrison: leave their actual Commerce rules unchanged because the statutory ground makes the contingent constitutional question unnecessary.

### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Souter dissents, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. They read the defined waters term, the aquatic-protection objective and section 404(g)(1)'s state-administration structure to authorize this supported habitat-water application. Riverside Bayview leaves isolated waters open; its adjacency facts do not establish an exhaustive limit. Sweet Home and PUD No. 1 support applying broad protective language according to its actual conditions. The agency's interpretation reinforces their textual conclusion without turning every preamble into a source of binding authority or disregarding section 101(b)'s state role.

They would also sustain this application under Home Builders' complete independent externality route. The regulated subject is discharge of dredged or fill material into the claimed waters. Filling removes aquatic habitat used by a shared mobile population; loss in one State can diminish the resource available elsewhere, while another State cannot restore that out-of-state habitat through ordinary territorial authority. The regional production and conversion evidence supports substantial class-wide importance, and the agency's aquatic-use and interstate-bird findings, together with the breeding-colony material, connect the site functionally to that mechanism. Direct permit review of habitat filling reasonably fits the resulting spillover. These are supported inferences and bounded predictive judgments, not new measurements of population loss.

Their strongest contrary concern is that the regional report does not describe every pond or quantify this fill's effect. They find the combined functional evidence sufficient without requiring a separate substantial effect for each discharge, a site percentage, a specified scientific method or proof of failed interstate agreements. The estimate of up to 50 percent of United States migratory-waterfowl production is not a percentage attributable to this site or to Illinois. Neither bird presence nor conservation's national importance alone is sufficient. They identify no independent necessary-regime predicate, and Holland's treaty holding is not a Commerce holding. Their preserved view that actual habitat-altering construction may itself be economic conduct does not replace their full join to this externality ground. They would affirm jurisdiction without reopening the abandoned permit-merits challenge or awarding a permit.
