### Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices — 2000-12-11

Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, sustains Maine's $250 per-election legislative contribution ceiling, 6-3. The State retains the burden to demonstrate that a direct candidate-contribution ceiling is closely drawn to an objectively supported, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest while preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy in the actual campaign setting. Russell and Shrink Missouri supply that standard, not a fixed dollar floor, general $250 safe harbor or requirement that challengers prove campaigning impossible. Maine's evidence of contributor pressure for official accommodation, campaign scale, actual fundraising, per-election operation and usable private channels meets its burden on the combined record. Completed bribery and statistical proof are unnecessary. Static receipt projections establish a serious risk, not future causal loss; special-election experience is corroborative rather than conclusive, and donor percentages are not dollar-loss measures. Referendum support cannot settle constitutional rights, and public financing cannot cure an independently invalid private restriction.

Part II has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Both distinct triggers survive 5-4: responsive grants measured by private candidate financing, and grants measured by qualifying independent advocacy. A capped, support-qualified mechanism may respond to either where its demonstrated programmatic connection and actual operation establish neither an independent legal restraint nor substantial practical suppression of opposing lawful activity. The private speaker retains its funds, message, lawful fundraising and spending, without endorsement, attribution or an expenditure ceiling. The qualified recipient accepts restrictions in an actual contest; supplements are finite and adjusted for specified favorable independent spending. The record establishes competitive effects rather than substantial suppression. This is neither unlimited permission to offset speech nor a categorical claim that subsidies cannot burden it. The State cannot avoid examining the consequence by calling enrollment voluntary.

Buckley preserves the contribution/expenditure distinction and voluntary public funding with participant conditions; independent expenditures do not become coordinated contributions because a grant formula measures them. Reform Party rejects an automatic entitlement to parity or insulation from an opponent's public resources while preserving actual suppression review. Day's invalidating judgment and actual vote-supported propositions remain binding for its materially different mechanism. Neither its four-Justice expressive-burden rationale nor Stone-Zsela's separate rationale becomes controlling by aggregation. Daggett's five direct Part II joins require no narrowest-rationale inference.

Part III has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. It rejects an additional whole-system coercion claim because the combined program and sustained ceiling leave a meaningful private campaigning alternative on this record. Lawful components can combine into an unconstitutional burden; nominal voluntariness, attractive incentives or a better-funded opponent do not alone resolve that inquiry. Actual contribution capacity, the grant cap, participant duties and conditional shortage rule support the result. No fixed funding percentage, mandatory study or proof of a disappeared candidate is required. O'Connor's Part III join preserves her matching objection; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not reach this additional theory after their proposed targeted invalidations and supply no sustaining votes. All three rules preserve review of materially different suppressive applications. No component is invalidated and no severability remedy becomes operative.

**Operative statutory qualifications.** The legislative ceiling is per election; the individual candidate's own and spouse's contributions retain §1015(1)'s exception. That exception does not remove separate restrictions accepted by a certified public participant. A legislative participant must file the declaration, obtain ballot qualification and compliant seed funding, and collect qualifying $5 contributions from at least 150 registered district voters for a Senate race or 50 for a House race. Contributions go to the fund by check or money order during the qualifying period with the candidate's knowledge and approval, an identifying receipt, and no exchanged value. Seed money is limited to $100 per individual, including candidate or family funds, and aggregate legislative amounts of $1,500 for Senate or $500 for House, subject to Commission rule authority. Unspent seed money transfers to the fund at certification. Later private contributions require specific Commission authorization.

Contested-election initial grants use the corresponding office's average expenditures in contested races in the prior two applicable elections, with the initial 2000 amount reduced by 25 percent; inadequate data permits use of the most recent applicable elections. An uncontested primary uses the corresponding uncontested average or the contested average if lower. No grant issues for an uncontested general election.

The matching measure is the greater of expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, alone or with qualifying independent expenditures reported under §1019. Once that measure exceeds the applicable initial distribution, the Commission pays the opposing certified candidate the reported excess. Supplements stop at twice the relevant contested-primary or contested-general initial distribution, making the ordinary combined maximum three times that distribution. The regulatory deduction reduces matching payments for independent express advocacy favoring the participant or opposing the private opponent after that opponent exceeds the initial distribution; it never reduces the initial grant. No unpresented multiple-opponent formula is decided.

Section 1125(13) forbids spending beyond available fund revenue. If revenue cannot meet the initial or matching distributions, the Commission may authorize certified candidates to accept and spend private contributions up to the applicable funding amounts, reduced by seed money and subject to the legislative $250 per-donor-per-election ceiling and Commission rules. This is conditional permission, not a mandatory release or unlimited financing right. No actual shortage is found.

**Scope.** These qualifications describe the mechanism adjudicated; unchallenged administrative details receive no independent constitutional holding. The separate §1019 disclosure challenge, governor ceiling, unsupported party-specific theory, and unpursued primary-allocation and §1017 theories are outside this grant. Statutory participant terminology establishes no government command to stigmatize nonparticipants.

Souter's multipart opinion produces a 5-2-2 judgment: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer choose corrective vacatur and immediate remand; Scalia and Thomas choose independent Article II reversal with only conditional equality correction; Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm. These positions are not seven unconditional votes for one remand.

Part I has eight reasoned joins, all Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees only in taking review. Section 1257(a) permits review within Cox's fourth category where the highest state court conclusively authorizes challenged relief despite a substantial federal objection that would foreclose it, later nonfederal success could eliminate review, and postponement could seriously impair the asserted federal policy. Goodyear distinguishes a substantial complete-bar claim from its ultimate success. The preserved objections to the operative inclusion and examination decree satisfy those predicates; urgency, consent, absent rehearing and unfinished ballot disputes create no general finality exception. The December 6 presentation of particular Article II objections remains distinct from the December 8 refusal of leave to make a blanket claim that §102.168 never applies to presidential elections. Neither hypothetical implementation nor that blanket claim is adjudicated.

Part II has seven direct joins: Souter, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. A statewide judicial vote determination combining recovered votes and further examination may not use materially arbitrary operative criteria for legally comparable ballots. Existing legal-vote requirements need sufficiently definite, consistent and reviewable application. Relevant physical differences and immaterial administrative variation remain permissible; no national physical-mark code results. Spencer's conflicting Miami-Dade thresholds and Burton's successive Palm Beach approaches, without established consistent reconsideration of every affected earlier determination, support the identified criteria conflict. A general intent command and honest officials do not cure it. The rule requires neither bad faith nor proof that the winner changes and rests neither on complaint-only Broward allegations, proved duplicate votes, a matched-ballot study nor an assumption that every overvote contains a recoverable vote. Harper and Reynolds supply equal participation and weight; Burdick's neutral-administration rule and Shaw's rejection of territorial design alone remain effective. The equality rule applies beyond this election.

Part III has six reasoned joins: Souter, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela adds only a vote rejecting independent Article II reversal. Article II preserves legislative choice of elector-appointment rules while permitting ordinary judicial interpretation and enforcement of the enacted code. The contest, rejected-legal-vote, investigative, corrective and existing intent provisions support these directions without proved material legislative displacement. They permit examination before proof of the winner and do not confine a contest judge to appellate review of board discretion. Palm Beach requires actual statutory authority, without conclusive deference to a state label or a presumption that all interpretation changes the scheme. The December 11 statutory clarification supports the construction; general remedial power still cannot erase substantive voting rules, remaining certification functions or institutional allocation. No undervote automatically becomes legal, no new mark requirement or unlimited late amendment is authorized, and the exact state timing consequence remains distinct. Scalia and Thomas dissent on separate institutional-allocation and substantive legal-vote grounds.

Part IV has eight reasoned joins, all Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting separate due-process reversal. Supported official inducement or frustration of electoral choice requires examination of actual directions, dissemination, reliance and voting circumstances. No verified pre-election assurance, dissemination or detrimental reliance establishes the separate deprivation here. Missing proof is not litigant default, and the equality holding does not depend on inducement. Deliberately misleading directions, pervasive frustration and supported reliance claims remain distinct.

All nine join Part V-A: then-operative 3 U.S.C. §5 gives conclusive federal-count treatment to a final elector-appointment determination under state procedures enacted before election day and made pursuant to that law at least six days before the electors meet. No benefit qualification or forfeiture, comprehensive competing-submissions rule, actual feasibility or impossibility is inferred from dates alone.

### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The asserted bird-habitat criterion does not supply section 404 authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 404 of the Clean Water Act does not authorize federal jurisdiction over the isolated, nonnavigable intrastate ponds presented solely because migratory birds use them. The Corps must identify authority within the enacted water program; an asserted interstate environmental benefit alone does not supply the delegated jurisdiction, and the challenged jurisdiction and permit-denial ground must be set aside to the extent they depend exclusively on that criterion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Five Justices directly adopt this statutory ground, its limits and reversal with remand. The Court does not reach the contingent Commerce Clause question.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 404(a) authorizes permits for discharge of dredged or fill material into navigable waters. Section 502(7) defines those waters to include waters of the United States and the territorial seas. That expansion reaches beyond classical navigation without making the enacted water limitation meaningless. Riverside Bayview sustained adjacent wetlands because of their statutory and ecological relationship to covered waters and expressly left nonadjacent waters open. These isolated ponds are not wetlands under the Corps' vegetation definition and are not alleged to adjoin navigable waters. Bird use does not itself supply that missing statutory relationship.

Section 404(g)(1) allows state administration outside the waters reserved to federal administration, confirming coverage beyond traditional navigation without defining every remaining pond as covered. Section 101(a)'s biological-integrity and wildlife objectives operate consistently with the Act's provisions and alongside section 101(b)'s protection of state responsibilities. Neither purpose alone settles the disputed jurisdiction. MCI and FDA v. Brown & Williamson require authority in the connected statutory scheme before deference; Sweet Home construed a different enacted habitat-injury definition and supplies no substitute delegation here.

The Corps' change of position is not automatically disqualifying, and the preamble's form is not an independent reason to invalidate it. No universal rule governing Chevron, Skidmore or interpretation of an agency's own regulation is necessary: the asserted reach exceeds the statute on its terms. Christensen's limited advisory-letter holding is not enlarged. Because the statutory answer disposes of this assertion, Congress's possible broader power is not decided. The Court neither imposes present navigability as a universal prerequisite nor determines all isolated waters or materially different supported statutory connections.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes: preserves its adjacent-wetland construction and ecological reasoning, distinguishing the expressly reserved isolated-water application rather than treating adjacency as a universal statutory test.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: applies the requirement that an agency act within the actual statutory grant; an administrative criterion cannot supply omitted authority.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.: applies analysis of the connected statutory scheme before deference; the decision supplies no general rule against consequential agency regulation.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon: distinguishes its habitat-injury construction under a different statutory definition and preserves its actual-injury and causation limits.
- Christensen v. Harris County: leaves intact its specific advisory-letter and own-regulation holdings; it does not establish a categorical rule against every preamble or informal interpretation.
- United States v. Lopez, National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, and United States v. Morrison: leave their actual Commerce rules unchanged because the statutory ground makes the contingent constitutional question unnecessary.

### Lopez v. Davis — 2001-01-10

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The Bureau may apply this reasoned firearm-conduct category

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3621(e)(2)(B) permits the Bureau of Prisons to withhold a discretionary early-release reduction categorically from otherwise eligible prisoners whose current felony conduct involved carrying, possessing or using a firearm or other dangerous weapon, under the challenged 1997 regulation. A nonviolent conviction and successful completion of qualifying treatment are necessary conditions for a reduction, not an entitlement to individual selection or an award; any reduction remains limited to one year, and the category must remain within the statute and rationally applied to the actual qualifying conduct.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six adopt the entire statutory ground, its public-safety application and affirmance. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Kennedy dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress distinguished treatment qualification, threshold eligibility for a reduction and the Bureau's discretionary choice whether to grant one. The permission to reduce custody does not require the Bureau to decide each recurring safety issue anew. Heckler v. Campbell permits valid rules within a statutory delegation; it supplies no authority to disregard that delegation. Here firearm involvement in felony conduct rationally bears on safe early release. Congress's nonviolent-conviction threshold does not direct the Bureau to disregard that conduct at the separate selection stage. The 1997 rule does not reclassify the conviction as violent.

Yang preserves statutory limits, reasoned administration and the distinction between eligibility and relief. Its immigration-waiver rule forbids using qualifying fraud automatically to defeat every eligible applicant; this subset does not extinguish the release incentive for all eligible prisoners. Koray likewise requires the statutory categories to do their work and supplies no independent deference shortcut. The Court's conclusion rests on this delegation and reasoned safety criterion, not administrative convenience alone.

The Bureau's separate duty to make appropriate treatment available to prisoners it determines have a treatable addiction or abuse condition remains. Residential treatment and aftercare are required for eligible willing prisoners with a substance-abuse problem, subject to available appropriations and release-date priority. After the Director finds successful residential treatment, the prisoner remains in custody under appropriate conditions; if those conditions differ because of completion, periodic testing is required and the altered conditions must end when abuse recurs. None of those commands makes early release mandatory.

Lopez's firearm enhancement presents the challenged category. The record establishes treatment admission eligibility, not successful completion or entitlement to a reduction. Ordinary objections to a misapplied category, disregard of binding rules or supported arbitrary administration remain distinct. The Court does not validate every possible exclusion or automatic denial to all eligible prisoners. The amici rulemaking-procedure objection was not raised or decided below or presented in the petition and is not reached; no constitutional liberty-interest question is decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Heckler v. Campbell: applies the permission to resolve recurring issues by rules within the actual delegation; it does not make every categorical restriction lawful.
- INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang: preserves eligibility/discretion separation and reasoned, consistent administration; distinguishes its prohibition of automatically defeating every eligible waiver application from this reasoned subset under a different statute.
- Reno v. Koray: preserves contextual statutory construction and its detention-credit holding; it supplies neither the present release rule nor an independently sufficient administrative-deference ground.

### Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Brentwood_Academy_v_Tennessee_Secondary_School_Athletic_Association_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Brentwood_Academy_v_Tennessee_Secondary_School_Athletic_Association_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Brentwood_Academy_v_Tennessee_Secondary_School_Athletic_Association_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The association's actual public operation makes this regulation governmental

**Holding and operative rule:** An athletic association's challenged regulatory activity is state action when public-school officials, acting in their official educational capacity, substantially govern and operate it as the common instrument for administering their schools' athletic programs. The demonstrated official operation here establishes governmental responsibility despite private incorporation, voluntary membership and private-school participation; membership percentages, funding or public benefit alone would not suffice.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six Justices directly adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas oppose the attribution ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron prevents government from avoiding constitutional responsibility through a corporate form. Its special-creation and permanent-majority-appointment combination is sufficient, not an exclusive checklist. American Manufacturers requires actual governmental responsibility and protects an independent private carrier's choice; it does not make an institution private when public officials actually operate the challenged function. Tarkanian's national association differs from the collective action of schools within one State. Its discussion of that distinction supports the comparison without itself deciding this case.

About 84 percent of the members are public schools. At the time of the penalties every voting governor was a public-school administrator, selected from principals, assistant principals and superintendents. Their governance of eligibility and competition, official school roles and meetings ordinarily during school hours establish collective public administration. Their official capacity in this work is the Court's legal inference from the established structure and function, not an undisputed lower-court conclusion. No factual remand is needed merely to recount that structure.

Private membership and voluntary selection do not turn this common administration of school athletics into an independent private choice. Public control need not be permanently guaranteed by the voting rules for this actual operation to be governmental. No separate state command to impose the recruiting penalty is required. Gate receipts supply the principal revenue and dues about four percent; staff are not state-paid, retirement eligibility includes no state-paid employer contribution, and State Board representatives do not vote. Those limited contacts are not substitutes for the governance ground. The 1996 recognition and voluntary-membership language replaced the earlier express designation. The district court's finding of unchanged material operation does not erase that amendment; the actual operation nevertheless supplies the necessary connection.

#### II. Attribution returns the preserved rights and relief questions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's no-state-action judgment is reversed and the case remanded with the association's challenged athletic regulation treated as governmental. The lower courts must adjudicate preserved constitutional merits and lawful relief; attribution alone neither invalidates the recruiting rule or penalties nor reinstates the district injunction or establishes damages.

**Authority:** The six-Justice coalition identified above directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron separates actor status from violation and remedy. The Sixth Circuit reversed the district court's summary judgment and injunction on attribution rather than resolving all the constitutional grounds. Correcting that premise returns the unreviewed questions under their own rules. The challenged action consists of four years of athletic-program probation, two years of exclusion from football and boys' basketball playoffs, and a $3,000 fine, imposed after recruiting communications about spring football practice. The Court does not decide whether that conduct was protected speech, whether the rule or procedure violated a constitutional right, or what relief any proved violation warrants. Those questions are distinct from the presented attribution issue and remain for ordinary lower-court adjudication; no further evidentiary hearing, damages award or particular injunction is directed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.: extends its governmental-responsibility principle to established collective official operation without requiring its precise special-charter and permanent-appointment combination; preserves that combination as sufficient and preserves the separation of actor status, constitutional merits and relief.
- American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan: preserves the requirement of actual governmental responsibility; distinguishes an institution operated by officials in their public capacity from a genuinely private choice merely permitted or reviewed by the State.
- National Collegiate Athletic Association v. Tarkanian, 488 U.S. 179: distinguishes the national association's relationship to one state university from this common administration by institutions of one State; the earlier decision remains effective and its single-State observation is not treated as an automatic attribution holding.

### Urofsky v. Gilmore — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Urofsky_v_Gilmore_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Urofsky_v_Gilmore_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Urofsky_v_Gilmore_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I-A. The mandatory condition can be challenged without a refused request

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff whose concrete intended or continuing activity is subject to an enforceable approval condition may challenge that condition without first seeking and being denied permission. The supported teaching and research activities here permit review of the condition itself; relief must still correspond to the plaintiffs' actual continuing injury under the amended coverage, and no hypothetical future refusal is adjudicated.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this threshold ruling and the general-entitlement holding that follows.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged burden is the necessity of advance written public permission, not only the possibility of a refused request. The record identifies removed Internet assignments, proposed research concerning Victorian poetry and other sexual subjects, scholarly databases, and Smith's website blocking and partial restoration. Published accounts identify summary-judgment submissions for material portions of those claims; this is not merely an unspecified fear of workplace regulation. None of these plaintiffs requested approval, and the record reports no denied request. Those facts limit any refusal claim but do not defeat this condition challenge.

Smith's blocking is not a denied statutory application. Nor does a document labeled a statement of undisputed facts establish that the Commonwealth conceded every assertion. The amended dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements narrow the statute; they do not establish that all the intended inquiry lies outside it. Article III power rests on the supported concrete condition burden, not an assumption of jurisdiction to decide disputed merits. The continuing covered activities and beneficiaries of final prospective relief must be determined at their proper scope.

#### I-B. Protected subject matter alone creates no unrestricted employee resource right

**Holding and operative rule:** The lawful or constitutionally protected character of material, its usefulness to an employee's job, and delegated professional discretion do not alone give every state employee a personal right to choose its use through government equipment free of approval. The reversal of the district court's wholesale declaration of invalidity remains in force, without sustaining every possible application to nonacademic employees or resolving the distinct individual scholarly claim.

**Authority:** Part I has all nine direct joins, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring requires identification of the employee's personal interest before balancing and permits lawful institutional control of an assigned instructional production. An employer ordinarily defines its prescribed work, allocates equipment and prevents misuse. The message and task matter; the computer's ownership is not a complete answer to an independently protected personal claim. The professors expressly disclaim personal recreational use, which is outside review.

The district court addressed the earlier, broader content definition and work-related activity across state employment. That reasoning cannot establish the amended law's substantial unconstitutional reach or support the same wholesale decree on these six faculty claims. NTEU preserves the distinction between a supported personal right and relief for unrepresented employees. This ruling does not adopt a rule that one lawful application defeats every First Amendment overbreadth challenge. Genuine institutional decisions remain subject to independent constitutional and other applicable limits, and the individual university question requires its own answer.

#### II. Independent scholarly expression requires its own protection and burden inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Independent university inquiry, teaching or communication on matters of public concern may carry a personal First Amendment interest according to its function and context; salary, connection to duties and use of public equipment do not alone make it the institution's prescribed message. Once that interest is established, Pickering and NTEU require the State to justify the actual prospective approval burden, including its effect on scholars and audiences, with a commensurate connection to legitimate operational needs; this rule grants no personal control over curricula, assignments, budgets, professional standards or equipment allocation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: six direct joins. The five Associates also join Part III's limited remand. Stone-Zsela joins this rule but concludes that the present record already establishes a violation requiring relief; his conclusion is not part of the Court's application or mandate. O'Connor assumes an interest without joining this affirmative rule; Scalia and Thomas reject the claimed entitlement.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring decided an assigned secondary-school production and expressly left university academic freedom and a universal duty-speech rule open. It cannot make every scholarly undertaking institutional expression. Keyishian protected individual educators and a library employee against the challenged speech and membership restrictions; its concern with official orthodoxy is not exclusively an institution's right. Sweezy's plurality and separate concurrence support attention to scholarly freedom on different grounds, not a single comprehensive academic-freedom test. Neither decision grants ownership of public research resources.

Pickering and Connick require contextual protection and employment justification. NTEU gives the actual prospective burden on speakers and audiences weight; it does not automatically classify every work activity as personal expression. Givhan confirms that location and private communication do not alone defeat protection. Waters preserves genuine lawful employer grounds but supplies no universal investigation procedure or answer to this statutory condition.

The inquiry must account for the amended content definition, required-use limitation, both approval branches, written approval, public availability and actual administration. Efficiency, resource management and preventing harassment are legitimate interests. The State may use operational evidence and supported predictive judgments; completed harassment, expert studies, automatic strict scrutiny or proof of actual disruption in every case is not required. Conversely, ownership and the availability of approval do not by themselves answer the burden of obtaining public advance permission for protected exploratory inquiry. Delegation to deans or department heads is relevant practice, not an enacted guarantee of universal peer review. Scholarly labels, underinclusiveness and a sexual subject alone establish neither protection nor invalidity. The standard preserves genuine academic governance and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions.

#### III. Apply the amended condition to the represented scholarly claims before directing relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is vacated insofar as it finally rejects the individual academic claim, and the case is remanded to identify continuing covered personal scholarly activity and apply the stated protection, burden and justification rules. Any declaration or injunction must redress an established violation and extend no further than the represented rights and effective relief require; the Court directs no present injunction, access approval, damages, special hearing or new discovery.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: five direct joins to the whole application and remedy. Stone-Zsela would reverse and direct confined relief now; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the academic disposition. The five-Justice holding requires no combination of opposing rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court's categorical employee-role and exclusively institutional academic-freedom rules bypassed the necessary inquiry. The district court's broader statutory definition and decree do not supply its answer. The existing record contains specific scholarly burdens and Commonwealth evidence. Five reported misuse incidents and complaints support preventive concerns without establishing five harassment judgments or criminal guilt. Two incidents preceded the Act and were reported as addressed under existing policies; that does not prove those policies prevent every future harm. Smith's asserted scholarly purpose and the complaints remain distinct from a statutory refusal.

Prompt approvals with minimal paperwork and blanket permissions support the contention that the condition is modest. They are examples, not statutory deadlines, assurances that every request will succeed or elimination of the written public-approval burden. A research connection does not replace the statutory requirement that access be required for an approved project or other undertaking. The lower courts must assess the actual protected scope and fit under the amended law, using the existing submissions and ordinary procedural rules for any properly authorized further proceedings.

This remand is not based on an evidentiary vacuum or unrecovered original exhibits. It presumes neither that plaintiffs must win nor that every approval system fails. No improper motive, unlawful refusal or unrestricted access entitlement is found. The remaining work applies the stated rule to activity, coverage, burden and remedy; it does not postpone the governing legal standard. No isolated invalidation of the public-records requirement or decision about an unpresented project follows.

### Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices — 2000-12-11

**Kennedy, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The dissent accepts Russell's and Shrink Missouri's contribution standard and recognizes that the influence evidence justifies some prophylaxis. It finds the State's operational answer inadequate for this amount because projected funding losses are substantial and concentrated in affected competitive races. Average expenses and a special election with different donor competition do not sufficiently establish replacement capacity. Voluntary initial grants are legitimate; making their program attractive does not justify burdening nonparticipants' expression or merely offsetting an opponent's advantage. The State has not shown why its anti-dependence interest requires grants measured by another speaker's activity. This objection does not erase the contribution/expenditure distinction or invalidate every public grant or adjustment.

The dissenters seek component-specific declaratory and prospective relief, implemented on remand under 1 MRSA §71(8): preserve provisions and applications that can operate independently, subject to the construction rule's plain-meaning qualification. ### Bush v. Gore — 2000-12-12

**O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join reviewability, the supported equality defect, rejection of separate Article II and due-process reversal, the section 5 distinction, and corrective vacatur. Their disagreement concerns state law: the December 11 majority's notes 17 and 22, read with its comprehensive-code account and the relation between certification and contest, establish December 12 finality. Palm Beach's recognition that §5 is not itself a federal prohibition does not erase a State's choice to meet that date. Correction must therefore stay within that state limit. They do not find that correction has already become impossible, infer current progress, authorize an extension, or award electoral votes. The supervising court must apply the retained deadline to the actual feasible process rather than receive an unsupported terminal factual conclusion from this Court.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting from the judgment.** They agree on reviewability, unequal treatment, absence of a separate established due-process violation, and §5's limited federal operation. Article II supplies an independent reason to reverse the challenged directions. First, de novo treatment of discretionary board decisions combined with automatic inclusion of late contest tallies removes the remaining legislative allocation among certification, executive discretion, and judicial contest. Palm Beach permits some late amendments; it does not abolish every residual limit. Second, the legal-vote construction conflates failure to tabulate a properly marked vote with failure to mark in the prescribed manner. The statutory instruction and machine provisions and the Secretary's delegated interpretation matter; §101.5614(5) does not furnish a general answer for these ballots.

They recognize §102.168's rejected-legal-vote ground, change-or-place-in-doubt language, and investigative authority, but find this use materially displaces the particular institutional and substantive limits. The pre-election existence of an intent provision does not decide its scope. Their disposition reverses the affected directions and remands for conformity with the pre-existing allocation and voting requirements, without invalidating the entire contest statute or deciding individual ballots. An equality protocol alone cannot cure unauthorized relief. Only if the Court rejects this Article II ground do they accept equality correction, confined to the state-law December 12 limit. They adopt neither an unsupported present impossibility finding nor a blanket termination of every lawful state action. The two rejected contest claims remain undisturbed.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting from corrective vacatur.** They join bounded review, rejection of independent Article II and due-process reversal, the federal-calendar rule, and refusal to impose a terminal state-law construction. They would affirm. A common clear-intent rule can produce different results from different physical evidence, and board disagreement, changing evidentiary judgments, or incomplete implementation detail does not itself establish that this judicial decree authorizes unconstitutional treatment. Spencer's and Burton's accounts warrant focused examination of disputed treatment through ordinary objections and judicial correction; they do not demonstrate that such correction is unavailable or that the operative statewide command is constitutionally defective.

Harper and Reynolds protect equal participation and weight, but do not demand mechanical uniformity for different evidence. Burdick permits neutral administration, and the actual Shaw rule requires an identified deprivation. The legislature's contest and corrective provisions and the state majority's substantial explanation support authoritative interpretation under Palm Beach; Yoshina prevents treating disagreement alone as fundamental unfairness. They require neither partisan bad faith nor proof that a winner changes. Their disagreement concerns the established defect and its attribution to the decree. If a constitutional defect is found, they accept a lawful corrective opportunity; that conditional position does not make them supporters of vacatur. They authorize no count beyond binding state law and make no feasibility or winner finding.

Sweet Home and PUD No. 1 support applying broad protective language according to its actual conditions.
