## Standards and Tests selection: Review of Guidelines departures resting on invalid grounds

### Review of Guidelines departures resting on invalid grounds

**Governing question:** When a reviewing court may affirm a Guidelines departure supported in part by an impermissible factor under 18 U.S.C. §3742(f).

**Current rule:** A departure based on a factor forbidden by the Commission, or already fully considered in the applicable range, is an incorrect Guidelines application under §3742(f)(1). An express prohibitory policy statement is authoritative guidance in applying the relevant Guideline; the arrest record itself cannot supply a departure ground. Once the challenger establishes reliance on an invalid factor, remand is required unless the party defending the sentence persuades the reviewing court, from the whole record, that the district court would have imposed the same sentence without it. The challenger need not prove that the error determined the sentence. Even if that error was harmless, §3742(f)(2) independently requires a reasonable departure extent justified by valid grounds the sentencing court actually identified. That the court could lawfully have imposed the sentence does not establish either actual causal harmlessness or permission for an appellate court to invent departure grounds.

**Authority by component:**
- Invalid-factor error and express policy prohibition: Williams v. United States, March 9, 1992, “An impermissible departure ground is a Guidelines application error.”
- Same-sentence inquiry and burden sequence: the same record, “Harmlessness requires the same sentence without the invalid factor.”
- Independent reasonableness inquiry and actual-ground limit: the same record, “Departure reasonableness remains a separate condition of affirmance.” O'Connor's opinion has seven joins at each level: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** Williams, March 9, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is nonconstitutional error governed by Rule 52(a); no Chapman beyond-reasonable-doubt burden is adopted. The decision does not make every policy statement binding for every purpose or decide whether old nonsimilar convictions support a departure.

**Holdings navigation:** Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992) — Guidelines departure review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary

### Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary

**Governing question:** What instructions govern authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary?

**Current rule:** Commentary interpreting or explaining a Guideline is authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to a federal statute, inconsistent with the Guideline or a plainly erroneous reading of it. Commentary cannot amend contrary text; an earlier judicial interpretation does not alone defeat a later permissible Commission interpretation. Amendment 433's exclusion of unlawful firearm possession by a felon permissibly interprets crime of violence for defendants to whom it applies. Governing version and temporal application remain separate; the decision does not resolve them. Williams's distinct departure-error consequences remain operative.

## Standards and Tests selection: Guidelines departure classification and appellate review

### Guidelines departure classification and appellate review

**Governing question:** Permissibility and appellate review of a departure from the Guidelines range.

**Current rule:** Departure decisions receive abuse-of-discretion review: legal interpretation and permission are independently reviewed; historical findings are protected unless clearly erroneous; supported case-specific judgments of unusualness and degree receive substantial deference. Under §3553(b), the range governs unless a circumstance inadequately considered in kind or degree by the Commission should produce a different sentence, assessed from Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Forbidden factors cannot support departure. Encouraged factors may support it if the applicable guideline has not accounted for them. Discouraged factors, and encouraged factors already accounted for, require exceptional degree or another supported distinguishing feature; when the guideline and adjustments account for the factor, §5K2.0 requires a degree substantially exceeding the ordinary case. For an unmentioned factor, examine the relevant guideline’s structure and theory and the Guidelines as a whole; such departures should be highly infrequent. Rarity alone does not suffice, and no compulsory classification sequence is imposed. Sentencing purposes remain relevant without creating extra categorical prohibitions. Section 4A1.3 forbids departure below category I’s lower range for a first offender on exceptionally low recidivism risk. Extraordinary prison vulnerability or an unusually significant burden from successive state/federal proceedings may be considered on supported findings under the unmentioned-factor inquiry; police status, notoriety or lawful successive prosecution alone creates no entitlement or prescribed reduction.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act

### Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition

**Governing question:** The permissible scope and causal limits of the regulatory habitat clause.

**Current rule:** The ESA permits “harm” to include significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavioral patterns, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Actual death or injury must be proximately caused with ordinary foreseeability; bare but-for linkage or speculative future harm is insufficient. An omission may count only where an independently existing legal duty supplies the obligation. This decision creates no general duty to maintain habitat. Independently, where the statutory definition leaves room on indirect injury, the Secretary’s actual-injury construction is reasonable and receives Chevron deference without becoming the only permissible construction.

Members are treated as threatened; a nonessential population is treated as proposed for listing under §7 except §7(a)(1), with the National Wildlife Refuge and National Park System exception. Critical habitat may not be designated for a nonessential population; earlier authorized separate releases retain the statutory regulatory-determination condition. No population application is decided.

The facial rule adjudicates no enforcement scienter or universal knowledge-of-illegality requirement. Separate §11 civil and criminal channels retain applicable notice and hearing requirements and the civil good-faith protection for action believed necessary to protect a person from bodily harm.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA plan commands and indirect economic effects

### ERISA plan commands and indirect economic effects

**Governing question:** When state benefit mandates or indirect price effects relate to covered plans under §514(a).

**Current rule:** ERISA §514(a) preempts a replacement-coverage obligation insofar as it uses a covered plan’s benefit content as the mandatory legal measure, even if replacement is separately administered through exempt workers’ compensation arrangements. Requirements measured solely by exempt governmental or church plans remain outside that holding. By contrast, the reviewed commercial-insurer hospital differentials and conditional HMO assessment are not preempted merely because price or participation effects influence plan choices: the measures neither use ERISA status or plan benefits as their legal benchmark nor command benefits, eligibility, uniform administration or an alternative enforcement remedy. Ordinary indirect economic influence is not itself such a command. The replacement-coverage obligation’s 52-week cap supplies no federal safe harbor. Direct payment by a self-insured plan that owns the provider does not itself convert a provider-receipts tax into a plan command. The tax remains unpreempted at the reviewed scope when licensed activity and receipts trigger it independently of ERISA status and its actual operation dictates neither plan benefits, eligibility, fiduciary standards, administration, enforcement nor choices through equivalent coercion. General applicability and a low rate alone supply no safe harbor. A mandatory state rule assigning an employer the insurer’s agency role because it performs benefit-plan administration directly connects with administration and cannot survive on a no-relation premise. This is distinct from saved insurance-contract content; ordinary voluntary or fact-specific agency principles are not categorically preempted.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA survivor protection and antecedent succession interests

### ERISA survivor protection and antecedent succession interests

**Governing question:** State succession claims competing with protected survivor annuities or based on transfer of undistributed pension interests.

**Current rule:** Section 1055's protected survivor-annuity entitlement cannot be diverted by a prior spouse's succession claim, whether collection is direct, after payment, through an accounting or through an equivalent capital award. ERISA likewise preempts a claim to pension payments, a rollover IRA or distributed ESOP property when its legal source is an impermissible succession transfer of an interest in then-undistributed benefits contrary to applicable beneficiary and anti-alienation provisions. Distribution changes possession or form but does not validate that earlier transfer. A genuinely independent later obligation remains outside this rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Boggs v. Boggs — The protected survivor annuity cannot be diverted through the first spouse's succession, 1997-06-02; Souter, all nine
- Boggs v. Boggs — The three other claims remain dependent on the impermissible interest in undistributed benefits, 1997-06-02; Souter with Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five

**Present operation effective:** Boggs, 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is not permanent immunity for all property traceable to a pension, a general succession code or a ruling that an IRA remains an ERISA plan. Section 408(g)'s tax treatment without community-property law and §408(d)(6)'s divorce-transfer tax provision do not supply the property rule. Survivor-annuity coverage, actually applicable statutory exceptions and independent later bargains, loans, gifts or tort obligations retain separate analysis.

**Controlling construction:** Under the governing survivor-annuity provisions, a qualified joint-and-survivor annuity pays the survivor for life 50–100 percent of the joint-life annuity with required actuarial equivalence. Waiver requires written spouse consent acknowledging the effect, witnessed by a plan representative or notary; beneficiary changes require further consent unless consent permits them. Consent may be excused upon the representative's satisfaction that no spouse exists, the spouse cannot be located or a Treasury-prescribed circumstance prevents consent; consent or excuse binds only that spouse. The optional one-year marriage condition and the applicable temporal version remain separate. Under §1056(d)(2), the exceptions include voluntary revocable assignments up to ten percent of a payment and irrevocable assignments executed before September 2, 1974, excluding assignments for plan-administration costs; a loan secured by accrued nonforfeitable benefits is not an assignment when exempt under §4975(d)(1).

**Exceptions and limits on domestic-relations orders:** A qualified order may recognize support, alimony or marital-property rights of a spouse, former spouse, child or other dependent under state domestic-relations law, including community-property law. It must identify the participant and last known address if any, alternate payees and their addresses, amount or percentage or means of determination, payment number or period and affected plans. It may not require an unavailable benefit form, increased actuarial benefits or payment already assigned by a prior qualified order. A qualified alternate payee is a beneficiary and §1144(b)(7) preserves the corresponding preemption exception; a probate label alone does not disqualify an order. REA §303(d) requires qualification of a pre-January 1, 1985 order if benefits were being paid pursuant to it on that date; qualification of other older orders despite noncompliance is permitted, not compelled.

**Holdings navigation:** Boggs v. Boggs (1997-06-02) — The protected survivor annuity cannot be recovered through a succession accounting; Boggs v. Boggs (1997-06-02) — Labor and Employment / Pension succession and survivor protection.

## Standards and Tests selection: Source-identifying color and functionality

### Source-identifying color and functionality

**Governing question:** Eligibility of color for Lanham Act protection.

**Current rule:** Color may receive trademark protection when it acquires source-identifying significance and is nonfunctional, subject to ordinary Lanham Act requirements including likelihood of confusion where required. Bare color requires secondary meaning; the law protects neither color in the abstract nor useful features. A feature essential to use or purpose or affecting cost or quality is functional, and protection cannot foreclose color shown competitively necessary. Source significance does not overcome functionality.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-03-28; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not resolve every aesthetic feature or color-market competition question or establish infringement merely from eligibility. The existing inherently distinctive trade-dress rule is not replaced by a universal secondary-meaning demand.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nonfunctionality burden for unregistered trade dress

### Nonfunctionality burden for unregistered trade dress

**Governing question:** Who must prove nonfunctionality in an infringement action involving trade dress not registered on the principal register?

**Current rule:** Under 15 U.S.C. 1125(a)(3), the party asserting protection for trade dress not registered on the principal register must prove that the claimed matter is not functional. This burden is independent of distinctiveness, secondary meaning and the other infringement requirements.

## Standards and Tests selection: Acquired source significance for product-design trade dress

### Acquired source significance for product-design trade dress

**Governing question:** The distinctiveness required for unregistered product-design trade dress under Lanham Act §43(a).

**Current rule:** Product design requires secondary meaning: its primary significance to the relevant public must identify a source rather than the product itself, even when consumers do not know the source’s name. Inherent distinctiveness is insufficient. Unusual design, producer intent, familiarity with the product and copying do not themselves establish acquired source association. Source significance, nonfunctionality and likelihood of confusion remain separate requirements; no universal survey method is imposed.

**Authority by component:**

- Acquired-source prerequisite and separate infringement requirements: Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Samara Brothers, Inc. — Product-design trade dress requires acquired source significance, March 22, 2000; Scalia’s opinion joined by all seven other Associate Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Wal-Mart, March 22, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** Qualifying restaurant presentation and packaging retain their separate inherent-distinctiveness routes. The opinion’s genuinely uncertain product-versus-packaging classification guidance is explanatory beyond the holding and supplies no additional operative component here.

**Related current rules:** Nonfunctionality burden for unregistered trade dress governs the distinct §1125(a)(3) burden. Wal-Mart supplies no decision about that 1999 provision’s application to antecedent conduct or the earlier trial.

## Standards and Tests selection: Inherently distinctive nonfunctional restaurant trade dress

### Inherently distinctive nonfunctional restaurant trade dress

**Governing question:** Whether acquired secondary meaning is additionally necessary under pre-1988 Lanham Act §43(a).

**Current rule:** Nonfunctional restaurant trade dress that is inherently distinctive may be protected without acquired secondary meaning if the remaining infringement requirements, including likelihood of confusion, are met. Protection does not expire after an unspecified period merely because acquired meaning has not developed. Functionality, distinctiveness and confusion remain independent requirements.

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual commerce and state-court enforcement of arbitration agreements

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.

## Standards and Tests selection: Substantial special need for suspicionless candidate drug testing

### Substantial special need for suspicionless candidate drug testing

**Governing question:** Fourth Amendment justification for compelled drug testing as a candidacy condition.

**Current rule:** Compelled urine collection and chemical analysis are searches, and candidacy does not waive bodily privacy. A suspicionless program justified by a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual governmental need, program operation and affected privacy. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not supply the substantial, concrete need required for broad advance-scheduled candidate testing; reduced intrusion or public prominence alone cannot compensate for that deficiency.

**Authority by component:**

- Chandler v. Miller — Candidacy does not waive bodily privacy, 1997-04-15; unanimous Opinion of the Court, holding 1, supplies the no-waiver boundary
- Chandler v. Miller — Georgia has not supplied a special need, 1997-04-15; unanimous holding 2 supplies the concrete-need requirement at this program scope

**Present operation effective:** Chandler, 1997-04-15.

**Exceptions and limits:** Materially different programs retain contextual special-needs review. No universal proof-of-catastrophe, numeric efficacy requirement or exhaustion of all alternatives is imposed.

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited drug testing of interscholastic athletes

### Limited drug testing of interscholastic athletes

**Governing question:** Fourth Amendment treatment of a documented school-athletic urinalysis program.

**Current rule:** Collecting and chemically analyzing a student’s urine is a search; enrollment and voluntary athletic participation do not waive protection. In the documented interscholastic setting, objective seasonal and random testing may be reasonable without individualized suspicion where the protective safety need, athletic privacy expectations, limited collection and drug information, confidentiality and restricted counseling or athletic consequences together justify the intrusion. The holding does not authorize testing all students, unrelated medical investigation or ordinary police use.

**Authority by component:**

- I. Urine testing is a search; athletic participation is no waiver: I. Urine testing is a search; athletic participation is no waiver, 1995-06-26. Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt the search and no-waiver rule.
- II. This interscholastic-athlete testing program is reasonable: II. This interscholastic-athlete testing program is reasonable, 1995-06-26. Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the rule and application.
- III. The facial prescription-verification objection does not invalidate the policy: III. The facial prescription-verification objection does not invalidate the policy, 1995-06-26. Part III of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this limited facial rule. Stone-Zsela does not join it. It independently answers this objection, not the entire testing challenge.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-26; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The upheld program used a concealed-identity student draw with adult witnesses, initial seasonal testing and weekly random selection of about ten percent of athletes, leaving no individualized official selection discretion. Boys remained clothed facing away from a monitor without genital viewing; girls used enclosed stalls with an outside monitor. Drug testing covered amphetamines, marijuana and cocaine and occasional LSD, not alcohol or steroids. Anonymous laboratory numbers, restricted school access, one-year retention and no police transmission, suspension, expulsion or general discipline limited use. Initial refusal excluded only the current season.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Prescription verification allows a prescription copy or doctor’s authorization, not compulsory personal delivery to teachers or coaches. Missing verification plus a positive result invokes positive-test procedures, not exclusion for missing verification alone. Confirmed positives lead to parental notification and a hearing. First offense allows counseling with six weekly tests or current-and-next-season athletic suspension. Written second-offense consequences are current-and-next-season suspension, with a counseling/testing alternative described in practice. Third offense excludes the current and next two seasons; testing precedes the next eligible season.

**Open question:** The facial prescription ruling does not approve every form, nonprescription disclosure, physician-name demand or actual disclosure practice.
