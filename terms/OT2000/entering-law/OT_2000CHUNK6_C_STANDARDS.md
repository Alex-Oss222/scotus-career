## Standards and Tests selection: Core-Preservation Review

### Core-Preservation Review

**Governing question:** Whether a restriction burdens constitutionally protected arms activity and what justification its practical operation requires.

**Current rule:** Stage 1 asks whether the law interferes with protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation of arms, with constitutional coverage determined from text, history and the Court's precedents. If protected activity is burdened, Stage 2 requires justification corresponding to the severity of the restriction's practical operation, subject to the independent categorical rule that government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home.

A core deprivation receives strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation receives intermediate scrutiny: an important governmental objective, a substantial relationship between the regulation and that objective, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate the protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right remain unconstitutional. Routine equipment specifications, administrative procedures and minor requirements qualify for that last category only when they do not materially obstruct the right. Actual operation controls: calling an effective prohibition a safety measure or administrative rule cannot reduce its level of review.

**Exceptions and limits:** The home boundary protects a meaningful core; it does not grant immunity to every weapon, person or use. Necessary ammunition and transportation receive protection as incidents of protected keeping or bearing, not through coverage of every item or transport method. That boundary, the graduated standard and the application to particular facts perform different functions.

**Application to particular persons:** The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation of particular persons under Wright's Core-Preservation Review and satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use challenge. Keeping and bearing remain covered at Stage 1; at Stage 2, preventing armed recurrence is an important objective, the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate and conviction safeguards substantially connect the restriction to that objective, and the defined eligibility and relief limits constrain its burden; the personal right does not compel an official-use exception.

*Love* recognizes personal protection, *Hale* distinguishes coverage from regulation, and *Rybar* rejects a setting-based denial of the right. Keeping or bearing does not lose protection merely because it occurs at work. There is, however, no independent fundamental entitlement to an armed public job. *Wright* measures justification by practical burden. This serious disability, confined by its adjudicated conduct predicate and statutory safeguards, is a substantial regulation of particular persons rather than incidental paperwork or a general abolition of ordinary defensive arms. The developed claim concerns access through the governmental channel for armed service; no individual's ordinary home-possession application is presented.

The contrary argument confuses an individual right with immunity from a justified conduct-based restriction. A service weapon remains accessible to its possessor; official employment does not neutralize the risk established by qualifying conduct. Intermediate scrutiny is satisfied on the presented record, independently of ordinary equality review. This does not authorize disarmament for any misdemeanor, a mere allegation or an unrelated offense. An improperly classified conviction, an unavailable statutory safeguard or a genuinely different individual private-possession record remains open. Wright's prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession remains intact; no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question is presented.

**Statutory scope of the official-use application:** The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

**Authority by component:**

- Stage 1 coverage, all three Stage 2 standards, practical operation and the independent categorical home rule: United States v. Wright, 1998-10-05, Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (five direct rationale votes).
- Both-stage remand direction and incidental registration application: Wright, Parts II-C and III; the same five. All nine support the bounded machinegun vacatur and registration affirmance. Stevens's concurrence, Part A, joined by all seven other Associates, independently sustains the tax-administration and registration ground; Stone-Zsela adds no independent tax ground.
- Substantial regulation of qualifying persons, intermediate scrutiny and official-use limits: Fraternal Order of Police v. United States, 1998-10-07, Core-Preservation Review permits this conduct-based disability and official-use exclusion; Statutory scope retained throughout. Souter's opinion is joined in full by all nine Justices.
- Preserved-claim boundary: Hiley v. Barrett, 1998-10-09, Part II, Personal arms protection does not revive abandoned claims; O'Connor, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight); Stone-Zsela agrees in judgment only.

**Present operation effective:** Wright, 1998-10-05; Fraternal Order of Police, 1998-10-07, for the application to qualifying persons in the defined official-use category.

**Open question:** Prohibiting ordinary functional handguns in the home and effectively eliminating all lawful public carry illustrate core burdens; no particular public-carry law is before the Court. Public-carry licensing, safety instruction, examinations, demonstrated proficiency, refresher training and recertification, including their classification and justification, are expressly reserved. No universal numerical rule, militia exclusion, military-weapon immunity or comprehensive prohibited-arms list is adopted.

The tier and ultimate arms validity of Wright's machinegun restriction remain undecided. Hiley does not adjudicate the abandoned arms claim or its private-possession application; Wright and Fraternal Order of Police govern preserved arms challenges.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

An authorized judge’s actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal even when the judge allegedly erred in assessing §2253(c)(2)’s constitutional showing. That alleged assessment error does not itself nullify the existing appellate authorization. The substantial-constitutional-showing condition remains necessary for proper issuance; this rule neither finds a bare rules claim sufficient nor authorizes future certificates without the statutory showing. When dismissal rests on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, certification requires reasonable debate both over whether the petition states a valid constitutional claim and over the correctness of the procedural ruling. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent one first and must identify any certified issues. Mere nonfrivolousness, a debatable procedural question alone or a bare constitutional label is insufficient; ultimate merits success is not required. For an actual merits rejection, reasonable jurists must be able to debate the assessment or find it wrong.

**Authority by component:**

- Appeal, certificate, district/circuit application and transfer procedures: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §§102–103, 28 U.S.C. §2253 and Fed. R. App. P. 22, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Hohn Holdings I–II: certiorari review and distinct certificate requirements: Hohn v. United States — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-15.

- Issued authorization distinguished from correctness of the constitutional-showing assessment: Canonical Decision Record `peguero_v_united_states_merits_1999-03-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The issued certificate supplied the appellate authorization in this case, 1999-03-02; eight Associate joins.

- Post-enactment appeal transition: Slack v. McDaniel — II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate, April 26, 2000; Ginsburg with O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
- Constitutional and procedural debate, no fixed order, and issue identification: Slack — III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings, April 26, 2000; all nine directly adopt the construction.

**Present operation effective:** Slack, April 26, 2000, for the appellate transition and two-component procedural-dismissal showing; the earlier issued-certificate and reviewability rules retain their authority.

**Effective transition:** Amended §2253(c) governs an applicant’s appeal initiated after April 24, 1996 even when identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier application. A CPC label or an earlier contrary legal position does not supply missing authorization. This does not apply amended §2254(d) to the earlier application or establish a universal temporal rule for every amendment. Authority: Slack v. McDaniel, Part II, April 26, 2000. The distinct timing rules in “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” retain their scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 22’s circuit-judge transfer language does not repeal the Supreme Court’s distinct original habeas authority; see “Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar.”

## Standards and Tests selection: Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

### Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation?

**Current rule:** When record-supported mitigation has material culpability significance that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Admission and partial relevance to future danger are insufficient if a truthful finding of deliberateness and danger prevents giving effect to youth's independent mitigating force. Disturbed upbringing need not involve a uniquely severe permanent handicap or lack any relation to future danger. The sentencer need not give favorable weight, and mere possibility of a restrictive reading is insufficient. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated this functional requirement before Graham's September 10, 1984 finality; no new Teague exception follows. Adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence can provide correction without disturbing guilt. No general positive-character rule follows from Graham's separate fractured component.

When a capital jury asks whether proof of an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response considered with the complete charge and trial must resolve a reasonable likelihood that supported mitigation cannot be given effect in choosing life. Referring to an existing instruction may suffice if it unambiguously answers the actual difficulty; no particular words or universal rephrasing duty are required. Evidence, advocacy, further deliberation or a recital that mitigation was considered does not conclusively cure an otherwise unresolved restriction on mitigating use.

**Authority by component:**

- The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality: Graham v. Collins — The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality, 1993-01-25. Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.
- Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force: Graham v. Collins — Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force, 1993-01-25. Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.
- The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration: Graham v. Collins — The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration, 1993-01-25. Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

- Express post-charge inquiry and effective response: Weeks v. Angelone, Public Projection / Holdings / IV. The referral left a reasonable likelihood that mitigation could not authorize life, January 19, 2000; Breyer Part III with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg (five). The functional protection applies as old law: III. The effective-mitigation protection applied here is not a new rule; Breyer Part II with the same five plus O'Connor (six).

**Present operation effective:** Weeks v. Angelone, January 19, 2000, for the express post-charge question and response component; Graham's original effective-mitigation rule remains.

**Holdings navigation:** Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993).

**Exceptions and limits:** Buchanan's original-charge holding survives. A constitutional response error is distinct from statutory habeas eligibility and actual effect on sentence. In Weeks's jury-response category, no majority chooses the effect standard, burden or treatment of uncertainty; the receiving court must resolve those questions consistently with the existing category-specific holdings before granting relief. No automatic writ, general Chapman extension or blanket substantial-and-injurious-effect rule follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Merits-relief restriction, factual presumption/rebuttal and cumulative hearing conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(2)–(4), 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)–(e), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court holdings and temporal limit, and objective legal inquiry: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by the seven other Associates.
- Materially truncated or objectively indefensible mitigation assessment: Terry Williams — III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- An additional Fretwell unfairness demand in an ordinary lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with the governing rule, with actual independent grounds still requiring examination: Terry Williams — VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part III with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Applicant-attributed deficient diligence and cumulative hearing exceptions: Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams) — I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure, April 18, 2000; Ginsburg’s Part I, unanimous. Reasonable available efforts and unknown concealed relationships: the same decision, III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead; all eight Associates.

**Present operation effective:** The two Williams v. Taylor decisions, April 18, 2000, for the stated legal-review and diligence constructions; the enacted factual presumption and other unchanged components retain their original authority.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Unresolved interaction:** Neither exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses commands a majority in Terry Williams; no Marks synthesis supplies one. No universal unreasonable-extension or refusal-to-extend rule is adopted. Section 2254(d)(2), §2254(e)(1), and the hearing conditions retain distinct work. Michael Wayne Williams does not replace the former-law Keeney rule outside the amendment’s temporal domain. Satisfaction of a statutory gateway does not itself establish the underlying constitutional violation or authorize relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

### Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

**Governing question:** When health or safety risk permits a covered provider to exclude a person.

**Current rule:** Exclusion requires a significant risk to others’ health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment using current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation. The assessment uses information reasonably available to the provider and profession when service is refused, not a subjective sincerity test; neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission is universally required. Sincere professional belief receives no special legal deference. Reasonable public-health judgments receive special but nonconclusive weight subject to credible scientific challenge, only within the source’s supported role, date and scope.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 3: unanimous objective method; Holding 4: five direct votes for founded nonconclusive public-health weight: Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment; neither an inferred official conclusion nor later information substitutes for the actual relevant source. An alternative hospital offer alone supplies no independent defense or duty to accept it; service outside professional competence is not required and objectively justified precautions remain permissible.

**Holdings navigation:** Bragdon v. Abbott — ADA disability and direct-threat assessment in public accommodations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Title VII punitive culpability and managerial attribution

### Title VII punitive culpability and managerial attribution

**Governing question:** When §1981a permits punitive consideration and attributes a manager’s conduct to an employer.

**Current rule:** Punitive eligibility requires intentional discrimination with malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights: at minimum, discrimination in the face of a perceived risk of federal illegality. Egregious conduct may prove culpability but is not an additional indispensable element. Ordinary liability does not automatically establish punitive culpability, attribution or jury submission. The court asks whether evidence and reasonable employee-favorable inferences permit the punitive predicates; if submitted, the jury retains discretion whether to award. For the managerial attribution route, actual entrusted authority and discretion plus action within employment scope control; title alone and an invariant top-management requirement do not.

**Authority by component:**

- Kolstad v. American Dental Association — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Punitive eligibility turns on culpability toward federal rights, not an additional egregious act”, 1999-06-22; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part I also supplies separate submission, discretionary award and amount inquiries.
- Kolstad v. American Dental Association — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Managerial punitive attribution depends on actual authority and scope”, 1999-06-22; O’Connor Part II-A with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Five direct supporters supply the managerial route.
- Kolstad v. American Dental Association — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Actual good-faith compliance limits managerial vicarious punishment”, 1999-06-22; the same five Justices in Part II-B. Supplies the confined actual-compliance limitation.

**Present operation effective:** Kolstad, June 22, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Managerial vicarious punitive liability does not attach when the discriminatory decision is contrary to meaningful, reasonably designed and actually implemented good-faith Title VII compliance efforts. Paper policy, nonreporting or a good-faith label alone is insufficient. This limit does not remove ordinary liability or compensation or excuse the employer’s own discriminatory policy or independently established wrongdoing. Training, reporting, enforcement and correction may matter without a mandatory checklist.

**Open question:** No majority allocates a universal burden or standard of proof for the compliance limit, makes the managerial route exclusive or decides unused authorization, hiring and ratification routes. No general harassment defense, employee reporting duty or additional clear-and-convincing requirement follows.

**Related current rules:** Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment retains its distinct ordinary-liability rules and unresolved defense questions. Punitive eligibility does not replace statutory caps or independent amount review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Mitigating measures in actual ADA disability

### Mitigating measures in actual ADA disability

**Governing question:** Whether actual-disability coverage under original §12102(2)(A) is assessed with measures the individual uses.

**Current rule:** Successful mitigating measures do not erase the underlying impairment. Whether it substantially limits a major life activity is assessed without treating the beneficial effects of correction or medication as eliminating that limitation. The individual still must prove a substantial limitation of a major life activity; a diagnosis or the use of lenses or a prescription alone does not establish disability, and no corrected or treated condition is automatically covered. Actual functioning with treatment remains relevant to qualification, reasonable accommodation and legitimate safety standards, which are resolved on their own elements. No duty to stop treatment follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Murphy v. United Parcel Service, Inc. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Actual disability is assessed without treating successful mitigation as eliminating the underlying limitation”, 1999-06-22; Ginsburg Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer.
- Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Holding 1— Beneficial mitigation does not eliminate underlying-impairment coverage”, 1999-06-22; Stone-Zsela Part I with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The same-day decisions independently resolve Bragdon’s general mitigation reservation.

**Present operation effective:** Murphy and Sutton, June 22, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Record-of and regarded-as branches remain separate. No general invalidation of EEOC authority or every regulation, new episodic-impairment threshold, qualification ruling or safety defense follows.

**Related current rules:** Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction retains Bragdon’s individual reproductive-risk application; Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat remains distinct from coverage.
