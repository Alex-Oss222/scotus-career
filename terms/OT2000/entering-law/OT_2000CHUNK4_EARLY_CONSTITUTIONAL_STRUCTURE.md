#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-A and III, gives this reserved route its fuller definition: Congress may include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce; the Government identifies that regulation, the covered intrastate class and a genuine functional relationship between them, without species-by-species, crossing or independent-effects proof. Part II-B resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. That independent route need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer also join Part II-B.

##### II. Necessary inclusion in a valid interstate scheme sustains the taking prohibition; an independent externality route remains available

**Holding:** Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two; Congress may not establish that relationship merely by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute. Where Congress has established an integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, it may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system. The Constitution does not require separate proof that every species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce, or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect if left unprotected. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual.

The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly within the Act's integrated system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers. This constitutional conclusion neither establishes an actual taking nor eliminates the statutory conditions for liability, permits or relief.

Congress may also regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. That route is an independent alternative. It need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power, and it is not applied to this fly.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule and its application are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II-A while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government identifies the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship; courts decide those boundaries independently

**Holding:** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision: the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For necessary inclusion, the relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis. Here the Act's text and structure identify each element: its valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, the taking class section 9(a)(1)(B) actually covers, and that class's function in preserving the populations the trade provisions regulate and keeping those provisions enforceable. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. The obligation does not presume that every individual act must cross a state boundary or produce a separately substantial effect.

##### IV. The judgment sustaining the challenged applications is affirmed

**Holding:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged habitat modification against this Commerce Clause objection is affirmed. Part II-A resolves the constitutional question presented, so no Commerce Clause evidentiary remand follows. The builders receive no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on the Commerce Clause theory; the Government may enforce the statute subject to its actual elements and exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sweet Home requires significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

The distinct threatened-species protections, section 7 agency and incidental-take provisions, scientific permissions, experimental-population provisions and enforcement channels receive no new application here. Sweet Home's reserved section 6(g)(2) question remains reserved; deciding its terms or application would require the operative official text and applicable State-agreement record. The Court inserts no commercial element into section 9 and announces no categorical immunity for traditional State subjects.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed, 7–2; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the disposition, and Scalia and Thomas would reverse and remand for relief confined to the reviewed applications. The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged habitat modification against this Commerce Clause objection stands, and no evidentiary remand follows. The builders receive no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on that theory; the Government may enforce the statute subject to its actual elements and exceptions. The disposition determines no actual taking, grants or denies no section 10 permit, resolves no independent property claim and does not enlarge the substation's existing permit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied and clarified. Its connection-before-magnitude sequence, enacted-class fit, independent judicial eligibility inquiry, no-insertion rule and rule against manufacturing a commerce connection from aggregate downstream consequences remain controlling. The Court gives fuller definition to the necessary-inclusion route Lopez recognized and reserved, and resolves its open question concerning a materially supported substantial interstate externality; generalized downstream effects remain insufficient.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Preserved. Its machinegun-regulation holding supplies a different example of including local possession within a valid interstate transfer-and-stock regime; no machinegun-specific premise is imported into conservation law. The anti-evasion and material-impairment relationship it applied is assessed for the regulated class within the federal scheme, not species by species, and statutory comprehensiveness alone does not establish it.
- **United States v. Rybar, November 24, 1997:** Preserved as an application of Hale to the demonstrated relationship between possession and prohibited machinegun stock; that relationship is not inferred for the ESA.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for the same fitted regime relationship and the absence of any judicially added interstate element; no machinegun premise is transferred to species protection without support.
- **McCulloch v. Maryland:** Applied: Congress may select means reasonably adapted to the effective execution of an enumerated power, and the Necessary and Proper Clause does not require every included intrastate act to be independently regulable under the Commerce Clause.
- **Wickard v. Filburn:** Applied for examination of a valid regulatory class and the practical relation of included conduct to the federal scheme, with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplying the decisive relationship. Aggregation measures effects after a sufficient connection; it does not manufacture the connection missing from a noneconomic taking class.
- **Perez v. United States:** Applied for class-wide assessment of conduct included in a valid federal regulatory class without requiring every act independently to cross a state boundary.
- **Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association:** Applied for regulation of an integrated class for environmental purposes within a valid interstate scheme; distinguished from treating economic features of particular projects as the enacted taking class.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Applied and left intact as statutory law governing habitat-caused actual injury, ordinary causation and conditional permissions. It decided no universal Commerce authority over intrastate takings.
- **American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Preserved for intentional conduct whose object and operation directly interfere with an actual interstate service market; that distinct mechanism does not establish all environmental applications.
- **United States v. Robinson, March 23, 1998:** Preserved for direct interference with operating commercial assets; the relevant market relationship remains necessary, rather than generalized consequences of local conduct.
- **United States v. Hinds, April 6, 1998:** Applied for the independent work of the enacted predicate and the insufficiency of an owner's economic associations by themselves. Its property-use construction does not insert a use element into the ESA.
- **United States v. Bird, April 13, 1998:** Preserved for direct interference with an actual service market. Its conduct-and-purpose mechanism remains distinct from the two noneconomic-conduct routes addressed here.

Sweet Home's habitat-injury and conditional-permission holdings remain at Environmental Law, Endangered wildlife and habitat-caused injury. The present entry concerns only the Commerce Clause applications.

#### United States v. Morrison / Brzonkala v. Morrison, 529 U.S. 598 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-5 and 99-29, consolidated.
**Decided:** May 15, 2000.

##### Commerce authority for the enacted private-assault action

**Holding:** Congress's findings of the serious economic consequences of gender-motivated violence do not supply the constitutionally adequate interstate connection needed to sustain §13981's presented private-assault action. The enacted class is not directed at an actual interstate market, included as an integral, reasonably adapted means of executing an identified, independently valid federal regulation of interstate commerce, or supported as a substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize; downstream economic magnitude cannot replace that missing connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court on the Commerce Clause, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for this holding and its stated limits; it is a holding of the Court although the judgment rests on Section 5. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurring, would also sustain the action under a rational substantial-class-effects approach; their proposal does not control.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical economic-conduct requirement, universal jurisdictional element, individual border crossing, numerical threshold or findings checklist is adopted. National prevalence alone does not establish a noninternalizable interstate externality, and inclusion in a broad federal package alone does not make a remedy integral to an interstate regime.

##### Section 5 and the relationship to discriminatory state protection

**Holding:** Section 5 sustains §13981's presented civil action against the perpetrator of gender-motivated violence as a corrective substitute remedy for discriminatory state denial of protection. Congress may create an independent civil remedy against private wrongdoers when (1) the constitutional injury involves discriminatory governmental denial of protection or of access to legal remedies; (2) Congress has a materially adequate basis to find that denial systematic; (3) the private conduct reached falls within the class of wrongs affected by that failure; (4) the substitute remedy meaningfully addresses the consequences of the deprivation; and (5) its reach is congruent and proportional. These are conditions on the legislation's validity, not elements a plaintiff must prove.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court on Section 5 and the judgment, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes for this holding, the statutory scope and the disposition. Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissents from the Section 5 holding and the judgment on remedial fit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State action remains the constitutional injury: the private assault is not itself a Fourteenth Amendment violation, and the assailant is not a state actor. State action includes official administration, judicial action and legally operative custom. Congress's findings, including gender-bias task-force reports from at least 21 States, documented stereotyped official responses to gender-motivated violence; the remedy requires qualifying violence and gender animus, does not federalize all assaults and commandeers no state official. National scope is sustained as prophylaxis; it would fail if the record showed only isolated violations or if liability extended substantially beyond the affected class. The Court adds no universal private-defendant, individual-violation, geographic or findings-count prohibition or requirement. Florida Prepaid's Section 5 holding is distinguished, not displaced.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The action concerns a crime of violence committed because of or on the basis of gender and due at least in part to gender animus. The referenced violence includes acts or a series constituting a felony against the person, or a felony against property presenting a serious risk of physical injury, within the State or federal offenses described in 18 U.S.C. §16; it also includes otherwise qualifying conduct excluded only because of the relationship between actor and victim. The definition does not require occurrence in federal maritime, territorial or prison jurisdiction. Gender motivation must be shown by a preponderance of the evidence; random violence unrelated to gender is excluded. No prior criminal complaint, prosecution or conviction is required.

Section 13981(c) includes a person acting under color of state statute, ordinance, regulation, custom or usage and provides compensatory and punitive damages, injunctions, declarations and other appropriate relief. Federal and state courts share jurisdiction. Neither §13981(c) nor 28 U.S.C. §1367, by reason of this claim, confers federal jurisdiction over state-law claims seeking a divorce, alimony, equitable distribution of marital property or child-custody decree. None of these distinct provisions supplies an omitted interstate-commerce or unconstitutional-state-denial element.

Hypothetical color-of-law applications and unpresented severability are not resolved. Title IX litigation, state remedies and other VAWA provisions remain outside the judgment. The Court adds neither an interstate-commerce element nor a state-failure element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the constitutional dismissal of the presented private-assault claim in both consolidated dockets and remand for adjudication under the claim's enacted requirements. Brzonkala must still prove the conduct, gender motivation, causation and any relief; no finding of assault, animus, causation or liability, damages award or new legislative-proof opportunity follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Lopez (April 26, 1995):** Applied its enacted-class, eligible-connection and fit sequence and its rejection of downstream magnitude as a substitute for connection. The Court declines the concurrence's proposed change; the rule remains controlling.
- **American Life League v. Reno; United States v. Bird:** Distinguished direct interference with an actual interstate medical-service market from the enacted class of private assault. Their service-market holdings remain effective.
- **United States v. Robinson; Reno v. Condon:** Distinguished operating business assets with an enacted commerce element and the identified interstate information market. Neither makes a victim's eventual spending or a crime's costs a sufficient connection.
- **United States v. Hale; United States v. Kirk; Hiley v. Barrett; Gillespie v. City of Indianapolis:** Preserved their fitted anti-evasion or material-impairment relationships to valid interstate supply regimes. The Court finds no analogous identified relationship for this action; domestic violence alone was not their constitutional mechanism.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied its necessary-inclusion rule, its independent substantial-cross-border-externality route and their identification, evidentiary and fit requirements. No identified valid interstate regulation includes this action as an integral means; the externality route remains available for noneconomic conduct, national prevalence is not its substitute, and neither route creates a general right to another evidentiary chance.
- **United States v. Hinds:** Distinguished its statutory property-use inquiry from the constitutional mechanism and text of §13981. It supplies no judicially inserted element here.
- **The Civil Rights Cases, 109 U.S. 3 (1883); United States v. Harris, 106 U.S. 629 (1883):** Applied the boundary between state constitutional denial and unsupported private wrong, retaining their recognition of official administration and appropriately adapted corrective legislation. Neither is read as a categorical private-defendant bar; the statutes they held invalid lacked a corrective relationship to a state wrong, which §13981's documented fit supplies. The 1883 Harris is distinct from the later Commerce case bearing that name.
- **United States v. Guest, 383 U.S. 745 (1966):** Drew on the separate opinions recognizing that Section 5 legislation may reach private persons who interfere with Fourteenth Amendment rights. The Court adopts only the narrower corrective route tied to a documented governmental denial; Guest's interstate-travel holding is unaffected.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Applied its congruence-and-proportionality requirement and prohibition on replacing the substantive right while retaining preventive coverage beyond individually proven violations. A corrective remedy may operate against private persons when its fit to the State's constitutional wrong is sufficient. No geographic, temporal or findings-count prerequisite is added.
- **Katzenbach v. Morgan, 384 U.S. 641 (1966):** Applied as Boerne reads it: Congress may adopt a measure aimed at discriminatory governmental treatment that operates through a remedy not itself constitutionally required, when the measure is adapted to that wrong. No substantive-redefinition reading is adopted.
- **Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank:** Its Section 5 fit analysis is applied and distinguished: exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative redress did not supply an adequate preventive relationship for undifferentiated patent liability, while prevention may still reach beyond individually proven violations. §13981 answers a documented, systematic discriminatory denial and is confined to the affected class. Its holdings remain intact.
- **College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board:** Applied the requirement of an actual constitutional injury rather than a statutory tort alone; the injury here is the States' discriminatory denial of equal protection.
- **United States v. Virginia (June 26, 1996):** Applied its requirement of an exceedingly persuasive justification for sex-based official action in identifying the constitutional injury.
- **Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents:** Its reservation of Section 5 supplies no decision of this question.
- **Saenz v. Heldenfels Brothers, Inc. (May 9, 2000):** Distinguished an official's own affirmative removal of protection from private harm not prevented by the State. The Section 5 holding rests on equal protection against discriminatory denial of protection, not on Saenz's due-process rule, and Saenz's direct-claim predicates are not conditions of preventive legislation.
