## City of Indianapolis v. Edmond

[Official opinion, 531 U.S. 32](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-32.pdf); [LII syllabus and writing index](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-1030.ZS.html). Argued October 3; decided November 28, 2000. Historical judgment: affirmance, 6–3. O'Connor wrote for Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Rehnquist dissented, joined by Thomas throughout and Scalia only in Part I. Thomas also wrote separately.

The Court distinguished programmatic suspicionless crime control from ordinary probable-cause stops and preserved lawful border and roadway-safety checkpoints. Footnote 2 did not decide whether a primarily lawful license/sobriety checkpoint could be expanded to detect drugs. Rehnquist Part I relied on objectively legitimate license/sobriety functions, restrained discretion and intrusion, and the absence of evidence that the exterior dog sniff lengthened detention. Part II separately rejected the primary-purpose inquiry for vehicle checkpoint seizures; Scalia did not join that part. Thomas questioned the founding compatibility of indiscriminate stops but followed Sitz and Martinez-Fuerte because overruling had not been briefed. This is historical comparator evidence only; the operative simulated Whren and other entering authorities must independently be checked.

## Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers

[Official opinion, 531 U.S. 57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-57.pdf); [LII syllabus and writing index](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-1038.ZS.html). Argued October 2; decided November 28, 2000. Historical judgment: affirmance, 9–0. Breyer wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurred in the judgment.

The Court used the narrow positive-law-grounded public-policy exception but expressly said it was not restricted to awards directly violating positive law. Scalia and Thomas withheld assent to that proposition; they accepted the remainder of the reasoning and reinstatement conditioned on the award and regulations. The award required a suspension exceeding three months, reimbursement of both arbitration proceedings, continued treatment and random testing, and a signed resignation effective upon another positive test within five years. The federal return-to-safety-sensitive-duty conditions included professional evaluation, compliance with prescribed rehabilitation, a negative return test, and at least six random tests in the first year. Reinstatement did not authorize driving before compliance. Statutory license suspensions concerned actual driving under the influence, not merely a positive test. None of these qualifications may disappear if used in the resulting decision.

## Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph

[Official opinion, 531 U.S. 79](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-79.pdf); [LII syllabus and writing index](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-1235.ZS.html). Argued October 3; decided December 11, 2000. Historical judgment: appealability affirmed unanimously; unenforceability determination reversed 5–4. Rehnquist's Part II was unanimous; Parts I and III were joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Ginsburg's separate writing was joined throughout by Stevens and Souter; Breyer joined only Parts I and III.

Part II applied ordinary finality to dismissal of the entire action; footnote 2 reserved whether a stay should instead have issued. The cost holding required the resisting party to show likely prohibitive expense; it did not decide how detailed the showing must be. Footnote 6 explained why the tendered cost information was speculative. Footnote 7 did not decide the alternative class-action ground, which the court of appeals had not addressed. Ginsburg Part II distinguished forum adequacy from financial accessibility and disputed importing the burden from the former to the latter; Breyer did not join that part. Her Parts I and III agreed on appealability and sought a remand for factual clarification of lender practices and actual access, rather than outright enforcement or invalidation. ## Bush v. Gore

[Official opinion, 531 U.S. 98](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-98.pdf). All 61 PDF pages, including every separate writing and footnote, were read. Argued December 11; decided December 12, 2000. Historical disposition: reverse and remand with immediate mandate; the five-member per curiam coalition comprised Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. The historical December 9 stay is not an admitted current-term action.

Rehnquist's concurrence, joined by Scalia and Thomas, also asserted Article II grounds and joined the per curiam opinion. Stevens dissented with Ginsburg and Breyer. Souter dissented with Breyer throughout, and Stevens and Ginsburg except Part III. Ginsburg dissented with Stevens throughout, and Souter and Breyer only Part I. Breyer dissented with Stevens and Ginsburg except Part I-A-1, and Souter only Part I.

The per curiam opinion found arbitrary ballot treatment and ended the recount on its reading of Florida's intended safe-harbor deadline. Souter Part III instead identified unjustified different treatment of identical ballots and would remand for uniform standards while allowing Florida to try to complete its work before December 18. Breyer Part I-A-1 accepted the need for subsidiary uniform standards in the special circumstances, rejected the unsupported overvote objections, and left the constitutional limits on the content of those standards undecided. Stevens and Ginsburg did not join that constitutional diagnosis but supported an opportunity for further state proceedings if a defect were assumed. All four rejected the terminal remedy. Their agreement with a remand must not be restated as agreement with the per curiam liability reasoning.
