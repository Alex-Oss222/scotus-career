## Event

Alexander v. Sandoval, No. 99-1908. Merits decision, April 24, 2001; argued January 16, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing its judgment affirming an injunction requiring Alabama's Department of Public Safety to accommodate non-English speakers in driver's-license examinations.

The Department receives federal assistance and adopted an English-only examination policy. The district court found prohibited national-origin effects; the court of appeals affirmed. The question is whether private individuals may enforce the Title VI section 602 disparate-impact regulations through the asserted direct action. Review accepts regulatory validity and the unchallenged impact finding, and does not reach a separate section 1983 claim.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment within the granted prospective-enforcement question.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The affirmance recognizes the asserted direct prospective enforcement route and sustains the reviewed injunction on the accepted findings. The injunction remains operative within the scope reviewed. No damages, regulatory invalidation, new section 1983 claim or funding cutoff is ordered.

## Holdings

### Prospective enforcement of Title VI's valid implementing requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VI's established private action permits statutory beneficiaries to obtain prospective relief requiring a covered recipient to comply with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 regulations implementing the statutory protection against discrimination, including the effects regulation assumed valid here. Congress supplies the action through Title VI read as a whole; a regulation cannot independently create an action under an otherwise remediless statute, and plaintiffs must prove the regulation's actual elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation rather than rely on statistical imbalance alone.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this rule, its explanation, the application and remedy, and the treatment of Powell below. These are five direct joins at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's protection and its authorized implementation operate together. Section 601 identifies the persons protected from discrimination in federally assisted programs; section 602 expressly directs implementation of that protection. Cannon supplies the established private-action setting and a contextual inquiry into congressional authorization. Congress's 1986 recognition of legal and equitable remedies in Title VI suits and the Civil Rights Restoration Act's preservation of the enforcement structure provide affirmative statutory context, not an inference from silence alone. Read together, these provisions support prospective enforcement of valid, definite administration requirements protecting those beneficiaries.

Petitioners correctly distinguish an agency's authority to impose a duty from Congress's authorization of a judicial action. But section 602's agency-facing language does not sever its implementing standards from the private action accepted within this particular statutory scheme. Administrative funding enforcement is not made the exclusive means of securing compliance. The additional effects obligation does not become an intentional-discrimination claim under section 601; prospective enforcement rests on the integrated statute. Guardians and Choate explain that substantive distinction without themselves conclusively deciding this action.

McClellan requires contextual authorization and rejects an unsupported Cable Act damages action; it does not impose an express-action-clause prerequisite or erase Title VI's established enforcement background. Central Bank preserves a confined received securities action and confirms the need to examine the particular scheme, without deciding this Title VI question. Franklin separates an action's existence from available relief; an injunction request does not supply its own authority. The practical loss of an effective individual remedy confirms the consequences of the competing readings, but does not create the action.

The Department receives federal assistance. The regulations' validity and the finding of unjustified discriminatory effects are accepted premises of this review. Sandoval may maintain this prospective action, and the reviewed injunction remains supported by that route.

### Powell's direct-action restriction is overruled to the stated extent

**Holding and operative rule:** Powell v. Ridge is overruled insofar as its direct-action holding forecloses the prospective Title VI enforcement just described. Its distinct section 1983 holding is not decided or abrogated, and today's judgment neither authorizes retrospective damages for unintentional discrimination nor disturbs a completed final judgment.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join this express partial overruling and its complete statutory stare-decisis explanation in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court does not distinguish Powell on the ground that Sandoval seeks an injunction. Powell itself rejected prospective enforcement of the additional effects obligation. Nor do a different state policy or a finding instead of an allegation of effects change its action-creation rule. Reconsideration is necessary.

Statutory stare decisis deserves substantial weight because Congress can correct an interpretation and courts ordinarily preserve settled constructions. Powell nevertheless gave insufficient weight to the integrated source of Title VI's accepted beneficiary action. It treated the difference between statutory intentional-discrimination liability and valid preventive implementation as dispositive of prospective enforcement, although that difference does not answer the statute-specific authorization question. The Court corrects that identified error in the enforcement arrangement, rather than making disagreement alone a reason to overrule precedent.

Powell is recent, and no concrete reliance arrangement has been identified here. Neither consideration independently permits overruling a statutory decision. Their significance is that covered recipients already owed the regulatory duties whose prospective enforcement was barred. Restoring that enforcement channel does not prescribe a new substantive language policy, impose retrospective damages, or reopen a final judgment. Reliance on the absence of a beneficiary's action does not outweigh the better-supported statutory arrangement in these circumstances. The Chief Justice acknowledges that he joined Powell's contrary holding.

The Court preserves Powell's distinction between regulatory validity and the source of a private remedy, while rejecting its conclusion about this prospective Title VI action. Powell's separate section 1983 analysis concerns a distinct enforcement vehicle and individual statutory-right inquiry. That question is outside this grant and remains untouched.

### Application, remedy and limits

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed insofar as it recognizes this private prospective action and sustains the injunction requiring accommodation of non-English speakers in driver's-license examinations. The injunction remains operative on the accepted findings and within the scope reviewed. No damages are awarded and no regulation is invalidated.

Regulatory validity, the correctness of the disparate-impact merits determination, damages availability for unintentional discrimination, and a separate section 1983 action are outside the granted question. The accepted section 601 action for intentional discrimination remains, with its own intent requirement; this judgment finds no intentional discrimination. Ordinary application of the announced rule requires proof of the actual regulatory elements, coverage and fitted relief. Those applications are work under the governing rule, not a reservation about whether this category of prospective enforcement exists. The Court creates no new claim, funding cutoff, private administrative-exhaustion requirement or statistical presumption.

## Law After Decision

Effective April 24, 2001, Title VI's established private action permits statutory beneficiaries to enforce prospectively valid, sufficiently definite section 602 requirements implementing their protection against discrimination. This includes valid effects regulations of the kind presented here. Plaintiffs must establish the regulation's actual elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation; statistical imbalance alone is not automatically enough.

Powell's contrary direct-action restriction no longer governs that category of prospective claims. Its separate section 1983 holding remains, as do section 601's intentional-discrimination requirement, valid administrative enforcement and the distinction between a regulatory duty and the congressional source of a private action. This decision neither creates private actions under otherwise remediless statutes nor resolves damages for unintentional effects. Its rule governs covered claims; it does not reopen completed final judgments.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas dissents, joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. They would reverse insofar as the injunction rests on this direct regulatory-effects action and remand for ordinary proceedings on genuinely preserved claims and defenses, without creating a claim or guaranteeing amendment.

The dissent applies Powell's directly controlling prospective-relief rule. Section 601 supplies the accepted private protection against intentional discrimination; section 602 directs agency implementation and public compliance machinery. The distinction between those statutory functions matters because a valid additional effects obligation does not itself demonstrate congressional authorization of the broader private action. Cannon permits contextual implication but does not establish every regulatory action, and Guardians and Choate do not conclusively settle this enforcement route. The beneficiary-and-implementation argument is substantial, but Powell already rejected it in this precise prospective setting. A request for an injunction cannot cure the absence of an authorized action; Franklin concerns relief after that antecedent question is answered.

The dissent preserves McClellan's contextual inquiry and Central Bank's actual received securities action. It adopts neither a universal express-action-clause requirement nor a categorical denial of every regulation's private significance. Its sufficient ground is Title VI-specific. Regulatory validity, intentional-discrimination enforcement and authorized agency remedies remain intact.

Statutory stare decisis adds a separate objection to the Court's reconsideration. Congress can alter the rule, and a different policy, a proved rather than alleged effect, the decision's youth and continued disagreement do not change the authorization question. The dissent does not find that a recipient actually arranged its affairs in reliance on Powell. It considers the Court's account of the existing duty insufficient to overcome the distinction between a duty and a private cause of action. It would leave the accepted regulations in force while withdrawing this action as the injunction's legal foundation. It reaches no separate funding-notice ground, finds no discriminatory intent and supplies no damages, funding sanction or section 1983 claim.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment stands insofar as it recognizes the prospective action and sustains the reviewed injunction. The accommodation injunction remains operative on the accepted findings and within its reviewed scope. The Court retains no return condition and directs no new merits determination, damages proceeding or agency enforcement action.

## Event

Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, No. 99-1408. Merits decision, April 24, 2001; argued December 4, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, reviewing its en banc affirmance of summary judgment for defendants on a section 1983 challenge to custody for an observed fine-only seatbelt misdemeanor. The question is whether probable cause alone establishes the reasonableness of the additional transport and confinement.

In March 1997, Atwater and her two young children were unbelted in the front of a pickup. Probable cause is conceded. She lacked physical license and insurance papers but supplied her license number and address; Turek knew her as a local resident. A friend took the children. Atwater was handcuffed, transported, booked and confined for about an hour before bond release. She later paid a $50 seatbelt fine after a no-contest plea; other charges were dismissed. The custody suit does not challenge those convictions.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment and remands the custody claim under the governing actual-enforcement rule.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

The categorical judgment on the additional-custody claim is set aside. The shared mandate returns the case under the actual-enforcement rule, preserving genuine record disputes, actual defenses and separate liability predicates. Four supporters would additionally hold the supported account constitutionally insufficient; that stronger conclusion has no majority. No new factual hearing, damages award or change to the seatbelt convictions is ordered.

## Holdings

### Additional custody for a known fine-only criminal offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor does not, by itself, justify additional station-house transport and confinement. That added custody must reasonably serve an actual enforcement function, considering supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence-protection or necessary-processing needs; the examples are nonexclusive, are not presumed, and require neither the least intrusive imaginable alternative nor citation whenever conceivable.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to this rule, the rejection of categorical summary judgment and the common mandate. The rule extends Ricci's civil-licensing holding to the fine-only criminal enforcement presented here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority to stop a person and the reasonable extent of custody are different questions. Ricci requires an actual enforcement connection for added station custody after a fine-only civil violation. The criminal label does not, by itself, justify the same added restraint where imprisonment is unavailable as punishment. The State's penalty choice reinforces the need to assess the scale of the seizure, without granting immunity from arrest.

Whren preserves an ordinary initial stop on objective probable cause while retaining independent limits on greater restraint. Knowles keeps search consequences separate from the antecedent lawful-arrest question. Robinson and Gustafson presuppose a qualifying lawful arrest; their incident-search rules cannot supply the missing justification for disputed custody. Wilson v. Arkansas treats relevant history as part of reasonableness without constitutionalizing every state authorization. A categorical historical breach-of-peace rule is unnecessary to decide this actual-function question.

Clear administration is a legitimate interest. But optional booking paperwork cannot conclusively establish the need for the custody that creates it. The inquiry permits practical judgments without hindsight perfection or proof that every alternative would fail. The lower court's probable-cause rule omitted that inquiry.

The record must include both the missing physical documents and the supplied license number, address and known local residence. The earlier warning followed recognition that the child was belted; it does not establish another seatbelt offense. The friend took the children. These facts cannot be replaced by assumptions of failed verification, flight risk, continuing danger or compulsory station processing. Neither berating nor a routine booking preference alone supplies an enforcement need. The correct rule defeats defendants' categorical entitlement to judgment, while the Justices differ on the ultimate application and the remaining record determinations.

### The common judgment does not establish a controlling application conclusion

**Judgment and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit judgment sustaining the categorical custody summary judgment is vacated, and the custody claim returns for proceedings under the actual-enforcement rule. The return permits genuinely supported disputes and preserved defenses, requires no new evidentiary hearing, invents no enforcement justification and reopens no established fact by speculation; it does not invalidate the uncontested seatbelt convictions or award damages.

**Authority and application limits:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer expressly support this vacatur and remand. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer also conclude that the supported plaintiff-favorable account does not establish a sufficient custody function and would reverse on that ground. Stone-Zsela does not join that application: he requires actual verification and custody-need determinations under the rule. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the arrest reasonable on their stated grounds and would affirm. There is no majority holding that this custody was constitutional or unconstitutional; the four-Justice application and the distinct concurrence cannot be combined through Marks into one.

Personal qualified immunity remains a separate conduct-date inquiry using March 1997 law; Ricci, Knowles, McArthur and this decision supply no retrospective notice. Municipal liability separately requires an attributable policy or custom, applicable fault, causation and injury. No final finding resolves those predicates. Other punishment classes are outside the presented question. Ordinary application of the rule and liability work under it are distinct from the Court's lack of a controlling answer on this custody's ultimate reasonableness.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights, May 4, 1998:** Its actual-enforcement requirement is extended from fine-only civil licensing custody to the added custody for a known fine-only criminal offense. Its rejection of categorical arrest immunity, least restrictive means and a universal citation duty remains. Its record-specific application fracture supplies no automatic application answer here.
- **Whren v. United States, June 10, 1996:** Its objective initial-stop rule is preserved and its independent limits on greater restraint applied. Hostility does not replace the objective enforcement inquiry.
- **Knowles v. Iowa, December 8, 1998:** Its distinction between actual lawful custody and search consequences is applied; it supplies no authority to justify an arrest by its ensuing search or booking effects.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their incident-search rules remain conditioned on a qualifying lawful arrest. Neither decides the contested antecedent custody authority here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas, May 22, 1995:** Relevant history remains part of constitutional reasonableness, without turning state arrest authorization into a complete federal justification.
- **Illinois v. McArthur, February 20, 2001:** Its particularized residential-preservation holding remains at that scope. Concrete need and fitted execution inform the comparison; its home setting and approximate duration create no traffic-booking permission or safe harbor.
- **Wilson v. Layne; Wallis v. Spencer:** Conduct-date immunity and independent municipal responsibility remain distinct from constitutional merits. Neither supplies a present finding of immunity, City policy, fault or causation here.

## Law After Decision

Ricci's actual-enforcement rule now governs added station transport and confinement for known fine-only criminal offenses as well as its prior civil setting. Probable cause and local arrest permission alone do not establish reasonableness; genuinely supported enforcement needs and the scale of restraint must be assessed. Neither a universal citation requirement nor categorical arrest immunity follows.

The Court supplies no controlling application conclusion about this custody, immunity or municipal liability. The four-Justice conclusion that the accepted account is constitutionally insufficient remains noncontrolling; the actual-function rule and bounded corrective mandate control further proceedings. Ordinary fact, defense and remedial work must respect the supported record rather than manufacture new justification.

## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, in Part II.** These four conclude that the supported plaintiff-favorable account fails the governing constitutional standard. Atwater supplied identifying information, was known locally, acknowledged the offense and arranged for a friend to take the children. General child-safety and appearance purposes do not establish why handcuffing, transport, booking and cell confinement served a sufficient added function. Missing physical papers remain relevant but do not establish inability to identify her, and the earlier warning supplies no recidivism premise. Booking cannot prove its own antecedent necessity merely by generating paperwork.

Their conclusion applies Ricci's functional rule to this record; it does not require citation in every case or impossibility of every alternative. Their Ricci application reservation was record-specific, and they do not repeat it here. They prefer reversal on this constitutional ground but expressly support the common vacatur because it removes the categorical judgment and returns actual disputes and preserved defenses under their rule. They do not join a reservation that the same established facts might suffice. Actual liability, March 1997 immunity, City responsibility and lawful relief remain distinct, with no speculative factual reopening or obligatory hearing.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment.** He joins the actual-function extension and categorical-rule correction but does not decide this custody's ultimate reasonableness. Missing papers may justify verification; they do not conclusively justify each later restraint, particularly in light of the supplied number, address and known residence. Berating and a routine booking preference alone cannot justify confinement. He would require the lower courts to determine whether supported identification or other practical enforcement needs justified the additional restraint, without inventing a verification failure or demanding a perfect alternative. His remand separately preserves conduct-date immunity and municipal policy and causation. He does not join Part II's present constitutional-insufficiency conclusion, award damages or disturb the conviction.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter.** Ordinary processing of an observed criminal charge, with the actual bounded execution here, supplies a sufficient enforcement relationship. The restraint involved handcuffing, transportation, booking and approximately an hour of confinement pending bond, not an unrelated investigation or a claimed permission for any duration or method. Their Ricci dissent treated ordinary same-offense processing as legally meaningful without proof that citation was impossible. That earlier case's documents and continuing-business problem are not transferred as facts here. This is a legal appraisal of criminal processing, not a finding of a special identity, appearance or safety need or compulsory station procedure. Supplied identification and local residence weaken those distinct factual rationales without eliminating ordinary criminal enforcement. The four would affirm on constitutional merits, leaving immunity and municipal predicates unadjudicated. Excessive force, unrelated additional detention and unjustified duration retain their own limits; no fixed permissible interval follows.

**Scalia, additionally dissenting, joined by Souter.** They would also sustain an ordinary criminal-arrest rule under which probable cause for an observed criminal offense and ordinary enforcement processing suffice, subject to independent execution, duration and enforcement-relationship limits. They distinguish Ricci's civil violation and give administrable criminal-arrest authority independent weight. The rule does not depend on predictions that an identified local driver will evade court, on subjective motive or on search consequences. They reject an added individualized showing merely because punishment is fine-only. Kennedy and Thomas do not join this broader categorical proposition; their dissent rests on the narrower actual processing and execution ground.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit with the categorical custody judgment vacated. The lower courts must apply the actual-enforcement rule to the supported record and address actual remaining issues, personal immunity under March 1997 law, and municipal attribution, fault, causation and injury as applicable. No particular hearing, newly invented defense, damages amount, punitive award or invalidation of the seatbelt convictions is directed. No Supreme Court return condition is retained.

## Event

Sterling v. Borough of Minersville; Third Circuit No. 99-1768, judgment reported at 232 F.3d 190. Merits decision, April 24, 2001. On review of the Third Circuit judgment sustaining the reviewed denial of personal immunity. The questions concern an adult arrestee's protection against an operative threat of intimate family disclosure and whether that application was sufficiently clear in April 1997. Hoban separately disputes his participation.

On April 17, 1997, police arrested eighteen-year-old Marcus Wayman and a seventeen-year-old companion for underage drinking. At the station, Wilinsky allegedly required Wayman to tell his grandfather his suspected sexual orientation or face disclosure by the officer, accompanied by a biblical admonition. Whether the youths admitted homosexual activity is disputed. Wayman died by suicide after release. The estate asserts constitutional and state claims; the unlawful-arrest claim was eliminated below, and this review does not restore it.

All nine Justices participated in merits consideration and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Holdings

### I. An operative official ultimatum can invade intimate confidentiality

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer violates the Fourteenth Amendment protection of intimate confidentiality by imposing a credible official ultimatum requiring an adult to disclose suspected sexual orientation to a family member or face disclosure by the officer, when no legitimate governmental purpose supports either course. The present infringement is operative compelled surrender of an objectively intimate confidence; completed officer disclosure is unnecessary, but an idle insult or nonoperative threat does not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the right, application on the supported assumptions and stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an officer cannot evade protected confidentiality by making the citizen perform the disclosure. Sheets protects objectively intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius recognizes that an official disclosure condition can itself burden intimate information before publication occurs. Today's bounded extension concerns an arrest-backed command to reveal an adult's intimate life to family, rather than every threat or a general right to keep all information secret.

The supported account describes an operative choice imposed through official arrest authority: Wayman must disclose his suspected orientation or the officer will disclose it. The alleged demand therefore does more than predict possible future misconduct. Its coercion presently targets the protected confidence. No supported drinking-enforcement purpose connects disclosure to his grandfather with this adult's arrest. Religious admonition supplies no policing justification. Disputed admissions do not establish that the information was already public.

Doe preserves the distinction between confined, justified benefits administration and an unrelated use; it neither requires public dissemination for every claim nor itself decides this extension. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection, and Nixon recognizes personal privacy without making ownership decisive. Their foundations and the actual holdings of Sheets and Denius support this confined coercion rule, not an all-purpose informational guarantee.

The officers' strongest objection distinguishes an unexecuted threat from a completed deprivation. That distinction remains valid for an idle remark or a demand without operative effect. It does not answer a credible, arrest-backed command to surrender the confidence itself. The plaintiff must still prove the actual demand, responsible actor, coercive effect, causation and compensable injury. This legal ruling finds none of those disputed historical facts after trial.

### II. The supported no-purpose ultimatum defeats legal immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** On the supported plaintiff-favorable account, the law effective on April 17, 1997 gave sufficiently particularized objective notice that using arrest authority to compel an adult's intimate family disclosure for no official purpose was unconstitutional; Wilinsky is not entitled to qualified immunity on that legal ground. An identical orientation-and-ultimatum case is unnecessary, but operative coercion and the complete absence of an official disclosure purpose are material limits, and subjective bad faith alone does not defeat immunity.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to the notice ground and affirmance. O'Connor and Kennedy join the present merits rule but not this conduct-date conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson retain an objective, particularized notice test. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law, not merely an identical earlier case or the authorities cited below. Sheets had taken effect in April 1996. Its completed-publication setting differs from the present ultimatum, but it established protection for objectively intimate information against exposure unrelated to the official purpose for access. The officer cannot avoid that boundary by coercing the person to perform the surrender.

The earlier Third Circuit authorities reinforce the distinction between confidential information and a genuine official need. Westinghouse addressed intimate medical material; Fraternal Order of Police addressed justified employment inquiry and disclosure safeguards; the 1995 circuit Doe opinion evaluated an actual health-plan monitoring interest. Those cases do not themselves decide this ultimatum, and their full balancing formulations are not adopted. Their justified-use boundaries, together with Sheets, make this no-purpose demand sufficiently apparent.

Walls is substantial contrary authority, but its employment-screening inquiry differs from an arrest-based command to disclose to family without a policing purpose. Decisions distinguishing ordinary threats from completed injuries likewise do not authorize operative compelled self-disclosure. The Court does not derive notice simply from the demand's offensiveness or an officer's subjective understanding.

Denius supplies present merits law, not April 1997 notice. The Supreme Court's May 1997 Doe decision, subsequent Shahar and Wilson v. Layne decisions, and still later authorities supply no retrospective notice either. The relevant cumulative law already existed when the alleged act occurred. Denial of the legal defense permits ordinary proceedings; it does not prove participation, causation of death or damages, and it orders no automatic trial.

### III. Hoban's factual participation challenge is outside immediate review

**Holding and operative rule:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal cannot determine whether pretrial evidence sufficiently establishes an officer's participation in the alleged conduct. Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention is dismissed from immediate appellate review; a genuinely separable legal defense may be decided only on the proper supported assumptions, without choosing between disputed accounts.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion has all nine Justices' direct joins.

Hunter leaves legal immunity for judicial decision under ordinary summary-judgment constraints, and Elder requires consideration of relevant law without authorizing factual retrial. Hoban's denial of actual participation therefore does not permit appellate selection of the true account. His role remains for the proper trial-level proceedings, while any properly separable assumed-fact legal issue retains its own lawful footing.

## Event

## Holdings

### Available original review does not become a renewed section 2255 forum

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2255 does not permit a prisoner to reopen an expired counseled state conviction for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims merely because that conviction enhances a current federal sentence, where meaningful original review was available and the prisoner identifies no external impediment to using it. A genuinely available earlier opportunity lost through unexcused failure is not an unavailable remedy; the Court does not declare the old conviction valid or decide the different case of faultless loss of meaningful original review.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five directly join this available-opportunity rule, its finality explanation, application and affirmance. Each Justice's separate position beyond it remains distinct.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the current federal sentence and the expired predicate have different lawful review histories. Custis limits constitutional attacks at ACCA sentencing, but expressly leaves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases open. Extending its channel distinction into section 2255 requires today's statutory holding; it is not an application of an already decided universal bar.

Section 2255 expressly permits correction of an unlawful current federal sentence. That text does not erase the original judgment's review structure or make every later enhancement a renewed opportunity to litigate constitutional objections that could have been presented in the proper forum. Finality, the original jurisdiction's responsibility and the difficulty of reconstructing old proceedings support enforcing that distinction when a genuine earlier opportunity existed. Administrative inconvenience alone does not establish that every alleged unlawful sentence must escape review.

Daniels's stronger incentive to contest the predicates after committing the federal offense does not establish that the earlier forum was unavailable. His counsel identifies neither a prior attempt to pursue these constitutional challenges while in custody nor an external obstacle preventing one. The Court does not infer deliberate personal choice from every attorney failure; it decides the opportunity and allegations actually presented. Parke preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful proof rules, not an irrebuttable finding that every final plea was valid.

Maleng distinguishes expired independent custody from collateral consequences. Garlotte concerns a continuous unexpired consecutive series and does not convert these wholly expired state sentences into present custody under those judgments. Daniels's current federal custody supplies the section 2255 vehicle, but not an automatic right to relitigate each predicate. His involuntary counseled pleas and deficient-representation allegations therefore cannot proceed through this motion on the review history presented.

### Complete unconstitutional counsel deprivation remains distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A predicate obtained through unconstitutional complete denial of constitutionally required counsel, without valid waiver, remains subject to challenge through section 2255 under applicable procedural requirements and upon a showing of its adverse effect on the current sentence. Deficient representation and an involuntary counseled plea are not that exception; ordinary procedural-default requirements remain, generally including presentation of the counsel-deprivation claim at federal sentencing or a legally sufficient excuse.

**Authority:** The same five Justices directly join the preservation of this exception, its procedural boundaries and its inapplicability to Daniels's allegations.

**Controlling explanation:** Gideon establishes the entitlement to appointed counsel when constitutionally required. Burgett and Tucker preserve the special restriction on subsequent reliance on a conviction obtained through unconstitutional complete deprivation of that protection. Custis retains this distinct exception while refusing to turn every alleged defect in counseled proceedings into an ACCA sentencing issue.

The difference concerns the kind of constitutional deprivation, not a judgment that ineffective assistance or involuntary pleas are unimportant. Daniels alleges defective counseled proceedings; he identifies no complete unconstitutional deprivation. The exception therefore supplies no route for his present motion. Nor does recognition of a qualifying claim dispense with applicable procedure, proof of invalidity or the connection between the predicate and the current enhancement. No such qualifying defect or adverse-effect determination is made here.

### The absent faultless-review issue has no controlling resolution

**Scope and authority:** No Court holding resolves whether section 2255 permits review when, through no fault of the prisoner, meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated. O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas leave that absent situation open. Stone-Zsela affirmatively adopts a faultless-loss exception in his separate access rule. Scalia joins the actual available-review holding but supplies an additional statutory objection to section 2255 as the forum for an outstanding state conviction's non-Gideon defect. A statement about the Court's limited holding does not attribute personal reservation or support for an exception to either concurrence.

Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg advocate a broader express-section-2255 route, while Breyer relies on a distinct proposal to reconsider the original sentencing restriction. Those positions do not establish five direct joins to a common faultless-loss exception, and no Marks synthesis makes one controlling. Daniels alleges no external impediment placing that issue before the Court.

### Remedy and other boundaries

The Ninth Circuit judgment denying this section 2255 motion is affirmed. The 176-month federal sentence remains operative against these claims; the Court orders no new sentencing hearing, state-predicate invalidation, evidentiary hearing or sentence reduction. The constitutional validity of the 1978 and 1981 pleas is not reached because the claims fail the governing access rule.

Statutory classification, counting and exclusions concerning a predicate are distinct from the constitutional attack decided here. No such objection is newly adjudicated or revived. A predicate independently invalidated in proper proceedings presents a different application question; any later request must identify its authorized vehicle, applicable prerequisites, effect on the remaining qualifying predicates and available relief, without guaranteed reopening. No current sentencing-fact jury claim is presented. Coss uses a different state-prisoner vehicle and receives its own decision; no companion result supplies a premise here.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the common available-review holding, counsel exception and affirmance, and additionally adopts an affirmative faultless-loss exception as part of his governing access rule. Section 2255 review should remain available when meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated through no fault of the prisoner, subject to applicable procedure, proof of constitutional defect and adverse effect on present custody. This is his present legal position, not merely a suggestion that an exception might be recognized later.

The distinction is between meaningful first review and repetitive litigation. Custis's forum rule and reservation do not establish that every completed conviction had an effective first opportunity, while Maleng's custody requirement must still be respected. A later enhancement creates a new incentive but does not make an earlier available remedy unavailable. Conversely, administrative inconvenience should not insulate a demonstrably unlawful current sentence from every authorized challenge. He does not equate every attorney failure with the prisoner's deliberate choice. Daniels identifies no complete counsel deprivation or external obstruction to meaningful first review, so even this affirmative exception does not allow his claims. Stone's exception has no controlling majority and is not borrowed from a decision in Coss. He orders no fresh sentencing or predicate invalidation.

**Scalia, concurring.** He joins the actual available-review rule and finality reasoning, but adds a statutory objection: section 2255's remedy for an unlawful federal sentence does not itself authorize adjudicating an outstanding state predicate's non-Gideon constitutional defect. Custis supports distinguishing an operative conviction from a collateral objection, although its actual sentencing holding did not already decide every expired-custody setting. A possible constitutional duty to provide review would concern the rendering jurisdiction and would not identify section 2255 as the remedy. He does not affirmatively endorse a section 2255 faultless-review exception. The Court's statement of what it has not decided is a scope description, not his agreement that such an exception exists. He orders no alternative forum remedy and decides no absent constitutional claim.

**Souter, dissenting, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** Section 2255 expressly authorizes review of the current federal sentence when constitutional error allegedly makes that sentence unlawful. Custis's restriction at the sentencing proceeding supplies no sufficient statutory basis for a new categorical exclusion from this express collateral remedy. Custis's available original-custody setting and reservation make the difference particularly important. The dissent's reading permits examination of an allegedly unlawful enhancement without treating Daniels as still in custody under the old state judgments.

Finality and stale evidence matter through applicable procedure and lawful proof rules; they do not justify the additional categorical bar. Parke preserves regularity and proof, Garlotte separates custody from remaining conditions, and Fiore and Glover distinguish an actual constitutional wrong and its causal effect from access requirements. The dissent preserves applicable defaults and does not automatically import all section 2254 exhaustion requirements into section 2255. It would reverse and remand for authorized consideration, not find either plea invalid, guarantee a hearing or order a lower sentence. The remaining qualifying predicates and the actual effect on lawful federal punishment would still require adjudication.

**Breyer, dissenting.** He also would reverse the categorical denial and remand, but on a distinct statutory and practical ground. Custis should be reconsidered: the sentencing scheme is better read to permit properly raised constitutional challenges to predicates at sentencing under lawful proof rules. His earlier opinion in United States v. Paleo supplies that approach. A prohibition at the original federal sentencing then generates restrictive or increasingly complex collateral exceptions; section 2255 is an awkward substitute for deciding the predicate issue when the enhancement is imposed. His disposition agreement with Souter does not make him an automatic join in every aspect of Souter's construction of section 2255. He proposes reconsideration, not an accomplished overruling, and establishes no plea defect, excuse of default, hearing entitlement or sentence reduction.

**Breyer, concurring.** He leaves a constitutionally based additional exception undecided. Before resolving that issue, he would examine whether the present petition was effectively the first and only forum for review, using the actual state process and the explanation for its long lapse. The mere pendency of the state petition proves neither a meaningful opportunity nor its absence. He does not affirm a broad constitutional entitlement to indirect review or adopt a universal bar. He also refuses a present no-effect adjudication: removal of points does not dispose of discretionary influence, and the lower findings and disputed presentation require proper appellate treatment. His individual first-forum concern does not enlarge the judgment into an unconditional inquiry; the operative former section 2254(b) direction applies only if an identified requirement is shown actually unresolved. No exception, fault finding, new-hearing requirement or immediate sentence relief follows from his concurrence.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** He would recognize a limited present-custody route when meaningful original review of the expired predicate was frustrated through no fault of the prisoner, subject to ordinary applicable procedural requirements, constitutional invalidity and proof of adverse effect on current custody. Finality protects adjudications and usable opportunities; it should not treat an unprovided adjudication as one already received. Custis left this different situation open, and former section 2254(b) recognizes an absent or ineffective state process. A nominally pending petition need not require indefinite waiting, but fourteen years of unexplained inaction alone does not establish diligence, blame or satisfaction of the applicable requirements.

He maintains that responsibility for the failed original review must be determined in every event. The inquiry should examine the actual filings, notices, testimony and explanation for the delay; it should not demand a statistical demonstration that fourteen years is excessive or attribute the lapse without supported facts. Removal of points bears on one causal route, while possible influence on the sentence selected within the range remains distinct. His preferred rule would permit an appropriately conditioned opportunity for lawful resentencing only upon satisfaction of its conditions, with no automatic release or freestanding vacation of the expired judgment. He nevertheless agrees with the exact narrower mandate and preserves his additional responsibility inquiry as his separate position. He joins no lead reasoning, and his proposed access rule does not become the Court's law.

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in Part I.** They would apply the general restriction against reopening an independently expired predicate through a later section 2254 sentence challenge after ordinary review opportunities, preserving the exception for unconstitutional complete denial of required counsel without valid waiver and the ordinary habeas prerequisites. Ineffective representation is a different claim. Finality and the practical difficulty of reviewing old convictions support the channel limitation; an excuse from exhaustion does not itself create an indirect-review channel. They accept the current-sentence construction without finding the old judgments valid, the petition abandoned or Coss responsible for the delay. Their proposed reversal removes the present federal retry-or-resentence direction while leaving independently authorized state review under its own law.

**O'Connor's Part II, joined by Kennedy.** They leave open whether faultless denial of meaningful earlier review permits an additional route. The current sentencing-effect issue can defeat relief even assuming such a route. They adopt no new innocence exception in a case presenting ineffective assistance rather than a newly discovered innocence claim. Scalia and Thomas do not join this additional-exception discussion.

**O'Connor's Part III, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.** They would independently reverse because the challenged convictions did not increase the actual six-to-twelve-year sentence. They accept consideration but regard the adverse-effect inference as clearly erroneous. They rely on the combined removal of score and range effects, the present aggravated assault, independent supported juvenile and adult history, and the sentencing judge's stated concerns with the present crime, community protection, criminal disposition, rehabilitation, testimony and continued lawbreaking. They conclude that the same sentence would have been selected without the challenged convictions; no single factor supplies a general harmlessness rule.

Their appraisal does not treat dropped-charge arrests as proved misconduct, count the challenged convictions again as independent valid history or invent an express same-sentence disclaimer. They acknowledge the contrary force of actual consideration and the absence of an explicit counterfactual sentence. Glover forbids disregarding a proved increase as too small; it does not require finding an increase whenever a conviction is mentioned. They regard the essential adverse-effect determination already addressed below as properly reviewable, without asserting that the prosecutor actually presented a within-range argument in the panel brief or announcing a general power to disregard waiver. Their no-effect ground is an appellate legal assessment, not an added neutral fact. Scalia does not join it.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded for the Court of Appeals to determine whether any pursued objection that the 1986 convictions did not increase the sentence selected within the range may be heard or was waived and, if it may be heard, to apply proper appellate review; and, only if an identified requirement of former 28 U.S.C. §2254(b) is shown to remain actually unresolved, to resolve that requirement on the materials bearing on it. The existing deficient-performance, trial-prejudice and consideration determinations keep their lawful scope unless lawfully displaced. The judgment directs no denial on a newly assumed absence of sentencing effect, no automatic release and no freestanding vacation of an expired judgment.

The case returns to the Third Circuit. Vacatur removes the existing retry-or-resentence direction; further disposition must follow the confined mandate and applicable law. The current sentence is not reduced by this judgment. No unconditional inquiry into responsibility for the failed original review, new evidentiary hearing or return to this Court is directed.

## Event

Randell v. Johnson, judgment below reported at 227 F.3d 300. Merits decision, April 25, 2001. On review of the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal of an in forma pauperis section 1983 damages complaint. The question is whether favorable invalidation bars a former prisoner's excessive-confinement claim when federal habeas relief is unavailable, considering necessary invalidity and realistic prior review opportunity.

Randell alleges that parole-warrant confinement from September 27, 1996 through June 25, 1997 was not credited and that he had to serve the same period again. He filed on June 18, 1999, after the disputed confinement ended, when federal habeas concerning it was unavailable, and requested $1,000 per excess day. The district court called the action frivolous; the Fifth Circuit affirmed for failure to state a claim. Neither ruling determined actual overconfinement.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in merits consideration and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the screening judgment and remands for claim-specific application of the governing rules.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

All nine support correction of the categorical screening dismissal and a claim-specific necessary-invalidity inquiry. Five—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer—add the controlling no-realistic-review exception and the complete conditional remand. The four remaining Justices withhold those additional grounds and directions. No finding of overconfinement or damages award accompanies the vacatur.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacatur and claim-classification remand, 9–0 | Necessary-invalidity inquiry and rejection of the categorical screening basis |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts II and III | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Breyer | Governing exception and conditional remand; five direct joins | No-realistic-review exception, unexcused-loss boundary, actual opportunity inquiry and resulting procedure |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg | Joins Part I and vacatur; supports classification remand | Retains invalidation for an actually contradictory claim despite release alone; withholds the affirmative exception and its directions |

## Holdings

### I. The precise damages claim must necessarily contradict an operative determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Favorable invalidation is not required for a section 1983 claim whose success is compatible with the valid sentence or custody determination, including a claim that officials executed a valid sentence contrary to its terms. Before imposing the prerequisite, the court must identify the operative judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and determine whether success on the actual civil theory necessarily establishes its invalidity; a sentence-administration label neither establishes nor avoids that consequence.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by all eight Associate Justices. All nine directly adopt this classification rule, rejection of the categorical screening ground, and vacatur and remand for the necessary comparison.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the legal consequence of the claim matters more than its label or its connection to custody. Heck distinguishes a necessarily conviction-negating claim from an independent injury compatible with the criminal judgment. Edwards applies that distinction to earned-credit proceedings: calling a claim procedural does not save it if success necessarily defeats an outstanding adjudication, but a genuinely independent injury requires its own analysis. Wolff preserves compatible civil relief, and Preiser's release channel does not turn every damages complaint concerning confinement into habeas.

Randell alleges that officials failed to credit parole-warrant confinement and required him to serve the same time again. That allegation could seek enforcement of a valid sentence, or it could necessarily contradict an operative credit determination. The screening record does not sufficiently identify the determination or establish that relationship. A court cannot assume every alleged excess day negates a judgment, nor assume every administrative-execution claim leaves the controlling custody determination untouched.

The lower court also treated Heck as already deciding a universal former-prisoner bar. The actual rule reserved that population and persons without realistic custody-review opportunity; Spencer preserved those reservations without adopting an affirmative civil exception. Existing precedent therefore did not supply the categorical ground used below. This error requires vacatur, not a finding that Randell was overconfined or already possesses a successful damages claim.

### II. Genuine loss of any realistic custody-review opportunity permits the civil claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When a former prisoner's damages claim necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination, prior favorable invalidation is not required if the claimant never had a realistic lawful opportunity to contest that confinement before custody review became unavailable, or lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. A claimant who lost a genuinely usable opportunity through an unexcused failure remains subject to the prerequisite; release or present federal habeas unavailability alone is neither the exception nor qualifying invalidation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Five directly adopt this new bounded rule within Heck's reservation, its statutory explanation and its unexcused-loss limit. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a rule coordinating available statutory remedies cannot require a route the claimant never realistically could use. Heck's controlling statutory explanation reconciles section 1983 with a lawful custody-review channel. Preiser supports that coordination where the remedies intersect. Patsy rejects a general requirement to exhaust state procedures before seeking section 1983 relief. The common-law favorable-termination analogy helps implement the statutory arrangement; it does not create an absolute civil disability whenever confinement once occurred.

The defendants correctly object that release cannot reward strategic delay or authorize a person to bypass a real review opportunity. The rule therefore turns on realistic availability and the circumstances of its loss, not custody status alone. An unexcused failure to use a genuine opportunity leaves the prerequisite in place. The exception concerns meaningful first review, not an entitlement to repeat unsuccessful review, and expiration does not invalidate the underlying determination.

The inquiry must use supported facts about custody, notice, court access, timing and the ability to pursue the particular challenge. Neither absence of an earlier successful writ nor the theoretical existence of another procedure proves what opportunity actually existed. An authorized qualifying invalidation route may matter where the prerequisite applies, but requiring proof that every conceivable state process is unavailable would recreate the general exhaustion rule Patsy rejects.

Spencer did not already grant this exception; it decided the live controversy in a habeas case and expressly preserved the civil question. This decision resolves the legal consequences of genuine unavailability and unexcused loss. Randell's actual circumstances remain for ordinary adjudication under that rule, without an assumed finding of diligence, abandonment or deliberate delay.

### III. The remand applies the rule without deciding overconfinement or damages

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Circuit's screening affirmance is vacated and the case remanded to compare the pleaded credit theory with the actual custody determination and, if necessary invalidity is established, decide realistic review opportunity and legally excused loss from supported facts. A compatible claim or one within the no-realistic-review exception may proceed under ordinary civil requirements; an otherwise covered claim lacking qualifying invalidation must be dismissed without prejudice for lack of present cognizability, not as an adjudication that the alleged misconduct did not occur.

**Authority:** All nine support vacatur and the classification return in Part I. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer directly join Part III's complete conditional directions, including application of Part II's exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal habeas concerning the disputed confinement was unavailable when Randell filed on June 18, 1999. That fact does not resolve earlier opportunity, a previously adjudicated credit issue, a usable qualifying invalidation route or the legality of the confinement. The court must permit ordinary consideration of pertinent allegations and supported facts, without assuming an unexcused bypass or imposing general state-remedy exhaustion.

Where the invalidation prerequisite continues to apply, Heck recognizes reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal or a federal habeas writ calling the determination into question. Failed review and mere expiration do not qualify. The covered claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation, so the proper remedy for present noncognizability is dismissal without prejudice, not conversion to habeas, an exhaustion stay or an invented tolling regime. Accrual and limitations for a claim outside that prerequisite remain for their independently applicable law.

Section 1915(e)(2) still requires dismissal at any time, notwithstanding any fee paid, for an untrue poverty allegation or an action or appeal that is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim or seeks money from an immune defendant. Correcting the legal failure-to-state-a-claim ground does not decide any other actual screening ground. Nor does failure to satisfy a legal prerequisite establish factual frivolousness.

Individual responsibility, constitutional violation, causation, injury, immunity and properly applicable preclusion remain separate. The requested $1,000 per day establishes no measure or amount of damages. The Court orders no custody recalculation, release, state reopening, new evidentiary hearing, stay, appointed counsel or present award.

### Scope of the decision

The governing no-realistic-opportunity standard is resolved for the former-prisoner category presented; its factual application is not postponed as an unresolved legal question. The Court adopts neither a categorical bar against all former prisoners nor an unconditional exception whenever federal habeas is presently unavailable. Ordinary proof, pleading, defenses and limitations retain their force. No finding establishes actual overconfinement, deliberate delay, a prior adverse credit adjudication or an adequate remaining state procedure.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Heck v. Humphrey, June 24, 1994:** Its necessary-invalidity comparison, qualifying invalidation routes, deferred accrual for covered claims and without-prejudice dismissal are applied. The Court now resolves part of its express reservation by adopting the bounded former-prisoner no-realistic-review exception and retaining the prerequisite after unexcused loss of a usable opportunity. Its original in-custody holding is not overruled.
- **Edwards v. Balisok, May 19, 1997:** Its legal-consequence analysis governs sentence-credit claims; procedural wording does not avoid necessary invalidity, and genuine independent injuries remain distinct. Its invalidation and accrual rules remain for claims still covered.
- **Spencer v. Kemna, March 3, 1998:** Its habeas mootness holding and preservation of the civil-remedy reservations remain. Its discussion did not itself create today's affirmative exception or a universal former-prisoner bar.
- **Preiser v. Rodriguez:** Its special channel for release from custody supplies the statutory coordination premise; this damages action is neither automatically converted into habeas nor denied solely because it concerns custody.
- **Patsy v. Board of Regents:** Its rejection of general section 1983 state-remedy exhaustion remains controlling. The realistic-opportunity inquiry cannot be converted into exhaustion of every conceivable state procedure.
- **Wolff v. McDonnell:** Its preservation of compatible civil relief informs the independent-claim boundary; factual overlap with custody does not itself require invalidation.
- **Carey v. Piphus:** Its common-law remedial guidance remains subordinate to the federal statutory remedy, and actual compensable injury remains necessary; no automatic per-day award follows.

## Law After Decision

A civil claim compatible with the valid custody determination does not require favorable invalidation. If success necessarily contradicts that determination, a former prisoner may proceed without invalidation when meaningful lawful custody review never realistically existed or was lost through circumstances beyond his control despite timely pursuit. Unexcused loss of an actually usable opportunity leaves the prerequisite in force; release and present federal habeas unavailability alone do not satisfy it.

Courts must identify the actual custody determination and review opportunity. This rule resolves the legal consequences of availability and unexcused loss, while leaving Randell's facts and ordinary civil requirements for adjudication. For a claim remaining subject to invalidation, Heck's qualifying routes, deferred accrual and without-prejudice dismissal continue. No general state-remedy exhaustion, automatic damages right or compulsory reopening follows.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia concurs in Part I and in the judgment, joined by Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. They agree that the complaint must be compared with the actual operative custody determination, that an independent execution injury is not automatically barred, and that this screening record cannot sustain categorical dismissal. Scalia's Edwards analysis requires the actual legal consequence, not a procedural label or assumption that every excess day contradicts the judicial sentence.

They retain the common-law-informed ground stated in their Heck concurrence: a damages claim necessarily contradicting an outstanding criminal or custody adjudication ordinarily requires invalidation even after release. Present habeas unavailability is not itself favorable termination. They regard the contradiction of an extant adjudication as distinct from general state-remedy exhaustion, and a remedial gap alone does not remove that concern.

The strongest objection is a claimant whose custody was too short or whose injury became knowable too late to obtain meaningful review. These four do not adopt the Court's affirmative exception on this undeveloped record; they would first resolve the actual determination and necessary-invalidity predicates and reserve a fully established genuinely impossible-review case. Their position does not establish deliberate delay, adequate state review, prior adjudication or actual unlawfulness here. They would remand for classification, preserve ordinary preclusion and defenses only when their predicates exist, and require no impossible universal showing about every possible state procedure. They join neither Parts II–III's new exception nor the conditional directions implementing it.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit for comparison of the civil claim with the actual sentence and custody determination. If the claim is compatible, ordinary civil adjudication may proceed without favorable invalidation. If it necessarily establishes invalidity, the court must determine realistic review opportunity and legally excused loss under the announced exception; an otherwise covered claim without qualifying invalidation is dismissed without prejudice. Ordinary screening, responsibility, causation, injury, immunity, limitations and supported preclusion defenses remain. No habeas conversion, state-court reopening, hearing, counsel appointment, custody recalculation, stay or damages amount is directed, and no Supreme Court return condition is retained.

## Holdings

### The integrated agreement clearly consents to confirmation

**Holding and operative rule:** A tribe clearly waives immunity from the contemplated state-court confirmation proceeding when its integrated agreement requires binding arbitration, expressly provides for judicial entry of the award, incorporates rules providing for that enforcement, and selects an applicable regime under which a competent state court may enter judgment. This agreement supplies that limited consent without a prescribed formula referring to sovereign immunity; commercial activity, a generic choice-of-law clause, or arbitration standing alone does not establish the same waiver.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices, controls the rule, its application and the limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Kiowa preserves tribal immunity for commercial and governmental contracts both on and off reservation land, while recognizing clear waiver. Santa Clara Pueblo requires unequivocal consent, and Potawatomi separates the underlying obligation from amenability to suit. The Court applies those principles to what this agreement promises. Its final-arbitration undertaking expressly ends in judicial entry under the selected Oklahoma enforcement law. Treating the competent-court qualification as requiring an entirely separate immunity waiver would deny that express judicial undertaking its operation. The qualification preserves ordinary court competence; it does not undo the consent the agreement itself conveys.

First Options requires actual agreement and attention to its scope. The Tribe tendered the form, but that conduct supports assent to the words rather than independently waiving immunity. Clarity concerns the undertaking's legal content, not special immunity vocabulary. The agreement links the arbitral decision and judicial confirmation under an identified regime. Enforcing that bounded forum choice respects both sovereign immunity and the Tribe's power to consent.

The commercial location and perceived fairness of requiring payment cannot independently supply waiver. The Tribe's refusal to participate in arbitration establishes neither default nor the correctness of the award. Ordinary confirmation defenses remain for their own adjudication after removal of the immunity bar.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding creates no otherwise absent federal subject-matter jurisdiction. Execution against particular assets and unrelated claims are outside the confirmation question. Signatory authority and former 25 U.S.C. §81 were not aired below and fall outside the accepted issue. The Court need not decide whether tribal and state immunity-waiver doctrines are parallel. Incorporated arbitration rules supply no new holding about delegation of arbitrability.

## Event

Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc., No. 99-2035. Merits decision, May 14, 2001; argued February 26, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. Review is expressly limited to the standard for appellate review of a trial court's ruling on a constitutional challenge to punitive damages, including the Seventh Amendment allocation.

Cooper modified a Leatherman tool and used its photographs and a touched-up drawing to promote the ToolZall before production was complete. Some materials continued circulating after a recall effort. The jury found passing off, false advertising and unfair competition and awarded $50,000 compensation and $4.5 million punitive damages. The district judge sustained the punitive amount, and the court of appeals affirmed under abuse-of-discretion review. Its separate functionality ruling made copying the useful appearance lawful and removed the design injunction; that distinct judgment remains outside this review.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The appellate punitive-damages judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent constitutional review.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 7–2. Supporting: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg. The Ninth Circuit must independently evaluate constitutional excessiveness while respecting supported historical findings. The Court fixes no sum, ratio, remittitur or new trial and does not hold the $4.5 million award unconstitutional. The $50,000 compensation and the separate functionality disposition remain undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Vacatur and remand, 7–2 | Independent constitutional review and factual protection; BMW treated as binding law without renewed endorsement of its foundation |
| Concurrence | Scalia | Thomas | Joins the complete Court opinion and judgment | Preserves objection to BMW's substantive foundation and availability of reconsideration in a proper case |
| Dissent | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela | Would affirm on the limited review-standard question | Meaningful abuse-of-discretion amount review with independent correction of legal error |

## Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** An appellate court must independently determine whether the punitive award exceeds the constitutional limit established by BMW, while respecting supported historical findings concerning conduct, culpability, causation and harm. The Seventh Amendment does not require abuse-of-discretion review of that constitutional determination because deciding the legal boundary of punishment does not retry those historical facts.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, directly controls the review allocation and its factual limits. Scalia and Thomas join on the premise that BMW governs this limited question, without affirmatively endorsing its substantive due-process foundation.

**Controlling explanation:** BMW supplies the existing substantive limit; this grant concerns how an appellate court enforces it. Bajakajian separates independent constitutional proportionality review from deference to historical findings. Ornelas likewise distinguishes ultimate constitutional evaluation from factfinding. Their task-specific reasoning supports independent application of the punitive limit without a second trial of the conduct, injury or intent.

Gasperini's deferential review of the trial court's supported compensatory valuation does not settle this distinct constitutional task. A jury's selection of punishment reflects a moral assessment and deterrent judgment; the constitutional limit on that punishment remains a legal question. The Seventh Amendment protects the facts underlying the assessment, not a district court's discretionary authority to settle the federal constitutional boundary. Oberg requires meaningful scrutiny of amount, and the existing substantive limit cannot become whatever amount an individual district judge finds tolerable. Independent review therefore corrects the allocation used below while preserving the jury's and trial judge's actual factual work.

The Court does not itself reweigh this award. The independent review must distinguish intentional deceptive promotion from product copying that the separate judgment holds lawful. Supported actual or potential harm and the adjudicated wrong govern; invented losses or wealth unconnected to that wrong cannot replace the required relationship. Those are existing-law boundaries for the required review, not findings that the lower courts committed every asserted guidepost error or a new determination of the maximum permissible amount.

**Limits:** Reopening BMW's substantive validity, Oregon's allocation of part of the award, and fixing a permissible sum are outside the limited grant. No rule is adopted for materially different legislatively fixed multiples or exact deterrence schemes. Supported factual findings remain protected; independent legal review creates no appellate factfinding power or universal standard for every mixed question.

## Precedent Treatment

- BMW: applied as the binding substantive premise; guideposts and their qualifications remain unchanged, without a fresh majority endorsement of their constitutional foundation.
- Bajakajian: extends its factual/legal division by analogy to this constitutional punitive-review task; its forfeiture holding retains its scope.
- Ornelas: applies task-specific independent constitutional evaluation with factual protection; no universal mixed-question command follows.
- Gasperini: distinguishes compensatory valuation and leaves its deferential appellate allocation intact in that setting.
- Oberg: preserves meaningful review of amount and its adequate-functional-substitute qualification; no numerical ceiling is added.
- TXO: preserves its record-specific procedural holding and absence of a controlling numerical or potential-harm formula.
- TrafFix: preserves lawful useful copying, which cannot be punished merely by relabeling it advertising misconduct.
- Buford: distinguishes its practical Guidelines task rather than displacing this constitutional allocation.

## Law After Decision

Appellate courts must independently apply the existing constitutional limit to ordinary civil punitive awards, while preserving supported historical findings. The independent legal task does not create appellate power to find new facts or require repeated valuation of compensation. The amount here awaits that review; no fixed ratio or constitutional dollar maximum is announced. BMW remains controlling at its existing scope without a fresh decision on its substantive constitutional foundation.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in the complete opinion and judgment on the premise that BMW supplies the binding substantive limit for this limited grant. Their agreement concerns the division between legal constitutional review and historical factfinding. They retain their opposition to BMW's substantive due-process foundation and willingness to reconsider it in a case properly presenting that issue. The Court's bounded reasoning does not require them to endorse that foundation or a universal rule of independent review for every mixed question.

Ginsburg, joined by Stone-Zsela, would affirm on the standard-of-review question. Appellate courts independently determine the governing law, correct impermissible premises and unsupported factual assumptions, and require actual review of amount. They may nevertheless review the trial judge's supported punitive assessment for abuse of discretion. The jury's appraisal of culpability, deterrence and the course of misconduct depends on the record heard by it and the trial judge. Gasperini's institutional respect for supported valuation informs this allocation despite its compensatory setting. Bajakajian's governmental forfeiture and Ornelas's search determination concern distinct tasks and do not command a second independent civil punitive valuation.

The dissent preserves BMW's substantive boundary despite disagreement with its foundation and Oberg's demand for meaningful amount review. An award is not immune merely because a judge sustained it. Disregarding guideposts, punishing lawful copying, inventing threatened losses or substituting wealth for a connection to the wrong remains correctable. But the limited grant establishes none of those supposed errors as facts and does not authorize selection of a preferred punitive figure. The ninety-to-one ratio alone does not decide whether this supported assessment must be replaced. ## Event

On the petition for review of the Ninth Circuit's December 7, 2000 judgment directing return to arbitration with instructions to award Garvey approximately $3 million. ## Holdings

### I. Timely review of the later judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A timely petition from the later judgment in the same litigation permits review of an antecedent ruling necessary to that judgment. Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe supply this rule; an earlier appellate mandate binds the district court, but does not immunize the predicate ruling from this Court's proper later review. ### II. The court may not replace submitted factfinding

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may not replace the credibility and factual assessment of a labor arbitrator who is arguably applying the agreed framework within the accepted submission merely because it regards the assessment as seriously mistaken. The court of appeals disagreed with his treatment of Smith's later letter because earlier collusion proceedings had rejected club testimony. The Court does not independently endorse the arbitrator's factual accuracy or find that a witness lied. The legal defect is the appellate replacement of the chosen decisionmaker while he applied the agreed criterion. Further arbitral proceedings depend on what the agreement permits; this rule neither orders another hearing in every case nor creates authority absent from the agreement.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Ginsburg withholds this additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Misco's remedial discussion and American Manufacturing preserve the contractual tribunal's role after a court identifies a valid enforcement defect. A court cannot use vacatur as authority to adjudicate the disputed merits and command the sum it considers inevitable. Garvey's assertion that only one factual result is possible does not itself transfer the submitted task to the reviewing court. This additional ground independently defeats the directed award; because vacatur was improper here, the operative mandate restores the denial rather than requiring a hypothetical second arbitration.

## Event

Rogers v. Tennessee, No. 99-6218. Merits decision, May 14, 2001; argued November 1, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Tennessee Supreme Court. The question is whether due process permits application of that court's abolition of the year-and-a-day murder limitation to conduct occurring while the limitation remained state law.

Rogers stabbed the victim on May 6, 1994. The undisputed death interval exceeded a year and a day. Tennessee's highest court held that the limitation survived the 1989 statutory revision, abolished it judicially, and applied the abolition to sustain Rogers's second-degree murder conviction. The causal medical account and the legal time limitation are distinct. Notice must be evaluated from law existing when Rogers acted.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining the murder conviction through retrospective abolition is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

## Holdings

### I. This acknowledged limitation could not be removed retrospectively without sufficient warning

**Holding and operative rule:** When the state court acknowledges that the year-and-a-day offense limitation remained in force when this defendant acted, its later abolition cannot sustain his murder conviction merely because the limitation had become medically obsolete, was infrequently used, or had been abandoned elsewhere. The wrongfulness of Rogers's assault does not answer whether the law then permitted murder liability after the recognized interval. Medical causation and the legal temporal restriction are different matters.

The State offers substantial reasons for abandoning an antiquated limitation now that causation can be proved after a longer interval. That justification does not establish warning of retrospective removal. Rare litigation does not itself erase an existing rule, and reform elsewhere does not itself change Tennessee law. Ordinary judicial interpretation, foreseeable clarification and prospective common-law development remain permissible. ## Event

United States v. Valentine, Third Circuit No. 00-1425; judgment below reported at 232 F.3d 350. Merits submission and decision, May 14, 2001. On review of the Third Circuit's reversal of suppression and remand of the federal firearm prosecution. The questions concern individualized suspicion from the reported gun possession and encounter, including the preserved permitting argument, and whether actual submission can occur during a brief interval before later flight.

At about 1 a.m. on May 8, 1999, a man flagged down two uniformed officers and reported recently seeing a nearby man carrying a gun. He gave a detailed description but refused to identify himself. Officers saw Valentine matching the description nearby, in an area described as having many shootings. The men began walking away. The officer's account describes a command to come over and place hands on the car, Valentine's inquiry about whom it concerned, a charge toward the officer and an attempt to push past his arms, followed by a grab and struggle. The gun was discovered only then.

Valentine asserted an earlier stop and name. All nine Justices participated at merits submission and decision.

The Third Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for correct legal treatment of individualized suspicion, the preserved permitting theory and the first actual seizure on the existing record.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The court below must apply the stated suspicion and submission rules to the supported chronology and consider properly preserved independent grounds under ordinary remedial law. No suppression, acquittal, dismissal, new hearing or reopening of proofs is commanded. The reported continuous-charge account remains a possible basis for admission if no earlier seizure occurred. | Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Holdings

### I. Reliable possession information must be connected to wrongdoing

**Holding and operative rule:** A recent face-to-face eyewitness report may materially strengthen the reliability of information that a person possesses a gun, but reliability about possession is distinct from a supported inference of criminal activity. The reported possession, late hour, generalized neighborhood shootings and ordinary walking away do not, without a supported further connection, establish individualized suspicion on this record; all actual circumstances must be evaluated together.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, directly controls all three holdings, their stated limits and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Wardlow requires particularized suspicion and rejects categorical reliance on location or departure; its actual application found a more substantial look-and-flight record insufficient. J. L. distinguishes accurate identification from reliable indication of wrongdoing and rejects an ordinary-gun exception. Personal delivery and immediacy have reliability features absent from the remote report there. The Court adopts no categorical prohibition on unidentified eyewitness reports. But greater assurance of possession does not itself establish an offense. This witness reported no threat or illegal transfer. Ordinary walking away and generalized danger cannot fill the missing connection by assumption.

The totality rule considers circumstances together, including grounded local experience and conduct capable of an innocent explanation. It does not demand that every observation independently indicate crime or that officers prove an offense. Ornelas requires independent constitutional evaluation while preserving historical findings and genuine grounded inferences. It does not permit imagined wrongdoing or assumed licensing facts. Consensual inquiry remains available, and a more particularized report or supported circumstances may justify immediate restraint. The preserved permitting argument and any conduct before the first actual seizure require their own assessment below; the Court does not find that every possible ground for admission fails.

### II. A bare evidentiary presumption is not a particularized fact

**Holding and operative rule:** A state-law evidentiary presumption that a handgun possessor lacks a permit cannot, by itself, replace the Fourth Amendment's requirement of particularized suspicion of wrongdoing. Officers may draw grounded inferences from concrete circumstances actually known before seizure, including supported licensing circumstances; the Government's complete permitting argument must be evaluated under that ordinary totality standard.

**Authority:** The same five Justices directly join this legal limit and its preservation of additional actual licensing circumstances. The four dissenters do not supply implied agreement by reserving the issue.

**Controlling explanation:** The strongest contrary argument relies on New Jersey's strict permit requirements, investigation and evidentiary presumption as making reported possession indicative of illegality under the modest reasonable-suspicion threshold. Those actual regulatory circumstances may matter when properly supported and applicable. A rule allocating evidentiary responsibility, however, does not itself establish a fact about this person or give officers unreported licensing knowledge. The presumption's trial function cannot substitute for evaluation of the circumstances supporting the investigative inference.

J. L. supplies the distinction between credited information and authority to intrude, not an already decided licensing rule. Wardlow requires particularized totality, not a prescribed third fact. No officer must invariably check a database, observe a completed offense or obtain a separately specified item of proof. Reasonable suspicion remains below probable cause and permits contextual inference. The Government's complete theory was not adjudicated below; its properly supported restrictive-licensing and investigation circumstances remain available. Absence of their detail from the published account is not a finding that officers knew none. The remand therefore preserves full ordinary application while rejecting automatic substitution of the bare presumption.

**Limits:** The Court does not decide Valentine's permit status, the presumption's validity as a trial-evidence rule, or the licensing scheme's constitutionality. It does not find permits common or rare or treat all public possession as lawful. The existing arms decisions distinguish coverage from regulatory validity and do not decide this licensing theory.

### III. Genuine submission can be brief

**Holding and operative rule:** Objectively manifested yielding to a police command can constitute a seizure even if brief; there is no fixed minimum duration, and later flight does not erase a completed seizure. A hesitation, inquiry or equivocal movement that does not manifest yielding remains distinct, and an unheeded command alone is not a seizure.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion directly adopts the rule and the existing-record return.

**Controlling explanation:** Hodari D. identifies physical force or actual submission as the boundary. Duration can help determine whether yielding occurred, but it creates no interval in which genuine submission lacks constitutional effect. The lower court therefore could not reject the assumed stop-and-name account solely because it was momentary. Conversely, its legal assumption establishes no actual pause. Its published account found no supporting evidence and described a report apparently inconsistent with the theory, while declining factual determination under the erroneous alternative rule. The district court's cutoff at the command likewise supplies no finding of submission.

On the reported inquiry, charge and attempted-pushing sequence, the first seizure may be the grab; the preceding concrete conduct can contribute to suspicion with the immediate gun report. If actual yielding occurred earlier, later conduct cannot retrospectively justify its inception. The gun found during the struggle supplies no earlier justification. Any independent exclusionary-rule or attenuation ground requires its own law and predicates. Proper review of the existing record may resolve admission without new testimony. The Court finds neither a period of compliance nor an evidentiary conflict requiring another hearing.

### Remedy and scope

The judgment is vacated for application of these rules to the supported first-seizure chronology and preserved independent grounds. Correct legal treatment, rather than a new factual finding for either party, governs the return. The Court neither commands suppression nor decides guilt, acquittal or dismissal. Remaining factual, licensing and remedial work is ordinary adjudication under the rules announced, not a reservation about whether brief actual yielding counts as seizure or whether a bare presumption alone supplies individualized suspicion.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Illinois v. Wardlow:** Applies its actual individualized totality rule and prohibition on universal location or flight shortcuts; the lower court's broader account does not govern.
- **Florida v. J. L.:** Applies the distinction between identification and wrongdoing and the absence of an ordinary-gun exception. In-person reliability is evaluated on its actual features, without a categorical anonymous-eyewitness rule.
- **California v. Hodari D.:** Applies force or actual submission and clarifies that genuine brief yielding is not categorically excluded by later flight.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Applies independent constitutional evaluation with protection for historical findings and grounded experience; it creates no missing facts or compulsory hearing.
- **Illinois v. McArthur:** Preserves justification at the actual intrusion. Its conceded probable cause and residential warrant-preservation setting do not supply this street encounter's predicates.
- **Atwater v. City of Lago Vista:** Its actual-enforcement limit on additional station custody after probable cause remains at that distinct scope. It does not decide the antecedent Terry suspicion or submission question, and this case imports none of its traffic-custody facts or application positions.

## Law After Decision

Reliable eyewitness information of gun possession remains subject to individualized suspicion of wrongdoing, evaluated under all supported circumstances. A bare evidentiary presumption of nonlicensure cannot itself provide the necessary particularized factual connection, while actual licensing context and other known circumstances may support grounded inference. Genuine submission has no minimum duration and is not erased by later flight. The existing-record remand preserves the no-prior-seizure charge-and-pushing alternative, the fuller permitting theory and independently supported remedial grounds without ordering suppression or new proof.

## Separate Writings

The response about whom the command concerned, charge toward the officer and attempted pushing do not objectively manifest yielding. With the recent in-person gun report, that concrete conduct supplied a particularized basis for restraint at the grab. Hodari D. distinguishes an unheeded command from seizure; their Wardlow positions permit grounded contextual appraisal of these specific actions rather than a categorical flight rule.

They do not adopt the panel's minimum-duration premise, infer justification from the discovered gun or suggest that every charge or flight in a high-crime area suffices. Their difference with the Court concerns the proper disposition on this existing record. Scalia retains his objection to Ornelas's allocation while applying that governing rule here.

The court below must identify the first seizure through force or actual submission and evaluate the information known then under the announced rules, including the properly supported permitting circumstances. The continuous-charge account may sustain admission if no earlier seizure occurred. If an earlier unsupported seizure is established, suppression and any preserved independent remedial theory require ordinary analysis. ## Event

**Bartnicki v. Vopper / United States v. Vopper**, Nos. 99-1687 and 99-1728, 532 U.S. 514. Merits decision, May 21, 2001. Argued December 5, 2000. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.

Both petitions concern the same judgment. The question is whether the First Amendment permits civil damages for disclosure of an accurate recording about a matter of public concern when an unknown third party intercepted the conversation unlawfully, the defendants neither participated in that interception nor obtained the recording unlawfully, and they knew or had reason to know of the interception's illegality.

The recording concerned contentious public-school collective bargaining. Union negotiators discussed the dispute and pressure on school-board members, including language suggesting physical harm. Jack Yocum said he found the recording in his mailbox, played it for board members and delivered it to broadcaster Frederick Vopper. Vopper broadcast it in reporting about the settlement, and other media disclosed its contents. The accuracy of the recording is undisputed.

The District Court denied cross-motions for summary judgment. It rejected an interceptor-only statutory construction and the First Amendment defense, while identifying a factual dispute over intentional interception. On a certified interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b), the Third Circuit held the federal and Pennsylvania disclosure provisions unconstitutional as applied and directed summary judgment for respondents. The United States intervened to defend the federal enactment. Petitioners and the United States seek reversal; respondents seek affirmance.

## Holdings

The two holdings below are adopted by the Opinion of the Court, **Kennedy**, with the complete seven-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology. They control by direct majority agreement; no narrower-rationale inference is required.

### The disclosure provisions reach noninterceptors, but their neutrality does not end constitutional review

**Holding and operative rule:** For these claims, 18 U.S.C. §2511(1)(c) reaches intentional disclosure or attempted disclosure of a communication's contents by a person who knows or has reason to know that the information came from an interception violating the subsection; neither that provision nor the parallel Pennsylvania disclosure provision is confined to the interceptor. Their selection by unlawful source rather than message is content neutral, but their direct burden on communicating information requires constitutional examination even when enforced through civil damages against a nonmedia speaker.

**Authority:** The complete seven-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the statutory construction, classification and requirement of constitutional review.

**Controlling explanation:** The disclosure prohibition addresses the conduct and knowledge of a discloser. It does not require that the same person also made the interception. Constitutional avoidance therefore cannot replace the enacted coverage with an interceptor-only rule. The certified constitutional question proceeds on assumed intentional illegality and knowledge or reason to know; lawful receipt is consistent with the statute's separate prohibition on later disclosure.

Turner requires attention to what a restriction selects and why. These provisions select an unlawful interception as the source, not a viewpoint or bargaining position. Yet disclosure communicates the recorded words, and liability burdens that expression directly. Neither neutrality nor the use of damages removes the First Amendment question.

Cohen and Branzburg reject a categorical press exemption from valid general law; they do not make every direct restriction on publication valid. Yocum's communication to board members and delivery to a broadcaster are expression even though he is an intermediary rather than professional media. The Court therefore examines the justification for all the challenged disclosures under the same relevant constitutional principles.

**Precedent treatment:** Turner supplies source-sensitive classification and substantive review; Cohen and Branzburg retain their rejection of special press immunity without dispensing with review of this publication burden.

### The First Amendment defeats these disclosure claims on the certified premises and public-bargaining record

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment bars the requested federal and Pennsylvania disclosure damages against these respondents, who lawfully received an accurate recording without participating in its unlawful interception and disclosed this conversation concerning the contentious public-school bargaining controversy and apparent physical pressure against its participants. Their knowledge or reason to know that a stranger intercepted the conversation unlawfully does not defeat that defense on these premises; the rule protects Yocum's communications as well as the media disclosures and requires affirmance of the direction to enter summary judgment.

**Authority:** The complete seven-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts this bounded rule and its application, including both the deterrence analysis and the independent conversational-privacy analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Daily Mail, Florida Star and Landmark protect truthful information of public significance lawfully obtained by its speaker. They left open the unlawful-source problem. The Court extends their protection to this application, where recipients lawfully obtained the recording and took no part in interception.

Deterring unlawful interception is legitimate. Turner nevertheless requires examination of the actual expressive burden and adequate fit while respecting supported legislative predictions. The ordinary target is the person responsible for interception. General incentive reasoning does not sufficiently justify suppressing these nonparticipants' disclosures to discourage an unknown stranger. This conclusion neither requires an empirical study in every case nor makes deterrence irrelevant whenever a recipient is innocent of interception.

Dissemination also causes a distinct privacy injury. Protecting conversation encourages candid private speech, and that interest remains substantial even when later liability adds little deterrence. But the actual information here bears directly on public-school negotiations and apparent pressure involving physical harm against board members. The participants directed that public dispute; the public has a strong interest in their actual words. Settlement does not erase that significance. The Court need not find a punishable threat or a continuing danger at publication to recognize this public interest. In these circumstances, the asserted privacy protection does not justify the burden imposed on the disclosures.

Rice preserves responsibility for purposeful, materially effective assistance to crime, while distinguishing awareness and ordinary publication. Knowing the completed interception's unlawful origin does not establish that assistance. The rule neither excuses independently unlawful acquisition nor turns all information concerning a public participant into public property. Sheets, Denius and Sterling protect confined intimate interests against unjustified official dissemination, compulsory access or coercive surrender; they do not decide these private recipients' disclosure liability. Because the defense succeeds even assuming intentional illegality, the disputed interception intent cannot defeat summary judgment on the disclosure claims. Interception, procurement, independently unlawful acquisition, purely private or intimate material, trade secrets and separately asserted use restrictions lie outside the accepted disclosure question and are not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Daily Mail, Florida Star and Landmark are extended to the described unlawful-source application; Turner and Rice retain their justification and participation distinctions. Sheets, Denius and Sterling remain confined to their official-conduct holdings and are not extended into a private-publication damages rule.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 21, 2001, the federal and parallel Pennsylvania disclosure provisions cannot support these damages claims against nonparticipating lawful recipients who communicate the accurate recording in the public-bargaining circumstances decided here. Both media publication and Yocum's nonmedia communications are protected. Source illegality and the recipient's awareness do not alone defeat the constitutional defense on these premises.

The decision adds this application to existing truthful-publication law. It leaves the statutory coverage of knowing noninterceptor disclosure intact, with a constitutional defense controlling the application before the Court. Courts must retain the actual speech and privacy context; neither an automatic public-concern exception nor a professional-press privilege follows. The fact that the recorded language strengthens the public interest does not establish that proof of a criminal threat is required for constitutional protection.

Interception and procurement remain distinct from lawful receipt and disclosure. Rice's actual purposeful-assistance rule continues to govern its predicates. Official acquisition, disclosure and operative compelled surrender of objectively intimate information remain governed by Sheets, Denius and Sterling within their decided scopes. The Court neither decides the constitutional treatment of purely private or intimate material, trade secrets or separate use restrictions outside the accepted question nor imposes an additional test for those subjects. There is no Act-wide invalidation, new mandatory scrutiny formula or special transition.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring, joined by O'Connor.** They join the Court's opinion in full because it resolves this application without making truthful publication automatically superior to every privacy interest. The statutes protect private speech as well as privacy: people may speak less freely when an illegal interception can be disseminated without consequence. The competing public-speech interest is therefore assessed against a real constitutional and practical value, rather than against a mere dislike of publicity.

The recipients acted lawfully in obtaining the recording and did not cause its interception. The speakers were directing a public-school labor dispute, and the recorded discussion concerned apparent physical harm against board members. Those circumstances create an unusually strong public interest and reduce the legitimate claim to secrecy for this particular discussion. They do not establish a criminal true threat, a plan of violence, a continuing danger when broadcast, or surrender of every private conversation by people who take public roles.

Their Turner, Rice, Sheets, Denius and Sterling merits positions support actual review, lawful responsibility boundaries and serious protection of genuine confidentiality. They preserve legislative latitude to protect private conversation and to prevent the distinct harm of dissemination in materially different settings. They do not adopt automatic strict scrutiny whenever substantial public-speech and private-speech interests conflict, a closed balancing checklist, or a rule that deterrence can never justify liability absent complicity. Their additional explanation qualifies the reach of their agreement while remaining consistent with every proposition of the bounded Court opinion.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** They would reverse the constitutional ruling and summary-judgment direction in both dockets, then return the claims for ordinary adjudication of statutory elements and preserved defenses. The law regulates the defendant's knowing disclosure as a separately harmful act; it does not merely attribute a stranger's interception to an innocent recipient. Dissemination enlarges the intrusion and undermines the privacy that permits candid conversation.

The source criterion does not select a viewpoint or message. Under the content-neutral framework, the dissent finds adequate justification in preventing the additional disclosure injury, reinforced by the reasonable legislative judgment that limiting downstream dissemination can reduce the reward for interception. They would not require lawmakers to accept a route around the prohibition through recipients who did not themselves conduct the tap. That argument is a judgment about permissible prevention, not a finding of a quantified deterrent effect or respondent participation.

They distinguish Daily Mail, Florida Star and Landmark because the information here remained private until unlawful interception and the knowing disclosure itself is prohibited. Cohen and Branzburg preserve application of valid general law to the press; Rice supplies no universal requirement that a discloser must have assisted the antecedent crime before any publication restriction can be justified. Their objections to constitutional extensions in Sheets, Denius and Sterling do not deny legislative authority to protect conversation. They accept the need for First Amendment review and the significance of lawful receipt and public relevance, but find those considerations insufficient to invalidate this application. Reversal would not establish intentional interception, the remaining statutory elements, damages or any criminal threat.

## Holdings

### I. The earlier statutory affirmance does not bar this later review

**Holding and operative rule:** On proper review of the later judgment in this litigation, the earlier affirmance for want of a quorum does not foreclose consideration of the antecedent constitutional liability questions. The statutory affirmance retains its lawful case-specific force, including the mandate that bound the Court of Federal Claims on remand; it does not insulate those questions from the present review.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the threshold rule and explanation. Thomas accepts the Court's resolution as the predicate for deciding the merits but does not join this rationale or cast a contrary threshold vote.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the earlier inability to act does not prevent consideration of the constitutional questions properly presented through the later judgment. Section 2109 gave the earlier order the effect of an equally divided affirmance. That order was effective below; absence of an opinion does not erase a judgment's binding force.

The judges invoke Pink, which distinguishes the binding effect of an equally divided affirmance between the parties from its lack of precedential reasoning. That distinction is sound, but Pink's statement concerned a division after consideration of the controversy. Here the Court could consider neither the merits nor whether to undertake merits review. The absence of that consideration, and the difficulty of providing an equivalent substitute for this Court's review, defeat the asserted bar in this continuing litigation. The conclusion rests on that confined difference, not on a general power to disregard unreasoned judgments or on the constitutional importance of the question alone.

Garvey, applying Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe, supplies the lawful channel: review of a later judgment may reach an antecedent ruling necessary to it, even though the earlier mandate bound the court below. Garvey does not itself answer the additional section 2109 objection; the no-quorum distinction supplies that answer here. The Federal Circuit's treatment of liability as law of the case explains the proceedings below but does not make the antecedent ruling immune from this Court's present review. Both compensation questions are therefore reached without denying the earlier mandate's lawful operation.

**Limits:** The Court does not announce that every statutory affirmance lacks binding effect between its parties or that every final judgment without reasons may be reconsidered. It adopts no general extraordinary-circumstances exception to finality.

### II. Ordinary nondiscriminatory taxation is compatible with protected judicial compensation

**Holding and operative rule:** The Compensation Clause permits ordinary nondiscriminatory taxation of incumbent judges' compensation, including generally shared taxes enacted after their appointment. Evans v. Gore is overruled insofar as it forbids that taxation; direct reductions of compensation and discriminatory indirect reductions remain prohibited, and the generally extended Medicare tax is valid as applied here.

**Authority:** Parts II and III of Souter's Opinion of the Court are joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt the ordinary-tax rule, the Medicare application, the discrimination rule and its Social Security application. Scalia and Thomas do not join either part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial independence requires protection of compensation, not permanent insulation from ordinary civic obligations. O'Malley sustained general taxation for a judge appointed after the governing tax policy and explained why sharing that burden does not compromise the office. Its holding did not resolve the present incumbent question, but its constitutional distinction applies here. Will protects compensation once legally due and rejects direct salary reductions even when other officials are included; it also distinguishes ordinary general taxation from those reductions.

Evans treated a tax imposed after appointment as an impermissible diminution. That categorical rule cannot be maintained alongside the distinction between a protected compensation entitlement and ordinary taxation. The expectation that stipulated compensation will remain protected does not entail an immutable tax regime. Overruling the broader tax immunity preserves the reliance that matters to judicial independence: Congress may neither cut the compensation owed nor accomplish an equivalent discriminatory reduction indirectly.

Acker requires attention to an exaction's operation rather than its label, but expressly reserved this independent constitutional question. Its statutory consent analysis supplies no automatic Article III answer. The Medicare extension applies generally to federal employees and contains no discriminatory transition of the kind presented by Social Security. The fact that these judges previously enjoyed an employment-related exemption does not convert this ordinary shared tax into a prohibited compensation reduction. The contrary package theory would preserve that exemption despite the absence of the discrimination or direct reduction the controlling rule requires. Medicare liability is therefore valid, and compensation recovery on the rejected constitutional exemption theory is unavailable.

**Limits:** A tax label does not validate a disguised reduction, and including other officials does not save a direct salary cut. No claim by a newly appointed judge or materially different pension classification is presented; O'Malley's existing holding remains intact.

### III. The Social Security transition discriminatorily diminishes these incumbents' compensation

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may not materially single out incumbent Article III judges for a new reduction of protected compensation through the combined operation of tax coverage and pension classifications. The Social Security transition violates that rule as applied to the affected incumbents because compulsory coverage imposes an additional burden without the opt-out or contribution protection afforded other current federal employees; proof of subjective legislative hostility is unnecessary.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by the five-Justice coalition identified in Part II. Scalia and Thomas also support affirmance of Social Security liability, on their distinct grounds, but supply no additional joins to this discrimination holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual transition, considered as a whole, places these incumbent judges outside the ordinary-tax rule. Most existing federal employees could remain outside Social Security. Mandatory participants in contributory retirement systems could avoid an additional payroll burden through a contribution adjustment. These judges' noncontributory retirement system afforded neither protection from the new compulsory charge.

The relevant comparison is among current federal employees facing entry into the program. Acker's attention to actual operation and the pertinent comparison informs that inquiry, while the Compensation Clause supplies the independent constitutional protection. The difference between retirement systems is relevant, but does not itself answer why these incumbents must bear an additional deduction when the transition protects other incumbents through the two identified mechanisms. Pension coordination cannot be used to offset the compensation protection attached to the judicial office.

This is not a requirement that all pension plans be identical or that every exemption elsewhere invalidate a judicial tax. The combined compulsory transition and unavailable protections produce the forbidden diminution on this record. The structural protection does not depend on proving that Congress sought to intimidate, influence or punish judges. Evans's protection against indirect evasion and Will's protection of compensation otherwise due remain effective, even though Evans's ordinary-tax immunity does not. The Social Security liability judgment is affirmed for the affected incumbents; the amount of lawful recovery remains for determination below.

**Limits:** The decision neither exempts every judge from Social Security nor invalidates the tax for other taxpayers. Materially different pension classifications and claims of later appointees lie outside the questions presented. No mandatory checklist, hostile-purpose element or individual-benefit finding is added.

### IV. The general salary increase does not cure the surviving unconstitutional deductions

**Holding and operative rule:** A later salary increase does not automatically satisfy or end an unconstitutional deduction merely because the increase exceeds the deduction; its legal relationship to the injury must be considered. The general increase at issue supplied independently owed compensation and did not cure the Social Security diminution, so timely, proven unlawful deductions remain recoverable under the surviving limitations judgment and individual computation below.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six directly adopt the contextual no-cure rule and its application. Thomas agrees with rejection of the defense on his separate compensation-entitlement ground, without joining the complete explanation. Scalia's Part IV join does not extend to Part II's elimination of Medicare recovery.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a general raise cannot simply be counted as payment of a different legal obligation. Once the increase becomes compensation due, withholding an unconstitutional charge from that new entitlement continues the injury. Comparing the judge's new net pay with an earlier salary therefore does not establish satisfaction of accrued deductions or permission to continue them.

Will protects compensation once an increase takes effect. The Government invokes the later increase that ended the pleaded recovery interval for one of Will's claims. But that interval did not adjudicate the present claim that a general raise extinguishes or cures an ongoing unconstitutional tax. The legal relationship between the measure and the injury matters. The increase described here was generally owed judicial compensation, not a payment making these claimants whole for the unlawful deductions. A lawful, genuinely compensatory measure is not excluded in every circumstance; none is established here, and numerical excess alone supplies no cure.

Liability and recoverable periods remain distinct. The en banc Federal Circuit displaced the panel's blanket Medicare limitations bar and treated recurring deductions as continuing claims, excluding those more than six years before the relevant filing. That existing limitations framework governs the surviving claims within the present review's scope. The Court announces no new general accrual rule. The Court of Federal Claims must determine each claimant's timely deductions and lawful amount; the Court enters no numerical award and orders no new factual procedure merely because computation remains.

**Limits:** Claim-specific entitlement and arithmetic are work on remand under the governing rules, not unresolved constitutional law. The decision establishes no new filing condition, particular compensating formula or required evidentiary hearing.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Evans v. Gore, 253 U.S. 245:** Overruled insofar as it forbids ordinary nondiscriminatory taxes imposed after a judge's appointment. Its protection of judicial independence against direct diminution and discriminatory indirect evasion remains effective; its categorical tax exemption does not.
- **O'Malley v. Woodrough, 307 U.S. 277:** Its civic-obligation distinction is applied to incumbent judges. Its holding concerning a later appointee is preserved and is not portrayed as having already resolved the question it left open.
- **United States v. Will, 449 U.S. 200:** Applies protection of compensation once legally due and preserves the distinction between ordinary taxation and direct salary reductions. Its pleaded recovery interval does not establish an automatic cure by a later raise.
- **Jefferson County v. Acker:** Applies attention to actual operation and the relevant comparison while resolving the separate Compensation Clause question it reserved. Its statutory consent, federal-source discrimination and local-function boundaries retain their existing scope.
- **United States v. Pink, 315 U.S. 203:** Preserves the distinction between interparty force and precedential authority, while distinguishing an equal division following consideration of the controversy from the no-quorum inability to consider the case presented here. The earlier mandate's lawful effect is retained.
- **Major League Baseball Players Association v. Garvey, Part I; Mercer v. Theriot; Hamilton-Brown Shoe Co. v. Wolf Brothers & Co.:** Apply review of an antecedent ruling through the later judgment. They do not alone determine the effect of the separate statutory affirmance.
- **Durant v. Essex Co.; Hertz v. Woodman:** Preserve the force of the judgments actually entered despite the absence of a majority rationale. Their treatment of a final merits judgment or another controversy does not decide the confined no-quorum issue here.
- **Christianson v. Colt Industries Operating Corp.; Messenger v. Anderson:** Their distinctions concerning law-of-the-case practice and judicial power remain intact. The Court derives no general extraordinary-circumstances test for the present statutory disposition from those different procedural settings.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 21, 2001, incumbent Article III judges bear ordinary nondiscriminatory taxation even when enacted after appointment. Evans no longer supplies the contrary categorical immunity. The Constitution continues to prohibit direct salary cuts and materially discriminatory indirect reductions, including the combined Social Security transition adjudicated here. Courts must examine actual operation and the relevant incumbent comparison; no finding of legislative hostility or mandatory list of factors is required.

The earlier no-quorum affirmance retains its lawful operation below but does not bar consideration of these constitutional questions on review of the later judgment. Garvey's ordinary antecedent-review rule remains unchanged; the confined section 2109 holding supplies the additional answer here.

A general salary increase is not an automatic cure for an unlawful deduction. The increase's legal relationship to the injury controls that inquiry, with lawful compensation and genuine satisfaction kept distinct. The surviving en banc limitations framework and individual computation govern Social Security recovery. Medicare compensation recovery is unavailable on the constitutional theory rejected here. No numerical award or new Supreme Court accrual rule follows.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Parts I and IV, including the confined basis for present review and the contextual rejection of the general-raise defense. He would affirm liability for both taxes. Government acting as sovereign may impose ordinary general taxes, so Evans's universal tax immunity should not survive. Government acting as employer, however, may not withdraw an employment-conditioned benefit from a sitting judge's compensation package. The prior payroll-tax exemptions were such benefits. Their withdrawal diminishes compensation even if the judge thereafter pays the same tax as other citizens or every federal employee suffers the same withdrawal.

Will's prohibition on direct compensation reductions supports his distinction; generality alone does not save a reduction of the employment package. He therefore dissents from Medicare reversal and agrees only in the Social Security liability judgment, without joining the Court's discrimination rationale. Acker's classification of an exaction as compensation taxation reserved Article III and does not answer the separate withdrawal-of-benefit question. His position does not decide the unpresented exchange of an exemption for a different benefit equal in value. He would permit timely, proven recovery for both taxes under the lawful limitations framework, and does not treat a general raise as satisfaction of either injury. His merits ground is noncontrolling.

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He accepts the Court's threshold resolution as the predicate for deciding the constitutional merits, without joining Part I or stating a different threshold rule. He would retain Evans and affirm liability for both taxes because a tax reducing a sitting judge's net compensation violates the Compensation Clause. O'Malley's ordinary-civic-burden reasoning and Will's criticism of Evans do not persuade him to abandon that protection. Acker left this constitutional question open.

He agrees with Social Security liability on Evans, not on a requirement of discriminatory operation, and dissents from the denial of Medicare recovery. He also rejects the general-raise defense: a generally owed increase does not satisfy the compensation entitlement protected by his reading of Evans. This agreement with the no-cure result does not adopt the Court's complete contextual explanation. He would allow timely, proven recovery for both components, with individual amounts determined below. He joins none of Souter's opinion; his constitutional ground and proposed Medicare relief are noncontrolling.

## Procedure After Action

The Supreme Court merits proceeding concludes with partial affirmance, partial reversal and remand. The Federal Circuit must give effect to the Medicare disposition and the surviving Social Security liability and no-cure rulings. Remaining compensation proceedings belong in the Court of Federal Claims, applying the en banc limitations treatment to timely deductions and determining individual lawful amounts. The mandate preserves lawful limits on recovery and does not direct a new evidentiary procedure or predict an award.
