## Holdings

### Conforming implementation of the two approved agreements

**Holding and operative rule.** In this continuing original action, an implementing decree must incorporate the quantities and limits the Court approved on June 19 while preserving every expressly reserved claim. Clauses A–G conform to that approval and are entered without a new allocation rule or an adjudication of reserved rights.

**Authority.** The Court's supplemental decree controls the parties and the administration of this original action. Its authority is the Court's retained original jurisdiction and June 19 settlement judgment; the unsigned instrument publishes no individual joins or tally.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court's June 19 decision approved the two agreements and required implementing provisions. Those agreements establish the entitlements, releases, and reservations against which the proposal is measured. The aggregate quantities, California entries, and limited boundary-adjustment exception implement that approval. Nebraska v. Wyoming distinguishes enforcement of an established decree right from a request for a new allocation requiring proof of injury; no new allocation showing is required to incorporate these approved adjustments. A provision inconsistent with the approval would require correction. The proposed Fort Mojave dates, however, repeat different dates already present in the general entitlement and California table. Entry preserves that inherited distinction and changes neither priority. The absence of objections supplies no consent beyond the approved agreements. Retained jurisdiction permits enforcement and later adjudication of separately preserved matters; it does not decide them now.

**Settlement scope.** Fort Mojave receives the lesser of an additional 3,022 acre-feet or water sufficient for 468 acres. The Colorado River Reservation receives the lesser of an additional 2,100 acre-feet or water sufficient for 315 acres. Its agreement bars further reserved Colorado River water claims by the United States and Tribes for California reservation lands. That condition's remaining shortage, calculation, use, and priority rules continue to govern on their existing terms.

**Authority.** Clauses C and F of the Court's supplemental decree preserve the unamended condition; no broader replacement is entered.

**Controlling explanation.** The approved releases remove the western California boundaries of the two named reservations from the proviso for future entitlement adjustments. Under the preserved condition, a determination under Article II(B)(3) that mainstream water is insufficient to satisfy present perfected rights requires the Secretary of the Interior first to satisfy in full the specified rights of the Chemehuevi, Cocopah, Fort Yuma, Colorado River, and Fort Mojave reservations before other nonmiscellaneous present perfected rights. The condition establishes no priority for other unspecified Indian water claims.

## Event

**Artuz v. Bennett**, No. 99-1238; 531 U.S. 4. Argued October 10, 2000; decided November 7, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 199 F.3d 116, which reversed a federal habeas limitations dismissal and remanded. The question is whether a state collateral application otherwise satisfying filing requirements becomes improperly filed under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) because its claims encounter mandatory state rules governing entitlement to relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Second Circuit on the proper-filing question.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The Second Circuit's remand remains in place. The district court must calculate timeliness without converting the identified New York relief bars into filing defects while addressing genuinely open dates and remaining defenses. No writ, retrial, or release is awarded.

## Opinion Topology

Ginsburg delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. That unanimous opinion supplies the holding and its stated limits.

## Holdings

### Filing an application is distinct from entitlement to relief on its claims

**Holding and operative rule.** A state collateral application is properly filed under §2244(d)(2) when delivery and acceptance comply with the rules governing filing the application. Claim-specific rules governing whether relief must be denied do not make an otherwise properly filed application improper; New York Criminal Procedure Law §§440.10(2)(a) and (c) are such relief rules.

**Authority.** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine participating Justices, directly controls this statutory distinction and its application.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress makes the application the object of the filing requirement and separately refers to the judgment or claim reviewed. A court may receive a properly filed application and nevertheless be required to deny its claims. Treating every mandatory relief bar as a filing defect erases that distinction and makes the filing inquiry depend on a later decision about relief. New York paragraph (a) requires denial when the ground or issue was previously decided on the merits on appeal, unless a subsequent retroactively effective change in controlling law has occurred. Paragraph (c) requires sufficient facts in the underlying record for adequate appellate review and the absence of review caused by an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or to raise the issue on a perfected appeal. Those conditions require claim-level inquiries into prior adjudication, legal change, record sufficiency, and unjustified omission. They govern denial of relief, not delivery and acceptance of the motion. Their mandatory language changes neither function. The warden's concern that futile applications may prolong the federal period cannot supply the relief-eligibility condition Congress omitted. The Court therefore rejects improper filing as a ground for restoring this limitations dismissal. Lindh's separate temporal construction does not answer the filing question or exempt this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA.

**Filing conditions and limits.** Acceptance by a clerk does not cure violation of an actual filing requirement. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, required fees, and filing preconditions remain distinct from the two claim-relief provisions; the Court does not classify every such rule without its actual terms. Whether exceptions to a filing deadline can keep a late application from being improperly filed remains open because the limited grant does not present that question. That reservation concerns filing deadlines, not the exceptions and conditions of the two New York relief bars.

**Record and ungranted questions.** Bennett's state conviction became final before AEDPA. A state court orally denied his collateral motion on November 30, 1995; he alleges that he never received a written denial despite requests. The Second Circuit assumed the absence of a written order and treated the application as pending because an appeal required that order. The Court makes no finding about receipt or absence of an order and decides no precise pending period, transitional grace period, exhaustion, procedural default, or ultimate habeas entitlement. Those matters fall outside this grant. Nor does the Court find that either New York bar or its exception actually governs a particular Bennett claim.

## Precedent Treatment

**Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997:** Preserves its provision-specific construction of AEDPA's temporal reach. Its treatment of pre-enactment applications supplies no exemption for Bennett's post-enactment federal application and no answer to the distinct proper-filing question.

## Law After Decision

Section 2244(d)(2)'s pending-state-review exclusion turns on a properly filed application, not success or relief eligibility of every claim within it. Courts must distinguish actual application-filing requirements from claim-relief bars. The one-year limitations framework and separate exhaustion and procedural-default doctrines remain in force; the ruling fixes no tolling duration.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

Further federal habeas proceedings continue under the Second Circuit's remand. Actual timeliness and the remaining defenses must be resolved consistently with the filing distinction. The Court grants no habeas relief.

Bennett, 531 U.S. 4](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-4.pdf), including the complete New York provisions at pages 7–8, note 1. The description of the absent written order preserves the lower court's assumption and Bennett's allegation; it is not a finding by this Court. The grant is stated in substance, not as a verbatim quotation from the petition.

## Event

**Cleveland v. United States**, No. 99-804; 531 U.S. 12. Argued October 10, 2000; decided November 7, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, which affirmed Cleveland's convictions. The March 20 grant was limited to whether Louisiana's video-poker licenses are property in the State's hands for purposes of mail fraud under 18 U.S.C. §1341. The separate materiality question was not granted. The government also asserts that the statute independently reaches obtaining a license valuable in the recipient's hands and distinguishes renewal from initial issuance.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment sustaining the two mail-fraud convictions on the charged licensing theory and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The two mail-fraud convictions resting on acquisition of these licenses are set aside. Each actually dependent conviction and sentence must be reconsidered under the indictment and verdict; independent convictions and surviving independent bases are not erased. No acquittal on an unreviewed theory or separate tax, laundering, or racketeering disposition is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. This common unanimous coalition directly supports both statutory answers and their application to renewal.

## Holdings

### Sovereign licensing authority is not the State's property

**Holding and operative rule.** A State's authority to issue, withhold, renew, regulate, or revoke a license held solely in its sovereign regulatory capacity is not property in its hands under §1341. Louisiana's video-poker licenses have that character, and their initial issuance or renewal through deception does not satisfy the charged State-property element merely because the permission is valuable to its recipient.

**Authority.** Thomas's unanimous Opinion of the Court directly controls this rule and its application to both initial issuance and renewal.

**Controlling explanation.** Louisiana grants permission to operate and tests the applicant's suitability; it does not operate the gaming business or transfer an owned commercial asset. The power to exclude, restrict transfer, renew, and revoke expresses regulation, not proprietary ownership. Expected licensing and gaming revenue does not change the object this prosecution charged: permission to operate, rather than unpaid money owed to the State. McNally preserves the statute's money-or-property boundary. Carpenter recognizes genuine commercially held intangible property; it does not convert every economically valuable governmental decision into such property. A license may become valuable property in the holder's hands without making the State its co-owner. That answers the renewal argument as well: continuing regulatory control does not turn the 1994 and 1995 renewals underlying the two mail-fraud convictions into acquisitions of State property. Material deception cannot replace the missing property object. The same distinction governs comparable sovereign regulatory licenses, while actual government money and commercially held assets remain protected.

### The acquisition clause retains the victim-property requirement

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 1341's acquisition-by-false-pretenses clause describes a means of property fraud; it does not independently criminalize obtaining something valuable in the recipient's hands when the charged scheme does not target property in the deceived victim's hands. The government's alternative license-acquisition theory therefore cannot sustain these convictions.

**Authority.** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this independent answer to the government's alternative statutory route.

**Controlling explanation.** The government's disjunctive reading and the absence of express language requiring acquisition from another identify textual features, but they do not overcome McNally's construction of the statute as a whole. The acquisition clause describes methods of accomplishing the property fraud the statute addresses. Its separate enactment does not establish a distinct offense whenever governmental approval creates private economic value. Reading the clause to dispense with the victim's property would erase the boundary McNally recognized. This is a requirement about the scheme's object, not a demand for completed economic loss or actual reliance; Neder's separate treatment of those matters remains intact. Because Louisiana's charged interest remains regulatory, the acquisition clause supplies no alternative basis for either license-renewal conviction.

**Limits and questions outside the grant.** The Court does not hold that a license is worthless to its holder or resolve every government franchise. A scheme actually targeting public money, unpaid revenue, or a genuine commercial asset has a different object and must satisfy its own elements. No independent honest-services route is asserted in the reviewed theory. Materiality was not granted. Count-specific work on remand determines actual dependence on the invalid predicates; it is application of the remedy, not an open property question.

## Precedent Treatment

- **McNally v. United States, 483 U.S. 350:** Applies its money-or-property limitation to the statute as a whole, including the acquisition clause; sovereign regulatory permission does not supply the missing property object.
- **Carpenter v. United States, 484 U.S. 19:** Preserves protection of genuine intangible commercial property and distinguishes that asset from licensing authority.
- **Neder v. United States, June 10, 1999:** Preserves materiality as a separate element and its rejection of completed-loss and actual-reliance requirements. It neither supplies the omitted property element nor brings the ungranted materiality issue before the Court.

## Law After Decision

Comparable permissions held by the government solely as regulator fall outside §1341's property requirement on the theory decided here, including deceptive renewals. The statute continues to reach schemes aimed at genuine public money or property. Recipient value alone cannot convert a regulatory scheme into property fraud under either statutory clause.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the consequences of setting aside the two mail-fraud convictions and for examination of the actual dependence of other convictions and sentences. Surviving independent grounds remain available for ordinary adjudication. The Court decides no unreviewed count or tax, laundering, or racketeering element.

## Source Notes

The record and licensing provisions appear in [Cleveland v. United States, 531 U.S. 12](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-12.pdf). The [United States' merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/cleveland-v-united-states-merits) supplies its acquisition-clause and renewal arguments and describes the prosecution. Different descriptions of the laundering provision do not establish a particular additional conviction's legal fate; that question must follow the actual indictment, verdict, and judgment on remand.

## Event

**Sinkfield v. Kelley**, Nos. 00-132 and 00-133; 531 U.S. 28. Submitted and decided November 27, 2000. These consolidated direct appeals by private intervenors and Alabama officials challenge the three-judge Middle District of Alabama's judgment, 96 F.Supp.2d 1301, invalidating seven majority-white legislative districts and enjoining their use. The questions are whether the plaintiffs established personal standing and, if so, whether their design-only constitutional theory supports that relief. No separate oral argument is reported.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated. The per curiam opinion publishes no numerical tally or individual join lineup.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment in both appeals and remands with instructions to dismiss the asserted complaint for lack of Article III standing.

## Judgment & Remedy

The three-judge court's seven-district injunction is dissolved. Both consolidated appeals receive vacatur and remand for jurisdictional dismissal, not a merits judgment against the asserted constitutional theory. No new map, election cancellation, or invitation to develop an unasserted dilution or participation claim is ordered. No numerical tally or individual vote lineup is published.

## Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion supplying the controlling personal-exposure ground. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### Personal exposure cannot be presumed from neighboring district design

**Holding and operative rule.** A plaintiff challenging electoral assignment must establish personal exposure to the challenged treatment, together with causation and redressability. Residence in a majority-white district adjoining majority-minority districts, and evidence that the neighboring districts influenced the overall map, do not alone establish the plaintiff's personal racial-assignment injury; the record here supplies no additional allegation or evidence connecting these plaintiffs to that treatment.

**Authority.** The per curiam Opinion of the Court directly controls this application of Article III and the resulting jurisdictional dismissal. It does not decide the constitutional merits.

**Controlling explanation.** Johnson v. Miller separates a plaintiff's personal electoral exposure from whether the asserted constitutional theory ultimately succeeds. Its evidence rule permits district shape to support an inference when linked to the claimant's actual treatment; geography is neither a universal gateway nor a substitute for that link. Bush v. Vera likewise preserves the need for a personal stake. These plaintiffs rely on unusual district shapes, adjacency, and a statewide objective of maximizing majority-minority districts. Their strongest inference is that constructing the neighboring districts necessarily sorted them by race. But the supplied allegations and evidence establish only the asserted neighboring-design effect, without the personal connection that inference requires. No independent exclusion, dilution, unequal voting weight, or legally inferior membership is established. Proof may be direct or circumstantial; the Court imposes no expert-study requirement, numerical racial threshold, or requirement of electoral defeat. Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before a contested merits judgment, and Ruhrgas's bounded permission to sequence nonmerits issues does not permit assuming the absent injury. The Court therefore directs dismissal without reaching the design-only merits question or creating an unasserted alternative claim.

**Limits and nonreach.** A plaintiff who proves personal unequal treatment may have standing even when the legal theory loses. This decision neither creates a universal district-shape bar nor adopts a residence-based entitlement to standing. The design-only merits question is not reached because these plaintiffs have not established Article III injury. Independently supported dilution, exclusion, or unequal-participation claims are outside this record; the Court decides none of them.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Johnson v. Miller, June 29, 1995:** Applies its distinct personal-exposure and shape-evidence holdings; its substantive districting rule is not reconsidered.
- **Bush v. Vera, June 13, 1996:** Preserves the separation between personal stake and substantive entitlement; it supplies no inference of injury from neighboring design alone.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co., June 2, 1997:** Applies the requirement of Article III jurisdiction before the contested merits.
- **Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., May 17, 1999:** Distinguishes permissible ordering of nonmerits questions from assuming Article III injury to decide the merits.
- **Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993:** Leaves its design-only substantive rule unchanged; this disposition rests on a separate threshold failure.

## Law After Decision

Personal exposure remains an independent requirement in districting litigation. The current substantive districting rules remain unchanged. Dismissal here carries jurisdictional, rather than merits, consequences and certifies neither the entire Alabama map nor a right to maximize majority-minority districts.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The three-judge court must dismiss the asserted complaint for lack of standing in accordance with the vacatur of its injunction. No replacement map or election cancellation follows from this judgment.

## Source Notes

The objective record and lower-court posture appear in [Sinkfield v. Kelley, 531 U.S. 28](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-28.pdf). The questions are stated in substance rather than as a verbatim petition quotation.

## Event

**Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College**, Ninth Circuit No. 98-15967; 203 F.3d 659. Decided November 27, 2000, on writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed dismissal. The question concerns retaliation against an employee for protected speech mistakenly attributed to her and the officials' preserved personal qualified-immunity defense. No separate oral-argument date is supplied.

Five anonymous letters and a flyer circulated in 1995 and 1996 criticizing the college president. A document examiner attributed them to Wasson, who denies writing or disseminating them. Officials recommended dismissal in January 1997 for alleged false and defamatory statements and unfitness; the board adopted the recommendation and placed her on paid leave pending appeal. The notice was withdrawn without prejudice and she was reinstated in March 1997. The amended complaint names President Agrella and Vice President Roberts individually. Her denial and properly pleaded retaliatory conduct are accepted at this stage; the writings' protected status is not established merely by calling them criticism.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms dismissal of the individual-capacity damages action on qualified immunity without adopting the court of appeals' actual-speech requirement.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 8–1. Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stone-Zsela. The complaint remains dismissed against Agrella and Roberts personally. No damages, reinstatement, declaration of constitutional liability, or renewed disciplinary action is ordered. No independent live prospective claim is established: the pleaded period for reviving the notice expired October 28, 2000. Reinstatement alone does not moot the past damages claim.

## Opinion Topology

Ginsburg delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela files a dissent seeking reversal and remand. His proposed constitutional extension and replacement immunity defense do not command another join.

## Holdings

### The preserved personal damages defense disposes of this action

**Holding and operative rule.** An official remains protected from individual damages when the asserted federal restriction was not clearly established by the law governing the conduct, assessed using all relevant authority at the necessary particularity. January 1997 law did not clearly establish the claimed personal First Amendment right against punishment for speech the employee denies making; these defendants receive qualified immunity without a decision recognizing or rejecting that constitutional extension.

**Authority.** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court and its seven joins directly support the immunity ruling, its conduct-date inquiry, and the reservation of the constitutional question.

**Controlling explanation.** Harlow and Anderson require objective, particularized legal notice, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant authority rather than only the cases the parties or lower court found. The question is not disposed of merely by the absence of an identical case. Pickering and Connick identify protection for employee expression and legitimate employment interests; Mt. Healthy separates protected conduct, substantial motivation, and the employer's same-decision defense. Waters controls specified mistakes about speech actually made, without a complete majority investigation framework. It did not decide a mistake about who spoke. McIntyre protects actual anonymous advocacy against a particular identification requirement, not the distinct employment-liability extension. Wasson's argument that purposeful suppression injures the person targeted, even if officials selected the wrong speaker, presents a substantial constitutional question. Those authorities nevertheless did not settle that extension before these officials acted. Immunity does not require resolving it now: Elder imposes no mandatory merits-first sequence, and Crawford-El permits a genuinely adequate legal defense to be addressed first. Crawford-El's June 1997 procedural decision supplies no January notice. Accepting the allegations, the Court therefore affirms personal damages dismissal without finding the investigation reasonable, the writings unprotected, or the alleged conduct lawful.

**Limits and nonreach.** The Court adopts no actual-authorship prerequisite, free-standing investigation tort, heightened motive-proof requirement, or rule that every identity mistake creates liability. The constitutional question is left open because the preserved immunity defense independently disposes of the only established live relief. No third-party standing holding is necessary; the asserted injury is considered as Wasson's own. No municipal claim or continuing prospective threat is established by the amended complaint.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton:** Apply and retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the defense is not replaced by an affirmative-authoritative-permission requirement.
- **Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994:** Applies independent review of all relevant legal authority and preserves the absence of a mandatory merits-first sequence.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Preserves its precise majority rules about actual speech and mistaken accounts, its distinct immunity defense, and its unresolved complete investigation framework; it does not settle mistaken speaker identity.
- **Pickering v. Board of Education; Connick v. Myers; Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle:** Preserve their substantive protection, employment-interest, causation, and same-decision rules without deciding their extension here.
- **McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission, April 19, 1995:** Distinguishes its protection for actual anonymous advocacy from the unresolved identity-attribution claim.
- **Crawford-El v. Britton, June 16, 1997:** Applies permission to resolve an adequate legal defense first and preserves ordinary motive proof; its date supplies no retroactive notice.

## Law After Decision

The clearly-established-law defense remains operative for individual damages. The Court leaves the constitutional status of retaliation for mistakenly attributed speech open and does not adopt the Ninth Circuit's categorical actual-speech rationale. Existing speech protection and ordinary motive-proof rules remain unchanged.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The dissent would recognize an employee's direct claim when attributed expression protected by the public-employment rules substantially motivates an adverse employment action, subject to the employer's proof that it would have acted for an independent lawful reason. The First Amendment constrains the punitive use of expression against the person officials target; mistaken identity does not remove that person's injury or justify suppressing protected criticism. Actual authorship would therefore be unnecessary. Pickering–Connick would determine protection and legitimate employment interests, and Mt. Healthy would govern causation and the same-decision defense. Erroneous attribution or an imperfect investigation alone would prove neither liability nor the other elements. Waters's limited protection for genuine reliance on a reasonable account of disciplinable speech would remain; it would not protect hostility to protected criticism. Letters, notices, chronology, testimony, and competent circumstantial evidence could establish the actual ground under ordinary Crawford-El procedure, without a statistical requirement.

The dissent would also replace Harlow's defense insofar as lack of sufficiently similar precedent defeats damages after an underlying violation is established. An official would instead have to prove actual and objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly permitting the challenged conduct. Legal silence, custom, or general supervisory power would not suffice. In the dissent's view, §1983's remedy and the protections for reasonable factual mistakes within the substantive inquiry make that narrower reliance defense adequate. Elder's independent consideration of relevant law and ordinary procedural protections would survive, as would independent absolute immunity. This would change existing immunity doctrine, including positions Stone-Zsela previously accepted. He would reverse and remand to determine protected status, adverse action, motive, causation, any supported reliance defense, and compensation for proved injury. No damages judgment would follow now, and prospective relief would require an actual continuing threat rather than a presumed revival of the expired notice.

## Procedure After Action

Dismissal of the operative individual-capacity action stands. No constitutional merits remand, damages award, reinstatement order, or revived disciplinary deadline follows.

## Event

**City of Indianapolis v. Edmond**, No. 99-1030; 531 U.S. 32. Argued October 3, 2000; decided November 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 183 F.3d 659, which reversed the denial of a preliminary injunction. The question is whether checkpoints principally designed to discover illegal narcotics may seize motorists without individualized suspicion. The program checks licenses and registration, looks for impairment, and uses drug-detection dogs around stopped vehicles. Its announced primary objective is narcotics interdiction; no immediate pursuit, particular emergency, or individualized drug suspicion justifies these stops.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms and remands for appropriate preliminary prospective relief preventing operation of the identified drug-interdiction checkpoint program while the case proceeds.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas. The relief restrains this program at the preliminary-injunction stage. It prescribes no replacement checkpoint, invalidates no independently supported traffic stop, and awards neither suppression nor individual damages.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Entire opinion; affirmance and preliminary prospective restraint |
| Dissent, Part I | Thomas | Scalia | Reversal under the genuine mixed-function roadway justification |
| Dissent, Part II | Thomas | No other Justice | Additional objection to the primary-purpose inquiry and reservation about checkpoint precedents |

## Holdings

### Ordinary drug investigation does not authorize a suspicionless checkpoint

**Holding and operative rule.** Stopping a vehicle at a checkpoint is a seizure before any exterior canine examination occurs. A checkpoint whose primary program purpose is gathering evidence of ordinary narcotics offenses cannot dispense with individualized suspicion merely because stops are brief and systematic or also perform license and sobriety checks; an independently lawful justification is necessary.

**Authority.** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer, directly controls the rule and this preliminary-relief disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** The city's strongest argument begins with the genuine license and impairment checks and asks why an added drug objective should invalidate those same brief stops. That assumes the antecedent authority for the suspicionless seizure. Whren permits an officer to act on actual probable cause despite another investigative motive; it supplies no probable cause here. Program purpose instead identifies the category of authority asserted for stopping everyone without suspicion. Sitz addresses an immediate highway-safety program, and Martinez-Fuerte a particular border-control setting. Prouse does not authorize every evidence-gathering roadblock simply because vehicle selection is systematic. Chandler confirms that reduced intrusion cannot replace an adequate actual need. If a secondary lawful task could justify a program principally directed to ordinary crime detection, the limited exceptions would become a general power to stop motorists for investigation. The serious drug problem and short duration therefore cannot supply the missing authority. The city's admitted principal objective makes a purpose-finding remand unnecessary. The dog sniff's classification cannot cure the antecedent seizure; the Court need not decide that separate question. The program must be restrained while the case proceeds.

**Scope and limits.** Program instructions, official statements, and actual practices may establish purpose; no statistical study, universal numerical-efficacy threshold, catastrophe requirement, or exhaustion of every less intrusive alternative is imposed. This rule reaches comparable ordinary crime-detection roadblocks. It does not invalidate every checkpoint associated with criminal enforcement or lawful action on evidence properly encountered during a genuinely justified stop. The Court does not decide whether a checkpoint primarily justified by license or sobriety checking may be expanded to detect narcotics. Urgent threats, emergency roadblocks, airport or government-building security, border authority outside the settings addressed, individualized-suspicion searches, and a particular canine examination are outside this challenge. Suppression and damages are not presented.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Whren v. United States:** Preserves objective probable-cause authority and purpose-limited administrative exceptions; an officer's motive in an authorized stop differs from the justification for a suspicionless program.
- **Chandler v. Miller:** Applies the need for an actual adequate justification without converting its contextual analysis into a universal checklist.
- **Michigan Department of State Police v. Sitz:** Distinguishes its highway-safety purpose and preserves its rule within that setting.
- **United States v. Martinez-Fuerte:** Distinguishes its border-control justification and preserves that limited authority.
- **Delaware v. Prouse:** Preserves its limits; systematic operation does not itself authorize ordinary crime-detection seizures.
- **Bond v. United States:** Preserves the distinction between lawful presence and authority for a separate intrusion; it supplies no independent checkpoint-purpose rule.

## Law After Decision

The primary ordinary-crime-control purpose forecloses the claimed suspicionless-checkpoint justification for this program and comparable roadblocks. Lawful roadway, border, and other independently justified programs retain their own requirements. Minimizing intrusion and adding an ancillary lawful task do not replace the missing authority.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia.** The dissent would reverse because this program genuinely performs systematic license and sobriety checks that provide an independent roadway justification. Under the dissent's reading of Whren and the checkpoint precedents, the added narcotics objective and exterior dog use do not invalidate a stop whose actual operation stays within that legitimate scope. The dissent answers the concern about nominal checks by requiring real, independently supportable roadway functions and lawful execution; it does not approve a drug-only checkpoint merely because it efficiently detects crime. It would return the case for proceedings under that objective mixed-function rule rather than approve every execution detail.

**Thomas, dissenting, Part II, joined by no other Justice.** Thomas would apply the restrained scope and objective reasonableness analysis of the checkpoint cases to these brief systematic automobile seizures. He rejects a primary-purpose threshold transferred from suspicionless searches of bodies, homes, or businesses. He also questions whether indiscriminate stops accord with the Fourth Amendment's original understanding, but does not seek to overrule Sitz or Martinez-Fuerte here because that issue was not developed for decision. Scalia joins neither this additional critique nor that reservation.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for appropriate preliminary prospective restraint against the identified program while litigation continues. No replacement program, suppression ruling, or damages award is ordered.

## Source Notes

Edmond, 531 U.S. 32](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-32.pdf). The Court resolves the admitted primary-purpose issue without presuming a different administrative objective or an emergency.

## Event

**Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America**, No. 99-1038; 531 U.S. 57. Argued October 2, 2000; decided November 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 188 F.3d 501, which affirmed enforcement of a labor-arbitration award. The question is whether the actual award conditionally reinstating a safety-sensitive truck driver after a second positive marijuana test violates an explicit governing public policy. The employer does not challenge the arbitrator's authority under the agreement to decide just cause.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms enforcement of the conditional reinstatement award.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The employer must comply with the award's actual sanctions and reinstatement conditions, while applicable rehabilitation, testing, and return-to-duty law remains independently binding. No immediate unrestricted driving entitlement, replacement disciplinary sanction, or additional damages are awarded.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. The unanimous opinion rests on absence of conflict between the actual conditional award and the law-grounded policy asserted against it.

## Holdings

### The actual conditional award does not conflict with the governing safety policy

**Holding and operative rule.** A court reviewing this labor award must identify an explicit, well-defined, dominant public policy grounded in law and legal precedent and a conflict between that policy and the performance the award requires. The transportation regime permits rehabilitation and qualified return to safety-sensitive work; because this award's sanctions and conditions preserve those safeguards, a general safety objection to the employee's repeated prior drug use does not justify vacating reinstatement.

**Authority.** Breyer's unanimous Opinion of the Court directly supports the legal-policy inquiry, its application to the complete award, and enforcement with all conditions intact.

**Controlling explanation.** Misco and W. R. Grace require law-grounded policy and demonstrated conflict, rather than a court's preference about proper discipline. Enterprise Wheel protects the contractually entrusted just-cause determination; Eastern does not contest the arbitrator's authority to make it. The legal question therefore concerns what the award requires now, not whether the employee's past drug use was desirable or permissible. The governing transportation law addresses this risk through disqualification, professional evaluation, any prescribed rehabilitation, testing, and permitted qualified return. Its rehabilitation provisions cannot be discarded in favor of a judicial permanent-discharge rule. The second award also imposes substantial sanctions, continued treatment and testing obligations, and a future-resignation condition. Repeated drug use strengthens the safety concern but does not establish that performing this conditional award conflicts with the governing policy. Employment reinstatement does not certify present fitness to drive or excuse a legal return requirement. First Options is consistent with separating assigned contractual questions from legal restraints, but the controlling review doctrine here remains section 301 labor law. The actual policy objection fails without a categorical ruling about every possible outer boundary of the exception.

**The actual awards.** After the March 1996 positive test, the first award imposed thirty days' unpaid suspension, substance-abuse-program participation, and testing at the employer's or approved professional's discretion over five years. Four intervening tests were negative before the July 1997 positive test. The second award imposes an unpaid suspension slightly longer than three months; reimbursement of both the employer and union for the costs of both arbitration proceedings; continued program participation; continued random testing; and a signed undated resignation effective upon another positive test within the next five years. No such future trigger is found to have occurred.

**Independent return-to-duty requirements.** Before return to safety-sensitive work, the driver must receive professional evaluation of treatment need, obtain professional certification of compliance with any prescribed rehabilitation, and pass a return-to-duty drug test. At least six random tests are required in the first year after return. Treatment depends on the professional assessment and resulting prescription; the award's program-participation condition remains independently operative. Reinstatement establishes no completed compliance or entitlement to drive on a particular date.

**Limits.** The Court resolves the entire objection to this award, not a universal right to reinstatement or immunity for unsafe work. A materially different award requiring performance contrary to governing legal policy remains reviewable. Whether every public-policy objection is confined to performance directly forbidden by positive law is not necessary to this no-conflict disposition and is not decided. The arbitrator's credibility and employment-history assessments are not retried; a positive test is not a finding of driving under the influence.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United Paperworkers International Union v. Misco:** Applies its law-grounded public-policy and actual-conflict requirements; courts may not substitute a preferred discipline for entrusted arbitration.
- **W. R. Grace & Co. v. Local Union:** Applies the explicit, well-defined, dominant legal-policy inquiry and independent judicial responsibility for that legal question.
- **United Steelworkers v. Enterprise Wheel & Car Corp.:** Preserves the arbitrator's contractually assigned just-cause and remedial function; that authority is not challenged here.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Preserves the distinction between actual contractual delegation and legal review; its commercial-arbitration rules do not replace section 301 labor-award doctrine.

## Law After Decision

The actual conditional reinstatement remains enforceable under the received narrow public-policy exception. The employer and employee remain bound by both the award and the independent safety-sensitive qualification rules. The decision creates no judicial permanent-discharge mandate or broader public-policy exception.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment enforcing the award stands. All award sanctions, treatment and testing conditions, the five-year resignation trigger, and applicable return-to-duty requirements remain effective on their own terms. No further judicial disciplinary remedy or finding of completed compliance is entered.

## Source Notes

The agreement, two awards, and recited transportation requirements appear in [Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America, 531 U.S. 57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-57.pdf). The arbitrator's assessments remain arbitral findings. No later drug test, actual return to driving, or completed rehabilitation is established by this decision.

## Event

**Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board**, No. 00-836; 531 U.S. 70. Argued December 1, 2000; decided December 4, 2000. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) to review the Florida Supreme Court's November 21 certification judgment. That judgment reversed the trial orders and supplied a final binding certification directive with no rehearing or further merits adjudication in that proceeding. The submitted federal questions concern alleged post-election alteration under due process, then-effective 3 U.S.C. §5, and the legislature's Article II authority. No independent equal-protection question is presented here.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the November 21 judgment and remands for immediate clarification of the identified state-law grounds and federal-timing premise, followed by lawful further proceedings in the state court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The state court must clarify two matters: first, whether the late-return discretion limits and selected acceptance date independently implement the pre-election code and what role state constitutional power plays; second, whether a particular federal electoral requirement, including §5, was understood to require that date or the full-participation condition and how that understanding affected the remedy. The Court directs no particular answer, tally, ballot rejection, elector selection, or winner. This disposition supplies no independent stay and prohibits no lawful recount.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing or part | Author | Joined by | Scope and judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per curiam, Parts I and II | The Court | All nine participating Justices | Review channel, state-law account, and Article II/federal-timing clarification ground |
| Per curiam, Part III | The Court | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Distinct due-process explanation; Stone-Zsela concurs only in this component's remand disposition |
| Per curiam, Part IV | The Court | All nine participating Justices | Complete vacatur and immediate, bounded remand |

## Holdings

### The state judgment requires two precise clarifications before the federal merits can be decided

**Holding and operative rule.** When a final state judgment's unresolved legal dependence prevents sound resolution of the federal challenge to that judgment, the Court may vacate and remand for authoritative clarification of the material state grounds. Here the state court must identify the statutory and constitutional basis for its late-return restrictions and chosen acceptance date, and the role of its federal-timing understanding, before the submitted Article II, §5, and due-process merits can be resolved.

**Authority.** Parts I, II, and IV of the per curiam opinion have all nine Justices' joins and directly support the clarification disposition under §§1257 and 2106 and Minnesota v. National Tea. Part III's distinct due-process explanation has the eight Associate Justices' joins; Stone-Zsela joins that component's disposition only.

**Controlling explanation.** Florida's opinion contains substantial statutory reasoning: it distinguishes tabulation from the tabulating system, reconciles mandatory filing language with the later permissive late-return provision, preserves the county filing deadline, and considers the pre-existing manual process. The Court cannot treat that analysis as nonexistent or revise state law merely because it would read the code differently. The concrete gap concerns the further two-condition restriction on exclusion and the equitable acceptance date. The opinion does not definitively identify whether those terms rest independently on construction of enacted law or on constitutional power to displace a legislative restriction, nor whether a particular federal requirement compelled the remedy. Those dependencies matter to the actual federal challenge. McPherson supplies Article II's legislative-direction setting, but Article II does not categorically exclude ordinary state judicial interpretation. Smiley concerns a different congressional-election setting and does not settle this application. Immediate invalidation would assume legislative displacement; immediate affirmance would assume that the disputed remedial terms independently construe legislative law. National Tea permits the defined antecedent clarification needed to avoid either assumption. The state court receives the case for clarification and lawful further proceedings; the Court does not retain jurisdiction while awaiting an answer.

**Known state-law predicates.** The following distinctions govern the scope of the clarification; they are not newly enacted federal recount rules:

| Provision or state-court construction | Material terms |
|---|---|
| Manual-recount request, §102.166 | An eligible candidate, political committee, or party may make a reasoned request before county certification or within 72 hours after midnight of election day, whichever is later. The board may authorize a sample of at least three precincts and at least one percent of the relevant vote, with precincts selected by the requester. |
| Qualifying sample error | An error that could affect the outcome requires one of three statutory responses: correction and a system recount of remaining precincts, software verification, or a manual count of all ballots. It does not invariably require the third. Public counting, statutory team procedures, and voter-intent procedures apply; an indeterminate ballot goes to the board. No rule makes every undervote an ascertainable vote. |

This is a judicial remedy, not an enacted date; no later office opening, return, or total is found here. |

### Federal timing and fundamental fairness remain distinct from the state-law clarification

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 5 offers conclusive federal-count treatment for a qualifying final determination made under pre-election law at least six days before the electors meet; it does not itself prohibit every count after that date. The due-process question separately remains governed by Yoshina's distinction between authoritative construction of ambiguity and fundamental unfairness, and is returned with the other federal components for consideration on clarified state-law predicates.

**Authority.** Part II's description of §5 and limited timing remand has all nine joins. Part III's due-process ground is the direct holding of the eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with its remand disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** The supplied federal dates are December 12 and December 18. The availability of conclusive treatment does not itself make December 12 an absolute state-law end date or establish that Florida has secured or forfeited the benefit. Foster addresses premature final congressional selection and expressly leaves presidential timing outside its holding. It supplies no automatic stopping rule here. On due process, Yoshina rejects the proposition that a neutral authoritative construction becomes fundamentally unfair merely because an official previously advised otherwise. Actual guidance, dissemination, inducement, reliance, and voting circumstances matter without becoming a rigid new checklist. This source establishes conflicting post-election advice, not a particular pre-election assurance or detrimental reliance; that limit proves no litigant default. The separate contention that a clear election rule was displaced rather than an ambiguity construed depends partly on the legal basis still requiring clarification. The Court therefore neither rejects the entire due-process question for lack of reliance facts nor treats interpretive change alone as sufficient for relief.

**Questions not decided.** The Court enters no final Article II or due-process violation, no §5 qualification ruling, and no categorical prohibition on state constitutional interpretation. Those merits depend on the unresolved authoritative state-law premises identified above. Later contest procedures, ballot standards, recount feasibility, elector totals, and the lawful winner lie outside this certification case.

Ordinary state interpretation remains available subject to enforceable federal limits, but no final Article II, due-process, or §5 merits rule is announced for this judgment. The federal benefit's conditions remain distinct from a prohibition on later counting.

## Holdings

### Maine carries its burden for the legislative contribution limit on this record

**Holding and operative rule.** A direct candidate-contribution ceiling must be closely drawn to an objectively supported, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest while preserving meaningful political association and effective advocacy in the actual campaign setting. Maine satisfies that burden for the presented $250 per-election legislative ceiling; the holding establishes neither a general $250 safe harbor nor a requirement that challengers prove campaigning impossible.

**Authority.** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, directly adopts the six-Justice rule and application.

**Controlling explanation.** Russell and Shrink Missouri require actual anticorruption fit and usable political association, not equality of influence or a fixed constitutional dollar floor. Maine's evidence connects contributor leverage in relatively small legislative campaigns to pressure for official accommodation, including threatened financial withdrawal to change a legislator's position. That is more than ordinary agreement or access; completed bribery and statistical proof are unnecessary. Modest campaign scale, actual fundraising under the new ceiling, per-election operation, and continued usable private channels together support the State's affirmative case. Contrary projections of lost receipts, particularly for challengers and competitive Senate races, are serious evidence. They freeze donor patterns and therefore establish a risk rather than every predicted future loss. Special-election experience corroborates capacity but differs from ordinary competition for donors and is not conclusive. Nor does a percentage of donors below the ceiling measure dollars lost. On the combined record, the practical capacity and direct-transfer risk justify this amount despite those concerns. Referendum support cannot itself settle constitutional rights. A different suppressive application would require its own examination; public funding cannot cure an independently invalid private restriction.

### Both bounded responsive-grant triggers survive the presented challenge

**Holding and operative rule.** Maine's capped, support-qualified public-financing mechanism may provide an enrolled candidate additional grants in response to either a private opponent's candidate-financing measure or qualifying independent advocacy, where its demonstrated programmatic connection and operation establish neither an independent legal restraint nor substantial practical suppression of opposing lawful activity. This holding sustains these two triggers, not an unlimited power to offset private speech or a categorical rule that subsidies never burden it.

**Authority.** Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to each trigger and its stated limits. No combination of separate opinions or narrowest-rationale inference supplies this majority.

**Controlling explanation.** The strongest objection is that protected fundraising or advocacy causes the State to provide a competitive advantage to an opponent, encouraging the initiating speaker to remain below the trigger. That consequence must be examined, not dismissed by labeling the program voluntary. Here the private speaker keeps its funds, message, and lawful fundraising and spending freedom; no endorsement, attribution, or private expenditure ceiling follows. The grant supports a qualified participant who has accepted financing constraints in an actual contest, is finite, and is adjusted for specified independent spending already favoring that participant. Those features establish the programmatic connection, while the record establishes competitive effects rather than substantial suppression of the private route. Buckley permits a voluntary financing option with participant conditions; Reform Party rejects an automatic right to parity or insulation from an opponent's public resources while preserving actual suppression review. Day's invalidating judgment remains binding for its materially different mechanism. Its four-Justice expressive-burden rationale never became a controlling rule, and Stone-Zsela's separate rationale supplies none. Maine's qualifications, cap, restrictions, adjustment, and demonstrated operation support this decision on their own terms. Independent expenditures remain independent speech, not coordinated contributions merely because a grant formula measures them.

### The meaningful private alternative defeats the additional combination claim

**Holding and operative rule.** A capped public-financing option and the sustained contribution limit do not establish unconstitutional coercion where their actual combined operation leaves a meaningful private campaigning alternative. The State may not rely on nominal voluntariness or public funding to excuse an independently unconstitutional restraint; this record establishes no additional substantial suppression requiring invalidation of the whole option.

**Authority.** Part III has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. O'Connor rejects an additional systemwide ground while preserving her separate objection to matching; Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas do not reach this additional theory and add no sustaining votes.

**Controlling explanation.** Lawful components can combine into an unconstitutional burden, so separate review does not end the inquiry. The public grant carries qualification, spending, and private-financing restrictions, while the record shows a usable private route in these campaigns. The actual contribution capacity, grant cap, participant obligations, and conditional shortage rule support that conclusion together. Attractive incentives and a better-funded opponent are not by themselves compulsion, but formal permission to remain private would be insufficient if practical suppression were established. No fixed funding percentage, mandatory study, or proof that a candidate disappeared is required. The cumulative record here supports rejection of the additional whole-system claim without approving every financing incentive or eliminating review of a genuinely different suppressive application.

**Operative statutory qualifications.** The legislative ceiling is per election; the individual candidate's own and spouse's contributions retain §1015(1)'s exception. That exception does not remove separate restrictions accepted by a certified public participant. A legislative participant must file the declaration, obtain ballot qualification and compliant seed funding, and collect qualifying $5 contributions from at least 150 registered district voters for a Senate race or 50 for a House race. Contributions go to the fund by check or money order during the qualifying period with the candidate's knowledge and approval, an identifying receipt, and no exchanged value. Seed money is limited to $100 per individual, including candidate or family funds, and aggregate legislative amounts of $1,500 for Senate or $500 for House, subject to Commission rule authority. Unspent seed money transfers to the fund at certification. Later private contributions require specific Commission authorization.

Contested-election initial grants use the corresponding office's average expenditures in contested races in the prior two applicable elections, with the initial 2000 amount reduced by 25 percent; inadequate data permits use of the most recent applicable elections. An uncontested primary uses the corresponding uncontested average or the contested average if lower. No grant issues for an uncontested general election.

The matching measure is the greater of expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, alone or with qualifying independent expenditures reported under §1019. Once that measure exceeds the applicable initial distribution, the Commission pays the opposing certified candidate the reported excess. Supplements stop at twice the relevant contested-primary or contested-general initial distribution, making the ordinary combined maximum three times that distribution. The regulatory deduction reduces matching payments for independent express advocacy favoring the participant or opposing the private opponent after that opponent exceeds the initial distribution; it never reduces the initial grant. No unpresented multiple-opponent formula is decided.

Section 1125(13) forbids spending beyond available fund revenue. If revenue cannot meet the initial or matching distributions, the Commission may authorize certified candidates to accept and spend private contributions up to the applicable funding amounts, reduced by seed money and subject to the legislative $250 per-donor-per-election ceiling and Commission rules. This is conditional permission, not a mandatory release or unlimited financing right. No actual shortage is found.

**Scope.** These qualifications describe the mechanism adjudicated; unchallenged administrative details receive no independent constitutional holding. The separate §1019 disclosure challenge, governor ceiling, unsupported party-specific theory, and unpursued primary-allocation and §1017 theories are outside this grant. Statutory participant terminology establishes no government command to stigmatize nonparticipants.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The dissent accepts Russell's and Shrink Missouri's contribution standard and recognizes that the influence evidence justifies some prophylaxis. ## Event

**Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph**, No. 99-1235; 531 U.S. 79. Argued October 3, 2000; decided December 11, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 178 F.3d 1149. The questions are whether an order compelling arbitration and dismissing all claims is appealable under 9 U.S.C. §16(a)(3), and whether an agreement's silence about costs makes arbitration of federal statutory claims unenforceable.

Randolph's mobile-home financing agreement required arbitration and insurance protecting the lender against default losses. She alleged inadequate Truth in Lending Act disclosure of the insurance requirement and an Equal Credit Opportunity Act violation associated with compulsory arbitration. The district court compelled arbitration and dismissed every claim with prejudice. The contract states neither arbitral fees nor their allocation; the record identifies no likely forum, charges, or amount Randolph would bear.

## Holdings

### A complete dismissal is a final appealable decision

**Holding and operative rule.** An order compelling arbitration and dismissing every claim ends the action and is final and appealable under §16(a)(3). A stay that leaves claims pending remains a distinct interlocutory disposition; appealability does not decide whether dismissal instead of a stay was proper.

**Authority.** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Catlin measures finality by what remains for the district court to decide. Nothing remained after this dismissal with prejudice. A possible later confirmation or other award proceeding is a separate action, not an uncompleted claim in this one. The assertion that arbitration was embedded in a suit containing substantive claims supplies no exception to §16(a)(3)'s final-decision language. Digital Equipment restricts collateral appeals before final judgment; it does not defeat review after an actual complete dismissal. The Court therefore sustains appellate jurisdiction without approving dismissal in every arbitration case or deciding the separate stay question.

### Contractual silence does not decide financial access

**Holding and operative rule.** Silence about arbitral costs alone makes an agreement neither unenforceable nor demonstrably affordable. Genuinely prohibitive required costs may prevent effective assertion of federal statutory rights; the present categorical ruling must be vacated for a focused opportunity to establish likely forum, allocation, and financial access before this cost objection is finally resolved.

**Authority.** Part II-A's rejection of categorical silence-based invalidity and recognition of genuine access barriers has all nine joins. Part II-B's required focused proceedings have five direct joins: Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** Mitsubishi and Gilmer permit an arbitral forum while preserving substantive statutory protections. Allied-Bruce preserves actual enforceability questions, and First Options preserves the relevant antecedent judicial inquiry within its consent and delegation limits; coverage does not answer every access objection. Randolph's assertion that she cannot pay and the contract's silence establish no likely fee or allocation. Yet the absence of those facts also does not establish an accessible forum. The correct response on this undeveloped record is a bounded inquiry into the actual mechanism and likely expense, not the court of appeals' categorical invalidity or an assumed lender obligation to pay. Randolph must substantiate an actual obstacle during appropriate proceedings. A binding allocation or undertaking may remove it; a nonbinding appellate assurance does not amend the contract. The inquiry remains confined to likely mandatory access costs and the claimant's ability to pursue these claims. It supplies neither a general entitlement to costless dispute resolution nor a universal lender-payment rule. No finding that discovery was previously requested, denied, or exhausted is necessary to this remand.

### General initial proof allocation remains divided

**No controlling general allocation.** The Court requires substantiation of a real cost obstacle and directs the focused opportunity stated above. Beyond those holdings, no majority adopts one complete initial-proof rule governing the trigger, consequences of nonproduction, and unavailable cost information. Stone-Zsela's concrete-likelihood requirement includes ordinary-procedure disclosure of material information controlled by the party seeking compulsion and the present opportunity to develop the objection. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would apply their likelihood requirement to reject this showing without that required opportunity and do not adopt his disclosure qualification. A shared label cannot establish an identical operative rule.

Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg reject deriving the financial-access allocation merely from the burden applicable to categorical congressional exclusion of statutory claims from arbitration, but supply no comprehensive replacement. Breyer joins the focused inquiry while reserving that broader critique. The general allocation question therefore remains open for lack of a majority at the necessary level, not because the present remedy is incomplete. No narrowest-rationale combination changes those joins.

**Remand conditions and limits.** The lower court must determine likely required costs, contractual or legally binding allocation, and effective access. If the obstacle is unproved or lawfully removed, the cost objection does not prevent arbitration; if established, enforcement must be withheld to the extent necessary, subject to a lawful binding solution. Properly presented independent grounds, including the class-action alternative the Eleventh Circuit did not decide, remain for adjudication. The Court decides neither the TILA or ECOA merits nor class relief, unrelated clauses, or whether the district court should originally have stayed the case.

## Holdings

### I. The operative decree is reviewable within the preserved federal challenge

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 1257(a) permits review under Cox's fourth category where the State's highest court has conclusively authorized challenged relief despite a substantial federal objection that would foreclose that relief, later nonfederal success could eliminate review, and postponement could seriously impair the asserted federal policy. Those conditions permit review of this operative decree; they do not authorize review of hypothetical implementation or unfinished individual-ballot rulings.

**Authority.** Part I; Souter and seven Associates, eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only in taking review. The eight-member rationale controls.

**Controlling explanation.** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn identifies this limited route through an otherwise unfinished state proceeding. Goodyear Atomic Corp. v. Miller distinguishes a substantial asserted federal bar, which can support immediate review, from success on that bar's merits. The state court conclusively ordered inclusion and examination despite the claim that Article II forbids that relief because it displaces legislative rules. Acceptance of that claim would foreclose the commanded relief, rather than merely alter an evidentiary ruling. A later victory on counting grounds could remove the petitioners' need for review; delayed review during the bounded presidential selection process could defeat effective vindication of the asserted constitutional allocation.

The contrary finality argument correctly observes that counting, disputed figures, and further relief remain unfinished. Those tasks do not defeat the stated Cox ground, but urgency, party agreement, and the absence of rehearing cannot independently create jurisdiction. The December 6 state brief presented Article II objections to the alleged new dimple rule and altered certification and contest periods, together with equality and due-process objections. The December 8 denial of leave concerned the different blanket claim that §102.168 never applies to presidential elections. The Court does not decide that blanket claim or treat its rejected filing as erasing earlier presentation. Review remains confined to the operative decree and the preserved constitutional objections.

### II. Materially inconsistent criteria cannot govern comparable votes in one statewide judicial determination

**Holding and operative rule.** When a statewide judicial vote determination combines recovered votes and further examination, equal protection forbids materially arbitrary differences in the operative criteria applied to legally comparable ballots. The supervising court must supply sufficiently definite, consistent, and reviewable applications of the existing legal-vote rule; relevant differences in physical evidence and ordinary variation without material unequal treatment remain permissible.

**Authority.** Part II; Souter, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer, seven direct joins. Stevens and Ginsburg reject the finding of a violation in this decree.

**Controlling explanation.** Harper v. Virginia Board of Elections and Reynolds v. Sims protect equal electoral participation and weight. Their principle reaches arbitrary criteria for crediting votes already cast without prescribing a national physical-mark code. Burdick permits reasonable neutral administration; it does not excuse materially conflicting legal criteria. Shaw's rejection of territorial design alone as a deprivation does not immunize unequal valuation of actual ballots.

The defect here is concrete. Spencer described differing Miami-Dade thresholds for dimples, paper separation, and light or another indication. Burton described successive Palm Beach approaches and refinement of the whole-ballot inquiry, without establishing that every affected earlier determination received consistent reconsideration. The decree combines recovered votes with further examination under a general intent command while leaving these operative conflicts unresolved. Honest officials and a lawful general standard do not themselves provide the necessary common method before those votes enter one judicial result.

Different physical evidence can rationally support different conclusions; mere disagreement, equipment variation, or an unclear mark establishes no violation. But differing thresholds for what evidence qualifies as a vote are not necessarily ordinary judgment under a common rule. The constitutional finding requires neither bad faith nor proof of a changed winner. It does not depend on complaint-only Broward allegations, proved duplicate votes, a complete matched-ballot study, or the proposition that every overvote contains a recoverable legal vote. The rule applies beyond these candidates and this election.

### III. The presented Article II objections do not independently invalidate the challenged directions

**Holding and operative rule.** Article II preserves the legislature's choice of the manner of appointing presidential electors but does not prohibit ordinary judicial interpretation and enforcement of the election code the legislature enacted. The challenged inclusion and examination directions, read within that code's contest grounds and existing legal-vote requirements, do not establish a material displacement warranting independent reversal; a remedy substituting materially new substantive rules would remain forbidden.

**Authority.** Part III; Souter, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer, six direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting independent reversal. Scalia and Thomas dissent on two distinct displacement objections.

**Controlling explanation.** Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board requires attention to actual statutory authority, rather than either conclusive deference to a state label or a presumption that all interpretation changes the legislature's scheme. The legislature enacted a contest distinct from the protest process, expressly covering rejected legal votes sufficient to change or place the result in doubt and supplying investigative and corrective authority. Those provisions permit examination before the contestant proves who won. They do not confine the trial judge to appellate review of a board's discretion.

The December 11 clarification grounds late-return treatment in the conflict between mandatory and permissive provisions, manual-review authority, and the code's other timing obligations. The clear-intent provisions supply a pre-existing substantive basis for deciding whether an ascertainable legal vote was rejected. Administrative practice and an asserted restrictive interpretation do not conclusively resolve the enacted language.

The opposing view correctly insists that general remedial power cannot erase substantive voting requirements, certification's remaining operation, or the legislature's allocation of responsibility. Here the particular contest and intent provisions support the state court's construction without that erasure. This conclusion does not convert every undervote into a legal vote, prescribe a new mark requirement, authorize unlimited late amendments, or resolve the precise remaining timing consequence. The distinct equal-treatment defect still requires correction, and any correction must implement existing law.

### IV. Interpretive disagreement does not establish the separate due-process claim

**Holding and operative rule.** A neutral judicial construction of an ambiguous election provision does not alone establish fundamental unfairness because it differs from an asserted earlier practice or advisory interpretation. A supported claim of official inducement or frustrated electoral choice requires examination of the actual directions, dissemination, reliance, and voting circumstances; this record establishes no separate due-process ground for reversal.

**Authority.** Part IV; Souter and seven Associates, eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition of this component.

**Controlling explanation.** Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina, January 21, 1999, requires an actual unfairness inquiry, not automatic invalidation of an authoritative construction after contrary advice. Palm Beach preserved that distinct inquiry and did not make the Article II and due-process questions interchangeable. The pre-existing contest and voter-intent provisions, together with the state court's statutory explanation, defeat an inference of unfairness merely from interpretive change.

No verified pre-election assurance, its dissemination, or detrimental reliance establishes the separate deprivation asserted here. The competing interpretation of legal votes and official authority does not itself supply those missing facts. The record establishes neither a litigant's default nor misleading conduct merely from the absence of such proof. Deliberately misleading directions, pervasive frustration of effective choice, and an otherwise supported reliance claim remain distinct; none is adjudicated against an imagined factual record. The equality holding addresses the operative treatment of comparable ballots and does not depend on finding such inducement.

### V-A. Section 5 supplies a conditional federal benefit, not a universal counting prohibition

**Holding and operative rule.** Under then-operative 3 U.S.C. §5, a final determination of an elector-appointment controversy by judicial or other procedures established in state law before election day receives conclusive treatment in the federal count if made pursuant to that law at least six days before the electors meet. December 12 is that qualifying date for the December 18 meeting, but §5 alone does not prohibit all later counting; independently applicable state limits remain binding, and §7's meeting date is not postponed.

**Authority.** Part V-A; all nine Justices join the complete proposition despite their different readings of state law and chosen judgments.

**Controlling explanation.** Palm Beach distinguished the conditions of a federal benefit from a command ending state proceedings and requested clarification of the state court's own premise. The December 11 response matters to the state-law question; it cannot change the legal character of §5 itself. Foster v. Love concerns final congressional selection before the federal election day and expressly supplies no presidential stopping rule. The Court neither adjudicates qualification for or forfeiture of the benefit on an assumed completed record nor creates a comprehensive rule for competing electoral submissions. Calendar dates do not themselves prove that constitutional correction is feasible or impossible.

### V-B. The established defect warrants defined correction

**Holding and operative rule.** The affected inclusion and examination directions must be vacated and returned immediately for consistent, reviewable treatment under existing state law, including affected earlier additions where necessary to cure material inequality. Correction requires lawful authority, accurate figures, a materially adequate process, and actual feasibility; the defect does not itself require ending the entire contest or awarding electoral votes.

**Authority.** Part V-B; Souter, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Breyer, five direct joins and the Court's chosen corrective judgment. Scalia and Thomas favor a different independent reversal, with only conditional agreement on equality correction. Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm.

**Controlling explanation.** The equal-treatment rule in Part II requires a remedy addressing the unequal criteria, not a presumed candidate result. Palm Beach preserves lawful state administration while requiring attention to constitutional and statutory limits. The state judiciary must reconcile affected prior inclusions with further examination, resolve the Palm Beach 215/176 dispute before any lawful inclusion, preserve the distinction between recovered votes and unexamined undervotes, and provide genuine objection and review. A supported claim concerning comparable excluded legal votes requires consideration; no automatic order to examine every machine overvote follows.

The record establishes neither a completed count nor present impossibility. A remedy cannot rest on an unverified implementation history, presumed available hours, or an assumed distribution of votes. Conversely, absence of an established impossibility does not establish feasible completion. The state court must determine those facts and the lawful scope of correction. It need not repeat a proper examination absent material inconsistency or discard a legal vote because its relevant physical evidence differs. The two rejected contest claims remain undisturbed limits, not new federal merits rulings. No separately presented stay question requires decision here.

### V-C. The precise state-law timing consequence remains for authoritative resolution

**Holding and operative rule.** The present state clarification does not require this Court to impose a categorical December 12 termination of all corrective contest proceedings. The state judiciary must identify the precise effect of its governing timing law on lawful correction and actual feasibility; this instruction authorizes neither counting beyond a binding state limit nor completion until December 18 as of right.

**Authority.** Part V-C; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer, five direct joins in this precise proposition. Stevens and Ginsburg remain dissenters from vacatur. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas read the clarification as establishing December 12 finality under state law.

**Controlling explanation.** Palm Beach called for a statutory explanation and distinguished §5's benefit from a federal prohibition. The December 11 majority supplies that explanation, including notes 17 and 22 concerning conclusive treatment, certification's relation to a contest, and the outside date. Its language supports serious attention to state finality; it does not conclusively resolve here every consequence for a constitutionally corrected contest. The five Justices joining this part decline to turn that language into this Court's categorical terminal instruction.

The four contrary Justices rely on the State's own construction of its comprehensive code, not on a federal prohibition in §5. Their objection therefore cannot be answered merely by repeating the section 5 distinction. Authoritative identification of the actual state limit and its application is required. The Court supplies no affirmative extension, postpones no elector meeting, and makes no forecast of a winner or completed count. This timing holding has its own five direct joins; it is not an inference of five identical reasons for corrective vacatur.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469:** Applied through its fourth category to the preserved substantial federal challenge to the commanded relief. Urgency, consent, and unfinished ballot disputes create no general exception to finality.
- **Goodyear Atomic Corp. v. Miller, 486 U.S. 174:** Applied to distinguish immediate review of an asserted complete federal bar from ultimate rejection of that bar's merits.
- **Harper v. Virginia Board of Elections, 383 U.S. 663:** Its protection of equal electoral participation informs the prohibition on materially arbitrary crediting criteria; no national physical-mark code is derived from it.
- **Reynolds v. Sims, 377 U.S. 533:** Its protection of equal voting weight extends to the supported criteria conflict in one statewide judicial determination; different relevant ballot evidence need not receive an identical result.
- **Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992:** Its allowance for reasonable neutral administration remains intact. It supplies no exemption for materially unequal treatment of comparable ballots.
- **Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993:** Its rejection of territorial design alone as a constitutional deprivation remains intact. An actual unequal-valuation injury presents a different question.
- **Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board, December 4, 2000:** Applied to ordinary statutory interpretation, the open possibility of proved legislative displacement, the separate federal-calendar question, and bounded corrective relief. Its vacatur and clarification request neither stayed counting nor declared a winner; the December 11 state response supplies relevant reasoning without becoming a new federal holding.
- **Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina, January 21, 1999:** Applied to reject due-process reversal based only on interpretive disagreement, while preserving supported official-inducement and fundamental-unfairness claims.
- **Foster v. Love, December 2, 1997:** Distinguished. Its congressional final-selection rule does not decide presidential counting deadlines or the consequences of a corrected state contest.

## Law After Decision

Statewide judicial counting must apply sufficiently definite, consistent, reviewable legal criteria to materially comparable ballots, including affected earlier inclusions when necessary to remove material inequality. Relevant physical differences, ordinary immaterial administrative variation, and lawful neutral procedures remain permissible. The rule is not confined to this election.

Existing legislative contest and voter-intent provisions can support ordinary judicial interpretation and correction. Article II still forbids material displacement of the legislature's scheme; due process still requires examination of a supported unfairness claim. Those distinct grounds produce no independent reversal here. Review under §1257 remains bounded by the actual Cox predicates.

Section 5 remains a conditional conclusive-treatment provision. The Court neither orders all counting to end on December 12 nor holds that state law permits counting afterward. A separate five-Justice proposition leaves the precise state timing consequence to authoritative resolution, without changing the December 18 meeting or authorizing a tally, winner, or later federal-count treatment.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join reviewability, the supported equality defect, rejection of separate Article II and due-process reversal, the section 5 distinction, and corrective vacatur. Their disagreement concerns state law: the December 11 majority's notes 17 and 22, read with its comprehensive-code account and the relation between certification and contest, establish December 12 finality. Palm Beach's recognition that §5 is not itself a federal prohibition does not erase a State's choice to meet that date. Correction must therefore stay within that state limit. They do not find that correction has already become impossible, infer current progress, authorize an extension, or award electoral votes. **Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting from the judgment.** They agree on reviewability, unequal treatment, absence of a separate established due-process violation, and §5's limited federal operation. Article II supplies an independent reason to reverse the challenged directions. First, de novo treatment of discretionary board decisions combined with automatic inclusion of late contest tallies removes the remaining legislative allocation among certification, executive discretion, and judicial contest. Palm Beach permits some late amendments; it does not abolish every residual limit. Second, the legal-vote construction conflates failure to tabulate a properly marked vote with failure to mark in the prescribed manner. The statutory instruction and machine provisions and the Secretary's delegated interpretation matter; §101.5614(5) does not furnish a general answer for these ballots.

The pre-election existence of an intent provision does not decide its scope. An equality protocol alone cannot cure unauthorized relief. Only if the Court rejects this Article II ground do they accept equality correction, confined to the state-law December 12 limit. They adopt neither an unsupported present impossibility finding nor a blanket termination of every lawful state action. The two rejected contest claims remain undisturbed.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting from corrective vacatur.** They join bounded review, rejection of independent Article II and due-process reversal, the federal-calendar rule, and refusal to impose a terminal state-law construction. They would affirm. A common clear-intent rule can produce different results from different physical evidence, and board disagreement, changing evidentiary judgments, or incomplete implementation detail does not itself establish that this judicial decree authorizes unconstitutional treatment. Spencer's and Burton's accounts warrant focused examination of disputed treatment through ordinary objections and judicial correction; they do not demonstrate that such correction is unavailable or that the operative statewide command is constitutionally defective.

Harper and Reynolds protect equal participation and weight, but do not demand mechanical uniformity for different evidence. Burdick permits neutral administration, and the actual Shaw rule requires an identified deprivation. The legislature's contest and corrective provisions and the state majority's substantial explanation support authoritative interpretation under Palm Beach; Yoshina prevents treating disagreement alone as fundamental unfairness. They require neither partisan bad faith nor proof that a winner changes. Their disagreement concerns the established defect and its attribution to the decree. If a constitutional defect is found, they accept a lawful corrective opportunity; that conditional position does not make them supporters of vacatur. They authorize no count beyond binding state law and make no feasibility or winner finding.

## Event

Mariani v. United States, Third Circuit No. 99-3875; judgment below, 212 F.3d 761. Decided December 12, 2000, on writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Third Circuit's May 18, 2000 en banc judgment. No separate oral-argument date is supplied. The civil proceeding under 2 U.S.C. §437h asks whether the First Amendment permits the presented direct corporate treasury restriction despite party soft-money channels and independently permits the prohibition on false-source candidate contributions. The corporate question includes categorical invalidity and invalidity in light of the developed soft-money comparison.

A 1997 indictment alleges that corporate officers reimbursed employee and associate contributions during 1994–96, including an asserted $80,000 Dole-campaign episode in 1995 while waste legislation interested the corporations. Those allegations prove neither the transactions nor knowledge, a completed exchange, actual influence, or guilt. The civil record describes candidate participation in party fundraising, party support, donor access, and some coordinated advertising. It does not establish the legality or legal equivalence of every described transaction. Mariani seeks invalidation of the corporate and false-source prohibitions; the United States defends the distinct direct-transfer and attribution mechanisms.

### Statutory scope and operative qualifications

The direct-treasury holding concerns the presented business-corporation-to-federal-candidate class. Section 441b(a) covers national-bank and congressionally organized corporate contributions or expenditures in elections for any political office, including candidate-selection primaries, conventions, and caucuses. For corporations generally and labor organizations, it covers elections for presidential and vice-presidential electors, Senators, Representatives, Delegates, and Resident Commissioners, and the corresponding nomination processes. It separately prohibits knowing acceptance or receipt by candidates, political committees, or other persons, and consent to prohibited organizational contributions or expenditures by corporate or bank officers or directors and labor-organization officers. A labor organization includes an organization, agency, employee committee, or plan with employee participation and at least a partial purpose of dealing with employers over grievances, labor disputes, wages, pay rates, hours, or working conditions. These distinct classes receive no new independent holdings merely because they share the section; independent expenditures and press activity remain outside this decision.

Section 441b(b)(2) reaches direct or indirect payments, distributions, loans, advances, deposits, gifts, services, and value to candidates, campaign committees, political parties, or organizations in connection with covered elections. It excepts ordinary-course national or State bank loans complying with applicable banking law and regulations. Three separate exclusions protect corporate communications on any subject to stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families, or labor communications to members and families; nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote activity directed to the corresponding groups; and establishment, administration, and solicitation for separate segregated political funds by corporations, labor organizations, membership organizations, cooperatives, and nonstock corporations. The distinct membership-communication exclusion and reporting proviso in §431, preserved in Akins, are not substituted for these provisions.

The political fund cannot use value obtained by physical force, job discrimination, financial reprisal or threats; payments required for labor-organization membership or employment; or commercial transactions. Any person soliciting an employee must disclose, at the time of solicitation, the fund's political purpose and the employee's right to refuse without reprisal. Corporate support for establishment, administration, and solicitation does not authorize treasury money as the fund's candidate-contribution money.

Ordinary corporate solicitation reaches stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families; ordinary labor solicitation reaches members and their families. Corporations, labor organizations, and their funds may also make two written solicitations per calendar year to corporate stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, employees, and their families, mailed to residences and designed to conceal from the solicitor who gives $50 or less and who gives nothing. Additional routes preserve member solicitation by membership organizations, cooperatives, nonstock corporations, and their funds, and trade-association or trade-association-fund solicitation with a member corporation's separate, specific approval, limited to one association per corporation per calendar year. That trade-association route reaches the approving member corporation's stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families.

Lawful methods of soliciting or facilitating voluntary fund contributions available to corporations for stockholders and executive or administrative personnel must also be available to labor organizations for members. A corporation using those methods, including its subsidiaries, branches, divisions, and affiliates, must provide them on written request to a labor organization representing members working for those entities at reimbursement of expenses only. Executive or administrative personnel must be salaried rather than hourly and have policymaking, managerial, professional, or supervisory responsibility. These statutory routes establish no finding that the particular companies possess a successful fund, sufficient donor pool, or practical resources.

Section 441f contains no internal blanket exception. The actual contribution definition, true source, attributable reporting, knowledge and enforcement requirements, and supported associational protections retain their separate work. None of the indictment allegations supplies a completed exchange, corrupt intent, knowledge, or actual influence finding.

## Holdings

### I. The statutory route permits resolution of the developed constitutional challenge

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 437h's authorized-plaintiff requirement, Article III injury, and the substantiality of each constitutional question are distinct. Mariani's uncontested eligibility to vote for President, concrete prosecution stake, and developed constitutional challenge satisfy those requirements here; review of the en banc judgment proceeds by certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), not the repealed direct-appeal provision.

**Authority.** Part I; O'Connor, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer, eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only in reaching the questions. No reason based merely on party consent or judicial economy is adopted.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 437h permits the Commission, a national party committee, or a person eligible to vote for President to bring an appropriate constitutional action, including declaratory relief, in district court, with immediate certification of qualifying constitutional questions to the circuit court sitting en banc. National Committee of the Reform Party v. Democratic National Committee's tribunal holding requires an independent substantiality inquiry. The district court's invocation of judicial economy for the false-name question does not dispense with that requirement. The Court can assess the question on this developed common factual theory and distinct constitutional challenge without assuming that the provisions impose identical burdens. The claim is substantial enough to decide even though it ultimately fails; no prior holding squarely disposes of this entire changed-system contention. A procedural remand would identify no further issue necessary to the answer. Section 437h's former direct Supreme Court appeal was repealed in 1988, before this proceeding and judgment. The Fund Act's different procedure supplies no substitute route. None of these threshold conclusions decides criminal liability or gives standing to litigate every hypothetical recipient application.

### II. The presented direct corporate-treasury restriction is constitutionally permissible

**Holding and operative rule.** Applying the sufficiently important anticorruption-interest and close-fit standard, the First Amendment permits the presented viewpoint-neutral prohibition on direct business-corporation treasury contributions to federal candidates. Its direct-recipient and commercial-source safeguard, coupled with the preserved voluntary human political channels, defeats the categorical constitutional objection without deciding independent expenditures or every corporate application.

**Authority.** Part II; O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose this corporate answer under the same contribution framework.

**Controlling explanation.** Russell, Shrink Missouri, and Daggett require the government to defend the actual restriction, including its complete exclusion of this treasury source. They provide neither a dollar safe harbor nor a presumption that corporate form eliminates constitutional protection. Buckley's contribution distinction permits attention to a transfer placed under the candidate's control. National Right to Work Committee supports separating commercially accumulated resources from a fund supplied by voluntary political contributions and protecting those whose economic payments were made for other purposes. The candidate-directed treasury transfer presents the obligation and circumvention risk regulated here; concern about concentrated wealth or ordinary access alone would not suffice. Corporate administration and lawful solicitation can support the voluntary political channel without converting treasury funds into its candidate-contribution money. That structure preserves human participation, but its statutory availability proves no particular company's fund resources or success. Austin adjudicated independent expenditures and does not automatically answer this contribution question. Its holding remains within that scope. The government has justified this source rule on the presented categorical challenge, while a materially different organization or supported operational burden must be assessed on its own record.

### III. The demonstrated soft-money practices do not invalidate the direct-transfer rule

**Holding and operative rule.** The existence of materially different party-financing channels does not constitutionally require permission for direct corporate treasury candidate contributions when the challenged prohibition remains closely fitted to the distinct direct-transfer risk. The developed evidence of candidate involvement in party fundraising, party support, donor access, and some coordinated advertising does not establish the constitutional invalidity of this prohibition or the legal identity of every party and direct-candidate transaction.

**Authority.** Part III; the same six-Justice corporate coalition directly supports this separate answer to the soft-money challenge.

**Controlling explanation.** The strongest objection combines the severity of a zero treasury allowance with large corporate party donations, candidate participation in fundraising, and resulting support and access. Those facts require a real fit inquiry; invoking a fund's name or an alternative association is not enough. But direct receipt gives the candidate control of the treasury transfer, a connection not established for every party use. Underinclusion can expose a failed justification without making every imperfect boundary invalid. Congress may address this concrete transaction without treating all influence as constitutionally interchangeable. Akins requires the actual candidate connection, definitions, and thresholds; ideological agreement alone proves no coordination, and reported coordination is not a finding that every described arrangement is lawful. Day's pooling and nonprofit protections and Daggett's cumulative-burden rule remain intact. The decision sustains this restriction on the developed comparison, not an all-purpose corporate-fund safe harbor, a judgment validating soft-money practices, or a requirement that association become impossible before relief is available.

### IV. False-source regulation has an independent constitutional basis

**Holding and operative rule.** On the presented constitutional objections and alleged knowing reimbursement scheme, Congress may preserve truthful source attribution and prevent evasion in covered candidate-contribution transactions through the prohibition on contributions in another person's name, knowing permission to use one's name for them, and knowing acceptance of them. This challenge fails independently of the corporate-source rule; different regulation elsewhere supplies no entitlement to substitute a false donor, while unpresented innocent-error applications and criminal enforcement elements receive no ruling.

**Authority.** Part IV; O'Connor and all eight other Justices join this independent constitutional answer within the actual three-clause text and the preserved criminal-proof limits.

**Controlling explanation.** Buckley's candidate-finance interests support knowing who supplied a covered contribution so that valid restrictions can be enforced. Reimbursement through a nominal donor can conceal both the source and the transaction to which those rules apply. The alleged reimbursements illustrate the asserted mechanism but prove neither their occurrence nor guilt. McIntyre protects truthful anonymous individual advocacy and preserves the distinct treatment of candidate-finance attribution and tailored impersonation controls; anonymity is not an entitlement to identify someone else falsely as a candidate donor. The independent attribution function remains even for the three Justices who would invalidate the corporate-source prohibition. Ordinary party access and unequal regulation elsewhere do not erase it. This decision approves no general identification rule for all political association. A supported reasonable probability of threats, harassment, or reprisals retains its own disclosure protection and possible nondisclosure remedy, but does not authorize false naming. No such showing is established in this challenge.

**Knowledge and criminal process.** Section 441f's first clause contains no express knowledge word; its second and third do. That textual difference is not a holding of strict criminal liability or a construction of the enforcement provision's elements. The government must prove corporate source where relevant, attribution, the knowledge applicable to the alleged knowing conduct, and every other applicable element. The constitutional decision determines no count's sufficiency, establishes no conviction or penalty, and does not find that Mariani was charged under every clause.

## Precedent Treatment

- **National Committee of the Reform Party of the United States of America v. Democratic National Committee, March 17, 1999:** Applied to the independent substantiality inquiry and distinct statutory tribunal routes. Neither judicial economy nor party acquiescence replaces those requirements; ordinary resource disparity likewise creates no automatic constitutional parity claim.
- **Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1:** Applied to distinguish contributions from independent expenditures and to the independent source-information and enforcement functions of candidate-finance rules. It supplies no automatic approval of every contribution restraint or general identification rule for political association.
- **Russell v. Burris, April 28, 1999:** Applied to the government's affirmative burden of sufficiently important anticorruption justification and close fit preserving meaningful association, including a complete source restriction. The present result supplies no nominal-dollar safe harbor.
- **Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC, January 24, 2000:** Applied within the same actual fit framework. Its contextual contribution ruling does not validate this distinct source restriction by analogy alone.
- **Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices, December 11, 2000:** Applied to actual contribution fit and cumulative burdens. Its Maine ceiling and responsive-grant judgments remain bounded to their reasons and operation; public financing does not cure an independently invalid private restraint, and no Day plurality rationale becomes controlling through it.
- **FEC v. National Right to Work Committee, 459 U.S. 197:** Its solicitation holding and commercial-source/voluntary-political-channel rationale support the candidate-treasury distinction. Protection of economic payers and corporate-specific prophylaxis beyond currently wealthy corporations retain force; deference in that setting does not displace the actual close-fit inquiry or prove a particular fund's capacity.
- **Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce, 494 U.S. 652:** Its adjudicated independent-expenditure holding is undisturbed and does not automatically resolve direct contributions. The separate statutory contribution prohibition was outside that challenge; neither the expenditure scrutiny nor its nonprofit exception becomes a direct-contribution rule here.
- **FEC v. Akins, June 25, 1997:** Applied to actual transaction definitions, candidate connection, thresholds, exclusions, and separate reporting provisos. Ideological agreement alone remains insufficient for coordination; neither all party spending nor the alleged reimbursements are classified without their elements.
- **Day v. Holahan, May 15, 1995:** Its independently supported pooling, meaningful-association, and nonprofit independent-spending protections remain intact. A different association's resources do not automatically cure the chosen association's burden, and its fragmented responsive-financing rationale is not enlarged.
- **McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission, April 19, 1995:** Distinguished as protection for truthful anonymous individual advocacy, with candidate-finance attribution and tailored impersonation controls remaining separate. Supported threats, harassment, or reprisal protections survive; a possible nondisclosure remedy does not authorize a false donor name.

## Law After Decision

The presented direct business-corporation treasury-to-federal-candidate restriction survives both the categorical and developed soft-money challenges under the contribution framework. Government must still justify the actual burden; corporate form, concentrated wealth, ordinary access, or a fund's nominal availability does not alone satisfy close fit. Independent expenditures, materially different organizations, and supported operational burdens retain their distinct questions.

Truthful source attribution independently supports rejection of the presented false-name challenge even though three Justices would invalidate the corporate restriction. All three clauses retain their actual terms. Criminal knowledge and other enforcement elements remain for ordinary adjudication, and a supported harassment claim retains its proper disclosure protection. This decision supplies neither a right to false naming nor a universal duty to identify every private political association.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They would sustain both corporate challenges within the presented direct business-corporation treasury-to-federal-candidate class while joining the independent threshold and false-source answers. Their common ground accepts the contribution standard and legitimate direct-candidate corruption risk but finds the government's justification insufficient for complete exclusion of this source. A fund made from other voluntary contributions is a different pool, and lawful corporate administration does not establish that it answers the chosen association's lost treasury support. Candidate-connected party financing sharpens the fit problem without proving that every transaction is identical or that all underinclusive laws fail.

National Right to Work Committee's solicitation holding, protection for economic payers, and corporate-channel rationale are substantial contrary authority. The dissent preserves those holdings but would not treat their deference as conclusive under the later actual close-fit rule and this complete-source challenge. Austin's independent-expenditure holding likewise remains undisturbed; the dissent's position is not expenditure scrutiny transplanted into contributions. No completed-bribe requirement, unlimited giving rule, new dollar ceiling, or finding of an actual failed fund is adopted. The three would reverse only the corporate constitutional answers and remand for conforming declaratory relief and ordinary determination of effects on actual charges. They would affirm the independent false-source answer and order neither acquittal nor a remedy against unchallenged party practices.

## Procedure After Action

The en banc constitutional answers are affirmed within the described scope. The matter returns for the pending criminal process. The government must prove corporate source where relevant, true attribution, the knowledge applicable to the alleged knowing conduct, and all other applicable elements; the defendant retains ordinary statutory and constitutional defenses. This decision finds neither guilt nor a completed exchange and does not determine whether every charged count is sufficient or whether every §441f clause was charged. No new penalty, refund, damages, replacement contribution ceiling, or order governing unchallenged party financing issues. Supported organizational, operational, or associational-disclosure claims remain available within the stated limits.
