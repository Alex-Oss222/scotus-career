## Questions, statutory terms, and record limits carried into every commitment

The two corporate questions are analytically distinct: categorical invalidity of the direct corporate treasury candidate-contribution prohibition, and invalidity of that prohibition in the demonstrated soft-money setting. The separate §441f question challenges false-source regulation on the asserted unequal treatment of allegedly comparable soft-money influence. None authorizes deciding every corporate expenditure, party contribution, or criminal count.

Section 441b(a) distinguishes national banks and congressionally organized corporations, whose election coverage is broader, from corporations generally and labor organizations subject to the enumerated federal-election and nomination coverage. It separately addresses knowing receipt and specified officers' or directors' consent. The question decided here concerns direct treasury contributions by the presented business-corporation class to federal candidates; no ruling about every other class or expenditure follows.

Section 441b(b)(2) covers direct and indirect money, services and value, with the ordinary-course bank-loan exception subject to applicable banking law and regulations. Its separate exclusions protect corporate communications to stockholders, executive or administrative personnel and their families, and labor communications to members and their families; nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote campaigns to the corresponding groups; and establishment, administration and solicitation for a separate segregated political fund. These are separate from §431's membership-communication exclusion and reporting proviso preserved by Akins.

The voluntary fund is a specific statutory channel, not a blanket exception for treasury-funded candidate donations. Its contributions cannot use value obtained by force, job discrimination, financial retaliation or threats, compulsory employment or membership payments, or commercial transactions. Any person soliciting an employee must disclose the fund's political purpose and the employee's right to refuse without reprisal. Ordinary corporate solicitation reaches stockholders, executive or administrative personnel and their families; ordinary labor solicitation reaches members and their families. The statute additionally permits two written residential-mail solicitations per calendar year from the specified corporate stockholder, personnel, employee and family groups, designed to conceal from the solicitor the identity of persons giving $50 or less and of noncontributors. Membership organizations, cooperatives and nonstock corporations retain their member-solicitation route. The trade-association route requires separate and specific member-corporation approval, limited to one trade association per corporation per calendar year. Lawful voluntary solicitation methods available to corporations are also available to labor organizations for their members; corporations, including subsidiaries, branches, divisions and affiliates, must provide the methods to a representing labor organization on written request at reimbursement cost. Executive or administrative personnel must be salaried and have the specified policymaking, managerial, professional or supervisory responsibilities. Statutory availability establishes neither an existing fund nor these companies' eligible pool, resources or practical fund capacity.

Section 441f has three clauses: making a contribution in another person's name; knowingly allowing one's name to be used for that contribution; and knowingly accepting a contribution made by one person in another's name. The first contains no express knowledge word; the latter two do. No commitment rewrites that difference, makes the first clause a strict criminal-liability rule, or determines the enforcement provision's elements. Section 441f contains no internal blanket exception. Constitutional protection for demonstrated associational harms requires its own supported application and does not itself authorize substitution of a false donor.

The reimbursement scheme, $80,000 Dole episode, timing relative to waste legislation, and source reporting are indictment allegations. None proves guilt, official exchange, knowledge or actual influence. The record's agreed candidate fundraising, party support, donor access and occasional coordinated advertising are relevant to the fit objection; they do not establish the legal identity of all direct and party transactions. Disputed editorial-based cynicism and legal conclusions are not independent facts. No existing fund, failed fund, waiver, missing defense, completed bribe, or lawfulness of every described soft-money arrangement is found.

## Shared threshold commitment and adverse alternative

All eight Associates reach the granted constitutional questions. Mariani's uncontested presidential-voter eligibility satisfies §437h's authorized-plaintiff requirement; the pending prosecution supplies a concrete personal stake in the challenged provisions. Eligibility and Article III injury remain distinct. The developed record and certified constitutional dispute permit en banc review, followed here by certiorari under §1254(1); the repealed direct-appeal provision supplies no route. Reform Party's tribunal holding, joined by every Associate, requires independent substantiality rather than certification merely for judicial economy. The changed-system constitutional contention is sufficiently substantial to decide despite its ultimate weakness for several Justices; no previous holding squarely resolves this complete claim in this setting.

The competing threshold path would dismiss or decline certification of the false-name issue as plainly foreclosed, or remand for a separate substantiality finding. The strongest argument is the district court's stated reliance on judicial economy for that issue. That reason alone is insufficient. The developed common factual theory and distinct challenge to §441f, however, permit the Court to assess substantiality itself, without a factual assumption that every provision produces the same burden. A procedural remand would add no material issue necessary to the constitutional answer. This conclusion adopts neither a judicial-economy exception to §437h nor standing to attack every hypothetical recipient application.

The scope is constitutional validity on the granted grounds. Discussing §441f's three clauses does not find that Mariani was charged under each. Affirmance would reject the declaratory constitutional challenge and return the matter for ordinary disposition while the criminal case remains independent. Partial reversal would remove only the unconstitutional corporate-source ground within the declared scope; lower courts would address its effect on actual charges and any remaining defenses without an appellate verdict of acquittal or conviction.

## Proposed legal propositions and remedy paths

### Corporate sustaining path

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reject both corporate constitutional challenges and affirm that part of the en banc judgment. The source restriction is closely drawn to preventing candidate obligation and its appearance from direct transfers of commercially accumulated corporate resources, with a statutory political fund that uses voluntary political contributions and may receive corporate administrative support. It is not justified by equalizing influence, treating ordinary access as corruption, or assuming every corporation has abundant wealth. National Right to Work Committee supplies the specific corporate-channel precedent; Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett require that its structural rationale be tested against the actual statutory burden and soft-money record.

The principal contrary argument has real force: a zero treasury allowance is severe, voluntary fundraising is not the same asset pool, and parties obtain large corporate gifts while candidates participate in fundraising and benefit from party support. The five would find those facts insufficient to defeat this particular corporate-source rule. Direct control of a treasury transfer by the recipient candidate, and conversion of commercially assembled funds into a candidate obligation, are the regulated connection. Party issue spending and donor access do not make every transaction identical to that direct transfer. Proven coordination remains subject to its actual legal definition; ordinary agreement is not coordination. Underinclusiveness can expose a failed justification, but the existence of another influence channel does not remove the function of this one. The five do not use a finding that these companies successfully operated a fund, or place on Mariani a burden to prove political participation impossible.

Their narrower boundary is essential: reject these two presented constitutional grounds, preserve supported future claims involving materially different organizations, fund burdens or disclosure injuries, and decide no independent-expenditure application. Day's nonprofit protection and Akins's precise transaction and membership rules remain operative. The holding does not validate every corporate restraint or adjudicate the legality of soft money.

### Corporate invalidating path

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the corporate answer to the extent it permits the complete treasury-to-federal-candidate exclusion for the presented business-corporation contributions, and remand for conforming declaratory relief. They accept Russell's contribution standard and the power to impose sufficiently justified contribution limits; they do not adopt expenditure scrutiny for all contributions, demand a completed bribe, or establish an unlimited candidate-giving right.

Their ground is the difference between a justified restriction on candidate-directed value and complete exclusion of the selected funding source. The corporate label and commercially accumulated funds support attention to possible obligation, but do not sufficiently explain why every direct treasury gift, including a modest gift subject to otherwise valid constraints, must be prohibited while the supported candidate-connected party practices remain available. The voluntary fund uses other voluntarily supplied money; administrative support and lawful solicitation do not by themselves answer exclusion of the treasury resources through which this association seeks to act. Their Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett dissents insist that a usable substitute or practical answer cannot simply be presumed.

The strongest opposing argument is National Right to Work Committee's acceptance of a prophylactic corporate system and the direct-recipient distinction. These three accept that precedent's actual solicitation holding and the legitimacy of preventing circumvention. They would decline to make its broad deference language conclusive of this later, complete-source challenge under Russell's operative fit rule and this developed soft-money record. That record is evidence against sufficient fit, not an automatic rule that any underinclusiveness invalidates a statute. Their conclusion is confined to the presented direct corporate candidate-contribution prohibition; it does not overturn all corporate-expenditure regulation, displace Day's reserved questions, set a replacement amount, or validate concealed contributions.

Austin is an additional serious contrary authority, not an omitted premise. Its controlling expenditure judgment permits a treasury restraint for the corporation there, and its majority accepts regulation of business-corporation contributions. But it expressly adjudicates only the expenditure prohibition. These three do not claim that their older Austin expenditure objections compel this contribution result. The selected contribution conclusion rests instead on the later actual Russell fit rule, their consistently published application of that rule, and the precise complete-source burden now presented. They leave Austin's independent-expenditure holding undisturbed within its surviving scope. The five sustaining Justices likewise do not turn Austin's expenditure judgment into an automatic answer to the two corporate questions.

A remand merely to collect more facts about these corporations' fund resources is a lawful narrower alternative but is not their selected remedy. The decisive burden is the statutory exclusion of the treasury source, and the government has defended that rule on its structure and this record. The absence of particular fund facts supplies no adverse finding; it means the fund's success cannot be presumed to answer that exclusion. The existing developed record permits their constitutional answer without inventing an actual failed fundraising attempt. Limits and accurate attribution remain available on their own terms.

### False-source sustaining path, independent of corporate result

All eight Associates would affirm rejection of the §441f challenge. For covered candidate contributions, forbidding a contribution in another person's name, knowing lending of a name, and knowing acceptance of such a falsely attributed contribution serves accurate attribution and enforcement of otherwise valid contribution restrictions. Each clause addresses a distinct participant in the same false-source transaction. The constitutional rejection preserves their different express knowledge terms and leaves all actual criminal elements and proof unresolved.

The opposing path would invalidate or remand the false-name restriction because soft money can yield comparable access without the same disclosure, or because anonymous political association is protected. Its strongest support is the actual soft-money evidence together with McIntyre and Day's refusal to accept disclosure burdens by label alone. It fails here because truthful independent anonymity and affirmative substitution of a different candidate donor are materially different. The rule does not impose a dollar ceiling or prevent a lawful accurately attributed contribution. Accurate source information continues to serve valid candidate limits even for the three Justices who reject the complete corporate-source prohibition. Party-financing underinclusiveness does not create a constitutional entitlement to make candidate receipts appear to come from someone else.

This is not a finding of fraud by Mariani or a holding that every disclosure demand is permissible. Genuine coercion, retaliation or other associational injury would require a supported claim; none is supplied as a factual basis for this challenge. No judicially invented blanket exception excuses false naming. The Court need not decide mens rea, whether a particular contribution is covered, whether an alleged reimbursement occurred, sufficiency of evidence, or the constitutionality of every hypothetical enforcement application to answer the presented question.

## Justice-specific enhanced commitments

### Stevens

**Corporate result and ground:** Affirm both corporate answers. His joins in National Right to Work Committee and Russell Parts I–IV support distinguishing economically accumulated corporate resources from a voluntarily financed political channel while requiring meaningful association. His Austin concurrence, 494 U.S. at 678–679, independently emphasizes actual or apparent candidate obligation as a justification for corporate candidate-election regulation, distinct from lobbying and public-issue debate. His joins in Shrink Missouri and Daggett Part I support the actual close-fit rule, not categorical deference. He accepts the direct corporate-source rule with the complete statutory channel and scope limits stated above; he need not import his broader Austin view about corporate independent expenditures to decide it.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** Invalidating a total treasury exclusion would protect the chosen pool, as his Day Part III join requires, and the soft-money record exposes important underinclusiveness. He nevertheless finds the direct candidate obligation and voluntary-source distinction sufficient here. He will not join an opinion relying on equality of influence, finding donor access alone corrupt, or treating every corporate expenditure as a contribution. A materially different nonprofit or demonstrated fund-burden application is a narrower question left open, not a ground to postpone these answers.

**False-source result and alternative:** Affirm on all three clauses at the constitutional level presented. His own McIntyre opinion protects truthful anonymous leaflets while expressly preserving different candidate-finance disclosure interests and tailored impersonation controls. Extending its anonymity holding to substituted candidate donors would eliminate the distinction the opinion preserves. He joins accurate-attribution and enforcement reasoning, but no categorical rejection of all future associational-disclosure claims. Remedy: affirm the constitutional judgment; no prosecution finding.

### O'Connor

**Corporate result and ground:** Affirm both corporate answers. Her National Right to Work Committee join supports the political-fund channel; her Russell opinion and Shrink Missouri opinion make the government's close-fit burden decisive. Her Daggett Part I join and rejection of an additional whole-system coercion ground show that differential financing channels are not invalid solely because an alternative exists. The corporate structure and direct recipient relationship justify this source rule without findings about these companies' actual fund resources.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** Her full join in Kennedy's Austin dissent protects independent corporate expression while recognizing greater regulation of candidate contributions; she authored no separate Austin ground. Her Day and Daggett matching positions reject adverse legal consequences attached to outsiders' independent expression, and her unanimous Day pooling join rejects merely nominal alternatives. Those commitments make a complete treasury restraint a serious burden. They do not equate direct candidate transfers with the independent advocacy at issue in those objections. She rejects both an automatic corporate-identity justification and an automatic invalidity rule from party soft money. She will join only if the opinion preserves the independent-expenditure distinction, actual statutory solicitation routes and later supported operational challenges.

**False-source result and alternative:** Affirm for the three distinct clauses. Her McIntyre join and Day special-notice join require a real disclosure connection; here the source is the subject needed to apply candidate-contribution rules, and §441f directly addresses falsification rather than a redundant report serving an invalid trigger. The contrary anonymity path fails on that functional difference. She will not join strict criminal liability for the first clause or erase the express knowledge limits of the others. Remedy: affirm with the pending prosecution left to its own elements.

### Scalia

**Corporate result and ground:** Reverse the corporate answer within the defined direct treasury-contribution scope. His joins in Kennedy's Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett Part I dissents preserve contribution/expenditure distinctions while requiring a practical justification for the chosen restraint. Complete source exclusion is a greater interference with that selected association than the invalid caps those writings addressed. Candidate-connected soft-money practices reinforce the failure to justify this complete exclusion; the theory does not depend on proving a bribe or equating every party transaction with a candidate gift.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** Sustaining under National Right to Work Committee is substantial because it upholds a prophylactic corporate system, including bounded solicitation. His own Austin dissent at 682 expressly recognizes substantial corruption risk when money passes to a candidate's control; that is support for the government's regulatory interest, not a reason to reclassify this as independent speech. He accepts NRWC's actual holding but declines to extend its deference into a conclusive answer to the complete-source question under Russell. His Austin separate-fund objection at 681 concerns another pool's failure to preserve the original association's expression; it reinforces a burden inquiry but does not itself decide the less-protected contribution setting. He will not join a sustaining proposition that treats a different pool of voluntary money as an automatic cure or assumes fund capacity. His narrower lawful alternative is a remand on fit, but the statutory zero-treasury rule and developed comparative record allow the bounded constitutional conclusion now. No broader corporate-expenditure overruling is necessary.

**False-source result and alternative:** Affirm independently. His Day Part II join preserved ordinary reporting despite invalidation of a specially connected notification duty; his Russell and Shrink Missouri dissent joins expressly preserve tailored conduit and earmark regulation. Even accepting the corporate invalidation path, falsely attributed candidate receipts prevent enforcement of surviving contribution rules. The competing theory that underinclusiveness defeats all attribution fails to connect the asserted burden to a protected right to substitute another donor. He joins the three-clause textual description, not an invented blanket exception or determination of enforcement mens rea. Remedy: partial reversal and remand on corporate validity; affirm §441f's constitutional answer.

### Kennedy

**Corporate result and ground:** Reverse the corporate answer in the same bounded scope. His authored Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett dissents require the government to defend the actual chosen restriction while preserving legitimate prophylaxis. Here direct candidate transfers create a permissible regulatory concern, but the government has not sufficiently fitted a total treasury exclusion to that concern in the demonstrated system. Requiring voluntary contributors to replace treasury resources does not itself establish a usable answer to the selected association's burden.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** National Right to Work Committee's corporate-channel analysis and the direct-recipient connection are the strongest sustaining grounds. His Austin dissent at 702–705 expressly permits greater contribution regulation because candidate transfers raise financial-favor concerns absent from genuine independent advocacy. His present position accepts those concerns as support for tailored regulation; it refuses to treat them as proof that every treasury contribution can be excluded. The Austin disagreement concerned independent expenditures and does not supply today's result by itself. His limited candidate-cash/conduit join in Akins Part III likewise permits genuine covered financing duties without equating independent advocacy with candidate connections. A contrary finding that corporate donor access itself establishes corrupt obligation would be a join barrier. His narrower option of a further fit remand is unnecessary on this developed legal challenge, but distinct nonprofit and actual fund-access claims remain undecided.

**False-source result and alternative:** Affirm all three clauses against the asserted constitutional objection. His McIntyre join protects truthful independent anonymity, while his authored Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett positions leave tailored circumvention rules available. He therefore rejects making §441f rise or fall with §441b. A rationale demanding universal public identification for all political expression would lose his join; one confined to true attribution of covered candidate contributions receives it. Remedy: partial reversal only on the corporate-source prohibition, no criminal judgment and no relief against party practices.

### Souter

**Corporate result and ground:** Affirm both corporate answers. His Akins Part III opinion makes actual covered candidate financing, rather than an organization's general purpose or ideological affinity, the operative connection. His Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett joins apply actual fit and meaningful association. Corporate commercial funds transferred directly to candidates present that connection; the voluntary fund and retained communications channels are relevant features of this source-specific regulation, not findings about these litigants' financial success.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** The invalidating path identifies a complete restraint and substantial candidate involvement in party fundraising. His unanimous Day pooling join makes that evidence consequential. He finds it insufficient to collapse party issue activity and direct treasury candidate transfers or to invalidate a defined source safeguard merely because another channel is imperfectly regulated. He will not join a rationale presuming coordination from alignment, disregarding Akins's separate exclusions, or using statutory fund availability to defeat every future concrete burden. A supported different organization or operation remains open.

**False-source result and alternative:** Affirm. Those commitments support §441f's source-identification function while barring an unexamined organization-wide disclosure rule. The alternative extension of anonymous-leaflet protection fails because replacing a donor identity corrupts the relevant transaction information. He joins the common three-clause analysis and no finding of guilt or compulsory disclosure in an unpresented protected setting. Remedy: affirm only the constitutional answers.

### Thomas

**Corporate result and ground:** Reverse within the bounded corporate-source scope. His joins in the Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett contribution dissents support an independent fit inquiry rather than a presumed constitutional amount or unlimited-giving rule. His Day Part III and Part IV joins preserve meaningful pooling and the distinction between genuinely political resources and commercial corporate resources, without deciding today's direct-contribution prohibition. ### Ginsburg

**Corporate result and ground:** Affirm both corporate answers. Her joins in Souter's Akins candidate-financing and membership treatment, O'Connor's Russell and Shrink Missouri opinions, and Daggett's controlling portions support precise source and transaction distinctions with real protection for association. She finds the commercial-treasury/direct-candidate mechanism and voluntary political-fund structure sufficient to sustain these presented challenges, without a premise of political equality or automatic validity for every corporation.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** The strongest invalidating argument combines total exclusion of this pool with substantial party access and candidate fundraising; Day's pooling and nonprofit holdings prevent dismissing it as merely formal. ### Breyer

**Corporate result and ground:** Affirm both corporate answers. He regards the commercial-to-candidate source restriction, voluntary fund, and statutory channels as a sufficiently fitted response to candidate obligation in this setting. The supplied soft-money facts warrant scrutiny but do not remove the direct-transfer mechanism or prove these provisions collectively suppress meaningful participation.

**Alternative, strongest objection, and barrier:** The invalidating path correctly emphasizes that a statutory alternative may be unusable and that zero differs from an ordinary cap. He sustains on the legal structure, direct transaction and preserved human political-funding route, not an invented operational finding. He will not join a categorical subsidy or fund safe harbor, a presumption that challengers must prove impossibility, or a rule immunizing the system from cumulative-burden review. **False-source result and alternative:** Affirm. The direct connection here is the identity needed to apply candidate-contribution law. The contrary systemic-inconsistency path cannot show that accurate attribution becomes useless because other influence escapes the same restriction. He joins all three clauses at their actual textual scope and reserves enforcement facts. Remedy: affirm the constitutional answers, without retrospective liability findings.
