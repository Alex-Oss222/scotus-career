The historical Scalia dissent read §§1252(b)(9), (a)(1) and (a)(2)(C) together as an unmistakable withdrawal; it rejected reliance on a missing statutory number or the words habeas corpus. Its broader historical constitutional grounds were that the Suspension Clause prohibits suspension rather than guarantees a fixed statutory writ, and alternatively that compelled consideration of discretionary release was outside the protected historic scope. It also rejected the asserted due-process and Article III guarantees of review. O'Connor joined the statutory construction and due-process/Article III portions, but declined the broader Suspension Clause theory: she assumed a constitutional minimum and found this claim outside it. These distinct grounds cannot be merged into a single historical objection to all immigration habeas.

No pleaded due-process or equal-protection merits claim is declared substantial merely because pleaded, and no constitutional entitlement to statutory eligibility review is finally decided.
