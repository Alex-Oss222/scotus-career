## Common legal and record limits

1. All nine Justices are seated and no nonparticipation is established. Six constitute a quorum and five votes are required for a judgment if all nine participate. These eight commitments do not establish the Court's disposition.
2. The review channel is §1257(a), not an original federal election contest. Cox's fourth category is assessed below; an urgent calendar and a prohibition on rehearing do not independently establish finality.
3. Palm Beach's actual December 4 decision vacated and remanded for defined clarification, retained no jurisdiction, and stayed no count. The admitted December 11 majority gives a substantial legislative explanation. It supplies lower-court reasoning and specified reported facts, not a new federal event or a verified tally. The separate routine-notice premise supplies no contested compliance fact.
4. Section 5 supplies conclusive federal-count treatment when its requirements are satisfied. It is not itself a federal prohibition on counting after December 12. A state-law choice to require finality by that date is a separate question. Foster supplies no presidential stopping rule. Section 7 fixes the December 18 elector meeting; no complete regime governing later disputed electoral submissions is adjudicated.
5. The challenged process combines recovered votes with examination of remaining undervotes. The Palm Beach net addition is disputed between 215 and 176. The Miami-Dade partial return is 436 additional votes, 302 Gore and 134 Bush, net 168, from twenty percent of precincts and fifteen percent of votes. About 9,000 unexamined undervotes remain from an original pool of about 10,750. Those figures establish neither a remaining vote distribution nor a winner.
6. Nassau's alleged net 51 adjustment and the approximately 3,300 already examined Palm Beach ballots were rejected below on their distinct grounds. Nothing here grants those rejected claims or orders repeated examination merely because a contest was filed.
7. Burton describes successive Palm Beach approaches and a later refinement of the whole-ballot pattern inquiry; the timing of complete reconsideration is not established. Spencer reports different Miami-Dade board-member thresholds, while acknowledging honesty and competence. Spargo's full-punch/dimple example is not a ballot with two full presidential punches. Lampkin describes precinct-envelope discrepancies and incomplete knowledge of reconciliation; no identified duplicate certified vote follows. Broward's detailed account remains complaint allegations. No quantified statewide disparity or completed matched-ballot comparison is invented.
8. Equal treatment does not require counting an indeterminate mark, treating every undervote as a vote, treating physically different ballots as identical, or using identical equipment in every county. Nor does a good-faith explanation alone answer a materially arbitrary difference in the legal criterion for crediting a vote. No Justice adopts a statewide fraud finding.
9. Unknown post-December 8 execution does not establish either completion or impossibility. No count was judicially stopped on December 9 in this timeline. The absence of such an order also proves no actual progress. A lawful remand can state governing limits while leaving execution facts to the state court; no commitment assumes a winner or orders a particular tally.

## Threshold resolution and preservation

The eight Justices would take jurisdiction over the substantial, predecision challenge that the state judgment commands a form of recount and inclusion of returns that Article II forbids because it displaces the legislature's rules. This is a limited Cox fourth-category conclusion, not an election-importance exception. The state court conclusively authorized the contested relief despite the federal objection; Bush could prevail on nonfederal counting grounds and thereby lose a live need for review; acceptance of the asserted federal bar would foreclose the challenged contest relief rather than merely govern an evidentiary ruling; and delayed review during the legally bounded presidential selection process could leave the asserted constitutional protection incapable of effective vindication. Cox permits assessment of substantiality and the effect of a reversal without deciding in advance that reversal is warranted. Goodyear Atomic supplies the particularly useful distinction between accepting jurisdiction over an asserted complete federal bar and ultimately rejecting that bar on the merits.

The strongest contrary path is dismissal: the trial court still must count, resolve the Palm Beach figure, and select further relief, and an objection seeking only better counting procedures ordinarily fails Cox's requirement that reversal foreclose the relevant cause. That objection is substantial. The present jurisdictional conclusion rests on the preserved claim of an impermissible legislative displacement barring the commanded relief, not merely on an appeal from individual ballot rulings. In that claim's substantial version, the alleged substantive counting-rule change and departure from the lawful certification/contest period make the remaining commanded relief unavailable; the two other contest claims have already been rejected. The jurisdictional conclusion does not rest on the distinct, late blanket claim that presidential elections can never be contested under §102.168. It permits review of the constitutionally challenged operative decree. It does not decide premature objections to hypothetical future orders, every individual ballot, or every unpresented election claim. None of these Justices would use agreement by the parties to supply jurisdiction if the Cox predicates were absent.

The November 30 answer and December 6 state brief establish prior presentation of equality and due-process categories involving inconsistent standards and selective counties or precincts. The bounded December 6 extract separately expressly invokes Article II against the alleged new dimple standard and altered certification/contest periods. That presentation is distinct from the December 7 blanket claim that the contest statute does not apply to presidential elections, for which leave was denied. The eight commitments do not reach that blanket claim as a necessary ground. They also do not adopt unverified duplication, every overvote theory, or an independent facial attack on the entire pre-election code under the guise of these preserved objections. The original statute's service and answer requirements supply no invented procedural default.

The state court's general clear-intent rule and its affirmative inclusion/examination command are the objects of review. Post-decree implementation allegations are not supplied as new adjudicated facts. The Court can decide whether that command requires constitutionally adequate consistency without pretending to adjudicate a later completed recount.

## Justice-specific enhanced comparison

### Stevens

**Review, finality, and preservation.** His full join in Goodyear Atomic supports immediate review of a substantial asserted federal bar despite an unfinished state remedy and the possibility of eventual nonfederal success. His actual Palm Beach join accepts federal review while respecting authoritative state grounds; it does not erase the different finality problem here. The strongest contrary position would dismiss because improved counting does not terminate the contest. He rejects that as the controlling characterization of the preserved legislative-displacement challenge, while excluding individual future ballot rulings. He does not infer waiver from denial of leave for a different late argument.

**Equality: rule and application.** He joins the rule that legal votes may not receive materially different weight through arbitrary state criteria. His Karcher join, Burdick dissent, and Shaw majority join protect actual electoral participation while requiring attention to the identified deprivation. Karcher's exact congressional-population rule is not a physical-ballot algorithm. He would uphold this decree: clear voter intent is one substantive standard; different physical evidence can rationally reveal that intent; board majorities, public objections, and judicial correction are not themselves unconstitutional. The strongest opposing case is Spencer's account of incompatible dimple thresholds, together with Burton's refinement and the decree's incomplete implementation detail. He regards those facts as supporting focused adjudication and correction of particular treatment, not a holding that the statewide court has authorized arbitrary distinctions or that its ordinary review is unavailable. He does not require partisan bad faith, a changed winner, or an expert study; the insufficiency concerns the attribution of a constitutional defect to the operative judicial command on this record.

**Article II and due process.** His Thornton opinion distinguishes genuine election administration from forbidden changes to constitutional qualifications; here the legislature itself enacted the contest, investigation, and corrective powers. Sections 102.168(3)(c) and (8) permit investigating whether enough ascertainable legal votes were rejected without first proving the winner. The clear-intent provision supports the relevant substantive definition, and the December 11 explanation grounds the acceptance remedy in statutory reconciliation. The strongest contrary argument treats those provisions as narrow authority subject to officials' prior no-dimple practice and fixed acceptance periods. His Palm Beach and Yoshina joins reject treating advisory practice as conclusively controlling ambiguous statutory law. He would reject the present federal displacement and fundamental-unfairness grounds, without adopting unlimited judicial power or deciding an unproved inducement claim.

**Timing.** He applies Palm Beach's §5 distinction. Footnotes 17 and 22 identify the benefit and explain the certification interval; they do not unambiguously adjudicate a separate, irrevocable terminal rule for every corrective contest. The strongest contrary reading treats the state's reference to an outside deadline as a binding account of legislative finality. He would leave the state court to apply its code and actual federal obligations, rather than convert those explanatory notes into a federal cancellation order. He does not guarantee a count after December 12 or postpone the December 18 meeting.

**Disposition, alternatives, and joins.** Affirm the reviewed judgment, preserving all five component treatments and the unresolved Palm Beach number. No new recount mandate issues from the federal Court. He could join a statement requiring materially comparable ballots to be governed by the same lawful criterion and preserving ordinary judicial correction. He would not join a finding that the existing decree violates equal protection, an independent Article II invalidation, a mandatory federal December 12 cutoff, or a winner declaration. If a majority vacates, he accepts the legal availability of a tailored corrective remand as a less destructive alternative, but that remedial agreement does not convert his dissent from vacatur into a vote for the judgment.

### O'Connor

**Review, finality, and preservation.** Her Palm Beach join permits federal examination of an asserted Article II displacement while requiring identified state predicates. Cox supplies the present interlocutory exception. Her Goodyear Atomic merits dissent does not express a jurisdictional objection and is not misdescribed as a join in its finality reasoning. She accepts review of the substantial claim that federal law bars the commanded contest relief; the possibility of a later nonfederal victory cannot answer the risk that the asserted protection becomes ineffectual. The strongest dismissal argument remains that uniform procedures would merely regulate further litigation. She confines jurisdiction to the authoritative challenged decree and the federal bar asserted against it, not every future procedural complaint.

**Equality: rule and application.** Her Karcher majority join demands actual justification for avoidable differences in electoral weight; her Burdick join also recognizes valid neutral administration. Neither supplies an automatic recount result. She finds a defect here because a statewide judicial redetermination cannot combine recoveries obtained under materially divergent operative thresholds with further examination governed only by a general command that leaves the demonstrated conflict unresolved. Spencer's specific board criteria and Burton's change/refinement establish a real problem beyond the mere possibility of human error. The strongest defense is that one clear-intent standard governs and courts can correct errors on objection. She finds that inadequate unless the supervising court supplies a common, reviewable method before the disputed results determine the statewide judgment. She makes no finding of every alleged Broward disparity, duplicate vote, or recoverable overvote.

**Article II and due process.** She accepts the enacted contest and investigatory authority and the state court's substantial statutory account. Section 102.168(8) defeats the contention that no inquiry may occur until the contestant already proves the final tally. Her Thornton dissent preserves state electoral authority but does not transfer that authority from the legislature to unlimited remedial discretion. The strongest displacement argument points to changed physical tests and the selected acceptance date; her answer is that ordinary construction can implement the prior clear-intent rule, whereas new substantive eligibility or abandonment of a binding legislative deadline cannot. She rejects independent reversal of the contest on the present showing and a reliance-based due-process violation unsupported by the actual record.

**Timing.** She reads the December 11 majority's comprehensive-code explanation and outside-deadline language as the authoritative state choice to obtain §5 finality by December 12. The limit derives from that state construction, not a federal prohibition in §5. The strongest contrary reading points to footnote 17's expressly conditional federal benefit and the absence of a terminal sentence in the contest decree. She concludes that footnote 22's account of the statutory scheme supplies the necessary state-law constraint, while recognizing that it proves no actual failure to comply. The remand may not authorize fresh substantive adjudication beyond that limit merely because a constitutional correction is desirable.

**Disposition, alternatives, and joins.** Vacate the challenged inclusion/examination directions only to the extent necessary to secure consistent treatment and remand immediately. The state court must determine the actual stage, apply lawful common criteria to the affected populations and objections, resolve the Palm Beach number, and determine whether a corrected final determination is available within December 12. If that cannot be done within the binding state limit, the challenged contest relief must end on that ground; this is a rule for state application, not a present finding of impossibility. The Nassau and already-reviewed Palm Beach denials remain undisturbed. She can join a decree that states these constraints without prescribing a federal two-corner rule. She refuses an unlimited continuation, assumed invalidation of every existing vote, or a candidate declaration.

### Scalia

**Review, finality, and preservation.** His Goodyear Atomic join supports Cox review of an asserted complete federal barrier without a prediction of success. His Steel Co. and Ruhrgas positions require a lawful threshold rather than assumed jurisdiction. The finality objection is strongest for a claim that merely requests improved counting; he accepts jurisdiction because the prior Article II challenge contends that the binding state command cannot lawfully issue at all. The specific predecision dimple/timing objections are not extinguished merely because leave for a broader later theory was denied. He will not decide that broader theory by pretending it was timely admitted.

**Equality: rule and application.** His Burdick join permits reasonable nondiscriminatory election procedures, not materially different legal tests for deciding whether equivalent evidence counts as a vote in one statewide judicial determination. His Russell and Shrink Missouri dissents reject abstract assurances as substitutes for examination of an actual electoral burden. Here the recorded different thresholds, shifting treatment, and combination of partially examined populations justify vacating the defective procedure. The strongest contrary argument treats every disagreement as an ordinary judgment about voter intent. He distinguishes evidentiary judgment from incompatible operative rules and accepts that good faith does not make those rules equal. He requires neither a prescribed national chad rule nor an unsupported factual finding about duplicate votes. He would not use alleged variation in equipment alone as sufficient.

**Article II and due process.** His Thornton dissent and Palm Beach join preserve both the legislature's constitutional role and ordinary state judicial construction. The strongest Article II path would treat the aggregation of altered physical rules, changed acceptance dates, and a new statewide remedy as a judicial replacement of legislative direction. He does not adopt that categorical conclusion here: the actual text authorizes investigation and correction, the intent rule predates the contest, and the clarified state account reconciles conflicting late-return provisions. He will not turn §102.168(8) into a blank check, but neither can its words be omitted. A corrective protocol must implement the existing legal-vote definition, not create new votes or a new election. Yoshina and his Palm Beach due-process join also prevent an advisory-guidance disagreement alone from becoming a constitutional violation.

**Timing.** He takes the state majority's outside-deadline explanation as a construction of the legislature's intended finality date. Section 5 itself remains conditional federal law. The contrary path would demand an express statutory December 12 sentence or a separate terminal decree; he rejects that demand because the authoritative state construction supplies the code's meaning, just as it does on other disputed provisions. That construction cannot be accepted for expansive remedial power and disregarded for its finality qualification. Actual timely completion and the status of any counting remain factual matters; the constitutional objection supplies no proof of either.

**Disposition, alternatives, and joins.** Vacate the affected directions and remand for lawful consistent application within the state December 12 limit, without deciding that such application is in fact still possible. End relief on state application if a constitutionally adequate final determination cannot satisfy that limit. Preserve the separately rejected claims and lawful unaffected returns. He will join a narrower common consistency rule and bounded remand, but not a new substantive physical-mark entitlement, a court-created extension beyond the state limit, an inferred double-counting finding, or an unproved winner. A general holding that every manual count is unconstitutional is broader than his commitment.

He accepts Cox jurisdiction over the operative, conclusively authorized contest remedy and the substantial asserted federal bar. He finds the present statewide judicial remedy inadequate because divergent operative physical criteria and selective earlier examination can determine whether a vote is credited without a common resolution mechanism specified for the resulting statewide judgment. The strongest defense is the general intent rule plus later objections. He would accept genuine case-specific judgments under a common rule, but not defer all reconciliation of known divergent criteria until a result has already acquired legal force. He demands no finding that the eventual winner changes and adopts no unverified duplicated-vote allegation.

**Article II and due process.** He rejects a rule excluding state courts from the legislative scheme; the legislature authorized this contest and broad investigation. The strongest opposite route treats the total remedy as replacing the prescribed manner of choosing electors. His Palm Beach join and the new statutory clarification require more than disagreement with a state reading, while his Thornton distinction between administration and substantive alteration preserves a real limit. He rejects an independent Article II reversal here, subject to fidelity to the state timing choice and existing legal-vote standard. **Timing.** He construes the December 11 majority as preserving the legislature's choice to achieve final settlement by December 12. He finds the stated outside deadline a material qualification on the contest opportunity for which certification time was preserved. Do not vacate the Nassau and already-reviewed Palm Beach rejections. No federal physical-mark code or estimate of required hours is adopted. He could join a narrower rule preserving ordinary differences in physical evidence; he cannot join a new substantive ballot entitlement or a judicial extension past the state-law constraint.
