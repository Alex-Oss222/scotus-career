## Marshfield — private operation and continuing public presentation

### Corrected decisive record and scope

The City genuinely conveyed the statue and parcel for fair market value through a sale the parties agreed complied with Wisconsin municipal-land-sale law. ### Governing law and tested alternatives

**M1 — continuing municipal presentation, requiring correction.** The remaining City conduct is its continued operation and presentation of the surrounding municipal park as an undifferentiated whole containing the historically central religious display, together with the retained public-park dedication of the enclave. ## Whitman / American Trucking — standards, scope, and conditional implementation

### W1. Both national standard-setting commands

All eight reach both primary and secondary standards within the supplied cost question. Brief treatment of the secondary standard is not abandonment. Their present rule excludes implementation-cost balancing from the standard-setting commands of §109(b)(1) and (b)(2), with the distinct statutory criteria preserved.

For primary standards, §108's pollutant-effects scientific criteria inform the degree of public-health protection requisite under §109(b)(1), with an adequate margin of safety. For secondary standards, §109(b)(2) requires the level requisite to protect public welfare from known or anticipated adverse effects associated with the pollutant's presence, based on those criteria. The secondary command does not contain or borrow the primary safety-margin requirement. Pollutant effects on welfare, including any effects properly covered by the statutory criteria, are distinct from the economic cost of meeting a standard. The word welfare does not itself authorize balancing implementation costs against the protection Congress required.

Section 109(d)(2)(C)(iv) assigns the committee advice about adverse health, welfare, social, economic, and energy effects of attainment and maintenance strategies. That provision is given effect as advice about strategies; it is not a cost-adjustment instruction for either standard. The periodic review requirement returns standards to §108 and the two distinct §109(b) commands. The statutory implementation conditions retain their own role rather than becoming an extra national-standard exception. Reported legislative materials support a technology-forcing objective but do not establish that a particular technology exists, authorize a new extension, or displace enacted qualifications.

The strongest contrary path is that requisite protection should take account of costs, especially under the welfare language, because compliance expense itself affects public welfare and health. Each Justice rejects that path at the stated scope because the prescribed criteria concern the effects of the pollutant's presence while Congress separately assigned strategy consequences and implementation tools. Neither this rejection nor the statute requires elimination of every imaginable risk or a judicially selected concentration. Union Electric supplies the received standards/implementation distinction; MCI, Brown & Williamson, and National Mining require fit with the connected commands rather than a universal agency-power presumption.

**Disposition:** Affirm rejection of cost consideration in No. 99-1426 as to both primary and secondary standard setting. No concentration, complete scientific validation, indirect-cost exception, or waiver finding is supplied. This corrects the original handoff's insufficiently resolved secondary-standard scope.

### W2. Guidance and EPA's own methodological choices

All eight reject the constitutional nondelegation challenge to the statutory assignment at the scope presented. Congress identifies the regulated pollutant subject, the scientific criteria, and the primary health/safety-margin and secondary pollutant-welfare objectives. Those are legal boundaries for consequential technical judgment; a unique numerical answer is not constitutionally required under Hampton, Mistretta, and Touby. The Court does not substitute the primary margin for the secondary criterion or treat purpose alone as adequate guidance.

Loving preserves Congress's actual policy and bounds and distinguishes execution from a transfer of unlimited legislative choice; its criminal and military qualifications are not a universal checklist. Thomas's military-specific judgment ground in Loving remains distinct and is not counted as a prior endorsement of a general civilian-delegation theory. The present case-specific statutory adequacy and the no-agency-cure rule are directly supported by each Associate under the governing authorities.

The strongest alternative claims that no safe threshold and the range of possible concentrations leave EPA the legislative choice. The answer is that scientific uncertainty and policy-relevant judgment can remain within Congress's specified protected interest and criteria. EPA may choose a reasoned scientific methodology within those boundaries and must explain its actual administrative choice under ordinary review. It cannot create missing legislative authority by selecting its own limiting principle. Thus the lower court's demand for an agency-authored cure would be insufficient if the delegation were genuinely standardless, but the actual congressional guidance makes that constitutional cure unnecessary. No conclusion establishes that EPA's every methodological choice is reasonable or that a health-only standard may use economic balancing to cure an imagined constitutional defect.

**Disposition:** Reverse the delegation ruling in No. 99-1257 and remove the delegation-based cure remand. Ordinary review of the actual science, explanation, and statutory administration remains distinct. The substantive constitutional result is unchanged; the primary/secondary scope and distinction between agency methodology and congressional authority are now explicit.

### W4. EPA's actual conditional transition

EPA's actual interpretation applies Subpart 1 immediately to the new eight-hour standard, retains Subpart 2 while an area fails the old one-hour standard, and treats Subpart 2 as inapplicable after attainment of the old standard. It is not immediate universal abolition. The White House memorandum is not interchangeable with EPA's explanation, and the latter's settlement after comment does not adjudicate its validity.

All eight reject the proposition that attainment of the old standard, by itself, authorizes EPA to discard otherwise applicable specific ozone constraints in administering the revised standard. Their common rule requires the interpretation to preserve the specific classifications and attainment-date constraints Congress made applicable; agency revision of a standard does not itself repeal them. Section 172(a)(1)(C) excludes the general classification paragraph where another provision supplies specific classifications; §172(a)(2)(D) separately excludes the general date paragraph where another provision supplies dates. Section 181 supplies the ozone design-value methodology and classifications, with its actual adjustments, exceptions, extensions, and new-designation provisions. Section 181(b)(1)'s treatment of new designations and corresponding absolute-date adjustment prevents a categorical inference that the specific regime concerns only the original historical designations. Section 182's opening command confirms corresponding supplemental-plan obligations, without resolving every such obligation in this case.

The defect is the asserted automatic transition criterion: attainment of the older standard is not itself the statutory demonstration that every otherwise applicable ozone-specific obligation has ended for a revised-standard area. EPA must explain the continuing reach of the specific provisions through their actual triggers. The Court neither decides a specific area's classification nor declares every earlier control perpetual. An actual statutory release, adjustment, redesignation consequence, or gap receives its own terms. Section 172(e) applies when a primary standard is relaxed; it supplies its stated anti-backsliding consequence and is not a general rule for every revision or a license to abandon another subpart.

The strongest sustaining path emphasizes that the old table, design-value method, and original dates were built around the one-hour standard; Subpart 1 expressly addresses revised standards. Continued Subpart 2 protection until old-standard attainment makes EPA's position narrower than total displacement and deserves separate consideration. The answer is that a role for the general subpart is compatible with continuing specific constraints, but the textual mismatch and attainment of the old threshold do not supply a categorical exemption from them. MCI does not forbid every consequential adjustment; it prevents removal of the operative obligation without authority. Brown & Williamson and National Mining similarly require the connected provisions to retain their actual operation. Lopez permits reasoned categories inside a delegation, while Solid Waste Agency demands an actual source for the asserted reach. Neither decides this statute mechanically.

**Disposition:** Sustain the rejection of EPA's asserted conditional exclusivity at the stated boundary and remand for lawful agency reconciliation in the proceedings concerning the final standard. Do not endorse a lower-court instruction mechanically transplanting every old metric or date, invalidate every possible use of Subpart 1, choose a replacement regime, vacate standards on a separate unproved scientific ground, or issue a plan-specific injunction. The statutory question reached is resolved: revision and old-standard attainment do not themselves authorize the described displacement. The remaining work is lawful application and agency construction under that rule.

## Cook — conceded authority, live intervention, and sections 15–19

### Corrected posture and distinct components

Gralike withdrew from the 1998 election. Harmon, a nonincumbent candidate in the 2000 Republican congressional primary in Missouri's Seventh District, intervened as an appellee, and no party contended that the case was moot in light of that participation. All eight accept the reported live-party posture for this continuing rule challenge. They do not rely on Gralike remaining a candidate, a universal election mootness exception, or the absence of an objection as itself conferring jurisdiction. Rednour's recurring-rule analysis supports continued review at its actual scope.

Both sides accepted that States have no congressional-election regulatory authority beyond constitutional delegation. That litigation premise is accepted for this case; it need not become a new holding repudiating every Justice's prior retained-power position. The actual question remains whether these labels are a permissible exercise of the manner power.

The reviewed injunction covers Article VIII §§15–19. The court of appeals declined proposed severance that would remove §§17–19 while retaining the rest. The State sought no different severance here if its constitutional defense failed and defended the provision in its entirety. Section 19 provides distinct voter review for an omitted label and candidate review for an imposed label in the Missouri Supreme Court, with the reported clear-and-convincing-evidence requirement for a candidate's showing that the label does not belong. That review process does not change the label's policy-compliance trigger into neutral administration. The separate automatic-repeal, challenge-forum, and severability provisions in §§20–22 are not the injunction's stated scope and do not dictate a new severance.

### Common supported ground and alternatives

**C1 — reviewability and accepted premise.** All eight reach the challenge on the corrected live-party posture and the parties' accepted constitutional-source premise. This does not adjudicate the truth of an abstract retained-power theory beyond what is necessary here.

**C2 — manner-of-election holding.** On that premise, an official adverse ballot designation triggered by refusal to perform or pledge the State's prescribed federal legislative program is not an authorized manner regulation. It imposes a political candidate disadvantage rather than administering identification, access, voting, or counting. The absence of exclusion narrows the Thornton analogy; it does not turn this policy penalty into ordinary information. Thornton and Rednour preserve genuine administration, and Burdick permits proportionate nondiscriminatory rules without treating state political preferences as neutral regulatory interests.

The strongest sustaining argument is that labels inform voters while leaving candidates eligible. The record's policy-compliance trigger and official adverse description answer it. Candidate review under §19 may correct an erroneous factual application, but it does not supply constitutional authority for an accurate application of the penalty. No finding about a lost election or impossibility of candidacy is needed. The referendum-versus-legislature argument is unnecessary to C2: even assuming this enactment can exercise the delegated manner power, these labels exceed it. The Court neither resolves that separate source-form question nor invalidates private or ordinary nonbinding political instructions as a category.

**C3 — independent speech alternative.** The official-viewpoint-penalty reasoning in the original handoff remains a supported alternative under Anderson, Burdick, and the actual candidate-selection context. Its strongest contrary concern is that the ballot is not a general expressive forum. The reply remains that the claim attacks the State's discriminatory treatment of candidates, not denial of a general platform. On the refreshed conceded premise, however, no Associate needs to reach that separate constitutional question to affirm; C2 is sufficient without the prior retained-power join barrier. No independent First Amendment holding is supplied by silence or by the original handoff's superseded choice of ground.

**C4 — actual mandate.** Affirm the judgment leaving the district court's injunction against §§15–19 in force. Do not replace it with an invented decree limited to a label's typography. The State's failure to pursue a different severance is a scope reason to leave the reviewed relief undisturbed, not a concession that every section is independently unconstitutional. No new state-law severability construction is undertaken and no federal Alaska Airlines rule is mechanically transplanted to Missouri's constitution. A new severance would require the actual material text, state-law standards, and a supported basis for reaching the unpursued remedial issue. ## Velazquez — actual organization-wide condition and remedy scope

### Actual statute, administration, and scope

The three textual components remain distinct: the broad ban on recipient participation in welfare-reform litigation, lobbying, or rulemaking; permission for eligible individual representation seeking specific relief from a welfare agency; and the condition excluding efforts to amend or challenge law in effect when representation begins. The present constitutional challenge concerns that last condition. LSC interpreted the broad reform-litigation ban to exclude both challenges to and defenses of existing reform law, and respondents did not deny that interpretation. That reported symmetry belongs to the broad ban; it does not establish viewpoint neutrality of the condition within the permitted individual representation.

Sections 504(d)(1)–(2), as reported in the refresh, extend recipient restrictions to non-LSC funds as well as LSC funds. This is not a federal-dollar tracing condition. LSC permits ordinary disputes about facts, misreading or misapplication of statutory terms, and agency policy inconsistent with existing law. It forbids representation designed to change welfare law or challenge constitutional or statutory validity, including federal preemption of a state law and facial or as-applied constitutional objections. Misapplication is not invalidity.

If a prohibited issue arises, the recipient must withdraw consistently with professional-responsibility rules. Counsel can and must explain why the representation cannot continue, may discuss views of welfare law, and may refer the client. Those permissions do not authorize continued presentation of the prohibited claim, establish available counsel, or remove the representation's interruption. The reported interpretation also bars answering a judge's constitutional inquiry while remaining in the prohibited representation, and requires withdrawal even when separate non-LSC counsel would present the validity issue in the same case while the recipient's lawyer continued other arguments. None of those interpretations is a finding that a specific such event happened to an identified client.

Separate affiliates may undertake outside activities under the reported regulations. The record establishes that legal permission, not an actual affiliate, adequate resources, eligibility, acceptance, or the impossibility of any alternative. The statutory legal-assistance purpose and political-pressure concerns are enacted purposes; they do not classify a particular benefits claim as partisan.

### Merits alternatives, fully tested

**V1 — impermissible restriction within independent representation.** The permitted activity is independent advocacy for an eligible client's specific welfare relief. The Government does not dictate the client's claim, and opposing government counsel defends its interests. Congress may select subject categories and eligible clients, but the challenged condition disables an otherwise available legal basis for relief inside that chosen representation and can terminate the entire representation when that basis arises. The validity/misapplication distinction is the actual line; the rule does not prohibit all statutory arguments. Its defect remains when a client's lawful specific relief depends on invalidity rather than erroneous application.

The strongest program-boundary objection invokes the broad ban's symmetry, the permission for routine benefits enforcement, and the Government's power to decline law-reform subsidies. The response distinguishes those components: excluding both defense and challenge in an outside reform program does not make the individual-representation condition neutral within the exception Congress opened. The program permits client advocacy but removes one class of legal grounds precisely when it challenges the governing regime. Advice and referral preserve important communication but not representation; an affiliate is a permitted outside structure, not a cure assumed on these facts. Organization-wide non-LSC reach and the prohibition on dividing arguments with independent co-counsel strengthen the conclusion that the condition suppresses the representational function rather than merely allocating federal dollars.

Rosenberger and Southworth require examination of actual independent expression while preserving lawful program definition. Finley's controlling narrow rule sustained a nonconclusive consideration, not a categorical exclusion. Urofsky rejects automatic governmental attribution from salary, duties, or public resources but preserves institutional control; it is an analogy about the activity's function, not an employment balancing test for every legal-services grant. Rust and Regan retain their actual lawful-program applications. The judicial setting reinforces why validity arguments are part of ordinary independent advocacy. No separate separation-of-powers violation, general forum classification, right to counsel, or right to finance all reform litigation is necessary.

**V2 — permissible restricted service and recipient selection.** The competing rule permits Congress to assist routine individual enforcement of existing welfare law while withholding assistance from the recipient's validity-challenging welfare representation. It recognizes private advocacy but finds no entitlement to funding every legal strategy. The broad ban's reported symmetry, genuine permission for substantial factual and legal enforcement arguments, allowed advice/referral, and legally permitted outside affiliates support treating the scheme as restricted assistance rather than a complete ideological speaking ban.

Its strongest objection is organization-wide reach beyond federal dollars and required total withdrawal even with independent co-counsel. The defending conclusion must accept both. It treats those restrictions as the limits Congress imposed on the assisted entity's participation in this category of representation, leaving separate outside organizations legally possible; it does not assert that an actual alternative is available or that this is only a federal-expenditure restriction. Under this view, interruption of the public undertaking when it reaches excluded work does not constitutionally compel financing continuation. The condition's validity cannot rest on reclassifying the lawyer as government counsel, denying the practical burden, or assuming the affiliate solves every client's problem.

The strongest counter is that entity-level conditions can become penalties on independent expression, which the Finley subsidy concurrence itself reserved. The defending Associates consider this condition directly tied to the selected welfare-service activity and permitted separation outside the recipient, rather than a ban on unrelated private ideology or legal practice. This is their disputed legal classification, not a holding validating every condition on nonfederal funds. O'Connor's narrower program-specific rationale does not automatically join Scalia and Thomas's broader subsidy theory.

### Severability and exact remedy alternatives

**V-R1 — ordinary question scope.** The grant was limited to the constitutional question. The lower court excised only the condition on the individual-benefits exception; severability was not briefed or argued here. On ordinary review, a merits affirmance can leave that unreviewed remedial ruling in force without declaring its legislative-intent conclusion correct or treating silence as a concession of entitlement to an enlarged program. A no-violation conclusion reverses the challenged invalidation and thus presents no unconstitutional provision to sever.

**V-R2 — discretionary plain-error enlargement.** Rule 24.1(a) permits consideration of an unpresented plain error evident from the record and otherwise within jurisdiction; it neither requires review nor makes lack of briefing an absolute bar. The strongest argument for its use is that severing the condition expands the express exception and could create assistance Congress did not authorize on those terms. The strongest opposing argument is that removing the entire exception would withdraw assistance Congress expressly chose to provide and that the purpose and structure do not establish the necessary preference without adjudication. Alaska Airlines requires both independent lawful operation and attention to whether Congress would have enacted the remainder; neither operation alone nor an unsupported assertion of the legislative bargain is enough.

On the present supplied record, none of the eight identifies an evident plain error sufficient to undertake a new severability judgment without the adversarial presentation. That is an affirmative scope judgment after testing the alternatives, not a rule that the Court lacks power. The lower court's report that intent was unclear does not establish that Congress necessarily opposed the remaining program. Alaska Airlines' rule does not infer nonseverability merely from the absence of established intent or a severability clause. Conversely, the legal-assistance purpose alone does not establish that Congress would enact unrestricted individual-benefits representation.

If severability were properly reopened on a supported record, excision of the condition, removal of the whole exception, and a legally justified further remedial remand would require distinct analyses. The present commitments choose none of those as a new substantive holding. They neither mechanically expand the appropriation nor mechanically remove the exception. No automatic hearing, entire-program invalidation, new appropriation, or actual replacement attorney follows.
