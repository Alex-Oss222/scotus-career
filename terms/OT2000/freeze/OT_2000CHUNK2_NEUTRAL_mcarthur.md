# Illinois v. McArthur

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Illinois v. McArthur
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1132; 531 U.S. 326
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-20 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether officers with probable cause to believe a residence contains contraband may briefly prevent its occupant from entering unaccompanied while diligently obtaining a search warrant, when they reasonably fear destruction of the evidence.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Illinois Appellate Court affirmed suppression of marijuana and related evidence because officers prevented McArthur from entering his home unaccompanied before obtaining a warrant.
* **Relief Requested:** Illinois seeks reversal of suppression based on the temporary restraint. McArthur seeks affirmance because the officers interfered with access to his home without prior judicial authorization.
* **Material Record:** On April 2, 1997, officers accompanied McArthur’s wife while she removed belongings from the residence. She reported that he had hidden marijuana under a couch. Officers prevented him from entering unaccompanied while an officer pursued a search warrant. The restraint lasted approximately two hours. He was allowed to enter for limited purposes with an officer observing; officers did not conduct the evidentiary search until the warrant issued. The later charges were misdemeanor drug and paraphernalia possession. The claimed risk was that a resident aware of the investigation could destroy readily disposable evidence before a warrant was obtained.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Probable cause, an actual evidence-preservation need, duration, diligence, and the scope of accompaniment must be assessed separately. A home receives substantial Fourth Amendment protection even where the underlying offense is a misdemeanor. The record does not justify equating temporary control of access with unlimited entry and search, or treating mere suspicion of drugs as an automatic exigency. Any disputed consent to the limited entries must not become a general consent finding.
* **Relevant Dates:** The police conduct occurred April 2, 1997. The historical and simulated event date is February 20, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

### Issue 1: A limited restraint pending a warrant

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Bond v. United States preserves the distinction between lawful presence and permission to conduct a further search. Whren v. United States preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis without dispensing with separate scope, duration, and warrant requirements. These holdings do not directly decide temporary control of residential access.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A particularized justification must support the actual intrusion. Probable cause does not alone create general authority to enter or occupy a home. A claimed urgent need is assessed by the facts, and a temporary measure must remain limited to that need.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments. Payton v. New York, Segura v. United States, United States v. Place, and Welsh v. Wisconsin provide received warrant, preservation, duration, and offense-gravity principles within their respective settings.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law distinguishes a warrantless evidentiary search from a bounded preservation measure. Neither the seriousness of the privacy interest nor the label misdemeanor conclusively resolves every temporary restraint.
* **Open Point:** Whether these facts justify the two-hour restriction and whether police acted diligently using a measure reasonably confined to preserving the evidence.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Illinois emphasizes the spouse’s direct information, the immediate destruction risk once McArthur knew police suspected drugs, the effort to obtain a warrant, and the limited accompaniment instead of a general search. McArthur emphasizes exclusion from his own home, the modest offense, asserted lack of a concrete exigency, and the risk that allowing police to manufacture a preservation rationale would erode the warrant rule. The lower court treated the warrantless restriction as an unlawful seizure, making the later warrant evidence derivative of that restraint.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal is available if probable cause, the particularized destruction risk, limited scope, and diligence together establish a lawful temporary restraint. Affirmance is available if a material predicate is absent or the restraint exceeded the reasonably necessary interval. A remand may address a genuinely unresolved predicate rather than presume it. Finding the restraint lawful removes this suppression ground; it does not resolve an independent warrant defect or authorize routine residential freezes based on less than probable cause.
