**Materials:** The [neutral addendum](../runtime/OT_2000CHUNK1_DAGGETT_NEUTRAL_ADDENDUM.md) was checked independently against the complete First Circuit opinion and footnotes in [Daggett, 205 F.3d 445](https://openjurist.org/205/f3d/445/beverly-daggett-v-commission-on-governmental-ethics-and-election-practices). The lower court's reasoning and descriptions of earlier decisions remain subordinate to the operative entering law.

The source confirms these operational propositions:

- Paragraph 12 describes a dollar-for-dollar match for the privately financed opponent's receipts exceeding the participant's initial distribution, together with matching for independent expenditures promoting the private opponent's election or the participant's defeat. Total matching payments stop at twice the initial distribution; initial and matching payments together cannot exceed three times that distribution. Paragraphs 88–91 and footnote 30 distinguish receipts from actual campaign spending and discuss the timing and limited usefulness of late payments. Those latter observations are the lower court's reasoning and reported evidence, not a statutory exception or a finding that every timely payment is ineffective.
- Footnote 29 reports a regulatory deduction under chapter 3, §6.3.B(2). It subtracts from matching disbursements independent expenditures that expressly advocate electing the certified participant or defeating the nonparticipating opponent. The addendum's broader shorthand about favoring a participant must retain this express-advocacy qualification. The deduction begins after the opponent raises more than the initial distribution. It does not reduce the initial grant; no deduction from that initial grant may be inferred.
- Footnote 4 supplies a relevant exception to generalized grant descriptions: there is no public distribution for an uncontested general election. Paragraph 11 distinguishes smaller uncontested-primary grants and describes the 25 percent reduction of the historical-average grant calculation for the 2000 elections. Neither fact changes the stated matching cap.

The source supports treating the adjustment as a reported regulation, not a statutory clause independently examined here. It does not provide a complete computation for every multi-opponent or timing scenario. No such scenario should be invented as a premise for judgment. The full opinion supplies no severability adjudication. If partial invalidation becomes a supported path, the governing severability law must be identified before selecting the operative remedy; the source does not authorize a replacement trigger or subsidy.
