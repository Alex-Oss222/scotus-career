Historically the Court reversed unanimously in judgment. Ginsburg wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Scalia concurred only in judgment. The main opinion rejected the Government's claim that payment language dispositively resolved backpay allocation: Nierotko's reading of corresponding language foreclosed that simple inference. It nevertheless distinguished benefits and taxes in context, recognized the competing fairness and administration considerations, and relied on the reasonable, longstanding agency interpretation of its regulations. It reserved whether the revenue rulings independently command deference.

Scalia expressly refused the main opinion's different-treatment explanation. If statutory text directly addressed these damages awards, he considered Nierotko's identical-language reading potentially favorable to the taxpayer. In his view the statute did not itself resolve how a damages award compensating lost wages should be temporally treated. That gap permitted a reasonable agency choice. Neither the benefit rule nor the tax rule was compelled by the phrase wages paid; both were permissible ways to resolve the gap. This is a substantive rationale distinction, not a stylistic preference or merely a disagreement about informal-guidance deference.

## Why the proposed Scalia departure is not yet supported

Actual Auer gives controlling weight to a responsible agency's fair, permissible construction of its own regulation, with authority, text and actual-ground limits. It does not direct Scalia to treat stipulated damages as ordinary wages whose temporal allocation is already resolved by the statute. Actual Christensen leaves his objection to automatic disqualification of authoritative informal interpretations intact, and rejects amendment of clear regulatory text; it supplies no reason to abandon his distinction between statutory coverage and permissible gap resolution. Actual Gitlitz enforces the operative classification and sequence enacted in that different tax scheme while expressly preserving room for authorized interpretation where genuine room exists. It does not create a general principle requiring different meanings for identical words in the tax and benefits provisions here.

No changed payment fact, changed wage stipulation, altered statutory phrase, displaced Nierotko holding, or materially new agency interpretation has been identified. The frozen position therefore lacks a concrete changed premise sufficient to explain Scalia's proposed broader join. Historical agreement on the judgment is not enough. This conclusion does not mechanically substitute the historical concurrence for the frozen row; it identifies the exact unanswered rationale issue that prevents final reconciliation.
