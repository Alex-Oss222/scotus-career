## Issue-specific paths and current law

The controlling Pinson rule, effective May 8, 1995, treats targeted examination of a closed home's thermal patterns as a search when it reveals otherwise unobservable concealed domestic operation. Myers, June 11, 1996, applies it to an Agema 210 inference of cultivation lights. The absence of an interior photograph and passive reception outside the dwelling do not distinguish those holdings. Neither makes market availability a condition. Katz protects concealed information without physical entry; Karo addresses concealed domestic information acquired technologically. Greenwood's deliberately discarded garbage and Place's contraband-confined detection are different.

Path A applies Myers: this deliberate scan is a search; no warrant or recognized exception is established; vacate the no-search suppression ruling and dependent affirmance for lawful resolution of the actual preserved claim. Its strongest objection is that the device measured only exterior warmth and officers inferred its source. The majority's existing rule expressly treats the reported pattern and concealed operational inference as protected information. The distinction between sensing and inference cannot eliminate that precise prior application.

Path B retains Kennedy and Breyer's published Myers dissent: comparative exterior warmth is the observed information; the unobserved source is an inference, so the scan does not obtain Karo's concealed fact. Its strongest objection is that both Pinson and Myers squarely reject that legal characterization. Those Justices must expressly adhere to their disagreement with that application; a purported factual distinction would be unsupported. Their preferred rule is content-specific, not abandonment of all escaping signals, an immunity for every passive instrument, or prediction based on future equipment.

The remedy is a separate issue. The January 1992 conduct date may matter to an actually preserved and supported reliance defense; it does not change the rule governing current direct review. The later warrant cannot retrospectively authorize the scan. Myers requires consideration of the preserved derivative-warrant claim without assuming final untainted probable cause, reliance, independent source or inevitable discovery. Leon and Evans retain their precise predicates.

## Mandate limits

The preferred six-Justice disposition removes the categorical no-search basis and remands the preserved suppression claim and dependent conviction affirmance. A properly available, independently supported warrant or exclusion ground may still support admission; if no such ground survives and causal dependence is established, suppression follows under governing law. The Court makes neither finding now. Leon does not protect knowing or reckless falsity, abandonment of judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia, or a facially deficient warrant precluding reasonable reliance; its inquiry includes obtaining and information-supplying officers and assumes execution within scope. Evans's qualifying non-law-enforcement clerical mistake is not this deliberate investigative scan. Intentional waiver remains effective; genuinely forfeited issues retain Olano's separate requirements. Rule 11(a)(2)'s plea-withdrawal consequence depends on prevailing on the appeal and is not ordered merely by this corrective remand. No acquittal, release, new hearing, forced affidavit excision or new defense is created.
