## 1. Record, reach and components

The Court reviews a final state judgment rejecting compensation. Corporate acquisition occurred in 1959, coastal regulation followed, and personal title passed to Palazzolo in 1978 after revocation of the corporate charter. The Council denied the 1983 application to fill marsh and the 1985 application to fill 11 of 18 wetland acres for a private beach club. The latter denial rests on the public-purpose condition of the special-exception provision. The record does not supply independent findings under its other two conditions. A residentially usable upland portion retains approximately $200,000 of development value. The requested $3,150,000 compensation uses a 74-lot subdivision that was not itself a submitted permit application. These figures are not a new valuation finding or an approved development plan.

The parcel in this review is the whole property. The wetlands-only denominator was not pressed below or presented in the petition. No separate-parcel total-loss judgment is available on that unpresented theory. The case concerns compensation, not an entitlement to fill or obtain a permit. No source establishes that regulation alone transferred a pre-existing compensation claim, that a taking occurred at enactment, or that all background ownership limits are absent; none of those propositions is assumed.

The separable components are:

- **F — Finality:** Whether the denials and regulatory standard sufficiently settle the permissible private use, notwithstanding an unsubmitted smaller development or the compensation subdivision.
- **A — Acquisition:** Whether acquisition after enactment categorically defeats the takings claim or converts the regulation into a background limit on title.
- **L — Complete loss:** Whether the retained meaningful upland use defeats Lucas on the whole-parcel premise presented.
- **P — Partial deprivation and remedy:** Whether the contextual claim must be considered under Penn Central without a categorical acquisition bar, and whether the Court can now determine compensation.

## 2. Operative law and competing paths

### F. Definitive use restriction versus untested alternatives

Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, May 27, 1997, is Thomas's unanimous opinion. A definitive prohibition on development is final when no further land-use procedure can change the permitted use. Selling rights usable elsewhere cannot reopen that completed use determination. The opinion preserves Williamson County and MacDonald's requirement of a definitive account of permitted development and separates it from valuation or compensation. It does not hold that two permit denials always suffice or that every demand for another application is valid.

Lucas, June 29, 1992, permits review of a finally rejected claim for an elapsed period of categorical prohibition when permission was unavailable during that period. It retains ordinary finality outside that setting. Here the question is whether the supplied denials and governing standard likewise settle the relevant private use, not whether the Lucas earlier-period facts can simply be assumed.

Section 130A permits a special exception to a prohibited activity only if and when the applicant demonstrates all three conditions. First, there must be a compelling public purpose benefiting the public as a whole rather than individual or private interests, consisting of one or more of: an activity associated with public infrastructure, such as utilities, energy, communications or transportation; a water-dependent activity generating substantial economic gain to the State; or an activity providing shore access to broad segments of the public. Second, all reasonable steps must be taken to minimize environmental impacts and/or use conflict. Third, there must be no reasonable alternative means or location for serving the cited compelling public purpose. Even satisfaction of those necessary conditions permits rather than requires an exception. The source text is the judicial quotation from joint-appendix pages 37–38; no OCR wording is silently repaired into a quotation here.

The **ripe path** treats the public-purpose condition and repeated denials as a definitive answer for the ordinary private filling whose loss supports this claim. A smaller private residential project cannot satisfy the necessary condition merely because it needs less fill; no calculation of compensation is needed to know that. A different undertaking providing qualifying public infrastructure, substantial water-dependent economic gain or broad public shore access would be a different proposed public-purpose activity, not an unexplored ordinary residential permit. The strongest objection is that the actual applications were large, that no 74-lot subdivision application was made and that section 130A retains some discretion. The ripe path answers at the level of the relevant prohibited private use, rather than asserting that all possible activities everywhere on the property are forbidden.

The **unripe path** reads the existing decisions as rejecting the submitted major filling uses without settling the amount or arrangement of development that a materially different proposal might permit. Suitum expressly required that no remaining procedure could change permitted use. A permissible special exception is not guaranteed, but its public-purpose categories can include privately operated water-dependent or public-access activity; private ownership alone is no disqualification. The strongest objection is that demanding a public-purpose redesign or further ordinary private application imposes a futile prerequisite where the governing standard already prohibits the claimant's use. The unripe path responds that futility of the rejected designs does not establish a definitive parcel-wide development answer when the compensable subdivision was never presented. This position does not find that a permit would issue or that any condition of section 130A has been met.

These are competing readings of the sufficiency and reach of the supported denials. The dispute does not authorize invention of additional exemptions, a new permit event, an agency concession or a missing factual finding.

### A. Acquisition and the present force of Dodd

Dodd v. Hood River County, November 19, 1998, Parts II–IV, is Stevens's opinion joined by all eight Associates. Part III holds that economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and governmental character must be considered together. Retained value, acquisition under an existing restriction or a legitimate purpose alone is not dispositive. Its rejection of the claim rested on profitable timber use, the actual necessary-and-accessory dwelling condition, nonassuring County communications and the forest-management character of the restriction. It expressly declines a rule that later purchasers lose constitutional protection. This is an existing direct commitment of each Associate, not a rule being supplied for the first time here.

The successor may therefore present the burden of the restriction on the title actually acquired; mere notice of enactment does not extinguish constitutional protection or automatically satisfy Lucas's background-law exception. The strongest contrary path emphasizes that existing law defines property and objectively reasonable expectations. That remains relevant through actual title law and Penn Central; it does not support making the enactment date dispositive. No current supplied circumstance warrants displacing Dodd's rule. The claim to compensation for a completed taking suffered by a previous owner, if presented with its own facts, is distinct and is not resolved through this general acquisition proposition.

Lucas requires an identified and substantiated antecedent state property or nuisance limitation when complete deprivation is established and the State invokes the exception. A newly imposed legislative harm description alone does not suffice. Actual background law remains available under its own requirements. Natural wetland characteristics and the legitimacy of environmental protection may bear on the relevant analysis, but neither supplies an unproved title limitation by assertion.

### L. Meaningful use and the correct property

Lucas's complete-deprivation rule applies only after complete economically beneficial-use loss of the relevant property. Dodd Part II, supported by all eight Associates, rejects complete loss when profitable use remains and keeps Penn Central distinct. Approximately $200,000 of residential development value on the upland is meaningful use on the whole-parcel premise presented. The strongest counterargument is that the claimed loss is severe relative to the proposed subdivision and concentrated in the marsh. Severity does not satisfy complete deprivation, and the wetlands-only parcel theory is not properly presented. No percentage threshold, guaranteed return or new denominator rule is selected. No background-law ruling is necessary to reject this Lucas theory because its complete-loss premise fails.

### P. Contextual review and remedy

If F permits review, A and L leave a contextual claim. Penn Central, as applied in Dodd, requires consideration together of economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of the governmental action. Existing regulation may inform the actual expectation; notice alone does not dispose of the claim. The restriction's public purpose does not alone establish that its particular burden is uncompensable. Likewise a large asserted diminution alone does not compel compensation. Dodd's summary judgment rested on its actual undisputed record; it is not a finding about this parcel.

The supported record does not establish the remaining contextual merits, actual compensation, duration or any dispositive background-law defense. A merits remand therefore orders consideration under the proper framework, preserves lawful regulation and awards neither $3,150,000 nor a building permit. Rejection of a total-loss theory does not resolve the partial-loss claim. If F fails, the judgment remains on finality grounds, without a merits holding adopting a categorical acquisition bar or a judicial finding that the regulation never effects a taking.

## 3. Independent Justice commitments

The entries below state decisional positions and reasons, not a transcript of deliberation. Authorities are the Justice's own date-eligible writing or express prior joins. Conditional propositions describe exactly what may be joined if the Court reaches a component; they do not erase a threshold objection.

### Stevens

**F:** Sustain the finality ground. His Lucas dissent resisted premature adjudication where permitting and the actual burden were insufficiently settled; his Suitum join required that no further procedure could alter parcel use. The unsubmitted subdivision and the scale of the rejected designs leave a materially different-use inquiry here. **Strongest counterargument:** A necessary public-purpose condition forecloses ordinary private fill regardless of scale. He accepts that limitation on those uses but would not equate it with a definitive account of the parcel's allowable development for this compensation theory. He does not find that another application will succeed.

**A:** If reached, reject a categorical acquisition bar under his own Dodd Part III. **L:** If reached, reject complete deprivation under his Dodd Part II on the whole-parcel and retained-use premise. **P:** If the Court finds F satisfied, the present record does not support a final contextual rejection simply from acquisition or upland value; the proper alternative is Penn Central consideration with no compensation ordered. His Dodd opinion requires a combined inquiry, and his Lucas dissent preserves its importance for coastal regulation.

**Judgment and joins:** Affirm on finality, without affirming a categorical acquisition rule. He will expressly agree with A and L if the Court reaches them, while maintaining his objection to reaching and remanding the contextual merits now. He can accept the P framework and remedial limits as a conditional instruction, but not a present vote for the merits remand. **Join barriers:** No presumed futile range covering untested qualifying activities; no erasure of notice from contextual expectations; no taking or compensation finding. A separate threshold disagreement is necessary if the Court orders the merits remand.

### O'Connor

**F:** Find the presented private-use restriction final. Her Lucas majority join preserves review of an established prohibition despite further irrelevant procedure; her Suitum join and concurrence separate the agency's fixed use answer from later valuation. Section 130A's public-purpose prerequisite does not become satisfied merely by reducing an ordinary private filling plan. **Strongest counterargument:** Smaller development and discretionary exceptions remain theoretically possible. She requires that the opinion confine finality to the use restriction actually settled; it must not announce that all smaller applications are futile everywhere or that approval is mandatory if the three conditions are met.

**A:** Reject the categorical acquisition rule, adhering to her Dodd Part III join. **L:** Reject complete deprivation on the whole parcel; her Lucas join and Dodd Part II supply the rule. **P:** Remand for the full contextual inquiry. Her Dodd join permits existing regulation to bear on objectively reasonable expectations along with economic impact and governmental character; notice is neither conclusive nor irrelevant. The retained upland is actual use on this parcel, unlike the off-parcel transfer rights addressed by her Suitum concurrence.

**Judgment and joins:** Vacate the compensation-denying judgment to the extent resting on lack of finality and categorical acquisition, preserve rejection of Lucas's total-loss theory, and remand the partial-taking claim. **Join barriers:** A categorical post-acquisition exemption from the Takings Clause and a categorical rule excluding regulatory history from expectations are both unacceptable. She joins the shared bounded rule in full. A separate qualification is needed only if the final writing fails to preserve the ordinary role of actual regulatory context.

### Scalia

**F:** Find finality satisfied for the prohibited private filling. His Lucas opinion distinguishes the established burden from needless later procedure, and his Suitum join and concurrence reject an additional act incapable of altering the relevant land-use answer. **Strongest counterargument:** The 74-lot compensation model was not submitted. The claim need not obtain an advisory rejection of that precise design when the mandatory public-purpose prerequisite already prevents the private filling it requires; valuation of that design remains a separate issue.

**A:** Reject automatic extinguishment upon transfer. His Lucas opinion distinguishes actual background property law from newly imposed restriction; his Dodd join rejects a single-factor acquisition rule. **L:** Reject complete deprivation because real beneficial upland use remains on the parcel properly before the Court. **P:** Remand contextual review under Dodd; no anticipated subdivision profits or compensation follow. His Suitum concurrence addresses substitute off-parcel rights, not a license to disregard actual upland use.

**Judgment and joins:** Support the bounded vacatur and remand with the Lucas rejection intact. **Join barriers:** Mere enactment cannot be restated as an antecedent title limit; mere awareness cannot independently render a later owner's expectations constitutionally unprotected. He accepts Dodd's consideration of actual regulatory conditions together with all other relevant facts and does not demand a new rule excluding all pre-existing regulation from Penn Central. He would refuse treating a possible public-purpose redesign as a required ordinary private permit attempt. No separate writing is required by the bounded shared formulation.

### Kennedy

**F:** Find the record sufficiently final for the settled private-use limit. His Suitum join asks whether additional action can change the agency's permitted-use answer. His Lucas concurrence accepted review of an established earlier restriction while preserving actual ability, duration and loss for later proceedings. **Strongest counterargument:** The agency never evaluated the compensation subdivision. That limits proof of impact and legitimate expectations but does not itself unsettle the prohibition imposed on private marsh filling under this standard.

**A:** Reject the acquisition cutoff under his Dodd join. **L:** Reject complete deprivation on the whole-property premise and meaningful upland use. He follows the controlling Lucas category as subsequently applied in Dodd, without claiming to have joined Lucas's original categorical explanation. **P:** Require contextual review. His Lucas concurrence places objectively reasonable expectations in the whole legal tradition, including the special character of vulnerable coastal property; that remains compatible with Dodd's refusal to make notice alone conclusive.

**Judgment and joins:** Support vacatur and remand for the partial claim, retaining rejection of complete deprivation. **Join barriers:** No representation that environmental restrictions lack legitimacy, that title includes every intended development or that notice has no permissible bearing. No assumption that compensation is due for any specific period. He joins the shared rules at that scope without reopening his disagreement with the original Lucas rationale.

### Souter

**F:** Sustain the finality ground. His Lucas statement declined adjudication resting on an inadequate factual premise; his Suitum join reached finality only after the agency had completed the no-development determination and no remaining procedure could change it. The two rejected designs and never-submitted subdivision do not provide that same account here. **Strongest counterargument:** Section 130A categorically excludes the proposed private purpose. He accepts the denial's legal effect for that purpose but finds the remaining range of use insufficiently settled to determine the asserted burden on this parcel. A qualifying water-dependent or public-access undertaking cannot be ruled out solely because privately undertaken.

**A:** If reached, agree that acquisition alone does not defeat the claim, under his Dodd Part III join. **L:** If reached, agree that meaningful upland use defeats the whole-parcel total-loss category, under his Dodd Part II join. **P:** If F is resolved against him, the only supported merits continuation is contextual review, not an immediate declaration either of taking or of no taking. Existing regulation and coastal conditions may inform expectations without becoming dispositive.

**Judgment and joins:** Affirm on finality; expressly agree with A and L if reached but dissent from the present merits remand. The P framework is a conditional concurrence in the governing instruction, not support for reaching it now. **Join barriers:** No conversion of a rejected large project into a conclusive finding that every materially different proposal fails; no judicial invention of the extent or value of permitted development. His separate-writing function would be confined to threshold sufficiency and the distinction from the fixed agency decision in Suitum.

### Thomas

**F:** Find finality satisfied for the private filling at issue. His own Suitum opinion makes finality turn on a definitive answer about permitted use, not completion of a useless additional act. The necessary public-purpose condition and actual denials supply that answer for ordinary private filling. **Strongest counterargument:** The conditional exception means some development remains possible. A possibility for an activity meeting different public-purpose conditions does not reopen the regulatory answer to this ordinary private development; the opinion must preserve the distinction and the upland allowance.

**A:** Reject the categorical acquisition rule under his Lucas and Dodd joins; actual background title law is not equivalent to regulatory notice. **L:** Reject complete deprivation under Lucas and Dodd because actual upland residential use remains on the whole parcel. **P:** Remand under the combined contextual rule he joined in Dodd. His Suitum concurrence treated third-party transferable rights as compensation-related; it does not make actual use of this parcel legally irrelevant.

**Judgment and joins:** Support vacatur and remand with rejection of the total-loss claim preserved. **Join barriers:** No forced permit, no automatic compensation, no severance of the marsh into an unpresented denominator and no assertion that section 130A requires an exception upon satisfaction of its conditions. He joins the bounded shared rationale without a separate opinion requirement.

### Ginsburg

**F:** Sustain the finality ground. Her Suitum join depended on a completed parcel-use decision beyond alteration by any further land-use procedure; her Dodd join permitted merits adjudication on an actual, adequately defined record. Here the designs denied and the unsubmitted compensation plan leave the allowable scope materially unsettled. **Strongest counterargument:** Reducing a private plan cannot overcome the necessary public-purpose condition. She accepts that proposition but does not treat it as proof that no materially different proposal could change the agency's permitted-use answer. She does not treat the possibility of an exception as proof of entitlement to one.

**A:** If reached, reject a categorical acquisition bar under her direct Dodd join. **L:** If reached, reject total deprivation under the retained whole-parcel use recognized by Dodd's Lucas application. **P:** If the Court reaches the merits, require the actual combined Penn Central inquiry. She will not independently sustain the state judgment from the same acquisition-only ground Dodd rejects, nor infer that the record proves an unreasonable expectation simply from notice.

**Judgment and joins:** Affirm on finality; conditional express agreement with A, L and the governing P instruction does not make her a supporter of the present merits remand. **Join barriers:** No new finding about alternative designs, no invented state title restriction and no compensation calculation. A separate threshold position would explain the missing definitive development answer and preserve the governing merits rules without adjudicating the claim prematurely.

### Breyer

**F:** Sustain the finality ground. His Suitum join distinguished a finished land-use decision from a transaction that could affect only valuation. Here a revised application could concern the permitted activity itself, and the presented private beach-club denial does not definitively answer the scope of every material development alternative. **Strongest counterargument:** The public-purpose prerequisite fixes the fate of ordinary private filling. He accepts its force but regards the extent of lawful use as insufficiently developed for the particular compensation demand, unlike Suitum's completed no-development record. This is not a demand to perform an act already shown to be legally incapable of changing use.

**A:** If reached, reject the categorical acquisition bar as contrary to his Dodd Part III join. **L:** If reached, reject the total-loss theory on the whole-parcel and meaningful-use premises. **P:** If F is resolved as ripe, join contextual consideration of impact, expectations and governmental character, retaining the environmental and legal context without making it dispositive. His Dodd join supplies no basis to announce this coastal regulation uncompensable merely because it serves a public purpose.

**Judgment and joins:** Affirm on finality. He may expressly agree with A and L and the conditional P instruction while dissenting from the immediate merits remand. **Join barriers:** No general rule that repeated denials always establish ripeness, no special procedural exhaustion rule beyond a definitive land-use decision, no arbitrary loss percentage and no award. His separate function would concern the still material land-use choice rather than a disagreement with Dodd's acquisition rule.
