## 1. Components, authority, and decisional paths

The constitutional analysis uses the actual ordinary Central Hudson/Fox framework unless a Justice expressly supplies a distinct ground. The controlling commercial-speech register, Discovery Network, Edenfield, Rubin, 44 Liquormart, and Greater New Orleans require a demonstrated relationship to real harm and reasonable proportional fit, without invariably requiring a controlled study, the least restrictive means, or regulation of every source at once. Their actual judgments and joins, rather than descriptions of historical decisions embedded in the briefs, supply Justice continuity. Actual Cipollone is applied by proposition and statutory period. Its entire amended-Act analysis is not attributed to one majority. Medtronic, Geier, Buckman, and Egelhoff retain their particular statutory predicates. Reno's adult-access concern is relevant by analogy; its criminal Internet strict-scrutiny and overbreadth rules do not automatically govern these commercial restrictions. United Foods resolves a distinct compulsory-private-financing question and does not replace Central Hudson here.

The two cases warrant coordinated treatment of their shared law and record, with separate product and party applications. They are fully briefed merits controversies suitable for signed treatment. No author is selected.

### P — Cigarette advertising preemption

This component concerns only cigarette applications of §21.04(5)(a)–(b), asserted by Lorillard, Brown & Williamson, R.J. Reynolds, and Philip Morris. It does not extend the FCLAA to cigars or smokeless tobacco, decide cigarette retail-access preemption, or reopen the unappealed §21.04(6) severance.

**Path P1: express displacement.** The current §1334(b) covers a state prohibition based on smoking and health with respect to advertising or promotion of federally labeled cigarettes. A tobacco-specific geographical or height ban is such a prohibition even though it regulates placement instead of requiring a different warning. The change from required statements to requirements or prohibitions, the separate wording of subsection (a), and the activity phrase defeat a content-only limitation. General signage controls resting on other grounds, actual sales regulation, and independently actionable deception retain their own predicates.

**Strongest objection to P1:** Cipollone preserves independent duties, the enacted scheme principally establishes nationally uniform smoking-health information, and the State traditionally controls locations and children's access. Reading the clause to disable local spatial protection can exceed the warning scheme and its clear displacement. **Answer sufficient for P1 supporters:** the State's reasons for protecting minors from tobacco consumption remain smoking-and-health reasons; the rule directly selects tobacco advertising, rather than incidentally affecting it. Neither a generally worded enabling statute nor the location label changes that operative duty. Express displacement does not require a second showing that a geographical prohibition makes federal compliance impossible.

**Path P2: preserve the location rules.** Read the clause within its warning-and-information architecture and against displacement of traditional state police powers. These rules leave the federally prescribed cigarette message intact and confine its placement near schools and playgrounds; they do not impose a new smoking-health statement or warning-neutralization duty. Cigarette preemption therefore supplies no ground for reversal.

**Strongest objection to P2:** Congress broadened both the covered obligation and its relation to advertising; a positive prohibition on tobacco advertising seems to fit those words directly. **Answer sufficient for P2 supporters:** the expansion reaches more legal forms and negative controls over the federal informational subject, but the smoking-and-health qualification still limits the domain in statutory context. This is not immunity for all measures called zoning, and it does not disturb Cipollone's covered advertising-warning duties.

### O and H — Advertising under the First Amendment

O-C, O-S, and O-G concern outdoor/exterior-visible advertising of cigarettes, smokeless tobacco, and cigars, respectively. H-C, H-S, and H-G concern the corresponding below-five-foot point-of-sale restrictions within the same geographical zones. Cigarette speech positions of P1 supporters are expressly conditional alternatives; preemption is their sufficient cigarette ground. The cigar and smokeless speech questions are independently reached.

**Path A1: uphold these child-protection restrictions.** The existing record's reports, studies, marketing history, and supported inferences establish a material relationship between promotion and underage use, without a required experiment isolating each medium and product. The State may combine access controls with demand controls. Schools and playgrounds identify places where children are likely to encounter advertising; above-five-foot indoor expression, advertising outside the zones, other media, and the actual product-specific exceptions materially limit the burden. A legislature need not criminalize children's possession, await the failure of all existing programs, or prove an exact optimum radius before adopting a reasonable boundary.

**Strongest objection to A1:** the zones combine with existing restrictions to eliminate the ordinary public-facing channel across much of the urban market; existing channels may be inaccessible to a small shop. The rule reaches unobtrusive adult price and brand information rather than only large signs or child-directed appeals. The height distinction poorly corresponds to all minors' sight lines. The missing cigarette/smokeless small-sign permission and the cigar permission's very limited content must be counted. **Answer sufficient for A1 supporters:** the constitutionally relevant relationship is repeated exposure near children's destinations, not whether each sign specifically targets them or can be read from the school itself. Both aggregate urban coverage and remaining communication matter, but neither commercial third-party overbreadth nor a demand for zero adult spillover supplies the governing standard. The height rule reduces immediate visual encounters rather than purporting to prevent any child from looking upward.

**Path A2: invalidate the challenged categorical advertising applications for disproportionate fit.** Even crediting the State's substantial interest and the supported promotion/use relationship, the categorical rules impose an inadequately justified burden on truthful adult commercial communication. One radius covers very different signs, sizes, settings, and retail communications. The actual urban reach and cumulative existing restraints make this a major channel restriction, not merely a small adjustment. The height rule's treatment of any portion of a display, older minors, and upward viewing lacks a sufficiently responsive connection to its substantial adult burden. The Court can decide this proportionality question without declaring that advertising never affects youth use or converting disputed maps into an agreed incremental percentage.

**Strongest objection to A2:** Fox rejects least-restrictive-means review; youth demand is different from direct sales access; the State already uses substantial taxes, education, enforcement, and counter-advertising. **Answer sufficient for A2 supporters:** no exhaustion or alternative-policy mandate is imposed. Those efforts may support a need for additional action, but do not justify this rule's actual breadth. The Court need not prescribe a smaller radius, a replacement sign rule, or an alternative enforcement program to find the present balance deficient.

**Path A3: stronger protection.** Thomas would apply strict scrutiny to this content-based suppression of truthful information about lawful adult transactions: a compelling child-protection objective does not dispense with narrow tailoring and consideration of materially less restrictive ways to preserve adult communication. Commercial subject matter alone supplies no reduced protection. He recognizes the special legitimacy of protecting minors and does not apply his adult-ignorance objection as a bar to every child-specific rule. His sufficient ground here is the broad suppression of lawful adult speech in mixed-audience locations without the tailoring required to accommodate that protected audience. The government's objective may be compelling; the means still fail. No other Justice is committed in this file to a replacement commercial-speech tier.

### R — Physical retail access, with separate products and parties

R-C concerns only Lorillard and Brown & Williamson's First Amendment claims under §21.04(2)(c)–(d). R-S concerns USSTC's corresponding claim. R-G concerns the cigar petitioners' §22.06(2)(c)–(d) claim, including premium-cigars' sensory inspection. R-C is not attributed to R.J. Reynolds or Philip Morris, and no retail preemption holding is proposed.

**Path R1: uphold.** These rules control access to the physical tobacco product, promote clerk-mediated age checks, and inhibit unauthorized acquisition. They leave visible displays, informative empty packages, clerk-assisted transactions and inspection, and adult-only self-service facilities available on their actual terms. Ordinary sealed-package access is conduct, even though its display can communicate. For cigars, assuming protected informational inspection, the rule still proportionately advances access control while preserving assisted inspection and the adult-only option. This is an independent access justification, not a borrowing of disputed advertising causation.

**Strongest objection to R1 / path R2:** packages communicate prices, freshness, and brands; premium-cigar customers compare texture, aroma, weight, and other features. Empty boxes are not a complete substitute, and the adult-only condition excludes accompanied children. **Answer sufficient for R1 supporters:** the State has not prohibited the information or every lawful inspection. Clerk assistance changes physical access, which is the regulatory objective, without forbidding the adult transaction or the manufacturer's message. The burden is not justified simply by telling a shop to exclude children; assisted access itself remains. No record finding that every shoplifted cigar came from a self-service display, or that such theft supplies most youth cigars, is needed to recognize the direct access relationship. The regulation need not eliminate every route of acquisition to make a material contribution.

### S — Cigar sampling presentation and remedy

Section 22.06(1)(a) is named in the cigar brief. The complete submitted briefing develops no distinct sampling merits analysis. The proper options are to decide a distribution issue by analogy to R, or to decline a separate merits adjudication because this distinct restriction and its adult-only daily exception have not received a developed constitutional challenge. Mere absence of a separate heading would not require nonreach; the conclusion rests on the absence of a sufficient developed ground addressing this provision, not a missing research file, an invented narrow grant, or a default finding.

All eight Associates choose the latter course. Leave the judgment concerning sampling undisturbed, without affirmatively holding the sampling prohibition constitutional, declaring a waiver for other proceedings, granting a new trial, or importing a future filing. State the permitted one-sample-per-adult-per-day adult-only exception and its cigar/little-cigar quantities when describing what remains in effect. No separate cigarette or smokeless sampling challenge is added to the presently identified claims.

### V — Conditional smokeless severability

USSTC's derivative argument is preserved and must be acknowledged: it claims that cigarette preemption takes the shared smokeless advertising provisions with it. Section 21.06 preserves separate applications, and the smokeless prohibition can operate without prescribing any cigarette obligation. The district's particular severance of the generic §21.04(6) sign across both products does not decide this different application question. The text and independently stated interest in underage smokeless use support retaining the smokeless application if the issue is necessary to judgment. No direct FCLAA preemption of smokeless products follows.

The P1 supporters would reject the derivative inseverability argument if it independently mattered, but their constitutional invalidation already supplies USSTC the advertising relief described below. They do not require an unnecessary standalone state-law severability holding to enlarge the judgment. P2 supporters reject the triggering cigarette-preemption premise; they also record the same conditional separability conclusion if that premise is rejected in assembly. This conditional legal position is not an entered ruling or a reason to avoid USSTC's independently presented First Amendment claim.

## 2. Justice-specific commitments and alternatives

Each Justice's conclusions below are independently supported at the specified level. Common result labels do not establish common reasons beyond the express joins described.

### Justice Stevens

**P — Reject cigarette preemption.** Stevens's actual Cipollone predicate-duty analysis preserves the federal clause's distinct limits rather than giving cigarette manufacturers general immunity. His Medtronic concurrence, joined with the case-specific statutory comparisons, resists expanding a federal predicate beyond the duties resolved. His Geier dissent demands support for protected federal exclusivity even when state liability creates practical pressure. Those positions support P2: the federal warning arrangement does not clearly displace these local placement restrictions. The strongest textual answer is the amended requirement/prohibition language; he would acknowledge its force and its coverage of warning-neutralization duties, but decline the inference that every child-protective location rule belongs to that federal informational domain. He will not join a rule exempting all positive state commands, all common law, or every measure called zoning.

**O-C/O-S/O-G — Affirm enforceability under ordinary commercial-speech review.** His Discovery Network opinion distinguishes an unexplained selection among physically equivalent racks from a category tied to the actual harm. Tobacco's attraction to minors supplies the latter connection. His 44 Liquormart and Greater New Orleans joins conditionally apply the ordinary framework while preserving his broader opposition to manipulating lawful adults through ignorance; the present youth-protection objective is materially different. He accepts the existing reports and product-specific inferences as sufficient material advancement for these three categories, while expressly rejecting an inference that low cigar advertising expenditures erase its constitutional protection. He regards the geographical connection and surviving communication as a reasonable fit, even with §21.04(6) removed. The strongest contrary argument is exclusion of adult retail communication across extensive urban areas. His answer is that the same urban pattern places children in recurrent contact with the advertisements; ordinary commercial review permits this limited channel restriction, not a general adult information ban. For cigars, the actual §22.06(6) generic sign is an additional remaining channel, not the exclusive reason for his vote or permission for brand advertising.

**H-C/H-S/H-G — Affirm.** The immediate visual placement rule is modest in relation to an outright retail-advertising prohibition, retains above-five-foot indoor messages not visible outside, and has the amended adult-only exception. He accepts reduction of routine low-level exposure as the operative mechanism; he does not find that children cannot look upward or that every minor is shorter than five feet. Discovery Network requires a related distinction, not the most effective imaginable one. The strongest objection is the combination of weak height evidence and coverage of any portion of a sign. He considers that objection insufficient under Fox's reasonable-fit standard in the geographically limited child-exposure setting. A general state power to suppress unpopular adult messages would remain a join barrier.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm on R1.** His Discovery Network opinion preserves lawful physical placement regulation, and his United Foods concurrence distinguishes different expressive burdens rather than making every economic condition equivalent to compelled utterance. Physical access can be controlled while information and assisted inspection remain. He joins an express alternative reasonable-fit analysis for premium cigars without deciding categorically that sensory inspection is unprotected. He will not join an assertion that an empty box reproduces aroma and texture or that excluding children is costless.

**S/V/remedy.** Decline a separate sampling merits ruling for inadequate development; United Foods's review-discretion treatment does not compel adjudication of every named provision. Reject the derivative severability claim's triggering premise, with the conditional V position recorded above. Affirm the challenged advertising and access judgment on the truthful/nonmisleading assumption, without findings about every advertisement or a new trial entitlement. He favors a substantive separate explanation if a contrary preemption or advertising judgment issues, distinguishing child-protective placement from suppression intended to manipulate lawful adults.

### Justice O'Connor

**P — Preempt cigarette applications of both advertising restrictions.** Her joins to actual Cipollone's amended-clause analysis and Medtronic's inclusion of common-law requirements establish attention to the enacted obligation rather than its label. Her Medtronic partial dissent refuses an extra specificity condition against text she regards as sufficient. Those grounds support P1's direct treatment of the positive, tobacco-specific commands. The best contrary argument is traditional location authority and the warning-centered federal program; she preserves that authority for generally applicable signs and direct sales controls but finds no textual location exception for these smoking-health advertising restrictions. Her Geier join also rejects the proposition that an express clause must exhaust every conflict theory, although no implied-conflict ground is necessary here.

**O-S/O-G — Reverse on reasonable fit; O-C the same conditionally.** Her 44 Liquormart concurrence, joined by Breyer there, gives predictive evidence real weight while independently testing the breadth of the informational burden. She accepts material advancement from the existing record's health reports, historical promotion evidence, and product-specific discussion; she requires neither a state-specific numerical causal estimate nor identical cigarette, smokeless, and cigar studies. The decisive problem is the scope of the public-facing channel burden relative to the school/playground objective. The maps' disputed incremental attribution does not erase the admitted extensive reach, coverage of small signs and adult information, or the greater burden after §21.04(6)'s severance. The cigar sign mitigates but does not preserve prices, brands, or the retailer's full lawful message. Her Reno separate writing protects mixed adult/minor communication while permitting truly minor-only applications; that distinction supports protecting the adult audience here without immunizing targeted unlawful sales. She rejects the counterargument that any alternative medium makes the extensive chosen-channel burden proportionate.

**H-S/H-G — Reverse; H-C the same conditionally.** She finds the five-foot rule insufficiently fitted to the protected population and the full range of information suppressed: older minors are not confined to low sight lines and younger children see above them. This is not a finding of zero effect. The State's low-sign evidence and administrative simplicity make a real argument, but do not justify the categorical height line and any-portion rule. Above-height advertising and the adult-only exception do not answer the burden in ordinary mixed-age shops. Her 44 Liquormart fit position supports this conclusion without strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means rule.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm.** Her Edenfield dissent permits supported prophylaxis tied to a real risk; here the connection is particularly concrete because staff control bears directly on access, theft, and age checking. Her ordinary-framework Rubin and Greater New Orleans joins still require a proportionate burden. R1 meets it because information, assisted inspection, and the actual adult-only route survive. She joins the premium-cigar assumed-speech alternative and refuses a categorical holding that handling can never communicate.

**S/V/remedy.** Decline separate sampling merits adjudication. Her United Foods dissent would reach an actually developed alternative addressing the same assessment; that is not a commitment to decide this separately undeveloped distribution challenge. Record V only conditionally unless necessary. Reverse the cigarette advertising preemption ruling; reverse the smokeless and cigar advertising judgment as applied to the protected speech assumed and adjudicated; retain retail-access judgment. Do not order an evidence-gathering return to justify the same categorical rules, hold all ads truthful, rewrite a radius, or restore the severed sign. She can join a common ordinary-scrutiny opinion carrying these exact holdings and bounded relief.

### Justice Scalia

**P — Preempt.** Actual Cipollone's express-clause separate analysis, which Thomas joined, treats the words requirements and prohibitions according to their ordinary operation and rejects narrowing through the pleading form of the claim. A positive law directly prohibiting tobacco advertisements presents an even more direct application than a damages duty. His Medtronic position likewise rejects extra textual specificity requirements. He accepts P1 without a presumption against preemption that would alter otherwise clear language. The strongest contrary reading invokes local authority and uniform warnings; he finds it inadequate to insert a content-only limit. His support does not extend to an unpresented cigarette-retail preemption theory or general tobacco-field immunity.

**O-S/O-G — Reverse under the existing framework's reasonable-fit requirement; O-C the same conditionally.** He joined both actual Greater New Orleans grounds while retaining reservations about the doctrine. That demonstrates willingness to apply the existing framework without treating disagreement with its foundations as a replacement rule. He accepts the State's supported inference that advertising can promote underage use, including the product-specific material in the existing record, and does not make failure of all advertising-causation proof his ground. The combination of broad geographic exclusion, coverage of ordinary adult messages, and the missing cigarette/smokeless small-sign outlet makes the chosen fit deficient. Even crediting the cigar permission and the State's demand/access distinction, the blanket treatment of differently situated signs is not explained. Discovery Network's rejection of benign-purpose content neutrality defeats a freestanding neutral-zoning defense. He does not announce strict scrutiny for all truthful commercial speech.

**H-S/H-G — Reverse; H-C the same conditionally.** The height line inadequately matches the asserted reduction in minors' encounters, while suppressing ordinary commercial information throughout its categorical reach. The State may use administrable lines, but Fox's rejection of least restrictive means does not eliminate the requirement of a reasonable relation. He can join the same fit holding as O'Connor and Kennedy; he need not join an empirical finding that the rule could have no effect.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm.** The regulation concerns obtaining the actual goods. His Discovery Network join left neutral physical regulation available; his United Foods join concerned compulsory support for a message, absent here. Display messages remain available and personnel-assisted inspection is not forbidden. He joins R1's access-based answer and may join its assumed commercial-speech alternative without treating every product-handling practice as protected expression. The strongest objection, premium-cigar sensory information, does not defeat a rule allowing assisted access. He will not join a general power to prohibit informative display itself on the same conduct rationale.

**S/V/remedy.** Decline separate sampling merits review on the actual lack of developed grounds, consistently with United Foods's discretionary nonreach of a distinct issue. He does not convert that course into jurisdictional inability or a future waiver ruling. Conditional V as above. His relief is the same as O'Connor's, with no altered radius, replacement sign, or trial to validate the existing categorical advertising scheme. He can join the common ordinary-scrutiny holding while noting that the case does not settle his broader commercial-speech reservations.

### Justice Kennedy

**P — Preempt.** His actual Cipollone join to Blackmun's common-law position does not exempt positive legislative and regulatory commands. The present rules are direct prohibitions, not a private remedy for an independent wrong. His Medtronic reservations about deciding common-law inclusion generally likewise do not decide this case. The amended text and the direct smoking-health advertising obligation support P1. The strongest objection is that local location controls leave national messages unchanged. He finds that insufficient because the clause expressly covers prohibition of advertising, not only revision of its words. Geier's actual-conflict reasoning is not necessary to this express result.

**O-S/O-G — Reverse for disproportionate fit; O-C the same conditionally.** Kennedy authored Edenfield and Rubin and the component opinion in actual 44 Liquormart. Those authorities require responsive justification and distinguish advancement from fit. Here he accepts the existing record's sufficiently supported relationship between promotion and underage use, including the State's lawful preventive use of product-specific and related evidence. The claimed small cigar burden is not itself an advancement showing, and he will not join such an equation. Nevertheless, low expenditures do not immunize future promotion, and the reports plus supported inference suffice for the advancement proposition at issue. His decisive objection is the adult informational burden of removing the whole public-facing channel in extensive neighborhoods with insufficient differentiation by sign or setting. The State's efforts and the continuing youth problem explain the importance of regulation, but not this degree of suppression. The cigar generic sign does not cure loss of brands and prices. His stronger noncontrolling 44 Liquormart proposals support careful treatment of the adult audience; his commitment does not require elevating them into controlling strict scrutiny.

**H-S/H-G — Reverse; H-C the same conditionally.** The State's evidence and inference do not justify this inflexible placement distinction. The rule impairs the communication of ordinary lawful commercial information even when its purpose and likely audience are adult. The State need not prove perfect sight lines, but must answer the relation between the five-foot boundary, all minors, and the actual burden. His Edenfield analysis does not demand empirical studies invariably; the mismatch and breadth suffice here.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm.** Edenfield distinguishes materially different professional and vulnerability settings rather than treating every prophylactic rule alike. Actual Greater New Orleans preserves direct regulation of the underlying transaction. The staff-control requirement directly addresses unlawful youth acquisition while leaving the message and assisted inspection available. He joins the assumed-speech fit alternative for premium cigars and expressly distinguishes it from outdoor informational suppression. Empty boxes alone do not answer sensory information, but assisted product inspection does.

**S/V/remedy.** Decline separate sampling merits adjudication for its undeveloped presentation, not because all distribution disputes are unreviewable. Conditional V as above. He supports the same bounded advertising reversal and access affirmance as O'Connor, with relief protecting the lawful truthful advertising assumed in the proceedings. No universal concession, new deceptive-advertising finding, or automatic new trial is adopted. His reasons may be stated in the shared opinion; he requires no separate writing if the direct-advancement/fit distinction and adult audience remain explicit.

### Justice Souter

**P — Reject preemption.** His actual Cipollone join preserves the limited federal informational domain, and his Medtronic and Geier positions require identification of the substantive federal premise before displacing ordinary state authority. He adopts P2's statutory-context reading: local child-exposure controls do not impose a different national health message. He acknowledges that these are positive commands, so the common-law distinction alone cannot decide the case. His sufficient ground is the clause's subject in the enacted scheme, not categorical protection for state regulation or a universal impossibility-only test. The amended language is the strongest opposing argument; he will not ignore it or describe Cipollone as already deciding locations.

**O-C/O-S/O-G — Affirm.** His actual 44 Liquormart joins require both real advancement and fit, while his Greater New Orleans join accepts incremental regulation when coherently justified. These rules address a different, child-specific harm, with an extensive existing record rather than an unexplained exception-ridden adult gambling scheme. Product differences must be evaluated; they do not require one independent controlled causal experiment for every product or display medium. Falling smokeless use does not establish that continued prevention has no benefit, and the cigar evidence permits supported preventive inference without equating low advertising with an unimportant right. He finds the school/playground zones reasonably related to recurrent youth encounters. The strongest counterargument is the substantial adult channel burden, intensified by §21.04(6)'s severance. He considers the zones and remaining indoor/nonzone communications sufficient under ordinary fit, even though the policy could be drawn differently. The actual cigar small-sign permission helps that application but does not serve as a nonexistent smokeless exception.

**H-C/H-S/H-G — Affirm.** The same geographical nexus, immediate exposure rationale, retained high indoor messages, and amended adult-only exception provide a reasonable fit. He does not adopt a scientific finding about children's inability to look above five feet. His objection to A2 is that it treats imperfections in a practical exposure rule as sufficient to invalidate it despite the standard's room for supported prediction. His joins remain conditional on a truthful/nonmisleading commercial-speech analysis; the label unfair or deceptive cannot replace it.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm.** His actual speech decisions preserve direct, adequately fitted controls on transactions and conduct. The access requirement reduces immediate uncontrolled possession, and the premium-cigar burden can be addressed through personnel-assisted inspection. He joins R1 including its assumed-speech alternative; he does not treat all product display as outside the First Amendment or infer no burden on small retailers.

**S/V/remedy.** Decline a separate sampling merits determination on its undeveloped presentation, consistent with the distinct review-discretion choice he joined in United Foods. His willingness there to decide an included legal issue does not supply missing adversarial treatment of this one. Reject the derivative severability trigger; conditional V if necessary. Affirm on the existing summary-judgment assumption, with no new fact trial, no finding that every ad is protected or deceptive, and no revival of warning or sign provisions. A dissent from contrary advertising/preemption holdings should state his evidence-responsive ordinary-framework grounds, not a generalized deference to public-health labels.

### Justice Thomas

**P — Preempt.** His actual Cipollone express-clause join and Medtronic inclusion position give effect to the enacted advertising prohibition. His refusal to join a broader implied-preemption theory in Cipollone, and his Geier dissent on the asserted conflict, do not weaken this direct textual result. The strongest alternative preserves spatial police authority through the warning-centered context; he rejects that rewriting of the positive advertising prohibition while retaining the clause's actual smoking-health, activity, and compliant-label conditions. No general field or obstacle theory is necessary.

**O-S/O-G — Reverse on A3; O-C the same conditionally.** His actual 44 Liquormart and Greater New Orleans separate positions reject manipulation of lawful consumers through informational suppression; his United Foods concurrence rejects reduced protection merely because the burden concerns commercial financing. Here minors cannot lawfully buy the goods, a real distinction from the adult-purchaser cases. He therefore does not claim that every child-protective advertising regulation has an illegitimate aim. His commitment is that protecting minors cannot justify broadly withholding lawful adult product and price information throughout mixed-audience neighborhoods when the rule is not adequately confined to that audience or harmful communication. The retained cigar generic sign and other media are insufficient substitutes for the suppressed adult message. He credits the seriousness of the harm and may assume the causal evidence's strongest permissible reading, but does not join a Central Hudson balancing or affirmative empirical advancement holding. He will not join a categorical rule protecting direct solicitation of unlawful transactions or proven deception.

**H-S/H-G — Reverse on the same stronger account; H-C the same conditionally.** The inflexible height rule burdens adult communications without a satisfactory connection to which children actually encounter the message. It fails even allowing the State compelling reasons to protect young people. His agreement that a less demanding standard also would not save these rules is not a join in a reduced commercial-speech tier. Judgment agreement with the ordinary-fit Justices must remain separate from their full framework coalition.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm on the physical-access ground.** These provisions regulate transfer and possession of actual tobacco, not the information appearing on a display. The rule does not prevent visible messages, staff-assisted inspection, or sale to adults, and the amended adult-only self-service exception remains. His actual Discovery Network dissent allowed substantial local control of physical racks; his later objection to manipulating adult knowledge does not invalidate direct controls on goods. He does not join a rationale that discounts genuinely protected information simply because it is commercial. For premium cigars he accepts that handling communicates but finds that personnel mediation regulates physical access while permitting the adult to receive the information; he need not join a categorical no-speech holding or a Central Hudson alternative. The real sensory distinction cannot be answered merely by offering an empty box.

**S/V/remedy.** Decline a distinct sampling merits adjudication rather than extend the access holding to an undeveloped challenge concerning free distribution. His United Foods review-discretion join supports that course without calling it a jurisdictional or evidentiary default. Conditional V as above. Relief matches the advertising-reversal/access-affirmance disposition but rests on the distinct speech ground. A separate writing is needed if the principal advertising rationale uses Central Hudson; his join to a preemption or access part would not join that framework.

### Justice Ginsburg

**P — Reject preemption.** Her actual Medtronic joins and Geier dissent demand the identified federal predicate and reject general immunity from traditional state regulation. The local spatial rules leave the federally mandated health message intact. She adopts P2 without treating general applicability as an automatic exception or importing an ERISA outcome. Her Egelhoff opinion demonstrates that a direct state substitution for a federal instruction can be preempted even within traditional state law; the difference here is the absence, on her construction, of a federal spatial instruction or protected advertising-location choice. The expanded words are a substantial contrary argument, answered through the clause's qualified subject and scheme rather than a free-floating preference for state law.

**O-C/O-S/O-G — Affirm.** Her actual 44 Liquormart and Greater New Orleans joins demand reasoned advancement and fit; her United Foods dissent would sustain a commercial burden through an independently supported record instead of automatic immunity for economic regulation. This record supports a preventive inference concerning underage tobacco use that differs from the arbitrary or contradictory adult-information schemes in those earlier cases. She accepts separate attention to smokeless trends and cigar marketing and refuses to turn the small cigar advertising footprint into either automatic invalidity or negligible speech value. The school/playground nexus and remaining channels suffice for ordinary fit. Her Reno opinion protects adult expression from a substantially broader criminal network restriction; it does not require zero incidental adult burden in commercial location regulation. The strongest objection is the near-elimination of practical retail communication in affected neighborhoods. She considers that real burden against the particular child's repeated-exposure interest and would sustain the present balance, including the absence of §21.04(6), while retaining the distinct cigar sign permission.

**H-C/H-S/H-G — Affirm.** Lower-height placement is an imperfect but related reduction of immediate encounters in child-frequented areas. Above-height indoor communication and the adult-only exception make this materially different from complete suppression. She gives the evidence and practical inference constitutional weight without claiming that height determines age or that any low advertisement is directed to a child. Her strongest objection to invalidation is a shift from reasonable proportionality toward demanding an optimum placement policy. She would not endorse a mere rational-basis rule or allow the government's deceptive-practice label to avoid commercial scrutiny.

**R-C/R-S/R-G — Affirm.** Her actual commercial-speech joins distinguish informational restraints from direct conduct regulation. The personnel-access rule has a demonstrable access-control function and preserves assisted communication and inspection.
