# Kansas v. Colorado

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-11. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 11, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Overrule Colorado's immunity objection and categorical objection to interest on unliquidated damages. Accept the crop-loss methodology insofar as the report supplies affirmative evidentiary support. Sustain Kansas's objection to an interest cutoff based only on uncertainty or late formal assertion of the established obligation.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for loss-specific compensatory interest from each proven deprivation, with adjustments only for demonstrated duplication, legally material claimant-caused delay, or another specifically justified exception. Verify that chosen rates measure lost use of money rather than punitive leverage; enter no unsupported total.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Principal and interest for a compact violation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Kansas owns and controls this compact claim. Decide the sovereign-character objection before quantifying relief; established liability then requires adjudication of contested compensation rather than reopening the compact.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can Kansas measure its own compact loss through agricultural harm, and does delayed payment justify compensatory interest from the proven losses?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing compact liability and adopt an express extension of the compensatory-interest principle. The adopted loss-date rule is new to this compact remedy, not already dictated by the maritime holding.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Economic injury to agriculture may evidence the state's own loss under its compact right without converting the action into assigned private litigation. Proven compact damages ordinarily include the value of delayed compensation. Unliquidated amount, litigation uncertainty, and absence of a formal demand alone do not postpone accrual; a departure requires a concrete, legally sufficient reason supported by the record.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This decides the character of Kansas's claim and the interest principle. It does not allow all individual losses automatically, prescribe a universal sovereign interest rate, or establish a new allocation of water.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Colorado owed the compact obligation before a complaint made the dispute formal. If water loss and economic injury are proved for a particular year, withholding the value of that loss solely because its amount was contested undercompensates Kansas. The crop model must stand on its evidence, not on the collapse of Colorado's alternative. A calibrated interest calculation can reflect genuine delay attributable to Kansas and avoid double counting without turning uncertainty into an interest-free option to violate the compact.

  The compensatory function of prejudgment interest carries decisive weight once the compact obligation and proved loss are established. The maritime interest precedent is a reasoned analogy, not an assertion that its holding already governed interstate compacts. The loss-date presumption makes Colorado account for the time value of established injury while allowing a demonstrated exception; it does not transform disputed estimates into proven damages.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final dollar amounts, year-specific findings, and the appropriate supported rates remain reserved because the report record does not establish a completed final calculation. Private farmers' independent causes of action are outside these exceptions.
