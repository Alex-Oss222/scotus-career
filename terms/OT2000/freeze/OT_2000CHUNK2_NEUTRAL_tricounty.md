# District of Columbia v. Tri County Industries, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** District of Columbia v. Tri County Industries, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1953; 531 U.S. 287
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-17 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** What standard governs appellate review of a district court’s order granting a new trial unless the plaintiff accepts a remittitur, and whether that standard permitted reinstatement of the first jury’s damages verdict in this procedural-due-process action?
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The District of Columbia Circuit reinstated a $5 million first-trial verdict after the district court ordered a new trial unless Tri County accepted $1 million, and a second jury awarded only $100. The appellate judgment also vacated the second verdict. Its amended judgment addressed postjudgment interest. The underlying permit-related due-process violation had already been established in the earlier appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** The District seeks restoration of the new-trial ruling and second verdict, or reconsideration under deferential appellate review. Tri County seeks preservation of the first verdict and the associated interest judgment.
* **Material Record:** Tri County obtained a permit for a soil-remediation operation and invested approximately $536,421 before the District suspended the permit in September 1993. The damages trial concerned losses caused by the deprivation without adequate procedure, including a projected business opportunity. Its expert projected approximately $11.6 million in profits; the first jury awarded $5 million. The trial judge relied on failure to mitigate, asserted speculation, excessiveness, and reconsidered evidentiary rulings when ordering a new trial. The court of appeals found that the supposed categorical duty to pursue an administrative appeal misstated mitigation law and that the District had not established an available lawful revocation basis or adequately undermined the first-trial valuation evidence. The second trial included materially different defense evidence. Evidence introduced only at that second trial cannot retroactively supply the first new-trial order’s evidentiary basis.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case is before the Court for a decision on the merits. The first-trial record, the actual reasons given for a new trial, and the causation limits of procedural-due-process damages control. A finding that process was denied does not establish that every projected business loss resulted from that denial. The exact wording of the petition is unverified; the question presented states the issue established by the record. Do not reopen settled liability or treat the second verdict as an independent validation of the decision to order that trial.
* **Relevant Dates:** Permit actions occurred in September and October 1993. The first judgment was entered April 20, 1998; the principal appellate opinion issued January 18, 2000. The simulated event is January 17, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

### Issue 1: Review of a new-trial and remittitur order

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Gasperini v. Center for Humanities assigns the trial judge the initial excessiveness determination and the appellate court abuse-of-discretion review, while preserving independent correction of legal error. That case’s application of a particular state excessiveness measure in diversity does not import that measure into this federal civil-rights action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Appellate review distinguishes the governing legal standard from case-specific evaluation. Deference does not protect a rule of law that misstates mitigation or causation, but an appellate court may not substitute its own initial valuation merely because it prefers the jury’s assessment.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 59; the Seventh Amendment’s reexamination protection; 42 U.S.C. §1983; and 28 U.S.C. §1961 for the separately amended interest question.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rule controls institutional allocation. It does not establish a categorical heightened standard whenever a new trial is granted, or itself establish the recoverable amount.
* **Open Point:** Whether the reasons given here were legal errors or permissible discretionary assessments, and whether any independent valid reason sustains the new-trial order.

### Issue 2: Actual damages and mitigation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision establishes the amount of damages recoverable on this record. Ordinary procedural-due-process damages law requires injury caused by the constitutional deprivation; the right to procedure and the value of the underlying business are distinct.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Compensation must track a legally cognizable, proved injury. A damages judgment cannot rest solely on the abstract importance of a constitutional right. A reasonable mitigation inquiry considers the available remedy and its likely utility rather than imposing a universal exhaustion requirement.
* **Other Governing Law:** Carey v. Piphus supplies the received distinction between actual injury and nominal damages for a denial of procedural due process. The applicable evidentiary and mitigation rules remain necessary to any recovery.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Nominal damages may remain available without proved consequential loss. Conversely, uncertainty in the amount of a loss is distinct from absence of evidence that a compensable loss occurred.
* **Open Point:** Whether the first-trial evidence supplied a reasonable basis for this verdict and whether a properly supported lawful-revocation or avoidable-loss finding would limit recovery.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The District emphasizes the trial judge’s experience with the witnesses, the uncertainty of a proposed operation’s profits, administrative remedies that might have reduced the loss, and the danger that searching appellate review displaces Rule 59 discretion. Tri County emphasizes the legal error in treating failure to appeal as an automatic bar, concrete investment and expert evidence, the District’s limited first-trial rebuttal, and the jury’s reduction of the requested sum. The court below treated those deficiencies as reasons why deference could not sustain the new-trial order, rather than simply preferring a higher award.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance is available if each material ground for the new trial rested on legal error or lacked support under the correct deferential standard. Reversal or a focused remand is available if the court of appeals displaced a permissible first-instance assessment or failed to consider an independent sustainable ground. A merits disposition should specify the review standard and its application; it need not approve every description of “more searching” review in the opinion below. Neither outcome permits automatic recovery of profits unrelated to the loss of constitutionally required process. The interest amendment remains tied to the judgment actually reinstated.
