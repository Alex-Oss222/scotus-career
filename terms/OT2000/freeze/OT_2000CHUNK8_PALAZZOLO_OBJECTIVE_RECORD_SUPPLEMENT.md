## Facts and disputed characterizations for validation

1. At least one residential upland use and approximately $200,000 of value are supported. Whether this establishes only a minimum or the maximum permitted upland use is disputed. The state court's quoted finding says at least one home. No new finding that the parcel permits only one home is supplied.
2. Account A describes the State's acceptance of the $200,000 figure in its opposition and trial submissions; account B reports the opposition's actual wording as willingness to approve a single home and denies that it establishes a ceiling. Preserve the distinction between a cited value and an adjudicated maximum development opportunity. Do not infer a litigation concession or waiver beyond the actual supported words and context.
3. Account A reports possible additional uplands on an island requiring a wetlands road. Account B quotes testimony that applying for development at multiple locations would be realistic and that two, three or four lots might support building. These are testimony and reported characterizations, not additional permits, settled development entitlements or findings that any exception's conditions were satisfied.
4. Plaintiff's quoted complaint and trial memorandum emphasize complete deprivation under Lucas. The published accounts also identify Penn Central discussion in both state decisions and in the State's supplemental trial memorandum. Preserve that procedural record without inferring either that plaintiff litigated a developed partial-taking case throughout or that the partial-taking question was absent from the decisions. The scope of presentation and the consequences for this record require independent legal assessment.
5. One published recitation attributes an ordinary-use wetlands-filling limitation to the Attorney General's oral-argument responses at transcript pages 26 and 31. This addendum does not quote those responses or certify their exact scope. If a proposed path turns on a binding concession extending beyond the already quoted regulation and denials, obtain the bounded transcript passage through control. Do not treat the judicial characterization alone as an undisputed new litigant concession.
6. Petitioner is reported to have identified denial of his final filling application in 1986 as the alleged taking event. That is a party contention. The packet's 1983 and 1985 application dates remain distinct from denial dates. No finding establishes that a taking occurred at enactment, at denial, or at any other time, or that an accrued compensation claim transferred with title. The acquisition date and the time of a claimed injury must not be collapsed.
