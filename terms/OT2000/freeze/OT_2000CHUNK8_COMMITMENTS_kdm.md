## Record, components and common limits

The rule has four separate paragraphs. Paragraphs (1) and (2) concern different age groups, deny an individual entitlement after the parents reject an offered free appropriate public education and assign selection, services and delivery location to the relevant public agency under the specified procedures. Paragraph (3) requires a religiously neutral setting. Paragraph (4) separately requires genuine opportunities for equitable participation consistent with the number and needs of children. Invalidating the religious location restriction would not erase those other provisions or create an individual statutory entitlement.

The district furnishes the vision instruction and transportation. Its adequacy and safe travel are stipulated. The specialist would use a separate room even at Harbor Baptist. No actual nonsectarian onsite recipient is established. No lost classroom integration, unavailable service, public devotion or deficient supervision is found. The legal comparison concerns what locations the rule permits, not a fabricated secular child's experience.

The district decree enjoins enforcement of the regulation and religiously different terms but expressly disclaims an unconditional right to a specialist and permits equal nonreligious refusal consistent with IDEA. Thus the petitioner seeks constitutional protection against the categorical location disability; the reviewed decree is not an unconditional onsite order. Its formal breadth against the whole regulation nevertheless requires attention if constitutional relief is restored.

The modeled components are: **A**, free exercise and equal protection as applied to the express religious delivery classification; **B**, the establishment/separation justification and the scope of any constitutional permission; **C**, the resulting appellate disposition and precise prospective remedy. No independent IDEA entitlement, equipment claim, damages, fee amount, Oregon constitutional ruling or RFRA holding is made. The statutory cross-appeal's adverse conclusion is not reopened.

## Governing distinctions

Lukumi's objective targeting rule and strict scrutiny govern a distinctive burden on religious exercise; Smith's neutral-law rule supplies no automatic immunity to a religion-based classification. The religious-targeting register's present operation is Shahar, March 2, 1998, for the separate public-employment inquiry, with Lukumi, June 11, 1993, retaining its targeting and scrutiny components. Shahar contributes no school-services remedy.

Agostini permits independently controlled, operationally separate secular public instruction on religious premises and rejects religious location as a substitute for a prohibited governmental function. It does not itself require provision there. Mitchell, joined by all eight Associates in its substantive portions, requires an actual financed religious function and rejects identity, location, incidental savings or possible diversion as conclusive substitutes. Every Associate must respect that permission even if retaining a different view about whether equal protection or free exercise affirmatively requires equal site eligibility.

Good News Club protects private expression within an actually opened forum, including a legal subject comparison not dependent on proving an Aesop club actually met. Its absence-of-actual-recipient point is instructive for legal eligibility; its access holding does not transform the district's own vision teaching into a private speech forum. Children's Healthcare allows its limited religious accommodation but does not validate all religious classifications; its operational inquiry demands an actual funded-function connection. Freiler concerns the actual official religious undertaking of the complete required disclaimer. Official attribution, religious reference or absence of coercion alone does not decide establishment. It supplies no inference that a public vision specialist on religious premises would preserve or teach doctrine.

## Competing lawful paths and their consequences

**Path R — invalidate the special location disability, with limited relief.** Equal eligibility for possible onsite delivery is a term of the offered secular service. The legal restriction attaches because parents chose religious schooling; a useful alternative and modest practical cost do not make that explicit inequality disappear. Agostini and Mitchell eliminate the asserted categorical establishment necessity. Effective secular control and genuinely neutral educational, cost and administrative criteria are less restrictive means than excluding every religious location. This path reverses the Ninth Circuit's constitutional judgment and remands to tailor the declaration and injunction against paragraph (3)'s categorical religious-site exclusion, retaining paragraphs (1), (2) and (4), the statutory conditions and lawful secular delivery choices. It neither orders an onsite specialist nor requires reproof of stipulated adequacy and safety.

**Strongest objection to R:** It converts constitutional permission into a compulsory government service arrangement although the entire statutory benefit is adequate and safely available, no secular onsite recipient exists and the family need not abandon its religious schooling. **Response available to R:** Its affirmative duty is only to decide location without a prohibited religious disability. The district may retain offsite service for genuine neutral reasons and need not extend the program beyond statutory commitments. A rule expressly withholding eligibility differs from a merely hypothetical unequal administration claim. A speculative institutional separation concern cannot alone support the categorical religion-based disability after the actual-control holdings.

**Path A — sustain this delivery arrangement at its proved scope.** The State continues supplying the child's complete adequate secular service without requiring a change of religious school. A publicly financed instructional location is the government's delivery arrangement, not private expression or religious conduct excluded from a forum. Constitutionally permitted onsite teaching need not become a constitutional obligation to make every otherwise permitted site available. With no interference with religious instruction, unsafe journey, inadequate service or actual unequal service recipient proved, the modest separation choice does not impose the actionable religious-exercise or unequal-access burden asserted here. This path affirms the Ninth Circuit judgment and leaves the district decree displaced; it does not prohibit onsite secular services or approve every religion-based benefit exclusion.

**Strongest objection to A:** The regulation's face reserves a legal possibility for nonreligious sites and denies it to religious sites; neither good motives nor a manageable cost cures discrimination. **Response available to A:** The relevant benefit remains complete public instruction, and government can choose a separately administered location without regulating the parents' exercise or financing the religious school's instructional operation. The significance of actual provision and the character of the governmental service distinguish a religious disqualification from benefits altogether. The conclusion must stay confined to these adequate, safe, nearby services; it cannot become a rule that express religious classifications always receive rational-basis review or that practical coercion is necessary to every Religion Clause violation.

These paths disagree about the constitutionally relevant benefit and burden, not about the stipulated facts or whether Agostini is binding. A new hearing to discover hypothetical devotional use is unnecessary on either path. No neutral source gap establishes a party's default.

## Individual commitments and enhanced comparison

### Justice Stevens

**A — commitment:** Affirm rejection of the as-applied free-exercise and equal-protection claims. The complete service and safe nearby transport leave the parents' religious schooling intact; this state-selected location of its own employees' instruction does not deny the benefit or impose a constitutionally sufficient disability on this record. He would not announce that an explicit religious classification can never burden exercise absent outright denial.

**B — sufficient ground and own authorities:** His substantive Agostini dissent, joined with Souter and Ginsburg, distinguished governmental educational support from independent private expression. His Good News Club dissent again distinguishes a private discussion viewpoint from the character of an integrated instructional activity. Neither dissent controls; his unanimous substantive Mitchell join accepts lawful secular onsite services and forecloses a finding of establishment from school identity alone. The retained distinction supports state discretion over its own delivery arrangement rather than a conclusion that onsite service is forbidden. His Freiler join requires identification of actual governmental religious function; it does not make the location itself religious governance. His Children's Healthcare dissent objects to a preferential affirmative-payment route, whereas K.D.M. receives the same adequate secular service.

**C — judgment/remedy:** Affirm the constitutional reversal below; no reinstatement of the district injunction, onsite order or withdrawal of supplied equipment. Preserve claims resting on actual impairment, unequal services or a materially different classification.

**Adversarial test:** His Lukumi objective-targeting joins and his Mitchell rejection of categorical religious presumptions provide the strongest case for R. He distinguishes a legal restriction on religious practice from a complete public service supplied outside the school's premises, without finding hostility or treating separation as constitutionally compelled. The absence of an actual secular onsite recipient is supportive of the limited application, not an indispensable prerequisite to every facial discrimination claim. His narrower position is affirmance only on the stipulated arrangement. He cannot join an opinion presuming devotional use, resurrecting independent Lemon prongs, validating all faith-based exclusions, or ordering categorical termination of religious-school services. If R prevails, he can accept that its decree must preserve neutral selection and the three independent regulatory paragraphs, but that remedial agreement is not a vote for constitutional invalidation.

**Separate-writing function:** Explain why lawful public instruction does not entail the asserted equal-location entitlement, distinguishing this actual adequate service from private-forum exclusion and a denial of benefits.

### Justice O'Connor

**A — commitment:** Reverse the constitutional judgment. An express disqualification of the chosen religious site from ordinary location consideration is an unequal condition on the offered secular service. Safe adequate alternative delivery does not answer whether the government may impose that special condition solely for religious enrollment.

**B — sufficient ground and own authorities:** Her Lukumi judgment concurrence subjects substantial burdens to compelling justification even under general laws and necessarily condemns targeted religious disabilities. Her full Agostini joins directly recognize independently supervised onsite secular instruction and reject the location presumption. Her Mitchell joins preserve meaningful actual-use safeguards while denying conclusive religious-category reasoning. Her Good News Club full joins support equal treatment within the actual program and a separate functional establishment inquiry. Her Children's Healthcare joins permit a bounded accommodation and require genuine operational examination; no actual religious instruction or diversion justifies categorical exclusion here. Her Freiler join confirms that actual function, not public authorship alone, controls.

**C — judgment/remedy:** Reverse and remand for limited constitutional relief against the religious criterion, correcting the district decree's whole-regulation reach while preserving its express rejection of an automatic specialist entitlement. Independent statutory selection, genuine equitable participation and neutral site reasons remain.

**Adversarial test:** A's strongest point is no substantial educational or travel injury. She treats denial of equal consideration because of the religious exercise as the material disability, without inventing a substantial physical harm. The distinction does not require applying her broader general-law exemption theory or overruling Smith. Zobrest, which she wrote, separates lack of a federal duty from affirmative prohibition; it cannot supply a constitutional mandate or interpreter merits holding. She would join R only with operational secular controls, no automatic onsite award and no exemption from actual devotional-use prohibitions. She will not join a rule equating neutral allocation with constitutional compliance in all aid programs. No separate writing is necessary if those qualifications appear in the shared reasoning.

### Justice Scalia

**A — commitment:** Reverse. The text's express religious-site distinction supplies an objective disability; no search for subjective hostility or history is required. A state may decide to provide service offsite under a genuinely general location policy, but religion is not a sufficient categorical exclusion from the service options it leaves available.

**B — sufficient ground and own authorities:** His Lukumi concurrence joins the objective targeting, general-applicability and strict-scrutiny grounds while rejecting collective motive inquiries. His full Agostini and substantive Mitchell joins remove religious location and possible diversion as categorical constitutional barriers to independently controlled secular work. His Zobrest dissent join favors examining the actual child-directed service rather than withholding constitutional decision through an unnecessary assumption. His Good News Club and Children's Healthcare positions require an actual public religious function rather than attributing private faith to the State. His Freiler dissent likewise distinguishes official attribution from the substantive religious function, while not making absence of compulsion universally conclusive.

**C — judgment/remedy:** Reverse and remand to bar the express religious exclusion and conform the decree to that defect. Preserve nonreligious delivery discretion and paragraphs (1), (2) and (4). No onsite command, invalidation of all IDEA limits or requirement to commission new evidence.

**Adversarial test:** A correctly observes that permission is not obligation, but R imposes only neutral eligibility in a benefit the district supplies; it creates no duty to finance religion. He cannot join a rationale grounded solely in inferred official hostility, a broad substantial-burden exemption to neutral laws, or a supposed private-speech forum. The narrower objective-classification ground suffices. A separate concurrence is needed only if shared reasoning adds those rejected grounds; it would preserve the text-and-operation basis and the limited remedial obligation.

### Justice Souter

### Justice Ginsburg

### Justice Breyer

**A — commitment:** Reverse. The classification needlessly makes religious enrollment determine eligibility for a delivery location within an existing secular service. Adequate alternatives narrow the remedy and defeat an automatic onsite award, but do not supply the missing explanation for that categorical religious distinction.

**B — sufficient ground and own authorities:** His Agostini dissent joined the precedent and procedural objections, not Souter's affirmative substantive bar to separately controlled public instruction. His authored Mitchell rule requires actual funded religious use, reliable secular separation and fitted relief. His Good News Club opinion distinguishes genuine private access from governmental religious activity; its subject-comparison analysis shows why an actual secular recipient is not required to identify a legal exclusion, without creating a public-funding forum. His Children's Healthcare opinion permits a limited accommodation tied to an existing secular benefit and examines real operational control. That justification removes a conflict between religious refusal and care; the categorical site disability here instead adds a religious distinction unsupported by a demonstrated function. His Freiler join rejects actual religious means despite secular objectives but does not identify this vision service as such a means.

**C — judgment/remedy:** Reverse and remand for limited equal-consideration relief, revising the formal injunction against all of OAR 581-15-166, preserving the age-specific no-entitlement rules and equitable-participation duty. The district may select the same offsite arrangement for genuinely neutral reasons under the statutory framework; neither a new hearing nor onsite delivery follows automatically.

**Adversarial test:** A's pragmatic force is substantial: no shown educational deficit or unsafe travel needs correction. He therefore rejects an unconditional site right, but concludes that removing the express religious criterion preserves practical administration without sacrificing constitutional equal treatment. His Mitchell remedial holding supplies the reason to preserve lawful separable applications, not to invalidate an entire program. He cannot join an equation of neutral eligibility with automatic establishment compliance, a new categorical subsidy right, or an invented finding that classroom integration is necessary. A separate writing is unnecessary if the common rule remains at this constrained scope.
