# United Foods — additional predecision advocacy and preservation sources

**Status:** Source-extraction handoff for separate clean neutral validation and affected independent modeling. This document supplies no Justice commitment, current-matter Supreme Court disposition, rationale or lineup. It does not revise the existing frozen neutral packet or commitments.

**Cutoff:** Immediately before June 25, 2001. The sources below are the parties' predecision briefs. They report evidence and advance arguments; their characterizations are not findings. Historical descriptions of earlier Supreme Court decisions in the quoted briefs do not replace the actual entering law.

## Exact propositions and limits

The government reply, page 9 note 7 continuing onto page 10, expressly contests United Foods' claim that it waived an alternative Central Hudson argument. It also says that its understanding of the prior decision made a separate Central Hudson argument unnecessary. The surrounding text argues validity even under a less deferential standard and addresses governmental interest, advancement and the assessment's fit. These positions must be read together. This is not an admitted waiver, an automatic preservation ruling, or a judicial determination of which test applies.

The reply reports a February 2000 Food Marketing and Economics Group document titled Mushroom Council Program Effectiveness Review 1999, and states that copies were lodged with the Court. It quotes the report's page 7 conclusion about demand. This establishes the government's report and proffer, not the truth of the econometric conclusion, its methodological adequacy, a lower-court finding or the availability of an efficacy result on the present review. This handoff does not claim possession or full reading of the lodged review itself. Its quoted conclusion does not establish a benefit to United Foods individually.

The same reply reports testimony about unsuccessful voluntary industry campaigns, explains the small-producer exemption and absence of individual-advertising credits, and describes the national program. United Foods contests the free-rider, effectiveness, regional-benefit and tailoring accounts in its opening brief. Its references to another administrative case and to economics literature retain their respective status; scholarship supplies context rather than controlling authority. Neither side's advocacy becomes a conceded or adjudicated market fact.

Any analysis must separately decide the reviewability and legal relevance of this alternative under the actual entering law, then assess what the supported record permits. The alternative cannot be rejected merely because another prior rationale is limited, nor sustained by treating this proffer as an established factual finding. Material missing facts remain identified; the source gap supplies no litigant default.
