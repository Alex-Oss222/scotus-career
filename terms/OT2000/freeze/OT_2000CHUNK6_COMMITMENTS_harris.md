## Components and actual record

The disputed component is the existing action's monetary claims for mental or emotional injury barred solely by section 1997e(e), asserted by six plaintiffs confined when they commenced suit and released before the district court's judgment. Lack of the requisite physical-injury showing is a premise of this temporal question. The precise sufficiency and authorization of their later supplemental paper were assumed by the appellate court, not decided. The underlying shakedown allegations and entitlement to damages remain unproved. The five other plaintiffs receive no new disposition from the six plaintiffs' release.

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment already requires dismissal without prejudice of the six plaintiffs' monetary claims barred solely by this provision. An affirmance preserves that modification; it must not be described as affirming a dismissal with prejudice. A reversal addresses the legal impossibility of cure, not the actual allowance of the supplement or a finding of liability.

## Competing lawful paths

**Filing-time rule; affirm.** Section 1997e(e) selects the bringing of the action as the relevant event. An action commenced by a confined prisoner for the specified mental or emotional injury without the physical-injury showing remains subject to that restriction despite release before judgment. A supplemental pleading can disclose release but cannot change who brought the existing action or when it was commenced. Dismissal without prejudice respects the limited disability without adjudicating whether a new suit would satisfy limitations, preclusion or other defenses.

McNeil enforces an express prefiling restriction even when the missing condition later occurs and little litigation has taken place. Semtek confirms that a procedural rule cannot alter a substantive statutory condition. Mathews is not a universal rule that all commencement defects are curable: the later benefits application and the Secretary's particular stipulation operated within that statute, with a separate waiver of administrative exhaustion. Section 1997e(e)'s restriction on the action brought by the identified class is the distinct text here.

The strongest objection is that the former prisoner may bring the same action immediately after release, making dismissal and refiling duplicative while Rule 15(d) expressly permits supplementation of a defective original claim. The response is that cure under a procedural rule depends on the substantive statute allowing the subsequent event to remove the original defect. Here the selected historical condition remains true. The result does not rest on speculation that these plaintiffs used litigation for recreation or that refiling costs should punish them. It gives the enacted commencement language effect.

**Postrelease supplementation; reverse and remand.** Section 1997e(e) prohibits the covered filing during confinement but does not expressly make the disability irrevocable after the plaintiff ceases to be a confined prisoner. Rule 15(d) permits a court, on proper motion and reasonable notice and on just terms, to allow a supplemental pleading reflecting the later release even when the original pleading was defective. For the six released plaintiffs, the court may therefore consider continuation on a properly authorized supplement rather than treating dismissal and a new filing as categorically unavoidable.

Mathews recognizes a later event capable of curing even a nonwaivable application condition through supplementation; its separate exhaustion waiver does not erase that distinct application holding. The released plaintiff no longer seeks litigation under a personal status the restriction covers. McNeil's distinct statute required the agency's settlement opportunity before court proceedings began; that lost sequence could not be restored by allowing exhaustion during the same suit. Here release removes a personal limitation and does not deprive an agency or defendant of a congressionally specified prior adjudication. Semtek requires giving the Rule its procedural operation without changing substantive liability; permitting a now-eligible person to supplement does not create a damages right.

The strongest objection is the statute's express bringing language and the principle that Rule 15 cannot override a statutory filing condition. This path accepts that the original filing was defective; it does not redefine brought as maintained or pretend physical injury occurred. It instead finds no statutory direction excluding the ordinary cure mechanism once the personal disability has ended. Administrative economy confirms this reading but cannot independently supersede an unambiguous statute. Release alone does not compel supplementation, eliminate defenses or make the prior filing lawful when made.

Neither path depends on a supposed exhaustion holding in Booth or on a universal rule for all PLRA sections. Artuz requires identifying the actual filing/relief function of the particular provision; it does not classify section 1997e(e). Randell's limited review-opportunity exception supplies no automatic exemption from this express condition and no factual finding about these plaintiffs' diligence. The statutory construction remains open until this event.

## Joins, remedy and completeness

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas share the complete filing-time ground and affirmance of the relevant lower judgment. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer share the complete permissible-supplementation ground and limited reversal/remand. These are four commitments per path, not a final Court result. No claim about a majority or final assignment is made before assembly.

The affirming path does not prohibit every postrelease suit, declare section 1997e(e) jurisdictional, find that the allegations were false or prescribe a tolling rule. The reversing path does not require the district court to allow this actual supplement, cure notice defects automatically, or enlarge available monetary relief beyond the statute and Constitution. Neither decides the physical injury needed in another case, the treatment of nominal or punitive damages, prospective relief, independent economic injury, the constitutional validity of the provision, limitations of a new suit or preclusion. Those are not omitted alternatives to the accepted temporal question.

Any published separate explanation should preserve the real construction difference: the historical commencement trigger and limits of procedural cure on one side; removal of a personal disability, Mathews and discretionary supplementation on the other. No gratuitous speculation about prisoners or additional constitutional holding is needed. The source conflict is legal and has been fully modeled; it does not prevent a supported commitment. The actual supplementation dispute limits the remedy on the reversal path and is expressly left for remand.
