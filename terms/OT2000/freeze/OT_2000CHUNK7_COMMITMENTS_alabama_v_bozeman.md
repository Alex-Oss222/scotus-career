## Reachability and operative issue

All eight Associates participate. Direct review concerns a timely Article IV(e) objection to Alabama's return of a federally imprisoned defendant before trial. The constitutional merits of the firearm charges are not at issue. Alabama, rather than the United States, was the receiving jurisdiction. The federal receiving-jurisdiction modifications therefore do not supply discretionary dismissal here.

The statutory trigger is return to the original place of imprisonment before trial on the covered charges. The prescribed consequence is that those charges lose force and must be dismissed with prejudice. A later timely trial does not undo the prior return. The construction does not eliminate a legally effective waiver; it rejects an exception based solely on a brief trip, absence of proved injury, or the receiving State's view of the prisoner's interests.

## Alternative-path test and join boundaries

The strongest competing path treats this short return as outside the evil of repeated interruptions and avoids the severe result of setting aside an otherwise completed conviction. Its legal difficulty is that duration and rehabilitative injury are not statutory triggers, and the compact expressly states what follows when trial has not occurred before return. Reading purpose to erase that command would substitute a case-specific harm test for the adopted rule. Every Associate rejects that path on these facts.

A genuinely authorized waiver could matter, but the packet establishes no request or knowing agreement to this return. Hill concerns counsel's express agreement to an identified trial date, not general surrender of anti-shuttling protection. No Justice would remand solely to create an unpreserved waiver theory or infer assent from silence. No Justice joins a holding that every compact protection is unwaivable.

Reed's collateral-remedy route is legally unavailable as the standard for this direct appeal. Its distinction remains in force, including its assumed rather than decided compact violation. Neither an additional finding of bad faith nor a constitutional violation is necessary here.
