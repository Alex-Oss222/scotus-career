# Florida v. Thomas

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Florida v. Thomas. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-391; 532 U.S. 774. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Supreme Court of Florida's judgment. **Simulated Event Date:** June 4, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether New York v. Belton's vehicle-search rule is limited to an arrest in which the officer initiates contact while the arrestee remains inside the vehicle. A separate antecedent question is whether the state judgment is final under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a).

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the granted search question. Federal finality is an antecedent jurisdictional question. Exact petition wording has not been independently verified.

**Material Record:** Police investigating marijuana sales at a home encountered Robert Thomas after he drove into the driveway, parked, and walked to the rear of his vehicle. A license check disclosed an outstanding arrest warrant. An officer arrested and handcuffed him, took him inside the home, then returned alone and searched the car, finding methamphetamine. The criminal charges included possession and related narcotics offenses. [Florida v. Thomas](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-774.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The trial court suppressed the narcotics and paraphernalia. Florida's Second District reversed under New York v. Belton. The State Supreme Court rejected New York v. Belton's application because police contact began after Thomas left the car, but remanded for findings and a determination under Chimel v. California. The record did not permit that court to determine whether officer safety or preservation of evidence justified the search. **Relief Requested:** Florida seeks reversal of the contact-inside limitation. Thomas seeks to preserve the state court's ruling and remand.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The merits briefs did not raise federal finality, but jurisdiction cannot be established by silence or agreement. Florida has not conceded that it loses under Chimel v. California. The State can appeal a renewed pretrial suppression order under Florida's procedural law. This is not yet a conclusive order requiring a trial without the evidence. The arrestee's precise ability to reach the car and the remand findings must not be invented.

**Relevant Dates:** Argument April 25, 2001; review event June 4, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holdings in Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, Illinois v. McArthur, or Arkansas v. Sullivan must be considered for their relevance to the search issue. Their historical outcomes do not supply governing law.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal finality must be established independently of the parties' agreement or their failure to brief it. Florida's available pretrial review procedures matter to whether later federal correction remains possible.

### Issue 1: Finality of the state judgment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Jefferson v. City of Tarrant controls the §1257 issue. A state ruling that finally settles one federal legal question is not automatically a final judgment while substantive proceedings remain. Its preserved Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn routes require practical completion, an issue surviving every further outcome, unavailability of effective later review whatever the outcome, or litigation-preclusive reversal coupled with serious threatened erosion of federal policy. Importance and state law-of-the-case effect do not suffice.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The state-judgment-finality standard preserves those practical-finality routes and requires a concrete account of what proceedings remain and whether the federal issue can still receive effective review. Settlement of an interlocutory legal proposition alone is insufficient.

**Other Governing Law:** Section 1257(a) supplies jurisdiction over the relevant final judgments of a State's highest court. The relevant Florida appeal provisions permit the State to seek review of a renewed adverse pretrial suppression ruling. [Florida v. Thomas](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-774.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn performs the work preserved in Jefferson v. City of Tarrant; it does not supply a free-standing exception merely because an interlocutory federal question matters.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law governs substantive remands and the practical possibility of later review. The unresolved Chimel v. California ground and Florida pretrial appeal route must be tested against that rule.

**Open Point:** No new finality doctrine is needed. The issue is whether this judgment satisfies one of the existing routes on the actual procedural record.

### Issue 2: Search incident to arrest of a recent occupant

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Knowles v. Iowa rejects converting an unused power to arrest into a search incident to a mere citation. Whren v. United States separates objective grounds for initial enforcement from justification of later searches. Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights preserves independent scrutiny of additional custodial steps, but this case involves an outstanding warrant and presents a different asserted defect.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The corresponding search-incident and independent-intrusion standards require a valid source for the actual search, not merely lawful initial contact or an unexercised enforcement option. They do not resolve the first-contact-outside question or dispense with appellate jurisdiction.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment governs the vehicle search. New York v. Belton and Chimel v. California supply the background search-incident doctrines, subject to the independent limits described above.

**Material Other Authority:** The Florida courts' competing New York v. Belton readings are the review controversy. Neither is a controlling Supreme Court holding.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** A lawful arrest does not by itself settle every subsequent search. The existing doctrine's permitted passenger-compartment reach and ordinary safety-and-evidence rationale must remain distinct.

**Open Point:** The precise scope of New York v. Belton for a recent occupant remains open on this record, and the state remand independently leaves Chimel v. California unresolved. A federal merits answer requires appellate jurisdiction first.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The intermediate court applied New York v. Belton's categorical passenger-compartment rule. The State Supreme Court instead made the point of police contact the dividing line, with ordinary Chimel v. California analysis available afterward. Its remand therefore performs substantive work even though its New York v. Belton interpretation is settled for the ongoing state proceeding.

**State's strongest positions:** An occupant can exit before the officer has a chance to make contact, and a rule turning on that happenstance may create arbitrary distinctions and impede safe enforcement. A recent occupant's arrest may justify a contemporaneous passenger-compartment search. On jurisdiction, the State can argue that a definitive federal suppression rule ordinarily will escape review if it must proceed to trial and either obtain a conviction or face double-jeopardy barriers after acquittal.

**Thomas's strongest positions:** Search incident must remain attached to a lawful arrest and the actual justifications supplied by the doctrine. He was already outside, secured, and inside the residence when the officer returned alone. The federal Court should not extend a bright line beyond its existing basis. More immediately, the State has not yet lost the admissibility issue: it may prevail on Chimel v. California or obtain pretrial appellate review after an adverse determination.

**Government participation:** The United States appeared as amicus urging reversal on the search question. Its participation supplies no jurisdictional concession or additional record finding. A disagreement over the desirable merits rule cannot resolve the statutory finality requirement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold path:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction if the judgment fails the preserved practical-finality routes. Further factfinding prevents practical completion. The New York v. Belton issue may become unnecessary if the State prevails under Chimel v. California. Later pretrial review remains possible if it loses. Reversing the New York v. Belton ruling would determine evidence admissibility, not terminate the prosecution, and the ordinary delay inherent in suppression litigation does not establish serious erosion of a distinct federal policy.

**Plausible Holding Scope and Conditional Merits Paths:** Only after identifying a valid jurisdictional route could the Court uphold the contact-inside limitation, reject it and remand for a properly defined recent-occupant inquiry, or adopt another lawful application of search-incident doctrine. A merits reversal would not establish every remaining admissibility ground or guilt. The present remand's factual uncertainty would remain relevant to any noncategorical approach.

**Judgment consequences:** Jurisdictional dismissal neither affirms nor vacates the state merits ruling. It leaves the state proceeding to continue and permits a later petition from a reviewable judgment, subject to then-applicable requirements. No suppression mandate, new trial, conviction, exclusionary-rule exception, or damages remedy is warranted at this stage.

# Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-346; 532 U.S. 811. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Fourth Circuit's reversal of dismissal. **Simulated Event Date:** June 4, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether negligent breach of a general maritime duty of care is actionable under general maritime law when it causes death, as it is when it causes nonfatal injury.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully states the question the judicial opinion identifies; the petition's exact wording has not been independently verified.

**Material Record:** Christopher Garris was injured on April 8, 1997 while sandblasting aboard the USNS Maj. Stephen W. Pless, berthed in navigable waters, and died the next day. His employer, Tidewater Temps, subcontracted for Mid-Atlantic Coatings, which subcontracted for Norfolk. His mother, as estate administrator, alleged negligence by Norfolk and another subcontractor. Norfolk neither employed him nor owned the vessel. These are the pleaded and procedural premises of the dismissal posture, not findings that Norfolk owed or breached every alleged duty. [Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-811.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of Virginia dismissed the federal claim categorically because it recognized no general maritime negligence wrongful-death action for a nonseaman in territorial waters. The Fourth Circuit reversed and remanded, treating the principles of Moragne v. States Marine Lines as supporting the action. **Relief Requested:** Norfolk seeks reinstatement of dismissal; Garris seeks continuation of the federal claim. The separately pursued Virginia wrongful-death claim was resolved against her in state court and is no longer pursued here. This case does not reopen that judgment.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Admiralty jurisdiction was invoked under 28 U.S.C. §1333. No preserved alternative jurisdictional defect is established. The dismissal assumes a maritime duty and negligent breach for the purpose of the categorical remedy question. Liability, causation, available damage categories, and amount remain to be adjudicated; preserved defenses may be presented on remand.

**Relevant Dates:** Injury April 8, 1997; death April 9, 1997; argument April 18, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Lewis v. Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc. concerning maritime procedure must be considered if material to this negligence claim. Its historical disposition does not govern.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** The dismissal tests whether a pleaded negligence death claim exists under maritime law. It does not establish the factual elements of liability or a particular amount of recovery.

### Issue 1: Maritime wrongful death for third-party negligence

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun holds that Moragne v. States Marine Lines's maritime death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state death and survival remedies for nonseafarers in territorial waters outside an exclusive statutory regime. It expressly assumes, rather than resolves, some extensions of Moragne v. States Marine Lines and does not settle the present worker's negligence action or damages. Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co. refuses a parallel survival action where DOHSA governs and Congress has selected the compensation scheme; that rule is bounded by the Act's actual coverage.

American Dredging Co. v. Miller preserves the distinction between state forum administration and substantive maritime obligations and remedies. A procedural label cannot authorize alteration of a characteristic maritime right. Its preservation of Miles v. Apex Marine Corp. demands respect for Congress's applicable remedial choices, not universal statutory exclusivity across all maritime cases.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Maritime law distinguishes forum procedure from substantive rights and preserves the applicable congressional compensation scheme and the state remedies recognized in Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun. Those rules do not turn legislation in an adjacent maritime field into categorical displacement of every third-party negligence claim.

**Other Governing Law:** Admiralty jurisdiction rests on the constitutional and statutory grant, including §1333. The Jones Act concerns seamen; Garris's pleaded status is nonseaman. DOHSA concerns the specified high-seas area rather than this territorial-water death. The Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act supplies employer compensation and vessel-negligence rules while preserving claims against third parties. Those statutory boundaries must be examined before recognizing a maritime action; a judicial remedy cannot contradict an applicable exclusive remedy. [Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-811.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** Moragne v. States Marine Lines supplies the general maritime wrongful-death foundation. Negligence is an established maritime duty; the question is the death remedy's coverage. Moragne v. States Marine Lines's original unseaworthiness facts must not be disguised as a specific earlier decision of this negligence claim.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires respect for the actual boundaries of federal maritime remedial statutes, recognizes general maritime death remedies, and preserves state remedies in Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun's particular setting. It does not make death an automatic defense to every maritime negligence claim or authorize supplementing DOHSA contrary to Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co..

**Open Point:** No controlling decision resolves this exact third-party negligence death claim. Whether Moragne v. States Marine Lines's remedial principle extends to this established duty in a setting outside the asserted exclusive statutory regimes remains the merits question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Norfolk's strongest rule:** Congress has legislated comprehensively for maritime injuries and death. Judicial creation of a new cause of action could disturb the allocation among employer compensation, vessel liability, and state remedies. Moragne v. States Marine Lines involved unseaworthiness, and its broad wording should not be treated as a decision of every different duty. The LHWCA's elimination of some unseaworthiness actions may demonstrate deliberate remedial limits.

**Garris's strongest rule:** The action completes an existing maritime duty and death remedy in a setting Congress has not occupied exclusively. A third party should not escape a maritime negligence remedy solely because the injury proved fatal. The LHWCA's third-party savings provisions protect maritime as well as state claims; its abolition of covered unseaworthiness claims does not abolish negligence. Neither the Jones Act nor DOHSA reaches this claimant and location.

**Below and concessions:** The district court's categorical dismissal did not decide Norfolk's specific duty or breach; the appellate reversal likewise does not establish a damages entitlement. The analogy to Moragne v. States Marine Lines is direct in remedial logic but still requires candid identification of the extension from its unseaworthiness facts. The parties' treatment of the threshold issue does not concede ultimate negligence, a damage measure, or a survival claim.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Available paths:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit if an existing general maritime duty supports a death action and no applicable statute precludes it. Reverse if the relevant congressional scheme forecloses this specific remedy or if Moragne v. States Marine Lines is authoritatively limited in a way that requires denying it. A vacatur for a genuinely unresolved coverage issue would need to identify the issue and preserved factual dispute; generalized unease about common lawmaking is not itself a missing record finding.

**Plausible Holding Scope and Remedy:** Affirmance removes the categorical no-action barrier and returns the case for adjudication of preserved duty, breach, causation, and remedy questions. It does not award damages, hold that Norfolk was Garris's employer or vessel owner, or erase statutory defenses. It does not expand recovery under DOHSA, alter Jones Act seaman remedies, revive the separate state claim, or establish a universal punitive-damages or survival rule.

**Institutional boundary:** General maritime law includes a recognized judicial lawmaking role, but applicable enacted limits remain controlling. Both blanket displacement because Congress has legislated somewhere in the field and unlimited judicial supplementation would exceed what the governing decisions establish.

# United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-157; 532 U.S. 822. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Fourth Circuit's refund judgment. **Simulated Event Date:** June 4, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether an affiliated group filing consolidated returns calculates its product liability loss on a consolidated basis in the first instance or by aggregating product liability losses separately calculated for each member.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the calculation question reviewed; the petition's exact wording has not been independently verified.

**Material Record:** United Dominion's predecessor AMCA filed consolidated returns for 1983 through 1986. In each year its consolidated net operating loss exceeded all members' aggregate product liability expenses. Five subsidiaries nevertheless had positive separate taxable income, subject to immaterial exceptions. The amounts and characterization of their product liability expenses are undisputed; the disagreement concerns whether those expenses count toward the group's loss eligible for the special ten-year carryback. The group comprised 26 members. [United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-822.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The refund claims produced an administrative disagreement, followed by a refund action in the Western District of North Carolina. The district court accepted the consolidated calculation. The Fourth Circuit reversed, requiring separate-member computation and using the regulatory concept of separate net operating loss. **Relief Requested:** United Dominion seeks reversal and treatment of the disputed expenses as part of the consolidated product liability loss; the Government seeks preservation of the limitation. A conflicting Sixth Circuit approach helped frame the issue but is not controlling precedent.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The relevant Code and Treasury regulations are those governing the 1983-1986 tax years, not a later revision merely in effect on the merits date. No jurisdictional or refund-claim preservation defect is established in the available record. The special carryback's mathematical availability does not establish every remaining refund calculation or entitlement outside the litigated expense treatment.

**Relevant Dates:** Tax years 1983 through 1986; argument March 26, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion; related same-term tax matters are addressed below. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Gitlitz v. Commissioner or United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co. concerning tax interpretation must be considered before adjudication. Their historical dispositions do not govern.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** This refund action concerns the legal computation for the litigated tax years. A ruling on the calculation method does not establish an unsupported dollar award or eliminate any preserved issue in the remaining refund accounting.

### Issue 1: Consolidated product liability loss

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. requires courts to enforce enacted terms and actual delegation boundaries before according appropriate effect to a reasonable formally promulgated interpretation. It does not turn any agency litigation position into a binding regulation or cure a gap with administrative labels. Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes informal interpretations from force-of-law rules and preserves persuasion on the merits.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The statutory interpretation and delegated-authority standards require reading the whole operative scheme and identifying actual agency authority. United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Christensen v. Harris County preserve the distinction between an enacted or promulgated rule and the persuasive force of a litigation interpretation. They do not select a financial result before interpreting the law.

**Other Governing Law:** The applicable §172 provisions define product liability loss as the lesser of net operating loss and allowable product liability expenses. Sections 1501 and 1502 permit consolidated returns and delegate their detailed administration. The then-operative regulations calculate consolidated taxable income or consolidated net operating loss using members' separate taxable income, adjusted by items treated at group level. Separate taxable income excludes several items that would enter an ordinary corporation's net operating loss. A separate-net-operating-loss figure also appears for allocation to a member carrying a group loss to a year outside the group. Whether that different-use figure controls the initial group product-liability calculation is disputed. [United Dominion Industries, Inc. v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-822.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** The conflicting circuit approaches expose the accounting disagreement but are not controlling precedent. The agency's asserted anti-avoidance concerns must be tied to the operative provisions, rather than assumed to supply an unexpressed restriction.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing framework requires interpretation of the actual tax-year regulations under the Code. It does not invite invalidation of consolidated returns or an assumption that each affiliate possesses a separately defined NOL for every computation. An agency's power to adopt a different prospective lawful rule is separate from what the existing rule says.

**Open Point:** No controlling decision decides whether consolidated product liability loss is calculated using group NOL and aggregate expenses initially or by first limiting each member's expenses through a separate-loss calculation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**United Dominion's strongest rule:** The consolidated-return regulations provide the relevant NOL only at the group level. Once that NOL is calculated, the statute compares it with the group's product liability expenses. Positive separate taxable income is not a separate NOL calculation because it omits items the scheme places at the consolidated level. Importing an allocation rule designed for a member's nonconsolidated year would change its function and add a limitation Treasury did not adopt.

**Government's strongest rule:** Congress tied the special carryback to product liability losses, not every expense arising in a profitable subsidiary. The group approach can use another member's unrelated losses to carry profitable affiliates' expenses back ten years and may invite tax planning. A separate-member calculation better preserves the connection between the expense and the member's economic loss; the allocation regulation supplies an administrable figure.

**Lower reasoning and limits:** The district court accepted group-level comparison where consolidated NOL exceeded total expenses. The Fourth Circuit used member-specific losses and regulatory allocation language. The Government's concerns about duplicate benefits and avoidance are relevant to reading ambiguity, but no concession establishes an actual double deduction or abusive transaction here. Likewise, the taxpayer's correct arithmetic would not prove its legal interpretation without examining the regulatory definitions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Merits paths:** Reverse if the operative scheme creates only a consolidated NOL for these returns and supplies no separate-member product-liability ceiling. Affirm if the statutory and regulatory text actually incorporates the separate-member allocation figure for this purpose. A remand solely for Treasury to formulate a preferred limitation would not decide the parties' liability under the operative rules. If a genuinely unresolved application calculation remains after interpretation, it may be addressed on remand without reopening the legal question.

**Plausible Holding Scope and Consequences:** A group-level rule counts eligible expenses only up to the consolidated NOL and permits the special carryback only under its enacted conditions. It does not make product liability expenses deductible twice, allow losses beyond the group NOL, or establish every tax benefit claimed by an affiliated group. A member-level rule would exclude the disputed positive-income affiliates' expenses from the asserted group carryback to the extent of the separate limitation. Neither path should transform the allocation regulation's different context without explanation.

**Remedy:** The Court may reverse the appellate legal ruling and remand for recalculation and entry of the proper refund judgment. It should not invent an exact dollar award from aggregate expense figures or resolve tax years and provisions outside this controversy.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 4, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier controlling decision affecting intellectual-property exclusivity or antitrust treatment of unilateral conduct must be applied. New York Times Co. v. Tasini is scheduled later and cannot supply entering law; its separate copyright question is not resolved here.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Court reviews the legal basis for summary judgment; removing an erroneous categorical defense does not establish every element of the plaintiff's claim or entitlement to compulsory licensing.

### Issue 1: Intellectual-property exclusion and monopolization

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc., supported by six Justices, rejects equipment-market competition as a categorical answer to parts and service aftermarket power. A single-brand market is possible when actual substitutes and restraints support it. Monopolization requires both relevant-market power and willful acquisition or maintenance distinct from superior products, business acumen, or accident. Evidence of parts restrictions and service exclusion can raise genuine issues, while quality, inventory, and free-riding explanations remain subject to the actual record. That decision establishes neither a universal duty to supply competitors nor a final pretext or liability finding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc. requires factual inquiry into substitutes, switching costs, information, price effects, and exclusion. Patent ownership, brand identity, proprietary design, and numerical share alone do not establish monopoly power. Power, unlawful maintenance, antitrust injury, causation, and remedy remain distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 2 of the Sherman Act prohibits monopolization and attempted monopolization. The patent laws protect exclusion within the patent grant, and the operative patent-misuse provision protects a patent owner from loss of otherwise available relief merely for refusing to license or use patent rights. Copyright grants specified exclusive rights, including distribution. The interaction of those provisions with a sufficiently established antitrust wrong is the issue, not a premise of general antitrust exemption. [In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/203/203.F3d.1322.99-1323.html).
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Grinnell Corp. and Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp. retain the power, exclusion, and justification principles expressly preserved in Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc. The circuit's treatment of Data General and the later lower-court Image Technical Services litigation identifies competing approaches to intellectual-property justification, and does not establish controlling Supreme Court rules. Later refusal-to-deal decisions do not govern this adjudication.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law permits aftermarket monopolization claims and requires competitive proof, while preserving legitimate business explanations and the absence of a general supply duty. It does not decide whether valid patents or copyrights categorically foreclose the refusal theory whenever fraud, sham litigation, and tying are absent.
* **Open Point:** Whether intellectual-property exclusivity is a conclusive defense to this kind of unilateral exclusion claim, or a legally weighty interest assessed within a properly proved monopolization inquiry; what role objective effects and asserted pretext may play.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** For patents, the Federal Circuit treated refusal within the grant as protected absent illegal tying, procurement fraud, or sham litigation, and declined to investigate subjective motivation. It rejected the claim that effects in a separate service market necessarily exceeded the grant. For copyright, it invoked a presumptively valid exclusion justification and found no definitive rebuttal through unlawful acquisition or extension beyond the copyright grant. It rejected an inquiry resting on subjective pretext alone. [In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/203/203.F3d.1322.99-1323.html).
* **CSU's Strongest Position:** A patent or copyright provides exclusion rights in the protected work; it does not confer a right to use input control to maintain monopoly power in every adjoining commercial activity. Xerox supplied parts to end users while closing independent-service channels, allegedly protecting its service position rather than simply earning returns on invention or expression. Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc. makes actual aftermarket effects material. CSU seeks ordinary monopolization analysis with meaningful recognition of intellectual-property incentives, rather than an exhaustive three-exception immunity.
* **Xerox's Strongest Position:** Refusal is central to the statutory grant. A rule making a desire to exclude competitors sufficient evidence of unlawful intent would punish exercise of the right itself and undermine incentives to create protected products and software. A separate antitrust market does not automatically define the patent's legal scope. CSU must prove an antitrust wrong beyond harm inherent in lawful exclusion, and no fraud, sham suit, or illegal tie was alleged. Xerox seeks protection for a refusal within the statutory grant unless a distinct legally sufficient misuse or antitrust violation is established.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limits:** Intellectual property creates no automatic monopoly-power finding. Harm to CSU alone is not harm to competition. Subjective hostility cannot independently establish monopolization. Conversely, the existence of an exclusion right cannot immunize every related agreement, extension of control, or objectively exclusionary course of conduct. The published descriptions of amici identify participation but do not supply a necessary additional position that must be inferred here.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Decide the legal defense on the preserved antitrust claims. The absence of pleaded fraud, sham litigation, or tying cannot be repaired by inventing those claims. No independently identified jurisdictional defect requires dismissal.
* **Merits Paths and Plausible Holding Scope:** One path would protect unilateral refusal within the valid statutory grant from liability based solely on its exclusionary effects, while retaining distinct antitrust wrongs such as an illegal tie. Its scope would depend on determining what conduct falls within that grant. Another would reject categorical immunity and require ordinary proof of monopolization while giving genuine statutory exclusion and innovation interests appropriate legal weight. That rule could permit objective evidence of aftermarket maintenance and inadequate explanations without making a competitor's desire for access or a right holder's desire to exclude sufficient. A motive-only rule or automatic compulsory-license entitlement would exceed the supported antitrust framework.
* **Judgment and Remedy Consequences:** Affirmance ends CSU's affected antitrust claims on the established legal ground. Vacatur returns them for summary-judgment analysis under the correct rule and, only where necessary, further proceedings. Neither vacatur nor rejection of categorical protection awards damages, determines infringement, or supplies a license. Any eventual injunction must follow proved violation, causal injury, and a remedy fitted to that violation.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The published appellate opinion does not establish the actual relevant markets, durability of power, consumer effects, full scope of the patents, or causal allocation among protected and unprotected inputs. Those are material to eventual liability. The settlement's temporary access provisions do not by themselves establish that forced access is legally required, costless, or administratively appropriate.

It does not establish an alternative presidential, judicial, or department-head appointment that this Court may assume into existence.

The agency preserved its contention that de novo Board review and absence of prejudice defeated relief. The Court therefore faces a cure argument that the governing appointment-remedy precedent expressly left open in other review structures. Appointments classification and remedy should be resolved separately; finding a statutory office does not itself establish who validly appointed its occupant.

**Relevant Dates:** FDIC notice, April 30, 1996; Board order, May 25, 1999; appellate argument, November 19, 1999; judgment under review, March 3, 2000; assigned decision, June 15, 2001. The lower judgment predates the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Another bank participant's separate petition does not receive a brief or disposition here. United States v. Hilario is an earlier assigned same-term matter potentially relevant to inferior-officer classification and permissible appointing authorities. No controlling disposition is identified.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 15, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier controlling decision in United States v. Hilario must be applied within its scope. United States v. Mead Corp. is decided later and cannot govern this adjudication.

### Issue 1: Officer Status and Appointment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Edmond v. United States recognizes significant adjudicative duties and uses the actual statutory hierarchy to distinguish inferior from principal officers. Direction, assignment control, removal from judicial assignments, and superior review collectively established inferior status there. Neither review nor removal alone is a universal classification rule. Its unanimous statutory holding also distinguishes appointment from assignment: the authorized Secretary's appointment supplied the lawful act; administrative assignment did not substitute for it.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Edmond v. United States requires assessment of the statutory structure as a whole. Adjudicative independence does not automatically create a principal officer, and superior review does not automatically reduce an adjudicator to a mere employee. The civilian military appellate office remains the decided application.

**Other Governing Law:** The Appointments Clause identifies permissible methods of appointing officers. The statutory and regulatory scheme establishes continuing ALJ offices, prescribes their duties and compensation, authorizes formal hearings, and reserves the FDIC's final decision to its Board. The relevant provisions and their operation are reproduced and discussed in the [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

**Material Other Authority:** Freytag v. Commissioner and Buckley v. Valeo address the constitutional requirements for appointing officers. The lower court's disagreement concerns whether significant hearing powers suffice or whether the possession of final decisional authority is indispensable. The lower majority and separate opinion supply competing readings, and do not themselves establish controlling Supreme Court law.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Edmond v. United States prevents collapsing officer status into the question whether an adjudicator is subordinate. It does not itself decide the status of every administrative employee who assists a decisionmaker.

**Open Point:** Whether the continuing, discretionary powers exercised by this hearing ALJ make him an officer despite complete Board review, and the consequences of the FDIC's abandoned proper-appointment defense.

### Issue 2: Cure and Direct-Review Relief

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Ryder v. United States requires lawful fresh adjudication for a timely direct challenge to a substantial constitutional defect in adjudicators' appointments and rejects the de facto officer doctrine as a defense to that claimant. Its accompanying holding rejects the particular higher review there because it did not provide the displaced proceeding's factual weighing, credibility decisions, contested-fact resolution, and sentence review.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ryder v. United States reserves the availability of harmless-error review and cure in a different appellate structure. It also excludes automatic dismissal of underlying charges and universal collateral reopening.

**Other Governing Law:** The APA's prejudicial-error instruction and the FDIC's duty to make its own findings support the agency's cure argument. They must operate consistently with the constitutional requirement rather than determine its existence. [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

**Material Other Authority:** The lower majority regarded the demand for a causal link between appointment error and outcome as especially problematic where the alleged officer's only output was a recommendation. The separate opinion distinguished declaring the defect from granting relief and regarded future prehearing challenges as adequate enforcement.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The timely-direct-review rule governs. Whether review of an ALJ-created record supplies the constitutionally missing adjudication is an application and extension question. Neither automatic cure nor automatic failure of every imaginable cure is already established.

**Open Point:** Whether genuinely independent review of this existing record is sufficient when the initial adjudicator controlled evidence, hearing procedure, and the creation of that record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The majority accepted that the ALJ's office and duties were prescribed by law and that he exercised substantial discretion. It nevertheless considered the absence of any power to enter a final FDIC decision decisive. It rejected the agency's preliminary suggestion that lack of demonstrated prejudice foreclosed consideration of the structural objection. The separate opinion rejected the finality distinction, classified the ALJ as an inferior officer, accepted waiver of the valid-appointment defense, and would apply harmless-error principles because the Board independently reviewed the case and the evidence supported its order.

**Landry's Strongest Position:** A government adjudicator exercises public authority when he conducts the required hearing, compels compliance, decides what evidence enters the record, and makes findings that frame later review. Final decisionmaking is unnecessary to officer status; many inferior officers operate under superior supervision. If those functions require a valid appointment, review of their product cannot make the appointment requirement disappear in every such proceeding. A claimant who timely objected should receive a lawful hearing without proving how a hypothetical properly appointed officer would have ruled.

**FDIC's Strongest Position:** The Board remains legally responsible for the order, exercises independent factfinding power, and can reject the recommendation or reopen the record. The ALJ prepares a record and recommendation without final coercive authority. Alternatively, the Clause can be enforced by declaring officer status while preserving a substantively sound order independently entered by lawful Board members. Repeating a lengthy hearing where the critical proof comes from Landry's own admissions would impose cost without correcting a demonstrated decisional error.

**General Rules Requested and Necessary Concessions:** Landry seeks officer status for continuing statutory adjudicators with significant discretionary hearing powers and a fresh hearing on timely challenge. He must concede that ordinary staff advice, ministerial processing, and every temporary assistance arrangement do not necessarily constitute offices. The FDIC seeks a final-authority limitation or, alternatively, a meaningful de novo cure rule. It must concede that assignment cannot replace constitutionally required appointment and that nominal review is not automatically an adequate substitute for a lawful evidentiary proceeding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide classification first. Employee status would permit affirmance without resolving cure. Officer status requires addressing appointment validity, including the abandoned defense, before reaching the preserved cure argument. No determination of the bank misconduct merits is necessary to decide either granted question.

**Available Merits Paths:** The Court could affirm on employee status; classify the ALJ as an officer but affirm under a bounded de novo cure rule; or reject cure and require a fresh lawful hearing. A remand limited to an unresolved appointment fact is possible only if a genuine, preserved validity question remains, rather than an invented defense the agency relinquished.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** A new-hearing disposition vacates the judgment sustaining the order and returns the matter for proceedings before a validly appointed adjudicator and a fresh Board decision. It does not dismiss the enforcement case, exonerate Landry, resolve unrelated privilege issues, or reopen other parties' final orders. The agency may pursue the same charges through lawful procedures.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** This continuing statutory hearing office, this timely direct challenge, and Board review of the existing record. An actual new evidentiary hearing before a lawful decisionmaker is analytically different from reconsideration of a record created through the challenged proceeding.

**Material Uncertainty:** The original appointment instrument and complete administrative record are unavailable. The appellate account establishes the agency's litigation position and the functions exercised. Do not invent a department-head appointment, an unraised objection, or a finding that no procedural choice could have influenced the record.

# Saucier v. Katz

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Saucier v. Katz. **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1977; 533 U.S. 194. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of an interlocutory qualified-immunity ruling in a federal-officer excessive-force action. **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** The petition presents two questions: whether the Fourth Amendment excessive-force reasonableness test and qualified-immunity test are identical, so that unreasonable force necessarily defeats immunity; and whether Saucier should receive immunity for the carrying and shove described in the petition on the facts available at this stage, including the asserted absence of injury or pain. This paraphrases the verified petition questions without adopting the petitioner's factual characterization as a finding.

**Question Granted:** Both petition questions are verified in the Government's petition and correspond to the reviewed legal distinction and its application to Saucier. No separate grant-order reformulation has been verified. [Petition for certiorari](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/saucier-v-katz-petition).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of qualified immunity to Saucier, reasoning that under Graham v. Connor the reasonableness issues substantially merged. **Relief Requested:** Saucier seeks reversal and judgment on immunity; Katz seeks preservation of his damages action and factual adjudication.

**Material Record:** At a 1994 ceremony concerning transfer of the Presidio to national-park use, Vice President Gore was present. Katz, an animal-rights demonstrator, carried a concealed banner and moved toward a barrier before opening it. Military police officers Saucier and Parker removed him, holding his arms from behind. Katz described being partly dragged with his feet barely reaching the ground; he wore a leg brace that Saucier did not recall seeing. Katz alleges a shove or throw into a van and disputed resistance; Saucier's personal role and the degree and necessity of force must be assessed on supported facts. Katz caught himself and suffered no physical injury. He was briefly detained and released. The district court dismissed other claims or defendants but retained the excessive-force claim against Saucier because material facts were disputed. [Saucier v. Katz](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-194.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Interlocutory review may decide the legal distinction between immunity and the merits on facts assumed for Katz. It cannot resolve whose version of resistance, knowledge, or force is true. A defendant's lack of personal participation is distinct from a defense excusing a proved violation. The continued availability of the existing federal-officer remedial channel is not independently challenged in the described grant. **Relevant Dates:** The alleged conduct occurred in 1994, fixing the immunity notice date; argument March 20, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** Any intervening controlling Fourth Amendment decision must be applied. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Denius v. Dunlap and Sterling v. Borough of Minersville concern personal immunity in distinct constitutional settings; their assigned dates precede this event, but no controlling disposition is identified. Illinois v. Spencer concerns municipal rather than personal liability. Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated into the immunity and substantive-force analyses.

### Issue 1: Fourth Amendment force and personal immunity

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Plakas v. Drinski applies Graham v. Connor to the complete supported circumstances, including actual threats and reasonable factual perceptions, without requiring the least dangerous possible tactic. Hunter v. Bryant requires lawful summary-judgment treatment of favorable supported facts and rejects choosing a more favorable competing account for the officer. Elder v. Holloway requires consideration of all relevant pre-existing law when deciding clearly established rights. Wilson v. Layne distinguishes constitutional violation from conduct-date qualified immunity. These holdings retain the Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton notice framework; they do not replace it with a reliance-only defense.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The collateral-appeal standard permits separable legal review on assumed facts, not reconsideration of factual sufficiency. The operative immunity standard asks whether the particular unlawfulness was sufficiently established, without an identical-case requirement or a mechanical count of precedents.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment governs force during the seizure. The existing federal-officer damages channel and ordinary proof of personal responsibility, causation, and loss remain distinct from immunity.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Lanier addresses criminal fair warning with particularized clarity, possible obvious applications of general law, and distinct criminal elements. It does not authorize importing criminal willfulness into a civil excessive-force claim. Conn v. Gabbert does not establish a mandatory merits-first sequence for every immunity appeal.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The substantive rule already accommodates reasonable factual mistakes. Existing immunity nevertheless protects certain reasonable legal mistakes; the Ninth Circuit cannot erase that separate inquiry by equating two uses of reasonableness. No controlling precedent prescribes a categorical sequence governing all cases.

**Open Point:** Application to the assumed facts, the appropriate decisional sequence here, and whether existing personal-immunity doctrine should be retained or expressly revised remain for decision. Any intervening controlling rule must be incorporated before adjudication.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Saucier's Position:** Security surrounding the Vice President, Katz's movement and banner, and the rapid removal required objectively reasonable action. Even if force crossed the constitutional line, existing law did not sufficiently notify this officer that the specific conduct was forbidden. The appeals court collapsed a separate legal protection into the merits.

**Katz's Position:** A supported account of unnecessary force against a restrained, physically limited demonstrator can establish a violation under settled law; public security does not authorize every method of removal. Factual disputes about resistance and the shove prevent final judgment. A broadly worded immunity claim must not permit judicial credibility selection.

**Reasoning Below and Contested Premises:** The Ninth Circuit treated general force doctrine as largely disposing of both inquiries. That legal approach can be reviewed independently of its recognition of factual disputes. Rejecting merger does not itself establish that Saucier actually used reasonable force or that the particular unlawfulness was uncertain in 1994.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Available Merits Paths:** Reject merger and grant immunity if even Katz's supported account lacked sufficiently established unlawfulness; reject merger but remand application under the correct distinct standard; resolve that the assumed conduct was constitutionally permissible, making immunity unnecessary; or openly revise the defense and provide a fair procedure for its application. The Court may not resolve disputed resistance or participation as a shortcut to any result.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** A legal immunity judgment bars the covered individual damages claim without validating all challenged conduct or deciding unrelated relief. A remand leaves liability, causation, and damages to their lawful factfinder. Absence of physical injury is relevant to context and damages but is not an automatic constitutional license. A new defense or revised sequence must be identified as a change, with its temporal and procedural consequences stated.

# United States v. Mead Corp.

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Mead Corp. **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1434; 533 U.S. 218. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the degree of judicial respect due a Customs tariff-classification letter. **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** The petition presents two questions: (1) whether courts should defer to Customs tariff-classification interpretations in administrative ruling letters; and (2) whether the provision for bound diaries, notebooks, and address books reasonably includes spiral- and ring-bound day planners. **Question Granted:** The petition questions are verified in the Government's petition; this is a paraphrase, not a verbatim quotation. The judicial record addresses controlling and persuasive interpretive weight and the disputed classification. No separate grant-order reformulation has been verified. [Petition for certiorari](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/united-states-v-mead-petition-writ-certiorari).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit reversed the Court of International Trade's summary judgment for the United States. The appellate court rejected both controlling deference and persuasive weight for the ruling letter and determined that Mead's imported day planners did not fall within the agency's chosen dutiable classification. **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of that treatment and recognition of the agency's classification authority. Mead seeks preservation of the appellate judgment. [United States v. Mead Corp.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-218.pdf).

**Material Record:** Mead imported loose-leaf day planners consisting of three-ring binders with calendars and pages for notes, telephone numbers, and similar information. Customs had treated the goods as duty-free articles under a residual tariff subheading. In January 1993, Customs headquarters changed the classification to bound diaries subject to a four-percent duty. After Mead protested, headquarters issued an unpublished explanatory letter relying on dictionary meaning, commercial usage, and a broad understanding of binding, including ring mechanisms. The record therefore concerns more than a bare unexplained conclusion, but also more than a longstanding unchanged interpretation.

The statutory scheme gives Customs classification and duty-assessment responsibilities and gives the Secretary rulemaking powers. Ruling letters are issued by headquarters and numerous port offices in large numbers. Under the relevant regulatory practice they guide the specified transaction and Customs officials, can be modified or revoked, and do not bind unrelated importers as a generally applicable regulation would. The relationship between operational binding effect and delegated lawmaking effect is disputed. Later statutory notice provisions for modifying certain rulings were enacted after the classification at issue; their chronology matters.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is an authorized tariff protest and judicial-review dispute. No standing, exhaustion, or mootness defect is identified in the record. The Court must distinguish interpretation of tariff language, allocation of delegated authority, evidentiary presumptions, and the reviewing court's independent factfinding. An entitlement to consideration of agency reasoning would not itself establish the correct tariff classification or a refund amount.

**Relevant Dates:** January 1993 classification change; the Federal Circuit judgment preceded the grant; argument November 8, 2000. **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. concerns a regulation and is a prior authority, not a separately briefed companion. Appalachian Power Company v. Environmental Protection Agency may affect treatment of operationally binding agency guidance if decided before this case. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Appalachian Power Company v. Environmental Protection Agency concerns the legal consequences of agency guidance; any operative holding must be applied within its scope. Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation concerns appointments and does not itself resolve tariff classification or interpretive deference.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** Statutory Customs protest review permits judicial determination of the import classification. The actual delegation and type of agency action must be identified before a standard of judicial respect is selected.

### Issue 1: Binding interpretive authority and persuasive weight

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. requires courts to identify actual delegated authority, the statute's meaning, and the regulation's fit before accepting a reasonable agency choice within a genuine statutory gap. Its rule concerned a formally promulgated Customs regulation and expressly left informal ruling letters unresolved. Christensen v. Harris County denies controlling treatment under Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. to the nonbinding advisory letter there, while preserving consideration of thoroughness, valid reasoning, experience, and consistency under Skidmore v. Swift & Co. It does not decide that every informal action has the same legal status.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Christensen v. Harris County require identification of delegated authority before determining interpretive weight. Format alone neither creates delegated power nor establishes that no persuasive agency expertise exists. A statutory command or unambiguous regulation cannot be amended by interpretive assertion.

**Other Governing Law:** The Harmonized Tariff Schedule's relevant headings, the Customs classification and rulemaking provisions, and the then-operative ruling-letter regulations govern the actual source and effect of the decision. The later change to the statutory procedures for modifying established rulings cannot silently be treated as governing this 1993 transaction. [United States v. Mead Corp.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-218.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. and Skidmore v. Swift & Co. perform the distinct functions recognized in United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Christensen v. Harris County. The former's delegated-choice rule and the latter's persuasive-reasoning inquiry cannot be collapsed into an all-or-nothing account of judicial consideration.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** A tariff regulation receives the treatment specified in United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. when its statutory predicates are met. A nonbinding advisory letter receives no controlling deference merely because an administering agency issued it. Neither decision makes the classification letter here a regulation or determines the tariff meaning.

**Open Point:** Whether this letter exercises congressionally conferred authority to make a binding legal determination, and what weight its actual reasoning merits, remain to be decided. This requires construction of the Customs scheme rather than merely choosing a label for an agency document.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Federal Circuit treated ruling letters as different from regulations because they lacked public procedures and equivalent general legal force. It also rejected persuasive deference, reasoning that Customs' expertise did not justify displacing the court's tariff reading. The Court of International Trade had adopted the classification reasoning without a developed explanation of the deference issue.

**United States' Position:** Congress assigned classification to Customs, and a headquarters interpretation exercising that responsibility should receive controlling deference if reasonable. Repetitive administration of a complex national tariff scheme supports uniformity and agency expertise. Alternatively, even if the letter is not controlling, its explanation should receive reasoned consideration.

**Mead's Position:** The power to classify particular imports does not make every classification letter a lawmaking act binding a reviewing court. The letter's limited reach, revocability, lack of public process, and departure from earlier treatment support independent interpretation. The planner's removable pages also resist the chosen ordinary meaning of bound diary.

**Limiting Principles and Alternative Grounds:** The Government's view requires a legally grounded delegation, not merely expertise or administrative convenience. Mead's objection to binding force does not answer whether the agency offers useful reasons. The Court can resolve the standard and remand application without selecting the tariff category itself. No independent constitutional invalidity claim is needed to decide these questions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Paths:** Address the preserved standard-of-review dispute within the tariff appeal. If the letter's legal character cannot be determined from the identified scheme, remand for an assessment of the letter’s statutory and regulatory authority rather than presuming authority from its issuance.

**Merits Paths and Plausible Holding Scope:** A finding that Congress delegated controlling classification authority through this mechanism could require acceptance of a reasonable interpretation within the statute's actual limits. A contrary finding could deny controlling deference while requiring examination of the letter's persuasive reasoning. A conclusion that the text independently settles the classification could render any permissible difference in interpretive weight immaterial. The record supports a rule about Customs ruling letters of this legal character; treating every informal agency statement identically would require additional statutory analysis beyond the facts of this classification.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** Reversing or vacating because the appellate court disregarded permissible persuasive weight would require renewed classification analysis, not automatic judgment for Customs. Affirming the tariff reading would preserve Mead's prevailing judgment, subject to the actual assessment and refund procedures. Upholding binding deference would still require a lawful delegation, statutory fit, and a reasonable classification. No nationwide regulation or retrospective monetary amount is established by choosing the review standard.

**Uncertainty:** The petition's questions are verified, but the complete administrative file is unavailable. The agency's demonstrated reasoning, consistency, and transaction-specific legal consequences must be assessed as they existed when the letter issued.
