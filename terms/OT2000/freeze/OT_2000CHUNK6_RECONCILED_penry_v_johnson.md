## Historical differences and effect discipline

The actual Graham and Johnson holdings strengthen the six Justices' available pre-1995 rule while requiring Scalia and Thomas to distinguish binding effective-vehicle law rather than invoke the historical decisions' more restrictive premise. Their specific-permission distinction has support in their own Buchanan and Weeks applications and in the historical Penry dissent's treatment of the complete instructions. No changed judgment vote is proposed, and no new join is inferred from authorship.

**Historical departure:** The present psychiatric disposition does not adopt the historical unanimous III-A's additional general petitioner-burden Brecht premise or its prediction about effect. Actual Brecht, O'Neal, Roy and Coleman establish defined categories rather than that universal premise; O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Coleman/Weeks positions support a confined Chapman extension, Stevens and Kennedy's own writings require substantial influence with grave doubt against harmlessness, and Scalia and Thomas require petitioner-proved influence outside binding Chapman categories. These concrete prior positions explain the retained separation, while the common psychiatric statutory ground makes an effect ruling unnecessary. The historical mitigation opinion supplies no separate effect-standard alignment to import.

Coleman's State-borne Chapman holding concerns commutation misinformation. Weeks leaves its response-error standard, burden and uncertainty rule unresolved. Neither already classifies this original-charge mechanism. Four present Associates favor Chapman; Stevens and Kennedy favor demanding substantial influence with grave doubt; Scalia and Thomas conditionally favor petitioner-proved substantial injurious influence with a different treatment of uncertainty. The latter four do not share one complete standard. The six finding error share the corrective judgment, not all its proposed effect methodology. No arithmetic or narrowest-ground synthesis establishes a Court standard from these commitments.
