## Resolution and two paths

All eight provisionally affirm the Third Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal without prejudice for nonexhaustion. Amended section 1997e(a) requires completion of an available grievance process capable of taking responsive action concerning the complaint even when that process cannot award the money damages the prisoner now seeks. A claimant's election to request only money in court does not make such an otherwise available process unavailable. The decision does not hold that a wholly powerless, nonexistent or actually inaccessible procedure is available.

The statutory object is available administrative remedies in the context of exhausting them, which identifies the administrative process, not a guarantee that it provides each judicial remedy requested. Congress replaced the old discretionary arrangement and removed the adequate-and-effective qualification. Investigation and corrective action remain possible here even without compensation. The former McCarthy result rested on no express congressional mandate and a particular balance of remedial capacity, delay and procedural usefulness. Its statutory-primacy rule now requires reading the new command on its own terms; its previous damages-only exception does not survive as an automatic exception to that command.

The competing reversal path reads available remedy as relief capable of redressing the particular demand. Its strongest case is a completed injury for which the prisoner wants compensation and no administrative officer may award it: mandatory exhaustion imposes time without giving that relief. McCarthy's unanimous prior result and Scalia's no-money concurrence support the seriousness of this objection. The new text nevertheless does not retain the old adequacy criterion, and the process can respond to the complaint in a way Congress could require before suit. The claimant cannot defeat that requirement solely by limiting the prayer. This is a legal reading of the changed enactment, not an assertion that Congress repealed every part of McCarthy or that all administrative procedures are useful.

No factual remand is needed on the presented availability issue: an accessible multilevel process capable of responsive action is the supported premise, and Booth did not complete it. His allegations of cover-up and retaliation are not adjudicated obstruction of those steps. If an obstruction claim were actually established or presented as an unresolved review premise, its different availability question would require separate treatment; no such premise is adopted here.

## Scope and consequent procedure

The reviewed question is exhaustion despite absence of damages authority. It does not newly decide whether every excessive-force action falls within prison conditions, define all forms of unavailability, decide procedural default or a universal proper-exhaustion rule, or adjudicate force, medical neglect, immunity or damages. Section 1997e(c)(2)'s permission to dismiss specified defective claims without requiring exhaustion is preserved and does not become a right to nonexhausted merits consideration.

Affirmance leaves dismissal without prejudice effective. It orders no damages, administrative award, prospective prison reform or new judicial supervision. All eight can join a single bounded explanation; no separate opinion is required. The neutral legal and factual premises support complete commitments, with no merits blocker.
