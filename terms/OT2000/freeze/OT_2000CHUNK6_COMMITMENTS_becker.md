## Components and sufficient ground

Reverse the Sixth Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal and remand to permit prompt signature correction and continue appellate review. An otherwise sufficient notice actually filed within the appeal period does not lose its jurisdictional effect solely because its signature was omitted. Civil Rule 11(a) applies to this paper filed in district court, and its signature requirement includes its express opportunity to cure promptly after notification. The correction may occur after the appeal deadline; it perfects the existing timely notice rather than creating a late appeal. The ordinary paper notice requires the party's own signature; the typed name alone does not establish compliance. Authorized electronic signing under a valid local rule remains distinct and is not decided by this paper filing.

Rule 3(a) protects validity after timely notice despite other omitted steps; Rule 3(c) supplies the identifying contents and protects informal form or title. Rule 1(a)(2), Civil Rule 5(e) and Rule 11(a) provide the applicable district-filing framework. The same rules cannot be combined to impose a signature obligation while deleting the remedy they expressly attach to its omission. The statutory thirty-day period remains binding. Smith v. Barry supports attention to objective timely notice without treating every defect as jurisdictional; it does not eliminate any required identifying information.

The opposing path would treat the signature as part of the timely jurisdictional act and require affirmance once the thirty days passed. Its strongest point is that a notice must be the litigant's act, not merely an unidentified paper. The stipulated notice already identifies Becker and his intended appeal; the governing rule deals expressly with authenticating an unsigned paper after notice. That specific mechanism defeats the proposed incurability. A second possible route, treating his typed name or later signed brief as already satisfying every requirement, is unnecessary and is not adopted: the original notice is timely, the proper cure is available, and no universal equivalence between a typed name and a paper signature is established.

## Remedy, limits and readiness

All eight support the same disposition and operative grounds. They permit correction through signing the notice on file or a signed duplicate; they do not require recharacterizing the later brief as a timely original notice. Becker acted to correct after first notice; no earlier missed warning is supplied. Promptness remains a real condition. They decide no prison-conditions merits, PLRA exhaustion, pleading defect, damages, extraordinary time extension or hypothetical electronic-filing question. No separate-writing function is necessary to preserve a material disagreement.
