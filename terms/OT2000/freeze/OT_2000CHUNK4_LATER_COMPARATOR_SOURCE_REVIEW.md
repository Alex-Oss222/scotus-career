This is a comparator/control artifact. It is excluded from neutral preflight and independent modeling, and contains no Stone supplement. It establishes historical comparison material only; no post-divergence Supreme Court result is imported into current law.

## Historical results and material distinctions

| Matter | Historical result and writings | Material comparator qualifications |
|---|---|---|
| Texas v. Cobb | Reversed, 5–4. Rehnquist for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; Kennedy concurrence joined Scalia and Thomas; Breyer dissent joined Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. | The majority applies the same-elements definition to the represented burglary and the multiple-murder charge. It does not decide the separately granted unilateral-waiver question. Kennedy questions Jackson and proposes an unambiguous-invocation limitation while joining the Court fully. Breyer treats closely intertwined conduct as within the represented offense and rejects unilateral waiver on this record. |
| Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers | Reversed unanimously; Rehnquist, no separate writing. | State action and the asserted contract-claim property interest are assumed. Ordinary judicial contract process suffices on the supported availability premise. Assignment is one route; the direct contractor action is an alternative whose final state-law construction belongs to California courts. July 1, 2001 replacement procedures are not yet operative. |
| United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co. | Reversed unanimously in judgment. Ginsburg for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; Scalia concurs only in judgment. | Main opinion defers to the longstanding reasonable construction of the regulations; it reserves independent deference to revenue rulings. Nierotko addresses benefits allocation. Scalia regards stipulated wage damages as a gap the agency may reasonably fill and refuses the majority's asymmetry reasoning. The refund suit seeks employer taxes only. |
| Easley v. Cromartie / Smallwood v. Cromartie | Both appeals reversed, 5–4. Breyer for Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; Thomas dissent joined Rehnquist, Scalia and Kennedy. | Historical merits turn on predominance and clear-error review. Thomas defends trial deference and rejects the additional alternative-map burden. The actual entering-law rejection of a predominance-only cause changes the question; neither historical position decides whether a separate injury was preserved under the actual Hunt mandate. |
| Shaw v. Murphy | Reversed and remanded unanimously; Thomas for all; Ginsburg separately concurs. | No enhancement of Turner merely for inmate legal advice. Ordinary as-applied Turner assessment remains. Ginsburg preserves separately pleaded vagueness and overbreadth issues, including the rule's intent/direction terms and the uncontradicted affidavit. The historical opinion does not remove ordinary First Amendment protection or convert access-to-courts doctrine into a free-standing speech bonus. |
| Clark County School District v. Breeden | Certiorari granted and judgment reversed by per curiam opinion; no numerical vote or Justice lineup published. | Two distinct retaliation theories fail on their own records. Reasonable-belief opposition is assumed without deciding its general scope. Actual notice is not established by the ten-day legal notice command. The right-to-sue letter was introduced only in appellate reply. No tally may be inferred from the absence of noted dissents. |
