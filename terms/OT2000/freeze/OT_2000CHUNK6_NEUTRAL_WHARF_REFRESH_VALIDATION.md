## Entering law and decisional paths

The slice preserves Central Bank's distinct affirmative-assistance rule and O'Hagan's transaction-connected deception rule. It also contains Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith v. Kravitz, November 12, 1998: a unanimous holding that knowingly accepting a securities undertaking with an intention to disregard it may be primary fraud, subject to materiality and every independent private-action element, while negligence, inferior performance, status and breach alone do not suffice. Kravitz concerns best execution, not the status of an oral option; its relevance is the difference between an initially false undertaking and later nonperformance.

Affirmance remains a supported path because an actual acquired option and contemporaneous fraud are accepted, while the state-law objection has already been resolved outside the grant.
