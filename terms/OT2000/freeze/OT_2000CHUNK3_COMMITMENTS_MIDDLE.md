## Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield

**Event and reach:** Merits, February 27, 2001; review of 203 F.3d 487, No. 99-1639, within the granted divestiture-and-continuing-attribution question. All eight accept review of continuing municipal conduct. A genuine sale does not moot that claim. Attribution, establishment, and the permissible scope of corrective relief remain distinct.

### Entering law and operative distinctions

Agostini, Holding I, supplies the unified Lemon inquiry: a controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine governs at its actual scope; an established permissible relationship governs at its actual scope; otherwise attribution and function resolve legally sufficient allegations under ordinary procedure. Purpose, effects, and institutional relationships are evidence, not independent fallback prongs. Agostini reserved monuments, so this application requires judgment rather than a claim that it already decided the parcel.

Pinette protects its actual private-access proposal and rejects automatic attribution from location; Chabad protects genuine comparable access and supplies its contextual private-attribution rule. Neither establishes a forum here. The supplied park was historically centered on the statue, rather than organized as a generally available venue for competing private speakers. Robinson distinguishes private origin from actual civic adoption, but its unanimous permanent-civil-identifier prohibition is a seal holding, not a rule that every park monument is a seal. Brentwood requires attention to actual public operation despite private form; its public-school governance finding is not a fact about this Fund. Mitchell preserves proof of the governmental function and correction at the proved scope, without converting possible misuse or religious identity into proof.

The sale is genuine. The continuing public-use covenant, lack of intelligible parcel boundaries, the municipal park's centered presentation, and uncertainty created by the disclaimer are also established. These are the relevant combined facts. Neither the bare deed nor a passerby's unsupported mistake is conclusive. No supplied fact establishes a sham purchaser, a current municipal expenditure on the private statue, a Fund-directed municipal program, an installed effective new sign, or a uniquely necessary fence.

### Supported paths and strongest objections

**Path M-A — affirm the need for corrective separation.** The City continues the integrated public-park presentation of the religious centerpiece while retaining public use of its undifferentiated site. The combination, rather than private ownership or religious content alone, supports continuing municipal sponsorship under Agostini. The judgment vacating City/Fund summary judgment and requiring corrective proceedings can be affirmed on that ground. The strongest objection is that public pedestrian access and nearby municipal land do not ordinarily turn a private owner into government. This path answers that objection through the City's continuing presentation and dedication of this particular centered park, without deeming all public-access private land governmental. Relief addresses the City's role, not the Fund's right to express a religious message on genuinely separate private property.

**Path M-B — vacate and return for the correct attribution inquiry.** A real conveyance defeats the inference that historical municipal sponsorship necessarily continues. The covenant can preserve public access without preserving municipal authorship; boundaries and a disclaimer can concern viewers' information rather than control. The lower court's independent endorsement analysis does not establish the actual current governmental adoption required by Agostini. The strongest objection is that an invisible division can preserve the very park function the City previously performed. This path therefore does not direct judgment for the City or find that all governmental responsibility ended. It returns the actual retained function and remedy for decision under the correct rule, on the existing record where sufficient.

**Path M-C — direct judgment that only private expression remains.** Private-access precedents support this result when actual independent control and cessation of the challenged municipal function are established. Its strongest objection here is that genuine conveyance does not alone resolve the covenant's operation or the integrated public presentation. No Associate commits to unconditional City/Fund victory on a newly assumed complete relinquishment of control. Path M-B supplies a lawful bounded disposition for those who reject the present liability inference.

## Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.

**Event and reach:** Merits, February 27, 2001, No. 99-1551. All eight reach the source and scope of the external preclusion rule. They do not entertain a collateral attack on the California federal judgment's validity or finality. Section 1738 concerns state judicial proceedings and does not itself prescribe the effect of this federal judgment.

### Components and supported paths

**S1 — the source of external effect.** Federal law governs the effect of the federal judicial act. That conclusion does not require a single nationwide substantive preclusion rule for every diversity dismissal. O'Melveny and Atherton reject general uniformity as sufficient justification for displacing state-created rights and preserve exceptional federal fields. Federal judgment effect is a genuine federal subject; choosing its content still requires attention to Erie and the particular interest. Baker's distinction between adjudicative effect and additional consequences reinforces the need to identify the effect actually sought rather than treating every final judgment label as an answer.

**S2 — the content of the rule.** The supported default is federal common law incorporating the preclusion law that the courts of the State in which the rendering diversity court sits would apply. An identified incompatibility with a significant federal interest in the federal adjudicative function can require a different federal rule. The exception is not automatic whenever a federal tribunal acted, whenever finality is desirable, or whenever a federal party prefers broader preclusion.

**S3 — Rule 41.** Rule 41(b)'s default merits operation, subject to the order's contrary specification and the jurisdiction, improper-venue, and Rule 19 nonjoinder exceptions, does not itself command nationwide claim preclusion. Read with Rule 41(a)'s without-prejudice structure, it addresses the effect of dismissal in the rendering action and forecloses treating the ordinary merits dismissal as an automatic right to refile the same claim in that court. External preclusion remains governed by the appropriate law. Section 2072(b)'s prohibition on abridging, enlarging, or modifying substantive rights strongly opposes treating the procedural rule as an unexpressed nationwide extinction of state claims.

**S4 — disposition.** Reverse the Maryland Court of Special Appeals' automatic-preclusion judgment and remand for application of the appropriate California-derived rule and any properly demonstrated incompatible federal interest. Do not decide California preclusion law without the missing lower-court determination, vacate the California judgment, guarantee that the Maryland suit survives, or authorize renewed litigation in the rendering court.

**Alternative path S-U — uniform external preclusion.** Its strongest support is the federal court's authority over its own final merits judgment and the need to prevent relitigation from defeating that act. Its strongest objection is that a limitations dismissal of a state claim may have different external consequences under the rendering State's law, and removal should not enlarge those consequences solely through a procedural label. The actual federal interest supports a federal source and a concrete-conflict exception; it does not establish the need for this blanket nationwide rule. Rule 41's context and §2072 make the proposed automatic rule particularly difficult.

**Alternative path S-D — state law directly governs with no federal-law role.** Its strongest support is Erie and the state origin of the underlying claims. Its strongest objection is that the act whose effect is being prescribed is a federal judgment and §1738 does not directly govern it. Borrowing the rendering State's rule as federal law respects both interests. None of the eight adopts either S-U or S-D as the sufficient ground.

## Whitman v. American Trucking Associations / American Trucking Associations v. Whitman

**Event and reach:** Consolidated merits, February 27, 2001, Nos. 99-1257 and 99-1426. Four distinct components remain separate. No later Appalachian Power or Mead rule enters. The standard-setting dispute, delegation dispute, implementation reviewability, and implementation construction do not amount to a single up-or-down approval of EPA.

### W1 — Implementation costs in national health-standard setting

**Present common rule:** Section 109(b)(1) directs EPA to set the primary ambient standard requisite to protect public health, based on §108's scientific criteria and allowing an adequate margin of safety. EPA may not adjust that standard by balancing the costs of attainment against the health protection Congress specified. The statutory judgment remains about the degree of health protection and safety margin supported by the criteria; it is not a judicially imposed zero-risk concentration or an instruction to ignore the distinction between primary health and secondary welfare standards.

Section 109(d)(2)(C)(iv) separately directs the scientific committee to advise about adverse public-health, welfare, social, economic, or energy effects of strategies for attaining and maintaining standards. That advice is real statutory work. It is not permission to replace §109(b)'s criteria with implementation-cost balancing. Section 109(d)'s review and revision duty returns the agency to §108 and §109(b). Union Electric supplies the received standards/implementation separation. MCI, Brown & Williamson, and National Mining require each connected statutory command to do its own work; they establish no general hostility toward significant regulation.

**Alternative W1-C:** Read requisite protection to allow cost moderation, supported by the practical consequences of compliance and the express economic-effects advice. Its strongest objection is the difference between advice about attainment strategies and the enacted primary-standard criterion. The chosen health rule need not demand elimination of every conceivable risk; bounded scientific judgment supplies meaning without inserting a separate cost criterion. The counter to the no-cost path is that costs can have health consequences and extreme compliance burdens. The statutory allocation still controls: supported health effects within the enacted health inquiry remain analytically different from comparing dollars with health protection, and implementation provisions perform their assigned work. No new indirect-cost exception is adopted.

**Judgment:** Affirm rejection of cost consideration in No. 99-1426. Do not choose a concentration or find that every scientific rationale supporting the 1997 standards is valid.

### W2 — Congressional guidance and the impermissible agency-created cure

**Present common rule:** Congress supplied adequate guidance by identifying the pollutants and scientific criteria, the protected public-health objective, the necessary degree of protection, and an adequate safety margin. The resulting technical judgment is substantial but bounded. The governing intelligible-principle authorities do not require Congress to legislate a numerical concentration for every pollutant. The delegation challenge therefore does not invalidate §109 or the standards on this ground.

Loving's eight-Justice rationale preserves congressional policy and legal boundaries, while rejecting executive safeguards as a cure for missing legislative authorization. Its capital and military setting is not a universal checklist. Hampton, Mistretta, and Touby support applying the ordinary guidance inquiry at their actual scopes. The distinct agency-cure proposition follows independently: if Congress had delegated genuinely standardless authority, EPA's selection of its own preferred limit could not retroactively supply Congress's missing legislative decision. The lower court cannot sustain that cure merely by requesting a better agency formula.

**Alternative W2-N:** Because health risks may exist across concentrations, the statutory terms leave an intolerable range of legislative choice. Its strongest support is the absence of a risk-free point and the consequential nature of the choice. Its strongest objection is that the Constitution permits technical judgment under a defined objective, criteria, and margin; nondelegation does not require a unique scientific answer. The opposing concern for the adequate-guidance path is that naming a good purpose alone can disguise unlimited power. Here the pollutant/criteria/health/safety-margin combination, not purpose alone, supplies the boundary. None of the eight adopts a general declaration that every broad public-interest formula is sufficient.

**Judgment:** Reverse the delegation ruling in No. 99-1257 and remove the delegation-based agency-cure remand. Ordinary scientific, record-based, and statutory challenges retain their own scope; this judgment does not approve every EPA exercise.

### W3 — Finality and ripeness of the implementation determination

**Present common rule:** The settled published determination selecting the legal framework for the revised ozone standard is reviewable under §307(b)'s route for nationally applicable final action. Its final resolution of which statutory implementation constraints apply is the relevant legal consequence. Practical planning pressure alone would be insufficient. Continued choices about individual plans do not reopen that completed statutory determination.

Bennett supports distinguishing completed consequential action from tentative advice; its incidental-take liability mechanism is not imported into this statute. Section 307(b) supplies the actual review channel and preserves finality despite a reconsideration petition without extending the review period or postponing effect. It also restricts later enforcement-stage review of action reviewable through the direct route. No new holding on an absent timeliness dispute is made. The delay of review of particular classifications under §172(a)(1)(B), incorporated by §181(a)(3), does not turn this different completed determination of the governing statutory framework into an unreviewable individual-classification challenge.

**Alternative W3-P:** Dismiss implementation review as premature because no final individual implementation plan is before the Court. Ohio Forestry gives that path real force where additional decisions authorize the actual conduct and meaningful later review remains without sufficient present legal hardship. Its strongest objection here is that EPA has finally resolved a separable statutory position associated with the final standard; later plan details do not determine whether the ozone-specific subpart may be discarded. The pure legal compatibility issue is fit for decision, and §307's review structure reinforces timely review. Conversely, finality cannot be inferred from publication or expense alone; the present commitment depends on the packet's completed statutory determination, not a generic prediction about future plans.

**Judgment:** Affirm immediate reviewability of this implementation determination. Reach W4. Reviewability supplies no merits approval or independent statewide implementation injunction.

### W4 — General and ozone-specific implementation provisions

**Present common rule:** EPA cannot treat the revised ozone standard as sufficient reason to remove applicable Subpart 2 constraints and rely exclusively on the more general Subpart 1 scheme. Section 172(a)(1)(C) preserves specifically provided classifications, and §172(a)(2)(D) preserves specifically provided attainment dates. Section 181 supplies ozone-specific classifications, its design-value methodology and table, bounded adjustments and extensions, and rules for later designations. Section 182 begins the corresponding supplemental-plan obligations. Those enacted constraints cannot be made optional merely because the revised standard creates a difficult fit.

The real fit problem matters. Section 181's table uses the earlier design-value methodology and specified concentrations and dates; its existence does not answer every application to the revised standard. But §181(b)(1) expressly addresses later nonattainment designations and adjusts absolute dates, defeating a simple claim that the whole specific subpart necessarily expires with the original designations. Section 172(e)'s anti-backsliding command concerns relaxation of a standard; its conditional trigger cannot be rewritten as a general exemption from Subpart 2 when a standard is revised. The general classification/attainment provisions and the ozone provisions must retain their actual exceptions, permissions, and duties.

MCI forbids removing an obligation's essential operation through bounded adjustment authority; Brown & Williamson and National Mining require the connected commands to retain their force. Chevron leaves room for a reasonable agency reconciliation of genuine gaps but cannot authorize the asserted wholesale displacement. Christensen preserves the separate legal-force inquiry without creating a blanket rule against every informal statement. Solid Waste Agency confirms that the statutory source must support the asserted reach, while Lopez permits reasoned categories inside actual delegated discretion; neither supplies authority to discard an applicable provision.

**Alternative W4-G:** Use only the general subpart because the old one-hour classifications cannot be transposed mechanically onto the revised standard. Its strongest support is the mismatch in design values, original dates, and changed standard. Its strongest objection is that Congress expressly subordinated the general provisions where classifications or dates are specifically supplied and addressed new designations in §181 itself. An actual gap may call for reconciliation, not erasure. The counter to rejecting EPA's interpretation is that courts might impose an unworkable old regime. The present rule does not prescribe a mechanical transposition or a replacement plan; it returns that lawful reconciliation to EPA with the specific constraints preserved.

**Judgment:** Uphold the rejection of the asserted displacement interpretation and remand for EPA to formulate a lawful reconciliation. Do not endorse every lower-court implementation statement, direct a new concentration, select a plan, classify a particular area, or resolve every §182 application absent a concrete issue and its necessary record. This is a merits legal remand under a stated rule, not an indefinite reservation of whether Subpart 2 can simply be discarded.

## Cook v. Gralike

**Event and reach:** Merits, February 28, 2001, No. 99-929. All eight reach the challenge to the official adverse congressional-ballot labels and support affirming the injunction at that scope. ### Alternative grounds and adversarial comparison

**C-A — Elections Clause authority.** The delegated power over the manner of congressional elections permits genuine election administration, not use of the official ballot to impose an adverse political consequence for declining the State's prescribed federal legislative program. The labels' compliance trigger and official adverse description make them a state-directed candidate disadvantage rather than neutral identification. Thornton preserves national qualifications and the distinction between administration and substantive controls; Rednour's genuine-procedure holding preserves, rather than erases, that boundary.

The strongest objection is that no candidate is excluded and voters receive information relevant to a legitimate policy preference. That difference limits the direct qualifications analogy: the present rule need not call every label an additional personal qualification. It does not make the manner power a general authority to influence congressional electoral choice through official political penalties. Nor does the Tenth Amendment provide the States the national-office control rejected by Thornton's governing premise. The strongest counter to that response, preserved by the Thornton dissenters, is that the people's retained electoral authority extends beyond Article I's express manner grant. Their commitments below do not silently abandon that view.

**C-B — First and Fourteenth Amendment protection against the official viewpoint penalty.** The State may administer the ballot without making it a general speech forum. It nevertheless cannot attach this adverse official designation to a candidate because the candidate refuses a prescribed pledge or legislative position. Burdick's calibrated burden inquiry and Rednour's full-system analysis protect reasonable nondiscriminatory administration; they do not justify the government's political preference. Anderson v. Martin supplies the received distinction between information on the official instrument and its discriminatory electoral operation. The asserted voter-information interest cannot justify this selective adverse treatment when its very trigger is disagreement with the State's program.

The strongest objection is that the ballot itself is a governmental instrument and that failure to finance or repeat all private expression is not censorship. The present case concerns the State's treatment of identified candidates in the instrument by which votes are cast, not a demand for an official platform or arbitrary-message tally. Burdick's rejection of fictional and protest write-ins is preserved. No proof that the label changes the winner, makes access impossible, or completely bars speech is needed to identify this official political penalty. The speech route supplies relief even for a Justice who does not accept Thornton's reserved-power analysis.

**C-C — authorized neutral information.** This route could sustain ordinary identifiers or a neutral informational scheme. Its strongest objection is the supplied mechanism: only compliance with the particular state program avoids the adverse official judgment. None of the eight finds those facts neutral. A rule permitting ordinary identifying information remains distinct and is not invalidated.

Palm Beach and Gore are now effective. Their presidential authority, state-law clarification, counting-equality, and remedy rules do not decide congressional-label power. Daggett demonstrates that an actual funding consequence and private alternatives must be assessed at their own scope; its statutory participation terminology did not establish an official stigmatizing command. It supplies no permission for these labels, and its particular subsidy holdings are not transferred mechanically.

## Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez / United States v. Velazquez

**Event and reach:** Consolidated merits, February 28, 2001, Nos. 99-603 and 99-960. All eight reach the challenged welfare-law condition. The severability determination is uncontested. The case does not present a general entitlement to public civil counsel or a challenge to every LSC activity restriction.

### Statutory scope and legal alternatives

Section 504(a) denies the appropriation's financial assistance to a covered recipient. Paragraph (16) addresses initiating representation or otherwise participating in litigation, lobbying, or rulemaking involving reform of a federal or state welfare system. It then permits representation of an eligible individual seeking specific relief from a welfare agency, subject to the condition that the relief not involve an effort to amend or otherwise challenge existing law in effect when the representation begins. The permission and its challenged limiting condition are distinct. The entire welfare-reform prohibition, lobbying rules, class-action rules, and unrelated program limits are not the challenged clause.

The restriction operates on the recipient organization, not merely on a single quoted sentence paid for by a particular federal dollar. The packet establishes client-directed advocacy and the possibility of forced withdrawal when a necessary challenge emerges. No commitment relies on an assumed available replacement lawyer, a particular affiliate, a separately funded continuation arrangement, or a factual finding that all clients can readily litigate without counsel.

**V-A — unconstitutional restriction within independent representation.** Congress may select categories of civil legal assistance, clients, and lawful program administration. When it funds an eligible client's independent representation in a welfare dispute, it may not require the lawyer to refrain from an otherwise available challenge to the governing law merely because that argument questions the existing legal regime. The lawyer represents the client, while government counsel defends the government's position. The restriction selects among arguments inside the representation Congress authorized and can force withdrawal precisely when a legal claim becomes adverse to existing law. It therefore restricts the private advocacy rather than simply transmitting a government-authored message.

Rosenberger protects independently controlled viewpoints within the actual benefit and preserves genuine boundaries; Southworth likewise distinguishes independent private expression from government messages and curriculum. Finley's controlling narrow rule sustained a nonconclusive consideration in selective arts patronage, not a categorical exclusion of specified legal arguments. Urofsky confirms that public means and professional duties alone do not decide whose expression is at issue, while preserving institutional program choices. None makes all subsidies forums or creates a universal professional-speech exception. Rust and Regan remain valid at their actual program-definition scopes.

The strongest objection to V-A is that Congress selected routine benefits representation, not litigation changing the welfare system, and no general duty to finance law reform exists. The answer is the clause's operation within an already authorized individual representation: a legal challenge can be the ground for the client's specific benefits relief. Preventing that argument is not equivalent to declining an unrelated subject category. The ordinary judicial role of testing a claim against valid law reinforces the speech distinction; no freestanding separation-of-powers holding is necessary. The recipient-level reach and forced withdrawal strengthen the objection; absence of an alternative lawyer is not an indispensable constitutional fact and is not presumed.

**V-B — lawful definition of subsidized services and recipients.** Congress may finance individual relief under existing welfare law without financing recipients' efforts to amend or invalidate that law. Legal services are not thereby transformed into government-authored speech, but public assistance need not cover every useful argument or all forms of litigation. The condition marks a subject/function boundary for the funded service; its force comes from subsidy eligibility rather than a direct prohibition on a private organization operating without this assistance.

The strongest objection to V-B is that it disables only the law-challenging side of the very dispute the program authorizes, reaches the recipient, and can terminate an existing client relationship. The defending commitment must confront those consequences rather than assume replacement counsel or affiliate access. Its answer is that a limited public undertaking may end when the matter requires a service Congress did not choose to support; the Constitution does not compel expansion of the appropriation to avoid that boundary. This is a disputed legal characterization, not a finding that withdrawal is painless or that funds purchase the government's own views. Finley's broader Scalia/Thomas subsidy ground supports that characterization, while its preserved limits on independent penalties and disguised regulation require the present condition to be understood as eligibility for this service grant, not a general license to penalize unrelated private expression. O'Connor's narrower version rests on this program's defined service and retains review of independent coercive or discriminatory administration.

The strongest counter to V-B's subsidy/penalty distinction is that entity-level selection may be used to evade the protection for independently funded expression. The present defending positions do not declare all recipient conditions valid. They regard this directly service-related welfare-litigation boundary as defining the assisted undertaking; they would not sustain an unrelated ideological loyalty pledge or a separate prohibition on private legal practice merely by calling it funding. The disagreement with V-A remains whether this particular restriction is such a boundary or instead an impermissible argument restriction within the chosen undertaking.
