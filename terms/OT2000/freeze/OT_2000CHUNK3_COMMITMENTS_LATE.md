## Department of the Interior v. Klamath Water Users Protective Association

**Review and proposed disposition:** Reach the Exemption 5 category question and affirm the Ninth Circuit. The seven communications cannot be withheld under Exemption 5 on the supported interested-claimant facts. No judgment concerning ownership, priority or quantity of water rights follows.

**K1 — Statutory category.** Even assuming an outside adviser can sometimes function within an agency's deliberative process, an outside claimant's submissions advancing its own claim to a limited resource against competing claimants are not intra-agency communications merely because the recipient agency has trust duties to that claimant. The trustee relationship explains the communication but does not turn a distinct claimant into the agency for Exemption 5. This is a functional interested-claimant holding; it does not decide that every outside expert, contractor or adviser is categorically excluded, or that any incidental personal interest always defeats the category.

**K2 — Separate privilege requirement and relief.** Failure of the agency-communication condition defeats Exemption 5 regardless of whether a communication otherwise might receive deliberative-process or work-product protection in litigation. Affirmance removes that claimed basis for withholding these seven communications. The district court implements disclosure of nonexempt records, subject only to independently supported and properly preserved other grounds; no such ground is found or revived here. Any reasonably segregable nonexempt material must be released. The Court need not decide the actual litigation privileges because the independently necessary category is absent.

## United States v. Hilario

**Review and proposed disposition:** Reach the preserved issues and affirm the First Circuit's reversal of dismissal. Gil's appointment has not ceased under the cited duration provisions and is constitutionally permissible on the supplied structural facts. Prosecution may continue; no guilt finding or validation of every prosecutorial act follows.

**H0 — Authority for the appeal notice.** Reject Hilario's threshold objection independently of Gil's appointment validity. The assistant's appointment under section 542, the Department's authority under section 516, the Solicitor General's appeal control under 28 C.F.R. 0.20(b), and Rule 3(c)'s lack of a required personal United States Attorney signature support the notice. Gil's alleged defect does not itself invalidate an independently authorized Department appeal. The reported procedural ground supplies this conclusion; no new signature or ratification is invented.

**H1 — Duration.** Section 546(d) authorizes the district court's successor appointment after the Attorney General appointment expires at 120 days, and permits that appointee to serve until the vacancy is filled. The distinct four-year provision in section 541 does not terminate the section 546(d) appointment at four years. The broad wording of section 541(b) is considered with its own command that duties continue until a successor is appointed and qualifies; it cannot establish automatic cessation even on Hilario's proposed reading. Section 546(c)'s earlier-of presidential qualification or 120-day expiration governs the Attorney General route, while subsection (d) supplies the judicial route's separate duration. The appointment remains subject to presidential removal under section 541(c). Section 546(b)'s Senate-rejection prohibition addresses the Attorney General's appointment power; this case neither extends nor eliminates it. Subsection (d)'s filing command remains operative; no filing defect is presented or inferred.

**H2 — Inferior status through the actual executive structure.** United States Attorneys, including this court-appointed incumbent, remain inferior officers in this statutory organization: the Attorney General directs their duties and litigation under sections 516 and 519 and can take or reassign litigation under section 518; the President can remove them under section 541(c) and fill the vacancy through the ordinary appointment process. The combined controls leave no independent prosecutorial office beyond executive direction merely because the appointee exercises substantial district-level authority. The Attorney General is not assigned a general power to discharge a United States Attorney. The conclusion concerns the regular office's actual controls, not an acting label, a universal supervision-only test, or a claim that removal alone classifies every office.

**H2-S — Souter's fuller sufficient classification ground.** The duties are substantial and include broad federal prosecution in a district; territorial jurisdiction and prolonged, potentially indefinite tenure do not resemble a narrowly limited special assignment. Those features weigh against treating temporary status as a sufficient classification answer. Nevertheless, this prosecutor's work is subject to direct executive control over duties and cases, the President may remove the officer, and the appointment ends when the vacancy is filled. Considered with duties, jurisdiction and tenure, that actual subordination makes the office inferior. Hierarchy is necessary here but not an adequate substitute for examining those other features. Souter reaches the same classification without joining a rationale declaring a superior relationship alone sufficient.

**H3 — Interbranch appointment and judicial integrity.** Congress may use the Constitution's court-of-law option for this inferior office. The appointment power alone gives the district judges no direction of charging or litigation, removal power or continuing supervisory role; executive control remains. Morrison and Siebold support the permissibility of interbranch appointment within their scope, while Morrison and Mistretta require attention to incompatibility and judicial integrity. Section 546(d) differs from Morrison because the district court appoints as a court and appointing judges are not automatically excluded from later cases involving the appointee. Those differences are considered, not erased: later adjudication is not an exercise of prosecutorial supervision, and a choice of officer without continuing control does not itself establish partiality or an impermissible merger. The statute is upheld against the structural objection presented. A supported actual-bias, coercion or judicial-direction claim remains distinct; none is established here. No general immunity from disqualification rules or permission for judges to supervise enforcement is announced.

**H4 — Prolonged service as applied.** More than six years without presidential replacement does not alone invalidate this otherwise lawful appointment. Because the regular office is inferior under its continuing controls, passage of time does not convert a merely temporary inferior into an acting principal. The text supplies no shorter judicial term; neither institutional inconvenience nor perceived nomination delay authorizes creating one. The President's power to remove and the ordinary appointment route persist. This conclusion does not excuse an actual change in control or a different supported constitutional defect.

**H5 — Judgment and contingent remedy.** Affirmance on appointment validity restores continued prosecution without reaching the Government's preserved alternative that dismissal would be improper even if the appointment were defective. The lower court expressly left that alternative open. If an invalidity ground were instead adopted, the Court could not automatically dismiss, assume independent authorization, find harmlessness, or invent later ratification: the supplied record does not establish the necessary prosecutorial acts. It would need a supported resolution or a remand for that preserved remedial question under the identified defect. Ryder forbids treating long service as an automatic cure for a qualifying substantial defect but supplies no automatic dismissal rule for this prosecutor's indictment. This conditional route is not an entered remedy or an additional affirmance ground.

## Shafer v. South Carolina

**S1 — The jury's actual stage controls.** Once South Carolina's jury has unanimously found a statutory aggravator and is choosing the sentence, its actual alternatives are death and life without ordinary parole. A lesser sentence the judge could impose if no aggravator were found is not an alternative available at that later jury choice. The revised preliminary sequence therefore does not categorically remove the case from Simmons.

**S2 — Conditional due-process application.** If the State placed future dangerousness in issue at that sentencing proceeding, Simmons requires an effective opportunity to present accurate ordinary-parole ineligibility through an accurate defense explanation or an adequate instruction. Directing attention to the ordinary meaning of life does not supply the legal answer when that protected opportunity is denied. No fixed wording, assurance against clemency or future legislation, or prohibition on truthful prison-danger evidence follows. The jury's parole question is material to misunderstanding and possible effect, but does not alone establish the State-raised-danger predicate. The supplied description of evidence and argument is not a finding that the predicate is satisfied.

**S3 — Disposition and remaining work.** Vacate the South Carolina Supreme Court's judgment insofar as it rejects this federal challenge to the death sentence, and remand under S1–S2. The state court must determine whether the prosecution actually put future danger in issue on the supported record and whether an effective accurate opportunity was denied. If a constitutional error is established, the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to selection of death. If it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if death is pursued, while a lawful noncapital disposition remains available. This handoff makes no conclusive harmful-error finding and orders no present new hearing or automatic sentence substitution. Guilt remains untouched. These are factual and remedial inquiries under the stated law, not unresolved legal alternatives or a basis for continuing the categorical statutory bar.

**S4 — Independent Souter–Stevens instruction ground.** Under their published Simmons position, a capital defendant who requests a judicial explanation of a legal sentencing term is entitled to one when there is a reasonable likelihood that a juror will misunderstand the term. The jury's explicit parole inquiry and the refusal to explain the actual life-without-parole alternative satisfy that narrower application of their instruction principle. An ordinary-meaning direction is inadequate, and counsel's presentation alone is not a substitute for the judge's authoritative explanation under this separate ground. The violation does not depend on State-raised future danger. They would remand the consequence of that error under the constitutional harmlessness standard rather than decide harm on the incomplete account. This is their additional sufficient legal ground, not entering law or an assumed majority proposition.
