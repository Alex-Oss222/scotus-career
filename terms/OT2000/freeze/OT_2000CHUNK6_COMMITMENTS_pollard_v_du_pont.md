Event: merits, June 4, 2001. Argument: April 23, 2001. Research cutoff: June 4, 2001. O'Connor takes no part in consideration or decision. The seven other Associates are modeled from the approved neutral packet, validation C, its official enacted-text supplement and entering law. No Stone position or same-matter historical Supreme Court outcome was supplied. No author assignment is made.

## Common result and controlling scope available

Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer independently favor reversal of the front-pay cap ruling and remand for ordinary equitable determination. Compensation bridging the period between judgment and reinstatement, or substituting for reinstatement when reinstatement is inappropriate, is relief authorized by section 706(g). Section 1981a(b)(2) expressly excludes that preexisting category from the added compensatory damages subject to subsection (b)(3)'s caps. Both forms of reinstatement-related front pay fall outside that cap. Monetary form and payment after judgment do not change the statutory source.

Section 706(g)'s authority is not confined to its enumerated examples. Its appropriate affirmative-action and other-equitable-relief language permits the reinstatement substitute needed to restore employment-related loss when an immediate return cannot properly be ordered. Section 1981a(a)(1) adds compensation to existing relief, while (b)(2) excludes all section 706(g) relief, not merely backpay and interest. Landgraf's description of that separation is respected without converting its temporal holding into an already decided front-pay classification. The decision resolves the classification now.

The competing path would treat all postjudgment lost earnings as future pecuniary losses under (b)(3), or distinguish temporary bridging from payment where no return is appropriate. Its strongest argument is that future earnings are monetary compensation and the broad future-loss language must do work. The answer is statutory sequence: (b)(2) defines what compensation under this section excludes before (b)(3) limits damages awarded under this section. Future pecuniary damages remain within the cap when they are not authorized reinstatement-related equitable relief. The split between delayed and inappropriate reinstatement lacks a basis in the preserved equitable category and would selectively narrow preexisting remedial authority through an enactment expressly adding remedies.

No routine entitlement to years of wages follows. The court must determine whether reinstatement is appropriate, whether front pay is an appropriate substitute or bridge, the employment loss causally attributable to discrimination and a supported amount and duration. Ordinary mitigation and avoidance of overlapping recovery remain relevant; no new mandatory formula, fixed duration, discount rate or factual finding is established here. Emotional-distress and punitive awards retain their separate statutory limits. The granted question does not reopen liability, backpay or fees.

## Joins, precedent and implementation

All seven can join the same statutory ground, both front-pay forms, the remedy-source distinction and the limited reversal/remand. No separate-writing function is necessary for their actual differences in other remedial cases. No general agency-deference holding, jury-allocation holding or universal future-loss classification is required. The Government and EEOC position supports the same reading but supplies no substitute for the enacted text.

Landgraf is applied to its actual separation of preexisting relief from added remedies and is extended only by today's direct classification; its temporal holding survives. Kolstad remains a punitive-eligibility and attribution decision and supplies no front-pay rule. Hudson v. Reno's contrary classification is rejected to this extent, not treated as Supreme Court precedent. The lower court reconsiders an equitable front-pay award without the section 1981a cap, retaining ordinary causal, evidentiary and equitable limits. The existing awards remain in their independent categories and cannot be duplicated. No sum, reinstatement order, new trial or factual outcome is prescribed.

Frozen result: seven participating Associates reverse and remand on these common grounds; O'Connor does not participate. Final Court tally awaits assembly.
