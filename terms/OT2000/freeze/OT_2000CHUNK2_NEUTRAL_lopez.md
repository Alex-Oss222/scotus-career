* **Case Name:** Lopez v. Davis
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-7504; 531 U.S. 230
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-10 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Bureau of Prisons may categorically deny the discretionary early-release benefit under §3621(e)(2)(B) to otherwise statutorily eligible prisoners whose felony conduct involved firearms.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, 186 F.3d 1092: reversed an order requiring reconsideration of Lopez's eligibility and sustained the regulation as an exercise of discretion.
* **Relief Requested:** Lopez seeks restoration of individualized eligibility consideration; the Bureau seeks affirmance of its categorical regulatory exclusion.
* **Material Record:** Lopez was convicted of methamphetamine possession with intent to distribute and received a firearm-related sentencing enhancement. The Bureau admitted him to drug treatment but treated him as categorically ineligible for early release under its regulation covering felony conduct involving carrying, possession, or use of a firearm. The challenged rule invokes discretionary selection among statutorily eligible prisoners rather than defining all such drug convictions as violent offenses.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must distinguish treatment eligibility, statutory nonviolent-offense eligibility, and entitlement to discretionary sentence reduction. The question is not whether Lopez has a right to immediate release. A separate objection to the rule's adoption procedures must be preserved before adjudication.

### Issue 1: Eligibility and bounded categorical discretion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang distinguishes statutory eligibility from a favorable discretionary decision and requires reasoned, consistent administration. Reno v. Koray concerns statutory custody credit and does not itself decide this discretionary release program.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A lawful grant of discretion permits relevant considerations but not disregard of the statute, pretext, or unexplained departure from binding rules. The exact delegation controls whether categories are available.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §3621(e)(2)(B); 28 C.F.R. §550.58(a)(1)(vi)(B); applicable administrative review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Heckler v. Campbell recognizes rulemaking for recurring issues within a lawful delegation; Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council applies only within its proper statutory setting.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The word may preserves discretion after the statute's threshold conditions. It does not by itself authorize every exclusion or negate mandatory duties elsewhere.
* **Open Point:** Whether Congress permits this conduct-based categorical choice and whether the firearm relation supplies a reasonable fit.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eighth Circuit read may reduce as authorizing uniform eligibility rules. Lopez argues that Congress chose conviction of a nonviolent offense as the relevant criterion and that using a sentencing factor circumvents that choice; he also urges individual consideration and emphasis on treatment success. The Bureau answers that statutory eligibility is necessary but not sufficient and that firearm involvement reasonably bears on public safety. Its limiting principle requires a relevant, reasoned rule, not automatic denial of every eligible prisoner or categorical evasion of the incentive Congress created.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Sustain the category:** Affirm if the statute permits categorical selection and the supported firearm criterion reasonably relates to the discretionary purpose. Lopez remains eligible for treatment without entitlement to the reduction. **Require individualized consideration:** Reverse if the statute reserves that decision or forbids this use of offense conduct; remand for lawful consideration, not automatic release. **Procedural defect:** Consider only if preserved and supported. The legal outcome does not reclassify every qualifying offense as violent, modify the sentence itself, or establish a new constitutional liberty interest.
