## Fiore v. White

- The Pennsylvania Supreme Court accepted the certified question on December 20, 1999, received submission on March 14, 2000, and answered on August 21, 2000. Its answer says that Commonwealth v. Scarpone, 535 Pa. 273, 634 A.2d 1109 (December 15, 1993), clarified the existing statutory language and stated the law when Fiore's conviction became final. The answer is an authoritative state-law construction, not an assumption about a future state response. [Fiore v. White, 562 Pa. 634, 636, 644–646, 757 A.2d 842 (2000)](https://static.case.law/pa/562/html/0634-01.html).
- The underlying federal habeas case concerns TWO no-permit convictions under 35 P.S. §§6018.401(a) and 6018.606(f): one after a joint jury trial and one after a separate bench trial. The district court's writ addressed the portions of the sentence attributable to both convictions. Fiore had sixty convictions across the two trials; the other convictions are outside this no-permit dispute. Avoid wording that accidentally confines relief to one of the two challenged convictions or releases him from every conviction. [Fiore v. White, 149 F.3d 221, 222–224 & n.1 (3d Cir. 1998)](https://static.case.law/f3d/149/html/0221-01.html); [state answer, 562 Pa. at 637–638 & n.6](https://static.case.law/pa/562/html/0634-01.html).
- The Commonwealth did not dispute that Fiore possessed a permit. It treated departure from the permit's terms as an unpermitted operation. The state construction rejects that equivalence for this offense; other statutory provisions separately address violations of permit conditions. [Commonwealth v. Scarpone, 535 Pa. 273, 278–280, 634 A.2d 1109 (1993)](https://static.case.law/pa/535/html/0273-01.html); [state answer, 562 Pa. at 638, 645–646](https://static.case.law/pa/562/html/0634-01.html).
- Section 6018.401(a) contains distinct clauses governing authorization of hazardous-waste handling, permits for owning or operating a facility, and licenses for hazardous-waste transportation. This case concerns the owner/operator permit clause, not every act regulated by that subsection. The complete provision appears in note 2 of the certified answer. [562 Pa. at 636 n.2](https://static.case.law/pa/562/html/0634-01.html).
- The federal petition was filed after April 24, 1996. The court of appeals expressly did not decide exhaustion or whether the state courts adjudicated the federal claim on the merits. It supplied no exact federal filing date. Those are expressly unresolved questions in the judgment under review. [149 F.3d at 224 n.3](https://static.case.law/f3d/149/html/0221-01.html).
- The state and federal recitations differ in their expression of the precise finality date and the minimum sentence. No exact finality date or minimum term is needed to identify the certified answer's temporal conclusion or the two challenged convictions. Do not choose one without further verification if an exact date or term becomes material.

## Glover v. United States

- The presentence report recommended grouping the labor-racketeering, money-laundering, and tax counts under the November 1994 Guidelines. The government objected; the trial court separated the money-laundering counts. The resulting offense-level increase produced a range of 78–97 months, and the sentence was 84 months. Glover contends the correct range was 63–78 months. The alleged difference of 6–21 months depends on the asserted grouping error and is not an adjudicated resolution of that error. [531 U.S. 198, 200–202, factual narrative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-198.pdf).
- Counsel submitted no papers and offered no extensive oral argument on grouping at sentencing, and did not raise grouping on direct appeal. These are the described acts and omissions; they do not themselves establish deficient performance. The district court did not decide counsel's competence because it disposed of the petition under the circuit's minimum-duration prejudice rule. [531 U.S. at 201–202, procedural narrative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-198.pdf).
- The government's court-of-appeals argument relied only on the asserted absence of prejudice. Its additional contentions in this Court included adequate performance, correct grouping, and a possible increased sentence if grouping were adopted. Glover disputed those contentions. The available appellate record did not contain a resolution of them. [531 U.S. at 202, 205, description of the parties' submissions and prior proceedings](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-198.pdf).

## Lopez v. Davis

- Lopez's conviction was for methamphetamine possession with intent to distribute, with a two-level firearm-possession enhancement. The proceeding is a federal-prisoner petition under 28 U.S.C. §2241. The district court ordered reconsideration of eligibility, not immediate release. [531 U.S. 230, 236–237, factual and procedural narrative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-230.pdf).
- The challenged 1997 regulation invokes the Bureau's discretion to establish additional early-release criteria. It differs from the earlier 1995 definition of crimes of violence. The relevant 1997 category covers current felony conduct involving carrying, possessing, or using a firearm or other dangerous weapon. Lopez was found qualified for residential treatment; that finding is distinct from eligibility for or entitlement to an early-release award. [531 U.S. at 233–237, regulatory history and record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-230.pdf).
- The notice-and-comment challenge was advanced by amici. It was not raised or decided below and was not presented in the petition for certiorari. This is an established scope fact rather than a generic possibility of preservation. [531 U.S. at 244 n.6, statement of procedural record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-230.pdf).

## City News & Novelty, Inc. v. City of Waukesha

- The petition presented three questions: assignment of the persuasion burden, discretion to vary punishments, and whether prompt review requires a judicial determination or access to court. The grant covered only the third. City News did not contest the sufficiency of the evidence supporting the ordinance violations in the proceeding described here. [531 U.S. 278, 282, procedural narrative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-278.pdf).
- In letters dated June 12 and June 19, 2000, City News stated that it would withdraw the renewal request and close when a larger competitor received its license. The competitor received a license on June 20. City News ceased the adult business, no longer sought renewal, and stated no current plan to pursue another license. [531 U.S. at 282–283, description of the letters and argument record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-278.pdf).
- Waukesha's last finding of violations was in 1995; City News recognized that disabilities from those findings expired in 2000. Its claimed disability through 2005 depended on an assertion that operating into 2000 without a renewed license would count as a further violation. No further violation had been found by the council, and the council expressly allowed continued business during the state-court proceedings. Do not state that a through-2005 disability was actually imposed. [531 U.S. at 285 n.2, ordinance and prior-proceedings facts](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-278.pdf).

## Director of Revenue of Missouri v. CoBank ACB

- CoBank sought refunds of Missouri corporate income taxes paid by the National Bank for Cooperatives for 1991–1994 through amended returns filed in March 1996. The National Bank had been formed in 1989 from ten district banks and the Central Bank for Cooperatives. CoBank succeeded to its rights and obligations. [531 U.S. 316, 319, factual narrative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-316.pdf).
- The 1985 amendment deleted BOTH the sentence granting the stock-related tax exemption and the sentence limiting it to periods of federal stock ownership. It also eliminated the relevant official position and federal stock-purchase authority. Calling the deletion merely obsolete or describing its substantive legal effect is an argument; the objective amendment fact is deletion of both sentences alongside elimination of stock-purchase authority. [531 U.S. at 322–324, statutory texts and amendment history](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-316.pdf).
- The surviving §2134 language expressly concerns notes, debentures, and other bank obligations, including tax exceptions and the federal income-tax treatment of interest in a holder's hands. It is not an express blanket exemption for all bank income. Other Farm Credit institutions have separate tax provisions. [531 U.S. at 318–319, statutory text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-316.pdf).

## Original petition scope: Glover

- The original petition's two Questions Presented concern (1) whether an additional 6–21 months caused by a sentencing-guideline error satisfies Strickland prejudice and (2) whether a two-level guideline error is categorically insufficient even when it produces those additional months. The introductory statement assumes deficient performance and an erroneous calculation for purposes of those questions. The original petition therefore supports a prejudice-focused review scope; a broader formulation in a respondent's merits brief is not itself the granted question. The docket copy reports an unrestricted grant of this petition. [Petition, printed p.i, PDF p.2](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0402/micro_IA40385014_0402%2002.%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari.pdf); [docket copy](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0402/micro_IA40385014_0402%2001.%20Docket_djvu.txt).
- The district-court opinion reproduced at petition App.4–5 reports that the government disputed deficient performance there, while the court did not resolve that issue. Its discussion of the government's additional tax-count calculation appears in App.4 n.1. Thus, a statement that no alternative ground was ever raised anywhere below would be too broad. The relevant distinction is what was raised and decided in the court of appeals and what lies within the petition's questions. [Petition appendix, PDF pp.19–20](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0402/micro_IA40385014_0402%2002.%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari.pdf).

## District of Columbia v. Tri County Industries, Inc.

- The original petition asks whether Gasperini invalidates the circuit approach that constrains district-court discretion to grant a new trial and correspondingly expands appellate review. That is a verified primary-source question, not merely an unverified reconstruction from the lower opinion. [Petition, printed p.i, PDF p.2](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0379/micro_IA40385014_0379%2002.%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari.pdf).
- The July 23, 1998 order denied judgment as a matter of law but granted a new trial unless Tri County accepted a reduction to $1 million within thirty days. It separately addressed mitigation, speculative profits, excessive damages, and reconsideration of the exclusion of defense evidence. ## Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association

- The association's rules limit voting seats on its legislative council and board of control to principals, assistant principals, and superintendents selected by member schools. At the time of the 1997 penalties, every voting member was a public-school administrator. The rules did not themselves require a particular public-school proportion. About 290 public schools and 55 private schools belonged; membership was voluntary. [531 U.S. 288, 291–293, institutional facts](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-288.pdf).
- The principal revenue was tournament gate receipts; school dues supplied about 4%. Staff were not state-paid; eligibility to participate in the state retirement system did not include state payment of the employer contribution. State Board representatives were nonvoting members. [531 U.S. at 291,301,307–308 & nn.1,4, descriptions of institutional arrangements](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-288.pdf).
- In 1996 the State Board replaced the former express designation with language recognizing the association's role and authorizing voluntary public-school membership. The actual text changed before the 1997 enforcement proceeding. The district court found no material change in conduct after that amendment. Do not replace the amended text with the repealed designation or treat its legal effect as an agreed fact. [531 U.S. at 292–293,301–302, description of enacted language and district findings](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-288.pdf).
- The penalties were four years of athletic-program probation, two years of football and boys' basketball playoff ineligibility, and a $3,000 fine, following letters to incoming students and parents concerning spring football practice. [531 U.S. at 293, enforcement record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-288.pdf).
