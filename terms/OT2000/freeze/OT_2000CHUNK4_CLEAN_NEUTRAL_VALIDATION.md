## Buford: ready on the bounded review question

The complete Seventh Circuit opinion, 201 F.3d 937, paragraphs 1–13, supports the packet's distinction between the standard of appellate review and the substantive definition of related prior convictions. Its own functional-consolidation rule is a lower-court rule under review, not a Supreme Court holding already established by the packet. Nor does the choice of deferential review establish that every sentencing application receives that standard.

The record must include both sets of concrete circumstances. Buford's four robbery convictions indisputably count together. The drug charge was separately filed, assigned to a drug branch, prosecuted by a different prosecutor, and resolved by a separate plea before a different judge. Sentencing nevertheless occurred before one judge on one occasion, with one presentence report, a letter from counsel consenting to that procedure, and concurrent sentences. Two judgments were entered, with distinct drug and robbery sentences. No consolidation order appears in the record. Absence of an order is not itself proof against functional consolidation, because the circuit's rule expressly permits it without a formal order. The court rejected common occasion and common scheme as grounds on this record; Buford's claimed addiction connection remains an argument rather than a finding of a common plan. The sentencing consequences are 188 months imposed under the 188–235 month career-offender range, against a reported 84–105 month range without that classification.

Application Note 3 distinguishes an intervening arrest, which excludes relatedness, from the alternative routes of the same occasion, a single common scheme or plan, or consolidation for trial or sentencing. This appeal concerns the last route. Neither the unexplained older convictions nor the lower court's description of Buford as a career criminal supplies an alternative substitute for the actual qualifying-conviction calculation.

Supported path A is deference to the supported, context-dependent classification while independently correcting the legal criteria. Koon and Powell preserve that allocation for their own sentencing task; Stinson supplies the binding status and limits of commentary. The strongest contrary argument is that uniform interpretation of the classification governing a substantial sentence increase requires independent appellate resolution. The answer must distinguish authoritative definition from record application, rather than rely solely on the word factual.

Supported path B is independent appellate application to the established facts, with the judgment returned for that review if necessary. Ornelas supplies functional allocation and the reasons for independent constitutional review, not a universal mixed-question command. The strongest contrary argument is the local, variable sentencing context and limited precedential utility of classifying this particular hearing. Selecting independent review would not itself determine that the five convictions form one qualifying sentence or entitle Buford to a particular replacement sentence. A separately identified error in the substantive grouping criterion remains independently correctable under either allocation.

The lower opinion is sufficient for this review-standard dispute. A comprehensive new consolidation definition, a new classification of the five convictions, or a conclusion about an unexamined older conviction requires separate support and cannot be supplied by the review holding alone.

## Children's Healthcare: corrected neutral framing and statutory terms

The complete Eighth Circuit majority, dissent and notes, 212 F.3d 1084, were read. The original packet is usable with the clarifications below. The full enacted section 4454 is copied mechanically at `runtime/OT_2000CHUNK4_CHILD_STATUTE.md` from Public Law 105-33, August 5, 1997, 111 Stat. 426–432, official source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-105publ33/html/PLAW-105publ33.htm. The proposed 2000 Code pages could not be retrieved; the enacted text supplies the relevant provisions, and the date-eligible lower opinion cross-checks their operation. No later amendment is imported.

**Eligibility is religious, not simply conscientious.** The institution serves patients who choose to rely solely on religious healing and for whom medical services would conflict with religious belief. The enacted institutional definition uses both conditions; a paraphrase using only either condition is inadequate. The beneficiary election requires a signed statement of conscientious opposition to nonexcepted treatment and inconsistency of that treatment with sincere religious beliefs. An equally sincere secular objection does not meet that election condition. Sect-neutral language does not itself establish neutrality between religion and nonreligion; the two questions must remain separate.

**The objection is not an absolute promise never to receive medical care.** Under section 1821(b)(5), excepted treatment is treatment received involuntarily or required under federal, state or local law. Nonexcepted treatment means treatment outside those categories. The election ordinarily takes effect on execution and continues until revoked. Voluntary written notice may revoke it; receipt of nonexcepted treatment reimbursed under the Medicare title is a statutory deemed revocation. The election form separately warns that receipt of nonexcepted services revokes the election and may limit later benefits. Once an election has been made and revoked twice, the next election cannot become effective until one year after the most recent revocation; subsequent elections require five years. No particular beneficiary's compliance or revocation is at issue or presumed.

**The institution and the payment conditions do different work.** The institution must satisfy the tax-exempt, lawful-operation, nonmedical staffing and round-the-clock physical-care requirements. Its religious objection to medical provision and its restrictions on medical-provider ownership and affiliation do not eliminate the express exceptions: ownership interests below five percent are disregarded for the specified ownership clause; uncompensated governing-body service, the defined family relationship, and shared vendor relationships do not themselves constitute prohibited affiliation. The Secretary may accept accreditation only insofar as it gives reasonable assurance that specified conditions are met. The statute's new Medicare provision permits inpatient or post-hospital extended services only with an effective election and a condition that would qualify for the corresponding benefit in an ordinary hospital or skilled nursing facility, subject to the spending and sunset provisions. It does not create unrestricted coverage for ordinary assistance with daily living or for spiritual healing. The proposition that the funded services are a subset of ordinary covered care is the lower majority's legal and operational conclusion, challenged by the dissent; it is not an independent finding proving every application compliant.

**Religious protection and retained review must both be stated.** Section 1861(ss)(3)(A)(i) bars compelled medical screening, examination, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment or another medical service when the patient or representative objects on religious grounds. Clause (ii) preserves the Secretary's power to require sufficient information about the patient's condition as a benefit condition. Subparagraph (B)(i) bars medical supervision, regulation or control contrary to institutional or personnel religious beliefs; clause (ii) preserves review of billed items and services to determine noncoverage, excess or fraud. The institutional utilization plan must cover admissions, duration, extended stays and services, require an appropriate committee and records of its actions, and satisfy further requirements necessary for effective review. The institution must supply required coverage and quality information and meet necessary health-and-safety requirements. These are enforceable statutory powers; their existence and their practical effectiveness are distinct questions. The provision does not assign final federal entitlement to the religious committee, but the absence of compelled examination does limit the available information.

The Medicare spending provisions separately authorize proportional reductions, or alternative coverage conditions and limitations, on the specified expenditure trigger, increase permissible patient charges by the reduction, and provide a contingent future sunset for new elections after three excessive-expenditure fiscal years beginning no earlier than fiscal 2002. The enacted provisions do not establish that any trigger occurred here. Their restriction on review of expenditure estimates and reductions is not a withdrawal of review of ordinary coverage recommendations. Medicaid's statutory substitutions and supervision exemptions are separate; the Medicare election language must not silently be treated as the complete text of every Medicaid obligation.

**The operational evidence is contested, not nonexistent.** The majority relies on physical nursing work and its separability from devotional activity. Dissent note 10 reports the Cook affidavit, appendix pages 178–187, describing nurses' religious training, Bible reading and prayer; that is identified record evidence, not merely an unsupported hypothetical. It does not by itself establish which billed service or payment financed prayer, or prove that all physical care is inseparable from devotion. Dissent note 12 quotes a prior program manual according great weight to institutional necessity judgments while retaining the intermediary's final responsibility. That quotation supports scrutiny of practical deference; it does not prove that the revised statute abolishes governmental review. The original affidavits, payment detail and operative implementation materials are not supplied here. Their absence cannot be converted into an evidentiary default by either side.

**The existing law leaves distinct, supported alternatives.** Agostini requires the unified governmental-act and function inquiry, with specific denominational, accommodation and civil-authority doctrines at their actual scope. The lower court's independent Lemon prongs do not control. Kiryas Joel preserves accommodation beyond constitutional compulsion while rejecting a particular denominational governmental franchise. Mitchell distinguishes actual financed religious use from affiliation or potential diversion and requires relief at the proved scope. Marshfield, effective February 27, 2001, reinforces examination of continuing governmental conduct despite formal changes; its monument facts supply no automatic benefits rule.

One supported path treats the condition-equivalent payment for separable physical care and retained federal review as a permissible accommodation of access to an existing benefit. Its strongest objections are exclusion of comparable secular refusal, the claimed religious design of the eligibility criteria, and the operational evidence just described. Those objections require answers on their own terms; public savings or a secular label is insufficient.

A second path finds a forbidden religious preference in the eligibility rule, or concludes that a proved allocation of public authority or purchase of devotional activity exceeds accommodation. Its strongest objections are the established room for noncompulsory accommodation, the health-condition limitation, the statutory refusal to fund spiritual healing, and meaningful retained public control. It cannot assume that all care in a religious institution is devotional, that every applicant receives otherwise excluded custodial care, or that inability to compel a medical examination makes review necessarily fictitious.

A bounded remand is independently available if the ordinary summary-judgment standard requires resolving a material dispute about actual financed services or operative review under the correct law. The concrete affidavit and manual issues identify the inquiry; a remand may use the existing record and does not necessarily require a new hearing or unrestricted discovery. Facial and as-applied issues must remain distinct. A facial eligibility ruling can be reached without inventing the missing payment nexus; an as-applied finding of actual payment for prayer requires that nexus. Any extension or withdrawal of benefits must rest on severability and remedial law, rather than an assumed judicial power to fund all secular objectors.

The existing taxpayer challenge concerns a specific congressional spending enactment; the lower court rejected the intervenor's standing objection under Flast. No new jurisdictional defect is established. The approved grant and routine full-merits submission premise supply review of the two specified questions, not a fabricated historical Supreme Court event. The case is ready for independent modeling on these alternatives and stated limits. An eventual decisive as-applied finding beyond the supported record remains subject to the exact source requirement above.

## Early addenda

**Egelhoff.** The supplied enrolled Washington statute reproduces the complete section and closes the missing-exceptions gap. Revocation does not apply to the extent the governing instrument expressly provides otherwise; the decree requires the death benefit for the former spouse or children and no other assets fulfill that requirement at death; or the decedent could not have revoked unilaterally immediately after the decree because of its terms or another reason. These entitlement exceptions differ from payor liability protection before defined actual knowledge, permission to withhold disputed payment, notice and security options, and protection for specified value purchasers or debt recipients without knowledge. Payor actual knowledge requires sufficiently specific postdeath written notice with a reasonable opportunity to act; recipient actual knowledge has its own personal-knowledge/document rule. The more-than-thirty-day and fewer-than-five-business-day presumptions are rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence. The two definitions cannot be merged. The source's transition provision and January 1, 1995 effective date must be preserved if temporal application matters.
