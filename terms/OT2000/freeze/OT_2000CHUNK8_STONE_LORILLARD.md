# Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly / Altadis U.S.A. Inc. v. Reilly

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In both consolidated matters, affirm the disposition sustaining the challenged physical access and sales practices; reverse the judgment sustaining covered cigarette advertising against preemption and the challenged noncigarette outdoor and point-of-sale advertising restrictions.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for conforming declaratory and injunctive relief and unresolved severability. Leave inadequately presented cigar-sampling questions undecided; prescribe no replacement radius or display design.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Cigarette advertising

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the preserved express-preemption question before the cigarette speech claims.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a smoking-health advertising location prohibition falls within the amended federal clause.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Cipollone v. Liggett Group’s predicate-duty method to this previously unresolved application, without creating cigarette-wide immunity.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state prohibition selecting cigarette advertising because of smoking-health concerns is a requirement or prohibition with respect to advertising even when its operative command concerns location. The federal clause preempts these cigarette advertising restrictions where the statutory federal-labeling condition is met.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The covered cigarette advertising restrictions. General zoning rules unconnected to smoking and health and independent restrictions on sales to minors are not displaced by this holding.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress used both requirements and prohibitions and identified advertising as the regulated activity. Treating every location ban as outside that language would exempt a direct prohibition while covering a lesser warning command. The actual duty and its basis, rather than a zoning label, control.

  The express preemption text is decisive, with Cipollone's attention to the predicate duty supplying the method of application. It resolves covered cigarette advertising before constitutional review because that statutory ground gives complete relief on that component, while leaving the separate noncigarette questions for their own analysis.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Speech and product access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved cigar and smokeless-tobacco advertising challenges and the product-access rules.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the geographic and height restrictions satisfy commercial-speech fit, and whether the separate physical-access restrictions validly advance age verification.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing commercial-speech test without creating a tobacco-specific tier.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The State’s supported interest in reducing youth tobacco use does not justify the 1,000-foot advertising prohibition’s substantial suppression of lawful adult communication without adequate proportional tailoring. The five-foot rule likewise lacks adequate justification responsive to visibility and child exposure. The challenged direct-access restrictions survive because interaction with a salesperson directly supports age verification while retaining practical adult inspection and lawful purchasing.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The particular outdoor and point-of-sale advertising restrictions and the challenged physical-access requirements. Supported narrower youth protections, deception restrictions, and lawful age limits remain available.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Evidence that advertising affects youth demand does not finish the fit inquiry. The geographic rule’s extensive coverage requires an explanation beyond the importance of its objective. The height line inadequately distinguishes youth exposure from adult access to information. Clerk-controlled products address access to the product itself through a functional sales safeguard.

  Central Hudson's proportional fit inquiry governs the advertising restrictions; no special tobacco tier is invented. The map coverage, design of the height line, practical adult communication, experience, and testimony concerning youth exposure all bear on fit, without requiring one exclusive statistical form of proof. Product access is separately assessed by the actual age-verification function of clerk control. This combination does not balance away truthful adult speech simply because the public-health objective is weighty.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Inadequately presented cigar-sampling questions are not decided. A replacement radius, different display arrangements, and unchallenged regulations are outside the presented record.
