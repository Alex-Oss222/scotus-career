# Department of the Interior v. Klamath Water Users Protective Association

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Department of the Interior v. Klamath Water Users Protective Association
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 1; No. 99-1871
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-05 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether communications between Indian tribes and the Department of the Interior concerning tribal water claims qualify as inter-agency or intra-agency memorandums protected by Freedom of Information Act Exemption 5.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment permitting the Department to withhold seven communications under Exemption 5. The dispute concerns the Department’s communications with tribes asserting water interests in the Klamath Basin.
* **Relief Requested:** The Department seeks reversal and restoration of its exemption claim; the water-users association seeks affirmance and disclosure of nonexempt records.
* **Material Record:** The Bureau of Reclamation was developing a long-term operating plan for the Klamath Project while federal and tribal water claims also arose in an Oregon adjudication. The Department consulted tribes in its trust capacity. The requester represents irrigation interests competing for the same limited water. Seven withheld documents concerned tribal interests in these processes. The Department asserted deliberative-process and attorney-work-product protections. The tribes supplied information to advance their own claims, not simply detached advice about the Department’s institutional choices. These are documentary relationships, not findings about the ultimate validity or quantity of any water right. [Department of the Interior v. Klamath Water Users Protective Association](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-1.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The requester challenges withholding, not ownership of tribal water. Whether a document satisfies the statutory agency-communication requirement precedes whether litigation or deliberative privileges would protect it. No need to adjudicate the underlying trust or water claims follows.
* **Relevant Dates:** The operative FOIA request and agency proceedings predate the March 5, 2001 modeling event. The tribal trust relationship is evaluated as it existed during the exchanges.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically established as necessary to resolve this matter. Any actual intervening simulation decision must be checked before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** FOIA places the burden of justifying a claimed exemption on the agency; segregability and the precise documents remain part of the disclosure remedy.

### Issue 1: Exemption 5 and outside participants

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision resolves whether an interested outside claimant can be an intra-agency consultant. Under United States Department of Justice v. Landano, confidentiality requires an assurance or a supported inference, and an unsupported agency-wide exemption presumption is insufficient. That Exemption 7(D) holding supplies no Exemption 5 consultant rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** FOIA requires proof of the particular exemption and disclosure of reasonably segregable material. Confidentiality and tribal immunity do not substitute for Exemption 5’s statutory requirements.
* **Other Governing Law:** 5 U.S.C. §552(b)(5) requires an inter-agency or intra-agency memorandum that would be unavailable by law to a party in litigation with the agency. Its two conditions are distinct.
* **Material Other Authority:** Department of the Interior’s trust obligations explain the exchanges but do not expressly amend FOIA. The consultant analogy rests on the participant’s actual function.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law supplies the statutory sequence, burden and segregability obligation. It establishes no blanket trust-communications privilege under FOIA.
* **Open Point:** Whether the consultant understanding, assuming its validity, can include tribes pursuing their own competing claims.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** A self-interested outside claimant is not transformed into the agency by consultation or the federal trust responsibility. The Ninth Circuit did not need to decide the claimed litigation privileges.
* **Department:** Trust administration requires candid communication and assistance comparable to agency consultation; protecting these exchanges preserves effective representation and deliberation. Its proposed rule covers tribes participating through the federal trustee, rather than all ordinary lobbyists.
* **Association:** Congress required an agency communication independently of privilege. Tribes assert interests against other water users, so their messages remain outside submissions even when Interior has a legal duty toward them.
* **Limits:** The identity of the communicating party alone does not settle every contractor or expert relationship. Conversely, confidentiality or privileged content cannot erase a failed statutory category. No adverse determination of tribal rights is necessary.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirming the category ruling defeats Exemption 5 for the seven communications and permits the district court to implement disclosure under any properly preserved, independently supported exemption. Reversing would require further consideration of the actual privileges and segregability; it would not automatically protect every page. A narrower disposition could turn on the demonstrated competing-claimant role without resolving whether all outside consultants can ever qualify.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The interested-claimant rule would exclude submissions advocating the sender’s own scarce-resource claim from the consultant extension of intra-agency communications. A broader trust rule could protect communications serving federal representation, but would need a statutory boundary distinguishing tribal claims from other represented private interests.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Ohio v. Reiner

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ohio v. Reiner
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 17; No. 00-1028
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-19 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a witness who denies participation in a crime may nevertheless possess a Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination, and whether a state judgment resting on a contrary federal premise can stand.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ohio Supreme Court affirmed reversal of Reiner’s conviction on the ground that an immunity grant to the babysitter was improper because her asserted innocence meant she had no Fifth Amendment privilege.
* **Relief Requested:** Ohio seeks correction of that federal premise and remand; Reiner seeks preservation of the judgment reversing his conviction.
* **Material Record:** An infant died after injuries attributed to shaking. The father was prosecuted, and the defense suggested the babysitter rather than the father caused the injuries. Medical timing evidence and the persons caring for the child made responsibility disputed. The babysitter denied wrongdoing but invoked the privilege before testifying under transactional immunity. The Ohio court treated that immunity as improperly conveying innocence to the jury. Neither a denial nor the grant itself establishes who injured the child. [Ohio v. Reiner](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-17.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A state-law immunity judgment may be reviewed when its reasoning necessarily rests on an erroneous interpretation of the federal privilege. The Court must distinguish correction of that premise from a holding that Ohio law required immunity, or that all independent grounds for a new trial fail.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically established as necessary to resolve this matter. Any actual intervening simulation decision must be checked before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The federal privilege protects answers presenting a reasonable danger of incrimination, including links in a chain of evidence. The inquiry concerns exposure, not whether the witness confesses guilt.

### Issue 1: Innocence and incriminating exposure

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Hubbell preserves protection for compelled testimonial incrimination and does not restrict the privilege to guilty witnesses. United States v. Hubbell concerns compelled production; it does not determine this witness’s entitlement to a particular state immunity grant.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Testimonial compulsion, incriminating tendency, and the adequacy of immunity present distinct inquiries. A defendant’s guilt and a witness’s reasonable exposure remain different matters.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth Amendment, applicable to state compulsion, and Ohio’s transactional-immunity law supply the distinct federal and state questions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hoffman v. United States requires a supported risk rather than imaginary exposure. Grunewald v. United States rejects an inference that innocent people cannot invoke the privilege.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Established law already rejects categorical denial of privilege based on professed innocence. The factual setting supports exposure because the defense identifies the witness as a possible perpetrator.
* **Open Point:** Whether independent state-law grounds invalidate immunity or require a new trial remains for the state court; it is not a newly open federal rule.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The state supreme court connected the witness’s denial of involvement to absence of a federal privilege, then concluded the immunity grant prejudiced the defense.
* **Ohio:** A witness facing a credible accusation can incriminate herself through apparently innocent answers. The federal premise is erroneous even if other grounds could support reversal.
* **Reiner:** The immunity procedure allegedly signaled the babysitter’s innocence and impaired his alternative-perpetrator defense; the state court may retain independent authority to remedy that problem.
* **Limits:** Recognizing a privilege does not compel a finding that immunity was proper under Ohio law, eliminate the defendant’s trial rights, or resolve causation of the child’s injuries.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the federal premise and remand for the state court’s remaining immunity and trial-fairness questions. An independently sufficient state ground, if actually established, would limit federal relief; the Court cannot manufacture one from possible state-law reasoning. Neither correction automatically reinstates a final conviction nor orders an acquittal.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An innocence-neutral exposure rule applies to witnesses facing reasonable incriminating risk, without recognizing a privilege for every speculative fear. It leaves state immunity authorization and the consequences for the accused separate.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# United States v. Hilario

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-03-19. **Date status:** organizationally assigned.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Hilario.

**Citation or Docket:** 218 F.3d 19.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review, organizationally assigned March 19, 2001 within OT 2000. No historical Supreme Court merits date or docket number is identified.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-19 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).

**Question Presented:** 1. Whether 28 U.S.C. § 546(d) permits a district-court-appointed United States Attorney to remain in office for more than four years while the vacancy remains unfilled by a presidential appointment?
2. Whether a United States Attorney is an inferior officer whose appointment Congress may vest in a court of law under the Appointments Clause?
3. Whether authorizing a federal district court to appoint the United States Attorney who prosecutes cases before that court violates the separation of powers?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses all three questions stated above.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit reversed dismissal of Hilario's indictment. The district court relied on statutory duration and did not reach the constitutional challenges. Hilario seeks restoration of dismissal; the United States seeks continued prosecution under a valid appointment.

**Relief Requested:** Hilario seeks restoration of the indictment's dismissal based on statutory expiration or constitutional invalidity of the appointment. The United States seeks affirmance and continuation of prosecution under a valid appointment.

**Material Record:** A Puerto Rico vacancy arose in May 1993. The Attorney General's interim appointee served the statutory 120-day period. On September 9, 1993, the district judges appointed career Justice Department lawyer Guillermo Gil. More than six years later no presidential nominee had replaced him. The appointment provision supplies no express four-year limit for this judicial appointment. The statutory office remains within Justice Department supervision; judges have appointment authority but no statutory authority to direct prosecutions or remove Gil. The President retains removal and the ordinary nomination route.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a preserved direct challenge to the authority supporting an indictment, not collateral reopening of completed prosecutions. Duration, officer classification, and interbranch appointment are distinct issues. The record establishes no actual judicial direction, bias, or improper prosecution.

**Relevant Dates:** First Circuit decision July 17, 2000, available before the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Other defendants' parallel challenges do not become separate briefs.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation concerning officer classification or remedy must be considered before adjudication.

### Issue 1: Duration of a district-court appointment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision imports a four-year cap into this judicial appointment route. The statutory issue must therefore be resolved from the operative enactment, without treating long service itself as constitutional validation or invalidation.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The appointment standards require a lawful source of appointment and distinguish appointment authority from subsequent assignment or supervision. They supply no freestanding power to revise an express statutory duration for institutional convenience.

**Other Governing Law:** The contemporaneous statute gives the Attorney General an initial 120-day appointment and allows a district-court appointment until the vacancy is filled. The ordinary presidential appointment provision separately states a four-year term and a holdover mechanism. Later amendments are not eligible law.

**Material Other Authority:** The First Circuit opinion reproduces the contemporaneous provisions and their application to Gil's appointment. It is the judgment under review, not a controlling Supreme Court construction.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The provisions must be read together while respecting their different appointment routes. Neither the ordinary four-year term nor the Attorney General's 120-day limit automatically governs a judicial appointment under distinct text.

**Open Point:** Whether the vacancy condition permits a judicial appointee to remain beyond four years when the President has not filled the vacancy through the ordinary appointment process.

### Issue 2: Inferior-officer status and judicial appointment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Edmond v. United States requires examination of the actual statutory hierarchy, supervision, removal, and review. No single factor classifies every office. Ryder v. United States preserves meaningful relief for timely substantial appointment defects rather than validating them by continued service.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The appointment and timely-challenge standards require a lawful appointing source and distinguish appointment from assignment. Actual authority and superior control matter more than an interim label or the mere significance of official duties.

**Other Governing Law:** The Appointments Clause permits Congress to vest appointment of inferior officers in courts of law. The United States Attorney remains within Justice Department supervision; the President retains removal and the ordinary nomination route. Judicial appointment supplies no statutory prosecutorial-supervision power.

**Material Other Authority:** Morrison v. Olson supplies surviving structural authority concerning interbranch appointment, within the limits of its holding. Edmond v. United States's military-adjudicator application does not itself classify every federal prosecutor.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law requires applying the hierarchy inquiry to this different office and respecting an express constitutional option for inferior officers. It does not authorize ongoing judicial control of charging or enforcement merely because judges made the appointment.

**Open Point:** Whether the United States Attorney's executive supervision and removal establish inferior status, and whether judicial selection without prosecutorial supervision nonetheless creates a constitutionally incompatible interbranch arrangement.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The First Circuit read “until the vacancy is filled” as controlling duration, found United States Attorneys inferior within executive supervision, and held that appointment alone did not give judges prosecutorial control. It rejected an as-applied challenge based solely on the unusually long tenure.

**Hilario's strongest position:** An indefinite appointment that outlasts the regular term can defeat nomination and confirmation in practice. Prosecutorial power is substantial, and judges choosing the prosecutor who repeatedly appears before them may compromise institutional neutrality even without express supervisory power. He requests a time limitation or invalidation of this appointment route.

**Government's strongest position:** Congress expressly chose different expiration conditions. Executive superiors control the work and can end the appointment; the President and Senate can fill the vacancy. The Constitution expressly allows courts to appoint inferior officers, and an appointment does not transfer the appointee's executive responsibilities to the judiciary.

**Limits and unreached grounds:** The government cannot use institutional convenience to supply authority absent from the text. Hilario must distinguish substantial duties from independence of superior control. The district court's statutory ground did not decide the constitutional questions, but the appellate court did. No actual-bias claim is established.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Merits paths:** Affirm on the distinct statutory text and the office's actual inferior status, with judicial appointment limited to selection and executive control preserved. Alternatively, find a statutory cap, or hold the office principal or the interbranch arrangement incompatible with separated powers. The latter alternatives require explaining why executive supervision and removal do not suffice.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A duration holding governs this statutory appointment route; it need not authorize judges to appoint unrelated officers or supervise enforcement. A constitutional defect would require an effective remedy for this timely challenge, with attention to who authorized the indictment and any properly preserved reauthorization question. It would not automatically void every prior prosecution. Affirmance permits proceedings to continue and decides no guilt. No factual basis supports converting institutional concern into a finding of actual partiality.

# Shafer v. South Carolina

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Shafer v. South Carolina
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 36; No. 00-5250
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-20 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a capital defendant must be allowed to inform the sentencing jury that a life sentence carries no parole when future dangerousness is placed in issue and, after an aggravating circumstance is found, the jury chooses only between death and life without parole.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The South Carolina Supreme Court affirmed Shafer’s death sentence, treating the State’s revised sentencing structure as outside Simmons v. South Carolina because a lesser term remained possible if the jury found no statutory aggravator.
* **Relief Requested:** Shafer seeks reversal of the penalty judgment and a lawful new penalty determination; the State seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** South Carolina’s procedure first requires a unanimous statutory-aggravator finding. If an aggravator is found, the jury selects death or life without parole; absent that finding the judge has different sentencing options. The jury asked about parole eligibility, but the court declined an accurate parole instruction and directed attention to the ordinary meaning of life. The prosecution presented evidence and argument that could bear on dangerousness, including misconduct and concerns about the defendant returning. Whether that presentation actually placed future dangerousness in issue was not resolved by the state supreme court. [Shafer v. South Carolina](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-36.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The jury’s decision stage matters: a sentence available to the judge if no aggravator is found is not necessarily an available alternative at the jury’s death-or-life selection. The federal claim was presented; a remaining dangerousness predicate and any applicable harmlessness inquiry require attention.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically established as necessary to resolve this matter. Any actual intervening simulation decision must be checked before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Sentencing relief does not disturb guilt. The tribunal must apply the governing direct-review error standard without importing a habeas standard from a different posture.

### Issue 1: Accurate parole information

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simmons v. South Carolina requires an effective opportunity to present accurate parole ineligibility when the State places future dangerousness in issue and the relevant noncapital alternative is life without ordinary parole. Its operative remedy and rejection of misleading general-life language control within those predicates. Ramdass v. Angelone preserves accuracy about actual and contingent eligibility.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Capital-sentencing law requires consideration of the actual sentencing alternatives and an effective defense presentation or adequate instruction. Neither a fixed script nor a guarantee about clemency is required.
* **Other Governing Law:** Due process and the applicable South Carolina sentencing statute govern. The constitutional informational rule addresses the jury’s legally available penalty choice.
* **Material Other Authority:** Skipper v. South Carolina reinforces meaningful rebuttal of dangerousness. The State’s statutory architecture must be identified before applying Simmons v. South Carolina.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Simmons v. South Carolina supplies the rule once its predicates are satisfied; the structural difference claimed here concerns when those predicates arise. Ramdass v. Angelone does not authorize false statements about present ineligibility.
* **Open Point:** Whether a conditional preliminary stage removes the informational right at the later death-or-life stage, and whether the dangerousness predicate was satisfied on this record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The state court treated the existence of a possible lesser judicial sentence anywhere in the process as sufficient to avoid Simmons v. South Carolina, and did not decide the disputed dangerousness predicate.
* **Shafer:** Once the jury finds an aggravator, its only lawful alternative to death is life without parole. Its question demonstrates the practical importance of information the court refused to supply.
* **State:** The revised procedure differs from the earlier binary regime; it also disputes whether the prosecution actually relied on future dangerousness. It can defend on that independent predicate without claiming a power to misstate the available punishment.
* **Limits:** A jury’s curiosity alone does not establish every Simmons v. South Carolina condition. Accurate ordinary-parole information need not promise that executive clemency or future legal changes are impossible.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the categorical statutory-structure distinction and remand for the unresolved dangerousness and prejudice questions. If those predicates and harmful error are established, set aside the death sentence for a new lawful penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital disposition. If the State did not place future danger in issue, Simmons v. South Carolina alone does not require relief.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The stage-specific rule applies whenever the jury reaches an actual death-or-life-without-parole choice after preliminary findings. It does not require parole instructions in every criminal trial or turn hypothetical earlier sentencing alternatives into the jury’s final options.

**Material Uncertainty:** The lower court left the dangerousness ground unresolved. The material record must support that determination; a refusal to answer the parole question cannot substitute for it.
