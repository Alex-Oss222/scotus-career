## Adversarial comparison of the grounds

The strongest ground for restoring the order is real rather than nominal: medically appropriate treatment may reduce danger during unavoidable staff encounters, and prolonged seclusion can impose medical and institutional burdens. Henderson expressly identifies those considerations and would use the ordinary confinement reasonableness and administrative-review standards. The source also establishes the treating psychiatrist's concession that the immediate separate room, limited access and continuous observation adequately prevented harm. The district court's safety finding did not reconcile that concession with an independently sufficient treatment necessity. Clinical appropriateness and the seriousness of the diagnosis cannot do that legal work alone.

Actual Riggins requires overriding justification and consideration of reasonable less intrusive means after objection; it does not make seclusion permanently preferable, prohibit medication whenever another conceivable measure exists, or demand a universal proof quantum. On this record, the unresolved adequacy, burdens and duration of containment prevent reinstatement on an assumed sufficient safety ground. The current order remains unavailable while a renewed supported justification is considered. Nothing finds Weston harmless in every future setting or requires staff to accept an actual danger not adequately controlled.

Competency restoration is an expressly stated Bureau purpose. The district court therefore could not remove it from the case merely because the medical reasons were not pretextual. For the six Associates joining ground B, Riggins's identified risks to comprehension, counsel communication, testimony and trial presentation require a preliminary individualized assessment of likely effects before authorization on this record. This is not the prediction of a completed trial with certainty. Available clinical information, possible dose or drug adjustments and realistic safeguards must be considered; actual effects and trial competence require later reassessment if treatment is lawfully authorized.

The contrary timing argument has force: some effects cannot be known until treatment occurs, and treatment may improve meaningful participation. Tatel expressly permits an initial likely-effects assessment followed by an actual-effects hearing rather than a perfect forecast. The six adopt that bounded distinction, not his every inference about the jury. An effective insanity defense does not invariably require displaying active untreated delusions. Recordings and psychiatric testimony may help, but their adequacy is a question, not a fact established here; neither can automatically repair impaired present communication or comprehension. Henderson's heat-of-passion analogy and confidence in explanatory testimony do not answer those additional capacities.

Scalia and Thomas instead rest on the deficient refusal-interest justification. Their actual Riggins dissent distinguished a direct liberty remedy from reversal of a completed conviction based on possible trial impairment. This case has a preserved prospective liberty claim, established unwanted treatment and no conviction. Thomas's additional objections there about involuntariness and preservation have no corresponding factual premise here. Applying the binding refusal-interest holding to set aside this unsupported authorization is consistent with those differences; it does not require adopting the additional prospective Sixth Amendment rule. Their judgment agreement must not be used to manufacture eight votes for B.

Kennedy's actual Riggins concurrence proposes an extraordinary showing against significant impairment of reactions, participation and counsel assistance. That stricter ultimate position necessarily rejects the present omission and supports a preliminary inquiry as a necessary minimum. It does not mean an ordinary likely-effects assessment alone satisfies his ultimate authorization requirement. The common ground leaves the complete restoration standard open, so his qualified join is direct and coherent without turning his added position into the Court's test.
