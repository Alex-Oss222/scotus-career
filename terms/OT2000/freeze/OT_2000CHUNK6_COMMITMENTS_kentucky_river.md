## Questions and governing baseline

All three petition questions are within the unrestricted grant: the professional-direction construction, the supervisory-exclusion burden and the actual six-nurse classification. Representation findings are reviewed through the refusal-to-bargain order. The Sixth Circuit's opinion is October 4, 1999; its judgment is January 27, 2000; rehearing was denied March 23. The political-subdivision ruling and rehabilitation-counselor inclusion are not reopened by these questions.

Health Care, May 23, 1994, controls in this timeline. It permits the Board to distinguish skilled patient-care direction from genuine statutory supervisory authority, requires an actual agency rationale and preserves every listed power as an alternative. Independent judgment may be professional or technical. Credentials do not establish supervision, and their professional source does not categorically prevent it. Possession of a listed authority may suffice even if exercised infrequently; hiring, firing, policy control and disciplinary sanction are not universally necessary.

Holly Farms requires independent identification of statutory boundaries before review of a permissible functional construction on the agency's actual rationale. Allentown distinguishes permissible policy from substantial evidence under the announced standard and forbids silently changing the proof requirement. It also preserves deference between fairly conflicting views. Greenwich Collieries addresses the proponent's persuasion burden without invalidating genuine statutory-exception or affirmative-defense allocations. None automatically disposes of this classification or transfers all unfair-labor-practice burdens to an employer.

## Component I: burden of proving supervisory exclusion

All eight provisionally uphold the Board's allocation to the party asserting supervisory status, here the employer, and reject the Sixth Circuit's contrary requirement that the Board prove the negative in this representation dispute. The NLRA expressly excludes supervisors from its broad employee category but does not prescribe the disputed allocation. The Board may reasonably require the proponent of that exception to establish the qualifying actual authority. The rule is not inherently employer-specific: a union or the General Counsel asserting supervisory status bears that same affirmative burden in the corresponding setting.

The contrary path treats protected employee status as part of the General Counsel's case and thus demands proof that each nurse is not a supervisor. That objection correctly preserves the General Counsel's burden to prove unfair labor practices. It does not require making the agency disprove every asserted exclusion in the antecedent representation classification. Greenwich's preservation of genuine defense burdens and Transportation Management's surviving allocation distinction answer the claim that every burden placed on an employer is inconsistent with the proponent rule. The present holding neither revives Greenwich's rejected production-only reading nor makes the employer bear proof of every unfair-labor-practice element. No new quantum or presumption is added.

## Component II: permissible distinction and forbidden shortcut

All eight accept this common operative boundary: the Board may distinguish ordinary skilled collaboration or routine implementation of prescribed care from actual authority to perform a listed supervisory function with independent judgment in the employer's interest. It may not exclude a direction from independent judgment merely because it rests on professional or technical skill, nor demand hiring or disciplinary power when assignment or responsible direction independently qualifies. Conversely, professional skill, higher rank, the building-supervisor title, patient-care responsibility or physical absence of managers does not automatically establish statutory supervision.

The supported alternatives are thus application of the permitted functional distinction and correction of a professional-category shortcut. Neither side may repeat the historical Health Care account in the petition as present law. The prior actual holding already permits functional distinction; it did not sustain every later classification, make the construction mandatory or validate every conceivable professional-judgment rationale. No reconsideration of Health Care is proposed by these commitments.

## Component III: agency rationale, record and remedy

**Enforcement path.** Reverse the nurses' exclusion from the unit and direct enforcement of the Board's existing order as to those employees. The regional director evaluated actual authority: prescribed staffing, voluntary retention and local calls for coverage, lack of power to compel attendance, ordinary incident reporting independently investigated by management, no effective recommendation of personnel action and continuing management availability. It considered the conflicting building-supervisor memoranda and found the demonstrated additional role to be obtaining coverage. Its final description of remaining work as medical services sits within those actual functional findings, not a categorical rule that professional judgment can never be independent.

The strongest objection is the contrary employer evidence about interunit reassignment, directions to write up noncompliance and authority to send an employee home. The agency cannot dismiss genuinely conferred authority merely because it has rarely been exercised. The enforcement path treats the director's supported findings as evaluating whether those paper descriptions reflected real delegated authority: any employee could report problems, reports were independently investigated, an actual report did not produce the claimed consequence, and established staffing procedures constrained discretion. Lack of firing power is a supporting fact within that broader inquiry, not a mandatory element. Allentown requires consideration of contrary evidence but does not permit replacing the agency's supported choice with the appellate court's preferred view. Chenery allows the rationale the director actually gave, without inventing an alternative.

**Agency-reconsideration path.** Reject the appellate burden ruling and its conclusive judicial declaration that the six nurses are supervisors, but withhold enforcement of the disputed nurse portion and remand for application of the correct criteria to the actual authority. The director's routine-coverage findings can support some conclusions; its treatment of other medical services and emphasis on absent discipline or nonexercise do not adequately resolve the particular evidence of assignment and responsible direction. The disputed memoranda and send-home testimony make it necessary to distinguish lack of authority from lack of recorded use and to determine whether clinical direction also exercises a listed power independently. Courts may not answer that question through a new agency rationale or their own classification.

The strongest objection is that this demands a pointless second proceeding where the agency already assessed the documents and actual roles. The response is confined to the unaddressed legal significance of possessed assignment or responsible-direction authority, not a demand that every agency decision recite a checklist or prove the negative. Health Care expressly preserves those independent routes. Allentown requires the real standard, and Chenery prevents the court from repairing a material omission by attributing a new rationale. The employer still bears its burden; remand is not a judicial finding that its evidence wins or that clinical responsibility inherently entails statutory supervision.

The two paths disagree about the adequacy and supported scope of the explanation actually given. They do not disagree about an automatic professional exemption, and they do not treat a court's characterization of Board policy as dispositive evidence of what the director did.

## Exact proposition coalitions and resulting procedure

Burden allocation: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer all support the proponent-of-exclusion rule and rejection of the Sixth Circuit's opposite premise.

General statutory rule: the same eight support preserving the functional distinction and all listed alternatives, allowing professional judgment to satisfy independence when attached to genuine supervisory authority, and rejecting both an occupational exemption and an automatic rule based on skill or title. Agreement at this level does not count as agreement about whether this agency decision used a forbidden shortcut.

Agency application and enforcement: Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find the existing actual rationale and findings sufficient and would enforce as to nurses. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas require the bounded agency reconsideration described above and would withhold enforcement of that disputed portion. All eight reject a judicial holding that every nurse, or conclusively these six nurses, must be a supervisor merely because care serves the employer.

The enforcement disposition reverses the nurse-exclusion portion and gives effect to the actual certified unit and existing order, with ordinary further appellate implementation. The alternate disposition vacates the nurse-classification portion, corrects the burden and returns the classification for agency determination; it does not affirm the appellate finding that these six are supervisors. Both preserve unrelated final components, impose no bargaining terms or monetary award, and do not order a new election by inference. No issue-specific Marks synthesis is needed: these are direct agreements and an identified application disagreement, not nested theories supporting a different existing judgment.

A separate position, if published, should explain the actual agency-rationale disagreement at depth: the supported routine-role findings versus the concern that possessed assignment/responsible-direction authority was treated as irrelevant because of nonexercise or professional character. It must state the common burden and statutory boundary and the exact enforcement or remand consequence. No author is assigned here.
