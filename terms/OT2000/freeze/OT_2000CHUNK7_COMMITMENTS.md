## Competing lawful paths by component

### Patent component

**Vacate and remand under an ordinary, rights-sensitive section 2 inquiry.** The valid patent and its scope supply a substantial lawful justification, and liability cannot arise merely because exercise of the exclusion right disadvantages rivals. But a court must still distinguish that protected exercise from a supported course of conduct that unlawfully maintains an independently proved service monopoly. The absence of fraud, sham litigation or tying does not make those three examples an exhaustive definition of possible commercial monopolization. Evidence concerning selective end-user access, termination of alternative channels and service-market effects may be relevant to that distinction; none establishes it by itself. The legal defense cannot remove the entire inquiry by labeling the inputs patented.

The strongest objection is that the only conduct alleged is precisely the withholding Congress protects, so further antitrust examination would make lawful exclusion contingent on a jury's assessment of motive. The response is to preserve lawful exclusion as a real limit: desire to exclude, rejection of a rival's request and effects inherent in the grant are insufficient. A plaintiff must identify and prove unlawful monopoly maintenance beyond those propositions, with rights, objective operation and innovation interests given their proper weight. A different antitrust market alone does not establish an extension beyond the grant.

**Affirm the patent judgment.** The competing rule protects unilateral refusal within a valid patent grant from liability founded solely on the refusal's competitive effects, subject to a distinct legally sufficient antitrust wrong. Section 271(d)(4) reinforces the patent owner's entitlement, and CSU presents no pleaded tie, procurement fraud or sham suit. On the lower court's account, the requested inquiry into exclusionary motive cannot transform that refusal into the missing wrong.

The strongest objection is that an exhaustive gate insulates objectively unlawful service-market maintenance and treats protected input control as an exemption from section 2. The affirming position answers that the claim must first identify conduct beyond the statutory exclusion itself; it does not immunize agreements, restraints exceeding the grant or independently actionable antitrust conduct. It regards the presented refusal as falling on the protected side of that line.

### Copyright component

**Vacate and remand without erasing the strong exclusion justification.** The lower court recognizes a presumption rather than the patent formulation, but its application rejects inquiry unless CSU shows unlawful acquisition or monopoly power extended beyond the grant. A new factual determination is not warranted merely because a plaintiff calls a reason pretextual. The reviewing court should nevertheless ask whether the legal definition of acceptable rebuttal improperly excludes objective proof of a distinct maintenance scheme. Lawful acquisition does not alone decide that question. The copyright interest deserves substantial protection; it is not an automatic factual answer to every properly proved section 2 claim.

The strongest objection is that the lower court already identified the correct strong justification and found no rebuttal, so vacatur would merely offer a second chance to an unsuccessful litigant. The response is limited to the rule applied: objective statutory scope and the actual competitive mechanism must be assessed without treating subjective motive as either sufficient or universally dispositive. The lower court remains free to find no genuine issue under that rule on the existing record. No particular jury finding, compulsory discovery or new evidence is required.

**Affirm the copyright judgment.** The competing view treats the lower court's strong presumption and its absence-of-rebuttal determination as a sufficient reason for summary judgment. A right holder need not repeatedly prove Congress's incentive judgment; refusal and hostility to a rival cannot by themselves rebut lawful exclusion. The strongest counterargument is that the court narrowed possible rebuttal too far. The affirming view would preserve independently proved conduct outside the grant, but finds no supplied evidence establishing that additional wrong here and no entitlement to motive inquiry alone.

## Law entering the event

Section 172(j)(1) caps product liability loss at the lesser of net operating loss and allowable product liability expenses; section 172(b)(1)(I) supplies the ten-year carryback. Regulations 1.1502-11(a), -12 and -21(f) construct consolidated taxable income and consolidated NOL from modified separate taxable income and group-level items. Separate taxable income omits items that would enter a complete separate-return loss calculation. Section 1.1502-79(a) allocates a consolidated loss to separate return years; its paragraph (a)(3) adjusts the separate taxable figure for that purpose.

Haggar's unanimous statutory-delegation rule requires fidelity to the enacted scheme and actual delegated authority before reasonable administrative choice receives controlling treatment. The register title "Delegated Customs regulations in tariff litigation" operates from April 21, 1999. ## Competing supported paths

**Group computation; reverse.** The initial consolidated product liability loss compares the consolidated NOL with aggregate allowable member product liability expenses. The current-return scheme provides a consolidated NOL, whereas positive separate taxable income is only a modified intermediate figure. Section 1.1502-79 supplies an allocation definition for a different task and does not, by its stated scope, impose a separate-member ceiling on this initial computation. Congress delegated ample power to adopt coherent consolidated-return limits, but the power to write a restriction does not establish its presence in the operative rules.

The strongest objection is that losses from an unrelated affiliate can make a profitable member's product liability expenses eligible for the special carryback, weakening the relation between the expense and that member's economic loss. The response is legal, not an assumption that the resulting tax policy is ideal: consolidating the NOL already combines member gains and losses, the statute retains its consolidated-NOL cap, and no operative provision adds the proposed member ceiling. The allocation mechanism cannot be moved to another function merely because it would supply a convenient number. Eligible expenses remain deductible only once, and the special carryback is a timing rule within its enacted limits.

**Member computation; affirm.** The competing construction reads the product-liability-loss exception as attaching first to the taxpayer that incurred the expense, using the section 1.1502-79 adjusted member loss to preserve a complete member-level account before aggregation. The strongest objection is that the regulations expressly confine that adjusted measure to separate-return allocation and supply no general member NOL for consolidated initial computation. A mere positive separate-taxable-income proxy is weaker still because it omits relevant loss items. This path therefore must defend an interpretive incorporation of the adjusted measure, not pretend that separate taxable income already is an ordinary NOL or that Treasury promulgated a new product-liability regulation.

No justice may solve the case by an unsupported finding of abuse, duplicate deduction or a concession to a final refund amount. No remand simply inviting Treasury to choose a new rule can determine liability for these old tax years.

## Entering law and available paths

Section 1962(c) distinguishes a person from the enterprise whose affairs that person conducts. Sections 1961(3) and (4) expressly encompass natural persons and corporations. Reves requires some role in directing enterprise affairs; Scheidler refuses an extra economic-motive limitation while preserving every enacted element; Holmes independently requires factual and proximate causation. Bestfoods preserves separate corporate personality while distinguishing liability for one's own conduct from derivative ownership liability. Its CERCLA operator test is not imported.

The reversal path treats the natural person and incorporated enterprise as legally distinct even when the individual owns the corporation and acts within corporate authority. Its strongest objection is that incorporation alone could make almost every owner's corporate wrong into a RICO claim. The answer is that legal distinctness supplies only this one element; direction, predicates, pattern, injury and causation must still be proved. The affirmance path demands additional functional separation or action outside corporate authority. Its strongest objection is that it inserts an unexpressed immunity for the people most capable of directing a corporate enterprise through racketeering. Copperweld's antitrust conspiracy inquiry does not justify that addition to a different statutory structure.

A later public defense of the classification cannot silently supply a procedure or legal status that the 1993 transaction lacked.

**Independent judicial interpretation with persuasive weight.** The strongest contrary answer accepts the public endorsement as real and still distinguishes the agency's institutional preference from a congressionally authorized interpretation controlling the court. **Tariff classification and disposition.** The representation supports attribution of the existing reasons to the Government's defended institutional position. ## Preservation and limits

The original freeze remains intact for provenance. This refresh replaces any suggestion that higher institutional public endorsement is unknown with the exact supported litigation-position fact. It does not convert unknown internal approval into a fact, impute an original signature to the Secretary or Commissioner, create a regulation, or retrofit later procedural law into the 1993 action. It changes no current law or prior adjudication. No fresh neutral blocker remains for the bounded issue; reconciliation must consider this refresh before treating Mead's non-Stone handoff as final.
