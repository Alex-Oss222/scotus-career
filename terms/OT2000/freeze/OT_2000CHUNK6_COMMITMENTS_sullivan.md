## Proposed action and bounded common rule

All eight Associates commit to granting the petition, vacating the Arkansas judgment on its stated federal rationale and remanding for proceedings under the controlling federal rules. They support a reasoned summary merits correction of that rationale, not an unexplained disposition that decides nothing and not a finding that every challenged intrusion was lawful. The merits judgment's coalition may be published; the internal certiorari poll may not.

An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by probable cause does not violate the Fourth Amendment solely because the officer also seeks evidence of a different offense. A state court cannot supply greater protection by assigning the Federal Constitution a meaning inconsistent with this Court's controlling interpretation. It may apply an adequately supported independent state-law protection, but none is invented to preserve this expressly federal rationale.

The stop does not authorize every later restraint or search. Atwater now extends Ricci's requirement of an actual enforcement function to added station transport and confinement for a known fine-only criminal offense. Such custody must reasonably serve supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence-protection or necessary-processing needs; the examples are not exhaustive, no such need is presumed, and neither least restrictive means nor a universal citation duty is required. The actual punishment and legal basis for Sullivan's asserted offenses must be addressed where material. No finding that each was fine-only, that the hatchet supplied a valid weapons arrest, or that identity could not be verified is made here.

Impoundment and an asserted inventory remain separately justified. Genuine inventory authority retains caretaking and standardized-practice limits under Bertine and Wells, as preserved in Whren. Investigative interest does not invalidate every otherwise lawful act, but evidence of an investigatory ruse can show that the inventory exception's own conditions are missing. Calling a policy an inventory does not prove its constitutional application. Knowles preserves the antecedent lawful-custody requirement and does not allow search fruits or the prospect of an incident search to establish arrest authority.

The remand permits adjudication of supported, preserved custody, impoundment, inventory and independent state-law grounds under the proper standards. It commands no new hearing, factual finding, admission of evidence, conviction or acquittal, and supplies no drug probable cause from the fruits found later. The Court does not decide every search-incident alternative absent a supported and preserved premise.

## Enhanced alternative-path test

Path A vacates and remands because the stated motive-only federal rationale and claimed state freedom to redefine controlling federal law are erroneous, while current law and the supported record leave independent grounds for consideration. It resolves the actual legal error without presuming whether those remaining grounds prevail.

Path B would reverse outright on the premise that traffic probable cause conclusively validates the arrest and search. Its strongest support is administrability and the objective nature of ordinary criminal enforcement. That premise exceeds Whren's initial-stop holding, conflicts with effective Atwater where its fine-only custody condition is met, and bypasses the inventory exception's separate requirements. A valid weapon charge or routine criminal-processing basis might affect the remaining analysis, but the packet does not establish them conclusively.

A further lawful possibility is affirming suppression because the purported inventory was an investigatory ruse. Its strongest support is the trial court's pretext characterization and the sequence after recognition of Sullivan. The difficulty is that the state court used pretext as an arrest rule rather than necessarily finding failure of a genuine inventory's distinct predicates. The complete policy and suppression findings would matter to a final inventory judgment. The bounded remand avoids adding that finding while retaining its possible legal relevance. No unavailable document is treated as a State failure to carry a historical burden.

## Procedural and remedial limits

The ongoing prosecution does not prevent review of the finally settled federal suppression ground under the packet's otherwise-evading-review route. No adequate independent state ground is established in the challenged reasoning. Grant plus a reasoned summary disposition is a proposed present action, not an imported historical grant or a fictional prior plenary submission. No merit is attributed to the internal petition poll.

The policy and complete suppression record would be material to a final inventory or evidence-admissibility adjudication. Their absence is expressly avoided by the supported legal-error remand, and does not block that disposition. Any stronger final application requires its own supported source and fresh affected analysis.
