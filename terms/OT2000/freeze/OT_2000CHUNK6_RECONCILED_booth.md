## Verified comparator and operative legal premise

The full official report, 532 U.S. 731–741, including every footnote, confirms historical affirmance, 9–0, with Souter writing for the unanimous Court and no separate writing. The historical Court required exhaustion of an available process capable of responding to the complaint even though it could not award the money demanded. Footnote 2 assumes for the issue on review that only damages remain; it does not conclusively determine that transfer mooted every other request. That limited assumption matches the frozen review question and changes no commitment.

The actual entering McCarthy decision rests on the absence of an express congressional mandate and the particular BOP process; Scalia and Thomas supported its result on the narrower absence-of-compensation ground. Congress's 1996 replacement of section 1997e(a), already admitted into entering law, removes that absence-of-command premise for covered actions. This is a statutory transition, not a silent overruling of McCarthy or a different current Court event. The historical and present statutory premises thus agree in the relevant respect despite differences in the earlier opinion topology.

## Final non-Stone commitments

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the Third Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal without prejudice. They join one common operative rule: a prisoner pursuing a covered action must complete an actually available administrative grievance process that can take responsive action concerning the complaint, even when it cannot award the monetary relief sought. Limiting the prayer to money does not make that process unavailable.

## Scope, response to the alternative and procedure

The strongest reversal argument is that a procedure unable to compensate a completed injury cannot provide the remedy the prisoner seeks. The enacted requirement concerns exhausting administrative avenues; the old discretionary and effectiveness limitations were removed, while investigative and corrective response remains possible here. Practical usefulness alone is not the holding. McCarthy's general statutory-primacy rule is applied, and its former damages-only conclusion remains within its surviving no-mandate setting.

All eight preserve the distinction between an imperfect responsive process and one that is nonexistent, wholly powerless or actually inaccessible. Allegations of cover-up or retaliation are not converted into a finding that officials obstructed all review. This decision does not prescribe the complete law of procedural default, proper exhaustion or every form of unavailability. Whether excessive force is generally a prison condition is outside the accepted question, although the lower court considered it. Section 1997e(c)(2) permits dismissal of a claim facially frivolous, malicious, failing to state a claim or seeking money from an immune defendant without first requiring exhaustion; that permission creates no general entitlement to nonexhausted merits review.

The dismissal without prejudice remains effective; neither constitutional wrongdoing nor damages is adjudicated. No new prison injunction, administrative award or prospective supervision is ordered. Every Associate joins the judgment and bounded reasoning; no partial join or separate function is needed. No material historical departure or unresolved merits blocker remains.
