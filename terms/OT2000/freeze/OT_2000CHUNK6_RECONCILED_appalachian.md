## Final common commitments by component

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each join all three components below and affirm the D.C. Circuit's judgment.

1. **Reviewability.** This settled national directive is final agency action under Clean Air Act section 307(b). EPA completed its position and applies it through the statutory permit-objection system, under which an objection prevents the state authority from issuing a permit. That present effect, not expense or influence alone, supplies the legal consequence. A disclaimer does not negate the operative commitment. The facial legal issue is ripe without an individual permit record; later permit review does not defeat the statutory route for this national action.
2. **Regulatory authority and procedural consequence.** Section 70.6(a)(3)(i)(B) supplies periodic monitoring where the applicable requirement lacks periodic testing or instrumental or noninstrumental monitoring, including qualifying recordkeeping. The required monitoring must yield reliable, representative compliance data and use terms, methods, units, averaging periods and other statistical conventions consistent with the applicable requirement; recordkeeping may suffice. Paragraph (A) incorporates applicable monitoring/testing and permits streamlining only with at least equivalent compliance assurance. Paragraph (C) supplies necessary use, maintenance and, where appropriate, installation requirements. Paragraph (c)(1)'s compliance-assurance requirement is expressly consistent with (a)(3). Together they do not authorize universal sufficiency review that replaces already prescribed recurring monitoring merely because EPA considers it inadequate. Missing periodic testing, unspecified frequency and a one-time startup test remain legitimate gap-filling cases. The challenged enlargement is a substantive amendment requiring the applicable rulemaking procedures, not a permissible reading within the existing condition.
3. **Remedy.** The challenged and remaining directions are intertwined, and the supported record gives substantial reason to doubt independent adoption of the remainder. Affirm whole-guidance vacatur on that record, without adopting a universal severability test. Existing emissions limits, monitoring requirements, the 1992 regulation itself and independently authorized stricter state requirements remain effective. No permit, frequency or substitute federal rule is ordered. The conditional challenge to the 1992 rule is unnecessary, and no other alternative attack becomes an independent holding.

## Actual entering-law test

Bennett requires completion and legal consequences separately. The earlier Whitman decision unanimously applies that distinction to a settled Clean Air Act implementation framework even before an individual sanction or plan determination. The directive here is not merely a prediction of influence: EPA's statutory objection power gives its completed position operative permit consequences. The result does not adopt all of the lower opinion's general commentary about guidance.

Auer's eight-Associate regulatory-interpretation rule and Thomas Jefferson retain controlling weight for a responsible, fair and considered interpretation that fits the regulation. Christensen's six-Justice regulatory-text holding forbids amendment of an unambiguous permission through interpretation; its five-Justice advisory-letter rule does not categorically classify this directive. All eight must therefore consider EPA's strongest reading on the merits. The program's compliance objective and 1992 commentary do not erase the precise absence trigger. The agency's contemporaneous promise to revise deficient federal standards through rulemaking corroborates that reading; it is not an estoppel or a prohibition on every change in interpretation. Whitman's requirement to preserve connected statutory commands reinforces the distinction but does not decide this regulation by analogy alone. Solid Waste supplies no general anti-guidance rule. Later Mead law is excluded.
