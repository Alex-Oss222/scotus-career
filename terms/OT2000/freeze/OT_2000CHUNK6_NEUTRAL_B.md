## Becker

The record supports a timely notice identifying the litigant, judgment and appellate court, a typed name but omitted manual signature, no earlier warning, a later signed brief and a prompt attempt to correct upon notification. Those are distinct facts; the later brief need not be treated as the original timely notice. The original form's lack of a signature direction does not itself repeal the signature requirement.

Control's freeze/OT_2000CHUNK6_NEUTRAL_BECKER_RULES.md supplies mechanically copied Civil Rules 5 and 11 and Appellate Rules 1, 3 and 4 from the 2000 Code appendix. Civil Rule 11(a) imposes a signature requirement and directs striking an unsigned paper unless omission is corrected promptly after notice. Its 1993 note permits signing the paper on file or submitting a signed duplicate. Rule 5(e) recognizes electronic filing and signing only under a compliant local rule; a typed name on this paper form is not shown to be such authorized electronic execution. No blanket holding about all electronic signatures follows.

Appellate Rule 1(a)(2) directs use of district-court procedure when the appellate rules provide for a district-court filing. Rule 3(a) distinguishes timely filing from other steps; Rule 3(c) identifies the notice's required information and protects against dismissal for informal form or title. These provisions are read together with the cure mechanism, without extending the statutory filing period. The 1993 Rule 3 naming amendment is not confused with the earlier Rule 3 text applied in Smith v. Barry.

The lower judgment is appellate dismissal for signature omission. Its correction does not decide exhaustion, pleading, constitutional liability or damages in the underlying prison case. A later failure to cure promptly after proper notice remains governed by Rule 11; it is not an established present fact.

## Booth

The supplied review assumes an accessible procedure capable of investigation and nonmonetary response, and incomplete administrative review. The 1996 amendment removed the former adequate-and-effective qualification and discretionary scheme and commands exhaustion of available remedies. McCarthy survives for statutory primacy and outside an express mandate. Its damages-only result does not itself construe the later availability language. The legally material alternatives are a process capable of responsive nonmonetary action and a requirement that the process furnish the relief the claimant seeks. A nonexistent or wholly powerless process, and a process actually obstructed, remain distinct from this record. The screening permission in section 1997e(c)(2) permits qualifying dismissals before exhaustion; it does not compel general merits review despite nonexhaustion.

## Buckhannon

The supplied record establishes closure orders, a lawsuit filed October 28, 1997, legislative repeal during litigation, mootness of the merits dispute and denial of fees under an exclusionary circuit rule. It does not establish that the suit caused repeal, that the former law was invalid, the amount of reasonable fees or any admission of liability. A fees determination after mootness is collateral and does not revive the merits controversy.

Farrar's enforceable nominal judgment is a sufficient eligibility route; the actual controlling opinion does not decide a categorical judicial-decree requirement. Its amount rule remains an explained Hensley inquiry, including zero where supported, with no mechanical proportionality rule. Its separate immediate-zero positions concern that case's demonstrated success, not the causation question here. Laidlaw supplies no catalyst holding, fee award or rule that fees prevent mootness.

Hewitt v. Helms, 482 U.S. 755, and Texas State Teachers Association v. Garland, 489 U.S. 782, are date-eligible primary opinions. Hewitt rejects favorable legal language without personal relief and expressly leaves the unresolved catalyst issue where the plaintiff received no benefit from the change. Garland separates actual benefit and material legal alteration from the degree of success. Neither alone decides the new question. The strongest competing constructions are a judicially sanctioned material alteration and legally grounded litigation materially causing concrete requested relief. A catalyst route would require actual benefit, causation and legal substance; timing alone, a nuisance payment or a purely moral victory would not suffice. Those predicates would remain for lower-court findings.

## Harris

The full question concerns six plaintiffs who were confined when this action was commenced on March 10, 1997 and released by the district judgment of June 24, 1998. The lower appellate decision is June 27, 2000. The Eleventh Circuit upheld the filing-time interpretation but required dismissal without prejudice of those six plaintiffs' monetary claims barred solely by section 1997e(e). The other five plaintiffs and other resolved components do not acquire relief merely because the six were released.

The appellate majority and dissent disagree about what release may do under Rule 15(d). The majority assumed an adequate supplemental pleading for the legal question; it did not find the rule's motion, notice and discretionary-permission requirements actually satisfied. The dissent's favorable account of the filing is argument, not a new binding fact. Any reversal permitting supplementation requires remand for the real procedural question rather than an automatic finding of proper supplementation.

The relevant text restricts a federal civil action brought by a confined prisoner for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without a prior showing of physical injury. The accepted temporal issue does not decide every category of constitutional damages, nominal relief, punitive relief, economic injury or prospective relief, or establish a physical-injury threshold for the underlying constitutional wrong. It supplies no adjudicated merits finding about the shakedown.

Additional controlling comparison: McNeil v. United States, May 17, 1993, unanimously requires the FTCA administrative prerequisite before instituting the original action and rejects a later-exhaustion cure even without substantial litigation. The prisoners' strongest distinction is that release removes a personal statutory disability without forfeiting a prelitigation administrative process; the officials' strongest reply is that both statutes select commencement and that Rule 15 cannot change a historical filing condition. Mathews v. Diaz, 426 U.S. 67, 75 and note 9, allowed a supplemental pleading reflecting a benefits application filed after commencement; its separate exhaustion issue involved the Secretary's stipulation and waiver. The application condition and waivable exhaustion are not collapsed. Rule 15(d) permits, rather than invariably requires, supplementation even where the original pleading was defective. Semtek preserves the Rules Enabling Act boundary; Artuz distinguishes genuine filing conditions from claim-relief bars and requires examination of the particular text. Neither decides the section 1997e(e) temporal question.

Randell's bounded favorable-invalidation exception turns on realistic prior review opportunity, not release alone. It does not erase an express PLRA condition or supply a finding of helplessness, diligence or misconduct in Harris. Whether a newly filed postrelease case is timely or precluded is outside the record. Dismissal without prejudice supplies no tolling determination or promise of later success.

## Kentucky River

The petition and appendices materially enlarge the factual basis for the agency-rationale question. The court of appeals issued its opinion October 4, 1999, then judgment January 27, 2000; rehearing was denied March 23, 2000. The packet's reference to a 1999 appellate judgment is refined accordingly. The petition presents all three questions: the Board's professional-direction construction, the supervisory-exclusion burden, and application to the six registered nurses. The provided unrestricted grant includes them; no application question is excluded merely because a rule question might be easier.

The regional director's February 28, 1997 decision appears in Appendix D, with the Board's March 21 denial of review in Appendix C and the July 10 unfair-labor-practice order in Appendix B. The agency did not rest on credentials alone. Its findings state that staffing levels were prescribed; replacements were first sought through volunteers and then an established local-call list; nurses could not compel attendance under threat of discipline; reports were independently investigated and could be submitted by any employee; nurses did not evaluate other employees or effectively recommend personnel actions. The director considered both somewhat conflicting building-supervisor memoranda and found that the demonstrated extra task was obtaining needed coverage. The label did not itself settle actual authority. These are the agency's findings, reviewed under the whole-record standard, not new findings by this Court.

The employer's contrary position rests on the building-supervisor memoranda, direction of medication and staff, transfers between units and nurses' place as the highest on-site employees. Lack of hiring, firing or disciplinary power is not itself dispositive: assignment or responsible direction may independently suffice, and possession matters even when exercise is infrequent. The two versions of the memorandum do not authorize assuming one was definitively operative. On-call managers' continuing availability is relevant to actual delegation; it does not automatically negate all on-site supervisory power.

The agency's analysis expressly invokes genuine management prerogatives, considers actual authority and routine tasks and then describes other duties as typical medical services. That actual rationale supports the enforcement path under the entering Health Care holding. The competing path treats the final medical-services characterization as excluding potentially independent responsible direction without examining it sufficiently. This is a concrete rationale dispute; it cannot be resolved merely by asserting that litigation counsel adopted a categorical professional exemption or that the Court can invent replacement findings.

The Health Care rule permits a functional distinction but neither mandates a professional exemption nor excludes professional judgment from independent judgment. Allentown requires review under the standard actually announced and on the whole record, retaining respect for fairly conflicting views. Greenwich Collieries preserves the distinction between the proponent's persuasion burden and lawful exception or defense burdens. Representation proceedings determine the disputed exclusion before the refusal-to-bargain proceeding; the General Counsel retains its distinct unfair-labor-practice burden. The NLRA does not itself expressly assign the supervisory-exclusion burden.

The separate political-subdivision ruling and inclusion of rehabilitation counselors are not reopened by these questions. Enforcement as to nurses gives effect to the actual certification and bargaining order; it supplies no new wage terms, backpay, individual-liability finding or occupational classification beyond the record. A remand path must specify that the employer bears the exclusion burden and that no present judicial finding makes the six nurses supervisors.
