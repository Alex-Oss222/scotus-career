## What the completed Ferguson decision actually supplies

Ferguson rejects the special-needs justification for the public hospital's systematic collection and testing of urine under the arranged disclosure, evidence-preservation and arrest program, assuming legally insufficient consent. The actual coordinated prosecution operation lacked an adequate independent justification. Genuine clinical work, a health objective and the program's clinical origin were acknowledged; those features did not themselves justify that operation. The ruling reverses the special-needs affirmance and returns the unreviewed consent challenge for appellate consideration. It neither finds final liability nor invalidates the jury verdict on facts the appellate court had not reviewed.

The Court does not adopt a general immediate-versus-ultimate-purpose rule or disqualify every mixed-purpose program. Chandler requires an actual, adequate operational need; Edmond requires an independently lawful justification for the ordinary evidence operation; Vernonia preserves both bodily-search protection and the significance of the actual nonpolice setting. Griffin remains valid for supported probation supervision and enforcement, including its conditional-liberty relationship and regulation. No universal custody requirement, emergency requirement, quantified program-failure showing or exhaustion of all alternatives is announced.

The trial instruction separating collection, testing and disclosure permissions is not made a universal constitutional formula. Disclosure alone is not held to constitute a separate search, and medical attendance is not a waiver. True emergency treatment, materially different authorized clinical programs and mandatory reporting are outside the decision. Personal participation, municipal attribution, immunity, causation, injury and relief remain separate. No automatic damages, injunction or new evidentiary hearing follows.

Kennedy's controlling opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer among the Associate Justices. Scalia's published dissent, joined by Thomas, treats the actual clinical function that preceded police cooperation and the genuine protective purpose as independently sufficient against the intrusion. It does not rely on a merely nominal protective label, classify ordinary patients as probationers, decide consent, or adopt an abandonment or third-party-disclosure theory. Its treatment-before-arrest disagreement does not erase the initial arrests. These published positions identify a real disagreement about functional justification; neither supplies an automatic answer for child protection.

Applied neutrally here, Ferguson requires attention to what the particular examination accomplished and what actually authorized its bodily intrusion. It does not establish that every examination ordered during protective custody is a prosecution program, that every protective purpose is sufficient, or that custody transfers all parental decision-making authority. A claimed child-protection or medical justification must be supported on this record and fitted to the act at issue.

## Separate questions the record presents

**Removal and continued separation.** The children's seizures and the parents' family-association interests must remain distinct, even where the lower court used the same emergency standard. No actual removal order existed. Whether CPS told officers otherwise, what individual officers knew, whether CPS requested removal, and how the accounts changed remain contested. The City did not independently argue below that mistaken reliance on an asserted order alone supplied lawful authority; the lower court reserved that legal question. Neither an asserted order nor a phone-verification practice may be converted into an adjudicated fact about a particular communication.

The reported threat concerned Jessie and a specified date. There was no corresponding allegation that Lauren would be harmed or that Becky participated. The aunt's disclosed psychiatric condition, prior unfounded report, conflicting accounts of the therapist conversation, investigation actually performed, and the children's observed condition matter to objective urgency. Their significance is disputed; a preflight cannot settle credibility. The scope and duration of separation require their own justification, including the separation from the unimplicated mother and the period after the asserted danger. Possible protective arrangements with her bear on scope without establishing a universal duty to exhaust every conceivable alternative.

Troxel protects a fit parent's direction of care and rejects displacement based solely on a judge's preferred arrangement. It supplies no controlling comprehensive custody or emergency rule, universal parental-unfitness prerequisite, harm threshold, burden of proof or scrutiny level. McArthur supplies particularized justification, actual urgency, limited scope and attention to duration and judicial authorization; its residential preservation holding does not itself authorize removal or a later bodily search. Limited permission for one act cannot be expanded into permission for another. Becky's permission to enter the home is not consent to removal or examinations.

**Examinations, consent and presence.** The invasive anal and vaginal examinations occurred about three days after removal. The source reports no parental consent, notice or prior judicial authorization. It identifies the City as requesting agency and testimony about the detective's role and a hospital contract; the significance and municipal sufficiency of that evidence are disputed. It does not establish urgent medical need, imminent loss of material physical evidence, parental waiver, a valid substitute-consent determination or a judicial order authorizing these examinations. No such predicate may be supplied by inference from custody alone.

The lower court's investigative-examination rule requires notice and judicial approval in the absence of consent, except for a reasonable concern that material physical evidence might dissipate or an urgent medical problem requiring immediate attention. Its parental-presence rule allows exclusion from all or part of the procedure for a valid reason, with a waiting room or other nearby location instead. Those are lower-court rules presented for review, not holdings already compelled in full by Ferguson or Troxel. The right to decide, the opportunity to object, presence for comfort and decisions, and the children's own bodily interests are distinct. A result concerning one does not automatically decide all. Denius concerns justified access to objectively intimate information in public employment; it is not permission for physical examinations and creates no universal strict-scrutiny or least-intrusive-means test.

**Municipal attribution and preservation.** Carter preserves separate proof of a federal deprivation, the City's own policy or legally effective custom, the applicable municipal fault, and actual causation. An omission theory requires the deliberate-indifference, notice and identified-deficiency inquiry; that formulation must not silently replace the analysis of an alleged affirmative policy. The requesting-agency entry, contract evidence and officers' testimony support arguments about attribution, not an established Monell finding. Cooperation does not make county personnel or the physician City employees, and federal liability is not respondeat superior.

Rymer disputes whether an independent examination-policy claim was ever pleaded, argued or supported against the City. The majority draws a contrary evidentiary inference. The grant of review does not cure preservation, amend the complaint, restore dismissed defendants or resolve this disagreement. The independent examination-policy route must therefore be distinguished from examination injuries as consequences of a proved unlawful removal policy. Lower note 15 expressly declines to require a second independent policy as a prerequisite to recovery for examination injuries. That preserves a possible causal route, not an award or a finding that every later injury was caused by the removal. A procedural rejection of the separate policy theory does not itself extinguish that different consequential-injury question.

Continued detention after the juvenile hearing presents an additional causal stage. The later medical report, judicial intervention and other actors neither automatically cut off nor automatically establish City responsibility. The lower court left that inquiry for remand. The initial removal, prehearing examinations and posthearing detention must not be treated as one undifferentiated municipal act.

**Immunity and state law.** Only the City remains in this appeal. It cannot borrow an officer's personal federal immunity or a state immunity against federal constitutional liability. Denius preserves the objective, particularized conduct-date notice inquiry for personal damages; neither Ferguson in 2001 nor any other later holding supplies notice in September 1991. There is no occasion to adjudicate a restored individual claim or invent an officer's immunity defense here.

The state tort route is different: state respondeat superior and derivative employee immunity may apply under their own terms. Section 820.2 protects the relevant discretionary investigation functions but does not settle whether CPS enlisted these officers, whether a challenged act was discretionary, or which intentional torts fall outside it. The lower court leaves those questions open. Its rejection of the section 821.6 argument rests on the reply-only presentation and the City's disclaimer of participation in the dependency prosecution; this preflight does not revive it. Section 324.5 was enacted in 1998 and supplies no 1991 authorization or waiver.

## Supported resolution paths and remaining limits

The sources permit an independent model to evaluate: a fact-sensitive removal and scope remand; a narrower ruling correcting municipal-immunity or attribution errors without deciding every examination rule; adjudication of a preserved independent examination claim under its actual protective or evidentiary justification; or a preservation-limited disposition leaving causally attributable examination injuries within a surviving removal claim. A defense summary judgment would require a legally sufficient ground supported under the proper Rule 56 view, not resolution of the recorded factual disputes against the family. Survival of summary judgment establishes neither a completed constitutional violation nor City liability.

A model may test the lower court's full investigative-examination and parental-presence rules against the narrower holdings above without assuming that all mixed-purpose care is forbidden or all protective care authorized. Any emergency, valid exclusion reason, consent, delegated medical authority or statutory exception used to sustain these acts needs actual record support. A statutory substitution theory would require the applicable 1991 text and the facts triggering it; the 1998 enactment cannot fill that gap. None is needed merely to decide whether existing disputed evidence defeats summary judgment or whether a theory was preserved.

Under Engine section 14, the authenticated lower recitation is sufficient to present these bounded questions. There is no general source blocker requiring reconstruction of the whole record. Exact disputed communications, preservation, municipal causation and any claimed authorizing predicate remain issues for ordinary adjudication within the surviving claims. No missing source proves default, supplies consent or warrants an unrestricted new evidentiary opportunity.
