| Question | Denius's actual rule and limit | Consequence for Urofsky |
|---|---|---|

| Actual employment justification | Denius requires adequate justification for the actual burden and preserves pertinent, confined inquiries. An undisclosed screening intention cannot narrow the written authorization. Both decisions require attention to what the condition actually imposes. |
| Public approval records | Denius protects covered independent consultation and objectively intimate personal content. Its acquired-information rule is bounded by those interests. | Studying sexual subjects does not itself establish that a proposed research description reveals the scholar's own intimate personal or family affairs. Its written and publicly available approval requirement remains a First Amendment burden under II; Denius supplies no separate holding invalidating that clause or enlarging the questions granted. |

It changes no Court seat, recusal, review channel or Urofsky filing. | Nothing in that judgment alters Urofsky's all-nine participation, summary-judgment posture, questions, amended statute or ordinary remand procedures. |

## Published-position continuity

The published positions reveal no additional displaced premise. O'Connor's Denius join concerns an unrestricted condition without an established employment reason for the independent consultation demand. Her Urofsky writing assumes a scholarly interest and finds justification in the narrowed statutory coverage, actual misuse concerns and approval practices. That distinction is compatible with her supplied NTEU position and Denius's express refusal to impose a universal higher test. Urofsky treats favorable administration as relevant evidence, not a substitute for the enacted required-use limitation, both approval branches, or written public permission.

Scalia and Thomas's Denius objection concerns the constitutional source for extending consultation and acquisition protection. Their published Urofsky writing separately disputes the claimed resource entitlement and supplies an independent assumed-interest employment balance. Neither position rests on a universal rule that public employment defeats every independent constitutional right. The scholarly-interest and remand opinions likewise remain compatible with Denius's recognition that a right's existence and the prerequisites for relief are distinct. These are comparisons of already published grounds, not new determinations of any Justice's vote or join.
