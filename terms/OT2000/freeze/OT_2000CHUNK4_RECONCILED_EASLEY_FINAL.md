## What the historical writings actually establish

Historically Breyer wrote for Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg to reverse the judgment. Their ground was clear error in the finding that race, rather than politics, predominated. They distinguished voter registration from actual voting behavior, examined the expert analyses, competing political objectives, proposed alternatives and direct statements, and concluded that the supporting racial-purpose evidence did not carry the demanding historical burden in the correlated race/politics setting. Their concluding alternative-plan requirement was tied to that predominance theory; they did not purport to adjudicate every other independent districting claim.

Thomas dissented, joined by Rehnquist, Scalia and Kennedy. He treated predominance as a factual finding subject to ordinary Rule 52 deference, rejected a more searching review because the case lacked an intermediate appellate court or used documentary and expert evidence, and regarded the direct and circumstantial evidence as permitting the trial court's conclusion. He did not claim that he necessarily would have weighed every fact the same way as the trial judges. His notes reject an added significant-racial-balance burden, preserve the distinction between first-instance weighing and review, and identify limitations in the majority's treatment of the alternatives. The disagreement was substantial; it cannot be reduced to one side favoring race-conscious districting and the other opposing it.

Both historical writings nevertheless assumed that proved racial predominance subordinating ordinary criteria supplied the relevant constitutional trigger. Both also assumed a valid earlier mandate directing adjudication of that claim. Those are the two material premises displaced here. The actual Court's Shaw/Miller/Vera rule rejects predominance alone as the asserted injury, and actual Hunt expressly refused a predominance-only trial while permitting only actually presented independently cognizable claims. Rule 52 cannot supply a cause of action that those decisions reject.

## Actual law and the two remands

The frozen A path is substantively supported. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the actual governing boundary and actual Hunt's confined mandate. Even a fully accepted predominance finding would not sustain this injunction on that basis alone. Actual Miller already demonstrates the distinction: it accepted a supported predominance finding while rejecting its claimed constitutional consequence. The historical majority's factual reversal therefore is not a necessary additional holding for these Justices. They may leave the factual conflict unresolved where its resolution cannot cure the legal defect.

The strongest objection to A is that a completed trial has now replaced the earlier summary-judgment record. That changes the evidentiary posture, but not the premise of the actual mandate. The trial findings are authenticated objective recitations and can remain useful if a properly presented claim makes them material. They do not show that the parties or court conducted the independent-claim adjudication authorized in this timeline. Conversely, their failure to establish such proceedings does not prove that all independent claims were absent or forfeited. A final universal validation of the plan would need that additional premise. A legal vacatur of the existing predominance-based judgment does not.

The B path remains a genuine contrary constitutional choice. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas previously joined Kennedy's actual Hunt separate writing to retain predominant racial assignment as a scrutiny trigger. They may expressly seek reconsideration of the governing boundary. They must confront the majority's distinction between a shared territorial electorate and actual exclusion, dilution, unequal participation or inferior civic status, and they must acknowledge that their trigger is proposed law. No one can describe it as already binding simply because it was the historical rule.

The B supporters' present vacatur and proposed remand require particular explanation. Historical Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would have sustained the trial finding and opposed the factual reversal; historical O'Connor reached the opposite factual conclusion. The final reconciliation does not erase those differences or make all four agree that race predominated. Their common ground here is to restore the claim as legally available and obtain a disposition that openly addresses its status after the actual Hunt mandate.
