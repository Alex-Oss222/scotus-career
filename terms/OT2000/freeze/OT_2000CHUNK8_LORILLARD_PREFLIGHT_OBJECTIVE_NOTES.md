# Lorillard / Altadis: objective party-source notes only

**Status: Uncertified.** A boundary hold was received before any final neutral packet was written. These notes preserve source-attributed observations only. They are not a neutral-framing release, a preservation determination, a merits assessment, or authority for an adjudication. A fresh context must validate any later handoff. The source cutoff used was June 28, 2001.

Source abbreviations refer to the saved party-body copies: CIGARETTE, CIGAR, CIGAR_REPLY, SMOKELESS_CLEAN, and STATE, each with the prefix `OT_2000CHUNK8_LORILLARD_SOURCE_` and suffix `.md`. Citations below use the filings' printed pages.

1. **Separate retail challengers.** CIGARETTE p. 5 n. 2 says all four cigarette manufacturers challenge section 21.04(5)(a), (b) on preemption and First Amendment grounds, and identifies Brown & Williamson and Lorillard as the cigarette challengers to section 21.04(2), on First Amendment grounds only. SMOKELESS_CLEAN p. 2 n. 2 independently identifies USSTC's challenge to sections 21.04(2)(c), (d), and 21.04(5), while its accompanying text describes separately maintained claims. Thus the cigarette filing's limitation does not establish that USSTC has no retail challenge. SMOKELESS_CLEAN pp. 5–6 describes product comparison, freshness and price information as its asserted expressive interest. CIGAR pp. 23–24 n. 9 and CIGAR_REPLY p. 10 n. 7 separately develop the cigar-handling information theory; STATE p. 47 n. 26 argues that physical product placement does not implicate the First Amendment and invokes theft prevention.

2. **Conditional smokeless nonseverability.** SMOKELESS_CLEAN p. 4 n. 5 says USSTC argued below that, if cigarette regulations are federally preempted, their smokeless application should also be invalidated because the applications cannot be severed. This is a conditional argument, not a claim that the federal cigarette statute directly covers smokeless tobacco on identical terms. STATE p. 16 n. 7 characterizes the noncigarette petitioners as making no federal-preemption claim; the USSTC footnote must remain separately attributed rather than erased by that description. The regulatory severability clauses do not themselves establish the answer to the conditional argument.

3. **Warnings and small signs have different procedural histories.** CIGAR pp. 2–3 n. 1 reports that the court of appeals accepted the Commerce Clause objection to sections 22.04 and 22.05 and that the Attorney General did not seek further review. SMOKELESS_CLEAN p. 4 n. 5 reports that the district court's section 21.04(6) preemption and smokeless nonseverability determination was not appealed by the Attorney General and was not at issue in the court of appeals. STATE p. 9 n. 5 describes both chapters' small-text-sign provisions and reports that the district court held the provision preempted without an appeal by the Attorney General. Mere reproduction of these regulations does not establish a live merits challenge to each provision or restore an already displaced permission.

4. **Sampling and premiums.** CIGAR p. 2 expressly identifies retail cigar sampling under section 22.06(1)(a) as at issue. No sampling-specific developed constitutional argument was located in the five complete party bodies read; STATE discusses sampling generically among youth-access measures and describes earlier cigarette-preemption challenges to samples and mail distribution (pp. 11, 18 n. 8, 47 n. 26). This does not establish abandonment, inadequate preservation, or a merits answer. No developed current challenge to premium or branded-merchandise restrictions, or explicit statement proving an unappealed premium ruling, was located in these five bodies. Their inclusion in the reproduced regulations is a text source, not proof of review scope.

5. **Geographic maps are contested evidence descriptions.** CIGARETTE p. 4 attributes 87–91 percent coverage in three major cities to the advertising prohibition; CIGAR pp. 6, 21 similarly relies on extensive urban exclusion. STATE p. 10 disputes that interpretation of JA 164–167: it says the percentages aggregate the school/playground zones and other areas unavailable for advertising for separate reasons, including residential areas, colleges, cemeteries and parks. It says the affidavit does not isolate the area occupied by the new zones alone. These are competing descriptions of the survey; these notes do not resolve the map, quantify the incremental coverage, or adopt either description as a judicial finding.

6. **Summary-judgment assumption and contested preclusion.** STATE p. 35 n. 17 expressly denies a general concession that the advertising concerns lawful activity and is nonmisleading, says the issues were not stipulated or adjudicated, and describes an assumed premise for its own summary-judgment motion while claiming that trial proof could otherwise remain available. CIGARETTE p. 4 n. 1 and SMOKELESS_CLEAN pp. 6–7, 16 n. 13 describe a summary-judgment concession. CIGAR_REPLY p. 2 n. 1 accepts that the State's own motion would not itself foreclose a later challenge, but argues that its failure to contest truthfulness when opposing petitioners' motion would. The motions and any resulting preclusion have not been independently adjudicated by this source review.

7. **Product-specific evidence and later lodging.** STATE pp. 4–7 relies on studies, health-authority reports, longitudinal material and historical campaigns; p. 39 n. 19 separately invokes smokeless marketing history and the NCI cigar material. CIGAR pp. 9–19 and CIGAR_REPLY pp. 1–9 dispute extrapolation from cigarette evidence, emphasize limited outdoor cigar advertising and distinguish cultural publicity from the restricted advertisements. SMOKELESS_CLEAN pp. 19–22 disputes the causal force, currency and product relevance of the submitted material and points to declining reported youth use. STATE p. 4 n. 1 expressly distinguishes material submitted below from the Surgeon General's 2000 report and other evidence published since submission to the court of appeals, included in its lodging. Later lodging is not automatically a finding below. The study contents and competing interpretations remain attributed to the filing parties.

8. **Alternatives and settlement coverage.** Petitioners propose stronger sales enforcement, licensing, counteradvertising and controls on purchases or possession by minors; STATE pp. 7–8, 47–49 describes existing measures and argues that restricting supply does not answer advertising-driven demand. STATE p. 42 n. 22 limits the cigarette settlement's binding force to signatories. SMOKELESS_CLEAN p. 6 n. 7 says Massachusetts did not join USSTC's separate settlement, so its outdoor-advertising terms do not apply there; CIGAR_REPLY p. 11 says cigar manufacturers were not parties to the cigarette settlement. No single settlement baseline should be attributed to all three products from these filings.

The existing regulatory text, amendment instructions, source copies and original neutral refresh were left unchanged. No final validated Lorillard packet was produced by this review.
