# Calcano-Martinez v. INS

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Calcano-Martinez v. INS

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-1011; 533 U.S. 348.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the 1996 immigration amendments permit lawful permanent residents removable for aggravated-felony convictions to obtain judicial consideration of a legal objection to their exclusion from discretionary relief, either by direct appellate review of their removal orders or by district-court habeas corpus. This is a reconstruction of the merits issue from the judicial record, not a purported verbatim grant order.

* **Question Granted:** The direct-review and preserved-habeas questions described here; the merits of each petitioner's waiver request are distinct.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 232 F.3d 328, dismissing the consolidated direct-review petitions for lack of jurisdiction while recognizing that the legal claims could be pursued in district-court habeas proceedings.

* **Relief Requested:** Petitioners seek a judicial forum to challenge the Board's legal determination that they cannot apply for former §212(c) relief. The government defends dismissal and argues that the statutory review restrictions also foreclose the asserted habeas route.

* **Material Record:** Deboris Calcano-Martinez, Sergio Madrid, and Fazila Khan are lawful permanent residents subject to administratively final removal orders. They concede removability based on convictions falling within the relevant aggravated-felony provisions. Each filed both an appellate review petition and a district-court habeas petition. Their disputed proposition concerns statutory eligibility for discretionary waiver, rather than a demand that a court exercise the Attorney General's discretion favorably. The present record does not establish that all three petitions share identical plea dates, application dates, custody arrangements, or waiver equities. Those details cannot be supplied by analogy to INS v. St. Cyr. [Calcano-Martinez v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-348.pdf).

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The conceded statutory predicates make this an ordinary-review channel case, not a dispute about citizenship or whether an offense qualifies. The government's recognition of some predicate and constitutional review does not itself resolve the pure statutory question. Habeas requires its own jurisdictional predicates, appropriate respondent and forum, and legally applicable exhaustion rules. Dismissal of appellate petitions need not extinguish separately filed district-court actions. A preserved legal claim is different from appellate reconsideration of facts or discretionary balancing.

* **Relevant Dates:** AEDPA was enacted April 24, 1996; IIRIRA September 30, 1996, with the relevant permanent review system generally effective April 1, 1997. Argument occurred April 24, 2001.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** INS v. St. Cyr concerns closely related jurisdiction and retroactivity questions but remains a separately listed matter.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** INS v. St. Cyr shares this calendar date but follows this matter in the assigned order. Its later disposition cannot govern this case unless the effective order of decisions changes.

### Issue 1: Allocation of direct and habeas review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Kolster v. INS permits withdrawal of ordinary appellate review on conceded criminal-removability predicates while preserving independent habeas review of substantial legal or constitutional objections to the government's authority. Reno v. Goncalves confirms that a legal exclusion from eligibility is reviewable even though the eventual grant of relief is discretionary. Reyes-Hernandez v. INS distinguishes the specifically named commencement, adjudication, and execution acts from a universal jurisdictional prohibition covering every completed-order challenge.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The immigration-transition standards require identifying the actual statutory regime and preserving the distinction between direct review, habeas legality review, and discretionary relief. Habeas cannot be assumed available merely because another channel closes, but an independent habeas grant is not silently repealed by general review language.

* **Other Governing Law:** The version of §1252(a)(1) governing final removal orders and §1252(a)(2)(C)'s criminal-removability limitation, together with §2241, must be read as an integrated allocation of remedies. [Calcano-Martinez v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-348.pdf).

* **Material Other Authority:** Felker v. Turpin preserves the importance of the distinct habeas jurisdictional source; it does not resolve all immigration procedure.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Kolster v. INS and Reno v. Goncalves reject treating eligibility as unreviewable discretionary balancing. They do not automatically convert an appellate petition into habeas, establish custody in every individual case, or grant immigration relief.

* **Open Point:** The precise effect of the permanent IIRIRA review provisions on these petitions and the extent of any remaining appellate jurisdiction over other categories of claims.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Second Circuit treated the criminal-removability provision as eliminating direct review of these petitions but not §2241 review of legal claims. It distinguished eligibility from favorable exercise of discretion. That reasoning is reviewable as lower-court analysis, and does not independently establish controlling Supreme Court law.

**Petitioners:** Congress should not be understood to have left an admitted resident without any court capable of correcting an unlawful eligibility rule. General restrictions on review do not clearly eliminate the independent habeas grant. Their preferred forum may be district court; alternatively, if habeas were eliminated, ordinary review must be construed to preserve the legal claims. They need not prevail on waiver equities to show the need for a lawful eligibility determination.

**Government:** The permanent review statute concentrates immigration challenges in a specified system and expressly removes judicial review for the conceded criminal category. A litigant should not circumvent that restriction by relabeling review as habeas. The eligibility issue concerns a discretionary form of relief, and Congress can narrow judicial intervention. Its concessions about threshold predicates and substantial constitutional issues need not concede jurisdiction over every statutory disagreement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Determine the governing permanent-rule provisions and the conceded jurisdictional predicates. If some individual petition falls under a different transitional regime, address that petition separately rather than assume uniformity.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may sustain the direct-review bar while recognizing the independent, limited habeas channel; retain direct legal review under a narrower reading of the bar; or conclude that Congress displaced both routes and then confront the constitutional consequence. The third path cannot avoid Kolster v. INS and Reno v. Goncalves by citing discretionary relief in the abstract.

**Judgment and Relief:** Affirming direct dismissal with a preserved habeas route does not grant any waiver, authorize removal notwithstanding a lawful stay, or decide the separately filed habeas cases. Reversal of direct dismissal returns legal review to the appellate court, with eligibility and remedy still to be adjudicated. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Limit the rule to conceded criminal removability and a legal challenge to eligibility; reserve predicate disputes, factual review, and actual discretionary denials. **Material Uncertainty:** The record does not establish each individual habeas predicate; any intervening controlling decision must also be applied.

[Calcano-Martinez v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-348.pdf).

**Authorities:** Kolster v. INS; Reno v. Goncalves; Reyes-Hernandez v. INS; Felker v. Turpin.

# FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-191; 533 U.S. 431.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the First Amendment permits Congress to limit expenditures that political parties coordinate with their federal candidates, as a means of preventing circumvention of candidate-contribution limits.

* **Question Granted:** The facial challenge remaining after the earlier independent-expenditure litigation; this formulation reconstructs the merits issue from the judicial record.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed the district court's facial invalidation of the coordinated party-expenditure limitation.

* **Relief Requested:** The FEC seeks reversal and rejection of that facial challenge. The party seeks affirmance, protecting its coordinated electoral expression against the statutory ceiling.

* **Material Record:** The original enforcement dispute concerned radio advertisements attacking Timothy Wirth before the party chose a nominee. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC protected those genuinely independent advertisements and remanded the broader challenge. The present record concerns the operation of party financing, including testimony about donors giving to parties with an understanding that preferred candidates will benefit, candidate fundraising credited in party tally systems, and the practical difficulty of tracing money through general party treasuries. The evidence does not establish that every party contribution purchases an official act or that every party communication is coordinated. The statutory limit covers genuinely coordinated spending, while independent expression remains separately protected. The Senate ceiling is the greater of an indexed $20,000 base or two cents per voting-age resident, also adjusted for inflation; House ceilings follow separate provisions.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a facial constitutional challenge, not a new adjudication of whether the original advertisements were coordinated. The definition of coordination and permissible applications therefore matter.

* **Relevant Dates:** Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC was decided June 26, 1996.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** The earlier Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC litigation remains controlling at its actual scope.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier controlling decision in Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics and Election Practices or Mariani v. United States must be applied within its scope.

### Issue 1: Coordinated party expenditures and circumvention

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC protects genuinely independent party advertisements and requires the actual statutory candidate connection before treating spending as a contribution. It expressly leaves coordinated party expression unresolved. Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC applies the government's burden to demonstrate an objectively grounded, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest and close fit preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy. Its record-specific monetary approval supplies no general dollar safe harbor.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The contribution/expenditure distinction turns on operation, not a speaker's name. Actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request, or suggestion cannot be presumed from party affiliation. A genuine conduit can be regulated; independent speech cannot be recategorized merely to make a regulatory interest easier to satisfy.

* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment; the then-operative Federal Election Campaign Act's coordinated-spending definition, party ceilings, earmarking rule, and interparty-transfer provisions, reproduced in the primary judicial source.

* **Material Other Authority:** Buckley v. Valeo supplies the surviving contribution/expenditure framework; it does not itself decide the party-specific question reserved in Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Independent party expression is protected, and direct contribution regulation requires evidentiary justification and fit.

* **Open Point:** Whether actual party-candidate coordination sufficiently resembles a contribution to sustain these ceilings, and whether the existing earmarking rule adequately addresses the demonstrated circumvention risk.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The lower courts treated the party's relationship with its candidates and the burden on the party's electoral mission as reasons to reject limits on its coordinated spending. The distinct independent-spending holding did not itself compel that result.

**FEC:** A candidate can receive the practical equivalent of a contribution when a party spends in consultation with the campaign. Larger permissible gifts to a party can circumvent smaller candidate limits, particularly through tacit understandings and fundraising credits that avoid express earmarking. The government seeks contribution scrutiny for genuine coordinated spending and relies on the developed evidentiary record. It must still preserve genuinely independent expression and cannot treat routine political agreement as coordination.

**Party:** Working with candidates is central to party association and effective advocacy. A spending ceiling directly limits joint expression, and restrictions on donations to the party plus earmarking rules provide less restrictive alternatives. Corruption cannot be inferred from a party's wish to elect its candidates or from policy agreement. Its broader requested rule would protect coordinated party advocacy even where an intermediary-funding concern is proved, making the boundary between expression and a candidate-controlled financial benefit decisive.

**Material Alternative:** The Court could distinguish candidate-directed purchases from a party's own jointly developed political message, but the facial challenge and statutory definition must support any such distinction. No record finding establishes that all coordinated expenditures can be separated along that line.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold:** Resolve the preserved facial claim without reopening the independent-advertisement judgment. **Merits:** The Court may sustain the ceiling for genuinely coordinated spending as a closely drawn contribution safeguard; invalidate it because the burden or fit fails; or reject the facial claim while reserving narrower applications supported by a later factual record.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Reversal removes the facial invalidation and returns any remaining proceedings for lawful enforcement within the established independence boundary. Affirmance leaves the coordinated-spending ceiling unenforceable at the adjudicated scope. Neither path permits liability for the earlier protected advertisements merely because the broader claim fails.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A functional rule can cover candidate-connected party spending without presuming that all party expression is coordinated. A contrary rule could protect coordinated advocacy while leaving direct cash and genuine earmarked conduits subject to contribution limits. A universal party exemption extending to those transfers is not necessary to decide this case. **Uncertainty:** The precise treatment of particular coordinated communications, enforcement liability, and any effective same-term decisions requires separate attention.

## E. SOURCES

[FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-431.pdf).

**Authorities:** Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC; Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC.

# INS v. St. Cyr

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** INS v. St. Cyr

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-767; 533 U.S. 289.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether AEDPA and IIRIRA eliminated district-court habeas jurisdiction over a legal challenge to the denial of eligibility for former §212(c) relief; and whether the repeal of that relief applies to an otherwise eligible resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a guilty plea entered before those enactments. These questions reconstruct the granted merits controversy from the judicial record.

* **Question Granted:** The jurisdictional and statutory-retroactivity questions, not whether the Attorney General should grant a waiver.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 229 F.3d 406, affirming a district-court judgment recognizing habeas jurisdiction and rejecting the government's application of the new restrictions to the respondent's preenactment guilty plea.

* **Relief Requested:** The government seeks reversal and enforcement of its ineligibility determination. St. Cyr seeks preservation of a judicial forum and consideration for discretionary relief under the prior eligibility rules.

* **Material Record:** Enrico St. Cyr, a Haitian citizen, became a lawful permanent resident in 1986. He pleaded guilty on March 8, 1996, to selling a controlled substance under Connecticut law. The conviction made him deportable, but he was eligible to seek a discretionary waiver under the law then in force. Removal proceedings began April 10, 1997. The Attorney General regarded the 1996 amendments as eliminating authority to grant him the former relief. His habeas claim contests that legal conclusion, not the validity of the conviction or an unfavorable balancing of waiver equities. [INS v. St. Cyr](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-289.pdf).

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Eligibility is distinct from entitlement. The temporal issue involves a completed plea transaction predating both enactments, not merely past residence or a conviction following trial. The record supports eligibility at the plea date; it does not establish a guaranteed waiver or require a finding that a prosecutor promised immigration relief. The Court must identify the independent §2241 grant and the particular statutory provisions said to displace it. Statutory construction can resolve the case without deciding the full constitutional extent of the Suspension Clause.

* **Relevant Dates:** Plea March 8, 1996; AEDPA April 24, 1996; IIRIRA September 30, 1996; permanent-system effective date April 1, 1997; proceedings April 10, 1997; argument April 24, 2001.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** Calcano-Martinez v. INS raises the companion direct-review channel question.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Recheck Calcano-Martinez v. INS and any earlier OT2000 immigration decisions for actual event sequence and adopted propositions. A same-day calendar listing supplies no governing result by itself.

### Issue 1: Independent habeas jurisdiction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. Goncalves preserves §2241 review of a substantial legal objection to categorical ineligibility despite the discretionary nature of the ultimate relief. Kolster v. INS separates withdrawn direct review from properly invoked habeas legality review. Reyes-Hernandez v. INS prevents expansion of §242(g) beyond its named acts into a universal bar.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Use the actual transitional or permanent statutory regime; identify custody and remedy independently; distinguish statutory eligibility, discretionary balancing, and factual reconsideration.

* **Other Governing Law:** AEDPA's repeal of the immigration-specific habeas provision and IIRIRA's review-channel language must be assessed against §2241's independent grant. [INS v. St. Cyr](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-289.pdf).

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified in this issue.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A discretionary remedy does not turn every legal condition on access into unreviewable discretion.

* **Open Point:** Whether this permanent-rule statutory combination clearly displaces the independent remedy.

### Issue 2: Temporal operation of the repeal

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products asks first whether Congress supplied a clear temporal instruction and then whether the proposed application attaches new legal consequences to completed events. Reno v. Goncalves protects waiver applications pending on AEDPA's enactment, including administrative appeals, but expressly leaves later applications and IIRIRA's repeal open. Reyes-Hernandez v. INS addresses surrendered colorable deportability defenses; Kolster v. INS rejects a universal reliance prerequisite while finding no protected completed transaction on its particular facts.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Analyze the particular new disability and completed event; neither a procedural label nor the date proceedings commence answers the inquiry.

* **Other Governing Law:** The former waiver provisions, the 1996 amendments, and their transition rules. [INS v. St. Cyr](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-289.pdf).

* **Material Other Authority:** Lindh v. Murphy supports attention to Congress's differentiated transition choices without resolving this immigration question.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Preservation of a pending application does not decide every old conviction.

* **Open Point:** Whether the completed guilty plea while eligible is sufficient, without individualized proof of a particular immigration promise or surrendered deportability defense.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The lower courts recognized a judicial forum for the legal claim and treated application of the new ineligibility rule to the earlier plea as impermissibly retroactive. They did not award a discretionary waiver.

**Government:** Congress comprehensively replaced the immigration review scheme and removed relief for the criminal category. New proceedings are governed by new law, and deportation regulates future presence rather than punishes a past crime. A discretionary possibility is not a vested entitlement. Requiring individualized reliance would expose weaknesses in any claim of a settled expectation that the immigration laws would remain fixed.

**St. Cyr:** The independent habeas grant survives absent a clear repeal, especially where no effective alternative can decide the legal claim. A guilty plea exchanges trial rights for known legal consequences. Removing an existing opportunity for discretionary relief after the government has obtained that plea adds a serious disability to the completed exchange. An entitlement to consideration can be legally protected even though the ultimate decision is discretionary.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Recognize or reject independent habeas jurisdiction before issuing a merits judgment. If the statutory argument succeeds, no complete Suspension Clause holding is necessary.

**Merits Paths:** Preserve former eligibility for the preenactment plea; apply the repeal based on clear temporal language or the absence of a retroactive effect; or adopt an individualized completed-transaction rule requiring findings not yet made. A reliance-remand route must explain why ordinary eligibility at the plea and the exchange of trial rights are insufficient under Landgraf v. USI Film Products, Reno v. Goncalves, Reyes-Hernandez v. INS, and Kolster v. INS.

**Judgment and Relief:** Affirmance restores access to the agency's discretionary process, not permanent immunity from removal. Reversal on jurisdiction alone leaves the merits undecided; reversal on temporal construction sustains the ineligibility rule. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Guilty pleas entered before the statutory change by persons then eligible for the old waiver. **Material Uncertainty:** Consequences for trial convictions, persons not yet eligible at the plea, and later pleas are not established by this record. Same-term decisions remain unavailable until adopted and verified.

# K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District
* **Citation or Docket:** 196 F.3d 1046; Ninth Circuit judgment of November 15, 1999.
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the lower-court judgment; merits disposition.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 25, 2001, organizationally assigned.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Free Exercise and Equal Protection Clauses permit a State to withhold onsite special-education services solely because a child attends a religious school, when adequate services and transportation are provided at a nearby secular location?
* **Question Granted:** Review encompasses the question presented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit reversal of the district court's constitutional judgment and injunction; both courts rejected an independent statutory entitlement to onsite delivery under IDEA.
* **Relief Requested:** K.D.M. seeks restoration of constitutional relief against the religious-location restriction and provision of the vision specialist at his school. The school district and state superintendent seek preservation of their delivery arrangement.
* **Material Record:** K.D.M., a blind child with cerebral palsy, formerly received special-education services in public school. His parents transferred him to Harbor Baptist Church School for religious reasons. The district continued supplying equipment there, including braillers and computers, but provided the vision specialist at a fire hall nearby because Oregon required a religiously neutral setting. Services occurred about ninety minutes twice weekly, with district transportation. Their adequacy and the safety of travel were stipulated. Even at the religious school, instruction would occur in a separate room rather than in the ordinary classroom. The case was tried on stipulated facts. An asserted loss of necessary classroom integration therefore cannot be invented to support relief.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The constitutional claim concerns a religion-specific delivery restriction, not a demand for services never made available to comparable private-school children. The statutory cross-appeal was resolved against an individual right to onsite services. Constitutional permission, statutory entitlement, and nondiscriminatory administration remain separate questions. The actual educational need and the continuing location restriction support the prospective claim; the record establishes no mootness.
* **Relevant Dates:** Appellate judgment November 15, 1999, before the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No separately briefed companion. Any earlier controlling decision concerning religious funding must be applied within its scope.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Apply any earlier controlling decision in Good News Club v. Milford Central School, Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle, or Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education within its scope.

### Issue 1: Religious classification in delivery of an available service

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah holds that objective religious targeting defeats neutrality and requires compelling justification with narrow tailoring. Employment Division v. Smith remains applicable to genuinely neutral, generally applicable regulation. Agostini v. Felton permits publicly controlled, operationally separate secular instruction on religious premises; that permission creates no unconditional onsite entitlement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A religious disability must be assessed through its text, operation, and actual comparators. Religious impact alone is insufficient; formal religion-based differential treatment cannot be absorbed into a generic administrative balance. Effective secular controls address the funded function.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Free Exercise and Equal Protection Clauses; IDEA's private-school services framework and Oregon's location requirement as reproduced in the opinion.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mitchell v. Helms rejects affiliation or possible diversion as a substitute for actual prohibited religious financing. Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District requires an antecedent-law remand and does not decide the Establishment Clause merits.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Onsite public service is not presumptively devotional. Nor does constitutional permission dictate every administrative site choice. Comparable secular logistical restrictions remain possible.
* **Open Point:** Whether a sufficient and safely accessible alternative eliminates a constitutional objection to the express religious classification, and what remedy follows if that classification is invalid.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit majority emphasized the absence of demonstrated educational or travel harm and concluded that the regulation, as applied, did not impermissibly burden religious exercise. It also rejected the district court's premise that onsite delivery necessarily meant classroom integration and upheld the State's interest in separating the public service from a religious setting. The dissent considered the express difference between religious and secular private-school children sufficient to trigger rigorous review despite the alternative service.

K.D.M.'s strongest rule is that government cannot impose a special condition on receipt of an available secular benefit solely because of religious enrollment, even when compliance is manageable. He must accept the stipulated adequacy of the alternative and cannot derive an absolute right to onsite delivery from IDEA or Agostini v. Felton.

The government argues that it fully supplies the relevant benefit, that safe transport and a nearby site impose no meaningful interference with religious education, and that it may organize publicly financed instruction to maintain institutional separation. It asks the Court to distinguish service availability from preferred location. Its account must explain why religion alone changes the delivery rule when the same secular instruction can remain independently controlled onsite. Actual devotional use cannot be assumed from the setting.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance could hold that this delivery arrangement imposes no actionable impairment of religious exercise or unequal access given the stipulated equivalent service. That rule would have to preserve claims involving actual loss of access or more onerous religion-specific conditions.

Reversal could hold the express religious criterion unjustified and require a new location decision under neutral educational and administrative criteria. Such a ruling would not itself mandate the child's preferred room or foreclose offsite delivery for comparable secular reasons. Direct restoration of an injunction requires attention to whether it forbids only the religious criterion or orders unconditional onsite services. A remand can tailor the decree without relitigating the stipulated adequacy and safety facts.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The question supports deciding whether an express religious condition on service location escapes scrutiny because its practical cost is modest. It does not require a universal constitutional right to private-school services, compulsory subsidy of religious instruction, or reconsideration of IDEA funding levels. Any remedy must separate equal eligibility for onsite delivery from guaranteed delivery there.

## E. SOURCES

[K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/196/1046/601439/).

**Authorities:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah; Agostini v. Felton; Mitchell v. Helms; Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District.

# Nevada v. Hicks

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nevada v. Hicks

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1994; 533 U.S. 353.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a tribal court may adjudicate tribal-law tort and federal civil-rights claims against state officers arising from searches of a tribal member's home on tribal land for evidence of an off-reservation state offense, and whether the officers must exhaust immunity objections in that forum.

* **Question Granted:** Reconstructed from the judicial record, covering tribal authority, the federal cause of action, and exhaustion.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed federal summary judgment recognizing tribal jurisdiction and requiring exhaustion of the officers' qualified-immunity claims.

* **Relief Requested:** Nevada and its wardens request a declaration that the tribal court lacks jurisdiction; Hicks and the tribal respondents defend the forum's authority.

* **Material Record:** Hicks, a Fallon Paiute-Shoshone member, lived on tribe-owned reservation land. State wardens suspected an unlawful off-reservation killing of a California bighorn sheep. For a 1990 search they obtained a state warrant expressly conditioned on tribal-court approval, then obtained a tribal warrant and searched with a tribal officer. They found a head of a different, unprotected species. A later search likewise proceeded under state and tribal warrants with tribal officers. Hicks alleges damage to mounted heads and a search exceeding the warrant. Those allegations are not adjudicated constitutional violations. He brought trespass, abuse-of-process, and §1983 claims. Claims against tribal defendants were dismissed; he voluntarily dismissed the State and the wardens in their official capacities. The remaining damages claims run against the wardens personally. The tribal trial and appellate courts upheld jurisdiction before the federal action.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Jurisdiction precedes liability and immunity. Land status, the scope of accepted tribal authorization, the source of each cause, and the difference between official conduct and official-capacity liability matter separately.

* **Relevant Dates:** Searches occurred in 1990 and approximately one year later; the Ninth Circuit decided in 1999.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** Any earlier controlling decision in Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley must be applied within its scope.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley may affect nonmember authority; Saucier v. Katz may affect immunity procedure. Neither historical result governs here.

### Issue 1: Authority over the tribal-law claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Strate v. A-1 Contractors applies the Montana v. United States framework to an authorized public highway where ordinary public use displaced exclusion. Its controlling majority also requires adequate substantive governing authority for nonmember adjudication. A meaningful relationship between consent and the asserted authority is necessary, and ordinary injury does not itself establish an existential self-government threat.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify land, exclusion rights, persons, conduct, governing source, and actual nexus. South Dakota v. Bourland addresses acquired lands with displaced exclusion and does not establish state exclusivity throughout reservations.

* **Other Governing Law:** The tribal land's federal status, the state and tribal warrants, and applicable sovereign-authority principles.

* **Material Other Authority:** Montana v. United States supplies the nonmember framework where applicable.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Completed tribal exhaustion supplies no missing substantive power.

* **Open Point:** The effect of retained tribal ownership and accepted tribal search authorization where state officers investigate off-reservation conduct.

### Issue 2: Federal claims and exhaustion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie requires federal determination of a specifically assigned statutory channel before prudential tribal exhaustion; it does not create tribal removal or general tribal jurisdiction.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Exhaustion and comity do not manufacture authority, and an injunction requires its own predicates.

* **Other Governing Law:** §1983, federal-question jurisdiction, and the procedural law governing the federal declaratory action.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified in this issue.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Ordinary state-court concurrent jurisdiction cannot simply be attributed to every tribunal.

* **Open Point:** Whether §1983 authorizes this tribal forum and whether any remaining tribal-law case requires exhaustion of immunity defenses.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Tribal ownership of the land was treated as sufficient to support civil adjudication; immunity was left initially to tribal proceedings. **Petitioners:** State enforcement of off-reservation law cannot depend on tribal regulation or adjudication, and obtaining a tribal warrant does not consent to civil jurisdiction. Tribal courts lack a congressional grant over §1983 claims. Requiring further proceedings in an unauthorized forum would be pointless.

**Respondents:** A tribal member's home on retained tribal land differs from an open public highway. Officers sought and used tribal legal authorization, so claims that they exceeded its limits have a direct connection to that relationship. State enforcement interests need not eliminate all accountability in the territorial sovereign's courts. The United States supported affirmance as amicus in the historical litigation, emphasizing the relevant tribal interests; that position is advocacy, not governing authority.

**Limits:** Personal-capacity pleading does not alone defeat an officer's jurisdictional defense. Conversely, official status does not establish immunity from every forum or make alleged misconduct lawful. Federal statutory and tribal tort claims need separate analysis.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Paths:** Reject jurisdiction over both categories; recognize tribal-law authority based on retained territorial power or a sufficiently related consensual arrangement while separately rejecting the federal claim; or recognize both and leave immunity to the competent forum. The lower court's land-only rationale can be rejected without establishing that land never matters.

**Judgment and Remedy:** A no-jurisdiction declaration terminates the unauthorized tribal claims but awards no damages and decides no search violation. A partial disposition preserves only claims supported by actual authority. Remand may be needed to resolve the terms and legal effect of accepted tribal authorization; no factual finding of express consent to suit may be invented.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule limited to off-reservation state enforcement differs from a universal bar on nonmember claims on tribal lands. A consent-based rule must connect the tort allegations to the accepted arrangement rather than every future dispute. **Uncertainty:** Neither a final finding of excessive search nor a finding of express consent to civil adjudication appears in the source record.

# New York Times Co. v. Tasini

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** New York Times Co. v. Tasini

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-201; 533 U.S. 483.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a publisher's statutory privilege to reproduce a freelance contribution as part of a collective work, a revision, or a later collective work in the same series permits the electronic database uses shown here without the author's permission.

* **Question Granted:** This is a reconstruction of the statutory merits question from the judicial record.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reversed summary judgment for the publishers and directed judgment for the authors concerning the challenged database uses.

* **Relief Requested:** The publishers seek restoration of their statutory defense; the authors seek affirmance and subsequent appropriate relief for unauthorized reproduction and distribution.

* **Material Record:** Six freelance authors contributed articles to the New York Times, Newsday, and Sports Illustrated as independent contractors. Their contracts did not secure consent to these electronic database uses. Print publishers licensed articles to electronic publishers. NEXIS presents searchable text articles individually with source information but without the original edition's surrounding contents and presentation. New York Times OnDisc similarly provides searchable text. General Periodicals OnDisc preserves images of particular printed pages but does not present the complete periodical edition. Search results can sometimes be selected to collect articles from an original issue; that user-created grouping differs from the database's ordinary presentation. This case concerns the proved licenses and interfaces, not every electronic archive or every contract governing freelance work.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory privilege is a defense to otherwise implicated reproduction and distribution rights. Authorship, ownership, contract authorization, and actual presentation must not be collapsed into the physical storage medium. The remedial consequences have not been fully adjudicated.

* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant contributions and licensing arrangements predate suit; the Second Circuit judgment preceded this term.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No consolidated companion.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No identified intervening decision resolves this statutory privilege; any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

### Issue 1: The collective-work privilege

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling precedent resolves whether these separated database articles are reproduced as part of a revision under §201(c). Existing copyright rules do not confer an automatic digital-format exemption or turn ownership of a collective work into ownership of every contribution.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the particular exclusive right, owner, transfer, statutory limitation, and challenged act. Distinct statutory permissions must be proved according to their own predicates.

* **Other Governing Law:** The Copyright Act provisions reproduced in the primary source distinguish copyright in a contribution from copyright in a collective work; absent an express transfer, the publisher receives the limited §201(c) reproduction and distribution privilege. Section 106 identifies exclusive rights; §103(b) limits the collective author's rights in preexisting material; §201(d) permits separate ownership of divisible rights. These are the provisions operative for the transactions, not later amendments or licensing settlements.

* **Material Other Authority:** Stewart v. Abend is relevant to preserving the author's distinct ownership, but does not decide this database question.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A publisher may exercise the privilege actually granted; changing the medium alone neither creates nor destroys it.

* **Open Point:** Whether the database's user-facing separation from the edition is incompatible with reproduction as part of the original collective work or its revision, and what relief follows if the privilege fails.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The appellate court treated the electronic compendia as uses beyond the publisher's limited privilege, rather than revisions of each source issue. The district court had accepted the publishers' contrary understanding.

**Publishers:** The database contains the articles that constituted the editions, keeps source identifiers, and enables retrieval of an issue's contents. A revision can change selection and presentation, and statutory media neutrality should allow electronic dissemination comparable to microform. They argue that a contrary reading threatens completeness of historical databases and imposes renegotiation costs.

**Authors:** The database sells separate articles outside their original collective context. Source labels establish historical provenance, not present inclusion in a revised edition. Treating the whole database as a revision of each component would consume the authors' reserved licensing rights. The image-based product also detaches pages from the complete issue. They concede that electronic publication pursuant to actual authorization, or a genuinely preserved edition, presents a different case.

**Remedial Positions:** The practical value of a complete historical archive is relevant to lawful remedial discretion, but neither establishes a statutory license nor proves that deletion is the only available remedy. Actual agreements, damages, and equitable terms require their own legal and factual predicates.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold:** Confirm that the challenged uses lack contractual authorization and that the statutory defense is properly preserved. **Merits Paths:** Read revision broadly enough to include these databases; read it as requiring the article to remain perceptibly part of an edition or its recognizable revision; or distinguish products if their actual presentation materially differs under the chosen construction.

**Judgment and Remedy:** A valid privilege supports publisher judgment on this theory. Failure of the privilege sustains author liability judgment and returns damages and equitable relief for individualized adjudication. It does not compel removal of all freelance material from every database.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A contextual rule can apply across media while distinguishing intact digital editions from extracted individual articles. A broad archival rule must identify a limiting principle that leaves the author's reserved rights operative. **Uncertainty:** Contract terms outside the litigated articles, later interface changes, damages, willfulness, and the feasibility of negotiated access are not established by this record.

# United States v. United Foods, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. United Foods, Inc.

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-276; 533 U.S. 405.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the First Amendment permits compulsory assessments principally financing generic mushroom advertising where the producers remain otherwise independent market competitors.

* **Question Granted:** The compelled-subsidy question reconstructed from the judicial record.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit reversed summary judgment sustaining the assessments.

* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks enforcement; United Foods seeks protection from the challenged compulsory promotional payments.

* **Material Record:** The Mushroom Promotion, Research, and Consumer Information Act authorizes an industry council and mandatory assessments on fresh-mushroom handlers. Most money supports generic promotion. United Foods wishes to advertise its own mushrooms as superior and objects to funding a message promoting the generic product. There are no comparable marketing orders restricting output and sales, no antitrust exemption, and no comprehensive cooperative marketing structure requiring producers to surrender their independent market decisions. United Foods challenged the assessment administratively; the government brought an enforcement action. After denial of administrative relief, review and enforcement proceedings were consolidated. The district court considered Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott controlling.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The government's separate government-speech theory was not raised in the court of appeals. Whether it is properly considered here is a distinct preservation question. The record concerns the advertising assessment, not every research or information expenditure authorized by the Act.

* **Relevant Dates:** The Act was enacted in 1990; the appellate judgment preceded this term.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No consolidated companion.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any effective compelled-speech ruling must be checked; no historical result is imported.

### Issue 1: Compelled promotion without comprehensive market regulation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott sustained germane, nonideological generic promotion within comprehensive collective marketing that displaced ordinary competitive autonomy. It expressly reserved advertising-only programs in otherwise independent markets. The record's common benefit, majority support, and absence of attribution were relevant but not independently sufficient.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Compelled financial support can implicate speech even if the payer need not utter the message and the disagreement is commercial rather than ideological. Identify the lawful associational purpose independently of the compelled message before judging germaneness.

* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment and the Mushroom Act provisions reproduced in the primary judicial source.

* **Material Other Authority:** Abood v. Detroit Board of Education and Keller v. State Bar of California concern compelled association for independent purposes; their germaneness reasoning cannot establish that advertising is germane to itself.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott protects the actual comprehensive program it adjudicated.

* **Open Point:** Whether the independent mushroom-market arrangement justifies compelled generic advocacy, and whether another properly preserved justification supports it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The district court treated generic agricultural promotion as governed by Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott. The Sixth Circuit distinguished the absence of comprehensive cooperative restrictions and held the compelled payments unconstitutional.

**Government:** Generic advertising promotes a common economic interest, can avoid free riding, and does not attribute the message to any particular producer or prevent brand-specific counteradvertising. Commercial disagreement should receive correspondingly limited protection. It also invokes government speech, subject to the preservation problem.

**United Foods:** The government cannot force competitors to fund another seller's preferred promotional message merely because officials or an industry majority regard it as beneficial. Its own claim of superior quality is at odds with generic promotion. The comprehensive collective-market justification in Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott is absent. Allowing the advertising program to supply its own associational purpose would remove any meaningful boundary on compelled promotional subsidies.

**Limits and Alternatives:** This is not a misleading-advertisement disclosure requirement imposed on the producer's own speech. Nor does objecting to the advertising establish a constitutional objection to every neutral economic assessment or government-funded message. Any independent government-speech argument must be both properly presented and factually supported rather than assumed from the statute's existence.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold:** Decline the unpreserved government-speech theory or determine whether exceptional review is warranted without inventing control findings. **Merits:** Sustain the assessment by extending Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott to common-benefit promotion; distinguish that holding and invalidate the advertising requirement; or remand if a properly presented alternative genuinely needs further factual determination.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Upholding supports government enforcement within the adjudicated assessment. Invalidating defeats enforcement of the unconstitutional advertising obligation and permits appropriate prospective and monetary proceedings under the applicable remedial law. No automatic nationwide refund, damages award, or invalidation of unrelated council functions follows.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule requiring an independently sufficient collective purpose can preserve Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's actual cooperative program while preventing advertising from bootstrapping its own compelled association. A commercial-speech justification would need to explain why economic benefit and freedom to disagree sufficiently answer the compelled-payment burden. **Uncertainty:** The disposition of severable nonadvertising expenses and any monetary recovery needs the relevant accounting and remedial record.

**Authorities:** Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott.
