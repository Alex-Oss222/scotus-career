**Material Alternative:** The Court could distinguish candidate-directed purchases from a party's own jointly developed political message, but the facial challenge and statutory definition must support any such distinction. A universal party exemption extending to those transfers is not necessary to decide this case. **Uncertainty:** The precise treatment of particular coordinated communications, enforcement liability, and any effective same-term decisions requires separate attention.

**United Foods:** The government cannot force competitors to fund another seller's preferred promotional message merely because officials or an industry majority regard it as beneficial. Its own claim of superior quality is at odds with generic promotion. The comprehensive collective-market justification in Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott is absent. Allowing the advertising program to supply its own associational purpose would remove any meaningful boundary on compelled promotional subsidies.

**Limits and Alternatives:** The actual Southworth holding supports an independently justified, broad and viewpoint-neutral educational private-expression program; it does not establish a generic exemption for compelled financing or make every institutional message governmental. Lebron's actor classification similarly answers who bears constitutional duties, not whether every message and earmarked assessment is the government's own speech. Keller, 496 U.S. 1, 10–14, rejects treating the integrated Bar's state-law agency label and public purpose as sufficient to exempt its compelled-dues expression, while distinguishing ordinary representative-government speech and taxation. Its advisory professional structure and mandatory membership matter; it does not establish that all statutory industry councils are private speakers or that all assessments are taxes. The governmental/private-speech distinction here must be decided on the Council's actual statutory structure and established record if the alternative is reached. This is not a misleading-advertisement disclosure requirement imposed on the producer's own speech. Nor does objecting to the advertising establish a constitutional objection to every neutral economic assessment or government-funded message. Any independent government-speech argument must be both properly presented and factually supported rather than assumed from the statute's existence.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold:** Under Yee and the actual Lebron rule, first decide the argument-versus-question boundary and the adequacy of the established record. The Court may consider a properly included legal argument despite nonpresentation below, but need not do so; a distinct question requires its own exceptional justification. Do not equate an included new argument with a mandatory exception or invent operational control findings. **Merits:** Sustain the assessment by extending Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott to common-benefit promotion; distinguish that holding and invalidate the advertising requirement; or remand if a properly presented alternative genuinely needs further factual determination.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Upholding supports government enforcement within the adjudicated assessment. Invalidating defeats enforcement of the unconstitutional advertising obligation and permits appropriate prospective and monetary proceedings under the applicable remedial law. No automatic nationwide refund, damages award, or invalidation of unrelated council functions follows.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule requiring an independently sufficient collective purpose can preserve Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's actual cooperative program while preventing advertising from bootstrapping its own compelled association. A commercial-speech justification would need to explain why economic benefit and freedom to disagree sufficiently answer the compelled-payment burden. **Uncertainty:** The disposition of severable nonadvertising expenses and any monetary recovery needs the relevant accounting and remedial record.
