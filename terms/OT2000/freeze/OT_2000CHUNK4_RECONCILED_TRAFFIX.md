## Common ground and mandate

All eight Associates directly join: an expired utility patent is strong evidence of functionality of its claimed utilitarian feature; the claimant seeking protection for that feature faces the substantial evidentiary obstacle the patent creates but may identify a genuinely ornamental, incidental or arbitrary feature; essential use or purpose or an effect on cost or quality establishes traditional utilitarian functionality; and once that functionality is established, alternative arrangements or concealment do not cure it. Consumer recognition and secondary meaning do not save the proven functional feature. These propositions do not impose an automatic patent-wide bar or decide a new aesthetic-functionality test.
