# Reconciled non-Stone commitments: NLRB v. Kentucky River Community Care

Event: May 29, 2001; No. 99-1815; argued February 21, 2001. Research cutoff: the event date. Inputs are the frozen neutral packet and B addendum, actual entering-law slice B, frozen Kentucky River commitments, assigned comparator, official petition with agency and lower-court appendices, and the full official historical report. No private current position or raw current Decision Record was received. No assignment is made, and the initial commitments remain unchanged.

## Comparator verified at each level

The full report, 532 U.S. 706–730, including every writing and footnote, confirms historical affirmance of the Sixth Circuit's refusal of enforcement. Scalia wrote: Part II was unanimous; Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas joined Parts I and III. Stevens concurred in part and dissented in part, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The latter four expressly joined Part II, not the other portions of the lead opinion, and would reverse. Their separate opinion additionally maintained that even under the majority's rule the proper alternative would be a return to the agency rather than categorical affirmance.

The historical unanimous Part II sustained the exclusion-proponent burden both at representation and at the derivative refusal-to-bargain proceeding. The historical five-Justice Parts I/III rejected the Board's asserted exclusion of ordinary professional or technical judgment from independent judgment and withheld enforcement. They relied materially on historical Health Care's rejection of the Board's earlier approach and its affirmance remedy, coupled with the conclusion that the Board supplied no lawful alternative ground. The separate opinion understood the actual agency work as functional and record-sensitive, considered the statutory professional-employee category, and rejected the categorical characterization. None of these historical descriptions overrides this timeline's Health Care or supplies a new agency finding.

## Actual changed premise and preserved record

Actual Health Care, May 23, 1994, directly permits a functional distinction between ordinary skilled direction and genuine supervisory authority. It sustains an actual supported agency rationale, preserves every listed supervisory function as an independent alternative, permits professional judgment to satisfy independence, and rejects an occupational exemption. The rule is permissible, not compulsory for every Board. Its public controlling explanation also requires attention to the actual delegation and preserves infrequently exercised authority. Its judgment reverses the Sixth Circuit rather than affirming refusal of enforcement.

The regional director's actual reasons, reproduced in Appendix D of the official petition, include prescribed staffing levels, volunteer and local-call replacement procedures, inability to compel attendance under threat of discipline, ordinary reporting subject to independent managerial investigation, lack of effective personnel recommendations, and continuing management authority. The director considered two conflicting building-supervisor memoranda and treated the demonstrated extra function as obtaining needed coverage. The concluding medical-services characterization must be read with those findings. A quotation from counsel or an imported historical characterization does not erase them.

The contrary record evidence is also retained: the more extensive memorandum describes interunit reassignment and writing up refusal, an official describes send-home authority followed by notice to the employee's supervisor, and nurses often are the highest on-site employees. Possession of genuine authority matters even when exercise is rare. The adjudicative question is whether the agency adequately assessed that evidence under the lawful functional boundary, not whether nurses possess skill or whether patient care benefits the employer.

Allentown requires the announced lawful standard and whole-record substantial evidence, while preserving deference between fairly conflicting views. Holly Farms requires independent statutory-boundary review and then examination of the actual functional rationale. Greenwich Collieries retains persuasion on the proponent of an order while preserving genuine exception and defense burdens. These actual authorities, not historical summaries of them, govern.

## Final component coalitions

**Burden:** All eight Associates—Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—join the full exclusion-proponent rule. The party asserting supervisory status must establish it; the rule can place the burden on an employer, union or General Counsel according to who asserts the exclusion. The General Counsel retains the burden on unfair-labor-practice elements. In this derivative proceeding, the employer's challenge to the certified unit does not transform supervisor status into an element the General Counsel must disprove. The Sixth Circuit's contrary allocation is rejected. No new quantum, presumption or general reversal of the agency's proof duties is adopted.

**Statutory boundary:** The same eight join the complete functional rule. Actual authority to perform any listed function in the employer's interest must require independent judgment beyond routine or clerical action. Assignment or responsible direction may suffice without hiring, firing or disciplinary power; infrequent exercise does not defeat possessed authority. The professional source of judgment neither establishes supervision nor categorically prevents independence. Titles, rank, credentials, patient-care responsibility and an off-site manager are not automatic substitutes for that inquiry.

**Application and remedy:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the nurse-exclusion portion and direct enforcement of the existing Board order as to those employees, with ordinary appellate implementation. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the nurse-classification portion, correct the burden, withhold enforcement of that disputed portion and return it for an agency determination addressing actual assignment and responsible direction under the governing criteria. They do not affirm a conclusive judicial finding that these six nurses are supervisors.

That line describes a real remedial departure, not merely changed drafting. It does not imply that a different prior judgment compels every future vote. Each Justice's application objection remains supported by the actual authority evidence and personal published positions. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their historical judgment direction; their agreement with the shared no-categorical-exemption boundary is consistent with their historical view that this agency decision was not categorical. No invented shift from endorsing an occupational exemption is attributed to them.

## Compatibility, limits and disposition precision

The enforcement group finds the director's actual findings sufficient under Health Care and Allentown, including its assessment of routine replacement arrangements and absence of actual personnel consequences. The remand group finds those findings incomplete for separate possessed assignment and responsible-direction authority. Their strongest counterarguments remain reciprocal: remand risks demanding redundant proceedings after a supported functional assessment; enforcement risks using nonexercise or medical character to substitute for a finding about actual authority. Neither objection is removed by counting the common burden and abstract statutory joins as agreement about application.

All eight agree that a categorical professional exemption is impermissible, but they disagree whether the actual agency decision used such a shortcut or adequately decided the authority question. Eight votes for the boundary do not convert into eight votes for either remedy. No Marks synthesis is needed or asserted. These are final non-Stone commitments, not a Court majority or an equal-division judgment.

Both remedy paths preserve the separate political-subdivision ruling and rehabilitation-counselor determination. They impose no new bargaining terms, backpay, individual liability, election or universal occupational classification. Enforcement implements the actual existing order. Remand leaves nurse status undecided by this Court, places the exclusion burden on its proponent and requires the agency's own lawful rationale. Final authorship and joins after the remaining participant enters await assembly.
