## Refreshed legal alternatives

**Path A: filing-time restriction.** Affirm the relevant appellate judgment, including its direction to dismiss without prejudice the six released plaintiffs' claims barred solely by section 1997e(e). The restriction attaches to the covered action brought during confinement; release recorded in a supplement cannot change that historical statutory condition. This is not a permanent disability to file a different postrelease action, a jurisdictional classification or a decision on the merits.

**Path B: ordinary Rule 15(d) consideration.** Reverse the categorical foreclosure of postrelease supplementation for those six plaintiffs and remand for the actual procedural question and ordinary remaining claims and defenses. The rule permits a supplemental pleading on proper motion and reasonable notice and on just terms even when the original pleading was defective. Its application remains discretionary; release does not require permission, establish compliance or supply a damages right. Mathews supports later-event cure, while McNeil's protected prelitigation administrative sequence remains distinct under this construction.

**Path C: exceptional discretion, but affirmance here.** Anderson would normally dismiss, recognize rare discretion in unusual circumstances and hold remand futile in this case. His example concerns a colorable physical-injury claim already tried to a jury where dismissal would manifestly waste judicial resources; it is not a complete rule or an exclusive trigger. This path would agree with A's judgment while rejecting A's categorical legal scope. It would agree with B that some supplementation is possible while withholding B's ordinary-discretion scope and present remand.

The strongest argument for C is a limited accommodation between the statutory filing restriction and the efficiency purposes of Rule 15(d). The strongest objection from A is that neither the statute nor the Rule explains why an unchanged commencement condition becomes curable when greater litigation effort has accrued; McNeil rejects curing an otherwise forbidden original action by assessing its progress. The strongest objection from B is that Rule 15(d) supplies no exceptional-case prerequisite, and the lower courts' categorical rule cannot itself demonstrate how lawful discretion would be exercised. Procedural economy may influence a proper discretionary decision without becoming a mandatory rarity test or conclusively establishing futility.

## Exact joins, remedy and remaining limits

There is no changed vote. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt A's complete filing-time ground and affirmance. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt B's complete permissible-supplementation ground and limited reversal/remand. No Associate Justice adopts C as the sufficient ground for judgment. The first group shares C's proposed disposition only; the second shares its recognition that some discretion exists only at that general level. Those overlaps are not joins in the full third rationale and are not combined to manufacture a controlling compromise.

For B, remand permits actual consideration of the supplemental pleading's motion, notice and permission requirements, the relevant just terms and all ordinary remaining defenses. It does not require granting leave, deem the original filing lawful, invent physical injury, decide underlying abuse or order damages. C's particular trial example does not establish that any such trial occurred here. Neither the elapsed fifteen-and-a-half months nor the mere absence of that example, standing alone, decides the proper discretionary result.

For A, the existing without-prejudice modification remains effective only for the six plaintiffs' monetary claims barred solely by the provision. The temporal rule supplies no ruling on limitations, tolling or preclusion in a later suit. It does not make every constitutional claim one for mental or emotional injury or adjudicate nominal, punitive, prospective or independent economic relief outside the accepted question. The other five plaintiffs gain no relief from the six plaintiffs' release.

All other expressly stated limits and the source distinctions in original COMMITMENTS_harris remain incorporated: no universal jurisdictional characterization, constitutional-validity decision, invented oral argument or new filing, automatic postrelease exception under Randell, or dependence on same-day Booth. The dispute over actual supplementation remains a remedial issue for B, not an unknown fact chosen to obtain a coalition.

The omitted lower-court third path has now been separately evaluated. The refresh is complete and supported; no additional source or factual premise is required, and no substantive blocker remains.
