# Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District
* **Citation or Docket:** 212 F.3d 738
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-09 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Equal Protection Clause permits a voluntary public-school transfer program to restrict eligibility by race to reduce racial isolation, absent a finding of de jure segregation?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define the scope of review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit vacated a mandatory preliminary injunction requiring admission to a suburban school and remanded for trial. It temporarily protected completion of the ongoing school year if the child had begun attending there.
* **Relief Requested:** The student and parent seek an enforceable opportunity to transfer without the racial exclusion. The districts and program seek preservation of their voluntary integration program and reversal of mandatory interim admission.
* **Material Record:** The Urban-Suburban Interdistrict Transfer Program permits minority pupils to transfer from Rochester to participating suburban districts and nonminority pupils to transfer in the reverse direction. Jessica Haak, a nonminority Rochester fourth-grader when the district court acted, was refused participation in the requested transfer because she did not meet the racial eligibility requirement. The district court ordered transfer to Iroquois Elementary School. The program had operated for decades; the appellate record raised questions about changing demographics, participation levels, and the actual effectiveness of its design. Those concerns are not final findings that every integration benefit is absent. [Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/212/738/632474/).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The exclusion is an actual individual racial classification, unlike a challenge resting solely on district shape. The challenged order is preliminary and mandatory. The record must support both the substantive merits assessment and the equitable prerequisites; a preliminary likelihood determination is not a final finding of educational necessity or entitlement.
* **Relevant Dates:** The appellate decision was May 11, 2000. Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools became effective May 30, 2000 and is part of the law entering this organizationally assigned January 9, 2001 event. Changing grade placement and the continuing effect of the temporary order require confirmation for present relief.

### Issue 1: Supported integration and individual racial burdens

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña establishes one common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications. Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools expressly recognizes supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoiding serious racial isolation as potentially important lawful objectives without a de jure finding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government must establish an important lawful objective and substantial evidence-supported fit; justify coverage, operation and individual burdens; and respond to concrete, practicable alternatives that comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. No presumption of validity follows from an inclusive label.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/212/738/632474/). The regulation and implementation must be evaluated on the actual record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Brown v. Board of Education supplies the prohibition of state-imposed racial caste. The later treatment of Croson and Metro Broadcasting in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña displaces categorical scrutiny formulations while retaining the demand for relevant justification.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools rejects both a universal decree prerequisite and automatic approval of numerical racial targets. It establishes no duty to integrate by preferences and no individual automatic seat remedy.
* **Open Point:** Whether this program’s directional racial eligibility and the burden on this child have the required evidence-supported relationship to actual educational benefits, rather than racial numbers alone.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Family:** Exclusion solely because of race denies an actual educational opportunity. Longevity, demographic change and a modest transfer scale raise substantial doubts that this particular exclusion serves the program’s stated goal. **Program:** Voluntary cross-district integration offers benefits unavailable within racially isolated districts; directional eligibility makes that mechanism possible, and absence of past de jure segregation does not invalidate it. **Unresolved adversarial point:** Statistical imbalance and good intentions cannot establish fit, but imperfect scale does not alone disprove educational benefits. Evidence about actual participating schools, effects, burdens and feasible alternatives is necessary.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A categorical no-decree prohibition is foreclosed by Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools. Affirmance of the program’s ultimate validity is premature without the required fit findings. The proper course may preserve vacatur of the mandatory order while remanding the substantive inquiry under current simulated law, with temporary arrangements reassessed through ordinary equity and the child’s current circumstances.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Eligibility by race is not categorically forbidden in voluntary integration, but this program must satisfy the common justification standard on its actual operation. No final merits endorsement follows from the permissibility of the objective.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.
