## Common commitments by component

All eight Associates support immediate review, reject the challenged expansion of the regulation, and affirm the lower court's whole-document vacatur on this integrated record. They can join the same bounded grounds below; no author is assigned.

1. EPA's settled national monitoring directive is final action under Clean Air Act section 307(b). The agency completed its policy decision and applies the directive through the legally operative permit-objection system, under which a state authority cannot issue a permit over EPA's objection. The disclaimer does not undo that completed commitment and its present consequence for the required permitting framework. Practical pressure or compliance expense alone would not suffice. The facial legal issue does not require a particular permit record, and later review of a permit does not defeat the available statutory route.
2. Section 70.6(a)(3)(i)(B) calls for periodic monitoring on its stated absence trigger; it does not authorize the guidance's universal review and replacement of otherwise prescribed recurring monitoring merely because EPA considers it inadequate. Paragraph (A) preserves applicable testing with its limited equivalent-assurance streamlining permission; paragraph (C) concerns necessary use, maintenance and appropriate installation; paragraph (c)(1)'s assurance language must remain consistent with (a)(3). Those connected provisions do not remove (B)'s condition. The agency may fill an actual gap, including absent periodic testing, no specified frequency or only a one-time startup test. It may adopt a valid new rule through the required procedures. It cannot accomplish this substantive enlargement merely by calling it interpretation.
3. On the supported integration of the challenged and remaining directions, the whole guidance is set aside. The decision adopts no universal severability formula and does not independently invalidate unchallenged monitoring statutes, the 1992 regulation, existing emissions or testing duties, or state action supported by a distinct lawful source. The alternative attack on the 1992 rule is unnecessary; particular permits and future rulemaking remain undecided.

## Enhanced two-path comparison

### Finality and review route

Path A sustains review under Bennett and the earlier Whitman holding. The final national command plus EPA's statutory objection authority establishes more than influential advice. Path B requires regulated parties to wait for an individual permit because the guidance disclaims enforceable rights and permits involve further judgments. Its strongest concern is premature judicial resolution of fact-dependent technical decisions. That concern controls hypothetical permit applications, but not the completed legal demand for universal sufficiency review: the statutory veto system gives the directive present effect in the permitting process. The agency concedes the central position is settled. The Court therefore need not treat all influential guidance as final.

### Interpretation, amendment and remedy

Path A rejects the guidance because the enacted regulatory condition cannot be erased by an assurance purpose. Christensen's regulatory-text holding and Thomas Jefferson's permissible-interpretation rule both require textual fit. The 1992 explanation promising rulemaking to repair deficient federal standards reinforces the conditional structure; it is not a separate estoppel or universally immutable-interpretation rule.

Path B sustains the directive as a reasonable interpretation of monitoring adequate to assure compliance. Its strongest argument is that recurring but useless measurement would frustrate the permit program, and the explanation acknowledged additional requirements. But the broader assurance language is expressly linked to the conditional rule. The text distinguishes the prerequisite monitoring/testing from the gap-filling periodic-monitoring obligation. Reading them as the same universal adequacy instruction eliminates that distinction. The stated problem can support prospective rulemaking; it cannot expand the present rule through an asserted purpose alone.

For remedy, partial vacatur is ordinarily possible when a supported independent remainder can function as intended. Here the lower opinion describes an intertwined directive organized around the challenged sufficiency review, and the supplied record establishes no independently adopted replacement. Whole-document vacatur is therefore supported without turning every interpretation error into total invalidation. The Court does not import a rule requiring notice and comment for every interpretive change, and does not independently adopt every circuit doctrine cited below.
