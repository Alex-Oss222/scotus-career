## Material passages and questions

1. **Comparable programs and forum coverage.** Petitioners' merits brief at pages 4–6 describes the adopted community-use policy and identifies Scouts and 4-H character-development purposes. Respondent's merits brief at pages 9–10 and 29 distinguishes the frequency and timing of Scouts' actual meetings. The reply at pages 5–7 argues that the forum was available at the requested 3:00 time and that exclusion applied to the Club's religious program rather than its meeting time. These are party descriptions; the selected superintendent testimony at JA N10–N11 and N16–N18, and the reproduced petition-appendix undisputed-fact statements and response, supply the underlying record passages for comparison.

2. **Dismissal and immediate meetings.** The briefs place the end of the compulsory day at 2:56 p.m. and the Club meeting at 3:00–4:00. Respondent emphasizes simultaneous school-sponsored activities and the closeness to ordinary instruction; petitioners distinguish private youth programs and challenge the claim that elementary pupils remained for school-sponsored activities. JA P91 through P101 supplies the actual witness passages on the meeting arrangement, prior offsite meetings, access application and parent convenience. The fact that counsel asserts an inference of an extended school day does not make that inference stipulated.

3. **Parental permission and adults.** Both briefing accounts discuss written parental permission. Respondent argues that children remain until parents collect them and that school activities also require permission, limiting the distinction's significance. Petitioners say the Fourniers are not school employees and no employee operates the Club. The reply denies a suggestion that teachers escort pupils. These assertions are not a fresh finding that every alleged form of school involvement is absent. The private-permission arrangement is material; its constitutional effect remains for the new model under entering law.

4. **Membership and recruitment.** Respondent describes an increase from approximately eight children to over twenty after meetings moved to school. The reply at pages 6–8 challenges the baseline and timing, identifying an initial application listing twenty and an affidavit listing twenty-eight before the injunction. The testimony located at JA P12 characterizes the earlier eight-to-ten figure as an estimate after the witness said he lacked records and could not otherwise supply a number. Increased attendance, the time of that increase, and actual onsite solicitation must not be equated without resolving their source support. No source conclusion about complete absence of recruitment evidence is certified here.

5. **Room 119 and institutional presentation.** Respondent describes an ordinary classroom vacated by pupils and teachers shortly before the Club begins. The reply at pages 8–9 calls it a resource room rather than an elementary classroom. The source excerpts preserve the competing accounts and relevant deposition. A classroom label does not itself resolve who conducts the activity or how it is presented.

6. **Procedural record and relief.** Both merits briefs describe cross-motions for summary judgment; the reply points to JA B7–B10 for stipulation of facts and waiver of trial after discovery. The existing packet concerns the defense judgment. The new context must distinguish the legal error in categorical exclusion from the Club's entitlement to final relief, without either inventing a factual dispute or treating counsel's statement of a contested inference as a judicial finding. Petitioners' claim that the establishment issue was not resolved by the appellate court and respondent's preservation arguments should retain their procedural status unless verified in the reproduced lower opinions.

## Remaining source limits

The materials demonstrate concrete competing characterizations of timing, room use, attendance and school activity. They do not authorize this exposed source-extraction context to adjudicate those disputes or decide whether any is material under the existing Bronx Household and Agostini rules. The fresh validator may conclude that the existing rule resolves the exclusion without reaching a disputed detail; it should identify the precise scope rather than demand a speculative new record.
