## Review channel, statutory terms and evidence

The existing grant brings the two stated questions after ordinary full merits submission; no new petition, invented oral argument date or later proceeding is supplied. The taxpayers challenge a specific congressional spending enactment. The lower court rejected the intervenor's Flast objection, and the packet establishes no new jurisdictional defect. The reviewed judgment is summary judgment for officials and intervenor. Facial validity and the actual operation of funded services or public review must be decided separately.

Section 4454, effective August 5, 1997 for services furnished from enactment, replaced named-denomination provisions with religious nonmedical institutions. The revised eligibility remains religious: an institution serves patients relying solely on a religious method of healing for whom medical services conflict with their beliefs; the beneficiary's signed election states both conscientious opposition to nonexcepted medical treatment and inconsistency with sincere religious belief. Equally sincere secular refusal does not qualify merely because it is conscientious. Removal of a denomination's name does not itself answer that distinction.

The Medicare payment rule requires an effective election and a condition that would qualify the patient for the corresponding inpatient-hospital or extended-care benefit in an ordinary hospital or skilled nursing facility, subject to the separate expenditure and sunset provisions. It is not an unrestricted payment for assistance with everyday activities and does not authorize spiritual healing. Institutional qualification, condition-equivalent eligibility, coverage of the particular service and actual payment are different propositions. The lower majority's conclusion that payments purchase a subset of ordinary covered care is a legal and operational conclusion disputed by the dissent, not proof that every payment complies.

Nonexcepted treatment excludes care received involuntarily or required by federal, state or local law. An election normally begins on execution and continues until revoked; voluntary written notice may revoke it, and receipt of nonexcepted treatment reimbursed under Medicare produces statutory deemed revocation. The election form separately warns that receipt of nonexcepted services revokes the election and may limit later services. After two elections and revocations, the next election cannot take effect for one year after the latest revocation, and later elections require five years. No beneficiary-specific compliance, treatment, payment, revocation or waiting-period fact is found here.

Institutions must meet the tax-exempt and lawful-operation conditions, provide the specified round-the-clock physical care through experienced nonmedical personnel, and satisfy the religious nonmedical-service and ownership/affiliation restrictions. The ownership rule disregards interests below five percent; the affiliation rule separately excludes the specified uncompensated governing-body service, defined family relationship and shared-vendor relationships. Accreditation may be accepted only to the extent the Secretary finds reasonable assurance that the corresponding conditions are met. These qualifications do not turn every religious institution or every privately provided service into an eligible recipient.

The institutional utilization committee initially reviews admissions, duration, extended stays and services, records its decisions and operates under further requirements necessary for effective review. The institution must provide required coverage and quality information and satisfy necessary health-and-safety conditions. The patient may object on religious grounds to compelled medical screening, examination, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment or other medical service, but the Secretary retains authority to demand sufficient condition information as a benefit condition. The institutional protection from religiously contrary medical supervision does not bar government review of billed items for noncoverage, excess or fraud. The statute retains final government coverage authority; initial recommendation is not an express religious veto or final civil entitlement determination.

The expenditure provisions separately permit proportional reductions or alternative coverage limitations upon the specified estimate trigger, permit corresponding increases in patient charges, and provide a contingent restriction on new elections after three excessive-expenditure years beginning no earlier than fiscal 2002. No trigger is found to have occurred. The restriction on review of those estimates and reductions is not elimination of ordinary coverage review. Medicaid's statutory substitutions and supervision exemptions remain distinct; the Medicare election text is not silently made the complete Medicaid regime. No severability or constitutional holding below depends on inventing such a merger.

The Cook affidavit described in the lower dissent, appendix pages 178–187, supplies actual evidence of nurses' religious training, prayer and Bible reading. It is not a fabricated concern. It does not itself identify which reimbursed service or payment purchased those activities or show that all physical care is inseparably devotional. The quoted earlier program manual accords great weight to institutional necessity judgments while retaining the intermediary's final responsibility. It supports inquiry into practical deference but does not prove that the revised statute abolishes public review or that every current reviewer rubber-stamps a religious judgment. The original affidavit, payment details and operative implementation materials were not independently supplied to this context; their absence is not litigant default.

## Governing law and competing judgment paths

Agostini's June 23, 1997 unified Lemon inquiry identifies the governmental act and function. Specific prohibitions and distinct accommodation or civil-authority doctrines apply at their actual scope; purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence, not independent fallback prongs. Government may purchase independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting with effective controls, but may not purchase devotional activity or allocate civil power on a religious basis. Kiryas Joel permits accommodation beyond constitutional compulsion while prohibiting its particular denominational governmental franchise. Mitchell, effective June 28, 2000, requires actual financed-function analysis, distinguishes statutory controls from their observance, and rejects affiliation, potential diversion and fungibility as substitutes for proof. Marshfield, February 27, 2001, requires examining continuing governmental conduct despite a real formal change; its monument application supplies no automatic benefits rule.

### Component E: facial religious eligibility and accommodation

**E1 — permissible accommodation in its stated terms.** The religious refusal condition can accommodate access to a preexisting health benefit where a patient otherwise meets the condition-based standard and public payment buys only the independently identifiable physical-care component. Congress may remove a religious obstacle to receiving that component without being constitutionally required to make the identical adjustment for every secular objection. Amos and Kiryas Joel preserve noncompulsory accommodation; the statutory medical-condition limit, defined election and nondevotional coverage limit tether this accommodation to the existing benefit. This permits the accommodation; it does not create a free-exercise right to subsidy or adjudicate every application.

**Strongest objection to E1:** The statute pays for an alternative only when the patient's reason is religious, so it can reward religion and sustain a specifically religious mode of care rather than remove a government-imposed burden. A sect-neutral label, fiscal savings or sincere belief cannot cure that concern. E1's answer is the actual limited function: eligible physical needs receive only the otherwise covered secular component while the patient may decline the medical component. That answer depends on maintaining the coverage limitation in law and operation, not proving that every present payment already satisfies it.

**E2 — forbidden religious financial preference.** The affirmative reimbursement route makes religious profession the condition for access to a benefit refused to an otherwise comparable secular objector. Kiryas Joel's equal-treatment concern and Agostini's limits on government-prescribed religious profession support invalidating that criterion, even without proof that a particular payment bought prayer. Accommodation of private exercise is permissible, but these Justices regard a new exclusive financial route as insufficiently tied to a governmental restriction that must be lifted.

**Strongest objection to E2:** Amos permits religion-specific accommodations beyond what free exercise compels, and the condition-equivalent secular-care limit prevents an independent devotional subsidy. The E2 supporters accept that accommodations need not always include secular objections; their conclusion concerns this public-payment condition and its dependence on religious refusal, not a universal ban on religious accommodation. Remedy must address the unconstitutional criterion under actual severability law; no automatic judicial extension of benefits or immediate nationwide cessation of every lawful service follows.

### Component D: facial allocation of coverage authority

**D1 — no necessary unconstitutional delegation in these terms.** An institution's initial recommendation, followed by governmental authority to require sufficient nonmedical information, review billed services and deny ineligible claims, is not the final civil veto in Larkin or the governmental franchise in Kiryas Joel. The medical-examination exemption restricts a means of obtaining evidence; it does not eliminate the condition standard, all usable information or final government responsibility. All eight Associates adopt this limited legal conclusion, independently of their disagreement about religious eligibility.

**Strongest contrary path:** The committee's religious commitments and the restriction on examination may leave government with no independent basis for rejecting its decision, making formal final review illusory. This could establish an operational defect if proved. It does not follow from the statute alone because nonmedical information and review powers remain available. Neither the old manual's deference language nor the exemption alone proves final control has passed to a religious body. No Justice here holds that formally retained authority necessarily proves meaningful supervision in practice.

### Component O: present operational summary judgment

**O1 — vacate the defense judgment for bounded correct-rule review.** The concrete affidavit and manual evidence require evaluation under the actual financed-function and governmental-control rules, with ordinary summary-judgment treatment of reasonable supported inferences. The lower court's independent-prong analysis and generalized descriptions of secular physical care cannot conclusively answer whether the challenged paid service is operationally separable or the retained review genuinely public. Remand identifies those issues; it does not find payment for prayer, reject all physical-care separability, presume a rubber stamp, reopen every provider, shift the ordinary proof burden or mandate new discovery. Existing material may suffice to grant or deny judgment after the proper analysis. An identified gap in operator retrieval is not a reason for an in-world production order.

**Strongest objection to O1:** Mitchell rejects presumed diversion, and the affidavit links prayer to religious nursing without linking public money to the devotional activity; the manual retains final government responsibility. Requiring another inquiry could transform affiliation and possible diversion into an evidentiary burden against religious providers. The O1 supporters answer that the actual operational evidence must first be assessed for its linkage and materiality under the governing rule; they neither declare that linkage proved nor treat religious identity as sufficient. The remand is a legally confined reassessment of summary judgment, not a compelled trial or a presumption against the program.

**O2 — affirm summary judgment on the supplied operational record.** The statutory controls, objective condition requirement and absence of demonstrated payment for prayer or abandonment of final public responsibility support judgment against these challenges. Mitchell does not require government to disprove every possibility of devotional benefit; the old manual's retained final authority and the distinction between private religious motivation and financed work are material. The strongest objection is the real Cook evidence and the risk that formal controls mask actual practice. The O2 supporters regard those facts as insufficient to show a material financed-function or delegation issue on this record, while preserving a future proved violation and rejecting automatic immunity from nominal supervision.

## Component arithmetic and remedy constraints

- **Facial accommodation permission:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support E1 (five Associates). Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support E2 (three). These conclusions are independent of final judgment votes and must not be flattened into a single bloc.
- **No inherent delegation from the stated statutory recommendation/review arrangement:** All eight support D1. This is not eight votes that actual review was proved effective in every application.
- **Operational disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support O1 and vacatur/remand (five Associates). Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support O2 and affirmance (three). The five share a correct-rule operational remand, while three of them additionally favor invalidating the eligibility criterion. Their additional facial theory is not a condition on joining O1.

The common O1 remand directs correct legal assessment of the supported existing operational claims and ordinary summary judgment, permitting use of the existing record where adequate. It makes no affirmative merits finding of financed prayer or religious final control. It does not order statutory benefits expanded, automatically enjoin all reimbursements, require a new hearing, impose a new documentation scheme, order repayment or decide unpresented individual claims. If a violation is later established, relief must reach the proved function and preserve independently lawful applications under Mitchell. Medicare and Medicaid effects require their own statutory connection and cannot be mechanically merged.
