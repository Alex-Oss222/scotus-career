# Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-901; 531 U.S. 288
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-20 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association’s enforcement of its recruiting rule is state action when public schools and officials substantially compose, govern, and operate the association.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit reversed summary judgment and an injunction for Brentwood Academy on the ground that the association was not a state actor. The district court had treated its action as governmental and reached the constitutional challenge to the recruiting restriction.
* **Relief Requested:** Brentwood seeks reversal of the attribution ruling and adjudication of its constitutional claims. The association seeks affirmance based on its formally private status and the asserted absence of governmental responsibility for this enforcement decision.
* **Material Record:** Public schools constitute approximately 84 percent of the association’s members. School officials serve on its governing bodies and ordinarily meet during school hours. The association regulates interscholastic eligibility and competition, receives substantial gate-receipt funding, and permits its staff to participate in the state retirement system. The State Board of Education has recognized its statewide regulatory role and participates through nonvoting representatives. Brentwood, a private member school, was penalized under a recruiting rule and sued under §1983. These structural facts are distinct from a finding that the rule or its particular enforcement violates a substantive constitutional right.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question concerns attribution, not a final judgment on the recruiting rule’s validity. Institutional governmental character and attribution of a particular genuinely private choice are different routes. Formal incorporation, public support, a public purpose, and official participation must be evaluated in their actual combination. Reversal of the court of appeals’ threshold merits ground does not automatically reinstate all relief ordered below.
* **Relevant Dates:** The historical decision, used for scheduling, issued February 20, 2001. The Sixth Circuit judgment under review is reported at 180 F.3d 758.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

### Issue 1: Governmental institutional action and actual control

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. treats special governmental creation plus permanent governmental majority-appointment authority as sufficient institutional governmental status, despite a private statutory label. American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan separately requires an actual basis for governmental responsibility for a private entity’s challenged choice and rejects attribution from regulation and permission alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether the institution itself operates as government or whether the challenged act is attributable through actual compulsion, encouragement, joint participation, or an exclusively public function. A subsidy or label alone establishes neither.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and 42 U.S.C. §1983. National Collegiate Athletic Association v. Tarkanian supplies a received comparison involving a national association and a state university; its materially different institutional composition must be respected.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. supplies a sufficient combined condition, not an exclusive constitutional checklist for every association. American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan protects genuinely private decisions, not governmental decisions routed through an incorporated body.
* **Open Point:** Whether the public institutions’ actual composition, governance, and official operation establish governmental action here without the precise special-charter mechanism present in Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.

### Issue 2: Consequences of an attribution ruling

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. expressly separates governmental status from liability and immediate reinstatement of an injunction. The substantive speech, procedural, and remedial questions require their own adjudication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Correcting an actor-status error does not establish a constitutional violation, irreparable harm, damages, or entitlement to a particular injunction.
* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary §1983 liability and equitable-remedy rules govern any further adjudication.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The appropriate mandate returns preserved, unresolved claims to the lower courts under the corrected actor-status premise.
* **Open Point:** Which merits and remedial issues remain unresolved after removal of the court of appeals’ attribution ground.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Brentwood relies on public officials’ actual governance and the association’s role in carrying out public-school athletics, arguing that government cannot avoid constitutional constraints by acting collectively through a private corporation. The association stresses voluntary membership, private-school participation, independent incorporation, lack of a state command to impose this penalty, and the nonexclusive character of athletic administration. The court below favored the latter features. The dispute requires evaluating who actually makes and administers the challenged policy, rather than counting public funding alone.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can find state action on the established institutional record and remand for unresolved rights and relief. It can affirm if the relevant public officials act only privately and no sufficient governmental responsibility is established. A factual remand would be appropriate if the officials’ actual role were genuinely disputed and material; it should not be ordered merely to repeat undisputed structural facts. No lawful path makes every statewide voluntary association governmental solely because public schools join it.
