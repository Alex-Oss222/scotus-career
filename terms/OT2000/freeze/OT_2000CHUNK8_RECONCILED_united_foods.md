## Exact material source gap and clean handoff

The original reply, page 9 note 7 continuing onto page 10, does not concede waiver of Central Hudson. It expressly rejects United Foods' waiver claim and explains that the government's reading of the prior decision made a separate presentation unnecessary. The accompanying text nevertheless argues that the program survives a less deferential standard, addressing interest, advancement and fit. The historical Court's statement that the Government did not rely on Central Hudson is a historical judicial characterization, not a substitute for the full original advocacy. The fresh neutral packet must preserve the difference and leave its legal effect for modeling.

The reply also reports a February 2000 Food Marketing and Economics Group document, Mushroom Council Program Effectiveness Review 1999, quotes a conclusion at page 7 and states that copies were lodged with the Court. It reports testimony that voluntary campaigns were unworkable, describes the national scope, and defends exemptions, credits and regional operation. United Foods' opening pages 15–21 dispute preservation, public benefit, efficacy, free riding and fit, invoke a different administrative record, and question regional and brand effects. These are attributed litigation materials, not newly adjudicated findings or a judicially established empirical causal effect. The complete lodged review was not recovered or claimed as read in this handoff.
