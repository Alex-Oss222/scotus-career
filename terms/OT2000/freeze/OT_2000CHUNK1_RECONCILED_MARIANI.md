## Comparison with actual law

The en banc Third Circuit rejected the corporate-source and false-name constitutional challenges on May 18, 2000. There is no historical Supreme Court merits vote, author, separate writing or joinder to replicate. The lower court's result is evidence of a legal path, not an instruction to the eight Associates. Its reliance on historical Shrink Missouri and Austin must be tested against actual Russell, Shrink Missouri, Day, Akins, Reform Party and the effective December 11 Daggett decision.

Actual Daggett sustains Maine's contribution ceiling under an affirmative government burden of sufficiently important anticorruption justification and close fit preserving meaningful association. It creates no dollar safe harbor and no presumption validating a complete corporate-source prohibition. Its responsive-grant holding has five direct joins to a bounded programmatic and operational rationale. It neither converts Day's four-Justice rationale into law nor reclassifies independent expression as a contribution. The same contribution standard governs all eight commitments here; the disagreement is whether the particular treasury exclusion is sufficiently fitted in the demonstrated soft-money setting.

National Right to Work Committee and Austin remain serious contrary authority for the three invalidating Justices, rather than being displaced by an analogy to a low dollar ceiling. NRWC's adjudicated solicitation restriction and its supporting corporate-channel rationale remain effective. Austin adjudicates independent expenditures, expressly leaving its statute's separate contribution prohibition outside the challenge; its reasoning and the distinct writings inform the present inquiry but do not supply the corporate-contribution result automatically. The three preserve those holdings while applying the later actual fit rule to the complete-source burden and developed comparative record. The five sustain on the direct-recipient and voluntary-source distinction, not on a conclusion that corporate speech has no protection, that access alone is corruption, or that every company can successfully operate a fund.

A separate source-only reader checked the complete NRWC opinion and notes and the complete Austin majority, Stevens concurrence, Scalia dissent and Kennedy dissent from the saved official reports. NRWC, 459 U.S. 207?211, also accepts protection of persons whose economic payments were supplied for purposes other than candidate support, corporate-specific prophylaxis extending beyond currently wealthy corporations, and deference in its solicitation setting. Those substantial supporting premises are not omitted or treated as having been overruled; they do not decide the unlitigated contribution question independently of the actual current fit rule. Austin, 494 U.S. 655, expressly limits the issue to independent expenditures. Stevens's candidate-obligation ground appears at 678?679; Scalia's direct-candidate risk concession at 682; and Kennedy's contribution distinction, joined by O'Connor and Scalia, at 702?705. The nonprofit independent-spending exception does not become a direct-contribution exception. These source confirmations preserve the original positions and add no new controlling rationale or join.

The lower record supports candidate participation in party fundraising, party support and donor access, and some coordinated advertising. It does not establish that every soft-money arrangement is lawful or legally equivalent to a direct candidate transfer. Actual Akins requires the applicable transaction, candidate connection, definitions and thresholds; ideological alignment alone establishes no coordination. Neither side may convert contested legislative or legal conclusions into binding adjudicative facts, or use the reimbursement allegations as proof of guilt, knowledge, corrupt exchange or actual influence.

The original full statutory description is retained. The corporate holding concerns the presented business-corporation treasury-to-federal-candidate class; distinct national-bank, congressionally organized corporation, labor, recipient and officer provisions do not receive new independent holdings. The ordinary-course lawful bank-loan exception, communications exclusion, nonpartisan registration/get-out-the-vote exclusion and separate-fund exclusion remain distinct. Section 431's separate membership exclusion and reporting proviso are not imported into §441b.

The common §441f conclusion covers three distinct acts: making a contribution in another person's name; knowingly allowing one's name to be used; and knowingly accepting a contribution made by one person in another's name. The first clause lacks the express knowledge term in the other two. Preserving that difference establishes neither strict criminal liability nor the elements of a particular charge. Discussion of every clause does not find that Mariani was charged under each. Accurate source information retains an independent function even under the three Justices' proposed corporate-source invalidation.

For clarity, the original reservation of supported associational-disclosure claims includes the existing protection where the evidence establishes a reasonable probability of threats, harassment or reprisals. No such showing is established here. That protection permits its own properly supported nondisclosure remedy; it does not create a license to identify another person falsely as the donor. There is no internal blanket §441f exception, no general anonymity forfeiture and no adjudication of every hypothetical enforcement application.
