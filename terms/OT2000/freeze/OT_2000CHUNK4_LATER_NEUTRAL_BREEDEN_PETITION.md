## Record and preserved issues

The October 21, 1994 incident arose during the coworkers' assigned review of a job applicant's psychological report. Breeden conceded that reading the applicant's sexual remark itself did not upset her. Her objection concerned a supervisor's response, a second employee's offer to explain, and their laughter. She complained to the employee and two assistant superintendents. An initial retaliation theory concerns action following that opposition. The distinct later-transfer theory concerns formal administrative and federal litigation activity.

The administrative charge was filed August 23, 1995; federal complaint April 1, 1997. On April 10 Rice told the union representative she was contemplating a transfer. Service occurred April 11. Rice's unchallenged affidavit said she learned of the lawsuit only after April 11; transfer occurred in May. The district-court causation presentation relied wholly on chronology. A preexisting plan does not become retaliation merely because implementation follows notice; actual retaliatory alteration or acceleration would require supporting evidence.

The right-to-sue letter was issued approximately three months before the transfer discussion. Breeden relied on it first in her appellate reply, not below or in her opening appellate brief. The record does not establish that Rice knew of the letter. A statutory or regulatory requirement to notify an employer within ten days is a command, not proof of actual receipt by Rice or a predecessor. Do not assert that she necessarily knew, or necessarily did not know, merely from the legal command.

## Entering law

Harris v. Forklift Systems and `Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite` require objective and subjective abuse assessed in context, without demanding psychological injury. Frequency, severity, threat, humiliation and work interference are relevant; isolated conduct may matter if extremely serious, so no unconditional one-incident bar follows. Faragher's current actual-authority rule and preserved environment finding do not create the historical general affirmative-defense formula and do not replace Harris.

Title VII's opposition and participation protections are distinct. For this decision the Court may assume that objectively reasonable, good-faith opposition to perceived discrimination is protected without resolving its entire boundary. That does not require an employee to prove a completed discrimination violation before seeking protection. Actual protected activity, adverse treatment and a causal connection must nevertheless each be established. No later Supreme Court articulation of retaliation scope, adverse-action breadth or ultimate causation is imported.

Summary judgment requires the evidence and reasonable inferences in Breeden's favor. It does not permit speculation contrary to unchallenged chronology or invention of decisionmaker knowledge. A court of appeals' new theory raises preservation and evidentiary questions separately.

## Supported institutional choices and alternative merits paths

**Possible summary correction.** The two alleged bases can be considered separately: the isolated report-review exchange may be incapable, even under an assumed reasonable-belief protection, of supporting the asserted unlawful-environment belief; the later transfer chronology may independently fail to establish causation from the federal suit or remote administrative charge. A summary disposition must explain why no genuine dispute survives and address the unpreserved right-to-sue theory without inferring actual notice from a statute. Its principal objection is that reasonable opposition can precede a completed hostile environment and that summary review may collapse belief, knowledge and causation. The proposed rule must leave those distinctions intact.

**Possible denial or fuller review.** The issue may be regarded as fact-specific error correction with unsettled opposition boundaries or an insufficiently developed preservation issue. That supports considering denial or plenary handling for an actual institutional reason, not inventing a split or assuming that every factual error deserves summary reversal. The strongest response is that the Court could decide evidentiary insufficiency under established law while assuming the contested reasonable-belief formulation in Breeden's favor, leaving broader doctrine open.

**Possible surviving claim.** Any trial/remand route must identify supported evidence of an objectively reasonable protected concern or retaliatory causation beyond mere sequence, and must address actual decisionmaker knowledge and the timing of the plan. A general statement that the evidence belongs to a jury is insufficient. Conversely, a conclusively adverse merits action cannot declare every future isolated complaint unprotected or every previously contemplated action nonretaliatory regardless of later evidence.

No split, Solicitor General request or specific recurrence record is supplied. Do not invent one. The live institutional considerations are legal clarity under Harris, fact-specific error correction, preservation, separation of two theories, completeness of the record and whether ordinary written submissions permit responsible summary resolution.
