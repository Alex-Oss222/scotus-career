## Reconciliation result

The provisional unanimous non-Stone summary-reversal position is not supportable in its full form. Six Justices retain the proposed summary reversal and its two grounds: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Ginsburg supports summary reversal and restoration of the arbitrator's award but withholds the additional hypothetical-remedy discussion. Stevens dissents from summary disposition; he does not supply an affirmance on the ultimate arbitration merits. These revisions concern the disposition form and scope of agreement, not new facts.

The unchanged source and procedural premises make the historical distinctions strong Justice-specific evidence. Actual Eastern Associated Coal preserves limited section 301 review but does not decide how to identify extreme factfinding that has ceased to apply the agreement or establish a universal remedy once such a departure is found. First Options resolves assent and delegation, and Wright protects independent statutory claims; neither supplies the missing answer. Circuit City's employment exclusion concerns FAA coverage, not this accepted LMRA submission. Nothing in those actual holdings eliminates Stevens's stated concern about resolving the difficult boundary and remedy on the petition record, or Ginsburg's reason for stopping once the award is restored. No plenary briefing or argument has been invented to eliminate either distinction.

## Components available for assembly

**Timely review of the later judgment.** The petition from the December 7, 2000 judgment permits examination of antecedent rulings in the same litigation under Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe. The first appellate mandate binds the district court, not this Court's proper later review. The seven summary-reversal Justices accept this reach. Stevens's procedural dissent does not contest this jurisdictional point but is not a silent join in a Court opinion.

**Award review and actual application.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer agree that courts may not replace an arbitrator's credibility and factual assessment while he is arguably applying the agreement within the accepted submission. Misco supplies the limited-review rule; Enterprise Wheel supplies the actual-contract boundary. The arbitrator sought corroboration and evaluated whether a specific extension offer had been withdrawn because of collusion. The appellate court's different assessment of Smith's credibility did not establish abandonment of that task. General findings against clubs did not establish this particular offer. The rule preserves a genuinely unsubmitted award, misconduct, corruption or other recognized enforcement objection; none is established merely by disagreement over facts.

This is a ruling on the court's power, not an independent holding that Smith or Garvey lied, that the arbitrator's factual analysis was correct, or that no serious error could ever occur. A gratuitous independent endorsement of the arbitrator's factual accuracy is unnecessary and is not supplied by Ginsburg's narrow commitment. Stevens disputes whether the present petition record justifies the Court's assessment of the contract/error boundary and seeks plenary treatment before resolution.

**Independent remedial ground.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer additionally agree that, even when a labor award is properly vacated, a court ordinarily cannot resolve disputed contractual merits and compel its chosen monetary result. Misco's remedial discussion and American Manufacturing preserve the assigned tribunal's function. Vacatur ordinarily leaves further proceedings available only if the agreement permits them; the rule neither orders an additional hearing in every case nor supplies arbitration authority the contract lacks. These six reject the directed sum independently. Ginsburg withholds this additional ground as unnecessary. Stevens disputes its summary resolution, particularly where a valid finding of departure from the agreement and an allegedly inevitable result are assumed.

**Operative remedy.** The seven summary-reversal Justices reverse the directed-award judgment and its erroneous vacatur premise, restoring the legal basis of the district court's denial of the motion to vacate and remanding for consistent proceedings. The mandate must not require a new arbitration simply because the opinion discusses the ordinary remedy for a different, properly vacated award. It awards no judicially selected amount and creates no new enforcement defense or independent statutory claim.
