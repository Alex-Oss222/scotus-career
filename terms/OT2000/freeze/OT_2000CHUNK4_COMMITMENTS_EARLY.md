## 1. Buford v. United States — March 20, 2001

### Question, record and two paths

The question is appellate review of the district court's fact-bound application of functional consolidation to the drug conviction and four concededly related robbery convictions. It is not a comprehensive definition of consolidation or a fresh sentencing classification. The common hearing, single presentence report, counsel's consent letter and concurrent sentences favor treating the proceedings together. Separate filing, drug-branch assignment, prosecutors, pleas and plea judges, two judgments and distinct sentences point the other way. No formal order appears, but its absence cannot be decisive under a doctrine permitting functional consolidation. The claimed addiction connection is an argument, not a finding of a common scheme.

Path A affirms the deferential review allocation for this application while preserving independent review of guideline text, authoritative commentary and legal criteria. Koon/Powell requires legal correction and deference to supported context-sensitive sentencing judgment. Section 3742(e)'s due-deference instruction fits a classification whose significance depends on practical sentencing arrangements and whose particular facts have limited value as a general appellate rule. Stinson makes valid commentary authoritative; it does not turn every application of commentary into independent appellate judgment.

The strongest contrary argument is that a classification increasing the reported range from 84–105 months to 188–235 months needs uniform appellate enforcement. That importance requires correct legal criteria, but does not erase the institutional distinction between defining those criteria and assessing a particular local proceeding. The 188-month sentence is not sustained by unexamined older convictions or a general characterization of the defendant.

Path B would independently apply the legal standard to established facts and return the appeal for that review, without awarding a new sentence. Ornelas and Fields explain why ultimate constitutional search thresholds require independent review while protecting facts and giving due weight to experience. Their functional approach is relevant, but their constitutional standard-setting task does not compel the same allocation for this consolidation determination. First Options likewise protects legal review without deciding all mixed questions. No separately supported legal error in the consolidation criterion is established in the authorized record; a demonstrated error would require correction under either path.

## 2. TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc. — March 20, 2001

### Feature, competing paths and remedy

The asserted claim concerns the overall sign-stand configuration, including springs, legs and uprights. It cannot be dismissed as though MDI expressly claimed only an abstract mechanism or conceded that every appearance feature is functional. But the supplied record identifies the dual springs' useful work in resisting wind and stabilizing the sign, reflected in expired utility patents, and establishes no independently material nonfunctional ornament within the actual claimed configuration.

Path A reverses the functionality-dependent appellate ruling because the claimed useful configuration is functional under Inwood and Qualitex. Patent claims and disclosures are strong evidence of the function of the feature actually claimed in the trade-dress action, not an automatic patent-wide exclusion. When the record establishes that a feature is essential to use or purpose or affects cost or quality, availability of alternative arrangements or concealment does not make that feature nonfunctional. Qualitex's competitive-necessity discussion does not replace its traditional utilitarian rule. Wal-Mart keeps source significance separate; recognition and copying cannot cure functionality.

Path B would require focused further proceedings if the preserved overall-appearance claim identified a distinct nonfunctional feature unresolved by the patent evidence. Its strongest argument is that a combination may create nonfunctional appearance even though its components are useful. The answer here is record-specific: no such distinct feature is established, while the requested concealment or rearrangement would burden access to the useful configuration. The Court cannot create a new ornamental claim. Conversely, Path A cannot rest merely on patent expiration or declare every detail shown in any patent forever ineligible.

The 1999 statutory nonfunctionality burden is not applied retroactively by assertion. The lower court already required proof of nonfunctionality, and the established useful role disposes of this claim without selecting a new trial burden. Secondary meaning is an independent ground unnecessary to reach after functionality disposes of the claim. Review does not disturb the independent WindMaster/WindBuster word-mark judgment or grant TrafFix antitrust recovery; only the functionality-dependent judgment and consequential relief return under the mandate.

## 3. Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams — March 21, 2001

### The competing statutory constructions

All eight reach the coverage question on the reported original federal compulsion action and appellate review. Adams is a retail sales counselor, not an established transportation worker. His antecedent state discrimination and tort action does not turn this federal suit into the state-court enforcement posture on which Scalia and Thomas dissented in Allied-Bruce. The FAA is not an independent jurisdictional grant. The targeted lower-court extract establishes that Circuit City asserted section 1332 along with FAA section 4. This identifies its asserted diversity basis, not a finding of citizenship, amount or satisfaction of every jurisdictional requirement. No contested jurisdictional defect is established.

Path A construes section 1's residual employment category by its named seamen and railroad classes, producing a transportation-worker exclusion. The distinct section 2 and section 1 commerce formulations do different work. It reverses the categorical exclusion of all employment contracts and remands. Its strongest contrary argument is that the language must be understood in its enactment-era labor and commerce setting and that broadening section 2 while freezing a narrow exclusion produces an artificial asymmetry. The response is that the enumerated worker classes and residual structure remain enacted limitations; statutory policy or later breadth cannot erase them.

Path B reads the employment exclusion in that broader contemporary labor context, treating the named workers as examples of employment contracts then within federal commerce authority rather than an exhaustive transport genus. Its strongest objection is that this deprives the enumeration and different commerce phrases of work. The response is that the named categories remove doubt about central federal employment arrangements, while the residual clause prevents that coverage from swallowing the wider employment exclusion; section 2's commercial reach and section 1's employment carveout perform distinct functions. The submitted sources support this interpretive argument but do not establish a particular legislative quotation or enacted finding. No such evidence is fabricated.

Gilmer arbitrated statutory employment claims under a securities-registration agreement and did not decide the employment-contract exclusion. It cannot resolve either construction by substitution. Allied-Bruce explicitly reserved section 1, while First Options and Casarotto preserve consent and genuine general defenses after coverage. Eastern Associated Coal applies labor-award doctrine and supplies no section 1 answer.

## 4. Egelhoff v. Egelhoff — March 21, 2001

### Actual state rule and distinct federal questions

The claims concern designated ERISA life-insurance and pension benefits after the April 1994 divorce and death just over two months later without redesignation. The targeted validated source establishes that the former spouse received $46,000 of insurance proceeds before the children's action against her. Pension payment status remains unestablished; the separate recovery action does not prove that the pension is unpaid. No qualifying domestic-relations order, independent later bargain, protected survivor-annuity entitlement or slayer claim is established. Neither pension anti-alienation nor section 1055 can be applied indiscriminately to both assets. The children assert the divorce-revocation statute, not a newly invented agreement; the caption identifies Samantha as a minor and does not establish that both children are adults.

The state statute revokes a predivorce former-spouse provision and directs the asset to pass as though the former spouse died when the decree was entered. Revocation does not apply to the extent the governing instrument expressly provides otherwise; a decree requires the death benefit for the former spouse or children and no other assets satisfy that requirement at death; or the decedent could not revoke unilaterally immediately after the decree because of its terms or another reason. No particular exception is established here.

Payor protection before defined actual knowledge differs from the rule determining entitlement. After qualifying knowledge, the statute imposes its payment liability; it also permits notice, withholding during dispute or uncertainty, and specified security. The payor definition requires sufficiently specific postdeath written notice and reasonable time to act. Recipient protections for value purchasers or debt satisfaction without knowledge and the separate personal-knowledge/document definition do different work. Their more-than-thirty-day and fewer-than-five-business-day presumptions are rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence. These terms do not create an unconditional duty to withhold, a universal safe harbor, or a finding that notice occurred. The statute's transition says it applies as of July 25, 1993 to decrees after July 24, 1993; the enrolled amendment's January 1, 1995 effective date remains separately identified. The April 1994 decree falls in that specified post-July-1993 category. No new dispute over later amendments or retroactivity is invented, and no other date or fact is inferred.

Path A preempts the divorce-revocation rule as applied to these plan designations because it replaces the governing beneficiary rule and directly controls payment, rather than merely changing outside costs. The instrument's ability to opt out does not eliminate that default command: without opt-out it substitutes the beneficiary; with notice it changes the payor's duties. Travelers and Dillingham protect independent state cost rules, not every general law. UNUM distinguishes saved insurance terms from administrative reassignment. This general nonprobate rule is not transformed into an insurance-specific rule merely because one asset is insurance. Boggs's controlling antecedent-transfer analysis prevents its covered pension claims from becoming valid solely through later receipt. Applying that source-of-title reasoning to this already paid welfare-insurance claim requires a new section 514 beneficiary-administration application; it is not an existing Boggs holding that pension anti-alienation governs insurance.

Path B preserves the paid-insurance recipient claim while preventing any change to plan payment duties, on the recipient-accounting distinction O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer affirmatively supported in Boggs. Its strongest argument is now concrete: the plan has paid its named beneficiary, and the suit seeks money from that recipient under a general family-property rule; the remedy can be confined to her without reopening plan administration. Their Boggs position did not require a new later contract: it disputed whether a preexisting state ownership rule necessarily creates a plan-beneficiary claim when enforced only against the recipient. Because the present asset is welfare insurance rather than the pension property addressed in Boggs, that distinction also requires attention to the different federal statutory protections.

Path B's strongest counterargument remains the legal source of the children's title: it derives from divorce-based displacement of the designation, rather than a genuinely independent later transaction, and the same statutory scheme can impose duties on payors. The five antecedent-transfer supporters reject separating recovery from that asserted entitlement. The three recipient-accounting supporters answer through relief confined to the paid insurance and recipient, with no plan liability or redirection and no pension anti-alienation rule imported into welfare benefits. They would not uphold the statute's direct plan-payment commands. For the pension claim, the unresolved payment and relief predicates prevent treating it as the same completed recipient operation; their proposed disposition is a bounded remand under the stated distinction, not an assumed unpaid-pension finding. Any departure they seek from Boggs's governing pension antecedent-transfer rule is identified below as maintained disagreement, not controlling law.

## 5. Ferguson v. City of Charleston — March 21, 2001

### Search, purpose, consent and relief are separate

The public hospital's collection and chemical testing are governmental searches under Vernonia, Chandler and Skinner. Obtaining prenatal care is no automatic waiver. For the reviewed special-needs question only, legally adequate consent is assumed absent. The jury's defense verdict was on instructions requiring consent to collection, cocaine testing and possible police disclosure; the patients preserved a sufficiency challenge, and the Fourth Circuit did not decide it. The verdict cannot be silently discarded or treated as finally affirmed on that issue.

Path A rejects the special-needs justification for the described program and remands the consent question and remaining litigation. Edmond requires examination of the program's actual operation rather than an ultimate benevolent aspiration. Police design and cooperation, chain of custody, specified arrest rules and actual arrests make criminal evidence collection an operative means of this program. Four patients were arrested under the initial version without a treatment alternative; later arrests followed noncompliance or another positive. Treatment and maternal/fetal welfare are legitimate ends, but prosecution pressure toward those ends does not itself move this collection outside ordinary law enforcement. Chandler requires a concrete qualifying need, not merely a serious social concern. Vernonia's confidential, nonpolice athletic program supplies no authority for this operation.

The strongest contrary Path B would sustain a genuinely independent clinical search within a supported medical purpose, or uphold an adequately consensual search. The supplied program is not ordinary clinical testing followed by incidental reporting, and no independently sufficient clinical operation is established that can detach the challenged collection from its prosecution design. That defeats the offered special-needs ground. It does not decide that every medical test requires a warrant or that discovery of evidence during otherwise lawful care cannot be reported. Actual valid consent remains an independent route, to be addressed through the preserved issue and proper review, not inferred from treatment attendance or eliminated by the purpose holding.

## 6. Gibbs v. Babbitt — March 21, 2001

### The statutory class and genuinely different routes

The challenge concerns the private-land application of the experimental red-wolf taking rule. Under controlling Home Builders, the regulated class is wildlife taking without a commercial element. Concern for cattle and the commercial character of an owner's operations do not rewrite that class. Mann's earlier guilty plea is background; no conviction is reviewed here. The supplied numbers, interstate howling visitors, volunteers and scientific work are actual reported record evidence; projected revenues and a possible future pelt trade are distinct predictive or speculative material. Prior interstate transportation of captive animals does not itself establish the current necessary connection.

**Route A — necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime.** The government must identify the particular valid interstate regulation and support why excluding private-land taking permits evasion or materially impairs that regulation. Species-wide conservation, the Act's comprehensiveness and federal refuge ownership do not themselves establish the commerce anchor. A relationship between the protected population and an operating interstate tourism or research resource is materially more concrete than national concern, but a court must still identify the actual federal interstate regime and the necessary-inclusion relationship. The present lower rationale did not resolve those elements under Home Builders. The strongest contrary argument to a remand is that the government has merely renamed conservation a regime without showing its commerce validity; that would require confined relief if the existing record supplies nothing more.

**Route B — independent substantial interstate externality.** The court must identify a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable effect generated by the taking class, materially supported substantiality, inability of affected States reasonably to contain or internalize that effect through ordinary territorial authority, and fit of the prohibition. Movement from federal refuge to neighboring private land shows local mobility and possible program vulnerability, not state-border movement or territorial incapacity. Interstate visits show actual interstate use, but do not alone prove that North Carolina cannot internalize the conservation and tourism consequences. The record's potential functional relationship deserves examination; no crossing, migration pattern, interstate population mechanism, quantified effect or failed agreement is invented. Solid Waste's four-Justice separate position involved a shared mobile interstate bird population and loss of habitat beyond another State's control; it does not supply those facts for red wolves.

**Path C — the broader proposed class-effects rule.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Morrison dissent would permit a materially supported rational substantial-class-effects relationship without the controlling connection prerequisite. Actual interstate visits, organized study and the irreplaceable live population can support a reasoned class-wide relationship more directly than a conjectural future pelt market. On that proposed rule the modest size of one population does not defeat a supported class analysis, and direct prevention of killing fits protection of the resource supplying the observed activity. This is a proposal to change governing law, not an assertion that several deficient Home Builders routes sum to a sufficient one. Its strongest objection is precisely the existing rule: allowing downstream tourism and research consequences to supply the connection risks the general police power that Lopez and Morrison forbid. Its supporters must own that doctrinal disagreement and retain substantiality, evidence, reasonable means and judicial review.

The protective exceptions can support fit after power is established. They include the supplied nonintentional/nonwillful and human-life terms, taking during an actual livestock or pet attack with freshly wounded or killed animals evident, nonlethal noninjurious harassment, and written approval after abandoned capture efforts, with reporting conditions. They establish no general permission to kill a wolf merely feared to threaten cattle. No treaty or Property Clause holding is substituted for the granted Commerce question.
