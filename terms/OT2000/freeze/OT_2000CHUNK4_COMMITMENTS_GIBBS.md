## 6. Gibbs v. Babbitt — March 21, 2001

### The statutory class and genuinely different routes

The challenge concerns the private-land application of the experimental red-wolf taking rule. Under controlling Home Builders, the regulated class is wildlife taking without a commercial element. Concern for cattle and the commercial character of an owner's operations do not rewrite that class. Mann's earlier guilty plea is background; no conviction is reviewed here. The supplied numbers, interstate howling visitors, volunteers and scientific work are actual reported record evidence; projected revenues and a possible future pelt trade are distinct predictive or speculative material. Prior interstate transportation of captive animals does not itself establish the current necessary connection.

**Route A — necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime.** Under Home Builders Part II-A, the court must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship in which inclusion of that class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing the regulation. The Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of listed wildlife may include unauthorized taking as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system, without separate proof that every species crosses state lines, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. Section 10(j) experimental populations are part of the Act's authorized preservation and recovery system, and the red-wolf rule's tailored exceptions show a regulation adapted to that function rather than a general claim of federal control over private land. Species-wide conservation as a declared objective, an assembly of unrelated provisions and federal refuge ownership do not themselves establish the commerce anchor, and an operating interstate tourism or research resource is evidence rather than the connection. The strongest contrary argument is that treating the Act's integration as sufficient makes a comprehensive statute its own constitutional justification; Part II-A answers it by allowing a challenger to show that the application is outside the class, that the interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual.

**Route B — independent substantial interstate externality.** The court must identify a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable effect generated by the taking class, materially supported substantiality, inability of affected States reasonably to contain or internalize that effect through ordinary territorial authority, and fit of the prohibition. Movement from federal refuge to neighboring private land shows local mobility and possible program vulnerability, not state-border movement or territorial incapacity. Interstate visits show actual interstate use, but do not alone prove that North Carolina cannot internalize the conservation and tourism consequences. The record's refuge/private-land relationship is not shown to be a cross-border mechanism; no crossing, migration pattern, interstate population mechanism, quantified effect or failed agreement is invented. Solid Waste's four-Justice separate position involved a shared mobile interstate bird population and loss of habitat beyond another State's control; it does not supply those facts for red wolves. Under Home Builders Part II-B the route remains an alternative; it need not be satisfied where Route A establishes power.

**Path C — the broader proposed class-effects rule.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Morrison dissent would permit a materially supported rational substantial-class-effects relationship without the controlling connection prerequisite. Actual interstate visits, organized study and the irreplaceable live population can support a reasoned class-wide relationship more directly than a conjectural future pelt market. On that proposed rule the modest size of one population does not defeat a supported class analysis, and direct prevention of killing fits protection of the resource supplying the observed activity. This is a proposal to change governing law, not an assertion that several deficient Home Builders routes sum to a sufficient one. Its strongest objection is precisely the existing rule: allowing downstream tourism and research consequences to supply the connection risks the general police power that Lopez and Morrison forbid. Its supporters must own that doctrinal disagreement and retain substantiality, evidence, reasonable means and judicial review.

The protective exceptions show how the rule is adapted to its function within the Act's system. They include the supplied nonintentional/nonwillful and human-life terms, taking during an actual livestock or pet attack with freshly wounded or killed animals evident, nonlethal noninjurious harassment, and written approval after abandoned capture efforts, with reporting conditions. They establish no general permission to kill a wolf merely feared to threaten cattle. No treaty or Property Clause holding is substituted for the granted Commerce question.

**Provisional structure:** Stevens and Souter support affirmance on the proposed broader rule and, independently, under Route A as Home Builders Part II-A states it. O'Connor and Kennedy support affirmance under Route A. Scalia and Thomas support reversal with confined relief. Ginsburg and Breyer support the broader affirmance ground and expressly record the narrower Route A affirmance if that broader rule is not adopted. These alternatives must be resolved in lawful assembly, after reconciliation and the remaining participating Justice's position; they are not simultaneous votes for inconsistent judgments, an equal division, or a present Court holding.

**Route A content supported by its six possible joiners:** Identify the valid federal interstate regulation of listed species, the intrastate class the private-land taking rule covers, and the genuine functional relationship by which that rule preserves the populations and enforceability of the interstate system. No proof is required that each animal crossed a state line, that the landowner acted commercially, or that the population independently produces substantial interstate economic effects. Actual interstate visitors are not erased because revenue forecasts are disputed, but they are not the connection; local wolf mobility is not silently enlarged into state-border ecology. The application supplies no new fact, federal land-title holding, permission to take animals beyond the exceptions, or compensation. Morrison prevents treating every inadequate argument as an entitlement to another chance; the Route A ground needs no further evidentiary proceeding.
