**H-F1 — Statutory duration.** Under the contemporaneous section 546(d), the district court may appoint after the Attorney General appointment expires under section 546(c)(2), and that appointee serves until the vacancy is filled. Section 541's four-year language does not terminate this judicial appointment at four years; its own holdover direction also defeats a claim of automatic cessation merely on an anniversary. Section 546(c)'s earlier-of presidential qualification or 120-day limit governs the Attorney General route; the judicial route has its separate endpoint. Presidential removal under section 541(c) remains. **H-F2 — Combined-control classification.** United States Attorneys, including this incumbent, are inferior officers under the actual statutory organization. Sections 516 and 519 place duties and litigation under the Attorney General's direction, section 518 permits taking or reassigning litigation, and section 541(c) permits presidential removal; the ordinary appointment route remains available. Substantial district-level prosecutorial powers do not create an office independent of those actual controls. The Attorney General is not attributed a general discharge power. ### Final judgment, propositions and remand

**Judgment:** Reverse the South Carolina Supreme Court's death-sustaining judgment on the federal parole-information challenge and remand for further proceedings consistent with S-F1–S-F3. **S-F1 — The actual jury choice controls.** When South Carolina's capital jury finds the statutory aggravator and chooses punishment, its alternatives are death and life without ordinary parole. A lesser judicial sentence available if no aggravator is found is not an alternative at that jury choice and does not categorically remove the proceeding from Simmons. At the preliminary aggravator factfinding stage the jury is not yet exercising the death-or-life sentencing discretion addressed by this rule.

**S-F2 — The actual combined explanation is inadequate if the State raised danger.** Where the State places future dangerousness in issue and the relevant noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to give the jury that accurate legal information through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. Here the repeated until-death explanations and permitted natural-life argument did not supply an adequate opportunity when the court refused the requested express parole explanation and, after the parole questions, directed the jury to put eligibility and ineligibility outside its consideration. The full communication left the distinction between the duration of a life term and the possibility of ordinary release unanswered and unavailable as the response to a State future-danger case.

This is a legal sufficiency determination on the corrected presentation, conditional on the danger predicate. The general wording of the questions does not prove the jurors' actual thinking, but does not make the actual life alternative irrelevant; the final exclusion of parole leaves the legal ambiguity unresolved. The Court does not require the word parole, a statutory recital, an invariable judicial instruction, or a guarantee against clemency or later legislation. Accurate effective defense presentation or an equivalent adequate judicial explanation remains sufficient. Truthful prison-danger evidence remains available to the State.

**S-F3 — What remains on remand.** The state court must determine whether the prosecution's evidence or argument actually put future dangerousness in issue. Neither the questions alone nor the limited prosecution recitation supplies that finding. If the predicate is satisfied, S-F2 has already resolved inadequacy and the state court must not reopen it as an undecided issue. It must apply the governing direct-review constitutional error standard: the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to selection of death. If the State cannot meet that burden, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it pursues death; lawful noncapital disposition remains available. If the State did not raise future danger, this Simmons due-process claim alone establishes no violation.

The remand orders no immediate new penalty hearing, present sentence substitution, or conclusive finding of harmlessness or harmfulness. It does not itself vacate the death sentence.
