## Component alternatives and their strongest objections

**Current custody and review channel.** One path permits a current-sentence section 2254 claim resting on the unconstitutional use of a prior conviction, subject to ordinary habeas gates and a real sentencing connection. Its strongest objection is that it allows recurring review of otherwise final, independently expired judgments whenever a later sentence considers them. A second path extends Custis's ordinary finality limitation to this indirect challenge, preserving at least its qualifying complete-denial-of-counsel exception. Its strongest objection is that Custis relied on a different statutory forum and expressly did not resolve the person denied meaningful review. A third, narrower path recognizes a current-sentence avenue where a properly pursued challenge received no meaningful adjudication through no fault of the prisoner; it must determine the actual review history rather than deem prolonged silence either default or faultlessness. Its strongest objection is an additional, undefined litigation over diligence and state process, potentially duplicating ordinary exhaustion and default law.

**Actual state process and access.** Fourteen years without a decision is powerful evidence of an ineffective corrective process, but a rule turning on absence of prisoner fault cannot be conclusively applied without the actual reason and materially available steps. The narrow exception may support vacatur and a bounded return; it cannot make the constitutional claim itself valid. A Justice supporting the broader channel need not impose this new faultlessness condition, but must retain the former statute's actual procedural requirements and any properly applicable default law.

**Constitutional invalidity.** The lower courts disagreed about prejudice at the 1986 trial, and the Third Circuit resolved that issue in Coss's favor. This packet supplies that disposition, not the trial evidence needed for fresh independent adjudication of Strickland prejudice. A channel ruling can leave that finding in place or unreviewed within its lawful scope; it cannot call the old conviction invalid merely from deficient performance, nor declare it valid because the claimant has other convictions. Glover's ordinary prejudice rule does not create a collateral-review channel or transplant its actual findings here.

**Effect on current custody.** Tucker requires a connection between unconstitutional predicates and present punishment. Removal of points does not answer discretionary selection within the range. Existing findings of consideration and their appellate treatment must be respected; their legal sufficiency can be assessed if properly before the Court. The strongest no-effect argument relies on the complete elimination of points and the sentencing judge's several independent reasons. Its strongest objection is that those circumstances do not negate the found consideration of the challenged conviction, and the published account raises a substantial preservation problem for a new within-range causation attack. No Justice is committed here to a final no-effect finding on this packet.

**Relief.** The current-sentence claim does not produce a freestanding writ against an expired sentence. The actual lower remedy gives the State a retry-or-resentence alternative; it must not be described as an unconditional sentence reduction or an already granted release. A lawful return preserves independent valid convictions and lawful custody, and does not establish an exact shorter sentence. Any necessary further review must concern a real open prerequisite, not an automatic repetition of the completed evidentiary hearing or reexamination of every finding.
