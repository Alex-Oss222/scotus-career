## Statutory qualifications remain operative

The complete then-applicable Washington section remains at `runtime/OT_2000CHUNK4_EGELHOFF_STATUTE.md`. Its revocation rule treats the former spouse as having predeceased the decedent at the decree's entry, subject to three separate exceptions, only to the extent each applies: the governing instrument expressly provides otherwise; the decree requires a death benefit for the former spouse or children and other assets satisfying that requirement do not exist at death; or unilateral revocation immediately after the decree would have been unavailable because of its terms or another reason. No exception is established merely by the default clause. The question whether an instrument expressly provides otherwise must be answered from actual language, not presumed from designation alone or from a contingent invalidity provision.

Entitlement, payor protection and recipient liability remain separate. The statute protects specified payor action before defined actual knowledge, authorizes liability after that knowledge, permits withholding where entitlement is disputed or uncertain pending written consent of all claimants or a proper court's direction, and permits written notice and the specified indemnity-security option. The security may be a bond for twice the asset's death-date fair market value or the adverse claim, whichever is less, or a similar indemnifying instrument. Payor actual knowledge requires sufficiently specific postdeath written notice and a reasonable opportunity to act. Specified purchasers for value or recipients satisfying enforceable obligations without actual knowledge receive separate protection; the recipient provision otherwise requires return or value liability from one not entitled under the statute. Recipient knowledge has its own personal-knowledge or document definition. Both knowledge provisions retain the more-than-thirty-day and fewer-than-five-business-day presumptions, rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence. The statutory asset definition and transition provision remain as supplied; no newly asserted fact establishes an applicable exception or notice event.

The existence of protected payment before notice is not a holding that the statute imposes no plan-administration duty. Conversely, a recipient claim concerning an already paid asset is not automatically an instruction to the plan to repay. Each asserted obligation and remedy must be identified rather than conflated.

One supported construction treats the state revocation rule as supplying a validity rule in a field the actual documents leave open. On this account, applying the investment plan's fallback after a valid state-law determination may implement its terms rather than replace them; the insurance claim may concern a recipient obligation after completed payment. This path must establish that state law may supply the particular validity rule consistently with ERISA, explain the fallback's actual trigger, and confront the statute's prospective payor duties and beneficiary-entitlement effects. Calling a rule interpretive or domestic-relations law is not enough. Insurance-plan silence about invalidity's consequences supplies neither affirmative adoption of state revocation nor immunity from it.

A second supported construction treats automatic revocation as the state-law substitution of a beneficiary rule for an otherwise effective plan designation. On this account, a document's provision for genuinely invalid designations does not license every state rule that labels a designation invalid, and permitting instruments to opt out does not necessarily remove the administrative command. This path must answer the actual default and silence, identify the federal conflict for each asset, and explain why the asserted validity rule is a replacement rather than permissible background construction. It cannot assume the answer by describing every designation as unconditionally valid under federal law.

The paid insurance claim requires its own recipient analysis.
