## Why the two departures are not yet supported

Scalia historically wrote Vernonia, joined Chandler and dissented in Edmond on a mixed-function theory. The existence of corresponding actual commitments therefore does not alone explain the changed Ferguson result. Actual Vernonia's no-waiver holding concerns participation in athletics; it does not by itself settle the scope of consent to voluntarily supplied clinical samples. Actual Chandler rejects an unsupported candidate-testing need; it does not decide that a genuine clinical purpose disappears when enforcement is added. Actual Edmond's requirement of a real independent justification must be applied, not converted into a factual finding that no clinical function existed.

Thomas's actual unjoined Edmond portion is more expressly confined to automobile stops than a general hostility to purpose review. That can matter to his framework here, but the historical Ferguson Part II argument itself assumed a bodily search and relied on medical purpose and Griffin. A traffic/body distinction alone does not meet the changed-premise test for that argument. No altered hospital policy, new patient-specific consent finding or different clinical chronology has been established.

The prior neutral handoff quoted the District Court's Fourth Amendment rejection of independent purposes and the appellate court's contrary understanding. It omitted the precise trial finding, made on the abandoned Title VI claim, that the policy's goal was treatment and protection of mother and child. It also did not supply the exact chronology showing clinical testing and referrals before the police-cooperation policy. Those passages do not decide the Fourth Amendment issue, but they materially constrain any statement that an independent clinical function is wholly unsupported or that the medical purpose is pretextual. The legal inference about the later program remains contested.
