## Final common ground and remedy

Section 706(g) authorizes appropriate affirmative action, including but not limited to reinstatement, and other equitable relief. Compensation for employment loss between judgment and reinstatement, or in place of reinstatement when it is inappropriate, falls within that existing remedial authority. Section 1981a(a)(1) adds relief to that scheme; subsection (b)(2) excludes all section 706(g) relief from the new compensatory category. Accordingly subsection (b)(3)'s ceiling for damages awarded under section 1981a does not cap these forms of front pay. Future pecuniary language still governs losses within the new category; monetary form and postjudgment timing do not override the express exclusion.

The contrary route would place all future earnings in the cap or distinguish delayed return from unavailable return. Each Associate rejects it because the cap applies only after the statutory category is identified, and the preserved affirmative-action authority encompasses both reinstatement-related forms. The holding concerns proper equitable restoration, not every award labeled front pay.

Reversal permits lower-court assessment of appropriate front pay without the section 1981a cap. The court must determine proper reinstatement or its substitute, causally attributable employment loss and a supported award, observing ordinary mitigation and avoidance of overlap. No fixed duration, discount rate, factual outcome, sum, reinstatement order or new trial is prescribed. Liability, backpay and fees are not reopened by this question. Compensatory and punitive damages retain their own limits and cannot duplicate the same loss. No general jury-allocation or agency-deference issue is decided. There is no unresolved reconciliation blocker.
