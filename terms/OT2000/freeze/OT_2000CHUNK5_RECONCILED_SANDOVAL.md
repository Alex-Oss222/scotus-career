## Adversarial and scope checks

The application path's strongest objection is that Title VI deliberately combines individual beneficiary protection, authorized implementing rules and a historically received action, making private prospective enforcement part of the integrated statute. That is the actual basis of the four reconsideration commitments. It cannot establish that Powell is distinguishable: Powell itself involved two prospective claims and decided the same broader regulatory obligation. The four application commitments find that argument insufficient for the particular action, consistently with their full Powell joins and McClellan's contextual but nonautomatic inquiry.

The reconsideration path's strongest objection is statutory stare decisis: Congress may amend the statute, the prior ruling is directly on point, and neither the English-only policy nor proved rather than alleged effects changes the action question. The four reconsideration commitments confront that cost expressly and retain the specific prior dissenting construction. Their support is for this Title VI route, not for a general cause of action for every valid regulation or a rule that prospective relief escapes action-creation requirements. They seek no holding on Powell's separate section 1983 branch in this grant.

The report's discussion of regulations genuinely interpreting an existing statutory right does not erase the distinction between such elaboration and the independent effects duty. It remains compatible with Blessing at its own section 1983 scope; the two enforcement channels require independent analysis. Administrative remedies, statutory prerequisites for agency sanctions and the established intentional-discrimination action remain distinct. No funding cutoff, private exhaustion requirement, damages entitlement, intent finding or invalidation of the effects regulations is supplied.

Reversal removes the asserted action as a basis for the injunction and returns the matter for ordinary proceedings consistent with that decision; no unpleaded claim or guaranteed amendment is invented. Affirmation sustains the granted enforcement route without independently deciding intent or regulatory validity. ## Components and lawful alternatives

The question is whether to apply Powell's April 3, 2000 direct-action holding or expressly reconsider it. Powell already concerned prospective relief. An injunction distinction therefore cannot preserve this action while pretending to follow Powell. The distinct section 1983 holding is not a claim supplied by this grant. Regulatory validity and the finding of prohibited effects remain assumed; neither becomes a constitutional intent finding.

Applying Powell requires reversal of the injunction insofar as it rests on this direct regulatory-effects action and remand under ordinary preserved claims and defenses. The best contrary argument is that Title VI's beneficiary language, expressly authorized implementation, Cannon's received enforcement background and subsequent legislative treatment authorize prospective enforcement of the implemented protection. McClellan permits that contextual inquiry, but does not erase Powell's directly contrary answer. Franklin supplies relief only after identifying an action; the requested injunction cannot create its own authority.

Reconsideration requires express displacement of Powell's direct-action branch. Its strongest objection is enhanced statutory stare decisis: Congress can change the statutory rule, and neither different regulated conduct nor a finding rather than an allegation of effects changes the action-creation question. Justices favoring reconsideration retain their previously published Powell objection; their present ground is correction of that recent departure from the established Title VI enforcement setting, not an invented intervening amendment or factual distinction. They give the rule's youth and absence of an identified concrete reliance arrangement weight without treating either as a sufficient general license to overrule statutory precedents. They do not seek a new holding on Powell's separate section 1983 branch here.

## Compatibility, remedy and limits

Four Associates support application of Powell; four support express reconsideration of its direct-action branch. This is a judgment split, not an entered tally for the Court. Each group has a shared ground at the stated scope. The reconsideration group can join a common prospective-action explanation that confronts statutory stare decisis; it cannot join merely describing the effects duty as already privately enforceable under controlling law. The application group can join a short application of Powell and contextual explanation without adopting a categorical ban on implied actions.

Reversal removes this action's support for the injunction; it finds neither that the policy is valid nor that the regulations are invalid. Affirmance on the granted question sustains the asserted enforcement route and does not adjudicate a separate intentional-discrimination theory or create an unpleaded claim. Neither position supplies damages, a funding cutoff, or a new administrative exhaustion requirement. No additional material source is required for these bounded legal positions.
