# INS v. St. Cyr

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment permitting habeas consideration and requiring access to former §212(c) consideration.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand through the appropriate agency process for a lawful discretionary determination. Do not grant the waiver, retry the criminal case, or adjudicate unavailable factual equities.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Independent legality review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide jurisdiction under §2241 as applied to the legal ineligibility claim. Because no clear repeal is established, the statutory ground supplies jurisdiction without deciding the complete constitutional minimum under the Suspension Clause.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the cited amendments clearly displaced that independent judicial remedy.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and extend the existing preserved-habeas doctrine to the permanent provisions.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** General restrictions on immigration judicial review, and repeal of a separate immigration-specific habeas provision, do not clearly repeal §2241 jurisdiction over a properly brought claim that executive action rests on a legally erroneous eligibility rule. The court may determine eligibility law without exercising immigration discretion.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The particular legal claim and jurisdictional source.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** St. Cyr asks whether the Attorney General has authority to consider relief, a legal issue already distinguished from discretion by Reno v. Goncalves. Neither the comprehensive nature of a new system nor the importance of immigration administration supplies omitted repeal language.

  The statutory clear-statement rule and the already recognized separation of legality review from discretion are decisive. The Suspension Clause and the historical function of habeas reinforce that reading; they are not used to invent a repealed jurisdictional grant. A narrow statutory resolution supplies an effective legal forum without deciding every possible restriction on judicial review.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Factual or discretionary review beyond this legal eligibility claim is outside the question presented.

### Issue 2: The completed plea transaction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the temporal construction after recognizing jurisdiction.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether eliminating eligibility changes the legal consequences of St. Cyr's earlier eligible plea.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** An application of Landgraf v. USI Film Products to a question left open in Reno v. Goncalves.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent a sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, repeal of former §212(c) eligibility does not apply to a resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a preenactment guilty plea and who was eligible to seek that relief under the law then governing the plea. The protected consequence is access to lawful consideration, not a guaranteed favorable exercise of discretion.

* **Reach Within the Question:** No universal exemption for every preenactment conviction.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The plea is a completed exchange of legal rights. The government received the trial-right waiver while an established avenue for relief remained available. Converting possible removal into mandatory ineligibility alters that transaction. A requirement of proof that a particular official promised a waiver would confuse protected eligibility with an impossible guarantee of discretionary success.

  Landgraf's legal-consequence inquiry controls. The completed waiver of trial rights and then-existing eligibility are the material circumstances, not an empirical prediction that every pleader would have gone to trial. Preserve the opportunity for discretion because that was the legal position attached to the completed plea; do not turn that opportunity into a promised immigration outcome.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Trial convictions, ineligible pleaders, postchange pleas, and unrelated later grounds of removal are outside the presented preenactment eligible-plea question.
