## Historical evidence and remaining disputed inference

Historically Stevens wrote for O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer to reverse and remand. Kennedy concurred in the judgment, rejecting a general immediate-evidence/ultimate-health distinction but agreeing that the integral penal implementation of this program was not sustained by the precedents. Scalia dissented; Rehnquist and Thomas joined only Part II, which assumed an unconsented bodily search and sustained the genuine mixed medical function under special needs. Scalia alone also advanced an independent consent, abandonment and third-party-disclosure theory in Part I. Thomas is not assigned that unjoined theory.

The refreshed independent analysis now correctly credits real clinical screening and referrals predating police cooperation, the genuine maternal and infant health goal, both district passages in their different contexts, and the initial arrests without a treatment alternative. It distinguishes the clinical function's existence from its legal sufficiency. Those corrections remove the original source deficit. They do not themselves alter history: the same clinical origin and mixed operation were central to the historical dissent, which expressly rejected treating the medical role as erased by added enforcement.

The remaining provisional analogy for Scalia and Thomas requires a custody-like supervisory basis, or another separately identified exception, before the clinical function can justify this operation. Griffin certainly involved conditional liberty and a valid probation regulation. But their direct historical Ferguson position already confronted the patient/probationer distinction and found the real protective clinical function sufficient notwithstanding planned law-enforcement cooperation. That was an application of the independent medical need, not an assumption that all patients are probationers or that a benign ultimate goal always authorizes a search.

Actual Vernonia's no-waiver and search classifications, Chandler's rejection of an inadequately supported need, and the requirement of genuinely performed independent functions in their actual Edmond dissent leave that concrete position available. The case supplies real clinical activity rather than nominal license checks attached to ordinary drug policing. Thomas's actual unjoined Edmond portion is expressly limited to brief automobile seizures, a meaningful difference from a general purpose-indifference theory. It does not compel rejecting a bodily search on the independent mixed-function analysis he actually joined historically while assuming nonconsent. Neither the unchanged Griffin facts nor that narrower roadway reservation supplies a concrete premise explaining the provisional contrary conclusion here.

Accordingly, after the completed source return and competing-path assessment, the direct historical special-needs reasoning is stronger evidence for these two Justices than the provisional inference that their prior cases require rejecting this particular clinical justification. Their reconciled sufficient ground is Path B and affirmance on special needs, not reversal. This is a reasoned Stage C correction of two provisional choices, not a rule that every historical vote controls. The independent artifacts remain intact.

## Common ground, competing ground and consent scope

The six-Associate common rule is limited to the challenged systematic collection, testing and prosecution arrangement. A real health objective and earlier clinical work do not by themselves justify dispensing with ordinary Fourth Amendment protection for that program's designed evidence, disclosure and arrest operation. The clinical and ordinary-patient relationship here does not supply the independently adequate justification found in Griffin's actual supervisory setting or in other properly supported institutional programs. This is not a rule requiring criminal custody in every special-needs case, excluding every mixed purpose, or forbidding lawful incidental reporting of information obtained during independently authorized medical care.

The two-Associate contrary ground accepts the real protective clinical function as independently sufficient here. The planned reporting and arrest machinery does not eliminate that function merely because it advances compliance through enforcement. The early absence of a treatment alternative is part of the record and cannot be rewritten, but in their analysis the direct maternal and infant diagnostic/protective purpose is not exhausted by the later treatment-before-arrest option. They reject the categorical inference that cooperation makes medical work only ordinary policing. Their position supplies neither a factual finding that every testing event had identical indications nor general permission for unsupported hospital evidence programs.

Scalia's historical Part I was independently sufficient for him and exposes why a mere reference to Vernonia's bodily-search classification could not answer his consent objection. It is not required to the reconciled common Scalia/Thomas special-needs ground, which assumes a search and insufficient consent. This handoff does not adjudicate the disputed preservation of an additional instruction objection, certify all patient-specific consent facts, or create a new final consent-based holding from a historical brief recitation. Scalia's refusal to treat the three-part instruction as the constitutional rule is preserved; Thomas supplies no join to the separate abandonment or third-party theory. A published separate explanation may state the special-needs disagreement fully without inventing an unprovided preservation finding.

For the six supporting reversal, the assumed absence of legally adequate consent is solely the premise for the exception question. The jury verdict remains a trial event; the preserved evidentiary-sufficiency challenge awaits ordinary appellate consideration. Neither collection, testing and disclosure nor their relevant consent scope may be flattened into an automatic waiver from hospital attendance or a universal requirement of three separately expressed permissions. The reversal does not itself resolve whether particular consent was voluntary and adequate or whether disclosure alone constitutes a new search.

The supported remand returns the preserved consent question and otherwise live liability and remedy matters. It orders no automatic new trial or evidentiary hearing, final damages, universal injunction, suppression, or finding against every plaintiff. Individual participation, municipal attribution, immunity and causation retain their own law and preservation requirements. Lawful medical reporting requirements and materially different independent clinical programs remain outside the holding.
