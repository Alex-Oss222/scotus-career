The independent packet, corrected preflight, exact territorial-design rule and complete actual Hunt public entry were compared.

Historically Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, reversed because the predominance finding was clearly erroneous. Thomas, joined by Rehnquist, Scalia and Kennedy, emphasized trial deference, defended the finding and disputed the additional alternative-map burden. Both positions operated within a constitutional cause of action triggered by racial predominance. Neither answers the actual governing-law question here.

Actual Shaw, Johnson v. Miller and Hunt reject predominance alone as a constitutional deprivation, even when traditional criteria were subordinated. Hunt's May 17, 1999 mandate expressly did not direct a trial on that design-only theory; it permitted only actually presented independently cognizable claims under their own elements and ordinary rules. The historical trial findings are supported objective recitations. They do not establish compliance with that materially different mandate or the existence, absence or forfeiture of an independent claim. A completed finding cannot cure legal insufficiency, and a disputed source cannot be converted into litigant default.
