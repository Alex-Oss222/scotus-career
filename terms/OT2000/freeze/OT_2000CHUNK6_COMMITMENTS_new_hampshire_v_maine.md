## Independent conclusions and competing paths

The seven participating Associates independently support granting Maine's motion and dismissing the present complaint on judicial estoppel. The sufficient ground is the same: New Hampshire successfully procured judicial acceptance of a middle-main-channel interpretation and now seeks sovereignty to the opposite shore under an incompatible reading of the same decree. The earlier consent's limited marine geography does not erase the accepted interpretive premise. The supported record supplies no mistake or changed circumstance warranting this reversal of position. The Court need not determine the inland line afresh or resolve claim and issue preclusion.

The governing rule should be equitable and contextual. Clear inconsistency, prior judicial acceptance and the advantage or unfair detriment from reversal are important considerations, not an exhaustive formula or mandatory independent elements for every case. A compromise position is not automatically binding: actual presentation, judicial use and the particular inconsistency matter. Sovereign litigants have no general exemption, but a supported public-law change or genuine mistake can matter; no universal government-estoppel rule is decided.

The competing lawful path would deny dismissal because the consent settled only an offshore line, leaving historical inland title for an original-action record. Its strongest point is that a negotiated settlement need not admit every factual proposition and that public boundaries should not be casually forfeited. The decisive answer is narrower than universal settlement preclusion: the Court accepted this very interpretation as a basis for its decree, after considering the Master's different construction. The newly preferred reading, on materials available then, is not a demonstrated mistake. Dismissal protects that judicial use without pretending the prior decree surveyed the inland river. Davis v. Wakelee supplies the inherited successful-position principle; Cleveland's unanimous rejection of presumed contradiction between different statutory labels reinforces the need for this actual, same-premise contradiction.

## Scope available for common opinion

All seven join: original jurisdiction and sufficient participation; actual accepted-position identification; the contextual estoppel rule; its application to the two incompatible interpretations; rejection of the supported mistake/sovereign arguments; and dismissal without reaching alternative preclusion grounds. None requires a separate writing on these conclusions. The narrower position is dismissal on this accepted premise alone. The broader proposed rule that any prior settlement position conclusively establishes all related sovereign boundaries is rejected by every modeled Justice.

The decree remains what it was. The judgment supplies no affirmative determination that every inland segment follows a newly surveyed middle channel, no title adjudication independent of estoppel and no future proceeding or deadline. No current-law premise depends on later Kansas v. Colorado. Source limitations are the absence of independently recovered original 1970s pleadings; the authenticated published recitation establishes the specific representations and judicial acceptance needed for this disposition, without claiming original-file possession.

Frozen conclusion: seven participating Associates favor dismissal on the common stated ground; Souter takes no part. Final Court arithmetic awaits the authorized assembly.
