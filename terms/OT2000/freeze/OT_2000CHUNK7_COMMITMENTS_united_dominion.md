## Law entering the event

Section 172(c) defines net operating loss by deductions exceeding gross income. Section 172(j)(1) caps product liability loss at the lesser of net operating loss and allowable product liability expenses; section 172(b)(1)(I) supplies the ten-year carryback. Sections 1501 and 1502 permit consolidated filing and delegate detailed administration. Regulations 1.1502-11(a), -12 and -21(f) construct consolidated taxable income and consolidated NOL from modified separate taxable income and group-level items. Separate taxable income omits items that would enter a complete separate-return loss calculation. Section 1.1502-79(a) allocates a consolidated loss to separate return years; its paragraph (a)(3) adjusts the separate taxable figure for that purpose.

Haggar's unanimous statutory-delegation rule requires fidelity to the enacted scheme and actual delegated authority before reasonable administrative choice receives controlling treatment. The register title "Delegated Customs regulations in tariff litigation" operates from April 21, 1999. It neither converts all litigation positions into regulations nor eliminates the regulatory scheme here. Christensen's "Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation," effective May 1, 2000, separates advisory statutory views from binding administrative acts. Its separate Auer holding forbids changing clear regulatory text by interpretation.

Gitlitz, January 9, 2001, enforces the actual corporate/shareholder classification and sequence despite an asserted unintended tax benefit; Breyer's published dissent rests on the coordinated statutory account rather than an independent policy veto. Cleveland Indians, April 17, 2001, gives an established, fair and textually permissible own-regulation construction controlling weight. Its actual prior revenue-ruling construction distinguishes it from an interpretation merely asserted during this dispute. Neither tax case resolves the present definition or permits the phrase "double deduction" to substitute for identifying two deductions of the same expense.

## Competing supported paths

**Group computation; reverse.** The initial consolidated product liability loss compares the consolidated NOL with aggregate allowable member product liability expenses. The current-return scheme provides a consolidated NOL, whereas positive separate taxable income is only a modified intermediate figure. Section 1.1502-79 supplies an allocation definition for a different task and does not, by its stated scope, impose a separate-member ceiling on this initial computation. Congress delegated ample power to adopt coherent consolidated-return limits, but the power to write a restriction does not establish its presence in the operative rules.

The strongest objection is that losses from an unrelated affiliate can make a profitable member's product liability expenses eligible for the special carryback, weakening the relation between the expense and that member's economic loss. The response is legal, not an assumption that the resulting tax policy is ideal: consolidating the NOL already combines member gains and losses, the statute retains its consolidated-NOL cap, and no operative provision adds the proposed member ceiling. The allocation mechanism cannot be moved to another function merely because it would supply a convenient number. Eligible expenses remain deductible only once, and the special carryback is a timing rule within its enacted limits.

**Member computation; affirm.** The competing construction reads the product-liability-loss exception as attaching first to the taxpayer that incurred the expense, using the section 1.1502-79 adjusted member loss to preserve a complete member-level account before aggregation. The strongest objection is that the regulations expressly confine that adjusted measure to separate-return allocation and supply no general member NOL for consolidated initial computation. A mere positive separate-taxable-income proxy is weaker still because it omits relevant loss items. This path therefore must defend an interpretive incorporation of the adjusted measure, not pretend that separate taxable income already is an ordinary NOL or that Treasury promulgated a new product-liability regulation.

No justice may solve the case by an unsupported finding of abuse, duplicate deduction or a concession to a final refund amount. No remand simply inviting Treasury to choose a new rule can determine liability for these old tax years.

## Proposed common rule, remedy and joins

For the seven reversing Associates, the complete rule is that a consolidated group's initial product liability loss under these tax-year provisions is the lesser of its consolidated NOL and its aggregate otherwise allowable product liability expenses. Positive separate taxable income does not independently disqualify a member's expenses, and the special separate-return allocation measure does not impose the asserted initial member ceiling. All remaining statutory eligibility, timing and amount conditions survive.

Reverse the appellate legal ruling and remand for proper refund implementation under that rule. The undisputed consolidated NOL exceeding aggregate expenses settles this disputed limitation; it does not authorize inventing a dollar refund or resolving every accounting issue. No double deduction is created, no loss above group NOL becomes eligible, and no prospective regulation is adjudicated. Breyer's contrary interpretation supports a substantive dissent, not a join at the majority's operative level of generality.

No source gap prevents the interpretation at this bounded level. Direct quotation or a novel clause-level qualification would require the original tax-year text or an authenticated quotation; neither is claimed here. If the separate-return definition's scope or a material additional operative provision is shown to differ from the verified receipt, the affected modeling must return to neutral review.
