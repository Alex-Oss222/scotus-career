## Validated neutral addition

The source extraction establishes that the Solicitor General filed a brief, cosigned by the Treasury General Counsel, representing the ruling letter's classification as Customs' official position. It separately identifies the original letter's signer as the Director of the Commercial Rulings Branch of the headquarters regulations-and-rulings office. The supported fact is an actual, institutionally attributable public litigation representation. It is not evidence that the Secretary or Commissioner signed the original letter, a recovered account of internal authorization, or a newly promulgated regulation.

The distinction matters in both directions. The Government's position can no longer be evaluated as though its public institutional endorsement were missing or merely conjectured. But the existence of that endorsement does not itself decide what interpretive authority Congress conferred, whether this ruling mechanism exercised that authority, or what the tariff means. A later public defense of the classification cannot silently supply a procedure or legal status that the 1993 transaction lacked.

## Refreshed alternative paths

**Official agency construction under delegated classification authority.** The strongest case for controlling weight now includes Congress's classification assignment, a reasoned headquarters application, and the express public representation by the Solicitor General and Treasury General Counsel that the interpretation is Customs' official position. This answers an objection that the interpretation is only an unconfirmed subordinate's view. Scalia considers that institutional representation sufficient for the official-position aspect of his authoritative-construction approach; he does not require discovery of an undisclosed internal endorsement or the agency head's original signature. Delegation and statutory limits nevertheless remain independent requirements, supplied in his analysis by the classification scheme rather than by the signatures on the litigation brief.

**Independent judicial interpretation with persuasive weight.** The strongest contrary answer accepts the public endorsement as real and still distinguishes the agency's institutional preference from a congressionally authorized interpretation controlling the court. The ruling mechanism's transaction-specific operation, numerous issuing offices, revocability and relationship to independent tariff review remain unchanged. The seven Associates selecting this path do not deny that Customs has an official position; they deny that identifying it completes the force-of-law delegation inquiry. Haggar's actual-authority requirement and Christensen's distinction between authoritative and persuasive treatment continue to do that work. Informality alone remains insufficient to decide another statutory mechanism.

**Tariff classification and disposition.** The representation supports attribution of the existing reasons to the Government's defended institutional position. It supplies no new dictionary definition, commercial evidence or finding about the planners. Mead's removable-page and residual-category arguments therefore remain distinct from the delegation dispute. Customs' binding-mechanism, calendar and note-page reasons remain available. The seven persuasive-weight Justices still select a return for judicial classification with the previously excluded agency reasoning considered; they do not require a search for an original agency-head signature or hold that missing papers defeat the Government. Scalia still finds the explained inclusion reasonable under the delegated-choice standard and would sustain the classification directly. Neither path determines a monetary assessment from the endorsement alone.

## Refreshed proposition-level result

No Associate changes a judgment vote or the operative review standard selected in the original freeze. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer retain seven direct commitments to denying controlling Chevron weight to this ruling mechanism, requiring persuasive consideration, and vacating for renewed classification analysis. Their sufficient ground is statutory delegation and the action's legal character; it is not the absence of an official agency litigation position.

Scalia retains his separate commitment to controlling reasonable-choice treatment and direct acceptance of the classification. His official-position premise now has the specific public source supplied in the supplement, rather than resting only on the headquarters origin of the letter. His vote remains distinct from the seven votes leaving classification open. Agreement that the Federal Circuit should not discard Customs' reasons does not make the remedy or delegation ground unanimous.

The original independent tariff path remains live: genuinely clear tariff text would govern regardless of an official position or the degree of respect due it. The new source does not settle that clarity question or supply missing reasons. A direct classification result must therefore rest on its tariff ground or on a properly supported reasonable-choice ground, not the mere existence of the cosigned brief.

## Preservation and limits

The original freeze remains intact for provenance. This refresh replaces any suggestion that higher institutional public endorsement is unknown with the exact supported litigation-position fact. It does not convert unknown internal approval into a fact, impute an original signature to the Secretary or Commissioner, create a regulation, or retrofit later procedural law into the 1993 action. It changes no current law or prior adjudication. No fresh neutral blocker remains for the bounded issue; reconciliation must consider this refresh before treating Mead's non-Stone handoff as final.
