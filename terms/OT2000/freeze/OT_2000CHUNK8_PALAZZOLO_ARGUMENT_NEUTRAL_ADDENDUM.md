# Palazzolo: neutral argument-source addendum

Official predecision oral argument, February 26, 2001, No. 99-2047. The independent modeler read the entire supplied transcript, pages 1?59. The passages below are copied mechanically and without repair from `terms/OT2000/sources/chunk8-c/Palazzolo_Argument.txt`. Bench questions and address labels are retained solely to preserve the context of counsel statements; they are not findings, votes or Justice commitments. The transcript contains no later disposition or opinion. Existing frozen sources and commitments remain unchanged.

## Neutral source findings

- Pages 26?28 expressly preserve uncertainty about additional upland use. The State characterizes one home and $200,000 as a supported minimum, not the maximum. Its acknowledgment that the opposition omitted the uncertainty is not an express waiver, and a bench remark does not turn it into one.
- At pages 31?32 the State accepts the stated restriction when the question concerns the wetlands, while distinguishing the rest of the parcel. Pages 34?38 continue its contention that actual remaining use/value is unsettled and that the whole parcel matters. Its statements do not concede a fixed maximum or resolve every possible access road.
- Petitioner asserts at pages 5?8 that additional uplands require wetlands fill and that residential purposes cannot meet the public-purpose condition. This is counsel's supported litigation position, not an agency finding adopted here.
- At page 16 petitioner identifies the asserted injury as the 1986 denial, after his personal acquisition. Pages 42?45 preserve the separate dispute over ownership and transfer of an accrued claim and actual state property limitations. No taking date, transfer or state-title conclusion is found.
- Petitioner requests a remand including Penn Central at pages 23?24, while continuing to insist on his Lucas theory at pages 54?59. The State distinguishes the trial focus at page 44. These statements supplement, rather than replace, the validated account of the complaint, trial memorandum and lower decisions. They establish no abandonment or complete preservation of every partial-taking argument.
- The State reports the trial nuisance finding and confirms that the state supreme court did not address it, pages 25?26. The parties dispute its basis and the asserted underlying property law. No lower judgment is newly reconstructed and no background-law defense is decided.
