## Controlling rule candidates and actual remedies

**D1, supported by six Associates:** Section 2244(d)(2)'s exclusion covers properly filed state postconviction or other state collateral review concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. A federal habeas application's pendency is not excluded by that subsection, including when it was timely filed and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. State modifies the coordinated category; the broader collateral wording accommodates other state procedures and custody judgments. The enacted distinctions between State and Federal proceedings elsewhere, together with the sovereign modifier's operative work, support that construction. The decision does not comprehensively classify every state procedure or filing defect.

The contrary reading correctly recognizes that federal habeas is collateral review in ordinary usage and that a delayed exhaustion dismissal can consume the filing period. The six do not treat ordinary category membership as answering the modifier's scope or those consequences as erasing State. Their statutory conclusion does not depend on a promise that equity will save every prisoner. A narrow text-and-structure opinion can resolve the question without declaring all federal-pendency relief contrary to AEDPA's purposes.

**D2, supported by Ginsburg and Breyer:** Other collateral review is an independently broader category that includes a properly filed federal collateral application concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. The competing grammar and statutory-neighbor arguments do not uniquely resolve the text; ordinary exhaustion administration should not randomly extinguish an otherwise timely first opportunity merely through the duration of the federal court's nonmerits consideration. The rule excludes only qualifying actual pendency, leaves proper filing and pertinent-judgment-or-claim requirements intact, and supplies no writ, exhaustion ruling, or immunity from actual remaining defenses. Under this construction, affirm reinstatement and remand for the petition's remaining requirements.

**D3, no permanent-first-filing rule:** All eight reject the proposition that one timely initial application permanently satisfies the limitation for every distinct later application. D2 is an exclusion of qualifying pendency, not permanent satisfaction. Lindh concerns the identified pre-enactment application; Slack's non-successive classification does not reset a limitation period; Artuz's filing/relief distinction does not decide the sovereign category.

**D4, preserved alternative and procedural limits:** All eight preserve ordinary consideration of the equitable contention actually raised below, conditionally for D2 supporters. Availability, its governing conditions, and Walker's entitlement remain distinct. Approximately nine months remained after the first dismissal, the later claims differed, and Walker did not return to state court. Those supplied facts defeat a claim that judicial delay necessarily caused his late refiling, but do not adjudicate every equitable basis. No universal compulsory warning, election, prompt-dismissal deadline, or stay regime is adopted. Section 2254(b)(2) permits merits denial of unexhausted claims; it does not require it. There is no current stay request or still-pending first application for this Court to act upon.
