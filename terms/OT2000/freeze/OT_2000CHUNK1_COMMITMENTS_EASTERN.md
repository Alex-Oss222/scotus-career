**Event:** November 28, 2000. The opening-law cutoff is June 29, 2000; no intervening material governing link among the independently modeled matters is identified. No case-specific nonparticipation is supplied. The eight commitments below exclude Stone and assign no author.

**Commitment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm enforcement of the existing conditional reinstatement award. The employer remains bound to perform the award subject to its conditions and the applicable federal return-to-duty requirements. The result does not authorize driving while disqualified, excuse the driver's marijuana use, or replace the arbitrator's disciplinary conditions with a judicially chosen remedy.

**Sufficient rule:** A court reviewing a labor arbitration award may refuse enforcement on public-policy grounds only on the basis of an explicit, well-defined, dominant policy identified in law and legal precedent and an actual conflict between that policy and performance the award requires. A general preference for transportation safety, or condemnation of the employee's earlier conduct, is not a sufficient substitute for that inquiry. When the regulatory system permits rehabilitation and qualified return to duty and the award imposes suspension, rehabilitation, and testing safeguards rather than directing prohibited driving, the prior drug violations alone do not require vacatur of conditional reinstatement.

Misco and W. R. Grace supply the narrow law-grounded public-policy inquiry; Enterprise Wheel requires respect for an arbitrator's resolution of a question entrusted by the collective-bargaining agreement. The agreement expressly assigns just-cause discharge disputes to binding arbitration, and the employer does not challenge the arbitrator's contractual authority in this Court. The Court therefore may not decide afresh whether it would discharge this driver or accept his prospects of rehabilitation. Independent legal scrutiny remains available for a genuine command to perform unlawfully. First Options preserves the distinction between actual delegation and independent legal constraints but does not replace section 301 labor-award review with a commercial FAA framework.

The Omnibus Transportation Employee Testing Act and applicable DOT regulations protect transportation safety through testing, removal when disqualified, rehabilitation, and lawful return-to-duty requirements. Their safety objective must be read together with the return-to-duty route they preserve. Nothing in the supplied record establishes a mandatory discharge rule for the repeated positive tests or an award term that dispenses with required qualification. The employer's challenge consequently identifies dangerous antecedent conduct, not a conflict created by the conditional reinstatement itself.

**Adversarial alternative:** The strongest vacatur path is that two positive tests by a safety-sensitive driver establish a policy so dominant that no reinstatement may be required. The recurrence strengthens the safety concern, but it does not identify a law-grounded incompatibility with the award's conditional performance where the regulatory regime retains rehabilitation and return to duty. The arbitrator's contractual authority is unchallenged; selecting permanent discharge would resolve the entrusted disciplinary question anew. If a particular term actually compelled driving before regulatory qualification or displaced a mandatory prohibition, vacatur of that command or appropriate arbitral reconsideration could follow. No such term is established. These alternatives therefore do not support vacating this award.

**Limits and remedy:** The holding enforces this award; it neither guarantees reinstatement without satisfying its conditions nor orders the employee to drive regardless of regulatory status. It decides no independent statutory claim, damages amount, or employer authority outside the agreement. It does not decide whether all applications of the public-policy exception are confined to directly illegal performance, because the claim fails under the received formulation as well. It announces no general right to a second or later reinstatement after a drug violation.
