## Historical findings for the later reconciliation context only

The complete report, 533 U.S. 606–655, including the syllabus, all opinions and every footnote, has been read. Its precise historical relationships are:

- Kennedy's Court opinion was joined throughout by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Stevens additionally joined Part II-A, finality. The disposition affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded.
- O'Connor's separate concurrence preserved regulation at acquisition as a relevant, nonexclusive consideration in the contextual inquiry. She rejected both a categorical acquisition bar and exclusion of the regulatory setting.
- Scalia's separate concurrence rejected any bearing from the fact that the restriction existed when the purchaser took title, outside genuine background property or nuisance rules. His disagreement with O'Connor concerned the rationale on remand, not the common judgment.
- Stevens expressly joined finality but dissented from judgment. His objection distinguished a later owner's ability to challenge a restriction from compensation for a taking suffered by a prior owner. He treated timing of injury and ownership of the claim as distinct from notice, and separately examined the interest acquired and the alleged later denial. His footnote 7 made the alternative unripe theory conditional on a materially new or unforeseeable regulatory interpretation. That is not an unconditional finality dissent.
- Ginsburg, joined by Souter and Breyer, dissented on finality. Her substantial record ground concerned potential upland uses, the floor-versus-ceiling significance of $200,000 and the prior litigation's Lucas focus. It was not simply a demand for another beach-club application. She acknowledged that the state decision permitted consideration of Penn Central while disputing the sufficiency and fairness of resolving its factual premises on this record. Footnote 3 preserved, at minimum, the relevance of title transfer to a merits assessment.
- Breyer joined Ginsburg in full and added that transfer alone did not always or automatically bar a claim. He regarded timing and circumstances as potentially important to expectations and rejected reward for strategic parcel manipulation. His conditional merits view did not turn him into a supporter of the present merits remand.

Historical authorship is recorded only to identify the evidence; it supplies no simulated assignment.
