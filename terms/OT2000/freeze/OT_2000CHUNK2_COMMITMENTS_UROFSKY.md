## Record and legal premises used

The operative statute is the amended law considered by the en banc court. It regulates an agency employee's use of agency-owned or agency-leased computer equipment to access, download, print, or store information-infrastructure files or services with covered content. The content must have as its dominant theme a lascivious description or depiction of the enumerated sexual subjects: sexual bestiality, lewd exhibition of nudity, sexual excitement, sexual conduct, sadomasochistic abuse, coprophilia, urophilia, or fetishism. The incorporated definitions remain those reproduced in the official lower opinion, including the forms of nudity, actual or explicitly simulated sexual conduct, genital arousal, and the specified torture, flagellation, or restraint. The amendment does not turn all covered material into unprotected obscenity.

An agency includes a public institution of higher education but excludes the Department of State Police. Information infrastructure includes telecommunications, cable, and computer networks, including the Internet, the World Wide Web, Usenet, bulletin boards, online systems, and telephone networks. The operative exception permits access only to the extent required in conjunction with a bona fide, agency-approved research project **or other agency-approved undertaking**. The second branch is not restricted to research. Agency heads must give approvals in writing, and approvals must be available under the state public-records law. The statute does not promise approval of everything merely related to work. Nor does it mandate an outside nonacademic decisionmaker or universal academic peer review. Reported delegation to deans or department heads is administrative practice.

The six professors challenge work-related activity and the approval condition itself. They disclaim a personal-use entitlement. None requested and was denied approval; the record identifies no denied request, and some other professors received waivers. That is different from proving that no current activity is burdened or that every future request must succeed. No damages claim, disciplinary dismissal, or adjudication of a refused project is presented.

The appellate opinion identifies the specific effects as allegations. The district opinion additionally identifies summary-judgment sources: a statement of facts concerning removed assignments, proposed student research, and work topics; a declaration concerning the state research database; and exhibits or depositions concerning Smith's images, blocking and restoration, and approval practices. These sources prevent treating the case as supported only by a subjective, unspecified fear. Their labels do not prove every assertion undisputed. The district opinion addressed the earlier definition; its examples and invalidity conclusion cannot simply be transferred to the narrowed statute.

The record also contains the Commonwealth's five reported misuse incidents, including complaints, and Smith's explanation of his research purpose. The reported blocking and partial restoration of Smith's images is not a refused statutory application. Some approvals were prompt with little paperwork, and some departments received blanket approvals. These facts support a real preventive interest and a potentially modest burden. They do not establish that every faculty activity receives an exemption, that the enacted word required means merely work-related, or that making approvals public has no effect. The reported incidents are not adjudications that every displayed image constituted actionable harassment.

### Controlling authority and its limits

* **Boring, February 17, 1999:** The assigned secondary-school production was institutional curricular expression; delegated discretion did not create the personal entitlement claimed. The decision expressly left university academic freedom and a universal duty-speech rule unresolved. Its five-Justice rationale, including O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, is binding. Souter's dissent, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer, preserved a limited personal interest against nonpedagogical punishment while accepting final school authority. Neither position grants a professor constitutional control of a curriculum.
* **NTEU, February 22, 1995:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer joined the controlling prospective-burden formulation for protected citizen expression. O'Connor used a distinct case-specific Pickering balance. Scalia and Thomas would have sustained the preventive compensation rule. The controlling doctrine requires an antecedent protected interest; it does not itself classify university research or grant unrestricted use of public equipment. Its nonparty remedial limit remains separate from the class remedy on its particular record.
* **Pickering, Connick, Givhan, and Waters:** Public concern and the employer's operational interests require context. Work location or a private communication is not dispositive. Waters's limited genuine-ground and reasonable-account rules do not decide this prospective approval condition or impose a universal investigation procedure. The Justices' distinct published Waters positions are kept distinct below.
* **Keyishian, 385 U.S. 589, 597–610:** The Court invalidated vague restrictions on utterances and teaching and overbroad membership disqualifications. Its protection against an official orthodoxy was not limited to an institution suing over its own autonomy. It does not establish ownership of public resources or a complete individual right to dictate academic policy. The present provision is neither a loyalty oath nor automatically invalid under Keyishian's specific holdings.
* **Sweezy, 354 U.S. 234, 249–255, 261–267:** The four-Justice opinion identified an invasion of the lecturer's academic and political liberties but reversed on the insufficiently authorized inquiry and due process; Frankfurter and Harlan relied on the inadequacy of the intrusion's justification and the intellectual freedom of the university community. These writings support attention to scholarly freedom but do not constitute a majority rule granting every faculty claim or making academic freedom exclusively institutional. Their separate grounds are not combined into a new controlling test.

Wasson preserves the underlying employee-speech questions and resolved a particular damages immunity defense. It creates no academic-freedom holding, no actual-authorship rule for this case, and no merits prerequisite requiring Denius first. No precedent is proposed to be overruled.

The intervening review preserves the distinctions especially relevant here: City News's actual abandonment supplies no general prior-request requirement; Lopez's confined prison-release discretion supplies no immunity from independently protected rights; and Kansas's conditional-funding ruling decides no individual speech or privacy claim. Brewer, Fiore, Gitlitz, SWANCC, and Seling likewise change no rule used in these commitments.

## Components and exact provisional dispositions

**T — Reachability.** A concrete intended or ongoing use subject to a mandatory approval condition can support a preenforcement challenge to that condition; a plaintiff need not first suffer a denial of the permission whose necessity is challenged. The supported particular teaching and research activities distinguish this case from generalized subjective chill. That permits review of the approval condition, not adjudication of an imagined refusal. The narrowed definition's application, continuing injury, and the beneficiaries of any final prospective relief must be established at the appropriate level. The Court does not assume Article III power merely to reject disputed merits.

**G — General employee entitlement and broad relief.** Reject a rule entitling every state employee to choose every work-related use of state equipment free of approval, and leave the reversal of the district court's wholesale declaration of invalidity in force. Ordinary institutional work, budget and equipment control, and genuine curricular decisions remain subject to their existing constitutional limits. No decree for every unrepresented employee or institution follows from these six faculty claims. This is not approval of every possible application to a nonacademic employee.

The rejection of that general entitlement and decree does not adopt a rule that one valid application defeats every First Amendment overbreadth challenge. The district court's treatment of the earlier definition and all work-related activity cannot establish the amended statute's substantial unconstitutional reach. The faculty claim remains for the separate analysis below; a professor's individual injury does not itself establish the scope of a broader facial remedy.

**A — Individual university claim.** Five Associates would vacate the appellate disposition insofar as it finally rejects the professors' individual academic claim and remand under the rules below. O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas would affirm that disposition. The broad district judgment is not reinstated by the proposed vacatur; no present injunction, access order, damages, hearing, or discovery order is directed.

## Five-Justice common rationale and remand scope

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer support the following at the same level of generality:

1. A university professor may possess a personal First Amendment interest when engaged in independent scholarly inquiry, teaching, or communication on matters of public concern. The interest depends on the activity's function and context, not the academic title. Salary, duty connection, and public equipment do not alone make that expression the institution's prescribed message. This does not establish a personal right to dictate institutional curricula, funding priorities, assignments, or the allocation of equipment. Genuine institutional decisions and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions remain.
2. Once such an interest is identified, the State must justify the actual burden of this prospective content-based condition under Pickering and NTEU. The inquiry includes the effect on the professor and audience, the narrowed content definition, the required-use limit, both approval branches, written and public approval, and actual administration. The State's efficiency, resource-management, and prevention-of-harassment interests are legitimate. Their weight and the restraint's fit must address the protected work actually affected. Ownership alone is not that justification.
3. The court of appeals cannot reject the claim solely because the inquiry is work-related or because academic freedom supposedly belongs only to the institution. It must apply the amended statute, distinguish independent scholarly activity from institutional output, and assess the condition itself. The record contains supported claims and employer evidence on both sides; it is not an evidentiary vacuum. The earlier district analysis of a broader statute and all state employees does not supply the final answer for this narrower faculty claim.
4. A remand is required because the categorical appellate rule bypassed the activity-specific protection and burden inquiry, while the district judgment addressed a different definition and substantially broader relief. No finding of improper motive, unlawful refusal, or entitlement to unrestricted access is made. No presumption that all approval systems fail or that these plaintiffs must win replaces the required inquiry.

The State may rely on appropriate operational evidence and supported predictive judgments. No completed harassment violation, expert study, special hearing, newly commissioned research, or universal proof protocol is imposed. Conversely, successful approvals do not automatically answer the chilling effect of requiring public advance permission for protected inquiry. A scholarly label does not establish coverage, protection, harm, or victory. Access to unprotected obscenity, personal use, and unrelated misuse are not rights established here.

On remand, ordinary procedural rules govern the existing submissions and any properly authorized further proceedings. Determine which plaintiffs' continuing activities are covered by the amended statute and implicate a personal interest, then decide whether the actual condition is justified at that scope. Any declaration or injunction must redress an established violation and reach no further than the represented rights and effective relief require. The Court does not direct the approval of a particular project, invalidate the public-records requirement in isolation, substitute itself for a dean, or require a particular future enforcement decision.

## Enhanced comparison: Justice-specific paths and join barriers

### Stevens

His controlling NTEU opinion treats future expression and the audience's loss as material burdens, and his Boring dissent join recognizes a limited personal interest even in an educational setting with final institutional control. His Waters dissent focuses on actual protected expression rather than substituting a potentially permissible employer account. Together these positions support examining independent university inquiry rather than accepting the employer label as dispositive.

**Competing path:** Sustain the amended rule because the approval route accommodates legitimate research and prevents documented misuse. **Strongest point:** Prompt approvals and the narrower definition substantially reduce the intrusion. **Conclusion:** Those facts count, but do not resolve the prospective burden of the required-use and public-permission conditions on the protected inquiry actually presented. Remand permits that assessment. The converse path of restoring the district's all-employee declaration fails because it skips Boring's antecedent-interest requirement and the amended coverage. He joins the five-Justice rationale in full; he will not join an exclusive-institutional-right theory or a rule requiring an actual denial before challenging the condition.

### O'Connor

Her separate NTEU opinion used the actual employment nexus and burden rather than treating every prospective employment rule as constitutionally suspect. Her Boring join preserves real institutional control while reserving the university question. Her Waters plurality recognizes the operational differences between an employer and a sovereign. These positions support a decision on this actual condition without denying all personal academic interests.

**Competing path:** Vacate for NTEU analysis of independent inquiry. **Strongest point:** Public prior approval can deter exploratory scholarship even when no application has been refused. **Conclusion:** Here the State has narrowed the covered material, retained both research and other-undertaking approvals, and located administration within the institution; the record contains quick and blanket permissions as well as genuine misuse concerns. A preventive workplace rule need not await actionable harassment. The burden of written accountability does not on this record outweigh those interests. She assumes, rather than affirmatively recognizes or rejects, the bounded academic interest. She will not join the appellate categorical rationale, a universal prohibition on advance approval, or a holding validating a future discriminatory refusal. She joins T and G and would affirm A on this case-specific balance.

### Scalia

His Boring join denies that delegated choice over an institutional activity itself creates a personal constitutional entitlement. His NTEU dissent, joined by Thomas, accepts preventive and administrable employment restrictions without proof of completed misconduct. His Waters separate ground gives significance to genuine permissible employer purposes without creating a comprehensive procedure. These positions support sustaining this work-resource condition.

**Competing path:** Treat independent university research as individually protected and require justification beyond ownership. **Strongest point:** Boring reserved university inquiry, and Keyishian defeats the claim that employment removes every independent constitutional restriction. **Conclusion:** Neither reservation nor scholarly value establishes the exemption claimed from this limited equipment rule. Keyishian's orthodoxy and association holdings survive; neither it nor Sweezy creates a general faculty entitlement to determine publicly supplied research means. He does not convert Boring into an already-existing universal rule for every duty-related statement. Alternatively, even assuming the asserted personal interest, the narrowed definition, real misuse problem, and available institutional approval procedure justify this condition under the employment balance. No expert proof of future incidents is required. He refuses the five-Justice remand and its extension of personal protection to this access claim, and supports a joint separate position with Thomas.

### Kennedy

His direct join to NTEU's prospective-burden holding protects individual expression and audience interests against a general employment condition. His Boring majority join turns on the assigned school production and does not decide independent university inquiry. His Waters concurrence join permits actual reliance on a genuine lawful employer ground; it does not license suppression simply because an employer owns a communication resource. These are distinct commitments rather than a generalized preference for the employee.

**Competing path:** Sustain the narrowed condition on O'Connor's balance. **Strongest point:** This is work-connected use of public resources, and institutions can approve every legitimate undertaking without an outsider selecting ideas. **Conclusion:** Institutional approval is relevant but not a substitute for identifying whose expression is being controlled. In a setting of independent scholarship, a general content restriction and public permission condition can burden the individual's inquiry even when ordinary institutional oversight is lawful. The appellate ownership-and-capacity rule never performs that inquiry. The earlier broad district decree cannot be restored without accounting for the amendment and the actual protected scope. He joins the common rationale and remand, but refuses a right to control curriculum, automatic strict scrutiny of every workplace rule, or a presumption that a university must approve any project a professor calls academic.

### Souter

His Boring dissent identifies a limited personal interest without denying final pedagogical authority; his NTEU and Waters joins require attention respectively to the actual reach of prospective burdens and to genuine, adequately supported employer grounds. The university distinction strengthens the need to classify the expression, but does not make all choices personal constitutional entitlements.

**Competing path:** Hold that the approval process is ordinary academic administration and therefore sufficient. **Strongest point:** Academic institutions themselves evaluate grants, assignments, and resources, and the recorded approvals show accommodation. **Conclusion:** The statutory selection of a sexual-content category is not automatically a particular academic judgment. The administrative examples neither enact a uniform peer-review guarantee nor settle the public written-approval burden. The alternative of striking every application would likewise ignore actual institutional work and the amended definition. He joins all five-Justice portions and would not require strict scrutiny, actual disruption in every case, or a court-administered research allocation system.

### Thomas

His Boring join treats the claimed personal interest as a prerequisite, not a consequence of professional discretion. His NTEU dissent join accepts genuine preventive employment restrictions and administrability, and his Waters concurrence join separates the employer's genuine ground from a freestanding investigative entitlement. No own date-eligible position in the supplied record establishes a constitutional faculty privilege over publicly provided equipment.

**Competing path:** Recognize personal scholarship as constitutionally distinct from the assigned school production. **Strongest point:** The university reservation is real and the Constitution independently protects against prohibited orthodoxy. **Conclusion:** An unresolved question is not an established access entitlement. The present statute preserves institutional decisions to permit required use and leaves personal equipment outside this restriction. Its scope does not itself dictate an academic conclusion or association. On the alternative assumption of a personal interest, the same preventive balance stated by Scalia sustains the condition. Thomas does not join an immunity from independent constitutional prohibitions or adjudicate a future refusal. He supports T, G, affirmance of A, and the complete joint separate position with Scalia.

### Ginsburg

Her Boring dissent join accepts ordinary educational control but rejects treating the actual personal claim as necessarily extinguished by that control. Her direct NTEU join supplies the prospective-burden rule; her Waters plurality join separates actual protection, reasonableness, motive, and remedy. Wasson's immunity opinion, joined by all Associates, resolved notice rather than denying an underlying constitutional extension. It furnishes no shortcut against this prospective claim.

**Competing path:** Uphold the condition because none of these professors suffered a refused request. **Strongest point:** Courts should not presume unlawful administration, especially where permissions have been granted. **Conclusion:** A challenge to the necessity and public character of prior permission does not depend on proving a refused application. Actual ongoing activity and burden still matter; the amended scheme and institutional allocation must be assessed. She rejects both a hypothetical future-denial adjudication and the conversion of all faculty employment disputes into constitutional claims. She joins the five-Justice rationale and a remedy confined to established represented rights.

### Breyer

His NTEU join requires a justification fitted to the burden of the prospective restriction, while his Boring dissent join preserves substantial educational discretion and rejects a compulsory strict-scrutiny or actual-disruption requirement for ordinary institutional decisions. Those published commitments support examining the narrowed condition's practical operation without creating a new procedural code.

**Competing path:** Treat narrowing plus easy waivers as a sufficient accommodation now. **Strongest point:** The Commonwealth can prevent workplace problems, and modest application costs may be justified without elaborate evidence. **Conclusion:** Those practical considerations are substantial, but the district considered a broader statute and the appellate majority bypassed the individual-interest and burden questions. Required access, exploratory research, and public approvals must be considered together; one cannot equate a waiver's availability with absence of burden. He joins the common limited remand. He would not join a categorical free-access right, invalidation based solely on underinclusiveness, mandatory expert studies, or relief reaching every state employee.
