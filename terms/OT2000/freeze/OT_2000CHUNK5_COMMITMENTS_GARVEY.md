## Governing propositions and disposition

The parties accepted the Framework's contractual labor-arbitration submission. Courts may determine its scope and enforce recognized limits on arbitral authority. They may not set aside the resulting award merely because they regard the arbitrator's assessment of credibility or facts as mistaken, so long as he is arguably applying the agreement within his assigned authority. Actual dishonesty, corruption, refusal to exercise contractual judgment, or an award beyond the submission presents a different ground; no such fact may be inferred simply from disagreement with the result.

The arbitrator considered the Framework's requirement of a specific extension offer later withdrawn because of collusion, sought additional documentation, and evaluated the particular testimony and letter. Prior findings of league-wide collusion did not establish the individual offer required for this claimant. The appellate court's disbelief of his credibility explanation did not convert that exercise of assigned judgment into abandonment of the contract. The denial therefore cannot be displaced on the reasons supplied. Independently, even a lawful vacatur would not authorize the reviewing court to decide the disputed contractual merits and compel Garvey's claimed sum instead of respecting the agreement's remaining arbitral decision process.

Reverse the December 7, 2000 directed-award judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with the foregoing limits. Its prior merits-substitution premise cannot stand. No court-selected amount, new finding about whether Smith or Garvey spoke truthfully, proof of collusion regarding this particular extension, or adjudication of a new independent statutory claim follows.
