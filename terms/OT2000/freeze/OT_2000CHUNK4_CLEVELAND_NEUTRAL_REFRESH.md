## What is settled, and what is not

The payments settled grievances concerning free-agency rights and compensated salary damages attributed to 1986 and 1987. They were paid in 1994; no recipient was then the Company's employee, and the Company paid them no other wages that year. The parties stipulated that these settlement payments are wages within FICA and FUTA. That stipulation removes a proposed nontaxable-damages disposition. It does not stipulate the year to which wages are attributed, whether the annual statutory wording directly resolves a damages substitute for prior salary, or the legal force of Treasury's interpretation. Describing the payments as damages and recognizing their stipulated wage status are compatible propositions.

The original packet's statement that earlier-year allocation generates no additional tax must retain the source qualification: all but one recipient had already reached the relevant earlier taxable maximum. The Company paid $99,382 in FICA and $1,008 in FUTA tax, while the refund judgment was $97,202. These figures do not establish an entitlement to refund every dollar originally paid. No model should flatten that difference or invent the exceptional player's calculation.

There are separate tax components. Section 3111(a)'s Social Security rates expressly refer to wages paid during specified years. Section 3111(b)'s Medicare rate has its own payment-date language; the stipulated dates also implicate the removal of Medicare's wage ceiling by 1994. Section 3301 imposes FUTA on total wages paid during the calendar year. Sections 3121(a)(1) and 3306(b)(1) separately define annual wage-base exclusions by payments to the individual from the employer in the calendar year. Agreement on a rate clause does not silently decide the wage-base clause, and a construction must explain its operation for each component actually presented.

The excerpts of sections 31.3111-3 and 31.3111-2(c) state payment-time attachment and application of the rate then in effect; the source identifies corresponding FUTA rules and continuity in basic substance since 1940. Those are substantial textual and administrative premises. They are not a stipulation that every damages payment falls within the actual-payment-year rule without further interpretation. The neutral inquiry must identify whether construing the wage substitute is still necessary and, if so, which statute, regulation or agency interpretation does that work.

## Nierotko's actual contribution and limits

Nierotko treated an NLRB backpay award for wrongful discharge as remuneration for employment even though productive work had not occurred during the compensated period. Service covered the compensated employment relationship, not only completed physical labor. The award made the worker whole for lost wages and was not a fine or penalty. The Court rejected an administrative exclusion of backpay from wages as outside permissible statutory interpretation. It recognized administrative expertise and possible borderline classification questions, but did not permit the agency conclusively to determine its own statutory limits.

The Court also allocated that backpay to the earlier periods for Social Security benefit eligibility and measurement. It expressly confronted statutory benefit language requiring wages to be paid in particular quarters and still adopted earlier-period allocation. Thus, the proposition that the word paid invariably means the actual receipt period in every Social Security provision is incompatible with the actual case. Its footnotes preserve the then-existing texts, including different tax-rate wording tied to employment during the specified years. Footnote 26's observation about distributive income-tax treatment is not a holding allocating the present FICA or FUTA liabilities.

Nierotko's discussion of identical wage and employment definitions across the earlier benefit and tax titles matters to the Company's analogy. Its actual judgment nevertheless concerned an individual benefit account, not an employer's liability under the later annual tax-rate and wage-base provisions. It establishes neither that every later tax phrase requires earning-period allocation nor that later payment language automatically defeats the analogy. Frankfurter's concurrence reinforces the wage character of enforced idleness and judicial review of its exclusion; it does not provide an independent present-tax timing rule. The present settlement is not shown to be an NLRB wrongful-discharge award or to entail every statutory employment premise in Nierotko. The wage stipulation narrows that difference without erasing the remaining timing question.

## Supported construction paths to test independently

**Direct statutory payment-year construction.** The model may conclude that the stipulated inclusion as wages, together with the actual annual rate and base provisions, directly makes the 1994 payment the taxable wage payment for each presented component. That conclusion must explain why salary damages substituting for earlier compensation fall within those provisions as written. It must address Nierotko's treatment of paid benefit language, the different statutory functions and wording, and the absence of current employment; it cannot use the stipulation as an unspoken stipulation to timing. If the tax text supplies the answer, Treasury's construction can confirm it without becoming the necessary ground. The principal objection is that legal attribution of a wage substitute remains antecedent to the annual calculation, as it did in Nierotko's benefit account.

**Interpretive room with a permissible payment-year administration.** The model may instead find that the statutes' annual wording establishes the ordinary payment framework but does not alone conclusively specify how these backpay damages enter it. It must then identify the regulatory interpretation and responsible agency position actually doing the work. The express attachment/rate regulations, their reported longstanding continuity, annual collection needs, and the distinct function of benefit restoration can support a reasonableness inquiry. This route must explain why actual-payment treatment reasonably covers these awards despite Nierotko. It is different from declaring the statutory text unambiguous. Its principal objections are that the regulation merely repeats the unresolved statutory concept, that a particular later interpretation may lack the claimed authority, or that the interpretation contradicts a statutory allocation requirement rather than filling a gap. Administrative convenience is a reason to assess within lawful interpretive space, not power to create that space.

**Earlier-period statutory attribution.** The Company can argue that a salary-damage substitute belongs legally to the period of the displaced wages, that payment language identifies the event of collection without independently fixing attribution, and that Nierotko demonstrates this distinction even where a benefit text refers to paid wages. This path must answer the later tax provisions' specific annual rate and base language and explain why those clauses do not compel the actual payment year. It must justify extending Nierotko's benefit allocation to the present employer tax, rather than assume identical accounting across the two programs. A lawful earlier-period holding would still require the proper component calculations and would not eliminate the exceptional player's liability by assumption. The strongest objection is that the statute's annual tax machinery and permissible implementing regulations deliberately permit a different timing rule from benefit eligibility.

**Judicial construction without controlling agency weight.** If genuine interpretive work remains but the particular administrative pronouncement is not entitled to controlling weight, the court can still consider its reasoning, experience and consistency under the governing persuasive standard and resolve the statutory question. Withholding controlling deference does not itself choose the Company's construction. Conversely, accepting that a responsible agency has interpretive authority does not establish that this application is textually permissible or reasonable. A defined remand would require a concrete missing agency premise or unresolved claim affecting relief; the agreed dates do not justify a factual remand merely to repeat them.

The agency inquiries must remain separate: the validity and scope of a regulation under the statute; an agency's fair interpretation of its own regulation; and the force or persuasive value of a revenue ruling or other statutory interpretation. Auer addresses the second. Christensen does not allow an advisory letter to bind merely because its author is an agency and does not permit interpretation to amend clear regulatory text. No later Mead rule enters this event.

## Exact source limits and readiness

The sources sufficiently establish the actual payments, wage stipulation, competing timing positions, essential rate/base wording, quoted attachment/rate rules, reported regulatory continuity, and Nierotko's complete legal content. The source's statutory quotation uses a 1994 edition with a later supplement, and the regulatory quotation uses the 2000 compilation while reporting basic continuity. They support the identified 1994 rates and timing framework; they do not independently prove every unquoted historical exception or later amendment effective for these payments.

No complete revenue ruling, its precise delegated authority, or its exact treatment of this category of backpay is reproduced in the authorized handoff. The broad claim of consistent administration cannot be inflated into a finding that a particular ruling binds courts or expressly decides every settlement variant. If a proposed rationale depends on such a ruling's exact language, authorization, qualifications or uninterrupted application, that is the narrow additional source required. It is unnecessary to obtain it merely to recognize the quoted regulations or to decide a construction supported independently by the statutory text. Likewise, the packet's general description of regulations covering services rendered at another time is not an exact quotation of an additional wage-base provision; a holding relying on its particular terms needs that text.

The fresh model can proceed with the alternatives above. It must identify for each tax component whether its ground is direct statutory command, permissible regulatory construction, persuasive agency judgment or earlier-period attribution, and must preserve any necessary unresolved source predicate. This refresh adopts no ground, vote, opinion assignment or disposition.
