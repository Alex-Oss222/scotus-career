Preserve these operative distinctions:

- **Matching trigger:** Under §1125(9), a campaign, finance, or election report must show the relevant excess. The comparison uses the greater of expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, either alone or together with independent expenditures reported under §1019. It is not confined to cash receipts or actual spending. The Commission must immediately issue an opposing certified candidate an amount equivalent to the reported excess over the subsection 8 distribution, subject to the specified cap of twice the original distribution under subsection 8(A) or (C), whichever applies. The regulatory deduction already validated and the statutory exclusion of uncontested general elections remain qualifications. No additional reporting threshold is substituted for §1125(9)'s financial trigger.
- **Participation and fund shortage:** Section 1125(6) limits both expenditures and obligations, including outstanding obligations, to distributed funds and prohibits contributions unless the Commission specifically authorizes them. Subsection 13 prohibits disbursements beyond available fund money. Upon determining that the fund cannot meet subsection 8 or 9 distributions, the Commission may permit specified private contributions and spending, reduced by seed-money contributions, subject to the stated $250 legislative or $500 gubernatorial per-donor, per-election ceilings, applicable subsection 8 and 9 amounts, and Commission rules. Permission is discretionary and bounded. No shortage or authorization in this case is established.
- **Candidate and spouse contributions:** The general §1015(1) limit exempts contributions by the candidate or spouse. That exception does not itself remove the separate restrictions accepted by certified public-financing participants under §1125. Do not merge the two provisions' conditions.
- **Severability:** The original Act contains no special clause; §1128 requires a study report. General 1 MRSA §71(8), as amended in 1991, preserves provisions and applications capable of operating without an invalid provision or application and treats repeal of a severability clause as removal of surplus language unless the enactment indicates otherwise. The general construction header yields to inconsistent plain meaning. The [official unchanged text and amendment attribution](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/1/title1sec71.html) confirm those terms.
