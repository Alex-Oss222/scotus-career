## Refreshed mitigation paths

**Adequacy or reasonable state application.** The strongest State path is now more concrete than reliance on the written supplement alone. Every empaneled juror had received a largely similar instruction and judicial explanation during selection. The reported explanations described using a negative answer for mitigation even when the ordinary factual answers would be affirmative. The defense renewed that operation during punishment closing. Read together with the final supplemental direction and the packet taken into deliberations, these events support understanding a negative answer as a legally authorized sentencing response rather than forbidden disregard of the charge. The initial explanations are judicial directions, not merely advocacy; their possible clarifying force cannot be dismissed by category. Even a reviewing court that prefers a more coherent form must separately ask whether the state court's favorable reading was objectively unreasonable.

The strongest challenge to that path is the unreconciled factual certification required by the final form. The earlier judge and later counsel explained how to produce life but did not remove the requirement that the negative answer attest reasonable doubt about the matter factually inquired into. The whole-context test therefore cannot stop after identifying an authorized desired result; it must examine whether the supplied mechanism lets a juror who believes every factual issue proven give independent culpability mitigation effect consistently with the required answer.

**Instructional error and unreasonable application.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer continue to select this path. They give the judicial explanations and renewed defense account real weight, but find they explain the same conflicting mechanism rather than reconcile its factual certification. A juror could understand that mitigation should yield life and nevertheless confront incompatible requirements about the answer by which life must be selected. The final packet retained both the permission and that certification. Defense counsel's contemporaneous account openly described a departure from a literally correct factual answer, confirming the difficulty to be resolved rather than supplying a noncontradictory means of expressing it.

The interval and intervening trials are contextual considerations, not findings that the jurors forgot. The prosecution's general oath/evidence/law emphasis is considered only in that reported form and supplies no independent finding that the State negated the mitigation permission. The six do not rest their result on either proposition. Their conclusion addresses the complete supported presentation: judicial explanation, argument, written supplement, ordinary factual instructions and the final answer form together. The earlier clear explanation of a permitted negative answer strengthens the State's argument, but does not answer the distinct false-certification problem Graham and Johnson already prohibited before state review.

Under Boyde, these six find a reasonable likelihood that this unresolved conflict prevented effective use of the supported intellectual impairment, possible organic impairment and abuse as independent culpability mitigation. Under section 2254(d)(1), they separately find the state court's acceptance of this actual mechanism objectively unreasonable in light of the pre-1995 effective-vehicle rule. This is not a conclusion that competing readings alone establish unreasonableness or that any uncertainty about wording invalidates a charge. No separate contrary-to holding is required.

Scalia and Thomas continue to select the State's adequacy path and, independently, the reasonable-state-application ground. The repeated explicit permission, including the judge's explanation to the jurors later seated, provides a stronger basis for reading the form together with the special authorization. They regard the factual form as describing ordinary answers while the supplement and judicial explanation establish the mitigation exception. The defense's final explanation renews that understanding; it need not supply judicial authority by itself. They do not infer actual recollection or comprehension, and do not treat the prosecution summary as an express endorsement of mitigation. They conclude that the full presentation creates no reasonable likelihood of exclusion; at a minimum, the state court could reasonably reconcile its operation. Graham and Johnson remain binding, but do not invalidate a vehicle they find actually authorized and explained. Neither Justice's retained objection to the mitigation doctrine substitutes for that application.
