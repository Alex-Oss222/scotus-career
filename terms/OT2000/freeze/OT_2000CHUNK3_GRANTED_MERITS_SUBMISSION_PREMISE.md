## Adopted bounded procedural premise

Under Engine section 14's authority for minimum routine continuation of an existing order, these already granted matters return after ordinary notice and full merits briefing for consideration on the supported record and opposed positions supplied in the approved packets. This is a routine procedural reconstruction, not a claim that historical Supreme Court merits briefs, hearings, or a previously unreported docket event were recovered. It adds no filing date, oral-argument date, quotation, litigant concession, preserved claim, evidence, disputed service fact, waiver, or satisfaction of a contested deadline. Analytical arguments remain analytical possibilities unless an actual source establishes that a party made them.

The premise clarifies the packets' existing merits-submission stage. It does not change their questions, record, participants, available relief, or frozen legal commitments. The Court undertakes plenary merits review of these granted matters; a signed lead opinion therefore falls within Engine section 8's counted category for decisions on full merits briefing, including granted lower-court cases. Opinion form and governing propositions remain independent legal choices. No hearing or separate grant action is created by the clarification, and no unsupported argument date may be published.

There is no unresolved service, timeliness, participation or record dispute that this routine premise resolves. If one is later identified, this preparation artifact cannot supply its missing fact. It is not an Admitted Source Record, a Court decision, or substantive law.
