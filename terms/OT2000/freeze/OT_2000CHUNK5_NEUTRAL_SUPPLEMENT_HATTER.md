## Lower-court sequence and judgment under review

The earlier dismissal was reversed by the Federal Circuit, and the case returned to the Court of Federal Claims. After its later merits rejection of the judges' claims, the Federal Circuit reversed in 64 F.3d 647 (1995) and directed summary judgment for the judges on liability. The adopted 1996 premise applies only as stated above. On remand from the Court of Appeals, the Court of Federal Claims then addressed limitations and damages in 38 Fed. Cl. 166 (1997). It treated some claims, including all Medicare claims, as time-barred and treated later salary increases as curing the asserted diminution so that recoverable damages were minimal.

The August 5, 1999 panel opinion, 185 F.3d 1356, treated the prior liability ruling as law of the case. Parts 1 and 2 rejected the use of generally awarded judicial salary increases to pay the government's preexisting liability: the panel viewed those increases as independent compensation to which the affected judges remained entitled. Part 3 rejected the continuing-claim theory and sustained the limitations barrier. That limitations analysis is not the final judgment below.

On February 9, 2000, the en banc court reinstated the panel judgment and left Parts 1 and 2 in force, but replaced Part 3. It held that the recurring, formula-based deductions were continuing claims under its pay-claim precedents, because entitlement did not require a discretionary administrative determination and the relevant questions were legal or sharply confined factual questions. Each timely deduction claim could proceed; deductions more than six years before the corresponding filing remained outside the recoverable period. The combined later judgment thus reversed both the Court of Federal Claims' cure determination and its limitations determination, and returned the matter for damages proceedings consistent with those rulings. It did not fix a present Supreme Court rule or a final claimant-specific award.

### I. Timely review of the later judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A timely petition from the later judgment in the same litigation permits review of an antecedent ruling necessary to that judgment. Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe supply this rule; an earlier appellate mandate binds the district court, but does not immunize the predicate ruling from this Court's proper later review. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this threshold ground in Part I of the per curiam opinion. Stevens does not join it.

- **Mercer v. Theriot; Hamilton-Brown Shoe Co. v. Wolf Brothers & Co.:** Apply review of antecedent rulings through a timely petition from the later judgment.

Garvey states the ordinary later-judgment review rule. It does not itself decide what effect the separately adopted Section 2109 affirmance has in this litigation. Both questions must be addressed without turning the Federal Circuit's law-of-the-case characterization into a Supreme Court resolution.
