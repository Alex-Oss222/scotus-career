## United States v. Hilario

Source supplementation: the complete reported lower opinion, 218 F.3d 19 (1st Cir. July 17, 2000), was read at https://openjurist.org/218/f3d/19 . Its chronology, arguments, procedural rulings and footnotes supplement the approved facts. Its descriptions of post-divergence historical Supreme Court precedents do not replace the Court's own versions of Edmond, Ryder or Weiss.

**Preserved threshold issue.** Hilario contested appellate jurisdiction because the notice was signed by Gil and an assistant United States attorney. The First Circuit rejected that objection independently of Gil's appointment validity: the assistant's appointment came from the Attorney General under section 542; section 516 supplies independent departmental litigation authority; the Solicitor General controls the criminal appeal decision under 28 C.F.R. 0.20(b); and Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 3(c) requires no personal United States Attorney signature. This is a reported procedural ground, not a new challenge invented here. The official statutory slice confirms sections 516 and 542. A defective appointment premise alone therefore does not establish an invalid notice; the model must address the independent authority actually supporting the appeal.

**Duration.** The statutory slice copies sections 541 and 546 in full, including section 541(b)'s holdover command; section 541(c)'s presidential removal; section 546(b)'s Senate-rejection prohibition; the earlier-of condition in section 546(c); and section 546(d)'s filing requirement. Neither the four-year language nor the 120-day language may be paraphrased away. The broad wording of section 541(b), the temporary-office purpose, and constitutional avoidance support Hilario's construction argument. The distinct expiration language and section 541's own holdover clause support the Government's response. Even an asserted four-year term does not establish automatic cessation of all authority without addressing holdover. The supplied appointment is accepted as an actual September 9, 1993 appointment; this research neither alleges a filing defect nor presumes one from lack of the original order.

**Officer classification.** The inferior/principal issue is preserved for regular as well as interim United States Attorneys, including Hilario's argument that prolonged tenure removes any temporary-status distinction. The statutory office, powers and executive controls are the focus. Sections 516 and 519 place litigation and direction under the Attorney General; section 518 permits executive reassignment of a case; section 541(c) places removal in the President. The Attorney General does not acquire a general discharge power from those supervisory provisions. Under the current Edmond rule, actual combined controls matter; no single-factor supervisory formula from the historical lower opinion becomes controlling law. Souter's published Edmond concurrence calls for duties, jurisdiction, tenure and removal in addition to the superior relationship. The office's substantial work and long tenure are considerations, not automatic classification rules.

**Interbranch appointment.** Both executive encroachment and judicial integrity are live components. The lower court acknowledged Hilario's concrete institutional distinction from Morrison: section 546(d) does not disqualify appointing judges from later cases involving their appointee, and authority is vested in the district court as a court. Those features must remain in the strongest contrary position. The Government answers that appointment does not confer judicial supervision, removal, charging control or influence over the prosecutor's execution of duties; presidential removal and ordinary appointment, and the Attorney General's case-control powers, remain. Morrison v. Olson, 487 U.S. 654, Ex parte Siebold, 100 U.S. 371, and Mistretta v. United States, 488 U.S. 361, remain pre-divergence authorities within their actual holdings. The reported opinion's discussion supplies the specific interbranch appointment and institutional-integrity issues; it does not prove actual bias or direction in Hilario's prosecution.

**As-applied duration and remedy.** The six-year tenure challenge is separately preserved, even if the route is valid on its face. If the ordinary office itself is inferior, duration is not a conversion from acting inferior to acting principal merely by passage of time; if classification or structural invalidity is found, its consequences require the appropriate separate reasoning. The Government also preserved an alternative argument that dismissal of the indictment was wrong even assuming Gil lacked lawful office; the lower court expressly did not reach it (footnote 6). Do not omit that alternative or treat it as an established cure. The presently supplied record does not establish the indictment's independent authorizing acts or a later ratification. An affirmance of appointment validity can avoid that factual question; an invalidity disposition must resolve the preserved remedial ground on supported facts or remand it without inventing authorization, harmlessness or mandatory dismissal. Ryder's meaningful relief for a timely adjudicator-appointment defect does not automatically dismiss a prosecutor's indictment.

Landry is scheduled June 15, 2001, after Hilario. It is not entering law here. The current neutral projection places the possible dependency in the other direction, Hilario to Landry. No future Landry proposition has been consulted.

## Shafer v. South Carolina

Simmons is the Court's own June 17, 1994 decision: a seven-Justice due-process holding permits an effective accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction once the State places future dangerousness in issue and the relevant noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole. A separate five-Justice controlling remedy holding applies the State-borne Chapman standard and directs a new lawful penalty proceeding if death is pursued when the error is not harmless. The rule is not structural error or automatic reversal. The packet correctly preserves the changed statutory sequence and the unresolved dangerousness predicate.

The statutory-stage question must concern the jury's actual legal alternatives once it finds an aggravator. An earlier possibility of judicial sentencing without an aggravator is analytically different from the jury's later binary choice. Conversely, a jury's parole question alone does not establish that the prosecution put future danger in issue. Rejecting the categorical structural distinction can leave that factual predicate and the appropriate error inquiry to the state court. A final harmful-error determination or new-hearing command must rest on a sufficiently supported record, and guilt remains untouched.

The lawful alternatives also include the published independent Eighth Amendment position of Souter and Stevens in Simmons: a requested judicial explanation of a sentencing term reasonably likely to be misunderstood, without a necessary State-raised-danger predicate. It has two prior supporters and is not governing law. O'Connor and Kennedy's Simmons writing confines their due-process ground to State-raised danger and reserves the further remedial determination. Scalia and Thomas's dissent disputes the disclosure duty on the absence of a misleading State assertion, while leaving a materially misleading release assertion distinct; it contains no conditional harmlessness determination. These positions must be considered individually, not counted as present votes.

Ramdass, effective June 12, 2000, preserves truthful descriptions of actual or conditional legal consequences. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined its controlling conditional-information ground; Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas defended the actual legal alternative at the jury stage, and O'Connor preserved her ministerial-entry qualification while relying on a separate habeas ground. Shafer involves actual statutory parole ineligibility after the aggravator, rather than an anticipated separate conviction. Ramdass's collateral-review conditions do not replace Chapman's direct-review inquiry here.
