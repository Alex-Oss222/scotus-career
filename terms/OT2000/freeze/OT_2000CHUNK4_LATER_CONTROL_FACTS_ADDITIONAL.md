# Later neutral facts — additional objective source recitations

These are preparation facts for fresh neutral validation. They contain no target Supreme Court action, merits rationale or lineup. Reported litigation assertions retain that status. They do not establish invented filings or admissions.

## Clark County School District v. Breeden

The source is the official report at [532 U.S. 268](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-268.pdf). The matter comes on a petition; no plenary argument date is reported. The lower judgment order is 232 F.3d 893, with reasoning at 2000 WL 991821 (Ninth Circuit, July 19, 2000), reversing the February 9, 1999 district summary judgment in No. CV-S-97-365-DWH(RJJ).

The amended claim alleges two adverse actions tied to different asserted protected activities. The October 21, 1994 hiring-review meeting involved an applicant's explicit remark in a psychological evaluation report. Review of that report was part of Breeden's and her coworkers' duties. The district opinion reports that she conceded reading the report's statement itself did not upset her. Her objection concerned the supervisor's response and the men's ensuing exchange and laughter. She complained to that employee and to two assistant superintendents.

Her administrative charge was filed August 23, 1995. Her federal complaint was filed April 1, 1997. Rice told the union representative on April 10 that she was contemplating transfer; service occurred April 11. Rice's affidavit stated she learned of the action only after April 11, and that assertion was unchallenged. Transfer followed in May. The district presentation of causation relied entirely on the filing/transfer chronology.

The right-to-sue letter was issued about three months before the transfer discussion. Breeden first raised it in her appellate reply, not in the district court or her opening appellate brief. The appellate court suggested it could have supplied first notice of the administrative charge. The reported record does not establish Rice's knowledge of the letter. The federal statutory and regulatory ten-day employer-notice requirements are legal commands; they are not findings of actual receipt by Rice or her predecessor.

## Shaw v. Murphy

The public report separately recites that Murphy's complaint included vagueness and overbreadth objections to the disciplinary rules as applied. The appellate court had not resolved those objections. The insolence rule refers to conduct intended to harass or alarm an employee and gives examples directed to an employee. The disciplinary report contained no finding that the letter was directed to Galle or intended to harass him. Murphy's sworn statement that he did not believe Galle would read the letter was not contradicted by record evidence. These are record assertions and omissions, not a new finding that the letter could not threaten legitimate prison interests.

Source: [Shaw report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-223.pdf), final page and footnote. The separate writing's proposed legal treatment is withheld from this extract.

## Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers

The report recites G & G's statement at oral argument that it had sued awarding bodies in state superior court under the statutory action provisions to recover payments withheld on previous projects. It does not establish an assignment for these three projects. Whether the ordinary contract action against the contractor remains open is a question of California law; the source describes J & K Painting Co. v. Bradshaw, 45 Cal. App.4th 1394, 1401 n.7 (1996), as rejecting a rule that failure to obtain assignment necessarily forecloses every other remedy. The existence of potential defenses does not establish their success.

Source: [Lujan report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-189.pdf). The historical Supreme Court's resolution is withheld.
