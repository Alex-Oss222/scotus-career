## Historical premises tested

The historical per curiam found an equal-treatment defect and reversed with an immediate mandate on the conclusion that a constitutionally adequate recount could not satisfy the state-law December 12 objective. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas joined that writing. Scalia and Thomas also joined Rehnquist's complete Article II concurrence, which supplied independent grounds for reversal. The concurrence did not maintain that every statutory contest or every judicial interpretation was forbidden. It attacked the particular allocation of decision-making authority, meaning of legal votes, treatment of deadlines and appropriateness of the ordered relief.

Stevens and Ginsburg rejected the liability conclusion and would have affirmed, while accepting a corrective opportunity if a defect were assumed. Souter and Breyer supported correction of materially divergent standards rather than the terminal remedy. Their historical cross-joins do not create a common seven-Justice rationale: Souter's Part III and Breyer's Part I-A-1 carry the relevant liability distinctions. The reconciled commitments below arise from the actual grounds, not from the historical label that seven Justices saw constitutional problems.

Actual Palm Beach has controlling force here. It recognizes ordinary state interpretation, the actual conflict between the late-return provisions, and §5's conditional federal benefit, but expressly leaves proved legislative displacement open. Its due-process treatment under Yoshina does not resolve Article II. The December 11 majority clarification is admitted because its necessary remand predicate survives. Its statutory reasoning was also available historically; it cannot itself justify a departure by being described as newly discovered. The routine notice-and-return premise remains distinct from that verified state source and proves no compliance, execution or tally.

The record here does differ in material remedial respects: no federal December 9 stay or consequent cessation is entered, and no post-December 8 execution, current progress, complete protocol, completed count or impossibility finding is admitted. Nor are historical descriptions of ad hoc teams and denied objections automatically facts of this timeline. The corrected neutral account also distinguishes testimony from Broward complaint allegations, a full punch accompanied by a dimple from two fully punched presidential choices, and sorting discrepancies from proved duplicate certified votes. These limits narrow the supported liability account and prevent importing a terminal implementation conclusion. They do not establish timely feasibility and do not defeat a separate statutory-displacement ground.

## Express corrections for Scalia and Thomas

The original across-the-board rejection of an independent Article II reversal by these two Justices is not sustained after full comparison. The historical concurrence expressly quotes §§102.168(3)(c) and (8), acknowledges statutory ambiguity and ordinary judicial interpretation, and discusses the December 11 clarification at 531 U.S. 118 n.2 and 120. The original reliance on those same powers and explanation does not identify a changed premise. Actual Palm permits their federal displacement inquiry; Yoshina regulates the distinct due-process ground. Further review therefore supports the following substantive corrections, rather than mechanically restoring every historical conclusion.

**Scalia.** Retain his equality objection, threshold conclusion and rejection of a universal ban on contests. Revise his Article II position to find an independent defect in the challenged inclusion and examination directions insofar as they replace the legislature's allocation of responsibility and substantive legal-vote requirements. His full historical join at 111–120 supplies direct, issue-specific evidence; his actual Thornton and Palm Beach positions supply no contrary binding premise. On his view, the combined de novo treatment of discretionary board decisions and automatic inclusion of late contest tallies improperly removes the remaining effect of certification and executive discretion. Some late amendments may lawfully be accepted under actual Palm; that does not decide whether this later decree may eliminate the residual limits altogether.

His separate legal-vote objection likewise survives. The historical analysis distinguishes a tabulation failure from failure to mark a ballot in the prescribed manner, invokes the statutory instruction and machine provisions and the Secretary's delegated interpretation, and rejects applying §101.5614(5) as a general answer for these ballots. The fact that an intent provision predated the election does not itself resolve its statutory scope. The strongest contrary argument remains the state court's reading of legal votes, rejected votes, the change-or-place-in-doubt language and broad investigative authority. Scalia recognizes those provisions but finds their use here a material displacement when read against the particular statutory allocation and pre-election voting rules. He does not infer that every undervote is illegal, decide an individual ballot or prescribe a federal two-corner rule.

**Thomas.** Make the same bounded Article II correction on his own full historical join, preserving his independent equality position and actual Thornton emphasis on the constitutional allocation. His original conclusion that the packet does not justify invalidating the contest itself answers too broad a theory: the historical concurrence recognizes the contest and rejects this particular exercise of it. General remedial authority cannot, on his view, erase the legislature's remaining substantive and institutional conditions. Neither actual Palm's recognition of permissible interpretation nor the same December 11 account supplies a reason for him to abandon the historically adopted allocation-of-authority and legal-vote objections. His position therefore supports independent reversal of the challenged directions, without invalidating §102.168, creating a new ballot entitlement or adopting a categorical immunity for administrative decisions.

These are competing legal conclusions about the supported decree and statutory scheme, not newly found facts about voter instructions received by a particular elector, actual reliance, count progress or a tally. They do not supply a separate due-process violation merely from post-election interpretive disagreement.

**Their remedy has two distinct branches.** On their preferred Article II ground, reverse the challenged inclusion/examination directions and remand for disposition consistent with the pre-existing allocation and substantive voting requirements; an equality protocol alone cannot validate the same unauthorized relief. Preserve the separately rejected Nassau and already-examined Palm Beach claims. If the Court rejects their Article II ground and grants relief only for unequal treatment, each retains the narrower conditional commitment to a corrective remand under the binding state-law December 12 limit. Neither joins an affirmative extension beyond that limit. The two branches must not be counted as unconditional support for the four other corrective-remand votes.

The historical concurrence's Part III separately declared the remedy impossible as of December 8, including objections and appeals. The absence of a December 9 stay does not by itself rebut that assertion, because the concurrence did not premise it solely on the stay. This reconciliation carries forward the state-law deadline position but does not import the impossibility assertion as an established fact of the present record. The independent displacement ground remains available without it. No blanket termination of every potentially lawful state action, winner declaration or numerical order follows from that correction.
