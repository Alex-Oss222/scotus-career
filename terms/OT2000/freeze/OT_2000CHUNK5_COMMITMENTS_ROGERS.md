## Reach, form and fixed premises

All eight non-Stone Justices participate. This is direct review of a preserved due-process objection; no habeas nonretroactivity or statutory gate applies. The November 1, 2000 argument and contested constitutional standard support a signed plenary decision, selected independently of author counts.

Tennessee authoritatively held that the year-and-a-day limitation survived its 1989 legislation. No Justice may affirm by adopting the intermediate court's rejected account that legislation had already abolished it. The challenged court action abolished an existing rule, rather than clarifying that it never applied. Fiore therefore supplies a useful distinction but does not decide the federal permissibility of this genuine change. Medical causal proof does not itself satisfy the abolished legal time limitation. The stabbing's wrongfulness and the defendant's personal reading or reliance are not substitutes for the objective legal-warning inquiry.

The two supported paths are foreseeable common-law development under due process, or impermissible retrospective enlargement despite authority to abolish prospectively. The Ex Post Facto Clause directly governs legislation. Its purposes inform the judicial question without automatically importing every legislative category into every common-law adjudication.

## Enhanced comparison and compatibility limits

The affirmance commitments rest on the common-law setting, the rule's limited Tennessee decisional history, and the date-eligible legal landscape identified in the neutral packet. They do not rest on later abolition, later medical developments, a finding of subjective knowledge, or the proposition that murder's general prohibition erases every limiting condition. Lanier is extended to this context through its reasonable-warning principle rather than treated as an already controlling year-and-a-day holding. The state court's reason for reform is relevant to foreseeability but is not itself a license for all retrospective change.

The reversal commitments distinguish a sound reason to change law from sufficient advance warning that existing law would be changed for past conduct. Their rule does not require a previous case matching Bowdery's medical sequence or a defendant who personally planned survival for more than a year. It protects the acknowledged legal time limit. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer can share that narrow ground; Scalia's and Thomas's stronger legislative analogy need not become a common holding. Breyer's refusal to equate every judicial and legislative change does not make his judgment-only unless a proposed opinion insists upon that broader theory.

All eight preserve Fiore's clarification/new-law distinction and Carmell's actual legislative proof holding. No Justice may treat medical causation as resolution of the separate legal limitation. No new fact about Tennessee reliance, another jurisdiction's specific abolition date, or an unmentioned Tennessee controlling case is adopted. The supplied record supports the bounded federal comparison; any claim requiring a more exact pre-May-1994 survey must return for sanitized source preparation before it is added.

## Remedy

The four affirmance positions leave the murder judgment in place against this federal objection, without adjudicating other offenses or broader constitutional questions. The four reversal positions reject sustaining this murder conviction through retrospective abolition and remand for lawful state proceedings consistent with the former limitation. They do not select or order a lesser-offense conviction, impose a new sentence, direct an automatic retrial, or decide double-jeopardy questions without their actual source and preservation predicates.
