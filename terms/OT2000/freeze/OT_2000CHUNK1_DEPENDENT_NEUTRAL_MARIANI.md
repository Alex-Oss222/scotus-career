**Event:** Mariani v. United States; assigned merits date December 12, 2000; en banc judgment below reported at 212 F.3d 761. This handoff supplements the Mariani section of the [frozen neutral packet](OT_2000CHUNK1_NEUTRAL.md). It supplies entering law and source limits, not an outcome or any Justice's present position.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling with this refresh, the original neutral packet, and the authorized entering-law slices. No new fact or procedural reconstruction is adopted. The two granted constitutional questions and the separation from the pending criminal prosecution remain unchanged.

## Actual earlier law

The December 11 decision in [Daggett's exact public reading slice](../entering-law/OT_2000CHUNK1_DAGGETT_PUBLIC.md) is now effective. It replaces the original neutral packet's statements that no controlling Daggett decision has been identified and that the dependency remains unresolved. The slice is a reading copy of the completed decision, not an independent source of authority. The current neutral workspace inspected for this refresh still ends on December 4; its lag does not negate the specifically supplied completed December 11 decision. No later calendar item or unestablished same-day sequence supplies additional law.

Daggett Part I applies Russell and Shrink Missouri: government must justify a direct candidate-contribution ceiling with an objectively supported, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest and a close fit preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy in the actual setting. Maine's $250 per-election legislative ceiling survives on its particular evidence and operation. That result supplies no dollar safe harbor, presumption that every contribution restraint is valid, completed-bribery requirement, or burden on challengers to prove that campaigning is impossible. Ordinary political agreement or access does not by itself establish the required corruption mechanism. The contribution restriction requires its own justification; public financing cannot cure an independently invalid private restraint.

Daggett Part II sustains Maine's two responsive public-grant triggers on a direct controlling rationale: the demonstrated connection to the capped, qualified financing program and its operation establish neither an independent legal restraint nor substantial practical suppression of opposing lawful activity. The actual qualification rules, cap, participant restrictions, favorable-independent-spending adjustment, and conditional fund-shortage exception matter to the adjudicated mechanism. Competitive consequences must be assessed, not dismissed as harmless merely because enrollment is voluntary. The ruling is neither blanket immunity for subsidies nor authority to reclassify independent advocacy as coordinated contributions. It does not decide the corporate treasury or false-name prohibitions presented in Mariani.

Daggett Part III rejects an additional whole-system coercion theory because the combined operation preserves a meaningful private alternative on that record. It requires attention to cumulative burdens even where individual components survive. An attractive public option or greater resources for an opponent is not itself compulsion, but nominal choice cannot excuse actual substantial suppression. This is not a general command that all political financing channels receive identical treatment.

## Retained baseline and issue-specific relevance

The [baseline slice](../entering-law/OT_2000CHUNK1_BASELINE.md) and [Holdings slice](../entering-law/OT_2000CHUNK1_HOLDINGS.md) remain operative within their distinct scopes:

- **Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit**, effective April 28, 1999: Russell's sufficient-interest, close-fit, and meaningful-association rule; Shrink Missouri's January 24, 2000 application retains that government burden. Daggett adds a new application, not a replacement standard.
- **Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association**, effective May 15, 1995, with Russell's April 28, 1999 formulation: actual candidate-conduit risk and real collective-advocacy burdens matter. Other associations or aggregate political success do not alone cure the burden on the chosen association. Candidate contributions and independent expenditures remain distinct.
- **Candidate financing, political-committee thresholds and membership exclusions**, effective June 25, 1997: Akins preserves actual transaction definitions, candidate connection, statutory thresholds, membership exclusions, separate segregated-fund channels, and their separate reporting provisos. Ideological agreement alone does not establish cooperation or coordination. This rule supplies no finding that a particular Mariani transaction is covered or exempt.
- **Support-sensitive public campaign financing**, effective March 17, 1999: Reform Party rejects an automatic resource-parity claim while preserving challenges to actual exclusion or suppression. Daggett's treatment of that principle does not decide every claimed inconsistency in private financing law.
- **Special statutory tribunals for election-financing challenges**, effective March 17, 1999: substantial qualifying FECA constitutional questions follow §437h to the en banc court of appeals; the distinct Fund Act route is not substituted. Standing, authorized-plaintiff status, and substantiality remain separate. Daggett changes no review channel for Mariani.

Day's particular responsive-financing judgment and independently supported propositions remain effective. Neither its four-Justice rationale nor an individual judgment concurrence became a unified controlling rule through Daggett. Day's protected nonprofit independent-expenditure application does not decide direct corporate candidate contributions.

## What remains for adjudication

The federal corporate-source prohibition in then-effective §441b is not merely another numerical contribution ceiling. The analysis must address the presented treasury-to-candidate restraint, its actual corporate channels and exceptions, and National Right to Work Committee within its proper scope. Daggett's close-fit framework informs that inquiry but supplies no automatic conclusion from Maine's sustained amount or public-grant program.

Mariani's asserted soft-money comparison remains relevant to the challenged provisions' actual justification and fit. It does not establish that party spending, direct candidate transfers, independent advocacy, and concealed reimbursements are factually or legally identical. The government must defend the challenged burdens on their own terms; its asserted ability to regulate a distinct direct-transfer or attribution problem without regulating every influence channel remains an argument to evaluate, not a new holding supplied by this handoff.

The §441f false-name question retains its independent attribution and circumvention functions. Its disposition need not follow automatically from the corporate-source disposition. Accurate disclosure, the identity of the true source, actual coordination where legally required, and the applicable statutory exceptions must remain distinct; no indictment allegation establishes guilt, mens rea, a completed exchange, or sufficiency of criminal proof.

The original lawful alternatives remain available only on their supported grounds and proper remedial scope. Neither the refresh nor the calendar decides them. No later campaign-finance enactment, blanket soft-money judgment, retroactive criminal liability determination, or unchallenged party-financing remedy enters the case.
