## Two paths and their limits

**A — ordinary Turner protection.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's enhanced-protection rule and remand. The inclusion of legal advice in an inmate-to-inmate communication does not itself increase its constitutional weight or create a special adviser privilege. Turner requires a reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, considering the actual connection, alternatives for exercise, effects of accommodation and ready alternatives. It does not authorize treating any administrative label as conclusive. Johnson v. Avery and Lewis protect meaningful access under their own predicates; Pearson protects against knowingly fabricated retaliatory discipline with actual causal proof. Those rights survive without a content-based bonus for all legal advice.

**Strongest objection to A:** Assistance can be indispensable within the prison's own access system, and content-blind treatment might disable a retained right. A rejects only automatic enhancement. Actual necessity, the availability of another effective adviser, the rule's operation and improper retaliation remain legally relevant to the proper native claim and to ordinary Turner application. Murphy was not assigned to Tracy because their security classifications prohibited the assignment, and another clerk was offered; those facts are relevant, not automatic proof that the restriction or its application is valid.

**B — enhanced legal-assistance weight.** Affirm a distinct enhancement because legal assistance facilitates access and petition rights. Its strongest support is Avery's protection of indispensable inmate assistance and the practical concerns preserved in the Lewis separate opinions. Its defect is conflating a derivative access claim with a categorical speech privilege independent of actual obstruction or the operative prison context. It makes the legal-advice label do work not supplied by those holdings and can privilege a mixed communication without deciding its real function or administrative effects.

**Application/remedy:** Rejection of B does not conclusively uphold the discipline. Murphy's complaint includes unresolved vagueness and overbreadth objections as applied. The insolence language concerns intended harassment or alarm and examples directed to employees; there is no finding that this letter was directed to Galle or intended to harass him, and Murphy's denial of anticipated readership was uncontradicted. Those limits cannot be converted into an affirmative finding of harmlessness to prison administration, but do prevent this Court from inventing the missing rationale. Ordinary rule construction, Turner application and preserved due-process questions return for adjudication. A recipient's lost qualifying claim or a fabricated retaliatory accusation is not established merely by this record. Prospective relief, if warranted later, remains subject to the original PLRA conditions.
