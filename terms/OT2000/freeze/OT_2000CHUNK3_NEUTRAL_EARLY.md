# Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1240; 531 U.S. 356
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-21 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Congress validly authorized private federal-court suits for money damages against state employers under Title I of the Americans with Disabilities Act.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit rejected the state defendants’ categorical immunity position and reversed the district court’s immunity disposition in the consolidated employment actions.
* **Relief Requested:** The state employers seek immunity from private damages actions. The employees and United States seek adjudication under the ADA’s express authorization.
* **Material Record:** Patricia Garrett alleged adverse employment treatment associated with breast cancer and her position at a state hospital. Milton Ash alleged employment problems related to asthma and sleep apnea and requested workplace accommodation. Their allegations present disability-employment claims against state employers. The immunity question does not establish that either claimant was a qualified individual, suffered prohibited discrimination, was entitled to the requested accommodation, or proved a compensable injury.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The presented question concerns Title I private money-damages enforcement, not the full validity of every ADA title or remedy. Statutory clarity, constitutional legislative power, substantive violation, and award-specific limits must remain separate. The historical Court’s Article I prohibition is not the simulation’s law. A valid Article I ground may resolve amenability without deciding the independent Fourteenth Amendment enforcement argument.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date is February 21, 2001. The Eleventh Circuit judgment is reported at 193 F.3d 1214.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000. Any intervening decision effective before the stated event date must be incorporated before adjudication.

### Issue 1: Express authorization and Article I power

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank holds that Congress may authorize private federal enforcement against a State when the underlying obligation and remedy fall within its Article I authority and authorization is unmistakable. Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents applies that rule to state-employment regulation and expressly rejects categorical immunity for the authorized ADEA action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Both the duty and enforcement measure must be within an enumerated power. Clear text alone does not establish power, but a nonconsenting State has no categorical immunity from a properly authorized federal-question action satisfying these conditions.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; 42 U.S.C. §12202’s express removal of state immunity; Title I’s covered-employer, qualification, discrimination, accommodation, defense, and remedial provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents supplies the controlling state-employment enforcement principle. Application to the ADA requires identifying a valid employment regulation and an appropriate enforcement measure, not pretending the ADA is the ADEA.
* **Open Point:** Whether the presented Title I action falls within that Article I authority and express authorization; no established violation or award follows from amenability.

### Issue 2: Independent Fourteenth Amendment enforcement ground

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores preserves congruence and proportionality between identified constitutional injuries and the remedial or preventive measure. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank preserves a valid Section 5 route as well as its independent Article I route. Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents reserved the ADEA’s Section 5 validity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish the constitutional protection from the broader statute. Congressional prevention may address constitutional injury without making every statutory violation an individual constitutional tort, but the statutory reach must satisfy the governing enforcement standard.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment; City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, subject to subsequent controlling precedent.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Section 5 question is independently substantial but is not required if Article I sustains this authorized enforcement measure.
* **Open Point:** If necessary, whether the legislative record and Title I’s coverage and remedies establish adequate congruence and proportionality. No conclusion on that record is required by the proposed Article I route.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The States argue that the ADA imposes obligations beyond constitutional disability-discrimination requirements and that Congress lacked a sufficient record of unconstitutional state conduct to justify private damages under Section 5. Employees and the United States rely on the clear abrogation provision, documented discrimination and accommodation barriers, and congressional enforcement power. In this simulation they also have the controlling Article I route recognized in Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank and Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents. The historical assumption that Article I cannot authorize the action cannot be silently incorporated into either side’s legal premise.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm amenability under Article I and reserve Section 5. It may independently examine Section 5 if needed. A categorical immunity reversal based solely on an Article I prohibition would require openly changing controlling precedent, rather than calling historical doctrine governing law. Any amenability ruling returns ordinary statutory liability and relief issues to the lower courts; it awards no damages and establishes no accommodation entitlement.

# Buckman Co. v. Plaintiffs' Legal Committee

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Buckman Co. v. Plaintiffs' Legal Committee
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 98-1768; 531 U.S. 341
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-21 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act preempts state tort claims whose asserted duty and causal theory depend on fraud in obtaining the FDA’s clearance of medical devices.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed dismissal of state-law fraud-on-the-FDA claims brought by patients allegedly injured by orthopedic bone screws. The district court had dismissed those claims on preemption and other grounds.
* **Relief Requested:** Buckman seeks dismissal of the fraud-on-agency theories. The patients seek to proceed under state tort law, maintaining that compliance with federal disclosure duties and state liability can coexist.
* **Material Record:** Buckman consulted for the device manufacturer in obtaining §510(k) clearance for uses involving arm and leg bones. Patients alleged that the screws were intended for spinal use and that the FDA would not have cleared them absent misleading representations. Physicians later used the devices in the spine, and plaintiffs alleged resulting injuries. The alleged fraud and the counterfactual agency decision are not adjudicated facts. Clearance through substantial equivalence differs from full premarket approval; off-label physician use differs from an applicant’s false statement. No FDA finding of the alleged fraud or withdrawal of clearance is established in the record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the state duty actually asserted and distinguish an independent traditional injury claim from a claim existing solely because of alleged deception of the federal regulator. The pleading posture permits assuming well-pleaded allegations, not finding fraud, injury causation, or a different agency outcome. The question does not submit every product-liability claim involving an FDA-regulated device.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date is February 21, 2001. The judgment below is reported at 159 F.3d 817.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000. Any intervening decision effective before the stated event date must be incorporated before adjudication.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified. A proposed position is not precedent.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific outcome-dependent earlier OT2000 matter is identified. Apply any intervening effective ruling that bears on the issue before final use.

### Issue 1: Express device preemption and parallel duties

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr requires comparison of actual federal and state conduct requirements. Common-law damages duties can be requirements, but genuinely parallel independently available state duties are not additional merely because damages are available. Substantial-equivalence clearance does not itself establish substantive FDA design approval.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under §360k(a), identify the applicable federal requirement, the asserted state requirement, an actual difference or addition, and the required connection to the regulated subject. Do not infer preemption from shared subject matter or mere compliance.
* **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. §360k and the applicable FDA rules, including the operative clearance requirements. Section 360k’s express text does not itself answer every separate conflict question.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr protects genuinely parallel traditional duties but creates no private FDCA enforcement action and no categorical immunity from tort law.
* **Open Point:** Whether the asserted fraud-on-agency duty is independently available state law or an attempted private enforcement of the federal regulatory relationship.

### Issue 2: Actual conflict with federal enforcement allocation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. permits independently established conflict preemption despite a saving provision, but federal compliance, overlap, agency preference, or silence alone does not establish conflict. An operative statutory command or supported regulatory objective must actually conflict with the particular state duty.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The comparison is duty-specific and source-grounded. Courts must identify the enacted allocation being displaced, not invoke a general preference for uniform regulation or presume that all common-law suits interfere.
* **Other Governing Law:** The FDCA’s enforcement allocation, including 21 U.S.C. §337(a), and its provisions for agency investigation and response to submissions and fraud. The Act’s exception language and the particular cause of action must retain their own scope.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Exclusive federal enforcement can materially distinguish an agency-fraud theory from an ordinary injury claim using federal standards as evidence, but that distinction requires the actual pleaded duty.
* **Open Point:** Whether allowing these plaintiffs to obtain damages based on a private determination that the agency was defrauded and would have denied clearance displaces the federal enforcement arrangement.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Buckman argues that the claims make private juries police the FDA’s submission process and reconstruct discretionary clearance decisions, despite Congress’s federal enforcement arrangement. The patients emphasize traditional state protection against fraud and injury, the absence of permission to deceive the FDA, and Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr’s preservation of parallel duties. The United States supports preemption of the agency-fraud theory while retaining the distinction between FDA regulation and physicians’ off-label treatment decisions. The court below found insufficient conflict; the question is whether its characterization overlooks the source and object of the asserted duty.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may preempt the particular theory because it depends solely on federal agency disclosure obligations and privately substitutes for the assigned enforcement process. It may reject preemption if an independently available state duty and no actual conflict are established. It may distinguish claims following an authoritative agency fraud determination, but no such finding exists here and the Court must not fabricate it. Dismissal of this theory does not immunize the devices, consultants, or manufacturers from every independently pleaded and supported state duty.

# Central Green Co. v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Central Green Co. v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-859; 531 U.S. 425
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-21 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether 33 U.S.C. §702c immunizes the United States from alleged subsurface water damage merely because the irrigation canal carrying the water is part of a larger federal project having a flood-control purpose.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed judgment on the pleadings dismissing the claim against the United States. It treated the Madera Canal’s place within the larger Central Valley Project as sufficient for flood-control immunity even though the canal itself serves an irrigation purpose.
* **Relief Requested:** Central Green seeks reversal of the categorical immunity dismissal and further proceedings on its damage claim. The United States seeks affirmance under the Flood Control Act.
* **Material Record:** Central Green owns approximately 1,000 acres of pistachio orchards crossed by the Madera Canal, a federal facility leased to the Madera Irrigation District. It alleged negligent design, construction, and maintenance caused subsurface flooding, orchard damage, and increased operating costs. The parties agreed that the canal belongs to the Friant Division of the Central Valley Project and that flood control is among the larger project’s purposes. The pleadings did not resolve the character of the particular damaging waters, the reasons for their release, the contribution of different flows, or negligence and causation. The irrigation district’s liability is distinct from federal statutory immunity.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal dismissal was on the pleadings, without the factual development needed for a water-specific inquiry. Allegations of subsurface flooding describe the claimed injury and do not by themselves concede that the water is a statutory flood or flood water. Conversely, an irrigation canal can sometimes carry water released for flood control. Rejecting categorical project-wide immunity does not establish FTCA liability or resolve another exception to its waiver.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date is February 21, 2001. The judgment below is reported at 177 F.3d 834.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000. Any intervening decision effective before the stated event date must be incorporated before adjudication.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified. A proposed position is not precedent.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific outcome-dependent earlier OT2000 matter is identified. Apply any intervening effective ruling that bears on the issue before final use.

### Issue 1: The statutory object of flood immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. James remains controlling within its holding concerning releases for flood-control purposes from flood-stage reservoirs. Broad language concerning all project-related water must be distinguished from the question actually resolved.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted immunity to the facts satisfying its own conditions. A general project label cannot substitute for a statutory predicate, and rejecting one immunity rationale does not establish every condition of the underlying cause of action.
* **Other Governing Law:** 33 U.S.C. §702c immunizes the United States from damage from or by floods or flood waters. The Federal Tort Claims Act supplies a separate, conditional consent to specified tort claims. Neither statute can be rewritten as a general exemption for all activity within a multipurpose project.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The received flood-release holding covers its described waters. It does not necessarily resolve ordinary irrigation seepage merely because it occurs somewhere in a project also used for flood control.
* **Open Point:** Whether the character and purpose of the particular damaging flows, rather than the project’s general purpose alone, govern immunity, and what factual proceedings that interpretation requires.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The United States relies on broad language in United States v. James and the administrative difficulty of separating functions within an integrated project. Central Green relies on §702c’s focus on the damaging water rather than a facility’s name or affiliation, emphasizing the canal’s irrigation function and the absence of flood-related findings. The court below applied project affiliation categorically. Neither side’s preferred generalization establishes the origin or purpose of every flow on this undeveloped record.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would adopt a broad project-related immunity rule, including damage by ordinary irrigation flows within a multipurpose system. Reversal can instead require a connection between the damaging waters and the statutory flood category, with factual development concerning their character and release purpose. The latter path leaves possible immunity for actual flood releases and requires further analysis of mixed causes. It does not itself award damages or decide whether negligent canal maintenance is otherwise actionable.

# Lewis v. Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lewis v. Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1331; 531 U.S. 438
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-21 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal court may dissolve its injunction against a seaman’s state-court personal-injury action when stipulations and retained federal jurisdiction adequately protect the vessel owner’s right to limitation of liability, although the seaman has not demanded a jury.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit reversed the district court’s dissolution of a limitation-action injunction. It reasoned that the owner had a right to federal exoneration adjudication and that Lewis’s state bench-trial action sought no remedy preserved by the saving-to-suitors clause.
* **Relief Requested:** Lewis seeks restoration of permission to pursue his Illinois action. The vessel owner seeks an injunction requiring the liability controversy to remain in the federal limitation proceeding.
* **Material Record:** Lewis alleged injury while working aboard the owner’s vessel. The owner commenced a federal action for exoneration from or limitation of liability and posted a surety bond representing its vessel interest. The federal court enjoined related actions under Supplemental Rule F. Lewis filed a state personal-injury action without demanding a jury and stipulated that his claim was less than the fund, that he would not assert a state judgment’s preclusive effect on limitation, and that limitation issues could be litigated in federal court. The district court found the owner’s limitation interests protected, dissolved the injunction, and retained the limitation proceeding to address any later need. It found the adequate-fund route satisfied and also regarded the single-claimant route as likely applicable.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Liability, exoneration, and limitation are distinct. A claimant’s stipulations must genuinely preserve the federal limitation right and cannot bind an absent claimant without a legal basis. The district court’s continuing jurisdiction and the established fund relationship matter. The absence of a jury demand does not itself determine the existence of a saved state remedy. The Court need not resolve negligence or the owner’s privity or knowledge.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date is February 21, 2001. The judgment below is reported at 196 F.3d 900.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000. Any intervening decision effective before the stated event date must be incorporated before adjudication.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified. A proposed position is not precedent.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific outcome-dependent earlier OT2000 matter is identified. Apply any intervening effective ruling that bears on the issue before final use.

### Issue 1: Reconciling saved remedies with federal limitation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Langnes v. Green and Lake Tankers Corp. v. Henn recognize state adjudication where the federal limitation interest is adequately protected, including the single-claimant and adequate-fund settings.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the substantive federal right requiring protection and tailor ancillary restraint to that right. Federal admiralty jurisdiction does not erase the statutory reservation of other remedies or give every vessel owner exclusive federal adjudication of ordinary liability.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1333(1); the Limitation of Liability Act as then codified at 46 U.S.C. App. §§183 and 185; Supplemental Admiralty Rule F. The applicable statute limits qualifying liability to the protected vessel interest and preserves its own privity-or-knowledge condition.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A federal court may protect limitation without monopolizing every liability question. The saved-remedies clause is broader than a right to a jury alone.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual stipulations, fund relationship, and retained jurisdiction adequately protect the owner here, permitting dissolution within the district court’s discretion.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The owner argues that Rule F allows a demand for exoneration and that federal adjudication of liability should accompany limitation, particularly when no state jury remedy is sought. Lewis argues that Congress saved state remedies generally and that the owner’s limitation right remains fully protected by his stipulations and the retained federal case. The district court reconciled those interests through controlled dissolution; the appellate court treated federal exoneration and the absence of a jury demand as independently dispositive.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can recognize that the limitation right, rather than a freestanding entitlement to a federal liability trial, defines the necessary protection, and restore the district court’s discretionary dissolution on this record. Affirmance would require an independent exclusive exoneration right or a demonstrated inadequacy in the protective arrangements. If protection later becomes inadequate through additional claims or other material developments, the retained federal proceeding permits a lawful response. The Court should not order final release of the security or adjudicate liability as part of this forum ruling.

# United States v. Tank

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Tank
* **Citation or Docket:** 200 F.3d 627 (9th Cir. 2000)
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-21 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Federal Rule of Evidence 901 permits admission of Internet chat-room logs based on participant testimony concerning their creation and the defendant's screen name, despite objections that the logs are incomplete and susceptible to undetectable alteration?
* **Question Granted:** Review is limited to the questions stated above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit judgment affirming the three federal convictions and remanding for resentencing. The grant concerns authentication of the chat-room logs, not the independent sentencing errors or the separately adjudicated automobile search.
* **Relief Requested:** Tank seeks reversal of the authentication ruling and appropriate relief from the affected convictions. The United States seeks affirmance of admission and of the conviction component, with the independent sentencing remand preserved.
* **Material Record:** Tank participated in the sixteen-member Orchid Club, whose members discussed, exchanged, and produced sexually explicit images of children. Member Ronald Riva programmed his computer to save conversations while he was online. Before the investigation, Riva deleted nonsexual conversations and extraneous material, including date and time stamps, to save disk space. At an evidentiary hearing and at trial, he described creation of the files and testified that the printouts accurately represented the retained conversations. Tank argued that incompleteness and the ability to alter words or screen names without detection defeated authentication. The court admitted the exhibits; the parties contested completeness before the jury. Tank acknowledged using the name Cessna in one recorded conversation. Other participants identified that name with him, including testimony that Tank appeared at an arranged meeting with its user. The jury convicted on a production conspiracy, a receipt-and-distribution conspiracy, and a distribution count. The original 235-month sentence contained grouping and role-adjustment problems independently requiring resentencing. The record does not establish that every retained line was independently corroborated or that a forensic expert certified that alteration was impossible. [United States v. Tank](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/200/627/521509/).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The foundation objection was preserved and heard before admission. Tank’s separate failure to move for acquittal affected review of his general sufficiency claim, not preservation of authentication. The question accepts that the exhibits are partial logs; it does not ask whether they are a complete archive. Relevance, attribution, authenticity, hearsay, completeness, and ultimate proof of guilt have different functions. The existing sentencing remand must not be erased by an affirmance of the conviction component.
* **Relevant Dates:** Lower-court judgment: January 4, 2000. Merits event: February 21, 2001. Apply the Federal Rules governing this prosecution, not later restyled wording or later digital-evidence standards.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Rule 901(a) makes authentication a condition of admission and asks for evidence sufficient to support a finding that the offered matter is what its proponent claims. The trial court screens sufficiency of that foundation; the jury retains the ultimate evidentiary assessment. The reviewed court used abuse of discretion for the foundation determination, subject to correction of an erroneous understanding of the Rule. Preserved nonconstitutional admission error, if established, requires an independent whole-record harmlessness inquiry.

### Issue 1: Authentication of an identified partial electronic record

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision specifically resolves authentication of Internet chat-room logs. United States v. Hale distinguishes authentication from the separate constitutional and substantive functions of evidence; its eight-Justice rule concerned accepted public-record certifications, not these private logs. Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, General Electric Co. v. Joiner, and Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael require attention to the governing evidentiary Rule and the proposition actually offered. Their specialized-knowledge screening rules do not turn a participant’s firsthand account into an expert proffer.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The expert-evidence cases preserve flexible screening within original Rules 104(a) and 702 and independently correctable legal error. It supplies no universal requirement of laboratory testing, publication, or a forensic integrity certificate for every electronic exhibit. Old Chief v. United States preserves the separate Rule 403 inquiry and ordinary harmless-error rules; it does not make all alternative evidence compulsory.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 901(a)’s sufficient-foundation threshold governs. Knowledgeable-witness testimony and identifying circumstances are recognized means of authentication, not a requirement to eliminate every possible fabrication. The Government must connect the offered communications to Tank. Satisfaction of Rule 901 does not displace otherwise applicable hearsay, completeness, prejudice, or proof-of-elements requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Black, United States v. Catabran, and United States v. Soulard supply the lower court’s authentication and completeness reasoning. Their circuit-level holdings do not establish a Supreme Court rule. Old Chief v. United States establishes, by five rationale joins on remedy, that preserved nonconstitutional evidence error requires fair assurance from the whole record that it did not substantially sway the judgment, with grave doubt defeating harmlessness.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing Rules require a real foundation but do not demand certainty. The received expert cases govern specialized opinions if offered; they do not adopt a digital exception to ordinary witness authentication. Old Chief v. United States governs a remedial inquiry only after an evidentiary error is established.
* **Open Point:** Whether a knowledgeable participant’s account, the defendant’s limited screen-name admission, and corroborating participant identifications sufficiently authenticate these retained conversations despite deletions and a theoretical opportunity for alteration. No controlling authority requires a complete or demonstrably alteration-proof archive.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit reasoned that Riva’s description and accuracy testimony supplied the initial foundation, and that the admission and independent identifications connected Cessna to Tank. It treated the disputed deletions and possibility of alteration as matters for the jury after that foundation was established. Tank’s strongest objection is that digital text is easily edited and deletion of surrounding material can disguise changes in authorship or meaning; a witness’s assurance should not automatically cure a concrete integrity defect. His proposed exclusion rule must, however, confront the independent identification evidence and distinguish actual inadequacy from mere technological possibility. The United States seeks a technology-neutral sufficient-evidence rule: firsthand knowledge plus corroborating circumstances can authenticate a partial record, leaving credibility and completeness for adversarial testing. It cannot plausibly claim that every objection to manipulation always concerns weight, because evidence insufficient to support authenticity must be excluded in the first place.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could affirm the conviction component by finding a sufficient foundation on this record, while expressly maintaining the sentencing remand. It could reject a categorical weight-only approach and remand for a genuine foundation determination if the court had treated missing authentication evidence as legally irrelevant. If it found admission erroneous, it would need either a supported whole-record harmlessness determination or a remand for that determination under the applicable preserved-error rule; exclusion would not itself require an acquittal or establish that every conviction was affected. A universal demand for unedited original logs would require a basis in the governing Rules beyond the possibility of alteration shared by many forms of evidence.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A sufficient-foundation holding could cover electronic conversations authenticated through a knowledgeable participant and corroborating attribution, without guaranteeing the truth or completeness of every statement. A contrary holding could require more when identified integrity defects prevent a reasonable finding of authenticity. Neither path supports an automatic rule admitting every screen-name printout or excluding every edited file.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.
