## Governing law and material alternatives

**F1 — Governing inquiry.** Agostini retains Lemon as one inquiry into actual governmental authority and function; purpose, effects, and institutional relationships are evidence, not independently operative purpose, endorsement, or entanglement prongs. An official message is attributable to the board, but attribution alone is not a violation. Government may not adopt religious doctrine as its own truth, solicit adherence through public authority, or use curricular authority to protect a favored religious account as an official religious task. Objective academic religious study, independent private expression, and genuine nondevotional conscience protection remain distinct. A prohibited governmental function does not require successful persuasion or a completed penalty.

Epperson's and Edwards's surviving curriculum rule denies schools constitutional immunity when they tailor teaching to a religious dogma; those cases do not prohibit every biblical reference or every scientific critique. Settle preserves genuine academic method and rejects categorical religious exclusions. Lee and Santa Fe concern actual school religious organization and representative religious choices; neither turns this nonprayer statement into a prayer. Good News Club protects its actual private forum and does not make the teacher's mandatory message private. Marshfield demands identification of a continuing governmental act; Children's Healthcare permits its limited accommodation and retains examination of the function actually performed.

**F2 — Official protection of the biblical account warrants affirmance.** The board requires science teachers to interpose its own assurance preserving a specifically identified religious explanation whenever evolution is taught. The adoption record and selective curricular placement explain the function of the text: protecting that religious account against the perceived implications of the science lesson, rather than merely informing pupils that education does not compel personal belief. Retention of the biblical reference after the failed creation-science initiative and explicit debate about the reference supports this conclusion in conjunction with the operative resolution; individual motives alone do not establish it. The generic reference to other concepts and the actual OR preserve personal choice but do not remove the board's particular official protective message. The final instruction to examine alternatives is read within that same message, not as an independent command that pupils affirm creationism.

The strongest contrary argument is that the school continues teaching evolution, commands no religious belief, permits independent judgment, and disclaims persuasion about all other concepts as well. F2 answers that the forbidden function need not be a prayer, a ban on science, or an order to believe. The State can preserve pupils' freedom of conscience without making protection of the biblical account part of its own required science presentation. The outcome depends on this complete text, actual directive, selective placement, and supported adoption context together; neither the word Bible alone nor a religious board member's motives is sufficient.

**F3 — Noncompulsion reassurance warrants reversal.** The statement tells pupils that receiving scientific instruction does not determine what they personally must believe. Its OR recognizes both independent judgment and parental belief; other concepts extends the reassurance beyond biblical belief; the invitation to critical examination does not prescribe the result. The board neither teaches the Genesis account as true, requires creation science, removes evolution, nor organizes worship. On this path the government undoubtedly speaks, but its actual function is a permissible assurance about the limit of its instruction, not religious adoption or solicitation. Protecting conscience may take account of a concrete religious concern without itself establishing that faith.

The strongest objection is the deliberately retained biblical reference, the creation-science history, and the restriction of the message to the subject at which that controversy is aimed. F3 accepts their evidentiary relevance but finds that they do not transform this operative noncompulsion statement into a forbidden religious function. The court should not substitute a presumed religious appeal for the actual OR text. This conclusion permits this disclaimer; it does not authorize compulsory creation instruction, religious grading criteria, hostility toward unbelief, or a parental veto over scientific content. No hypothetical new implementation is assumed in either direction.

**F4 — Remedy and severability.** F2 sustains the injunction against the required complete disclaimer while preserving lawful science teaching, objective religious study, private expression, and genuine conscience protection. It supplies no adjudication of the isolated first paragraph or a hypothetical amended resolution. F3 rejects the federal constitutional basis for that injunction and remands for conforming disposition, without ordering a new policy or prejudging any independently lawful issue outside the grant. No Associate votes for an evidentiary remand to obtain proof of conversion, because the existing legal dispute concerns the actual resolution and supported adoption record. No Associate treats the change from the panel's initial AND transcription to the actual OR as irrelevant; they disagree about what legal work it does in the full message.
