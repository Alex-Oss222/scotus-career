# United States v. United Foods — independent non-Stone commitments

**Stage:** Independent modeling, before comparator reconciliation and before the present matter's Stone position.
**Event:** Merits, No. 00-276, June 25, 2001.
**Research cutoff:** Immediately before the June 25 event. No uncoordinated same-day decision is treated as earlier law. The clean screen of newly entered June 18 public kernels remains an integration gate.
**Participation:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; no supplied nonparticipation. No author is selected. These commitments do not complete the Court's judgment.

## Reach, record and entering law

The Sixth Circuit reversed the consolidated judgment sustaining the advertising assessment and government enforcement. United Foods used the administrative petition/review channel. The government's brief, page 32 note 19, expressly states that the government-speech argument was neither raised nor addressed below and invokes Lebron's included-argument rule. This is an actual preservation fact, not an inference from an operator's missing document.

The program principally funds generic mushroom promotion. Producers remain competitors without comparable collective price, output and sales restraints or an antitrust exemption. United Foods wishes to promote its mushrooms as superior; an assessment financing the generic message therefore burdens a real commercial expressive choice even though it does not compel the producer to utter that message or identify it publicly as the speaker. The record does not establish an objection to every research or industry-information expenditure.

Section 6104 establishes four to nine Council members appointed by the Secretary through producer/importer nominations, regional production/import representation and the stated nine-member limit. No more than one member may be appointed from any single producer's or importer's nominations; failure to nominate permits appointments on the basis provided in the order. Appointment is statutory control, not a finding of ordinary popular election or unconstrained industry selection. Plans, projects and budgets require the Secretary's approval before implementation; implementing contracts require an approved plan and accounting. These controls supply a substantial governmental-speech argument. They do not establish that a federal official actually selected or controlled each advertisement's message.

Assessments are collected from first handlers for domestic fresh-market production, paid directly by importers, and remitted directly by producers selling their own mushrooms to consumers. The successive statutory ceilings are one-quarter, one-third, one-half and then one cent per pound. Certified mushrooms to be exported as mushrooms may not be assessed. Authorized expenses and a reasonable reserve are distinct from the challenged advertising obligation. Section 6104(h)'s prohibition on influencing legislation or governmental action or policy separately permits recommendations to the Secretary for order amendments and recommended voluntary grade and quality standards. None of those permitted recommendations is converted into an exception to a different provision.

Section 6105 requires the stated voting majority and its majority share of average annual volume; the mandatory five-year referendum differs from the discretionary referendum available after three years on a request from at least thirty percent of producers/importers. Industry approval is neither constitutional waiver nor sufficient justification for compelled speech. Section 6106 supplies petition, hearing, a ruling lawful to be final, timely district review and a mandatory lawful administrative remand when the ruling is contrary to law. Pending petition proceedings do not themselves impede §6107 enforcement. Constitutional invalidity, statutory procedure, collection liability and the amount or availability of monetary relief remain separate.

**Controlling law.** Actual Glickman (June 25, 1997) permits germane, nonideological promotion within a comprehensive collective agricultural marketing arrangement that already substantially displaces independent competitive decisions, with no personal attribution and freedom to advertise and criticize. It expressly leaves advertising-only independent-market programs outside its holding. Common benefit and majority approval alone are insufficient. The register **Generic promotion assessments in collective agricultural marketing**, present operation effective Glickman, June 25, 1997, carries that limited rule.

Actual Southworth (March 22, 2000) permits a distinct broad, objective and viewpoint-neutral private-expression program justified by the university's educational function. It does not convert each recipient's message into government speech or establish a universal compulsory-subsidy permission. Actual Rosenberger distinguishes independent editorial speech from official speech in its controlled private-publication program; it did not decide compulsory fees. Actual Lebron (February 21, 1995) classifies a specially created, permanently federally governed corporation as governmental for constitutional responsibility. It does not decide that every message funded by a public institution is governmental expression exempt from an objector's subsidy claim.

Keller, 496 U.S. 1, 10–14 (1990), rejects treating a state agency label and public purpose alone as an answer to compulsory professional dues. It distinguishes the Bar's member-funded, advisory professional structure from ordinary representative government and taxation. It supplies an analogy, not a holding that every industry council is private or every earmarked assessment unconstitutional. Abood and Keller's germaneness analysis requires the independent purpose justifying the compelled association; a program cannot establish that purpose merely by declaring its chosen advertising germane to itself.

The preservation register **Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review**, present operation effective Lebron, February 21, 1995 for the disavowal component, retains Yee's argument/claim and question-presented distinctions. All eight accept that the government-speech justification is fairly included as a legal argument concerning the same challenged assessment and First Amendment controversy. That does not compel its adjudication, establish all needed facts or enlarge the relief requested. Their actual exercise of discretion differs below.

## Adversarial paths and individual grounds

### Stevens

**Committed ground and source.** He joined actual Glickman's integrated collective-program rationale and its express advertising-only reservation. He will not turn common benefit, nonideological content and lack of attribution into an independently sufficient rule after joining their insufficiency there. His actual Southworth concurrence emphasizes the complete program's purpose, breadth and payer-message relation; the mushroom program promotes one commercial message rather than furnishing a broad arena of competing expression. On the private-subsidy theory, the judgment against this advertising obligation is supported.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** His full Lebron reach and remedial joins favor deciding an included legal basis where institutional facts make it concrete, with remand for a separate unadjudicated merits issue. The statutory appointment of the Council and mandatory executive approval of its plans distinguish this theory from a mere public-purpose label. Keller is the strongest counterargument: specialized producers funding their own industry program resemble the Bar's member-financed advisory body more than ordinary government. That comparison prevents immediate classification as government speech, but does not make the express federal controls irrelevant. The limited remand should establish who actually determines the message and whether officials adopt it as governmental expression, rather than presume either control or private independence.

**Judgment, joins and limits.** Vacate the categorical invalidation and remand for that alternative; no enforcement award follows until it is resolved. He can join a statement that Glickman's existing collective-market rationale is absent and that governmental status alone is insufficient. He cannot join refusal to consider the included ground merely because it was not raised below, or an unconditional final private-speech invalidation while the concrete alternative remains unexamined. A separate writing would explain the law/record distinction and the limited remand, not uphold advertising solely as beneficial economic regulation.

### O'Connor

**Committed ground and source.** Her actual Glickman opinion ties the valid subsidy to independently justified collective marketing and expressly refuses to make common benefit or majority support sufficient. The mushroom producers have not surrendered comparable competitive autonomy. Compelling them principally to finance the generic message creates the association whose purpose supposedly justifies the charge. Abood/Keller cannot make that circular explanation sufficient. Her actual Southworth join supplies a different independently justified broad expression program; it does not save this one-message promotion. Affirm.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** Her Lebron separate position recognizes that inclusion permits, rather than commands, review. Here the government-speech theory was not litigated below and requires a distinction between the statutory Council's status and the actual ownership/control of the advertising. The government's strongest answer is that appointment and mandatory approval are enacted, established facts, not an invented new record. They make the alternative substantial, but do not settle whose expression the particular program conveys. She declines the new operational theory in this review; that is prudential discretion, not a jurisdictional prohibition or a finding that all possible government-speech defenses were waived forever.

**Join limits and separate function.** Can join a limited affirmance preserving actual government speech and unrelated valid assessments. Cannot join an all-compulsory-advertising ban, a finding that the Council is categorically private, or a holding that included arguments may never be considered. No separate writing is needed if the lead explanation preserves the exact program and preservation limits. The strongest merits counterargument—free riding in a legislatively authorized, majority-approved commercial project—fails because promotion alone cannot supply the independent collective obligation her Glickman rule requires.

### Scalia

**Committed ground and source.** He joined Souter's actual Glickman dissent throughout and Thomas's additional compelled-financing ground. Those writings treat payment for commercial expression as a First Amendment burden even absent personal attribution or ideology. The rationale for independent collective representation is weaker here than in the program he rejected there. Generic advertising may benefit a producer without authorizing compelled support for the selected message. Affirm without overruling Glickman's actual limited holding.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** His Lebron opinion is the strongest objection to refusing the government's new argument. He accepts fair inclusion and rejects a categorical forfeiture rule. Lebron nevertheless resolved institutional constitutional responsibility on an established corporate record and then separated that threshold from speech merits. Here even treating the Council as governmental for constitutional duties would not classify all funded messages as the government's own. Keller shows why agency status and public purposes do not alone settle a specialized compulsory-funding dispute; Southworth, whose Court opinion he joined, likewise concerns private expression at a public institution. Appointment and approval are real, but the alternative depends on operational attribution not adjudicated below. He elects not to initiate that new factual theory at this stage.

**Judgment, joins and separate function.** Affirm the advertising judgment and remand only as needed for lawful implementation. Can join the existing Glickman distinction and the discretionary nonreach ground; cannot join a rule that constitutional actor status automatically immunizes every targeted speech assessment. His Glickman Central Hudson joinder permits a separate alternative commercial-speech argument if necessary, but this commitment does not invent an efficacy finding or require a least-restrictive-means trial. A separate writing is unnecessary if the lead sufficient compelled-subsidy ground is retained.

### Kennedy

**Committed ground and source.** His complete Glickman join adopted a collective-regulatory justification rather than a general permission to finance useful advertising. His Rosenberger opinion separates a government's own speech from privately edited expression it finances; his Southworth join permits a broad neutral expression opportunity for an independent educational purpose. This market supplies neither comparable collective regulation nor a broad plural expression program. The government cannot use advertising alone to justify compelling producers to associate in that very advertising. Affirm.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** His Lebron join means the note 19 omission does not itself forbid review. The strong opposing route would reach the government-speech theory because federal appointment and mandatory approval may make this official program speech. His limit is the difference between constitutional actor status and the funded message. No actual adoption or control of each advertisement is established; statutory permission to approve a plan is not treated as a finding that the government spoke every word. He declines this new fact-dependent basis rather than making that finding or requiring a remand to create it. Keller's distinction between ordinary government and special-purpose dues reinforces the need to preserve that boundary.

**Joins and separate function.** Supports the direct private-subsidy rule, case-specific nonreach and bounded remedy. Cannot join a finding that generic commercial disagreement is unprotected, that majority approval waives speech rights, or that government speech could never justify an assessment. No separate writing is required for the stated scope. The strongest merits contrary argument—no attribution, unrestricted counteradvertising and common benefit—does not eliminate the distinct burden of being made to pay for the chosen message.

### Souter

**Committed ground and source.** His actual Glickman dissent grounds the objection in Abood, Keller and Lehnert: a legally justified common representative duty cannot be replaced by any useful promotional campaign. The mushroom program lacks even the comprehensive marketing features that did not persuade him in Glickman. His Southworth concurrence accepts a many-speaker distribution program advancing no single ideology; it does not approve compulsory payment for an industry's one preferred sales message. Affirm on the sufficient compulsory-subsidy ground.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** His Lebron join permits review of the included government-speech legal argument. The exact statutory controls make the theory substantial, so he does not dismiss it as a label or pretend the Secretary lacks authority. Keller nevertheless requires distinguishing the industry's funded expression from ordinary governmental advocacy; executive oversight of a producer-nominated specialist council does not establish actual official message adoption. He declines a newly operationalized factual ground not addressed below. The strongest objection is that a limited remand could answer that question without importing facts. His prudential conclusion is to resolve the preserved private-subsidy case on the established theory, rather than vacate a supportable judgment to develop the unpresented alternative. No litigant default is inferred from missing operator material.

**Joins and separate function.** Can join the Glickman distinction without accepting Glickman's original result as his preferred rule. His dissent's Central Hudson alternative remains a possible additional explanation requiring its own joins; no unproved advertising-efficacy, credit or comparative-superiority conclusion is added here. He cannot join a general exclusion of commercial expression from compelled-speech protection or a holding that government-funded private expression automatically belongs to government. Relief is confined to the advertising obligation, subject to ordinary remedial proceedings.

### Thomas

**Committed ground and source.** His actual Glickman separate dissent rejects the idea that commercial content or payment through money removes speech protection; his narrower join to Souter's sufficient compulsory-subsidy ground does not embrace Souter's reduced Central Hudson standard. His Southworth opinion permits a genuinely broad, neutral educational opportunity rather than a selected orthodoxy. The mushroom message is the object of the compelled association itself. Affirm without making every regulatory fee a speech violation.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** His Lebron join supports consideration of included arguments, and the statutory appointment/approval arrangement creates a plausible alternative beyond mere public funding. But Lebron imposes constitutional responsibility; it does not answer whether an objector's earmarked support purchases governmental or industry expression. Keller rejects automatic exemption by an agency label in a comparable specialist-dues context. Because actual official message adoption is not established and the theory was not litigated below, he declines to reopen the record for it here. This is an exercise of the discretion he joined in Lebron, not rejection of that authority or a universal rule requiring lower-court presentation of every argument.

**Joins and separate function.** Can join the private-subsidy holding and bounded nonreach. Cannot join a statement that the case is governed by discounted commercial-speech protection or that lack of ideology defeats the claim. A separate writing may preserve his refusal to reduce constitutional protection, while recognizing that failure under a lesser test could also support the same result without his adopting that test. It adds no factual holding or categorical prohibition on government speech financed by taxes.

### Ginsburg

**Committed ground and source.** She joined actual Glickman's collective-marketing holding and actual Southworth's program-specific justification. The independent mushroom market does not satisfy the former, and the industry council does not maintain the latter's broad neutral arena for competing private speakers. The compulsory private-message theory cannot be sustained by common benefit, majority support or absence of attribution alone. Her Rosenberger dissent's attention to what public payments actually purchase also makes message attribution substantive, not a matter of labels.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** Her complete Lebron reach and remand joins support considering the included legal alternative and distinguishing a threshold institutional conclusion from ultimate speech relief. Mandatory executive appointments and plan approval supply enough concrete structure to warrant a bounded inquiry. The strongest counterargument is Keller's member-funded advisory body and the risk that every regulatory approval would transform private advocacy into government speech. She therefore rejects immediate enforcement or a government-speech classification from statute alone. Remand must determine who actually sets and adopts the promotional message, the Council's and contractors' roles, and the substance of the Secretary's approval; it cannot assume message control or place an unsupported evidentiary default on United Foods.

**Judgment, joins and separate function.** Vacate and remand for that question. The First Amendment's private-subsidy protection governs if the government cannot establish the premise on which its alternative depends through the proper proceedings; constitutional actor status alone is insufficient. She can join the existing Glickman limits but not an unconditional affirmance declining to examine this concrete alternative. A separate writing would explain why a targeted factual remand follows rather than immediate validation. No monetary recovery or collection amount is decided.

### Breyer

**Committed ground and source.** His actual Glickman join permits a common expense within substantial market cooperation; his Southworth concurrence examines the actual many-speaker educational program and attenuated payer-message relation without converting lack of attribution into immunity. Those sources do not yield a general commercial-promotion exception. The government must provide a different sufficient ground for this independent-market arrangement.

**Alternative path and strongest objections.** His Lebron reach/remand joins support examining an included legal ground when actual statutory structure makes it material. The Secretary's appointment authority, required plan approval and contractual oversight could support official speech rather than coerced private association. Keller is the principal caution: specialized dues and industry participation remain material, and a useful public purpose is not enough. The lawful response is a limited inquiry into the program's actual speaker and final message control, not an invented finding that officials wrote the ads or that producers controlled them free of government. The strongest objection is that this rewards nonpresentation below. The argument is nevertheless included, adversarially raised in the merits proceeding and anchored in the enacted structure; discretionary remand is permissible without becoming mandatory for future abandoned theories.

**Judgment, joins and separate function.** Vacate and remand for that alternative, with no present enforcement. Can join recognition that Glickman alone does not govern and that benefits, industry approval and counteradvertising do not independently answer the compelled private-subsidy burden. Cannot join an absolute rule that every industry-directed assessment must be private speech or a categorical refusal to consider included grounds. A separate writing would set out the functional statutory and operational inquiry and its limited remedy, not extend Glickman solely because the project might increase sales.

## Scope of the two judgment routes

**Affirmance route.** The five affirming Associates support the rule that government may not compel otherwise independent competitors to finance the selected generic advertising merely because the advertising is commercially beneficial, nonideological, not attributed to the payer, and open to private disagreement. The independently justified collective function supporting Glickman's particular program is absent. This applies Abood/Keller's need for a distinct justification without importing every union chargeability rule into agriculture. It preserves Southworth's separately justified plural-expression program and genuinely governmental expression. The Court could decline the new government-speech alternative in discretion while explaining its fair inclusion and the missing operational adjudication; it would not hold that the Secretary's statutory powers are nonexistent or that government speech is legally impossible here.

**Vacatur/remand route.** The other three Associates do not direct government enforcement or reject the private-subsidy objection. They would reach the fairly included alternative and require a bounded determination of the message's actual governmental or private character, considering the statutory nomination/appointment scheme, mandatory executive approval, actual adoption and control, Council and contractor discretion, and the specialized funding arrangement. These are identified sources of the disputed speaker classification, not a newly mandatory numerical or multifactor constitutional test. A government-actor label alone is insufficient; no actual message control is found. If the funded message remains the industry's private expression, the existing independent-market justification fails. If the government establishes that responsible officials actually adopt and control the challenged promotion as the government's own advocacy of the authorized public program, the government may finance that official message through the authorized assessment despite the producer's disagreement; earmarking the exaction to the benefited sector does not alone create a right to withhold it. The statute's governmental-actor structure and a general approval power do not alone establish that operative premise, and the officials need not personally compose every word if actual adoption and final control are established. This is the ordinary-governmental-advocacy branch distinguished in Keller, not an exemption for every nominal public agency or for private expression merely financed or reviewed by government. No independent attribution-to-United-Foods claim is invented. Either outcome requires the proper statutory and remedial proceedings.

No route invalidates every research or consumer/industry-information function, grants an automatic nationwide refund, awards damages, fixes an accounting allocation or decides a new assessment not before the Court. On a ruling that the administrative decision is contrary to law, §6106(b)(3) requires remand to the Secretary with lawful directions; the consolidated enforcement case must conform to the constitutional result. The separate statutory rule that pending review does not itself impede enforcement remains in force. Specific prospective and monetary relief depends on actual claims, accounting and remedial law; uncertainty there is returned work, not an unannounced substantive exemption.
