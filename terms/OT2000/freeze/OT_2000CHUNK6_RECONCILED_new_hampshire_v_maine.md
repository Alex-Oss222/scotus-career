## Ground, remedy and limits available to assembly

Judicial estoppel may prevent a litigant from obtaining judicial acceptance of one position and later gaining an unfair advantage through a clearly inconsistent position. Clear inconsistency, prior judicial acceptance and unfair advantage or detriment guide equitable application without becoming exhaustive prerequisites. A supported mistake or a material governmental-policy or factual change can alter that assessment. Here the accepted meaning of the 1740 words, not merely agreement to settle, supplies the operative premise; the present shore theory repudiates it without such an explanation.

Dismissal rejects this complaint. It does not affirm a lower court, draw every inland coordinate, issue a new boundary decree, determine title independently of estoppel, or decide Maine's separate preclusion defenses. The 1977 decree remains effective in its own scope. No further proceeding, deadline or survey is directed. There is no unresolved reconciliation blocker.
