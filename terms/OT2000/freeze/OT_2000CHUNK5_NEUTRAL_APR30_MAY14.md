# C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 411; No. 00-292
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-30 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federally recognized tribe clearly waives immunity from a state-court action to enforce an arbitral award by agreeing to binding arbitration, specified arbitration rules, the law of the project’s location and judicial entry of judgment on the award?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** On remand following earlier review, the Oklahoma Court of Civil Appeals directed dismissal for tribal sovereign immunity. It concluded that the arbitration and judicial-enforcement provisions suggested willingness to submit to suit but did not unequivocally waive immunity. The Oklahoma Supreme Court denied review.
* **Relief Requested:** C & L seeks reversal of the immunity dismissal and enforcement of its award. The Tribe seeks preservation of immunity despite its contractual dispute-resolution undertaking.
* **Material Record:** The Tribe proposed a standard construction contract for a roof on a commercial building on off-reservation, nontrust land in Oklahoma. The contract required arbitration of claims arising out of or relating to it under American Arbitration Association construction rules and provided that an award would be final and judgment could be entered in accordance with applicable law in any court having jurisdiction. A choice-of-law provision selected the project location’s law. Oklahoma’s arbitration statute provided for enforcement in competent state courts. Before performance began, the Tribe changed roofing material and hired another contractor. C & L demanded arbitration. The Tribe asserted immunity, declined participation, and informed the arbitrator of substantive defenses. An award granted $25,400, plus fees and costs. The Oklahoma trial court confirmed it. No congressional abrogation is claimed. [C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-411.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The present issue is consent to the judicial confirmation proceeding, not whether commercial activity alone defeats immunity. The court must read the actual agreement and incorporated prescriptions as a whole. Consent cannot create otherwise absent federal subject-matter jurisdiction, and immunity from execution against particular assets is distinct from amenability to this confirmation suit.
* **Relevant Dates:** The contract was executed in 1993. The lower judgment was reconsidered after Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. The merits event is April 30, 2001; incorporated contract terms must be evaluated as actually applicable, not silently replaced with later forms.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Tribal immunity is a threshold protection from suit. Clear waiver is measured by the authorized undertaking’s scope. A merits decision about breach, award validity or execution requires the ordinary separate legal predicates after that threshold is resolved.

### Issue 1: Clear contractual consent to judicial enforcement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. retains immunity for governmental and commercial contracts on and off reservation land unless the Tribe clearly waives it or Congress unequivocally authorizes suit. Signing a contract or defaulting alone is insufficient.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Tribal immunity permits suit upon an unequivocal waiver and remains distinct from the underlying substantive obligation. Tribal recognition, land status, regulatory applicability and a particular remedial forum must be analyzed separately.
* **Other Governing Law:** [C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-411.pdf) identify the agreed decision process and the judicial enforcement contemplated by the parties.
* **Material Other Authority:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma preserves the separation of immunity and substantive obligation. Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez requires clear relinquishment. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan reinforces the need to identify actual consent and its scope; agreement by one person does not automatically bind another.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering immunity rule forecloses reliance solely on the commercial location or perceived fairness of enforcing a debt. It permits a waiver actually expressed through an agreement to an identified adjudicative process.
* **Open Point:** Whether this combination of binding arbitration and express judicial confirmation states sufficiently clear consent despite omitting the particular words sovereign immunity.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

C & L argues that final arbitration plus express entry of judgment gives the dispute process a judicial endpoint and would be deprived of its stated operation if immunity barred the very confirmation promised. The Tribe argues that immunity may be relinquished only clearly and that courts having jurisdiction may mean courts possessing jurisdiction independent of any waiver. It characterizes ordinary arbitration language as insufficient to show surrender of a sovereign protection. The competing positions concern what this agreement actually says, not an implied waiver merely from doing business with a nontribal party.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find a clear waiver confined to arbitration and contemplated confirmation, leaving all other procedural defenses intact. It may instead conclude that the agreement preserves immunity because the judicial language is too conditional. Either path must honor Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc.; creating a commercial-contract exception would alter governing law unnecessarily. Recognizing a clear waiver does not require reinstating every part of the award without examining any still-preserved independent objection.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A tribe that expressly accepts binding arbitration and judicial entry of judgment under the specified enforcement regime consents to the contemplated confirmation action. The decision need not decide immunity for unrelated claims, unrestricted damages suits, or particular execution measures.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

Martinez; First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan. The public opinion is used here only for the underlying record, proceedings, quoted law and arguments. # Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 424; No. 99-2035
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-14 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a court of appeals must independently review the constitutional excessiveness of a punitive-damages award, or may defer to the district court under an abuse-of-discretion standard, and whether the Seventh Amendment changes that allocation.
* **Question Granted:** The October 10, 2000 order expressly limited review to: “What is the standard of review of a trial court's ruling on a challenge to the constitutionality of a punitive damage award?” [531 U.S. 923](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep531/usrep531orders/usrep531orders.pdf).
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed the district court’s refusal to reduce a $4.5 million punitive award under an abuse-of-discretion standard. In a separate published ruling, it set aside the injunction against copying the tool’s functional appearance. The punitive-damages judgment is the judgment presented here.
* **Relief Requested:** Cooper seeks vacatur and independent appellate examination of constitutional excessiveness. Leatherman seeks affirmance under deferential review. Neither the review-standard question nor an adverse ruling on that question itself fixes a replacement punitive amount.
* **Material Record:** Cooper prepared to market its ToolZall before production was complete. Employees modified a Leatherman Pocket Survival Tool, removed its trademark, and used photographs and a touched-up drawing as illustrations of Cooper’s product. The materials appeared at a national hardware show and in later marketing. Cooper ordered a promotional recall but some materials continued circulating. A jury found passing off, false advertising and unfair competition, awarded $50,000 compensation, and found the culpability necessary for $4.5 million punitive damages. It separately found infringement by the original tool but no resulting infringement damages; the Ninth Circuit subsequently held the appearance functional and copying lawful. The district judge sustained the punitive amount as proportional and needed to deter an enterprise of Cooper’s size. The appellate court accepted that evaluation while recognizing that the advertised photographs did not perceptibly promise superior features absent from the product delivered. These distinctions matter to culpability and harm: lawful product copying is not itself the adjudicated advertising wrong. [Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-424.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Cooper preserved its federal excessiveness objection and challenges the appellate review actually supplied. The underlying compensation and the separate functional-design ruling are not reopened by selecting a constitutional review standard. A court must distinguish fact findings, state-law discretionary assessment, and the federal constitutional boundary. A challenge to whether Oregon may receive part of the award is not presented merely because the judgment directs 60 percent to a state compensation account.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc. may affect a disputed functionality premise and must be considered before adjudication. The Ninth Circuit’s separate ruling establishes the present procedural posture.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Seventh Amendment protects jury-tried historical facts and restricts judicial reexamination. Federal new-trial practice and appellate review must be allocated by function. Review of a constitutional limitation can be independent without a new appellate trial of misconduct, causation or loss.

### Issue 1: Constitutional excessiveness and appellate review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore establishes by five direct joins a binding substantive due-process limit on grossly excessive punitive awards, with qualified guideposts of reprehensibility, actual or supported potential harm, and comparable sanctions. Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg requires meaningful review of amount or an adequate functional substitute. TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources has a majority-supported, record-specific procedural holding but no controlling numerical or potential-harm formula. United States v. Bajakajian independently requires de novo constitutional proportionality review for punitive forfeiture and clear-error review of underlying facts.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore preserves independent substantive protection despite adequate procedure, rejects mechanical ratios, and separates evidence of other conduct from punishment for that conduct. The forfeiture review rule supplies an analogy, not an already-decided rule for every punitive jury award. Ornelas v. United States similarly separates ultimate legal evaluation from historical findings, with no universal mixed-question rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process Clause supplies the asserted constitutional limitation; the Seventh Amendment governs the civil jury allocation. State punitive entitlement, federal trial procedure, and constitutional excessiveness remain separate inquiries.
* **Material Other Authority:** Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc. applies state compensatory limits through district-court new-trial review and requires deferential appellate assessment of that supported discretionary valuation, while independently correcting legal error. Its express treatment preserves the distinct protections of BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore and Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg rather than deciding punitive constitutional review.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A reviewing court must respect supported facts about conduct, injury and intent. Neither an enterprise’s wealth nor speculative market injury alone justifies a punitive sum. Constitutional review cannot punish copying that the separate judgment treats as lawful merely by relabeling it advertising culpability.
* **Open Point:** Whether the legal boundary under BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore requires independent appellate judgment when the punitive sum was set by a jury, and how that judgment coexists with the Seventh Amendment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Cooper argues that a constitutional maximum cannot vary with the district judge’s discretionary tolerance. Independent application gives the substantive guarantee operative force and keeps the court of appeals from approving punishment for lawful copying or conjectural profits. Its strongest application point is the mismatch between the limited advertising injury found and the district court’s broader assessment of competition and deterrence. Leatherman answers that the jury made a record-based judgment about deliberate deception and repeated conduct, the trial judge examined it, and Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc. protects the trial court’s superior position. It argues that treating the amount as a legal conclusion strips the jury’s moral and factual assessment of ordinary protection. These arguments must confront distinct propositions: independent constitutional review does not necessarily authorize an appellate damages valuation, while adequate trial process does not extinguish a substantive due-process objection.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could require independent constitutional review while retaining deference to supported historical findings, vacate the appellate punitive judgment, and leave application to the Ninth Circuit. It could instead hold that meaningful discretionary review adequately protects the constitutional boundary and affirm on the review issue, subject to an independently preserved legal misapplication. An application ruling setting or invalidating a particular amount would require examination of the actual record and permissible remedy beyond merely selecting the standard. The limited grant concerns the standard of review; overruling BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore's substantive rule lies outside that grant. Disagreement with it cannot silently erase its controlling force.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can address the appellate standard for constitutional punitive excessiveness without prescribing every state system of review, a numerical ceiling, or independent appellate valuation of compensation.

**Material Uncertainty:** The full trial record may affect the appraisal of threatened harm and the source of the jury’s punitive measure. The review-standard issue can be resolved without finding a particular amount lawful. No inference that every circulated promotional item generated a lost sale is warranted.

**Case authorities:** BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore; Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg; TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources; United States v. Bajakajian; Ornelas v. United States; Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc. The public opinion is used here only for the underlying record, proceedings, quoted law and arguments. Its disposition is confined to Section III.


# Rogers v. Tennessee

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Rogers v. Tennessee
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 451; No. 99-6218
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-14 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether due process permits retroactive application of a state supreme court’s abolition of the common-law year-and-a-day limitation on murder liability to a killing committed while that limitation remained part of state law.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tennessee Supreme Court affirmed Rogers’s second-degree murder conviction after holding that the year-and-a-day rule survived the State’s 1989 statutory revision, abolishing the rule judicially, and applying that change to Rogers. It rejected the intermediate appellate court’s different conclusion that the legislature had already abolished it.
* **Relief Requested:** Rogers seeks reversal of the judgment sustaining murder liability under the newly enlarged rule. Tennessee seeks affirmance because judicial correction of an obsolete common-law limitation was foreseeable and did not offend the governing fair-warning principle.
* **Material Record:** On May 6, 1994, Rogers stabbed James Bowdery in the heart. During surgery Bowdery suffered cardiac arrest, was resuscitated, developed cerebral hypoxia and remained comatose. He died from a kidney infection on August 7, 1995, approximately fifteen months after the stabbing. The medical examiner attributed death to hypoxia secondary to the wound. The material interval and causal testimony are undisputed. Tennessee’s homicide statute did not mention the year-and-a-day rule. Nevertheless, the state supreme court concluded that the common-law rule was part of Tennessee law and had not been eliminated in 1989. It then abolished the rule because advances in medicine and proof had removed its original justification. The court relied on its limited Tennessee decisional history and abolition elsewhere in deciding to apply the change to this conviction. The parties’ agreement about the rule’s prior existence and the state court’s authoritative construction are distinct from whether retroactive application satisfies federal due process. [Rogers v. Tennessee](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-451.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is direct review of a preserved federal due-process objection, not a collateral proceeding governed by a habeas nonretroactivity barrier. The Ex Post Facto Clause directly limits legislative enactments; the challenged actor here is a court. The federal court must accept the state supreme court’s account of what Tennessee law previously required while independently evaluating the federal consequence of changing it retrospectively. Medical causation and the legal time limitation cannot be merged into a single factual question.
* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant notice date is May 6, 1994. Death followed on August 7, 1995; the Tennessee Supreme Court’s decision was in 1999. The merits event is May 14, 2001. Later abolition or later medical understanding cannot by themselves supply conduct-time legal notice.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Fiore v. White distinguishing clarification of existing law from a new interpretation must be considered before adjudication. Tennessee characterized this action as abolishing a surviving rule.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Direct criminal review permits correction of a conviction sustained under an impermissibly retrospective legal enlargement. The state-law availability of another offense or a particular corrected judgment is a separate remedial question, subject to preservation, double-jeopardy and ordinary state procedure.

### Issue 1: Conduct-time fair warning and judicial enlargement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Lanier unanimously requires fair warning from law existing when the defendant acts, rejects later decisions and bad purpose as substitutes for missing notice, and forbids novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement. It also rejects a requirement for an identical prior factual case. Carmell v. Texas holds that a State may not retrospectively reduce the evidence legally necessary for conviction; ordinary admissibility and competence changes are different. Its six-Justice rule preserves the bounded categories associated with Calder v. Bull and Collins v. Youngblood, not a free-standing prohibition on every disadvantageous procedural change.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** United States v. Lanier requires a reasonably apparent prohibition at the conduct date. Its particular setting is criminal civil-rights liability; application to abolition of a state common-law homicide limitation requires an express extension. Carmell v. Texas supplies the importance of the offense-date legal boundary but does not itself apply the Ex Post Facto Clause directly to courts.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process Clause constrains unexpected retrospective criminal enlargement. The Ex Post Facto Clause’s textual limitation on legislative lawmaking informs but does not automatically decide the judicial question. The authoritative Tennessee construction establishes that the limitation survived the 1989 statute.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bouie v. City of Columbia bars an unexpected and indefensible retrospective criminal construction by reference to pre-existing law. Tennessee invoked that rule while stressing the common-law setting and developments in other jurisdictions. Its distinction between interpretation and abolition must be assessed rather than assumed to resolve federal fairness. United States v. Lanier requires conduct-time particularity and distinguishes legal notice from culpable intention; it supplies no contrary rule authorizing this common-law enlargement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Notice does not demand proof that Rogers read the rule or calculated the victim’s survival time. Conversely, the obvious wrongfulness of stabbing does not necessarily establish fair warning of every homicide liability later attached to it. Existing criminal prohibition, legally sufficient causation and the offense’s temporal boundary perform different work.
* **Open Point:** Whether judicial abandonment of this admitted, surviving limitation was sufficiently foreseeable to apply to earlier conduct, and whether abolition’s substantive wisdom can satisfy that distinct inquiry.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Rogers argues that the State cannot first acknowledge a substantive limit existing when he acted and then erase it to affirm his conviction. The applicable rule excluded a murder conviction when death occurred outside the specified interval, regardless of adequate proof of medical causation. Other jurisdictions’ departures and improved science justify future change but do not repeal Tennessee law. Tennessee answers that common-law doctrine develops through adjudication; an obsolete rule mentioned only rarely in dicta, never used as the ground of a Tennessee murder disposition, and widely abandoned elsewhere could foreseeably be discarded. The statutory homicide definition supplied notice that killing another was criminal. The strongest disagreement concerns the level of specificity required for warning: general liability for a fatal stabbing or the continuing legal conditions of the particular murder offense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could affirm if the pre-existing legal landscape made this common-law development adequately foreseeable under the due-process standard. It could reverse retrospective application while recognizing Tennessee’s power to abolish the rule prospectively. A conclusion that the 1989 statute already eliminated the rule would contradict the state supreme court’s controlling construction and cannot be used as an alternative federal factual premise. Automatically applying every legislative ex-post-facto category to all judicial decisions would be a broader rule than necessary and would require its own justification.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision can distinguish a permissible prospective correction from an impermissible use of that correction to sustain this conviction, without freezing common-law development or requiring a prior case with identical medical facts.

**Material Uncertainty:** The published record establishes the state-law premise and the death interval. It does not establish that Rogers personally relied on the rule, and no such individualized reliance finding is necessary to frame the notice issue. Available lesser-offense remedies must come from applicable law and the existing record.

## E. SOURCES

[Rogers v. Tennessee](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-451.pdf).

**Case authorities:** United States v. Lanier; Carmell v. Texas; Calder v. Bull; Collins v. Youngblood; Bouie v. City of Columbia. The public opinion is used here only for the underlying record, proceedings, quoted law and arguments. Its disposition is confined to Section III.


# United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 483; No. 00-151
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-14 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether medical necessity permits the manufacture and distribution of marijuana otherwise prohibited by the Controlled Substances Act, and whether a court may create that exemption through modification of an injunction enforcing the Act.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the district court’s denial of the Cooperative’s motion to modify a federal injunction, recognized medical necessity as a legally cognizable defense, and directed reconsideration of an exemption’s criteria. The separate contempt appeal had become moot after the Cooperative promised compliance. The live judgment concerns modification of the injunction.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of the medical-necessity ruling. The Cooperative seeks an exemption for distribution to seriously ill patients lacking effective alternatives, either as a statutory defense or through equitable discretion.
* **Material Record:** California’s 1996 Compassionate Use Act removed specified state criminal prohibitions for patients and primary caregivers possessing or cultivating marijuana on a physician’s recommendation or approval. The Oakland Cooperative operated a dispensary with a medical director, nurses, physician statements and screening interviews. Members received identification allowing them to obtain marijuana. The United States sued in January 1998 under federal law and obtained a preliminary injunction against manufacture, distribution and related possession with intent. The Cooperative did not appeal that initial order and distributed marijuana despite it. At the contempt stage, the district court found inadequate proof that every recipient faced imminent harm without marijuana. It later denied a motion to modify the injunction. After the appellate decision, the district court fashioned a four-part medical-necessity exception; a separate government appeal followed. The record does not establish that every patient satisfies those conditions. State authorization, medical recommendations and federal statutory authorization remain distinct. [United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-483.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The motion to modify presents a live injunction controversy reviewable under the applicable interlocutory provision. Mootness of the purged contempt proceeding does not eliminate that separate controversy. The question is not whether marijuana can benefit any patient as a medical fact. Constitutional objections concerning federal power and patients’ interests were advanced but not resolved by the court of appeals; they cannot be treated as adjudicated facts or silently rejected merely through statutory construction. The statutory issue and asserted alternative equitable ground are squarely presented.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Controlled Substances Act’s §882(a) authorizes district courts to enjoin violations. Authorization to issue equitable relief does not alone command an injunction in every circumstance; lawful discretion concerning relief must be distinguished from a substantive exemption from the conduct prohibition. The current proceeding does not adjudicate a criminal conviction or automatically revive the mooted contempt dispute.

### Issue 1: Statutory necessity and the lawful scope of equity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc. requires attention to Congress’s actual remedial scheme and preserves traditional equity within its statutory limits; emergency circumstances do not supply every omitted remedy. Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc. likewise distinguishes an established equitable power from a desired result and rejects general equity as a substitute for a missing source of authority. Neither case specifically adjudicates a necessity defense under the Controlled Substances Act. Both preserve authorized statutory injunctions and established equitable discretion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Statutory abatement and equitable relief require separate consideration of legal authority, the authorized remedy, and case-specific discretion. They establish no universal prohibition on injunction modification, no automatic remedy from serious need, and no rule importing the detailed procedures of one statutory scheme into another.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable §841(a)(1) prohibits knowing or intentional manufacture, distribution, dispensing and related possession except as authorized by the subchapter. Marijuana is listed in Schedule I; the statutory framework connects that classification to lack of currently accepted medical use and provides an authorized research route. The Cooperative claims no authorization under that route. California’s exception to its own law is not a federal authorization. These provisions are examined in the historical statutory version quoted in the judicial record.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Bailey discussed common-law necessity in construing a criminal statute while requiring an available lawful alternative to be considered. It does not establish an unlimited judicial power to rebalance a policy Congress resolved. United States v. Rutherford distinguishes statutory medical regulation from judicial creation of a treatment exemption. Hecht Co. v. Bowles and Weinberger v. Romero-Barcelo preserve equitable discretion without making a court free to contradict an enacted priority.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existence of discretion over an injunction’s necessity and form does not necessarily permit a declaration that forbidden distributions are substantively lawful. Conversely, rejecting a necessity exemption does not by itself decide every constitutional objection, compel the broadest possible injunction, or establish criminal liability without its elements.
* **Open Point:** Whether the federal scheme permits this medical-distribution exemption, and whether the same exemption can independently be sustained as an exercise of equitable discretion.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Government relies on the express conduct prohibition, Schedule I placement, authorized research pathway and Congress’s distinct treatment of substances that can be medically prescribed. It argues that the proposed exception would alter that scheme rather than interpret an ambiguity. The Cooperative invokes the common-law necessity background, the extraordinary suffering alleged, the absence of reasonable alternatives, and a narrow exception tied to physician-supported need. It also argues that the district court misunderstood its equitable discretion: even if no criminal defense exists, a civil court should retain flexibility in defining the injunction. The Government’s answer is that genuine remedial discretion cannot be used to grant the very substantive permission that the statute withholds. Neither side can equate California’s choice with a change to federal law.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the claimed statutory defense and the same exemption under general equity, reverse the appellate judgment, and remand without deciding the full constitutional challenge. It can recognize a necessity defense only by explaining why the statutory prohibition and classification leave room for it, then require actual proof of each condition rather than assume all members qualify. It can also distinguish lack of a substantive defense from discretion whether and how to issue an injunction; that distinction supports careful remedial treatment but not automatically the Cooperative’s requested exemption. A separate constitutional limitation, if properly presented and established, would require its own source, analysis and remedy.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can address manufacture, distribution and possession with that intent by this Cooperative. It need not adjudicate every individual patient’s separate possession case or the general existence of necessity in all federal criminal law.

**Material Uncertainty:** The record contains competing descriptions of medical need, not adjudicated universal necessity for all recipients. The published material does not supply a lower-court disposition of the independent constitutional objections. The litigation is not a scientific determination that marijuana can never provide therapeutic benefit.

# Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 504; No. 00-1210
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-14 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a reviewing court may set aside a labor-arbitration award because it rejects the arbitrator’s assessment of witness credibility and then direct entry of the claimant’s requested monetary award instead of leaving the contractual merits to arbitration.
* **Question Granted:** The question above identifies the merits issue presented for disposition on the petition. No separate limited-question merits grant has been authenticated; the contemporary proceeding used the petition record rather than a separately scheduled argued merits hearing. Exact petition wording is not represented as verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit judgment of December 7, 2000 directing the district court to return the matter to arbitration with instructions to award Garvey the amount he claimed. This second appellate judgment followed an earlier decision vacating the arbitral denial and a district-court remand for further arbitration.
* **Relief Requested:** The Players Association seeks reversal of the directed award and restoration of the limited judicial role in reviewing the arbitral denial. Garvey seeks preservation of the approximately $3 million award ordered by the court of appeals.
* **Material Record:** Following arbitral findings of club collusion in the free-agent markets after the 1985, 1986, and 1987 seasons, the Association and clubs settled through a $280 million fund. The Association’s Framework permitted individual review limited to whether its distribution criteria had been properly applied. A lost-extension claim required evidence of a specific extension offer made before collusion and later withdrawn because of it. Garvey alleged that the Padres withdrew an offer covering the 1988 and 1989 seasons. After the Association rejected his claim for lack of evidence of an offer, he testified at arbitration and presented a June 1996 letter from former club president Ballard Smith supporting his account. The arbitrator requested additional documentation and rejected the claim. He found substantial credibility problems in Smith’s letter because it conflicted with Smith’s prior testimony denying collusion and interest in extending Garvey’s contract; he also identified absent corroboration and Garvey’s age and injury history. The first appellate panel thought refusal to credit the letter inexplicable because prior arbitrators had disbelieved club witnesses about collusion generally. The second appellate panel held that its earlier decision permitted only a monetary award for Garvey. No dishonesty by the arbitrator is alleged in the described record. [Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-504.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The parties accept that the dispute arises under the Labor Management Relations Act because the settlement machinery remedies breach of the collective-bargaining agreement. Garvey contests review of matters resolved in the first appellate judgment because more than ninety days elapsed before the petition challenging the second judgment. A timely petition from the later judgment permits review of antecedent rulings in the same litigation; the earlier panel’s law-of-the-case instructions bind the district court but do not immunize them from Supreme Court review. This matter is briefed as merits review, without a petition addendum.
* **Relevant Dates:** Underlying collusion seasons: 1985 through 1987. Association rejection: February 1996; Smith’s letter: June 1996. Second appellate judgment: December 7, 2000. Merits event: May 14, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Any earlier effective holding in Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America concerning review of labor-arbitration awards must be considered before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America may affect the scope of review, public policy, or remedy. Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams and C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma do not by their placement alone determine this accepted collective-bargaining submission.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The cause concerns enforcement and review of contractual labor arbitration under the Labor Management Relations Act. Courts retain authority to determine what was submitted, whether the arbitrator acted within the agreement, and whether a recognized ground defeats enforcement. That role is distinct from deciding whether the arbitrator correctly found the facts. The remedy for a properly vacated award must respect the agreement’s remaining allocation of decisionmaking.

### Issue 1: Judicial review of contractual labor arbitration

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan unanimously requires independent judicial determination of antecedent assent absent clear delegation and preserves ordinary appellate review of that judicial determination. Its eight-Justice actual-delegation proposition permits only limited review once delegation exists. It authorizes no new merits-vacatur ground or retrial of an agreed arbitration. Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp. unanimously distinguishes collective contractual rights from independent statutory discrimination claims and refuses to supply a missing clear statutory-forum waiver. Here the claim is under the accepted contractual distribution machinery, not an independent statutory claim inadequately submitted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan preserves the separation between deciding whether parties agreed and reviewing the merits of what they agreed to arbitrate. Neither independent review of a judicial legal conclusion nor the absence of extra deference to a district judge creates independent review of the arbitrator’s credibility findings.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Labor Management Relations Act supplies the accepted statutory route. The Framework confines the arbitrator to applying the approved distribution criteria, including the requirement of a specific extension offer subsequently withdrawn because of collusion. Its limits are legally relevant; whether Smith persuasively proved the required facts is a different question.
* **Material Other Authority:** United Steelworkers of America v. Enterprise Wheel & Car Corp., United Steelworkers of America v. American Manufacturing Co., and United Paperworkers International Union v. Misco, Inc. supply received preterm limits on reviewing contractual labor awards. Judicial disagreement with facts or contract interpretation is insufficient when the arbitrator is arguably applying the contract within his authority. Mercer v. Theriot and Hamilton-Brown Shoe Co. v. Wolf Brothers & Co. support review of an earlier ruling through the later judgment in the same case.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law protects genuine contractual arbitration against a judicial merits substitution while preserving enforcement boundaries. The assent cases do not require fresh consent to each unfavorable finding after an accepted submission. Nor does the limited-review rule enforce an arbitrator’s exercise of powers the agreement did not confer.
* **Open Point:** Whether the described credibility reasoning demonstrates abandonment of the Framework or instead constitutes its application, and whether the appellate court could order the exact monetary recovery even if vacatur were justified. No controlling decision makes an appellate assessment of irrationality a freestanding factual appeal from an arbitral award.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit reasoned that the arbitrator’s reliance on Smith’s earlier denial conflicted with the prior collusion findings, and that Garvey’s account and the later letter left no permissible alternative to an award. It characterized the defect as dispensing independent industrial justice rather than simply making a factual mistake. Garvey’s strongest position is that deference cannot protect an arbitrator who contradicts the governing framework or his own established premises, and that further proceedings need not repeat a logically foreclosed denial. The Association responds that the arbitrator expressly applied the requirement of an actual withdrawn offer, sought supporting material, and made a credibility judgment. A general finding that clubs colluded does not establish this player’s individual contract offer. The Association seeks enforcement of the decisionmaker chosen for those disputed facts. The National Academy of Arbitrators and the Commissioner supported limited judicial review; their institutional interests do not replace the instrument-specific inquiry. Even a court accepting Garvey’s vacatur theory must separately justify replacing arbitration with a court-selected award.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the timing objection permits review of both the directed monetary award and its predicate. If the arbitrator was applying the Framework, the Court can reverse and restore the district court’s denial of vacatur. If an actual departure from the contractual submission is established, it can vacate without deciding disputed individual entitlement and allow further proceedings authorized by the agreement. The Court could reverse only the directed-award component if the defect in that relief is sufficient and the validity of vacatur remains unresolved. Upholding a court-selected award would require a supported legal basis for taking the merits from the agreed tribunal, not simply a strong disagreement with its factfinding.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A merits holding may distinguish failure to exercise contractual authority from an erroneous exercise of that authority. A remedial holding may prohibit a reviewing court from resolving disputed contractual facts and ordering the claimed sum merely because it considers the arbitrator’s explanation irrational. Neither rule requires enforcement of corruption or an award beyond the actual submission.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# United States v. Valentine

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Valentine.

**Citation or Docket:** 232 F.3d 350; Third Circuit No. 00-1425. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; disposition on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** May 14, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term.

**Question Presented:**

1. Whether an unidentified informant's face-to-face report of recently observing a handgun, combined with the suspect's presence in a high-crime area late at night and departure upon seeing police, supplies reasonable suspicion for an investigative stop?
2. Whether briefly stopping and giving one's name in response to a police command constitutes submission to authority under the Fourth Amendment when the person subsequently attempts to flee?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses both questions presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed suppression of a handgun and remanded the federal firearm prosecution.

**Relief Requested:** The Government seeks admission based on suspicion before the command or independent conduct before any seizure. Valentine seeks restoration of suppression or reconsideration under a properly identified seizure time. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Material Record:** Around 1 a.m. on May 8, 1999, a man flagged down two uniformed Irvington officers in a marked car. He said he had just seen a nearby man carrying a gun and described his clothes, beard, gold chain and companion. The informant refused to identify himself. Fifty to one hundred feet away, officers saw Valentine, who matched, with two others. The location was described in uncontradicted testimony as having many shootings. The men began walking away. An officer told Valentine to come over and place his hands on the car. According to the officer's account, Valentine asked “Who, me?”, charged toward him and attempted to push past. The officer grabbed him and wrestled him down; a loaded handgun fell during the struggle. No officer saw the gun beforehand. Valentine asserted that he first stopped and gave his name. The appellate court found no evidence supporting that account but also held it legally insufficient even if accepted. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The court must distinguish historical findings from constitutional sufficiency and determine the actual point of seizure. The informant's recent observation, personal presence and refusal to identify himself have different implications for knowledge and credibility. A reliable report of possession does not alone establish unlawful possession. The Government separately invoked New Jersey's permit requirements and possession presumption; the Third Circuit expressly did not decide that argument. Later struggle and gun discovery cannot justify an earlier completed seizure.

**Relevant Dates:** The November 2, 2000 lower decision precedes the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No companion matter supplies an effective earlier same-term decision resolving this encounter.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before May 14, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any intervening controlling Fourth Amendment decision affecting reasonable suspicion or seizure must be applied.

### Issue 1: Reasonable suspicion from the report and encounter

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Illinois v. Wardlow unanimously preserves objective, particularized suspicion under all circumstances and rejects location alone or a universal flight rule. Its five-Justice application holds the actual look-and-flight record insufficient. Florida v. J. L. unanimously rejects a forcible stop resting on an unaccountable gun accusation corroborated only as to observable identity and location. Ordinary firearm danger supplies no exception. Accountable eyewitness reports and other concrete reliability assurances remain open.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Individualized suspicion of wrongdoing is required for an investigative seizure. Local experience and circumstances may inform that assessment but cannot establish an observation absent from the record. Neither permission to ask consensual questions nor the presence of a gun automatically permits a seizure. A protective search requires its own lawful predicates.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and the inherited Terry v. Ohio framework govern the stop and any protective search. The Government also invokes New Jersey permit requirements and a possession presumption as a distinct argument; the lower court expressly did not decide that argument, and its predicates cannot be assumed.

**Material Other Authority:** Ornelas v. United States requires independent review of constitutional sufficiency while protecting historical findings and giving genuine weight to grounded local experience. The historical Third Circuit's categorical account of flight conflicts with the narrower governing holding in Illinois v. Wardlow. The firearm-right standards likewise preclude assuming that all possession is criminal, without themselves deciding the validity of this permitting law.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The settled totality rule governs this encounter. The Court has rejected reliance on identity corroboration, firearm danger, or location alone. Its existing applications do not resolve every recently observed, in-person report by a witness who refuses identification.

**Open Point:** The report's face-to-face delivery supplies a new reliability application, while the particular totality still requires proof of a connection to wrongdoing. The preserved permitting theory remains unadjudicated and may require further factual and legal development.

### Issue 2: Submission before later flight

**Controlling Simulated Law:** California v. Hodari D. preserves the distinction between physical force and actual submission; the cited precedents do not resolve this alleged brief compliance. Ornelas v. United States preserves the division between historical findings and independently reviewed constitutional sufficiency.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual seizure and the facts known at that moment. Later struggle or gun discovery cannot justify an already completed unsupported seizure. Conduct occurring before any seizure may contribute to its justification. Review of the legal effect of assumed facts does not authorize appellate invention of historical findings.

**Other Governing Law:** California v. Hodari D. supplies the inherited distinction between a show of authority and a seizure through physical force or actual submission. An unheeded command alone does not establish submission.

**Material Other Authority:** The lower court offered an alternative no-submission rationale even assuming Valentine's stop-and-name account, while also stating that the record did not support that account. Both aspects must remain separate. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Force and actual submission are recognized seizure mechanisms. Whether an actual stop and answer amount to submission cannot be decided simply by labeling the interval short; the later act's role depends on whether a seizure had already occurred.

**Open Point:** The categorical significance, if any, of subsequent flight after brief compliance requires resolution. Whether Valentine actually complied is a distinct record question for determination under the proper review allocation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Third Circuit emphasized a recent eyewitness account delivered in person, the opportunity to observe demeanor, proximity to the suspect, late hour, neighborhood shootings and departure. It treated these as sufficient before the command. Alternatively, it concluded that even a brief pause and name did not constitute realistic submission, allowing the later charge toward the officer to support suspicion. It did not rely on the gun's later discovery to justify the initial command. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Government's Strongest Position:** A person who approaches police within sight of a nearby suspect takes more personal risk than a remote caller. Immediate eyewitness knowledge, detailed description, prompt matching and contextual danger warrant a short investigation before violence occurs. Even if initial suspicion was insufficient, an unanswered command did not seize Valentine; his attempt to rush past and push the officer supplied new grounds before physical restraint. A real pause must be distinguished from feigned or equivocal compliance.

**Valentine's Strongest Position:** The witness deliberately retained anonymity, supplied no observed offense beyond possession, and was not shown to be accountable. Late presence and ordinary walking away do not identify criminal behavior. A person who actually yields to a command and answers is seized during that interval; later escape cannot retroactively erase that seizure. A minimum-duration condition would let officers use the results of an unsupported detention to justify its beginning.

**Limits on Both Accounts:** The record does not support inventing a reliable informant history, witnessed threat, unlawful transfer, identifiable permit violation, or a finding of continuous compliance. Conversely, subsequent flight is not legally irrelevant if no seizure preceded it. The informant's possible exposure to retaliation is a contextual inference, not proof of veracity.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Identify credited facts concerning compliance, force and chronology. Apply the correct review allocation rather than allowing appellate sufficiency review to make disputed historical findings.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may find the initial totality sufficient; find those stated grounds insufficient while considering the separately preserved permit argument; or sustain the encounter on conduct occurring before actual seizure. It may clarify that submission requires objectively manifested yielding without a fixed minimum duration. If initial suspicion independently suffices, the second question may be unnecessary to the judgment, but the lower court's alternative legal rule must be treated explicitly.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance permits further prosecution with the suppression reversal intact. Vacatur can require renewed analysis of the initial basis and seizure point. If an unsupported completed seizure is established, analyze the challenged evidence and any preserved independent grounds under ordinary suppression principles; do not automatically order acquittal or dismissal. The firearm's eventual recovery does not establish the antecedent justification.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The disputed stop-and-name account and the unadjudicated permitting argument counsel against a final finding that every lawful path to admission fails. No universal rule about unnamed in-person witnesses or departure from police is warranted.
