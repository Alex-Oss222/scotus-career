# Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-02-27. **Date status:** organizationally assigned.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield.

**Citation or Docket:** 203 F.3d 487; No. 99-1639.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits decision organizationally placed February 27, 2001. No historical Supreme Court merits docket is represented.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-27 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).

**Question Presented:** Whether a municipality cures an Establishment Clause violation by selling a religious statue and its underlying parcel to a private organization when the parcel remains dedicated to public use and visually integrated into the surrounding municipal park?

**Question Granted:** Review is limited to the question stated above.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit decision vacating summary judgment for the City and private Fund and remanding for a cure of continuing governmental endorsement.

**Relief Requested:** The City and Fund defend the transfer and seek freedom to maintain the display; challengers seek effective separation of the religious display from the City's park and authority.

**Material Record:** A fifteen-foot statue of Christ, with religious inscription, stood in a municipally maintained wayside park. In response to the challenge, the City sold the statue and approximately 0.15 acre to a private Fund. The conveyance was genuine rather than a sham sale, but a covenant retained public use. The parcel lacked clear physical boundaries within the park, and the disclaimer's location and visibility contributed to uncertainty over ownership. The park's historical presentation centered on the statue rather than a multiplicity of speakers. [Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/203/203.F3d.487.99-1639.html).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The continuing public arrangement, not private religious belief or the bare private ownership of land, supplies the state-action question. A real conveyance does not moot a claim directed at continuing municipal conduct. Conversely, the purchaser's speech and property interests limit the remedy.

**Relevant Dates:** Lower decision February 4, 2000, with later amendment; assigned event February 27, 2001.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No additional listed companion or participation restriction.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

### Issue 1: Municipal attribution after a genuine conveyance

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton replaces independent Lemon v. Kurtzman prongs with a unified inquiry. Specific prohibitions and distinct doctrines govern first; established permissible relationships retain their actual scope; residual questions turn on attribution and function under ordinary procedure. It expressly leaves monument programs undecided.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Purpose, effects, and institutional relationships supply evidence within the unified inquiry. Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette protects private expression in an actually available public forum with meaningful attribution safeguards. Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette preserves private access without either universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from public location.

**Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause restricts municipal sponsorship of religion; the Free Speech Clause and lawful private property interests constrain the cure. The conveyance, covenant, actual control, presentation, and expenditure determine what function the City continues to perform.

**Material Other Authority:** Robinson v. City of Edmond forbids continuing official identification with a particular faith while distinguishing private access and accurate history. The lower-court record supplies the sale and presentation facts, not a Supreme Court holding.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Private ownership alone does not answer whether the municipality continues presenting a religious message as its own. Proximity alone does not establish sponsorship when separation is real. The existing rules govern that attribution inquiry but have not settled the divested-monument application.

**Open Point:** Whether this parcel's retained public dedication and undifferentiated presentation preserve municipal sponsorship, and what operational and intelligible separation cures it without suppressing private expression.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The court accepted the sale's legitimacy but found continuing endorsement and preferential expressive access from the public-use covenant, undifferentiated landscape, and park history. It suggested boundaries and an effective disclaimer rather than mandatory removal.

**City and Fund:** Ownership and control passed to an independent religious organization; the Constitution protects that organization's expression. Continuing public pedestrian use does not convert all private property into official speech, and a prohibition based on religious content would be discriminatory.

**Challengers:** The City cannot continue the same official presentation through an invisible paper division. Its retained park function, historical sponsorship, and unclear disclaimer communicate ongoing municipal affiliation. A genuine sale resolves ownership but does not by itself end that governmental function. Neither side can substitute an abstract reasonable observer for the actual allocation of control and public presentation.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Decision paths:** Affirm the need for corrective relief if continuing municipal sponsorship is established under the attribution-and-function inquiry under Agostini v. Felton; reverse if the City has actually relinquished the challenged function and only private expression remains; or vacate for application of that inquiry if the lower court's independent endorsement analysis treated perception or location as dispositive.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A transfer may cure establishment when it creates operational and intelligible separation. A court can require the City to cease its own sponsorship, accurately mark its boundary, and provide clear attribution on land it controls. It cannot compel destruction or relocation of private religious speech merely because a passerby could misunderstand it. The remedy must account for the covenant and actual layout, without prescribing a particular fence where an equally effective, lawful separation works.

The Maryland court’s failure to determine California preclusion law prevents an automatic final ruling for either party on that issue.

### Issue 1: Source of law for a federal diversity judgment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC and Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation reject general federal common-law displacement of state-created duties absent enacted authority or a specific significant federal conflict. They preserve exceptional federal fields and do not decide the law governing a federal court’s own judgment. Baker v. General Motors Corp. separately requires lawful rendering-state effect under §1738 for state judgments and distinguishes recognition from additional coercive enforcement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual federal interest before selecting a uniform federal rule. State substantive claims do not acquire general federal substantive law merely through a federal forum; the effect of a federal judicial act nonetheless requires an appropriate federal source.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 41(b); the Rules Enabling Act, 28 U.S.C. §2072; the Rules of Decision Act; Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins and Hanna v. Plumer. Section 1738 is a comparison, not a direct command governing this federal judgment.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing simulation decisions reject unsupported uniformity rationales and preserve judgment-specific recognition principles. They leave this precise federal-diversity preclusion rule unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether federal common law should ordinarily incorporate the preclusion law of the State where the rendering diversity court sits, subject to an actual incompatible federal interest.

### Issue 2: Rule 41(b) and the dismissal’s external effect

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The function and authorized scope of the federal rule determine its effect. No controlling decision gives every Rule 41(b) dismissal automatic nationwide claim-preclusive effect.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A rule controlling dismissal in the rendering action must be distinguished from a rule conclusively determining the consequences in every later forum. A label does not bypass statutory or constitutional limits on the rule’s reach.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 41(b)’s default merits language and specified exceptions, read with Rule 41(a), §2072, and the actual judgment.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A dismissal with prejudice can bar renewed litigation in the rendering court without necessarily resolving every other forum’s claim-preclusion question.
* **Open Point:** Whether the “on the merits” label supplies the entire external preclusion rule, and what further state-law inquiry is required if it does not.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Lockheed argues that the final federal merits dismissal would lose force if the same claims could proceed elsewhere and that Rule 41(b) establishes its preclusive character. Semtek argues that a limitations dismissal traditionally need not extinguish the substantive claim in every forum, that removal should not enlarge its effect over an otherwise comparable California judgment, and that Rule 41(b) governs the rendering court’s procedure. The court below accepted automatic federal preclusion rather than deciding California’s actual rule.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance on a uniform rule would give the diversity limitations dismissal nationwide effect independent of California law and requires a sufficient federal source for that consequence. Reversal can establish a federal rule ordinarily incorporating California preclusion law and remand for its application, while preserving a specific federal-interest exception. The latter route neither vacates the California judgment nor guarantees that the Maryland suit survives: the correct borrowed rule and any actual federal conflict still must be applied.

On implementation, a threshold dismissal is available only if finality or ripeness is genuinely absent. If review is proper, the Court may reject an interpretation that eliminates applicable specific constraints and remand for a lawful agency construction without choosing the replacement plan. Each docket’s judgment should track these separable components rather than announce blanket approval of the standards and every implementation choice.

The lower courts rejected the State’s claimed authority. The lack of outright exclusion narrows the analogy to U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. ## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can affirm because the labels exceed the State’s Elections Clause authority, without resolving a general right to issue nonbinding instructions or an independent speech claim. It could alternatively find unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination if properly reached. Reversal would require treating the labels as authorized neutral election administration despite their policy-compliance trigger. Relief should prohibit the challenged official penalties, not suppress private term-limits advocacy or automatically invalidate unrelated neutral ballot information.

### Issue 1: Program definition and private advocacy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia forbids viewpoint exclusion within the actual independently edited private-publication benefit while preserving reasonable program boundaries. Board of Regents of the University of Wisconsin System v. Southworth requires viewpoint-neutral administration of its defined private-expression program and expressly distinguishes governmental messages and curricular choices.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whose speech the program supports and how the condition operates within the authorized activity. Not every subsidy creates a forum, and not every restriction can be defended merely by calling it program definition. Independent private advocacy retains its constitutional character.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment; the Legal Services Corporation Act and the challenged appropriations condition. Rust v. Sullivan and Regan v. Taxation With Representation supply received funding-program comparisons subject to their actual scope and later simulated treatment.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The private-publication and student-expression holdings do not themselves decide legal representation, but require an accurate account of attribution, independent judgment, and the funding condition’s effect.
* **Open Point:** Whether Congress is defining a funded service or selectively disabling a client’s legal arguments within the very representation it has funded.

### Issue 2: Judicial advocacy and the remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The governing speech decisions distinguish correcting an unlawful exclusion from ordering unrestricted funding or restructuring the whole program. They do not establish a general constitutional right to publicly funded civil representation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Relief must address the unconstitutional condition actually presented and preserve unchallenged program limits. A legal argument’s disfavored conclusion cannot be justified solely by government ownership of the funds where the authorized role is independent representation.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment and the judiciary’s adjudicative function; the actual appropriations text and the uncontested severability determination.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Court can remove a viewpoint-based limitation without deciding all possible congressional choices about which categories of civil representation to fund.
* **Open Point:** Whether the challenged restriction distorts the ordinary representational function sufficiently to require relief, and whether the existing limited injunction supplies the appropriate consequence.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Government and LSC argue that Congress may choose to subsidize routine benefits representation without financing challenges to the welfare system, relying on the distinction between denying a subsidy and banning private speech. Challengers argue that the funded lawyer represents an individual client rather than the government, and that allowing arguments for benefits only while barring necessary challenges to governing law selects one side of the legal controversy. They emphasize forced withdrawal and the practical scarcity of replacement counsel. The court below accepted the viewpoint objection and severed the restriction. A general claim that all funding conditions are permissible or all program limits are unconstitutional would exceed either supported position.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can hold that government may choose the service to fund but cannot selectively disable the client’s otherwise available legal arguments within this independent representation program. Reversal can follow only if the challenged restriction is a lawful program boundary under the applicable speech distinction. A narrow judgment need not establish a freestanding separation-of-powers violation or a right to unlimited public legal services. With severability uncontested, relief can leave the remaining program in place without a new appropriation or an injunction against unrelated restrictions.
