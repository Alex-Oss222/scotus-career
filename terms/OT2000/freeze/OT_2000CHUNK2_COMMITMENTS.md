## Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District — 212 F.3d 738

**Provisional judgment and component positions:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the vacatur of the mandatory preliminary-admission order and remand under Adarand and Eisenberg, with the qualifications below. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would join the affirmative proposition that supported educational benefits of meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation can justify a voluntary program without a prior de jure finding. Kennedy accepts that existing holding as governing law but does not add his own affirmative join to it. Scalia and Thomas would instead vacate the appellate judgment and remand with the merits prerequisite for preliminary protection against this racial exclusion treated as established on the existing record; they would require ordinary current equitable and remedial determinations rather than automatically direct present admission.

**Common-standard remand supported by six Associates:** Race restricted Haak's actual opportunity: written acceptance was withdrawn after verification that she was white. This is not Sinkfield's claim of injury based only on neighboring electoral design. Government must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and this racial restriction, including coverage, operation, actual individual burdens and a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Brown's prohibition on state-imposed racial caste remains, while Adarand displaces categorical strict-scrutiny formulations. Eisenberg expressly permits the supported K–12 educational objective but gives no presumption to demographic balance or a program's integrative label.

The preliminary record neither establishes the complete justification nor conclusively disproves the program's claimed educational benefits. A directional interdistrict program may operate differently from a restriction based only on county percentages. Its limited scale, changing demographics and long operation are relevant evidence, not categorical proof of futility. Conversely, the existence of a program and the possibility of a legitimate objective cannot justify this child's exclusion without examining actual educational conditions, operation and alternatives. The state funding text calls for a qualifying program designed to reduce racial isolation and annual demonstration; the court of appeals' account of inevitable funding loss is not itself an express statutory ban on every outward transfer by a white city pupil. That distinction matters to the claimed necessity of the categorical racial line.

The correct disposition preserves the appellate result setting aside mandatory admission while replacing its displaced constitutional framework. It does not finally sustain the program. A changed legal standard creates no entitlement to reopen evidence: existing supported findings and concessions retain their scope, and further proceedings require an identified unresolved material issue under ordinary rules. The mandatory-injunction standard and other equitable requirements remain independent; the preliminary merits assessment is not a final validity judgment or an automatic seat entitlement. Current enrollment, grade, seat availability and continuing injury must be established where material to prospective relief. The conditional protection for completion of 1999–2000 is not an operative January 2001 placement order. The unappealed rejection of contract and estoppel grounds and other claims outside this equal-protection grant remain outside the decision.

## Gitlitz v. Commissioner — No. 99-1295

**Provisional judgment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg would reverse and remand for application of the shareholder pass-through and basis increase before the required corporate attribute reduction. **Seven-Associate statutory position:** Debt-discharge income excluded from gross income under section 108(a)'s insolvency rule remains an item of income for section 1366(a)(1)(A). That provision includes tax-exempt income and is not limited to items currently taxable in the shareholder's hands. Section 1367(a)(1)(A) increases stock basis for those income items, and section 1366(d)(1) computes the loss limit with that increase. Section 108(b)(4)(A) then places attribute reduction after determination of the discharge-year tax. The sequence therefore permits the recognized basis increase and otherwise allowable loss deductions before reduction of the corporate attributes that remain.

Section 108(d)(7)(A) identifies the level at which exclusion and reduction occur; it does not say the excluded income ceases to be an item under section 1366 or reverse the enacted timing rule. Section 108(d)(7)(B) treats losses actually disallowed under section 1366(d)(1) for this year as a corporate net operating loss for attribute reduction. Determining which losses remain disallowed requires the basis calculation that section 1366(d)(1) itself directs. The provision still operates where losses exceed properly adjusted basis; it need not erase a basis increase to retain work. Section 1367(b)(1)'s return-inclusion rule applies to amounts required to be included in gross income, and therefore does not silently prohibit an increase for an excluded item that section 1366 recognizes. Section 108's insolvency ceiling and ordinary limits continue to apply; the rule does not create unlimited basis or free-standing deduction entitlement.

The strongest objection is that exclusion combined with released losses gives a shareholder a benefit without a new cash outlay and frustrates the corporate attribute bargain. The seven conclude that Congress's operative classifications and timing control despite that effect. Hill recognizes distinct tax accounts and supplies no general ban on multiple benefits; Bufferd confirms that an S corporation's corporate and shareholder tax consequences retain their different statutory functions. Atlantic Mutual permits administrative construction within genuine interpretive room, not contradiction of a clear direction or a general revenue-protection override. No applicable authorized regulation supplied here independently changes the enacted sequence.

## 2. Common alternatives tested

### Statutory exclusion and inclusion

**Exclusion proposition.** Sections 404(a) and 502(7), read within the water-protection scheme, do not authorize jurisdiction over these isolated intrastate ponds solely from their use by migratory birds. The statutory definition expands beyond traditional navigability, but it does not make every connection between a pond and an interstate interest a delegation of water jurisdiction. Section 404(g)(1) identifies waters available for state administration outside the federally retained navigation-related category; it does not conclusively identify every member of that remainder. Section 101(a)'s ecological objective operates consistently with the statute's provisions, while section 101(b) preserves an important state role. The narrow exclusion does not decide every isolated water, require present navigability for every covered water, or undo Riverside Bayview's adjacent-wetland rule.

The strongest opposing construction is that the broad defined term, the biological-integrity objective, and the nonnavigation remainder in section 404(g)(1) make habitat-bearing ponds an ordinary part of the delegated water program. Riverside Bayview accepts ecological rather than solely navigational considerations, and a bird's movement can connect aquatic ecological functions without hydrological adjacency. That argument cannot be answered merely by labeling the land use local or the policy consequential.

**Inclusion proposition.** The enacted definition and scheme permit the Corps to include these actual waters and their demonstrated aquatic habitat function. Section 101(a) informs the meaning of the coverage language rather than supplying power by itself; section 404(g)(1) confirms that federal coverage exceeds waters reserved to direct federal administration. The state role in section 101(b) coexists with federal permit coverage. Riverside Bayview's reservation leaves the question open rather than deciding it adversely. The asserted ecological relationship is consequently a permissible application of section 328.3(a)(3), subject to constitutional limits.

The strongest objection is that broad statutory purpose and a reasonable policy cannot replace a missing delegation, and the 1986 explanation cannot itself enlarge one. Inclusion therefore must stand on text and structure, not on treating the preamble as independently binding. The inclusion positions below would sustain the statutory reading independently of a universal claim to controlling weight for this preamble. Chevron supports permissible delegated interpretation; Christensen does not turn every interpretation outside notice-and-comment rulemaking into an invalid one, and no freestanding procedure judgment is entered.

### Commerce mechanisms and the exact inferential dispute

**Regulated class.** The enacted subject is discharge of dredged or fill material into covered waters. The challenged application reaches isolated intrastate ponds through the actual regulation's commerce language and the bird-habitat criterion. It is neither the municipal consortium's entire business nor all conduct affecting wildlife. A court must evaluate the relationship of this habitat-based water category to the asserted mechanism; it may not insert a new commercial-project element. A present application involving established habitat is distinguishable from a hypothetical assertion based only on a passing bird or unsupported future use, without rewriting the rule.

**Necessary-regime route.** The federal regulation of navigable and interstate waters supplies an identifiable valid interstate core. ## Lopez v. Davis — 2001-01-10

### Scope, gates and common premises

The lawful channel is the federal prisoner's section 2241 proceeding. The reviewed Eighth Circuit judgment sustained the 1997 discretionary category and reversed an order requiring eligibility reconsideration. No requested relief warrants immediate release. ### Material alternatives

**L1 — sustain this categorical exercise.** Congress separated threshold eligibility from the Bureau's selection power. ## Denius v. Dunlap — 2001-01-17

### Correct posture and common gates

The Seventh Circuit affirmed damages immunity on the attorney-client and financial-information claims; it reversed medical-information damages immunity and dismissal of the equitable claims. The base module's assertion that attorney-client immunity was reversed is not used. The review concerns the two granted constitutional questions and their actual component remedies, not a revived release-of-causes-of-action theory. The latter was waived below. No Sixth Amendment claim succeeds without attached criminal proceedings; Denius had none.

The challenged authorization lasts one year. Its broad opening and enumerated categories must be distinguished from the unchallenged 1994 criminal/education authorization, which had no time limit. Medical coverage is a disputed predicate reserved by note 8; the form does not separately enumerate medical records. Refusal to sign was the sole stated reason for nonrenewal on the summary-judgment record. Dunlap's uncommunicated intention to conduct only the already-authorized criminal check does not change the words he required Denius to sign. No actual collection or dissemination is found. The present claim concerns the employment consequence of refusing the demanded authorization.

All eight accept that injury from losing employment is concrete and that qualified immunity is a personal damages defense, not a reason by itself to dismiss prospective equitable claims. An appeal from the legal dismissal did not require a Rule 60(b) motion. Successor-official substitution preserves the proper prospective defendant; it does not establish a continuing threat or entitlement to reinstatement. Every remand below preserves ordinary summary-judgment burdens, personal causation, actual injury, appropriate defendants and the prerequisites for the relief requested. No automatic trial, discovery plan, reinstatement or damages award follows.

### Issues and competing paths

**D1 — confidential legal consultation.** The six Justices specified below would hold that the employer cannot condition continued employment on a blanket authorization of confidential communications used to obtain independent legal advice, without an adequate employment-related justification for that burden. Button and Trainmen protect access to legal assistance; Shelton's employment-disclosure principle and Pickering prevent the government from using the job to burden protected independent activity without justification. The constitutional wrong is the unjustified employment condition burdening consultation, not the conversion of every evidentiary privilege into an absolute constitutional right. The broad attorney clause reaches protected consultation and future ability to consult. No articulated reason connects that additional demand to Denius's ordinary teaching. A claim for equitable relief therefore cannot be dismissed on the employer's existing record.

The competing source-based objection is that collective litigation and expressive association cases do not create a general constitutional privilege for every individual civil legal communication, especially without an identified matter. That objection supports Scalia and Thomas's refusal of this extension. The six-Justice response is that confidential access enables a person to identify and assert legal rights, and this general demand reaches that activity without a limiting employment justification. It does not depend on a Sixth Amendment right, a finding of actual intercepted communication or a right to hide independently discoverable facts. Lawful investigation, genuinely pertinent tailored inquiries and ordinary privilege rules retain their separate operation; a privilege exception does not automatically authorize an unrelated employment demand.

**D2 — medical information.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would recognize a bounded liberty interest against an employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections appropriate to the use. Sheets supplies protection of objectively intimate personal information rather than a generalized secrecy preference. Whalen permits legitimate safeguarded collection; Doe did not reject acquisition protection and resolved a confined benefits-related inquiry on an assumed-right basis. Here neither an adequate purpose for the additional access nor safeguards limiting its use have been established. A request for prospective protection can therefore proceed if the authorization actually covers such records. The demand's one-year duration does not itself make its unlimited subject-matter reach proper.

Scalia and Thomas reject extending substantive due process from Sheets's binding public-dissemination holding to acquisition or authorization. Their Doe concurrence and Sheets dissent identify the missing constitutional source; intimate subject matter, bad administration and a state confidentiality expectation do not supply it. The six do not adopt a universal least-intrusive-means test, a seven-factor checklist or a right to veto relevant medical screening. The rule requires evaluation of actual coverage, intimate content, the employment connection and protection against unrelated use or disclosure; it does not equate every administrative imperfection with liability.

**D3 — financial information.** The six support returning the equitable financial claim under the same objectively intimate-personal-information boundary. An ordinary loan, credit rating or financial record is not constitutionally protected merely because it is called confidential. A financial record may fall within the protected interest to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. Denius has identified no particular protected financial item, so the lower court's categorical constitutional conclusion from the financial label and breadth alone cannot stand. Vacate that categorical merits conclusion while preserving the return of the equitable claim for identification of the protected content and application of the stated rule. The employment injury makes the dispute concrete; it does not itself prove substantive coverage.

The principal broader alternative would constitutionalize sensitive financial affairs generally. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg regard some broader protection as plausible under their Doe dissent, but accept the intimate-content rule as the supported decision in this record; no broader financial entitlement becomes controlling by implication. O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer require that boundary. Scalia and Thomas would reject the financial due-process theory outright, for the same source reason as D2. None would reopen undeveloped claims involving the other enumerated categories or the waived liability release.

**D4 — damages immunity.** Every Justice would affirm attorney-client and financial damages immunity. The relevant law did not give sufficiently particularized 1996 notice of these asserted extensions. Button's legal-access rules and the existing financial-privacy cases are relevant; the absence of an identical case is not dispositive. But neither the consultative confidentiality extension nor the protected financial reach was then clear enough on this authorization. Sheets's April 1996 intimate-publication holding does not automatically decide either acquisition or all financial records. The 1997 Doe decision, the 1997 Crawford-El decision and this 2001 decision supply no retroactive notice.

Medical immunity has a distinct basis. The six would affirm reversal of summary judgment on that component, conditional on actual medical coverage. Pesce recognized the potential constitutional problem with employment-conditioned confidential disclosure while upholding justified child-abuse reporting. Schaill recognized substantial medical confidentiality and upheld limited disclosure to one appropriate official with a concrete program need. Anderson v. Romero expressly distinguished the already-recognized civilian medical interest from the unsettled prisoner claim for which it granted immunity. These preconduct circuit sources, considered with Whalen and the now-effective Sheets rule, give notice that an unlimited medical demand without any articulated countervailing purpose cannot be justified simply by invoking public employment. The uncertain exact balancing formula does not erase that limiting proposition. This is an inference about the cumulative conduct-date law, not a claim that the three circuit cases held this exact authorization invalid.

The strongest immunity counterargument is that those cases sustained disclosures, involved different settings and did not adjudicate an unexecuted release covering disputed medical records. Scalia and Thomas would grant medical damages immunity independently of rejecting the right: the extension to this condition was not sufficiently clear. The six distinguish the unresolved factual coverage from the legal absence of any reason for the additional medical access. Their vote preserves further consideration of actual scope and supported defenses rather than entering personal liability. A valid objective employment need cannot be fabricated from the official's undisclosed intention; a genuinely supported material justification remains subject to ordinary proceedings.

## Seling v. Young — 2001-01-17

### Thresholds and actual entering law

Young's 1994 federal application uses the former chapter 153 framework under Lindh. Amended section 2254(d) is not the merits rule; independently directed provisions and ordinary habeas requirements retain their own operation. No new automatic federal evidentiary hearing follows. The due-process, equal-protection and commitment-procedure rulings Young did not petition to review remain undisturbed.

The actual Hendricks decision permits proof that a nominally civil commitment operates as punishment, requires the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect, and invalidated the retrospective application there. It was not a facial approval of every civil-labeled scheme. Its majority and its published dissent both reject treating a label as conclusive in the face of proved punitive operation; they disagreed about the original Kansas record. None of the eight may import a different prior Hendricks judgment as law.

All eight would preserve an opportunity to adjudicate Young's supported punishment claim under the actual standard. The formal disposition is to vacate the reviewed punishment-remand portion and remand with the corrected governing instruction, leaving the unpetitioned rulings intact. This changes the governing instruction and preserves the evidentiary issue; it awards no writ, release or finding of punishment.

### Alternatives, operative rule and remedy

**S1 — a bounded punitive-operation inquiry.** First identify legislative civil or criminal intent. A civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect, assessed from supported operation as a whole. Confinement, prior-offense connection, indeterminacy, deficient treatment or criminal-style safeguards alone are not conclusive. Actual implementation can be evidence; a nominal civil classification cannot categorically exclude it. The inquiry must separate an inadequately administered but genuinely civil program from confinement that actually imposes punishment. It neither requires successful cure nor converts every violation of a care obligation into retrospective punishment.

Young's security, confinement, abuse, staffing, therapy and practical-release allegations are not findings. The separate Turay injunction and reported later progress are relevant evidence within their actual scope, not conclusive proof of punishment, compliance or a cure of an earlier unlawful application. Washington's examination and release mechanisms exist but do not prove compliant operation for Young. This decision does not apply a particular unquoted conditional-release clause, rewrite those requirements or presume that release was unavailable. The lower court must evaluate the supported record and any material genuine factual disputes under the former habeas framework.

For Ex Post Facto relief, Young must establish both punishment and its prohibited retrospective application to pre-enactment conduct. If both are established, custody under that unlawful application may not continue; the court must give custody-directed relief under ordinary habeas law, not merely relabel the violation a conditions dispute. If only unlawful conditions are established and lawful civil custody remains, conditions relief belongs in its proper channel and does not itself require release. No new civil-commitment authority or replacement confinement is supplied.

Double Jeopardy is distinct. Punishment is a necessary predicate but does not by itself establish prior attachment, the relevant prior jeopardy or offense identity. Hendricks decided none of those. No independent double-jeopardy entitlement is fixed on this record. If the Ex Post Facto ground resolves the lawfulness of custody, there is no need for an additional ground. If a live double-jeopardy claim remains necessary after the punishment and temporal findings, its own preserved predicates and governing offense-identity law must be addressed, without treating a statutory label or the same prior misconduct as sufficient by itself. This is factual and legal work remaining on the remand, not an adjudicated double-jeopardy violation.

**S2 — categorical finality of the civil label.** The superintendent's strongest concern is that day-to-day conditions should not make a statute fluctuate between civil and criminal, and that conditions remedies should correct treatment deficiencies. That concern supports demanding proof and remedial separation. It cannot support a rule excluding actual punitive operation under the controlling Hendricks holding, or treating statutory promises as proof that the implementation is civil. A conditions-only rejection would be lawful if Young's supported allegations could establish no unlawful custody even if proved. Given the cumulative allegations and the present absence of dispositive findings, that rejection cannot be entered solely from the civil label. No majority or minority commitment here treats the allegations as already sufficient proof.

## Kansas v. United States — 2001-01-22

### Posture and operative disposition

All eight would affirm dismissal of the pleaded Spending Clause/Tenth Amendment challenge. The disposition concerns the identified conditional child-support and TANF arrangement and the actual allegations; it neither establishes that coercion is nonjusticiable nor holds that every condition attached to federal funds is voluntary merely because Congress calls it a condition.

The supplied historical FY1996 amounts are approximately $29.3 million in child-support funds and $101.9 million in welfare funds, with federal support covering 66 percent of program operations and 80 percent of computer expenditures. Those are not findings of present forfeiture, the percentage of Kansas's total resources, fiscal inability to withdraw or an adjudicated denial of particular funds. On review of dismissal, well-pleaded facts are accepted; the conclusion that those figures make refusal impossible is not itself an additional factual allegation that must be accepted as law.

Dole, New York's monetary-incentive holding and Steward Machine distinguish a substantial inducement from compulsion. Congress may define the supported program and its clear, related conditions without guaranteeing continuation on former terms. Child-support collection bears directly on the funded assistance system and resources available for supported families. Participation is conditional; no duty to administer these requirements after declining the program is established. The burdens alleged therefore do not show that Kansas lacks a genuine program choice.

The strongest contrary path is a remand if the complaint contains a concrete material allegation of actual compulsion that the lower court treated as legally irrelevant. A theoretical option to decline cannot answer every possible coercion claim, and the linkage to TANF requires its own program relationship. But the supplied record identifies fiscal dependence, administrative burden and consequences for beneficiaries rather than an unavoidable legal duty or a concretely established inability to exit. Neither Dole's five-percent setting nor the larger sums here creates a fixed numerical rule. This record does not justify discovery simply to search for an unpleaded coercion theory. The affirmance does not prevent adjudication of a different properly presented sanctions application or adequately supported coercion claim.

### Statutory qualifications that remain operative

The case does not rest on an assumption that every implementation defect automatically forfeits both entire funding streams. Section 602(a)(2) requires the chief executive's certification that the State will operate a child-support program under an approved part-D plan during the fiscal year. Complete refusal to operate that program differs from deficiencies within a continuing program.

Section 609(a)(8) concerns findings of failure to achieve paternity-establishment percentages or other performance measures, incomplete or unreliable submitted data, or failure to comply substantially with part-D requirements, for fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 1997. The performance finding rests on the statutory submitted data or review; the data and compliance findings rest on the identified statutory audits. In the succeeding corrective fiscal year the State must have failed to take sufficient corrective action to reach the required performance or compliance, or again submitted incomplete or unreliable data. The substantial-compliance branch excludes section 654(24), (27)(A) and (27)(B)(i). The reductions are 1–2 percent, 2–3 percent on a second consecutive finding, and 3–5 percent on a third or later consecutive finding. They apply to amounts otherwise payable for quarters after the end of that succeeding year, before the quarters following the end of the first full quarter in which the State achieves the required paternity-establishment percentages or other performance measures, or substantial compliance, as applicable. Technical noncompliance is treated as substantial compliance only when the Secretary determines that its technical nature does not adversely affect program performance; incomplete or unreliable data are treated as adequate only on the parallel determination that the technical defect does not adversely affect determination of paternity-establishment percentages or other performance measures. No present finding satisfying those triggers is made here.

Section 666(d) allows, but does not compel, an exemption from a law or procedure required by or pursuant to that section when the State satisfies the Secretary, using caseload, processing-time, administrative-cost, average-collection and other requested data or estimates, that it will not improve program effectiveness and efficiency. The exemption remains subject to continuing review and termination when circumstances change. It is not a general entitlement to avoid all program requirements. The decision does not depend on predicting that Kansas will obtain an exemption.

Section 654(26)'s safeguards remain binding: protection against unauthorized use or disclosure; nondisclosure of whereabouts to a party subject to a protective order; nondisclosure to a person when there is reason to believe physical or emotional harm may result; notice to the Secretary where the protective-order or risk conditions apply and there is reasonable evidence of domestic violence or child abuse and harmful disclosure; and court screening of the flagged information before any further disclosure, with no further disclosure if the court finds it could be harmful. The cursory privacy and procedural-due-process objections establish no facial independent constitutional bar and supply no decision validating every collection or disclosure. The passport function is not transferred to Kansas by imprecise lower-opinion shorthand. No particular registry or enforcement mechanism is separately adjudicated.

## Illinois v. McArthur

### Actual predicates, alternatives and entering law

Probable cause was treated as uncontested. The wife reported observed concealment under the couch; McArthur knew officers sought to investigate the drugs and declined a search. Refusal alone is not evidence of guilt or an independent exigency. The concrete risk concerns readily disposable evidence within a residence to which this informed occupant would otherwise have immediate unobserved access. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another limited unaccompanied entry for approximately two hours. Two or three limited entries for cigarettes and telephone use occurred with observation just inside the doorway. Consent to that observation supplies no general search consent or surrender of the home-access objection.

Both charged offenses were jailable misdemeanors: less than 2.5 grams of marijuana carried up to thirty days and paraphernalia possession up to one year. Their modest gravity remains relevant. The actual prewarrant restriction must be assessed independently of the evidence later found. No evidentiary search occurred until the warrant; probable cause alone does not authorize an unlimited home entry, occupation or search.

**Path A: reverse suppression on the temporary-restraint ground.** Particularized probable cause, a reasonable evidence-destruction concern, actual diligence and a carefully limited restriction together make this preservation measure reasonable. It holds the situation for a magistrate while avoiding a general search. The strongest objection is that exclusion from one's home is substantial and that generic ease of destroying drugs could become an automatic warrant exception. The answer depends on the actual knowledge, access, timing and limited execution here; generic drug suspicion or the occupant's refusal does not suffice.

**Path B: affirm suppression.** The threatened loss of evidence of these small possession offenses does not sufficiently justify depriving the occupant of private use of his home on an inferred risk without a demonstrated attempt to destroy it. The strongest objection is that police need not await the very destruction they reasonably seek to prevent, especially while choosing a less intrusive measure and diligently seeking a warrant. Stevens gives greater weight to the modest offense and home restraint; the other seven accept the bounded preservation judgment on this record.

Edmond's actual rule rejects suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints absent an independently lawful justification. This case has individualized probable cause and presents residential scope and exigency, not a program-purpose threshold. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not itself authorize residential restraint. Bond's actual holding separates lawful presence from an additional intrusion; it neither eliminates supported preservation measures nor supplies consent. Welsh preserves the home's special protection and offense gravity; its civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest facts differ. Segura's independent-source holding does not automatically validate every earlier seizure, and its broad securing-premises discussion in Part IV had only two votes. No majority holding is attributed to that Part.

## Record and legal premises used

The operative statute is the amended law considered by the en banc court. It regulates an agency employee's use of agency-owned or agency-leased computer equipment to access, download, print, or store information-infrastructure files or services with covered content. The content must have as its dominant theme a lascivious description or depiction of the enumerated sexual subjects: sexual bestiality, lewd exhibition of nudity, sexual excitement, sexual conduct, sadomasochistic abuse, coprophilia, urophilia, or fetishism. The incorporated definitions remain those reproduced in the official lower opinion, including the forms of nudity, actual or explicitly simulated sexual conduct, genital arousal, and the specified torture, flagellation, or restraint. The amendment does not turn all covered material into unprotected obscenity.

An agency includes a public institution of higher education but excludes the Department of State Police. Information infrastructure includes telecommunications, cable, and computer networks, including the Internet, the World Wide Web, Usenet, bulletin boards, online systems, and telephone networks. The operative exception permits access only to the extent required in conjunction with a bona fide, agency-approved research project **or other agency-approved undertaking**. The second branch is not restricted to research. Agency heads must give approvals in writing, and approvals must be available under the state public-records law. The statute does not promise approval of everything merely related to work. Nor does it mandate an outside nonacademic decisionmaker or universal academic peer review. Reported delegation to deans or department heads is administrative practice.

The six professors challenge work-related activity and the approval condition itself. They disclaim a personal-use entitlement. None requested and was denied approval; the record identifies no denied request, and some other professors received waivers. That is different from proving that no current activity is burdened or that every future request must succeed. No damages claim, disciplinary dismissal, or adjudication of a refused project is presented.

The appellate opinion identifies the specific effects as allegations. The district opinion additionally identifies summary-judgment sources: a statement of facts concerning removed assignments, proposed student research, and work topics; a declaration concerning the state research database; and exhibits or depositions concerning Smith's images, blocking and restoration, and approval practices. These sources prevent treating the case as supported only by a subjective, unspecified fear. Their labels do not prove every assertion undisputed. The district opinion addressed the earlier definition; its examples and invalidity conclusion cannot simply be transferred to the narrowed statute.

The record also contains the Commonwealth's five reported misuse incidents, including complaints, and Smith's explanation of his research purpose. The reported blocking and partial restoration of Smith's images is not a refused statutory application. Some approvals were prompt with little paperwork, and some departments received blanket approvals. These facts support a real preventive interest and a potentially modest burden. They do not establish that every faculty activity receives an exemption, that the enacted word required means merely work-related, or that making approvals public has no effect. The reported incidents are not adjudications that every displayed image constituted actionable harassment.

* **Pickering, Connick, Givhan, and Waters:** Public concern and the employer's operational interests require context. Work location or a private communication is not dispositive. * **Keyishian, 385 U.S. 589, 597–610:** The Court invalidated vague restrictions on utterances and teaching and overbroad membership disqualifications. ## Five-Justice common rationale and remand scope

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer support the following at the same level of generality:

1. A university professor may possess a personal First Amendment interest when engaged in independent scholarly inquiry, teaching, or communication on matters of public concern. The interest depends on the activity's function and context, not the academic title. Salary, duty connection, and public equipment do not alone make that expression the institution's prescribed message. This does not establish a personal right to dictate institutional curricula, funding priorities, assignments, or the allocation of equipment. Genuine institutional decisions and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions remain.
2. Once such an interest is identified, the State must justify the actual burden of this prospective content-based condition under Pickering and NTEU. The inquiry includes the effect on the professor and audience, the narrowed content definition, the required-use limit, both approval branches, written and public approval, and actual administration. The State's efficiency, resource-management, and prevention-of-harassment interests are legitimate. Their weight and the restraint's fit must address the protected work actually affected. Ownership alone is not that justification.
3. The court of appeals cannot reject the claim solely because the inquiry is work-related or because academic freedom supposedly belongs only to the institution. It must apply the amended statute, distinguish independent scholarly activity from institutional output, and assess the condition itself. The record contains supported claims and employer evidence on both sides; it is not an evidentiary vacuum. The earlier district analysis of a broader statute and all state employees does not supply the final answer for this narrower faculty claim.
4. A remand is required because the categorical appellate rule bypassed the activity-specific protection and burden inquiry, while the district judgment addressed a different definition and substantially broader relief. No finding of improper motive, unlawful refusal, or entitlement to unrestricted access is made. No presumption that all approval systems fail or that these plaintiffs must win replaces the required inquiry.

The State may rely on appropriate operational evidence and supported predictive judgments. No completed harassment violation, expert study, special hearing, newly commissioned research, or universal proof protocol is imposed. Conversely, successful approvals do not automatically answer the chilling effect of requiring public advance permission for protected inquiry. A scholarly label does not establish coverage, protection, harm, or victory. Access to unprotected obscenity, personal use, and unrelated misuse are not rights established here.

On remand, ordinary procedural rules govern the existing submissions and any properly authorized further proceedings. Determine which plaintiffs' continuing activities are covered by the amended statute and implicate a personal interest, then decide whether the actual condition is justified at that scope. Any declaration or injunction must redress an established violation and reach no further than the represented rights and effective relief require. The Court does not direct the approval of a particular project, invalidate the public-records requirement in isolation, substitute itself for a dean, or require a particular future enforcement decision.
