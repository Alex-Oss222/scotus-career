# Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1178; 531 U.S. 159
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-09 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether §404 of the Clean Water Act authorizes the Corps to regulate the isolated, nonnavigable, intrastate ponds at this site solely because they provide habitat for migratory birds, and, if authorized, whether that application lies within Congress's commerce power.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, 191 F.3d 845: sustained the Corps' jurisdiction and its Migratory Bird Rule as a permissible statutory interpretation supported by the Commerce Clause.
* **Relief Requested:** The municipal consortium seeks reversal of the federal jurisdiction ruling and relief from the permit denial. Federal respondents seek affirmance of statutory coverage and constitutional authority.
* **Material Record:** The proposed municipal landfill occupies an abandoned sand and gravel pit containing permanent and seasonal ponds. The Corps initially disclaimed jurisdiction, then relied on use by migratory birds to assert §404 authority and deny a fill permit. The ponds are not navigable and are not alleged to adjoin navigable waters. The record must distinguish the economic landfill proposal from the actual class of waters and discharges the jurisdictional theory reaches. Bird use is established; the precise substantial cross-border mechanism cannot be assumed from the word migratory alone.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Statutory coverage precedes constitutional power. Agency authority and Congress's constitutional capacity are separate. The Court may resolve an adequate statutory ground without deciding the full commerce question. No site-specific constitutional finding can be invented to cure a gap in the government's theory.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date fixes the decisional cutoff. The lower judgment and all facts relied on must predate that event; later law is excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

### Issue 1: Statutory reach

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Agency action must remain within the statutory delegation. United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes supplies the received adjacent-wetlands construction, not a decision on these isolated ponds.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The agency must identify authority under the particular statutory scheme; statutory terms and operative delegation remain limits even when environmental protection is important.
* **Other Governing Law:** 33 U.S.C. §§1344(a), 1362(7); 33 C.F.R. §328.3(a)(3); the 1986 Migratory Bird Rule explanation.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes; Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council; Christensen v. Harris County, with the latter confined to its actual nonbinding-advice holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Act reaches beyond traditionally navigable waters, but the existing adjacent-wetland rule leaves the isolated-water application unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory definition reaches these ponds solely through bird habitat, and whether the form and basis of the Corps' interpretation warrant controlling or persuasive weight.

### Issue 2: Commerce connection if necessary

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Lopez requires connection before magnitude. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt recognizes supported necessary-regime and substantial interstate-externality routes, with concrete mechanism and fit; United States v. Morrison preserves the rejection of generalized downstream effects.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The commerce framework requires identification of the enacted regulated class and eligible interstate mechanism before aggregation. National importance, speculative future value, and the commercial character of one project do not independently supply that connection.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Ecological cross-border effects are eligible if materially supported and beyond reasonable state internalization. Noneconomic character is not a categorical bar.
* **Open Point:** If statutory authority exists, whether this record supports either recognized route; neither universal congressional authority nor categorical state immunity follows.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Seventh Circuit relied on bird-related commerce and an expansive reading of waters of the United States. The consortium argues that isolated ponds lie outside the delegated water program and that bird-related economic consequences cannot erase constitutional limits. The government argues that the Act deliberately expanded beyond navigability and that migratory bird habitat has interstate ecological and economic significance. Its strongest constitutional theory under simulation law identifies a concrete spillover or regime necessity rather than aggregating generalized benefits. The statutory defense must still establish what Congress delegated.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Statutory exclusion:** Reverse the challenged jurisdictional ground without reaching commerce power. The holding can be limited to isolated intrastate ponds regulated solely under the bird-habitat criterion; it need not decide all wetlands. **Statutory inclusion with supported commerce connection:** Sustain jurisdiction while leaving any separate permit-merits issue for its proper review. **Statutory inclusion but inadequate constitutional analysis:** Vacate and remand under National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt for the actual record's mechanism and fit, without supplying missing ecological findings. The choice among these paths changes both remand work and future authority; no path automatically grants the landfill permit or overrides independent state law.
