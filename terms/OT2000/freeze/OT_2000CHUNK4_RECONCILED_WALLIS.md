## The competing paths were substantively tested

### Removal, scope and municipal responsibility

The City's strongest position is that a specific threatened killing of a young child, transmitted through a mandated reporter and protective-services personnel, justified prompt action without requiring a completed abuse investigation. That proposition cannot be rejected merely because the allegation ultimately proved false or originated with a person receiving psychiatric care. The supported summary-judgment record nevertheless does not compel its application here. The known source limitations, divergent accounts of the therapist conversation, actual investigative omissions, observed condition of the children, absence of an allegation against Becky and absence of a threatened injury to Lauren create material disputes. A threat tied to the equinox also does not resolve the permissible duration of every later separation.

The eight commitments adopt a minimum emergency and fit rule for unilateral removal from parental custody without prior judicial authorization: specific, articulable information must give objectively reasonable grounds to believe imminent serious bodily harm requires action before authorization can reasonably be obtained, and the intrusion's scope and duration must be reasonably directed to that danger. This is a necessary protection for the presented nonconsensual intervention, not an adjudication of voluntary placement, valid judicial orders or every lawful child-protection procedure. The children's Fourth Amendment seizure rights and parents' Fourteenth Amendment family interests remain distinct. No universal equivalence between the Amendments, complete family-law scrutiny rule, fixed corroboration category or exhaustion checklist is adopted.

McArthur supports attention to actual need, scope, duration and judicial authorization, while its limited residential restraint does not itself decide this much greater bodily and familial intrusion. Troxel's protected parental direction supports consideration of the unimplicated mother, but its fractured broader rules do not establish a universal unfitness prerequisite or harm formula. The lower court's statement assigning every reasonable-cause question to the jury is not adopted. Courts decide legal standards and may decide sufficiency on undisputed facts; they cannot resolve the genuine material conflicts and credibility issues against this family on the present motion.

There is also an independent institutional question. The City's argument that an employee's mistake or outside misinformation cannot alone establish municipal liability is correct. But the offered evidence describes a settled practice, testified to by a supervisor and the designated knowledgeable officer, and connects reliance on the supposed order to the removal and lack of investigation. That evidence defeats the categorical conclusion that no municipal-policy evidence exists. It does not prove unconstitutional policy, final authority, municipal fault or the causal scope of every later injury.

The reconciled ground preserves Carter, Collins, Monell and Canton separately: an underlying deprivation, the City's attributable policy or legally effective custom, the fault required by the actual theory, and causation. A failure-to-train, supervise or provide safeguards must satisfy deliberate indifference, an identified deficiency and supported notice through a pattern or sufficiently obvious recurring need. Neither the duration of a practice nor hindsight that verification would have helped automatically proves those elements. An affirmative-policy theory must not silently be relabeled an omission, or vice versa. No general requirement to inspect a paper order physically follows, and ordinary interagency communication is not held categorically unlawful.

The mandatory receipt accurately narrows R2: the City did not separately argue on the described appeal that mistaken reliance on a reported order alone supplied legal authority. The source does not establish that the contention was forfeited at every stage. The legal consequences of reliance and the actual communications remain unresolved; neither an invented alternative defense nor an invented waiver supports today's judgment.

### Examinations, preservation and consequential injury

The strongest argument for reaching an independent examination claim now is substantial: the district court addressed the examinations; the report identifies the City as the requesting agency; testimony identifies the detective's role and hospital contract; and both questions were granted. Those matters make the bodily intrusion concrete and provide possible attribution evidence. They do not settle Rymer's specific objection about whether this independent City policy claim was actually presented. Granting a constitutional question does not amend a complaint or cure a preserved procedural defect.

The eight choose the narrower E2 disposition: vacate the independent examination-policy adjudication and associated new constitutional rules insofar as they conclusively govern the remand, and require ordinary determination of the theory's presentation and scope before deciding its independent merits. They do not hold the claim waived, preserved, valid or invalid. Nor do they hold the examinations constitutional. A mandatory new evidentiary hearing, otherwise unavailable amendment or opportunity to manufacture a policy theory does not follow. The remaining claims proceed under ordinary rules.

This disposition answers the granted examination question at a lawful threshold; it does not leave a necessary merits question unanswered merely because it is difficult. Lower note 15 expressly distinguishes a second examination policy from injuries caused by a proved removal-policy violation. The latter possible causal route survives the preservation ruling. Conversely, an injury occurring after removal does not automatically become attributable to the City. The actual claim, underlying violation, causal connection and measure of injury remain necessary. Later judicial intervention and the medical report likewise neither automatically establish nor automatically sever responsibility for posthearing separation.

Ferguson now governs at its actual scope. Six Associates joined its confined rejection of the coordinated prosecution program's special-needs justification. Scalia and Thomas dissented on an independently sufficient clinical-function ground. Neither position dictates the merits here: the present record does not establish comparable independent clinical indications, valid substitute consent or an urgent treatment predicate, and protective custody alone does not supply them. But the Court does not infer that every protective examination is ordinary policing, that every mixed function is invalid, or that three separately expressed permissions are universally required. E2 leaves the actual independent merits question unadjudicated; the groups' differing Ferguson approaches remain available within their lawful scopes if that question properly returns.

The lower court's distinct notice, opportunity-to-object and parental-presence rules also do not become common Supreme Court holdings through a generalized reference to family integrity. Even that lower presence rule permits exclusion for a valid reason with nearby presence instead. Denius protects justified acquisition of intimate information in public employment; it neither authorizes bodily examinations nor supplies this complete medical protocol. The 1998 California enactment supplies no authorization for the 1991 acts. No unsupported emergency, consent, waiver or substitute decisionmaker is found.
