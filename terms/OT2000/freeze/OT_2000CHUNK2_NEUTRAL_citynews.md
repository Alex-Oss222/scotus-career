# City News & Novelty, Inc. v. City of Waukesha

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** City News & Novelty, Inc. v. City of Waukesha
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1680; 531 U.S. 278
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-17 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether an adult-business licensing system must assure a prompt judicial decision or only prompt access to judicial review, and whether this applicant retains a live controversy after abandoning the renewal application and the regulated business.
* **Question Granted:** Only the prompt-judicial-review question was granted; substantive licensing restrictions and evidentiary sufficiency were outside that grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Wisconsin Court of Appeals, 231 Wis.2d 93, 604 N.W.2d 870: upheld the license-renewal denial and treated prompt access to review as sufficient.
* **Relief Requested:** City News seeks reversal on the granted prompt-review issue. Waukesha seeks preservation of its judgment, including disposition without reaching merits if the controversy is no longer live.
* **Material Record:** City News held an adult-business license and sought renewal. The city denied renewal for ordinance violations, and the denial survived administrative and state judicial review. After seeking Supreme Court review, City News withdrew the renewal request and ceased the adult business. It identifies no present plan to reapply. Its suggestion of a possible disability through 2005 depends on disputed ordinance operation and a future business choice. No surviving damages claim is identified within this limited grant.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Article III requires a continuing concrete stake. The granted issue does not encompass the substantive ordinance restrictions or sufficiency of the violation evidence. Renewal of an operating business also raises a distinct question about interim continuation, which is not interchangeable with the speed of review for an initial applicant.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.

### Issue 1: Continuing controversy and limited review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona requires an actual surviving stake after a claimant voluntarily leaves the regulated setting without intent to return. Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw preserves a demanding defendant-cessation rule, not automatic standing for a claimant who abandoned the activity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Mootness, initial standing, and entitlement to an injunction remain separate. A speculative future return or fees alone does not establish a live prospective claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; the First Amendment; the granted licensing-review question.
* **Material Other Authority:** Freedman v. Maryland; FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas, for the limited licensed-expression issue.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Freedman v. Maryland and FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas supply the received prompt-review concern, but jurisdiction must exist before choosing between competing readings.
* **Open Point:** Whether any concrete surviving stake is shown. If one exists, the distinction between access, a timely decision, and interim continuation remains to be examined.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The lower court accepted access to review as sufficient. City News argues that an eventual decision may arrive too late to protect expression and that it has not promised never to reenter the business. Waukesha answers that the abandoned renewal and absence of any concrete plan remove the applicant's stake. It also distinguishes an initial censorship barrier from renewal enforcement where a stay might preserve ongoing activity. The possibility of future reentry does not become a present plan merely because the claimant refuses a permanent disclaimer.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Mootness:** Dismiss the Supreme Court proceeding without deciding the licensing issue. Equitable vacatur requires attention to the claimant's voluntary abandonment and the state-court posture; it is not automatic. **Live stake shown:** Decide the granted prompt-review question, distinguishing timely judicial resolution from mere filing access and considering the correct remedy for a renewal denial. **Vehicle limit:** Do not adjudicate a different stay-pending-review question simply because it better describes the grievance. The current record supports no concrete future renewal or surviving damages interest.
