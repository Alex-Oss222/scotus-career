## Decisional conclusion

The exact proposed vacatur and mandatory review-history remand is supported by Breyer. It does not obtain Stevens's, Souter's or Ginsburg's assent merely by preserving their broader review position and the existing findings. Their recorded narrower causal-preservation alternative remains available, but it does not require a first-effective-forum inquiry before the conditional relief can operate. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain reversal on their reconciled grounds. The proposal therefore lacks five votes for its exact mandate; the unaudited ninth position cannot cure that deficit. No additional review exception or unified indirect-review rationale is established.

The consequential distinction is between (1) requiring an account of the 1987 process in every event before restoring relief and (2) examining that process only if an identified, actually unresolved requirement of former habeas law makes it necessary. The proposal expressly imposes the first inquiry. Its later requirement to identify a genuinely unresolved prerequisite does not remove that mandatory direction. Respect for ordinary exhaustion does not itself establish that this judgment left an exhaustion issue undecided or that an additional responsibility inquiry is necessary. No such finding may be added during circulation.

## Justice-specific circulation conclusions

**Stevens.** His join in the actual Custis dissent supports adjudication of properly raised constitutional predicate attacks, with ordinary collateral rules. His Fiore and Glover joins require a real preserved prerequisite and preserve actual causal injury; they do not authorize automatic renewed gatekeeping. His historical Coss join in Souter's dissent, 532 U.S. 408–410, expressly supports the narrower preservation remand if the causal objection is pursued. Preserving the consideration and trial-prejudice determinations answers his objection to treating a newly inferred no-effect appraisal as settled fact. It does not explain why the conditional judgment must be vacated pending a new mandatory review-history inquiry. He retains affirmance; the recorded narrower alternative is not an already cast vacatur vote.

**Souter.** His Custis dissent and Nichols concurrence distinguish constitutional reliability from a conclusive forum exclusion. His Coss dissent identifies the specific alternative: the Court of Appeals should determine whether the causal challenge is available or waived before the Supreme Court substitutes its contrary appraisal. The proposal answers that appellate-role objection, but adds a separate compulsory access inquiry. His broader section 2254 position supplies no requirement that Coss first establish a denied original forum. Ordinary exhaustion or default remains applicable on its actual terms; an unexplained fourteen-year lapse establishes neither faultlessness nor an unfulfilled procedural step. He does not trade the existing relief for the combined mandate merely because one part is acceptable.

**Ginsburg.** Her Garlotte opinion separates custody from other prerequisites; her actual Custis join does not decide this different habeas channel. Her Fiore join permits resolution of an actually undecided gate while protecting established merits; it does not make an unspecified gate a new basis for withholding relief. Her historical Coss dissent join directly includes Souter's causal-preservation alternative. She can join that procedural protection while retaining her broader review ground. The mandatory access-first suspension remains unanswered, so she does not change her judgment commitment.

**Breyer.** His historical Coss dissent, 532 U.S. 410, expressly calls for vacatur because the court below did not consider whether this was the first effective forum, while rejecting present appellate displacement of the adverse-effect determination. His Garlotte, Fiore and Glover joins support separating the forum, actual procedural requirements, established constitutional findings and causal injury. The proposal leaves the additional exception undecided, preserves the existing findings within their lawful scope and orders no automatic hearing. Its causation instruction is compatible: only a properly available objection proceeds, and the lower appellate court addresses its availability first. He joins the whole bounded mandate as stated. This clarifies his reconciled commitment; it neither changes his disposition nor creates a new historical departure.

**O'Connor and Kennedy, separately.** Each retains the actual Custis forum limitation and its reservation rather than an affirmative extra exception. Each directly joined historical Coss Part III-C, which accepted consideration but treated the adverse-effect inference as clearly erroneous on the combined sentencing record. Their actual Glover joins reject a minimum quantum of proven additional imprisonment; they do not require a finding that any additional imprisonment occurred here. The revised preservation protection does not answer either Justice's existing view that this essential causal conclusion is reviewable now. Neither acquires a reason to require an access inquiry after finding no adverse effect. The proposal therefore changes neither commitment.

**Scalia.** His actual Custis join and historical Coss Parts II and III-A join support the channel ground. He did not join III-B or III-C. The proposal's refusal to adopt a new exception is compatible with his reservation, but its remand leaves this ineffective-assistance attack open rather than rejecting it on the ground he supports. Excusing exhaustion does not supply that channel. He refuses the mandate without inventing prisoner fault or a no-effect finding.

**Thomas.** His actual Custis join and historical Coss Parts II and III-A join support the channel restriction; his III-C join supplies an independent no-effect ground. Preserving the lower consideration finding does not answer his separate adverse-effect appraisal, and the proposed preservation limit conflicts with his supported view that the essential causal issue is reviewable. He joins neither the additional inquiry nor vacatur pending it. No new factual premise or changed rule supports departure.

## Narrowest supported adjustment and authority limits

For Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, remove the unconditional first-effective-forum inquiry. A genuinely pursued causal objection can instead receive the specific lower appellate preservation-and-review treatment their historical dissent identifies, preserving existing findings unless lawfully displaced. Any review-history work must be tied to an identified ordinary prerequisite under former section 2254(b), which allows exhaustion to be excused when state corrective process is absent or circumstances make it ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. That statutory rule is not itself an additional indirect-review entitlement.

This adjustment records an available narrower alternative; it does not declare those three to have changed their judgment votes or show that Breyer would abandon his necessary first-forum inquiry. Combining those different returns does not produce a common mandate. No complete rationale has the necessary support, and no Marks inference is warranted from a reservation, a rejected relief direction or different remand purposes. The supplied positions also do not establish an equal division.

No new source or approval is required to reach these conclusions. A final finding of prisoner fault, effective unused review or a definitive causal waiver would require the precise state-process or appellate materials already identified in the neutral refresh. This audit makes none of those findings and adds no new factual or historical-departure premise.
