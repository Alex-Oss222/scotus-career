# Tri County — original-record facts and competing submissions

This handoff describes underlying proceedings, evidence, and the parties' submissions. It states no conclusion on the disputed merits or preservation question. Page citations below use printed page numbers. The original sources are the [petition and appendix](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0379/micro_IA40385014_0379%2002.%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari.pdf), [joint appendix (JA)](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0379/micro_IA40385014_0379%2005.%20Joint%20Appendix.pdf), [petitioners' merits brief](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0379/micro_IA40385014_0379%2006.%20Petitioners%20Brief.pdf), and [respondent's merits brief](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0379/micro_IA40385014_0379%2007.%20Respondents%20Brief.pdf).

## Review scope and existing orders

- The petition's question asks whether Gasperini invalidates the circuit approach constraining district-court discretion to grant new trials and correspondingly expanding appellate review. It does not ask for a fresh determination of the underlying due-process liability. The 1997 appellate decision established a procedural due-process violation and remanded for damages, expressly identifying possible mitigation and stop-work-order issues. It rejected the separate substantive due-process claim. [Petition p.i; JA18–30; 104 F.3d 455, 457–462](https://static.case.law/f3d/104/html/0455-01.html).
- The July 23, 1998 order denied judgment as a matter of law. It said the District had not met its mitigation burden concerning the approximately $536,421 already spent, because the jury could reasonably reject expenditures approaching $1 million to recover that sunk investment. It separately characterized seeking future profits without pursuing permit reinstatement as unreasonable per se; considered the profits evidence too speculative; called the approximately $4.5 million lost-profit component excessive; and reconsidered exclusion of evidence about future community resistance and regulatory activity. The order conditioned a new damages trial on refusal to accept a reduction to $1 million within thirty days. Those are the judge's stated grounds, not agreed findings that a later lawful closure necessarily would have occurred. Petition App.12–14.
- The order's direction for retrial was conditional: if the plaintiff again introduced lost-profit evidence, the District could rebut it with evidence of the likely course of future community resistance and regulatory activity. It did not direct a specific second verdict. Petition App.14.
- The first judgment was entered April 20, 1998. The second jury awarded $100, with judgment entered February 4, 1999. Tri County challenged both the new-trial order and evidentiary rulings at the second trial. The appellate court's restoration of the first verdict meant it did not reach the latter challenges. Respondent therefore requests remand for those challenges if the first order is sustained. Petition App.11,15; respondent pp.12,41; [200 F.3d 836, 843](https://static.case.law/f3d/200/html/0836-01.html).

## Actual first-trial exclusions

- The October 15 and December 6, 1993 letters were initially presented and then struck. This fact is explicit in the court's ruling and its instruction to the jury, and is not merely a party's characterization. JA119,122–123. The first letter requested further information under the environmental-review statute. The second rescinded the environmental approval, cited failure to respond and alleged adverse effects on nearby residences, and announced a forthcoming notice of intent to revoke the building permit with charges and hearing procedures. The letter itself did not purport to complete that building-permit revocation. JA145–150.
- The court also struck Sweeney's testimony about houses 50–75 feet away, truck traffic and noise, and potential emissions from uncovered trucks; Dishian's comparison to pumping gasoline; and Selzer's, Cross's, and Sweeney's testimony about delivery of a transportation plan. Its jury instruction excluded the health-and-safety subject on the stated ground that the permitting process had already considered and resolved it. JA122–123.
- The District proffered expert Alfred Siess outside the final evidence submitted to the jury. He questioned thermal treatment, off-site transport, sampling, airstripping, heavy metals, and fire risks. The court found his testimony irrelevant to the specific permitted project and found no proper evidentiary basis for the jury to conclude that permission ultimately would have been denied on health-and-safety grounds. JA104–118. The ruling was made after the court repeatedly asked for the legal mechanism and evidentiary foundation for such a later denial. JA72–77,104–105,113–118.
- At JA116 the judge acknowledged that a proceeding brought by the community or based on alleged misrepresentations might have resulted in withdrawal of permission, but said that such proceedings had not occurred. The later new-trial order reconsidered the treatment of prospective resistance and regulatory activity. These are distinct rulings at different stages. Neither establishes as an undisputed fact the result of a proceeding that did not occur.
- At JA118 the court expressly distinguished the citation, stop-work order, unpaid fine, and failure to answer from the health-and-safety issue. The jury received instructions allowing the District to prove unreasonable failure to pay the fine or pursue an appeal and resulting avoidable loss. JA123–125. The record therefore does not support describing the first trial as excluding all mitigation or all intervening-regulation defenses.

## Source support bearing on the competing evidence arguments

- The December 11, 1992 approval covered construction and operation of a specified 25-ton-per-hour soil unit, subject to twelve conditions. Operation initially was limited to testing; an approved protocol, compliant results, inspections, emissions limits, records, and notification of changes or violations were required. Processing was limited to soil contaminated by gasoline, diesel oil, fuel oil, or kerosene; hazardous-waste treatment was prohibited. JA126–129. The December 22 environmental waiver expressly retained other legal requirements and ordinary permitting/construction reviews. JA130–131. These conditions qualify any assertion that earlier approval guaranteed unrestricted future operation.
- District witnesses described a nearby residence substantially closer than the application representation, alleged misleading traffic information, and concerns about trucks and emissions. Sweeney said the agency initially understood the nearest residence to be 700 feet away, later found houses across the street, and lacked proposed routes. JA94–97,102. Cross described the information requests as a further review process. JA89–93. These were District testimony and allegations, not an adjudicated finding of fraud.
- Countervailing testimony included Selzer's statement that the requested information had been provided during an extensive prior review; Sweeney's acknowledgment that the questions normally should have been addressed before approval and that he had not checked whether other agency divisions already had the information; his acknowledgment that soil kept inside would not adversely affect surrounding homes; and his lack of knowledge of truck routing. JA50–51,97–103. Sweeney personally had not seen the annual-tonnage application reviewed by another division. JA100–101. No finding of fraud should be substituted for these disputed and division-specific descriptions.
- The District proposed remaining occupancy inspections, repeat stack tests, later regulatory proceedings, and public opposition as reasons the project might not operate. JA72–77,104–118. Respondent argues that the District supplied no sufficiently supported lawful mechanism after repeated opportunities, that the existing permit conditions were the applicable requirements, and that conjecture about a citizen appeal or fraud proceeding did not cure the deficient proffer. Respondent pp.37–40. The District's request to reconsider an exclusion and respondent's objection to its legal foundation remain competing submissions.
- In its merits brief the District disputes both the initial exclusion and the appellate focus on whether that initial ruling was permissible, insisting that the decision under review is the judge's later reconsideration in ordering a new trial. It identifies the certificate-of-occupancy process under 11 DCMR §3203.1 and an aggrieved citizen's challenge under Woodley Park Community Association v. District of Columbia Board of Zoning Adjustment, 490 A.2d 628, as mechanisms for further review; respondent contests their applicability and the proffer's adequacy here. The District also treats the unanswered October letter as independently relevant to mitigation and a later opportunity for process. Petitioners' merits brief; JA72–77,113–118; respondent pp.37–40. These are concrete competing legal theories, not proof that a particular additional permit necessarily would be denied.

## Independent orders, mitigation, and damages evidence

- September 7 citation and September 22 stop-work order concerned use without a certificate of occupancy. Inspectors testified that payment and removal of the soil or obtaining the certificate would resolve the citation, and that a successful appeal, soil removal, or certificate would remove the stop-work obstacle. JA80–85. The administrative examiner upheld the $500 fine and imposed $1,000 for nonappearance plus $25 costs. The order's automatic permit suspension for nonpayment could be lifted by paying the assessment. JA85–89. The existence of these orders and their potential consequences must be kept distinct from the unlawful September 20 oral suspension.
- Selzer testified that the company declined an appeal because counsel estimated eight months to a year, the used equipment might be sold meanwhile, and continued rent and other commitments could approach $1 million. Counsel testified to that estimated delay and administrative route. JA46–55. The Board's executive officer confirmed emergency-appeal and stay procedures but said an estimate of the time to resolve an emergency appeal would be speculative. JA103–104. These are competing evidentiary premises about reasonableness, not a finding that any appeal was certain to fail or succeed.
- The claimed direct expenditures were $536,421.19. The lost-profit estimate was $11,628,174 over 1994–2000, using projected revenue and itemized operating/capital costs. JA65–70,132,139–142. The financial expert relied on other witnesses' industry figures and did not independently examine the surveyed competitors' profitability. JA68–69. Other witnesses described existing soil volume, available equipment, staffing, utilities, treatment capacity, and the asserted local transportation advantage. JA35–70.
- The trial judge questioned immediate full-capacity operation, startup and marketing assumptions, an unusually high profit margin, and competitors' experience. JA77–80. Tri County relies on the component testimony, the decreasing treatment-price assumptions, and the jury's substantially smaller award. Respondent pp.29–37. The March 1995 market report discussed declining prices, competing methods, a failed competitor, and a possible local transport advantage; it was not admitted as an exhibit when the District objected, although its author supplied oral opinions and the judge questioned him about it. JA60–64,133–138. Its presence in the JA does not make the entire report admitted first-trial evidence.

## Preservation and limits

- Respondent alleges that the District first challenged the circuit's more-searching-review formulation on rehearing, despite opportunities in the appellate briefs, argument, and later letters. It seeks dismissal on that ground or resolution under an ordinary abuse-of-discretion standard. Respondent pp.15–21. The actual rehearing orders provide no reason for denial. Petition App.16–17. The allegation therefore must not be restated as a judicial waiver finding.
- The District expressly answers in its [merits reply, pp.15–19](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA40385014_0379/micro_IA40385014_0379%2008.%20Reply%20Brief.pdf): its appellate brief requested deferential abuse-of-discretion review and wide new-trial discretion; it says the appellate panel rejected that position at argument and in its decision. It distinguishes a new argument supporting an existing claim from a new claim and invokes the court's power to identify governing law. It also says its original new-trial motion and supporting memorandum already raised speculative profits, mitigation, and excluded evidence; the postargument letters supplied that material in response to respondent's objection. Respondent disputes these descriptions and their legal sufficiency. Neither side's explanation of the unexplained rehearing denial is an established judicial finding.
- The reply's requested relief is reversal of the appellate decision, affirmance of the new-trial order, and remand to the appellate court for further proceedings. Reply p.20. Respondent separately requests consideration of its unresolved second-trial challenges if the new-trial order is sustained. Respondent p.41.
- The JA contains selected trial excerpts, not a complete trial transcript. It does, however, reproduce the actual pertinent exclusions, jury directions, relevant testimony, permit conditions, and correspondence. The petition reproduces the actual new-trial order. Where these materials identify a party allegation, judicial ruling, or excluded proffer, this handoff preserves that status.
