# Fiore v. White

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Fiore v. White
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 98-942; 531 U.S. 225
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-01-09 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether due process permits continued conviction for operating a hazardous-waste facility without a permit when the authoritative state construction establishes that lack of a permit was always an element and the State concedes the defendant held one.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit, 149 F.3d 221: reversed the district court's habeas grant on the premise that the state statutory construction announced a new rule unavailable on collateral review. The matter returns after the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's answer to the certified state-law question.
* **Relief Requested:** Fiore seeks reversal of the federal denial and relief from the conviction; the Commonwealth defends the conviction under its former permit-deviation theory and collateral-review position.
* **Material Record:** Fiore held a permit but allegedly operated outside its terms. His codefendant obtained reversal when the Pennsylvania Supreme Court construed the statute to require actual absence of a permit. That court's later answer to the certified question states that this construction clarified the law already in force when Fiore's conviction became final. The Commonwealth concedes that it produced no evidence that he lacked the permit.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The prior decision in Fiore v. White certified the temporal state-law question, retained jurisdiction, and reserved federal merits. Its answer removes the premise of a new-law retroactivity dispute. The federal application was filed after April 24, 1996 and is subject to the applicable AEDPA amendments. The Third Circuit expressly left exhaustion and whether the state courts adjudicated the federal claim on its merits unresolved because it rejected the claim outright. Reversal of that merits premise does not invent resolution of those independent gates. Relief must address this conviction without assuming that every alleged environmental violation or other conviction disappears.
* **Relevant Dates:** The federal application followed AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment; the retrieved lower opinion does not give a more precise filing date. Simulated certification occurred November 30, 1999; the authoritative Pennsylvania answer followed in 2000; the merits event is January 9, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The codefendant's state proceeding supplies the interpretive record, not another listed matter.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

### Issue 1: Preexisting elements and proof

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Fiore v. White left federal merits open pending state clarification. The certification ruling establishes neither the writ nor any state-law meaning; the state answer supplies the latter.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing proof principles require each crime element to be established beyond a reasonable doubt. Certification can clarify state law within an existing controversy; it cannot create jurisdiction.
* **Other Governing Law:** Due Process Clause; 28 U.S.C. §2254; Pennsylvania's hazardous-waste permit statute as authoritatively construed.
* **Material Other Authority:** In re Winship; Jackson v. Virginia; the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's certified answer.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** In re Winship and Jackson v. Virginia address conviction without proof of a required element. The answer establishes an old element, so no new-rule retroactivity holding is necessary.
* **Open Point:** Whether a conviction can survive the conceded absence of proof under the clarified preexisting elements.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit's reasoning depended on treating the state decision as a change. Fiore argues that the authoritative answer defeats that premise and leaves a straightforward missing-element violation. The Commonwealth's original theory treated substantial permit deviation as equivalent to having no permit; the state court has rejected that interpretation. The State can insist that relief remain confined to the invalid conviction, but it lacks a supported answer to the missing element on the accepted record.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Apply the certified answer:** Reverse the federal merits rejection and remand to implement the missing-element ruling, resolving any properly preserved, still-undecided habeas gates before issuing a writ. The rule governs proof of the statute as it actually existed, not general retroactive application of genuinely new state law. If an actual state merits adjudication triggers §2254(d), evaluate it against the preexisting rules in In re Winship and Jackson v. Virginia under Williams v. Taylor, rather than treating this decision as the first source of the proof requirement. **Material dispute about an element or answer:** A bounded remand could resolve such a dispute if one existed; the concession and authoritative answer identify none. The Court should not reopen the certified question or decide an unnecessary constitutional retroactivity rule. No immunity, damages, or all-count release determination follows.
