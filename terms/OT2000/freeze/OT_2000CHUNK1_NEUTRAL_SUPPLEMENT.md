## Cleveland: additional live statutory argument and remedial precision

The [government merits-brief extracts](../runtime/OT_2000CHUNK1_CLEVELAND_SOURCE_EXTRACT.md) add a material argument absent from the original neutral packet. They are script-copied from the [United States' date-eligible merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/cleveland-v-united-states-merits), including its statement, argument summary, and complete section 1341 appendix. Advocacy is not a judicial finding.

The government asserts two independent constructions. First, it argues that section 1341's clause addressing acquisition by false pretenses reaches a license that becomes property in the recipient's hands even if the State loses no property. It relies on the disjunctive wording, the absence of a requirement that the object be obtained from another, the separate enactment of the clause, and its reading of McNally. Second, it argues that Louisiana has a property interest of its own because it receives substantial payments and controls issuance, renewal, transfer, and revocation. The government also distinguishes initial issuance from renewal of a previously issued license. The prosecution concerns the initial license and subsequent renewals; the neutral question must not erase the renewal argument.

An analytical response to the first construction is that McNally construed the statute as a whole and that the later acquisition clause describes methods of property fraud rather than a separate license-acquisition offense divorced from the victim's property. This is an available legal response, not a claim about an unrecovered petitioner's filing. A model rejecting State ownership must still address the independent acquisition-clause theory before treating the property question as dispositive. Conversely, accepting that theory requires a reasoned account of the clauses, precedent, and the indictment/instruction basis for that route. None of these paths adjudicates the ungranted materiality question.

The government brief describes two mail-fraud convictions and additional racketeering, laundering, and tax convictions; the original packet's reference to section 1957 and the brief's description of a section 1956 laundering conviction should not be silently harmonized. The source difference is unnecessary to deciding the granted section 1341 question if relief extends only to the two mail-fraud convictions and to other convictions actually dependent on the invalidated predicates, with the dependence and any surviving counts determined on remand. Do not vacate every conviction or declare an independent laundering or tax holding from this incomplete count description. If a proposed disposition requires identifying a particular additional count or its independent evidentiary basis, obtain the indictment, verdict, and lower judgment first.
