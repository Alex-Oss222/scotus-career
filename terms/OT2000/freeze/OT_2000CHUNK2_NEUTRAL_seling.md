### Issue 1: Actual punitive operation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Kansas v. Hendricks makes civil intent rebuttable by the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect, and invalidated the demonstrated retrospective commitment in that application. Its result was not a facial approval immunizing every implementation. Lindh v. Murphy supplies the application-date rule for chapter 153 and preserves separately directed provisions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The punitive-purpose-or-effect rule assesses supported operation cumulatively, without making inadequate treatment, confinement, or indeterminacy alone conclusive.
* **Other Governing Law:** Ex Post Facto and Double Jeopardy Clauses; the applicable pre-AEDPA chapter 153 framework, including 28 U.S.C. §2254; Washington's commitment statute. Due-process and conditions remedies remain separate.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A civil label is not conclusive; actual punitive operation can be material. The demanding burden and temporal predicate remain, and a successful conditions claim does not automatically establish punishment.
* **Open Point:** What supported conditions and original-operation evidence Young can establish, and whether they satisfy the existing test. Double-jeopardy attachment and offense identity require independent treatment if reached.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The superintendent argues that an earlier civil classification cannot fluctuate with each individual's conditions and that treatment defects belong in conditions litigation. Young argues that a facial civil designation cannot authorize actual retrospective punishment and seeks an evidentiary opportunity. The Ninth Circuit permitted that inquiry without itself finding the clearest proof. Under controlling law, the superintendent cannot obtain a categorical bar by importing the historical Kansas v. Hendricks outcome; Young cannot obtain release merely by alleging deficient care or by treating every regulatory failure as punitive.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Apply existing Kansas v. Hendricks framework:** Preserve a bounded remand to determine supported punitive operation and the retrospective predicate. If proved, custody under the unlawful application cannot continue; if not, reject that ground without foreclosing independently available conditions remedies. **Conditions-only claim:** Direct an allegation that cannot establish unlawful custody to its proper remedial channel rather than award habeas release. **Double Jeopardy:** Reach only if necessary and independently establish its predicates; Kansas v. Hendricks did not settle them. No lawful path categorically treats the historical civil label as dispositive contrary to Kansas v. Hendricks. Missing findings require adjudication, not an automatic declaration of punishment.
