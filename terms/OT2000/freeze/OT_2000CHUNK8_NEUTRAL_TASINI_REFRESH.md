# New York Times Co. v. Tasini

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** New York Times Co. v. Tasini

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-201; 533 U.S. 483.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-25 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a publisher's statutory privilege to reproduce a freelance contribution as part of a collective work, a revision, or a later collective work in the same series permits the electronic database uses shown here without the author's permission.

* **Question Granted:** This is a reconstruction of the statutory merits question from the judicial record.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reversed summary judgment for the publishers and directed judgment for the authors concerning the challenged database uses.

* **Relief Requested:** The publishers seek restoration of their statutory defense; the authors seek affirmance and subsequent appropriate relief for unauthorized reproduction and distribution.

* **Material Record:** Six freelance authors contributed articles to the New York Times, Newsday, and Sports Illustrated as independent contractors. The general practice did not obtain an express transfer of freelance copyright, but David Whitford's Sports Illustrated article was governed by an express contract. Its first-publication clause was held below not to authorize this database license. The separate nonexclusive republication-license clause required payment of fifty percent of net proceeds, and the Time-publication republication clause required prevailing rates; Time had not compensated Whitford and did not invoke those conditional clauses as its defense. The Second Circuit therefore rejected the asserted authorization on this record. This is not a finding that every author lacked a written agreement or a universal rule against contractual electronic licensing. Newsday and Time do not press their respective contract-authorization defenses in this Court. The reported presentation fact leaves the lower rulings as the background of the statutory review; it does not invite a new holding construing Whitford's contract, a new waiver ruling on Newsday's defense, or a remand reopening either defense. Print publishers licensed articles to electronic publishers. NEXIS presents searchable text articles individually with source information but without the original edition's surrounding contents and presentation. New York Times OnDisc similarly provides searchable text. General Periodicals OnDisc preserves images of particular printed pages but does not present the complete periodical edition. Search results can sometimes be selected to collect articles from an original issue; that user-created grouping differs from the database's ordinary presentation. This case concerns the proved licenses and interfaces, not every electronic archive or every contract governing freelance work.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory privilege is a defense to otherwise implicated reproduction and distribution rights. Authorship, ownership, contract authorization, and actual presentation must not be collapsed into the physical storage medium. The remedial consequences have not been fully adjudicated.

* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant contributions and licensing arrangements predate suit; the Second Circuit judgment preceded this term.

### Issue 1: The collective-work privilege

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling precedent resolves whether these separated database articles are reproduced as part of a revision under §201(c). Existing copyright rules do not confer an automatic digital-format exemption or turn ownership of a collective work into ownership of every contribution.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the particular exclusive right, owner, transfer, statutory limitation, and challenged act. Distinct statutory permissions must be proved according to their own predicates.

* **Other Governing Law:** The Copyright Act provisions reproduced in the primary source distinguish copyright in a contribution from copyright in a collective work; absent an express transfer, the publisher receives the limited §201(c) reproduction and distribution privilege. Section 106 identifies exclusive rights; §103(b) limits the collective author's rights in preexisting material; §201(d) permits separate ownership of divisible rights. These are the provisions operative for the transactions, not later amendments or licensing settlements.

* **Material Other Authority:** Stewart v. Abend is relevant to preserving the author's distinct ownership, but does not decide this database question.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A publisher may exercise the privilege actually granted; changing the medium alone neither creates nor destroys it.

* **Open Point:** Whether the database's user-facing separation from the edition is incompatible with reproduction as part of the original collective work or its revision, and what relief follows if the privilege fails.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The appellate court treated the electronic compendia as uses beyond the publisher's limited privilege, rather than revisions of each source issue. It independently rejected Time's invoked first-publication defense for Whitford and did not require him to plead a contract count to pursue an asserted unauthorized use. The district court had adopted the broader revision reading and treated the statutory privilege as a baseline alongside the contract. The appellate court did not reach the authors' separate argument that the §201(c) privilege is nontransferable under §201(d); that issue is not silently resolved by a construction of the privilege's substantive reach.

**Publishers:** The database contains the articles that constituted the editions, keeps source identifiers, and enables retrieval of an issue's contents. A revision can change selection and presentation, and statutory media neutrality should allow electronic dissemination comparable to microform. They argue that a contrary reading threatens completeness of historical databases and imposes renegotiation costs.

**Authors:** The database sells separate articles outside their original collective context. Source labels establish historical provenance, not present inclusion in a revised edition. Treating the whole database as a revision of each component would consume the authors' reserved licensing rights. The image-based product also detaches pages from the complete issue. They concede that electronic publication pursuant to actual authorization, or a genuinely preserved edition, presents a different case.

**Remedial Positions:** The practical value of a complete historical archive is relevant to lawful remedial discretion, but neither establishes a statutory license nor proves that deletion is the only available remedy. Actual agreements, damages, and equitable terms require their own legal and factual predicates.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold:** Neither Newsday nor Time presses its contract-authorization defense here. Preserve the lower-court treatment of those defenses without independently adjudicating them, treating their nonpresentation as a new factual concession, or directing contract reinterpretation on remand. Whitford's actual terms explain the transaction and the separate source of possible licensed rights; they are not a separately presented merits issue. The review concerns §201(c)'s statutory privilege. The distinct §201(d) transferability alternative was not reached below; whether any decision of it is necessary and properly included must be assessed separately rather than inferred from the contract background. **Merits Paths:** Read revision broadly enough to include these databases; read it as requiring the article to remain perceptibly part of an edition or its recognizable revision; or distinguish products if their actual presentation materially differs under the chosen construction.

**Judgment and Remedy:** A valid privilege supports publisher judgment on this theory. Failure of the privilege leaves the appellate liability disposition supported on the presented statutory ground and returns any remaining damages and equitable relief for individualized adjudication. Neither disposition creates a new Supreme Court contract holding or, by itself, reopens Newsday's or Time's unpressed contract defense. It does not compel removal of all freelance material from every database.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A contextual rule can apply across media while distinguishing intact digital editions from extracted individual articles. A broad archival rule must identify a limiting principle that leaves the author's reserved rights operative. **Uncertainty:** Contract terms outside the litigated articles, later interface changes, damages, willfulness, and the feasibility of negotiated access are not established by this record.
