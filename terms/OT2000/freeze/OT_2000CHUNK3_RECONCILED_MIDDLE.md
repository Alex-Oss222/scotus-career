### W1 — both cost commands, with distinct explanatory joins

**Operative rule, eight:** EPA may not use the economic costs of implementation to set national primary or secondary ambient air quality standards under section 109(b). Primary standards must provide the protection requisite for public health, based on section 108's scientific pollutant-effects criteria and allowing an adequate margin of safety. Secondary standards require the level requisite to protect public welfare from known or anticipated adverse effects associated with the pollutant's presence, on those criteria; they do not borrow the primary safety margin. Neither compliance expense nor economic effects of an attainment strategy become pollutant effects merely by being described as health or welfare concerns.

**Text-and-structure explanation, seven:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg support the enacted distinction between the standard and its implementation. Section 109(d)(2)(C)(iv) requires committee advice about adverse health, welfare, social, economic, and energy effects of attainment and maintenance strategies; it does not insert cost balancing into either standard. Periodic review returns to sections 108 and 109(b). The separate implementation provisions do real work without altering the standard-setting commands. Constitutional avoidance cannot create a cost construction unavailable under those commands. This is contextual construction, not a rule that every consequential delegation requires special words.

**Breyer's limited common join and additional explanation:** Breyer directly supports the operative rule and the standard/implementation distinction, but does not join a claim that the result follows from isolated section 109 language or a general presumption excluding costs absent clear textual authorization. The reported 1970 sponsor and Senate materials and the 1977 and 1989 materials reinforce Congress's technology-forcing choice. He would ordinarily read ambiguity to permit rational attention to serious disproportionate adverse effects, but finds that choice displaced here by the actual structure and history. His discretion discussion concerns severity, numbers affected, distribution, uncertainty, contextually trivial risks, and comparative health consequences. It is not a disguised implementation-cost exception, a zero-risk command, or a prediction that a particular technology will arrive. None of those additional methodological propositions acquires seven further joins merely because the eight agree on the result.

**Judgment:** Affirm the rejection of implementation-cost consideration in No. 99-1426 for both standard types. No concentration, scientific-validity conclusion, waiver, or health-effects finding is chosen.

### W4 — the actual conditional transition and lawful agency reconciliation

**Eight direct joins:** EPA's position is not immediate universal repeal of Subpart 2. It applies Subpart 1 to the new eight-hour standard, retains Subpart 2 while an area fails the old one-hour standard, and ends Subpart 2 when that older standard is attained. Nevertheless, old-standard attainment alone cannot erase otherwise applicable ozone-specific classifications, dates, and associated constraints for implementing a revised standard. The general and specific subparts leave genuine fit questions, but EPA must reconcile them without making the specific provisions inoperative by its own transition criterion.

Section 172(a)(1)(C) excludes the general classification paragraph where other provisions supply specific classifications; section 172(a)(2)(D) independently excludes the general date paragraph where other provisions supply specific dates. Section 181(b)(1)'s later-designation rule defeats an inference from the 1989 heading that Subpart 2 can never reach later designations. Conversely, the old one-hour design-value method, revised eight-hour standard, areas below the old threshold but above the new one, and dates that have passed prevent treating every old metric and date as a mechanically complete answer. The statute leaves room for lawful agency construction; it supplies no authority for wholesale abandonment.

**Operative implementation qualifications retained:** These describe the actual statutory alternatives and constraints; they are not findings that any particular area qualifies.

## Velazquez — merits and the independent conditional remedy disagreement

The comparator is verified: historically Kennedy, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirmed; Scalia's dissent was joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, and Thomas. **V0, common actual operation, eight:** Section 504(a)(16) contains the broad welfare-reform litigation/lobbying/rulemaking restriction, the permission to represent an eligible individual seeking specific agency relief, and the condition excluding efforts to amend or otherwise challenge law in effect when representation begins. The agency's reported symmetry concerns the broad reform ban: it funds neither challenges to nor defenses of existing reform law. It is not a concession that the individual-benefits condition is viewpoint neutral. The entity restriction reaches non-LSC as well as LSC funds.

The reported interpretation allows factual objections, misreading or misapplication of existing law, and agency-policy objections grounded in existing law. It excludes constitutional and statutory validity challenges, including state/federal conflict and facial or as-applied constitutional claims. If the forbidden issue arises, counsel must withdraw consistently with professional obligations. Counsel may, and must, explain why representation cannot continue, may discuss views of the law, and may refer the client elsewhere. Those permissions do not authorize continued prohibited representation. The reported interpretation also requires withdrawal when independent counsel enters the same case to present the validity issue; it does not allow the LSC attorney to remain on the other arguments. Affiliate organizations are legally permitted, but no actual available affiliate or replacement attorney is established. No actual impossibility of substitution is found either.

**V1, invalidity, five:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer conclude that the condition impermissibly restricts independent client advocacy inside the individual-benefits representation Congress authorized. The lawyer does not convey a government message; a validity argument may be the legal basis for the same individual relief. Allowing facts and misapplication arguments while barring validity grounds and terminating the entire representation does not preserve that advocacy. The broad ban's symmetry answers a different statutory component. Advice, referral, and theoretical organizational separation do not preserve representation of the excluded ground and cannot be presumed to supply actual counsel.

Rosenberger and Southworth support attention to independent expression within the supported function without making every subsidy a forum. Finley's narrow controlling rule approved nonconclusive selection criteria, not this categorical disability. Current Urofsky rejects attributing all professional expression to government merely because public resources support it, while preserving legitimate institutional control. Rust and Regan retain their actual program applications; the professional relationship alone does not decide the distinction, but this program's independent legal advocacy and the condition's argument-based operation do. Its interference with the court's ordinary receipt of legal arguments reinforces the speech holding; it is not a separate unlimited separation-of-powers rule or a right to government-funded counsel or reform litigation generally.

**V2, contrary merits, three:** O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas would uphold the condition as defining a restricted welfare-service undertaking and the participating recipient, rather than penalizing independent speech outside it. They reject a constitutional obligation to subsidize continuation once the case falls outside the defined service. Rust's private professional relationship supplies their counter to treating professional independence alone as dispositive; the five answer with the actual different representational function and operation.
