# Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly / Altadis U.S.A. Inc. v. Reilly

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In both consolidated matters, affirm the disposition sustaining the challenged physical access and sales practices; reverse the judgment sustaining covered cigarette advertising against preemption and the challenged noncigarette outdoor and point-of-sale advertising restrictions.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for conforming declaratory and injunctive relief and unresolved severability. Leave inadequately presented cigar-sampling questions undecided; prescribe no replacement radius or display design.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Cigarette advertising

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the preserved express-preemption question before the cigarette speech claims.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a smoking-health advertising location prohibition falls within the amended federal clause.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Cipollone v. Liggett Group’s predicate-duty method to this previously unresolved application, without creating cigarette-wide immunity.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state prohibition selecting cigarette advertising because of smoking-health concerns is a requirement or prohibition with respect to advertising even when its operative command concerns location. The federal clause preempts these cigarette advertising restrictions where the statutory federal-labeling condition is met.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The covered cigarette advertising restrictions. General zoning rules unconnected to smoking and health and independent restrictions on sales to minors are not displaced by this holding.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress used both requirements and prohibitions and identified advertising as the regulated activity. Treating every location ban as outside that language would exempt a direct prohibition while covering a lesser warning command. The actual duty and its basis, rather than a zoning label, control.

  The express preemption text is decisive, with Cipollone's attention to the predicate duty supplying the method of application. It resolves covered cigarette advertising before constitutional review because that statutory ground gives complete relief on that component, while leaving the separate noncigarette questions for their own analysis.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Speech and product access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved cigar and smokeless-tobacco advertising challenges and the product-access rules.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the geographic and height restrictions satisfy commercial-speech fit, and whether the separate physical-access restrictions validly advance age verification.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing commercial-speech test without creating a tobacco-specific tier.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The State’s supported interest in reducing youth tobacco use does not justify the 1,000-foot advertising prohibition’s substantial suppression of lawful adult communication without adequate proportional tailoring. The five-foot rule likewise lacks adequate justification responsive to visibility and child exposure. The challenged direct-access restrictions survive because interaction with a salesperson directly supports age verification while retaining practical adult inspection and lawful purchasing.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The particular outdoor and point-of-sale advertising restrictions and the challenged physical-access requirements. Supported narrower youth protections, deception restrictions, and lawful age limits remain available.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Evidence that advertising affects youth demand does not finish the fit inquiry. The geographic rule’s extensive coverage requires an explanation beyond the importance of its objective. The height line inadequately distinguishes youth exposure from adult access to information. Clerk-controlled products address access to the product itself through a functional sales safeguard.

  Central Hudson's proportional fit inquiry governs the advertising restrictions; no special tobacco tier is invented. The map coverage, design of the height line, practical adult communication, experience, and testimony concerning youth exposure all bear on fit, without requiring one exclusive statistical form of proof. Product access is separately assessed by the actual age-verification function of clerk control. This combination does not balance away truthful adult speech simply because the public-health objective is weighty.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Inadequately presented cigar-sampling questions are not decided. A replacement radius, different display arrangements, and unchallenged regulations are outside the presented record.

# Palazzolo v. Rhode Island

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the whole-parcel total-deprivation theory, reverse the finality and categorical acquisition barriers, and remand.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Adjudicate the contextual takings claim and properly preserved background-law defenses under the applicable standards. Award no compensation and direct no permit at this stage.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Finality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide whether the Council has finally identified the permitted uses.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether materially equivalent applications are required after definitive denial of the proposed class of private filling and identification of the remaining upland use.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency and Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council to the agency's actual determinations.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A landowner need not submit materially equivalent proposals when the agency has definitively excluded the requested class of use and the remaining allowable use is established. The denials here, the compelling-public-purpose condition, and the recognized upland residential use sufficiently define the regulatory burden. The unsubmitted subdivision does not defeat finality merely because it supplies a damages valuation rather than a permit proposal.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Finality of these agency determinations, without deciding value or compensation.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The private beach-club proposal cannot qualify under the stated exception merely by shrinking it. This does not excuse applications where density, design, or a genuine exception could materially change allowable use. Finality decides neither value nor the existence of a taking.

  Suitum's final-decision principle is applied to what this agency has actually ruled out. The rule prevents an endless application requirement once the government's position is definitive; it does not waive a materially useful application. That procedural resolution permits the compensation question to be heard without deciding it.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Applications in which density, design, or a genuinely available exception could materially change permitted use present facts outside this definitive-denial record.

### Issue 2: Successor ownership and compensation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the acquisition and whole-parcel deprivation issues after finding the claim sufficiently final.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether post-regulation acquisition alone bars a claim, and whether meaningful retained upland use defeats only the categorical theory.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council on total deprivation and announce a new, limited rejection of the categorical acquisition defense.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Transfer after regulation does not by itself convert the regulation into a background property limitation or extinguish a ripe compensation claim. Acquisition circumstances may inform reasonable expectations under Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, but do not replace its contextual inquiry. Because the entire parcel retains substantial residential development value, the categorical total-deprivation rule is unavailable on this record.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The post-regulation transfer and whole-parcel total-deprivation theories presented here. Notice remains relevant to the contextual claim, and no universal right to anticipated subdivision profits follows.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Constitutional compensation cannot depend entirely on whether an owner survives or can afford to retain land through the permitting process. Equally, a purchaser's notice and actual investment remain relevant evidence rather than legally irrelevant facts. The upland is more than a token interest, and its separation cannot be invented after the preserved case was litigated on the whole parcel.

  Lucas supplies a categorical rule for complete loss of economic use, and the substantial retained upland value defeats that particular premise. Penn Central supplies the distinct multi-factor inquiry for the remaining claim. Acquisition after regulation is relevant to reasonable expectations within that inquiry, rather than an automatic loss of constitutional protection. The takings source determines which test applies; this is neither a physical-occupation case nor an occasion to add that separate per se rule.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Contextual liability, valuation, and a supported background-law defense require determinations absent from this record; a wetlands-only denominator was not presented below or in the granted question.

# Tyler v. Cain

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening controlling law before June 28, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply any intervening controlling decision and the complete instruction and state adjudication on remand.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's rejection of the successive-application exception and remand for review of the district court's separate merits disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Treat the Supreme Court-retroactivity requirement of §2244(b)(2)(A) as satisfied for the confined Cage rule described below. Preserve independent verification of the remaining statutory conditions and review under the applicable §2254(d) restriction. Determine whether the complete charge actually violated the governing reasonable-doubt rule before applying Sullivan's structural-error consequence. Passing the gate grants neither an automatic writ nor a finding of innocence; any warranted conditional relief permits a lawful retrial or termination of the custody supported by the invalid conviction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Supreme Court holdings that jointly establish retroactivity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Review concerns the final judgment after appellate authorization, not review of the authorization order itself. Felker preserves independent district verification. The statutory meaning and the legal effect of the identified Supreme Court holdings must both be decided to determine whether Tyler's claim may proceed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Cage has been made retroactive within §2244(b)(2)(A), and whether the rule's relationship to the Court's collateral-review doctrine permits this successive claim to pass that requirement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve an open statutory question and adopt a new application of the combined-holdings rule. Reaffirm Felker's two-stage process and distinguish a conclusion compelled by Supreme Court holdings from a lower court's preferred extension. Do not claim that Sullivan expressly decided collateral retroactivity or that O'Dell directly decided Cage.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Supreme Court makes a new rule retroactive through an express holding or through holdings whose necessary legal propositions jointly compel that result. Teague's preserved watershed exception and Sullivan's specific holding about a charge that destroys the governing reasonable-doubt standard throughout the verdict establish retroactivity for that confined Cage rule. The required relationship rests on Sullivan's necessary conclusion that no constitutionally valid jury verdict remains, not on the label structural error alone. Tyler therefore cannot be turned away on the ground that this Court has not separately issued a sentence expressly declaring Cage retroactive.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This conclusion concerns the verdict-wide reasonable-doubt defect addressed in Cage and Sullivan. It does not make every instructional error, every due-process violation, or every structural rule retroactive. A court must identify the actual holdings and the necessary connection; persuasive analogy or a prediction of what the Supreme Court ought to decide is insufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress assigned the retroactivity determination to this Court, but did not require that its controlling propositions appear in one opinion. Sullivan's operative premise is more specific than ordinary structural classification: an instruction that vitiates the burden governing all guilt findings eliminates the constitutionally required jury determination. That premise establishes both the fundamental reliability defect and the loss of a bedrock adjudicative protection. Treating the same defect as too peripheral to satisfy Teague's watershed category would contradict those necessary propositions, not merely choose a different assessment of desirable procedure. O'Dell's independently supported watershed holding confirms that the exception protects an actual class of fundamental procedures; its parole-information facts do not themselves supply Cage's retroactivity.

  The contrary argument correctly separates harmlessness from retroactivity and points to Sullivan's express reservation of collateral gates. Stone respects that reservation: Sullivan did not adjudicate §2244(b)(2)(A), and today's statutory application is a new holding. But reserving a later question does not strip necessary earlier holdings of their legal consequences when that question arrives. His conclusion depends on the particular destruction of the reasonable-doubt verdict, not an equation of the two inquiries. The decisive method is reconciliation of the operative holdings within the enacted gate; the constitutional function of the jury's burden identifies why those holdings compel this result. The rule is stated for analogous claims because the statutory phrase must operate consistently, while retaining a demanding necessary-implication boundary that prevents lower courts from creating retroactivity through preference.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Tyler's full charge, the state court's actual analysis, any genuinely applicable remaining statutory condition, and the district court's separate §2254(d) disposition require review beyond the appellate gate ruling. A freestanding innocence or Suspension Clause challenge is not presented.

### Issue 2: Consequences for the requested retroactivity determination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The alternative request to make Cage retroactive is addressed through the preceding conclusion that the Court's operative holdings already supply the requisite legal consequence. No advisory declaration about an unidentified future application is needed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What further retroactivity ruling or merits remedy follows for this application after the statutory gate is satisfied.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the retroactivity conclusion while preserving the distinct merits and remedial requirements. This decision does not add a newly invented constitutional rule to the law governing the earlier state adjudication.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Cage's confined reasonable-doubt protection is available in this application under the statutory exception, but availability does not establish an actual instructional violation or overcome §2254(d) by itself. The merits inquiry must use Supreme Court law established at the legally relevant time. If a properly available claim establishes the verdict-wide defect, Sullivan forecloses harmless-error substitution for the missing valid verdict.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The constitutional claim authorized for this successive application. No other rejected claim, final judgment, or unrelated instructional theory is reopened by this ruling.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Access to a court and entitlement to release are different judgments. Requiring the actual statutory showing protects finality without using a redundant formal announcement to bar a fundamental protection already established through the Court's holdings. Requiring examination of the complete charge also avoids treating isolated words or an asserted similarity to Cage as a proven violation. A remand is required because the Fifth Circuit stopped at the gate and did not review the district court's distinct merits ruling under the corrected premise.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The appropriate conditional-writ terms arise only if the remaining legal and factual requirements are established on remand; no present finding resolves them.

# Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate both appellate judgments and remand for prompt application of a common purpose-bound detention standard to the current records.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require supervised release if the government cannot establish continuing statutory authority under that standard. Preserve lawful supervision and the removal orders. Do not authorize detention under an uninvoked alternative regime.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Habeas and continued detention

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Exercise review over the legal authority for custody without reopening removal merits.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether removal authority continues when removal lacks a significant prospect in the reasonably foreseeable future.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the preserved habeas and substantive-custody principles and announce a new construction of this detention provision.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Post-removal-period detention must remain reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. A final removal order, past criminality, or ongoing diplomatic effort alone does not authorize indefinite custody when removal lacks a significant likelihood in the reasonably foreseeable future. Habeas courts must independently examine the authority and the current factual prospect while giving appropriate weight to supported executive judgments about negotiations and practical arrangements. When continued custody is challenged after the removal period with concrete evidence of repeated refusals or comparable impediments, the government must identify facts supporting continued removal-purpose detention. A speculative possibility of eventual removal is insufficient.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Post-removal-period detention of the admitted residents presented here. The rule adopts no six-month safe harbor or mandatory deadline. Duration, progress, receiving-country decisions, and obstruction matter to individualized necessity. Relief is lawful supervision when custody authority ends, without cancellation of removal.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute's detention-and-supervision structure addresses carrying out removal. It does not clearly create a permanent preventive regime for everyone with a criminal removal order. Zadvydas's repeated receiving-country refusals require more than the assertion that removal is not impossible; Ma's lack of a repatriation agreement requires examination of realistic alternatives and negotiations. Individual danger may inform custody within a live removal process, but cannot preserve that source after its purpose ceases to support confinement.

  Detention requires a live substantive basis, not merely periodic paperwork. The prolonged detention and concrete removal obstacles require prompt examination: the detainee identifies the obstacle, and the Government must support a reasonably foreseeable removal prospect rather than recite ongoing negotiations. The detainee must have a meaningful opportunity to answer that account. Stone adopts a purpose-based limit without an automatic day-91 release or a universal six-month safe harbor because the operative question is whether custody can still serve lawful removal. If that authority is absent and no independent custody basis applies, supervised release must follow. Other preventive-detention regimes require their own authority and safeguards.

  Statutory purpose and structure carry decisive weight in construing the duration of this custody authority; substantive liberty and the avoidance of indefinite executive confinement reinforce the available reading. The standard uses a justified burden shift once concrete removal obstacles are shown, rather than demanding that a detained person prove diplomatic impossibility. Country refusals, communications, travel-document history, the detainee's conduct, and other reliable information can establish the actual prospect. A universal six-month safe harbor could authorize custody after its purpose has already failed, while a day-91 deadline could disregard a genuinely imminent removal. The purpose-based rule therefore governs future cases without making a calendar replace the statutory justification.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Previously unadmitted entrants, distinct national-security authorities, and genuinely independent civil-commitment regimes are outside the presented statutory custody questions.
