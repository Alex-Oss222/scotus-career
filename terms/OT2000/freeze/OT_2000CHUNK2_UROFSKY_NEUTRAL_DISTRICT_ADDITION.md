# OT2000 chunk 2 - Urofsky neutral district-source addition

## Source and status

The complete February 26, 1998 district opinion, Urofsky v. Allen, 995 F. Supp. 634, including all eight footnotes and the order, has been read from the saved primary text. Its text and source-page hashes match the accompanying source metadata. The court-issued en banc opinion was used for bounded cross-checks of the statutory amendment, absence of approval denials, complaint allegations, and university administration. The district opinion describes the record on cross-motions for summary judgment. The underlying statements of undisputed facts, declarations, deposition transcripts, and exhibits have not been independently read here.

This addition supplements the released neutral materials without changing their bytes. It provides a more precise account of the reported evidence. A reference to plaintiffs' Statement of Undisputed Facts identifies a source submitted under that label; the label alone is not proof that the Commonwealth conceded every proposition. The district court's legal conclusions, evaluative inferences, and assessment of statutory validity remain lower-court reasoning, not new factual findings or law of this Court.

## The operative statutory version remains distinct

The district court reviewed the original definition in the 1998 Code supplement. The official en banc opinion, pages 4-5, expressly identifies the later 1999 amendment adding the dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements. The amended definition is the one presented for the February 20, 2001 event. The district opinion therefore cannot establish that every example it discussed remains covered by the amended definition.

The source adds historical evidence of operation and asserted burdens; it does not adjudicate whether each specific image, assignment, research subject, or database item satisfies the amended content test. Nor does the amendment itself establish that every asserted present burden disappeared. Preserve both the actual narrowing and the remaining inquiry into covered protected activity and present injury. The approval provision's distinct research-project and other-undertaking branches, written approval, public availability, and State Police exclusion remain as supplied in the existing neutral materials.

## Smith's website: blocking, partial restoration, and complaints

At page 639 the district opinion describes Smith's site on a state-university computer, his explanation that the images formed part of a discussion of censorship, pornography, and commercial control of the Internet, and complaints by several students and at least one faculty member. It identifies defendant's exhibit 1 and its attachment, pages 118-132 for the complaints, and plaintiffs' exhibit 1 at pages 137-138 and 178-179 for the resulting discussion. The source describes vice-provost John O'Connor initially blocking access to several images and later restoring some, citing plaintiffs' exhibit 1 at pages 123-126 and 170-173.

These are specific published recitations of the summary-judgment materials, not just an unelaborated complaint allegation that the site was censored. They establish neither an adjudication that the blocking was unconstitutional nor a denial of a request under the statutory approval procedure. The en banc opinion, pages 5-6, states that none of the six plaintiffs requested permission under the Act and that the record indicates no access request was declined. Preserve both accounts; they describe different actions. The source does not establish full restoration of every image, a presently continuing block, or whether particular images satisfy the amended definition.

District note 6 reports that no harassment or hostile-work-environment claim had been filed concerning the site and describes its continued availability tentatively. That is an account at the time of the district decision, not a guarantee about later filings or operation. The district judge's accompanying conclusion about the viability of a harassment claim is legal reasoning. Complaints, offense, discussion, and blocking do not themselves constitute an adjudicated harassment violation.

## Approval practices and their limits

Page 642 reports minimal paperwork and prompt approvals in identified examples: defendant's exhibit 10 records approval within twenty-four hours for a censorship project; plaintiffs' exhibit 36 records a two-sentence request concerning ancient art approved within forty-eight hours. Those examples are relevant operational evidence, not a mandatory decision deadline or proof that every future application would be approved promptly.

The opinion reports requests at three of thirty-nine higher-education institutions and at three state agencies, citing plaintiffs' Statement of Undisputed Facts paragraphs 145 and 147. Note 7 names Radford University, the University of Virginia, and the College of William and Mary and identifies particular requests in paragraphs 155-160. The three agencies reported are Education, the Virginia Museum of Fine Arts, and the Attorney General's office. These are historical reports of requests, not a complete census of compliant uses, suppressed projects, or statutory burdens. Low request counts alone establish none of those competing explanations.

Pages 642-643 also identify broad advance permissions. Plaintiffs' paragraphs 149-154 and Cantrell deposition exhibit 4 describe permission covering 8,300 employees at the University of Virginia's Health Sciences Center for material connected with or necessary to their duties. McClure deposition exhibit 14 describes comparable permission for information-technology employees, and deposition page 61 gives McClure's workload explanation for using blanket approval. The district judge considered those formulations broader than the statutory required-access wording; that construction and the judge's conclusions about workability, deterrence, or institutional disregard remain legal or evaluative conclusions. The factual existence and stated scope of the permissions can be considered without adopting those conclusions or assuming their current continuation.

The district account of the machine-shop supervisor's request must be read with the en banc majority's note 4: the administration determined that advance approval was unnecessary for the disability inquiry. It is not a denied research request. Similarly, the en banc account of academic delegation is an administrative description, not statutory language mandating peer review in every agency.

## Commonwealth's evidence and existing responses

At page 639 the district opinion identifies five nonwork incidents offered by the Commonwealth, defendant's exhibits 3-7. Its examples include a coworker's complaint about sexual images, a student's complaint after seeing a nude image while seeking class assistance, an allegation concerning a visiting professor's stored child pornography followed by resignation and referral to a prosecutor, and removal of material from state computers. These are reported incidents and responses, not findings of criminal guilt, five harassment judgments, or misconduct by each plaintiff. Smith's disputed scholarly website is discussed separately through exhibit 1 and must not silently become a sixth conceded nonwork incident.

Page 643 identifies existing personnel and institutional computer-use policies, including plaintiffs' exhibits 18-19, paragraphs 136-137, and exhibits 17, 31, and 32. The opinion says two of the reported incidents predated the Act and appeared to have been resolved under existing policies; it also notes the reporting route shown by defendant's exhibit 4. It does not establish that every incident was fully resolved, that the preexisting rules prevent every future harm, or that the Act necessarily adds no protection. The relative adequacy of those alternatives remains an issue for legal appraisal, not a supplied finding of this Court.

## Readiness

The old release and this addition support continued independent modeling. The source supplies material clarification on both sides and requires reconsideration of any premise that the record contains only complaint allegations, no concrete workplace incidents, no prompt approvals, or an actual denial of one of these plaintiffs' statutory requests. It supplies no outcome and no new adjudication of the disputed constitutional interest. No original-file absence is treated as no proof, waiver, default, or automatic need for new proceedings.

The model must use the amended definition, retain the evidentiary status and temporal limits above, and incorporate the addition before final commitments. The separate intervening-law review records the eight completed public-law entries checked, including Kansas. Denius still supplies no holding. Any further applicable material law requires the ordinary affected-stage refresh before assembly; no same-day precedence is inferred from processing order.
