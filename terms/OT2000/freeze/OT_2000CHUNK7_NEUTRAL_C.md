# Alabama v. Bozeman

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-11. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Alabama v. Bozeman. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-492; 533 U.S. 146. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of dismissal required by the Interstate Agreement on Detainers. **Simulated Event Date:** June 11, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Faithful formulation: Whether Article IV(e)'s requirement that pending charges be dismissed with prejudice when a prisoner is returned to the original place of imprisonment before trial admits an exception for a brief, allegedly technical violation that does not impair rehabilitation. Exact petition wording was not independently retrieved.

**Question Granted:** The preceding text faithfully formulates the issue reviewed; it is not a verified transcription of the petition.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Alabama Supreme Court reversed Bozeman's convictions because Alabama had returned him to federal prison before trial after obtaining his presence under the agreement. **Relief Requested:** Alabama seeks reinstatement of the conviction or rejection of mandatory dismissal; Bozeman seeks affirmance of the dismissal requirement.

**Material Record:** Bozeman was serving a federal drug sentence in Marianna, Florida. Alabama lodged a detainer for state firearm-discharge charges and obtained temporary custody. He arrived January 23, 1997, spent the night in the county jail, was arraigned and appointed counsel on January 24, and was returned to federal prison the same day. Alabama brought him back for trial about a month later. The trial court rejected his timely motion to dismiss under Article IV(e), and he was convicted. The intermediate appellate court affirmed; the Alabama Supreme Court applied the agreement's dismissal language. The record does not establish that Bozeman requested or knowingly consented to the premature return. [Alabama v. Bozeman](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-146.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is direct review of a preserved statutory objection, not a federal collateral attack demanding a fundamental miscarriage of justice. The United States was the sending sovereign, Alabama the receiving jurisdiction. Special federal receiving-jurisdiction remedial provisions cannot be applied merely because the prisoner originally came from federal custody. The parties' characterization of the trip as beneficial or harmless does not establish waiver. **Relevant Dates:** Transfer and return January 23-24, 1997; argument April 17, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate listed companion. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 11, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No identified intervening decision resolves this Agreement provision, its remedy, or waiver. Any intervening controlling decision on statutory interpretation or interstate agreements must be incorporated before adjudication.

### Issue 1: Antishuttling command and remedy

**Controlling Simulated Law:** New York v. Hill enforces an authorized counsel's unqualified express agreement to a specified trial date beyond the Article III period, absent timely objection, conflict, or lack of authority. It does not establish a general waiver of every compact protection or decide premature return under Article IV(e). Reed v. Farley imposes a distinct fundamental-defect threshold for collateral relief on an assumed statutory violation; it neither creates a direct-review prejudice requirement nor holds that every IAD breach is nonremediable.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The agreement standard distinguishes its trial deadlines, express scheduling agreement, good-cause continuances, and inability-to-stand-trial provisions. An actual violation subject to the agreement's dismissal command does not require additional bad faith or impaired defense. The collateral-review threshold must stay in its own procedural channel.

**Other Governing Law:** Article IV(e) provides that if trial is not had before return to the original place of imprisonment, the indictment, information, or complaint shall be of no further force or effect and shall be dismissed with prejudice. Article IV(c) separately gives a trial period after arrival; Article IX directs construction that effectuates the agreement's purposes. Congress adopted special provisions for cases in which the United States is the receiving jurisdiction; that is not this transfer.

**Material Other Authority:** The agreement's rehabilitative and administrative purposes explain why the states adopted its concrete rules. They do not supply a free-standing power to replace mandatory text with an individualized harm inquiry.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** New York v. Hill permits the specific express scheduling agreement it decided. Reed v. Farley governs a different remedy and forum. Neither resolves whether Article IV(e) contains an unwritten short-trip exception.

**Open Point:** The exact antishuttling application and any valid waiver require decision under the enacted language. No identified intervening decision alters the governing Agreement rules.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Alabama's Position:** The brief return was technical, interrupted rehabilitation less than continued detention would, and caused no prejudice. Literal dismissal imposes a disproportionate consequence where the agreement's practical objective was furthered rather than frustrated.

**Bozeman's Position:** Article IV(e) explicitly attaches dismissal with prejudice to return before trial and contains no one-day, technical, or harmlessness exception. A later timely trial cannot repair the specified violation. An opportunity to waive beneficial protections does not mean that the state may presume waiver.

**Reasoning Below and Adversarial Tension:** The Alabama Supreme Court followed the mandatory words. Alabama and the federal government emphasize rehabilitative purpose and efficient handling; the opposing response is that the compact embodies that purpose through an administrable return rule, and its drafters expressly created exceptions or special remedies where intended.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Available Merits Paths:** Affirm because the premature return triggers Article IV(e); identify a textually grounded exception actually covering this transfer; or remand a genuinely preserved, supported waiver issue. A de minimis exception would require a justified interpretive basis beyond judicial preference for the result. It cannot be derived from New York v. Hill's distinct scheduling agreement or Reed v. Farley's collateral threshold.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** An actual unwaived violation requires dismissal with prejudice of the covered charges. The receiving-state command does not authorize the federal discretionary remedy. No independent constitutional ruling, injury finding, or inquiry into defense impairment is necessary to apply the compact. A new waiver theory cannot be assumed from Bozeman's silence or from the state's judgment about his interests.

# Wood v. Meadows

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wood v. Meadows.
* **Citation or Docket:** 207 F.3d 708; Fourth Circuit No. 99-1069; district court No. CA-94-47-D.
* **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision on the accepted constitutional question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 11, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the First and Fourteenth Amendments permit Virginia to require independent United States Senate candidates to submit their ballot-access petitions on the date of the major-party primaries, approximately 150 days before the general election?
* **Question Granted:** Review encompasses the question presented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Virginia's election official following remand for application of the contextual election-burden inquiry.
* **Relief Requested:** Wood seeks reversal of the judgment sustaining the filing deadline and declaratory or prospective relief against its enforcement. The completed 1994 election cannot be reopened through ordinary prospective ballot-access relief.

**Material Record:** Wood sought a place on Virginia's November 1994 United States Senate ballot as an independent and was excluded for failure to comply with the filing requirements. Independent candidates had to file by the second Tuesday in June, when party primaries occurred. For that election, statewide candidates needed signatures of one-half of one percent of Virginia's registered voters, 14,865 signatures, including at least 200 from each congressional district. Signatures could be gathered beginning January 1. A signer could sign multiple candidates' petitions and vote in a party primary. Independents were not required to demonstrate previous nonaffiliation with a party.

Primary candidates faced the same signature requirement but had to file 60 days before the primary. Parties using another nomination method also had to complete nomination by the June date. Thus equal general-election nomination timing did not mean identical petition filing dates. A 1998 amendment substituted 10,000 signatures, including 400 per congressional district. That amendment must not be substituted for the historical requirements governing Wood's exclusion. Wood presented evidence that officials needed approximately 30 days to process petitions, and argued that administrative necessity therefore could not justify a 150-day deadline. The published record does not establish that every component of the revised signature requirement was independently litigated. [Wood v. Meadows](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/207/207.F3d.708.-94-47-.99-1069.html).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The original district-court judgment for Wood was vacated on an earlier appeal; after further proceedings the district court entered judgment for the Commonwealth. The current appeal concerns that later judgment. Continuing prospective relief requires a live controversy despite the completed election and intervening amendment. The lower court adjudicated the continuing deadline challenge, but a different finding concerning Wood's future candidacy or recurrence would require examination of the standing record. The accepted merits question does not establish standing by itself. Review concerns the deadline within its surrounding access system, not an independent claim invalidating every signature rule.

**Relevant Dates:** The challenged exclusion occurred in 1994; the judgment under review was entered March 29, 2000, before the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No independently listed companion.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 11, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Apply any earlier controlling election-law decision, including Cook v. Gralike, Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District, and Chen v. City of Houston, only to the extent its holding bears on this ballot-access question.

### Issue 1: Timing within the complete ballot-access system

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Burdick v. Takushi requires examination of the character and magnitude of the actual injury, the State's precise interests, and the extent to which those interests justify the burden. Its five-Justice controlling opinion applies compelling-interest narrow tailoring to severe burdens and proportionate important regulatory interests to reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens. Its approval of Hawaii's write-in restriction is system-specific. Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party likewise sustains the particular nomination restriction within its complete system through five controlling joins; it adopts neither pure rational-basis review nor a universal license to restrict alternatives. Norman v. Reed protects actual access while permitting support requirements appropriate to the electorate.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The election-access standards retain the calibrated inquiry in Anderson v. Celebrezze. Meaningful examination of interest and fit remains necessary even when strict scrutiny is inappropriate. A rule's calendar label, nondiscriminatory wording, or administrative purpose alone does not establish its validity. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every preference emerging afterward.

**Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern the asserted rights of candidate association and electoral choice. The relevant Virginia provisions govern nomination timing, signatures, and primary participation as described in the judicial record. Anderson v. Celebrezze supplies the inherited contextual framework; its presidential-election setting and substantially earlier deadline are material distinctions, not categorical answers for Senate elections.

**Material Other Authority:** Anderson v. Celebrezze's presidential-election setting and substantially earlier deadline matter to the analogy. Burdick v. Takushi and Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party require examination of the complete electoral system rather than transfer of an approved feature without its supporting context.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The calibrated burden-and-justification framework is settled. Applying it to this Senate petition deadline requires evaluation of the available nomination routes, realistic collection opportunity, primary participation, and the State's actual timing interests.

**Open Point:** No entering rule makes a deadline valid merely because it coincides with a primary, or invalid whenever it precedes the latest mechanically possible processing date. The constitutionality of this deadline in the demonstrated Virginia system remains an application for decision.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Fourth Circuit evaluated the entire system and found a reasonable, nondiscriminatory burden: signatures could be collected for more than five months, including spring; independent signers retained primary participation; independents had 60 additional days after primary candidates filed; and the deadline established the general-election field when party nominees were selected. It accepted verification, demonstrated support, ballot management, and a known candidate field as legitimate interests. It rejected Wood's argument that proof of a shorter processing period compelled a later deadline. Its further suggestion that the extent-of-necessity inquiry matters only for unreasonable or discriminatory burdens must be tested against the simulated Court's continuing requirement of proportionate fit.

**Wood's Strongest Supported Position:** Independent candidacies often respond to the nominees and positions produced by primaries. A deadline on primary day prevents that response and can burden unaffiliated voters differently from party participants, regardless of facial symmetry. A State cannot justify five months of exclusion merely by saying that earlier closure produces an earlier known field. Wood asks for effective review of the actual justification, emphasizing the evidence that petition verification required much less time. His strongest claim does not require an absolute entitlement to await every later political development.

**Meadows's Strongest Supported Position:** Virginia supplies a practical, reasonably open route to independent nomination and chooses a common date for establishing the general-election field. Administrative verification is only one interest; orderly nomination, demonstrated support, and a defined period for voter assessment also matter. The Constitution does not compel the last technically possible filing date whenever an earlier reasonable date is available. The State must nevertheless defend electoral administration rather than incumbent protection or suppression of independent competition. No distinct Solicitor General position is established by the record.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Paths:** If prospective standing is genuinely absent under the actual record, vacatur and appropriate jurisdictional proceedings could precede merits adjudication. The elapsed election alone does not resolve recurrence. The signature amendment does not automatically moot a surviving challenge to the unchanged deadline.

**Merits and Holding Scope:** An affirmance could hold that this June deadline is justified within the demonstrated access system while rejecting any categorical immunity for primary-day deadlines. Such a rule would require renewed analysis for substantially higher signature barriers, restrictive signer rules, or much earlier primaries. A reversal could hold that the demonstrated impairment of independent response is not justified by the State's asserted interests on this record. A narrower vacatur could require the lower court to conduct meaningful proportionality review after correcting its statement that fit is irrelevant whenever a burden is classified as reasonable.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance leaves the existing summary judgment and deadline in place. Reversal supporting prospective relief would require a declaration and an injunction directed to the unconstitutional deadline, with implementation allowing Virginia to adopt a lawful replacement; it would not place Wood on a completed ballot or judicially establish a universal 30-day deadline. A remand for factual or fit analysis must specify the governing standard and preserve lawful surrounding requirements. The strength of Wood's demonstrated practical burden and the current standing record are the principal uncertainties.

# Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation.

**Citation or Docket:** 204 F.3d 1125; D.C. Circuit No. 99-1230. The agency proceeding was FDIC 95-65e. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; decision on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** June 15, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar.

**Question Presented:**

1. Whether an administrative law judge who conducts evidentiary hearings and issues recommended decisions in Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation enforcement proceedings is an inferior officer subject to the Appointments Clause, although the Board retains final decision-making authority?
2. Whether the Board's de novo review of the administrative record cures an unconstitutional appointment of the administrative law judge who conducted the hearing?

**Question Granted:** Both questions presented. The grant concerns appointment status and the effect of the Board's review, rather than independent review of every banking, disclosure, or evidentiary ruling in the administrative proceeding.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit denied Landry's petition for review of the FDIC Board's removal and prohibition order. Its opinion treated the hearing ALJ as an employee because he could never issue the agency's final decision. A separate opinion considered him an inferior officer but would sustain the agency's order because the Board's de novo review eliminated prejudice. [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

**Relief Requested:** Landry seeks to set aside the adverse order obtained through the challenged adjudication and obtain proceedings conducted by a constitutionally appointed adjudicator. The FDIC seeks affirmance, either because the ALJ was an employee or because the Board's independent decision cured any defect.

**Material Record:** Landry was senior vice president, chief financial officer, and cashier of First Guaranty Bank in Hammond, Louisiana. The FDIC charged him with misconduct connected to efforts to recapitalize the financially troubled bank through Pangaea, a corporation that Landry and two associates formed. The agency found that the participants concealed their personal interests while using bank resources in speculative capital-raising arrangements. Landry disputed his degree of responsibility, the characterization of transactions, and the adequacy of the evidence supporting removal. His own correspondence, resignation letter, and deposition supplied substantial evidence relied upon by the agency. Those agency findings are record facts about the challenged decision, not independent findings of this Court.

The ALJ conducted a two-week formal hearing, ruled on evidence and discovery, and recommended removal and prohibition from further participation in insured depository institutions. Landry filed exceptions. The Board reviewed the matter de novo, adopted material findings and recommendations, resolved further disclosure disputes, and issued its final order. The Board's statutory responsibility to find facts independently did not eliminate the hearing record the ALJ had created. [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The appointment objection was raised in the agency proceedings and pursued on direct review. This is not a collateral attack on a long-final order. The FDIC had defended the appointment before its Board on the premise that the relevant banking agencies could exercise departmental appointment authority. In the court of appeals it abandoned the argument that the ALJ had been appointed by a department head and defended employee status instead. The separate opinion treated that abandonment as waiver. The record describes hiring by the Office of Thrift Supervision and assignment through the Office of Financial Institution Adjudication. It does not establish an alternative presidential, judicial, or department-head appointment that this Court may assume into existence.

The agency preserved its contention that de novo Board review and absence of prejudice defeated relief. The Court therefore faces a cure argument that the governing appointment-remedy precedent expressly left open in other review structures. Appointments classification and remedy should be resolved separately; finding a statutory office does not itself establish who validly appointed its occupant.

**Relevant Dates:** FDIC notice, April 30, 1996; Board order, May 25, 1999; appellate argument, November 19, 1999; judgment under review, March 3, 2000; assigned decision, June 15, 2001. The lower judgment predates the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Another bank participant's separate petition does not receive a brief or disposition here. United States v. Hilario is an earlier assigned same-term matter potentially relevant to inferior-officer classification and permissible appointing authorities. No controlling disposition is identified.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 15, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier controlling decision in United States v. Hilario must be applied within its scope. United States v. Mead Corp. is decided later and cannot govern this adjudication.

### Issue 1: Officer Status and Appointment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Edmond v. United States recognizes significant adjudicative duties and uses the actual statutory hierarchy to distinguish inferior from principal officers. Direction, assignment control, removal from judicial assignments, and superior review collectively established inferior status there. Neither review nor removal alone is a universal classification rule. Its unanimous statutory holding also distinguishes appointment from assignment: the authorized Secretary's appointment supplied the lawful act; administrative assignment did not substitute for it.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Edmond v. United States requires assessment of the statutory structure as a whole. Adjudicative independence does not automatically create a principal officer, and superior review does not automatically reduce an adjudicator to a mere employee. The civilian military appellate office remains the decided application.

**Other Governing Law:** The Appointments Clause identifies permissible methods of appointing officers. The statutory and regulatory scheme establishes continuing ALJ offices, prescribes their duties and compensation, authorizes formal hearings, and reserves the FDIC's final decision to its Board. The relevant provisions and their operation are reproduced and discussed in the [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

**Material Other Authority:** Freytag v. Commissioner and Buckley v. Valeo address the constitutional requirements for appointing officers. The lower court's disagreement concerns whether significant hearing powers suffice or whether the possession of final decisional authority is indispensable. The lower majority and separate opinion supply competing readings, and do not themselves establish controlling Supreme Court law.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Edmond v. United States prevents collapsing officer status into the question whether an adjudicator is subordinate. It does not itself decide the status of every administrative employee who assists a decisionmaker.

**Open Point:** Whether the continuing, discretionary powers exercised by this hearing ALJ make him an officer despite complete Board review, and the consequences of the FDIC's abandoned proper-appointment defense.

### Issue 2: Cure and Direct-Review Relief

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Ryder v. United States requires lawful fresh adjudication for a timely direct challenge to a substantial constitutional defect in adjudicators' appointments and rejects the de facto officer doctrine as a defense to that claimant. Its accompanying holding rejects the particular higher review there because it did not provide the displaced proceeding's factual weighing, credibility decisions, contested-fact resolution, and sentence review.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ryder v. United States reserves the availability of harmless-error review and cure in a different appellate structure. It also excludes automatic dismissal of underlying charges and universal collateral reopening.

**Other Governing Law:** The APA's prejudicial-error instruction and the FDIC's duty to make its own findings support the agency's cure argument. They must operate consistently with the constitutional requirement rather than determine its existence. [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

**Material Other Authority:** The lower majority regarded the demand for a causal link between appointment error and outcome as especially problematic where the alleged officer's only output was a recommendation. The separate opinion distinguished declaring the defect from granting relief and regarded future prehearing challenges as adequate enforcement.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The timely-direct-review rule governs. Whether review of an ALJ-created record supplies the constitutionally missing adjudication is an application and extension question. Neither automatic cure nor automatic failure of every imaginable cure is already established.

**Open Point:** Whether genuinely independent review of this existing record is sufficient when the initial adjudicator controlled evidence, hearing procedure, and the creation of that record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The majority accepted that the ALJ's office and duties were prescribed by law and that he exercised substantial discretion. It nevertheless considered the absence of any power to enter a final FDIC decision decisive. It rejected the agency's preliminary suggestion that lack of demonstrated prejudice foreclosed consideration of the structural objection. The separate opinion rejected the finality distinction, classified the ALJ as an inferior officer, accepted waiver of the valid-appointment defense, and would apply harmless-error principles because the Board independently reviewed the case and the evidence supported its order.

**Landry's Strongest Position:** A government adjudicator exercises public authority when he conducts the required hearing, compels compliance, decides what evidence enters the record, and makes findings that frame later review. Final decisionmaking is unnecessary to officer status; many inferior officers operate under superior supervision. If those functions require a valid appointment, review of their product cannot make the appointment requirement disappear in every such proceeding. A claimant who timely objected should receive a lawful hearing without proving how a hypothetical properly appointed officer would have ruled.

**FDIC's Strongest Position:** The Board remains legally responsible for the order, exercises independent factfinding power, and can reject the recommendation or reopen the record. The ALJ prepares a record and recommendation without final coercive authority. Alternatively, the Clause can be enforced by declaring officer status while preserving a substantively sound order independently entered by lawful Board members. Repeating a lengthy hearing where the critical proof comes from Landry's own admissions would impose cost without correcting a demonstrated decisional error.

**General Rules Requested and Necessary Concessions:** Landry seeks officer status for continuing statutory adjudicators with significant discretionary hearing powers and a fresh hearing on timely challenge. He must concede that ordinary staff advice, ministerial processing, and every temporary assistance arrangement do not necessarily constitute offices. The FDIC seeks a final-authority limitation or, alternatively, a meaningful de novo cure rule. It must concede that assignment cannot replace constitutionally required appointment and that nominal review is not automatically an adequate substitute for a lawful evidentiary proceeding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide classification first. Employee status would permit affirmance without resolving cure. Officer status requires addressing appointment validity, including the abandoned defense, before reaching the preserved cure argument. No determination of the bank misconduct merits is necessary to decide either granted question.

**Available Merits Paths:** The Court could affirm on employee status; classify the ALJ as an officer but affirm under a bounded de novo cure rule; or reject cure and require a fresh lawful hearing. A remand limited to an unresolved appointment fact is possible only if a genuine, preserved validity question remains, rather than an invented defense the agency relinquished.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** A new-hearing disposition vacates the judgment sustaining the order and returns the matter for proceedings before a validly appointed adjudicator and a fresh Board decision. It does not dismiss the enforcement case, exonerate Landry, resolve unrelated privilege issues, or reopen other parties' final orders. The agency may pursue the same charges through lawful procedures.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** This continuing statutory hearing office, this timely direct challenge, and Board review of the existing record. An actual new evidentiary hearing before a lawful decisionmaker is analytically different from reconsideration of a record created through the challenged proceeding.

**Material Uncertainty:** The original appointment instrument and complete administrative record are unavailable. The appellate account establishes the agency's litigation position and the functions exercised. Do not invent a department-head appointment, an unraised objection, or a finding that no procedural choice could have influenced the record.

**Authorities:** Edmond v. United States; Ryder v. United States; Freytag v. Commissioner; Buckley v. Valeo.

# Saucier v. Katz

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Saucier v. Katz. **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1977; 533 U.S. 194. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of an interlocutory qualified-immunity ruling in a federal-officer excessive-force action. **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** The petition presents two questions: whether the Fourth Amendment excessive-force reasonableness test and qualified-immunity test are identical, so that unreasonable force necessarily defeats immunity; and whether Saucier should receive immunity for the carrying and shove described in the petition on the facts available at this stage, including the asserted absence of injury or pain. This paraphrases the verified petition questions without adopting the petitioner's factual characterization as a finding.

**Question Granted:** Both petition questions are verified in the Government's petition and correspond to the reviewed legal distinction and its application to Saucier. No separate grant-order reformulation has been verified. [Petition for certiorari](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/saucier-v-katz-petition).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of qualified immunity to Saucier, reasoning that under Graham v. Connor the reasonableness issues substantially merged. **Relief Requested:** Saucier seeks reversal and judgment on immunity; Katz seeks preservation of his damages action and factual adjudication.

**Material Record:** At a 1994 ceremony concerning transfer of the Presidio to national-park use, Vice President Gore was present. Katz, an animal-rights demonstrator, carried a concealed banner and moved toward a barrier before opening it. Military police officers Saucier and Parker removed him, holding his arms from behind. Katz described being partly dragged with his feet barely reaching the ground; he wore a leg brace that Saucier did not recall seeing. Katz alleges a shove or throw into a van and disputed resistance; Saucier's personal role and the degree and necessity of force must be assessed on supported facts. Katz caught himself and suffered no physical injury. He was briefly detained and released. The district court dismissed other claims or defendants but retained the excessive-force claim against Saucier because material facts were disputed. [Saucier v. Katz](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-194.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Interlocutory review may decide the legal distinction between immunity and the merits on facts assumed for Katz. It cannot resolve whose version of resistance, knowledge, or force is true. A defendant's lack of personal participation is distinct from a defense excusing a proved violation. The continued availability of the existing federal-officer remedial channel is not independently challenged in the described grant. **Relevant Dates:** The alleged conduct occurred in 1994, fixing the immunity notice date; argument March 20, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** Any intervening controlling Fourth Amendment decision must be applied. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Denius v. Dunlap and Sterling v. Borough of Minersville concern personal immunity in distinct constitutional settings; their assigned dates precede this event, but no controlling disposition is identified. Illinois v. McArthur, Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, and Arkansas v. Sullivan could bear on distinct Fourth Amendment propositions to the extent their operative holdings address those propositions. Wallis v. Spencer concerns municipal rather than personal liability. Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated into the immunity and substantive-force analyses.

### Issue 1: Fourth Amendment force and personal immunity

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Plakas v. Drinski applies Graham v. Connor to the complete supported circumstances, including actual threats and reasonable factual perceptions, without requiring the least dangerous possible tactic. Hunter v. Bryant requires lawful summary-judgment treatment of favorable supported facts and rejects choosing a more favorable competing account for the officer. Elder v. Holloway requires consideration of all relevant pre-existing law when deciding clearly established rights. Wilson v. Layne distinguishes constitutional violation from conduct-date qualified immunity. These holdings retain the Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton notice framework; they do not replace it with a reliance-only defense.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The collateral-appeal standard permits separable legal review on assumed facts, not reconsideration of factual sufficiency. The operative immunity standard asks whether the particular unlawfulness was sufficiently established, without an identical-case requirement or a mechanical count of precedents.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment governs force during the seizure. The existing federal-officer damages channel and ordinary proof of personal responsibility, causation, and loss remain distinct from immunity.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Lanier addresses criminal fair warning with particularized clarity, possible obvious applications of general law, and distinct criminal elements. It does not authorize importing criminal willfulness into a civil excessive-force claim. Conn v. Gabbert does not establish a mandatory merits-first sequence for every immunity appeal.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The substantive rule already accommodates reasonable factual mistakes. Existing immunity nevertheless protects certain reasonable legal mistakes; the Ninth Circuit cannot erase that separate inquiry by equating two uses of reasonableness. No controlling precedent prescribes a categorical sequence governing all cases.

**Open Point:** Application to the assumed facts, the appropriate decisional sequence here, and whether existing personal-immunity doctrine should be retained or expressly revised remain for decision. Any intervening controlling rule must be incorporated before adjudication.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Saucier's Position:** Security surrounding the Vice President, Katz's movement and banner, and the rapid removal required objectively reasonable action. Even if force crossed the constitutional line, existing law did not sufficiently notify this officer that the specific conduct was forbidden. The appeals court collapsed a separate legal protection into the merits.

**Katz's Position:** A supported account of unnecessary force against a restrained, physically limited demonstrator can establish a violation under settled law; public security does not authorize every method of removal. Factual disputes about resistance and the shove prevent final judgment. A broadly worded immunity claim must not permit judicial credibility selection.

**Reasoning Below and Contested Premises:** The Ninth Circuit treated general force doctrine as largely disposing of both inquiries. That legal approach can be reviewed independently of its recognition of factual disputes. Rejecting merger does not itself establish that Saucier actually used reasonable force or that the particular unlawfulness was uncertain in 1994.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Available Merits Paths:** Reject merger and grant immunity if even Katz's supported account lacked sufficiently established unlawfulness; reject merger but remand application under the correct distinct standard; resolve that the assumed conduct was constitutionally permissible, making immunity unnecessary; or openly revise the defense and provide a fair procedure for its application. The Court may not resolve disputed resistance or participation as a shortcut to any result.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** A legal immunity judgment bars the covered individual damages claim without validating all challenged conduct or deciding unrelated relief. A remand leaves liability, causation, and damages to their lawful factfinder. Absence of physical injury is relevant to context and damages but is not an automatic constitutional license. **Reasoning Below:** The Federal Circuit treated ruling letters as different from regulations because they lacked public procedures and equivalent general legal force. **Mead's Position:** The power to classify particular imports does not make every classification letter a lawmaking act binding a reviewing court. The planner's removable pages also resist the chosen ordinary meaning of bound diary.

**Limiting Principles and Alternative Grounds:** The Government's view requires a legally grounded delegation, not merely expertise or administrative convenience. Mead's objection to binding force does not answer whether the agency offers useful reasons. The Court can resolve the standard and remand application without selecting the tariff category itself.
