## Garrett — No. 99-1240

### Components and supported paths

**G1 — authorization.** All eight accept that section 12202 expressly removes State immunity for chapter violations and makes legal and equitable remedies available on the same terms as against other public or private entities. Section 12117 supplies the specified employment-enforcement channels. The direct language resolves clarity, unlike the disputed incorporation in Kimel; it establishes neither power, actual consent, liability nor an award.

**G2 — Article I.** The affirmative route applies Florida Prepaid and Kimel. Covered paid employment and the terms of hiring, retention and compensation form an economic class participating in actual labor markets. This supplies Lopez's constitutional connection before aggregation; generalized productivity or social costs do not. Section 12111(5)'s industry-affecting-commerce condition, employee/week thresholds, original transition threshold and exclusions retain their own operation; jurisdictional words alone do not establish power. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming, applied in Kimel, permit direct regulation of covered State employment. Private adjudication enforcing the enacted duty is an appropriate measure under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. No compelled State administration of a federal regulatory program is involved.

The strongest opposing path distinguishes a valid employment duty from nonconsensual litigation against a State treasury. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas maintain that structural objection from their Kimel and Florida Prepaid dissents and would reconsider the Article I enforcement rule and Union Gas's retained constitutional conclusion to the necessary extent. They accept the commerce basis for the duty and the direct statutory authorization. Their objection proposes a change in governing law; it is not an existing categorical prohibition that lower courts may already apply.

**G3 — Section 5.** The constitutional baseline is Cleburne as applied in current Heller and Romer: a rational relationship to the actual disadvantage is required; bare prejudice is insufficient. Relevant generalizations and conceivable legitimate explanations remain available without formal findings, empirical certainty, heightened disability scrutiny or least-restrictive means. Both Heller's unequal-proof holding and its distinct family-participation holding remain effective. Olmstead supplies statutory integration law, not a constitutional accommodation right or an immunity/Section 5 holding.

The government brief reports employment exclusions, stereotypes and harassment, including accounts involving State departments and universities. Local-school and library examples, private employment, nonemployment segregation and fundamental-rights settings must be distinguished. The approximately 600 state-actor submissions are neither adjudicated violations nor a State-employment count. History can inform prevention without making every report a constitutional finding. State laws prohibiting discrimination prove neither effectiveness nor compliance; legal permission proves neither rationality nor constitutionality.

The affirmative Section 5 path finds a supported risk that exclusions and apparently neutral barriers conceal or perpetuate irrational official treatment. Title I's qualification, essential-function, known-limitations, accommodation and screening rules address that risk. Boerne and Florida Prepaid permit fitted prevention beyond independently unconstitutional acts. The strongest objection is that rationality may permit imperfect generalization or rejection of an accommodation that the Act requires. The affirmative answer rests on the supported preventive relationship and the actual statutory safeguards, not identity between statutory and constitutional wrongs.

The opposing path acknowledges real official prejudice but finds the link between the identified State-employment injury and the full private damages regime inadequate. The gap between constitutional rationality and accommodation, screening and justification duties remains substantial despite statutory limits. The strongest answer is that hidden prejudice and the continuing effects of official exclusion justify precisely those protections; the opposing Justices reject the demonstrated degree of fit, not preventive legislation in every rational-basis field. They require no formal findings, incident count, violation in every State, sunset, exhaustion or individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action.

**Material safeguards.** Disability retains impairment, record and regarded-as branches. Qualification requires essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation; employer judgment receives consideration and a pre-advertising/interview description is evidence, not a veto. Accommodation examples are permissive, including reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) addresses known limitations and permits the employer to demonstrate significant difficulty or expense under the statutory cost, facility, entity, resource, workforce and operational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately preserves screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately includes inability to accomplish performance by reasonable accommodation. A direct threat is a significant risk to others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No universal reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense substitutes for those provisions. The medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling and drug/alcohol clauses keep their distinct conditions; no such defense is adjudicated here.

**G4 — disposition boundaries.** Affirmance permits ordinary adjudication of the presented Title I actions, not a finding of qualification, discrimination, injury, accommodation entitlement or damages. Reversal would concern only the reviewed denial of Title I private-damages immunity. Both positions preserve outside-grant Title II claims, without assuming dismissal or abandonment. No waiver, officer substitution, exhaustion condition, actual-violation exception or award-specific approval is invented.

## Buckman — No. 98-1768

### Components and adversarial comparison

**B1 — express preemption.** Medtronic requires an actual federal/state conduct comparison. Substantial-equivalence clearance supplies no blanket design approval or tort immunity. Genuinely equal, independently available state duties are not additional merely because damages are available. Those rules create no private FDCA action and do not settle independent actual conflict. There is no need to decide that every common-law duty is expressly displaced by section 360k.

**B2 — actual conflict.** The present theory makes deception of the FDA in obtaining clearance its wrong and requires a private finding that the agency was misled and would otherwise have withheld clearance. As framed, it depends on the federal applicant-regulator relationship rather than an independently available traditional duty to the patient. Section 337 reserves direct enforcement to the United States, subject to its specific State-food exception. Private liability substituting a jury's agency-fraud determination for that arrangement conflicts with the enacted allocation. The ground is this displacement, not mere regulatory overlap, a uniformity preference, lawful fraud or blanket product immunity.

Section 337(b) permits a State, in its own name and jurisdiction, to civilly enforce or restrain violations of sections 341 and 343(b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (k), (q) and (r), for food located in the State. Proceedings wait thirty days after notice to the Secretary, or ninety days if the Secretary begins informal or formal enforcement about that food within the first thirty days. Proceedings are barred while the Secretary diligently prosecutes the pertinent court proceeding or after settlement of that proceeding or the related informal or formal action; the State may intervene as of right in the specified court proceeding. This exception supplies no private device action.

The strongest opposing path invokes traditional fraud and injury protection, truthful submissions and Medtronic's equal-duty rule. Parallel obligations can coexist, and the possibility of jury disagreement alone is insufficient. Here, however, the asserted predicate privately enforces the FDA relationship itself. An independently available traditional injury duty must receive its own analysis. Fraud, but-for clearance, injury and ordinary causation remain allegations, not findings. An authoritative FDA fraud determination and resulting withdrawal or other agency action could alter the conflict premise; none exists in this record and that different case is not decided. Lawful off-label treatment does not authorize false submissions, and alleged false submissions do not make every off-label use unlawful.

**B3 — disposition.** Reverse the Third Circuit's restoration of these fraud-on-FDA theories and remand for a disposition giving effect to their preemption. No finding of fraud, defect, injury, causation or damages follows. No blanket dismissal of independent product claims, mandatory FDA proceeding or withdrawal of clearance is ordered.

**L1 — federal right and saved remedy.** The Limitation Act protects the qualifying limit on liability. ## United States v. Tank — judgment below 200 F.3d 627

### Components and adversarial comparison

**T1 — preservation and threshold.** The foundation objection was preserved; failure to move for acquittal on general sufficiency does not forfeit it. Original Rule 901(a) asks for evidence sufficient to support a finding that the matter is what its proponent claims. Under Rule 104(b), as explained in Huddleston, the judge screens whether the jury reasonably could find the conditional fact; the judge does not decide authenticity under Rule 104(a). Knowledgeable-witness testimony and identifying circumstances may supply the foundation. The actual offer is retained partial conversations, not a complete unedited archive.

**T2 — application.** Affirm admission. Riva described creation and retention and testified to the printouts' accuracy. Tank's limited admission and other participant identifications, including the arranged meeting, connected Cessna with him. Together those facts permit a reasonable authenticity finding despite deletion of other material and technological editability. This is a genuine foundation beyond a screen name. Deletions and the possibility of alteration do not themselves establish false attribution or alteration of the retained conversations; neither does corroboration prove every line independently.

The strongest competing path is that missing context and timestamps may conceal fabrication or attribution errors and a witness's assurance cannot automatically cure a concrete defect. That defeats a categorical rule placing every manipulation objection beyond the judge's consideration. It does not require exclusion on this supplied foundation. An identified defect leaving no reasonable authenticity basis would require exclusion or proper further foundation. No such decisive defect is established here. Riva's firsthand account is not an expert proffer demanding a forensic certificate, alteration-proof original, testing, publication or Rule 702's preponderance inquiry. Abuse-of-discretion review protects reasonable application while legal error remains correctable.

**T3 — separate evidentiary functions.** Authenticity is not truth, constitutional reliability, completeness, hearsay admissibility, absence of unfair prejudice or proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Hale's public certifications neither authenticate private logs nor decide their constitutional treatment. Completeness and other ordinary objections retain their separate operation. This granted question supplies no new constitutional, automobile-search, general-sufficiency or offense-elements disposition.

**T4 — disposition and conditional remedy.** Affirm the conviction component insofar as challenged through authentication and preserve the independent resentencing remand for grouping and role-adjustment errors. No admission error means no harmlessness holding is needed. If a lawful later stage changes that premise, Old Chief requires the Government to establish fair assurance from the whole record that preserved nonconstitutional error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The supplied packet cannot support a new whole-record harmlessness finding. The conditional lawful remedy is remand for that inquiry and affected-count consequences, not automatic acquittal or relief on every conviction. This is not a present finding of error.
