## Rule candidates and counterarguments

**Payment-year path:** Reverse the refund judgment. The relevant 1994 versions of §§3111, 3301, 3121(a)(1) and 3306(b)(1) tie the applicable rates and annual wage bases to wages paid during the calendar year. The implementing regulations attach the tax when payment occurs and use the then-effective rate. Their longstanding, consistent application supports that reading. The stipulated wage payments were made in 1994; the earning periods were 1986 and 1987. Rate and base both follow payment year. The dispute presents employer tax collection, not an individual benefit award.

**Strongest objection:** Nierotko attributes backpay to the periods for which employment compensation was earned, and actual-payment treatment can enlarge taxes solely because the employer paid late. Payment language might identify collection time without settling allocation. The response is provision-specific: Nierotko's benefit determination protects earnings-based benefit entitlement under its statute; it does not erase the separate annual payment language governing these employer taxes. A common wage characterization does not compel a common temporal account. Gitlitz confirms that operative accounts and sequences must each receive their enacted work, not a general rule favoring either revenue or tax symmetry.

**Earning-year path:** Affirm only if the tax provisions, considered with the benefit system, legally assign the wages to the earlier service years. Its best rationale is statutory integration, not free-floating fairness. Its strongest obstacle is that both rates and ceilings expressly operate by payment year and the longstanding regulations implement that wording. No separate statutory attribution provision for these taxes has been supplied. On the agreed dates no factual remand can cure that legal defect.

**Interpretive authority:** Treasury's regulations are an identified construction implementing these tax provisions; their continuity and permissible textual fit matter. The result need not announce that every revenue ruling has controlling force. Auer concerns a responsible agency's fair own-regulation interpretation, effective February 19, 1997; Christensen's May 1, 2000 advisory-letter rule separately requires actual authority and preserves persuasive weight. Neither permits text amendment or imagined agency grounds. The common merits rule rests on the statutes as read with the valid regulations and their consistent administration. No universal classification of informal tax guidance is necessary; Mead is later and excluded.
