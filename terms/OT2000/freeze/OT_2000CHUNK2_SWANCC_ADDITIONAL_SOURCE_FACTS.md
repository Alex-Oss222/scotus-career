## Site-specific habitat and species material

The official factual narrative at 531 U.S. 164–165 reports the Corps' determination that approximately 121 bird species had been observed at the site, including species dependent on aquatic environments for a significant portion of their life requirements. The November 16, 1987 agency jurisdictional determination identified the former gravel operation, the natural character acquired by the water areas and spoil piles, and habitat use by migratory birds crossing state lines. Its identified underlying source is the Corps' Chicago District Permit Evaluation and Decision Document, petitioner's lodging, tab 1, page 6. These remain agency findings and stated grounds, not new judicial findings.

The same narrative at page 165 states that SWANCC proposed mitigation for likely bird displacement and preservation of a great blue heron rookery on the site. It separately reports the Corps' permit-denial reasons, including its conclusion that the project's impact on area-sensitive species could not be mitigated by restoring a landfill surface to forested habitat. The underlying agency citation is the same decision document, page 87. The distinct jurisdictional assertion and permit-merits decision must not be conflated; the separate permit-merits challenge was abandoned.

At 531 U.S. 194 the official report identifies the site as containing the second-largest great blue heron breeding colony in northeastern Illinois, citing petition appendix page 3a. It also describes waterfowl covered by international treaty and Illinois endangered-species law, citing the federal respondents' merits brief, page 7. Note 16 identifies additional observed birds from petitioner's brief, page 4 note 3, including great egret, green-backed heron, black-crowned night heron, Canada goose, wood duck, mallard, greater yellowlegs, belted kingfisher, northern waterthrush, Louisiana waterthrush, swamp sparrow, and red-winged blackbird.
