## District of Columbia v. Tri County Industries, Inc.

### Components and lawful alternatives

**T1 — Review scope and vehicle.** The granted question concerns the circuit's review of a Rule 59 new-trial/remittitur order in light of Gasperini. The underlying procedural-due-process liability remains settled. The review-standard objection is a legal argument concerning the same order and claim, not a new substantive claim. Respondent's assertion that the formulation was challenged only on rehearing and the District's contrary account remain competing submissions; the unexplained denial of rehearing establishes neither. On this record all eight Associates elect to decide the included review question. Lebron's argument/claim distinction permits this confined choice without finding an earlier filing that has not been supplied or announcing that every belated argument must be considered. No Justice uses a presumed waiver or a research gap to dismiss the matter.

**T2 — Governing review.** Gasperini's actual Part II assigns the trial court the initial new-trial assessment and permits appellate correction of legal error and genuine abuse-of-discretion review. Its New York material-deviation measure does not govern this federal civil-rights action. The jury verdict remains protected, but neither a special appellate preference for the first jury nor a bare invocation of discretion resolves the actual grounds. The different constitutional positions of Stevens and Scalia/Thomas are retained in their rows below.

**T3 — Grounds for this order.** Three inquiries remain distinct: the categorical mitigation premise for projected profits; the trial judge's assessment of the profit evidence and amount; and the decision to reconsider excluded evidence for a new trial. A universal requirement to pursue administrative reinstatement regardless of cost, delay and likely utility is legal error. The judge separately rejected mitigation as a basis to defeat the sunk investment. The preserved evidentiary record includes component evidence supporting profits, substantial assumptions concerning startup and capacity, and the judge's specific concerns. The smaller jury award does not itself answer a Rule 59 objection. Conversely, neither the untried-business label nor a later $100 verdict proves the first verdict excessive.

The first trial actually excluded the identified letters, health-and-safety testimony and Siess proffer. It did not exclude the citation, stop-work order, fine/nonpayment or administrative-appeal defenses addressed in the jury instructions. A new-trial ruling can reconsider an earlier discretionary exclusion; appellate review cannot end with the observation that the earlier exclusion was permissible. But reconsideration still needs a lawful relevance and evidentiary foundation. The conditional permit, retained reviews and proposed occupancy/citizen mechanisms contradict a guarantee of unrestricted operation. They do not establish fraud, an inevitable lawful closure or the admissibility of every speculative objection. The July 23 order's permission for rebuttal was conditional on renewed profit evidence.

**Path A: affirm reinstatement.** Its strongest ground is that the trial judge's categorical mitigation error and inadequately founded regulatory proffers supply no lawful reason to displace the first jury, while the admitted component evidence permits its valuation. Its strongest objection is that it treats permission to exclude evidence initially as disabling the judge from later ordering a fairer trial, and substitutes appellate evaluation for the trial judge's concrete doubts about the profit model.

**Path B: vacate and remand for ordinary appellate review.** This gives effect to Gasperini without treating a legally erroneous mitigation ground as dispositive of every independent ground. Its strongest objection is unnecessary reconsideration if the first record already defeats each ground. The majority-of-Associates position below answers that the appellate approach did not adequately separate the later reconsideration and supported trial assessment from its approval of the initial rulings. The remand must examine the actual July 23 grounds and first-trial record; it may neither manufacture a new ground nor use second-trial evidence retroactively.

**Path C: reverse and sustain the new-trial order.** Scalia and Thomas conclude that the independent trial assessment and conditional evidentiary reconsideration already sustain that order. The strongest objection is that the lawful ground may be inseparable from the mistaken mitigation premise or unsupported speculation. They regard the specific startup/profit concerns and limited decision to permit grounded rebuttal if profits are claimed again as independently sufficient. They do not find the District's allegations true or approve an unconditional $1 million judgment.

## Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association

### Operative alternatives and limits

**B1 — Governmental attribution.** The relevant combination is actual governance by public-school officials carrying out their schools' athletic responsibilities, public-school predominance, institutional integration and the State's recognized relationship with the association. At the time of the penalty all voting governors were public-school administrators; the membership rules did not legally guarantee a public majority. The 1996 language recognized the association's role and permitted voluntary membership; it did not preserve the repealed express designation. The district court found no material change in operation. Gate receipts, approximately four-percent dues, retirement eligibility without a state-paid employer contribution, and nonvoting state representatives retain their limited factual significance.

The affirmative path treats this established public operation as governmental action in athletic regulation. Lebron supplies a sufficient structural condition, not an exclusive test; American Manufacturers protects genuinely private choices rather than an institution operated by public officials in that capacity. The strongest objection is that employment by a public school does not make every voluntary associational choice official and that no permanent governmental appointment right or command to impose this penalty exists.

The negative path would require a sufficient actual attribution ground beyond this voluntary structure and finds none. Its strongest objection is that public schools can exercise their own official powers collectively without a legislative charter or an external command to each director. The issue is legal characterization of supplied, materially undisputed governance facts; no factual remand is warranted simply to recount them. No path makes public-school membership, subsidy, public benefit or athletic regulation alone sufficient.

**B2 — Consequences.** Attribution does not decide whether the recruiting rule, procedure or sanction violates the Constitution. The four-year probation, two-year football/boys' basketball playoff exclusion and $3,000 fine identify the challenged action; they are not declared invalid by actor status alone. An affirmative disposition reverses the Sixth Circuit's attribution ground and remands for preserved substantive and remedial questions. It does not automatically reinstate the district court's injunction or award damages.

## Director of Revenue of Missouri v. CoBank ACB

### Question and common supported ground

The state-court refund route is proper; Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services decided the federal forum bar and expressly reserved substantive tax privileges. The institutional label does not resolve this new merits question. The relevant years are 1991–1994 and the relevant institution is a bank for cooperatives, including the predecessor whose rights CoBank succeeded to.

The 1971 section 3.13 separately protected bank obligations and granted a broader bank/property/income exemption only during federal stock ownership, with its own property and holder-interest qualifications. Federal stock was repaid by 1968. The 1985 enactment removed both sentences concerning that conditional broad exemption while replacing the federal-stock authority. The 1988 amendment expressly preserved federal income taxation of obligation interest in the holder's hands. The surviving language's instrumentality designation and obligation exemption do not textually grant an exemption for the bank's operating income. Sections 2023 and 2098 separately retain explicit institutional-income exemptions for their specified categories, subject to their distinct real-estate and holder-interest qualifications.

**Path A: continued state income taxation.** Reading the enacted amendment in its actual statutory sequence, deletion removes the conditional exemption that could no longer operate; it does not confer a broader institutional-income immunity. This conclusion follows from the statutory structure, the removed provisions and the retained category-specific exemptions, without making purpose override contrary operative language.

**Path B: restored default instrumentality immunity.** CoBank's strongest argument is that deletion removed the former limitation on immunity, leaving a federal instrumentality without express consent to state taxation. First Agricultural National Bank requires respect for Congress's bank-specific tax allocation and leaves its broader constitutional question unresolved. The answer is that Congress's actual tax allocation is ascertainable from the whole enactment: the unchanged instrumentality designation did not previously itself create the claimed permanent income exemption, and deleting an entire conditional exemption alongside its funding mechanism does not enlarge it. No Justice needs to decide that all privately owned federal instrumentalities lack constitutional immunity, that taxation requires no congressional authority, or that every congressional deletion preserves prior law.

## Illinois v. McArthur

### Actual predicates, alternatives and entering law

Probable cause was treated as uncontested. The wife reported observed concealment under the couch; McArthur knew officers sought to investigate the drugs and declined a search. Refusal alone is not evidence of guilt or an independent exigency. The concrete risk concerns readily disposable evidence within a residence to which this informed occupant would otherwise have immediate unobserved access. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another limited unaccompanied entry for approximately two hours. Two or three limited entries for cigarettes and telephone use occurred with observation just inside the doorway. Consent to that observation supplies no general search consent or surrender of the home-access objection.

Both charged offenses were jailable misdemeanors: less than 2.5 grams of marijuana carried up to thirty days and paraphernalia possession up to one year. Their modest gravity remains relevant. The actual prewarrant restriction must be assessed independently of the evidence later found. No evidentiary search occurred until the warrant; probable cause alone does not authorize an unlimited home entry, occupation or search.

**Path A: reverse suppression on the temporary-restraint ground.** Particularized probable cause, a reasonable evidence-destruction concern, actual diligence and a carefully limited restriction together make this preservation measure reasonable. It holds the situation for a magistrate while avoiding a general search. The strongest objection is that exclusion from one's home is substantial and that generic ease of destroying drugs could become an automatic warrant exception. The answer depends on the actual knowledge, access, timing and limited execution here; generic drug suspicion or the occupant's refusal does not suffice.

**Path B: affirm suppression.** The threatened loss of evidence of these small possession offenses does not sufficiently justify depriving the occupant of private use of his home on an inferred risk without a demonstrated attempt to destroy it. The strongest objection is that police need not await the very destruction they reasonably seek to prevent, especially while choosing a less intrusive measure and diligently seeking a warrant. Stevens gives greater weight to the modest offense and home restraint; the other seven accept the bounded preservation judgment on this record.

Edmond's actual rule rejects suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints absent an independently lawful justification. This case has individualized probable cause and presents residential scope and exigency, not a program-purpose threshold. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not itself authorize residential restraint. Bond's actual holding separates lawful presence from an additional intrusion; it neither eliminates supported preservation measures nor supplies consent. Welsh preserves the home's special protection and offense gravity; its civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest facts differ. Segura's independent-source holding does not automatically validate every earlier seizure, and its broad securing-premises discussion in Part IV had only two votes. No majority holding is attributed to that Part.
