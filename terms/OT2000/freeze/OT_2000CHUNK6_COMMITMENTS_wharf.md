## Supported common disposition and rule

All eight Associates commit to affirmance of the reviewed federal securities ruling. A seller who sells an actual option while concealing a contemporaneous intention never to honor it deceives the purchaser in connection with that security's sale. Oral form does not exclude an otherwise proved option from the statutory category. Later nonperformance alone is insufficient. Purchase, actionable deception, required culpability, reliance, causation and recoverable loss retain their independent force; no new presumption is adopted. The jury's option and initial-intent findings control the reviewed legal question under the verdict-favorable posture.

This is direct seller liability, not an expansion of Central Bank's confined accessory action. The immediate purchase is the option, so the fact that the later shares were never transferred does not turn the claimant into a person who merely wished to purchase. Blue Chip Stamps's purchaser limitation and proof concern do not exclude a proven oral security by judicial addition of a writing requirement. Santa Fe's deception requirement is met by the sham undertaking, rather than unfairness alone. The concession concerning an honest promise followed by breach is indispensable. No state-law punitive award or fresh damage calculation is adjudicated.

## Enhanced comparison and individual commitments

The statutory boundary is sufficiently novel to compare two paths. Path A affirms actual transactional fraud. Path B treats this as only an oral contractual promise, reverses for lack of a securities connection, or remands for a breach-only instruction. Its strongest concern is converting every failed investment promise into federal fraud and allowing unreliable oral testimony to evade Blue Chip Stamps. Path A answers through an actually purchased statutory option, independently proved initial fraudulent intent and ordinary elements; no unproved instruction defect is supplied. Path B cannot survive those accepted findings merely by calling the security a future promise.
