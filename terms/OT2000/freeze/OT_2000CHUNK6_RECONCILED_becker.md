## Final commitments and compatibility

All eight Associates reverse the Sixth Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal and remand for acceptance of the prompt signature correction and continued appellate proceedings. All eight join the complete following ground: a timely notice otherwise providing the required identification invokes review despite the omitted signature; Rule 11(a)'s requirement and prompt-cure provision operate together. Signing the paper on file or supplying a signed duplicate cures that omission even after the appeal deadline. The typed name on this paper form does not by itself satisfy the required signature. Authorized electronic filing under a compliant local rule remains distinct.

The source's argument that Rule 3(c)(2)'s reference to a pro se signer creates a stricter deadline does not defeat these commitments: the provision identifies the persons covered by the pro se notice and does not detach Rule 11's signature requirement from its cure. Its spouse-and-minor-children provision operates only for persons who are parties and yields to a clearly contrary notice. No broader representation rule or new appellant is at issue. Rule 3(c)(4)'s protection for clear intended appeals and the unchanged required contents reinforce the same bounded result.
