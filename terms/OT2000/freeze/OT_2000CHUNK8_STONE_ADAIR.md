# Adair v. Sherman

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 27, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply any intervening controlling preclusion decision and assess the actual confirmation record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains dismissal through issue preclusion based on the unopposed valuation and remand for further proceedings.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate consideration of the FDCPA claim without treating the valuation as actually litigated merely because Adair could have objected. Require proof of the claim's statutory elements and permit consideration of distinct defenses properly available on remand, including any independently applicable effect of the bankruptcy judgment. Do not award damages, find fraud, revise the plan, or determine collateral value. The remand must not simply reproduce the rejected substitution of opportunity for actual litigation under a different label.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The effect of silence before confirmation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the actual-litigation component of the issue-preclusion question on the conceded notice and nonobjection facts. No constitutional ruling is necessary. The different legal functions of issue preclusion, claim preclusion, and statutory binding effect must remain explicit.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether adequate opportunity to challenge a proof of claim's collateral valuation, followed by silence and confirmation, alone establishes that the valuation was actually litigated for a subsequent FDCPA action.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing preclusion principles and adopt their extension to this federal bankruptcy setting; no precedent is overruled. The requirement of actual litigation concerns an adjudicative event. A forfeited opportunity to create that event is not its occurrence. Bankruptcy statutes can assign decisive legal effects to silence, but the Court should identify and enforce those particular effects rather than imply that an adversarial determination occurred. This preserves finality according to its source without creating a general fraud exception to judgments.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Where a debtor receives notice but does not contest a collateral valuation stated in a proof of claim, claim allowance and plan confirmation alone do not satisfy issue preclusion's actual-litigation requirement for that valuation in a subsequent FDCPA damages action. This adopts an extension of the actual-litigation requirement to the federal bankruptcy setting. It does not decide the action's ultimate statutory validity or the independently applicable binding effects of a confirmed plan.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Decide the presented notice-and-silence situation fully. Reject a rule that opportunity alone suffices. Do not make reversal depend on inadequate notice, an invented valuation hearing, or an unproved fraud finding. The existence of an earlier adjudication actually resolving the contested issue would produce a different premise and remains subject to ordinary issue-specific analysis.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The proof of claim's $19,841.43 figure was asserted by the creditor's attorney. On this record, no objection triggered a contested decision about its accuracy. Confirmation used the allowed claim in administering the plan; that establishes important operative consequences but does not demonstrate adjudication of the attorney's alleged deceptive conduct or a litigated finding of the vehicle's value. The lower court's observation that a correct valuation would defeat the fraud theory identifies overlap between issues. It does not supply the missing actual-litigation element.

Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz remains intact. The exemption became effective because the governing provision and deadline assigned that consequence to nonobjection. That rule is not a universal direction to deem every factual assertion in bankruptcy actually litigated. Dodd v. Hood River County likewise shows why a full and fair opportunity is necessary but not interchangeable with actual and necessary determination. Its state-law setting does not itself establish the result here; the federal application rests on the distinction those requirements preserve.

Heintz v. Jenkins forecloses categorical attorney immunity from covered debt-collection activity, while leaving actual coverage, actionable conduct, and defenses to be established. Adair's allegations may fail on one of those grounds. That possibility does not justify affirming through an inapplicable preclusion element. Enforcing distinct requirements allows both bankruptcy finality and the FDCPA to operate through the legal rules that actually govern each.

  The decisive distinction is between an issue actually adjudicated and a consequence that statute assigns to an unopposed filing. This applies a formal element of issue preclusion because that element identifies what has been judicially resolved, not because a debtor's failure to object lacks all legal consequence. Taylor's specific deadline consequence remains intact, and any distinct binding effect of confirmation must be established under its own rule. A reusable rule is warranted because treating opportunity as actual litigation would erase that element in every similar confirmation.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the Bankruptcy Code categorically displaces every FDCPA action based on a bankruptcy filing is reserved because the parties did not present it below, as the appellate opinion expressly records. The independent claim-preclusion effect of this particular judgment is outside the granted issue-preclusion question and would require analysis of the applicable judgment, parties, claims, and preserved arguments. Correct collateral value, knowing falsity, the defendants' statutory collector status, damages, and any defense requiring additional facts remain for further proceedings because the present record contains allegations rather than adjudicated findings on those matters.
