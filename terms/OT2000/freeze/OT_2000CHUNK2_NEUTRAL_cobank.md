# Director of Revenue of Missouri v. CoBank ACB

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Director of Revenue of Missouri v. CoBank ACB
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1792; 531 U.S. 316
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-02-20 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federally chartered bank for cooperatives remains subject to state income taxation after Congress removed obsolete statutory language concerning federal stock ownership and the corresponding temporary exemption.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Supreme Court of Missouri held the successor bank exempt from Missouri corporate income tax because the current statute did not expressly consent to the tax. It reversed the State’s denial of refunds for the predecessor’s 1991 through 1994 tax years.
* **Relief Requested:** Missouri seeks reversal of the exemption and refund judgment. CoBank seeks affirmance on federal-instrumentality immunity grounds.
* **Material Record:** The Farm Credit Act created several distinct categories of federally chartered lending institutions, with differing tax provisions. Banks for cooperatives historically paid state income taxes except while federal stock investment remained. That investment was repaid by 1968. Amendments in 1985 eliminated the authority for federal stock ownership and removed the related exemption language from the cooperative-bank provision. CoBank succeeded to the National Bank for Cooperatives’ obligations and filed amended tax returns in 1996 seeking refunds for 1991 through 1994. The dispute concerns this institutional category and this tax, not every Farm Credit System institution.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case arrives through the State’s own tax-review system, not a prohibited federal district-court tax injunction. Congress’s tax allocation must be read in context before resolving any broader constitutional instrumentality immunity. The text applicable to the tax years controls; removal of obsolete language must be distinguished from a substantive grant of new immunity.
* **Relevant Dates:** The disputed tax years are 1991 through 1994; the refund requests were filed in 1996. The event date is February 20, 2001. The judgment below is reported at 10 S.W.3d 142.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

### Issue 1: Congressional treatment of cooperative-bank taxation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas decided federal forum availability under the Tax Injunction Act, not the substantive tax immunity of every federally chartered lender. It expressly reserved the associations’ underlying tax privileges and does not resolve this state-court merits dispute.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the particular institution, operative tax provision, and asserted immunity. A federal charter or instrumentality label does not itself decide the statutory exemption’s content or create the sovereign’s procedural privileges.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Supremacy Clause; the Farm Credit Act, including 12 U.S.C. §§2121 and 2134 and the operative 1985 amendments. Congress may prescribe the extent to which such instrumentalities are subject to otherwise applicable state taxation.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering forum decision prevents reliance on a generic Farm Credit label. The present merits question depends on the cooperative-bank provision and its statutory development.
* **Open Point:** Whether deletion of the federal-stock exemption language removed a spent condition or silently conferred a broader exemption from state income taxation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

CoBank argues that a federal instrumentality is immune absent express congressional consent and that Congress removed the text previously allowing taxation. Missouri and the United States emphasize the continuous history of state taxation after federal investment ended, the elimination of an obsolete ownership condition, and Congress’s explicit broader exemptions for other Farm Credit entities. The court below treated current textual silence as dispositive without giving the deletion its operational setting.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse if the statute as amended continues the preexisting state-tax treatment, without deciding a universal constitutional immunity theory. It may affirm if the enacted amendment actually withdrew consent and the claimed instrumentality immunity otherwise applies. That latter route requires more than treating all federal lending institutions alike. The judgment concerns the claimed federal exemption; it does not establish compliance with every independent state refund requirement.
