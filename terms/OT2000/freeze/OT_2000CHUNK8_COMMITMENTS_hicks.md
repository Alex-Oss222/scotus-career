## Record and lawful channel

The federal action challenges the lawful extent of tribal adjudicatory authority under 28 U.S.C. §1331. National Farmers Union Insurance Cos. v. Crow Tribe, 471 U.S. 845, 850–857 (1985), supplies that channel; it does not itself establish the tribal court's ultimate power. The tribal trial and appellate courts have already considered jurisdiction. The question about further tribal exhaustion concerns immunity defenses, not an uncompleted opportunity for appellate examination of the previously determined jurisdictional issue.

Hicks's home is on his retained Indian trust allotment. This is neither non-Indian fee land nor a public highway stripped of ordinary exclusion. The 1990 state warrant required tribal approval; the tribal judge limited it to exterior premises and vehicles and permitted execution from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., whereas the state instrument said 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. The 1991 state warrant authorized the identified heads and premises from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.; the tribal approval expressly made no modification. These documents establish permissions, not express agreement to civil adjudication or a finding that any officer exceeded an instrument. Damage, wrongful search, malicious purpose and unconstitutional conduct remain allegations. No criminal charge or compensable injury is adjudicated here.

The State, official-capacity defendants and tribal defendants are no longer defendants on the retained damages claims. The Director's January 2001 motion to be dropped remains only a reported pending motion. The retained posture therefore includes the Director personally, without a finding of participation or supervisory liability. Hafer's actual holding makes personal-capacity officers §1983 persons and keeps state immunity separate from personal defenses; it grants no right to choose a tribunal without jurisdiction.

## Entering law and exact legal divisions

- **Montana v. United States, 450 U.S. 544, 557, 564–567 (1981):** The decision recognizes nonmember hunting-and-fishing regulation on retained tribal or trust land while rejecting the reviewed fee-land regulation. Its nonmember principle and two exceptions must be read with those land-specific holdings. Consensual dealings, contracts, leases and other arrangements and conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare remain distinct sources.
- **Strate, April 28, 1997:** The public-highway holding does not itself decide retained trust land. All eight Associates joined the requirement of adequate substantive governing authority, the meaningful-nexus requirement and the separate predicates for coercive federal relief. Express forum consent and contractual privity are not universally necessary, but an unrelated relationship is insufficient.
- **Bourland, June 14, 1993:** Congressional acquisition displaced the identified exclusion-derived regulation on taken land, with residual authority separately preserved. It does not establish state exclusivity throughout a reservation. Souter's published dissent additionally resisted equating every loss of exclusion with loss of compatible sovereignty.
- **Atkinson, May 29, 2001:** The controlling rule concerns the challenged nonmember fee-land transactions. Kennedy, Souter and Thomas separately would begin with Montana for nonmember conduct on every land status; that is their own published position, not the Court's rule.
- **National Farmers Union; Iowa Mutual Insurance Co. v. LaPlante, 480 U.S. 9, 14–20 (1987):** Civil nonmember jurisdiction is not categorically eliminated by the criminal rule in Oliphant. Ordinary tribal examination and appellate exhaustion are prudential, not restrictions on federal subject-matter jurisdiction. Exhaustion does not manufacture substantive authority. National Farmers' exceptions concern bad-faith or harassing assertions of jurisdiction, patent violation of an express jurisdictional prohibition, and futility because no adequate opportunity exists to challenge jurisdiction; none is invented as a fact here.
- **El Paso Natural Gas, May 3, 1999:** All eight Associates joined federal determination of the specifically assigned Price-Anderson coverage task before tribal exhaustion. Neither a universal federal-claim exception nor a new removal power follows.
- **Section 1983:** Liability requires a person acting under color of the law of a State, Territory or the District of Columbia who causes a deprivation of a protected federal right. Its action-at-law, equity and other-proper-proceeding language creates the remedial cause but does not name or confer general jurisdiction on tribal courts. The judicial-officer injunction qualification concerns acts in a judicial capacity and the alternatives of a violated declaratory decree or unavailable declaratory relief. It supplies no warden defense and decides no earlier-act temporal question.
- **Hafer, November 5, 1991; Saucier, June 18, 2001:** Personal capacity, governing authority, federal forum competence and personal immunity are separate. Existing Harlow/Anderson objective immunity remains, without a controlling universal merits-first sequence or an authoritative-reliance replacement. Clearly established law must be assessed at the 1990 and 1991 conduct dates. Uncertainty about the tribal forum in 2001 is not itself uncertainty about whether the searches violated then-established federal rights.

The relevant register entries are **Tribal adjudication of nonmember public-highway claims**, present operation effective Strate, April 28, 1997, and **Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion**, present operation effective El Paso Natural Gas, May 3, 1999. Their actual source, nexus, exhaustion and remedial qualifications govern; neither entry states a general jurisdiction-before-immunity sequence.

## Enhanced Justice-specific comparison

### Stevens

**Committed ground.** His Montana join preserves retained-land authority; his National Farmers opinion rejects automatic extension of Oliphant to civil cases; his full Strate join requires a source and meaningful nexus, not an express forum clause in every case. Together they support recognizing tribal power to enforce conditions actually accepted in obtaining access to retained Indian trust property when the tort arises directly from breach of those conditions. The 1990 amendment is concrete evidence of an arrangement, although it is not consent to every tort claim. Vacate the lower court's broader approval and remand to identify each challenged act, applicable warrant condition and necessary connection. Do not authorize a generally applicable tribal code regulating every state investigative act.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** Complete reversal has force because the wardens acted for the State in investigating off-reservation crime and judicial permission is not ordinary commercial dealing. It nevertheless risks turning official status into a categorical exception to retained territorial authority despite the officers' actual use of conditional permission. Conversely, bare land title cannot sustain the whole judgment: his Strate join precludes jurisdiction merely because the alleged injury occurred on the reservation. The limited accepted-condition rule answers that objection only for connected claims; it cannot cure an unrelated damage allegation or confer jurisdiction over the Director without a supported relationship and his own relevant acts.

**Federal claim and process.** His actual Hafer join supports personal liability in a competent tribunal, not universal forum jurisdiction. The §1983 phrase “other proper proceeding” presupposes a proper forum and supplies no independent tribal grant over these state-officer federal claims. Reject that component without denying §1983 personhood. His Iowa Mutual separate opinion distinguishes jurisdictional exhaustion from automatic deference on other merits; after the completed jurisdictional examination he will not join an unconditional command to exhaust all personal defenses. He supports an ordinary federal determination of an appropriately presented federal defense, without finding that any officer wins it.

**Join limits and separate function.** Can join the statutory-forum ruling and bounded relief. Cannot join an all-land Montana rule, the proposition that voluntarily accepted tribal conditions never matter, or a finding of qualified immunity from present-day forum uncertainty. A separate writing, if needed, would explain the conditional entry relationship, its claim-specific reach and the difference between initial jurisdictional examination and merits deference. It would not supply adjudicated misconduct.

### O'Connor

**Committed ground.** Her Iowa Mutual join recognizes the distinct role of retained reservation authority and ordinary comity. Her actual Strate and Atkinson joins preserve the difference between public-use or fee land and retained trust property and require a meaningful relation to consent. She accepts the limited rule that officers who obtain and use a particular tribal authorization may be subject to tribal tort adjudication of a breach of its accepted entry conditions. A state investigation's off-reservation origin does not alone nullify that voluntarily used arrangement. Vacate and remand for claim-by-claim application, retaining only the jurisdiction the accepted conditions and governing source actually support.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** The State's strongest argument is that enforcing its criminal law cannot depend on another sovereign's permission and that requesting assistance should not expose every officer to local civil regulation. This limits the rule to accepted conditions and connected tort claims; it does not establish a universal tribal veto or demand future permission. The strongest objection to recognizing authority is that a warrant is unilateral judicial permission rather than a consensual commercial relationship. Her conclusion treats the requested, accepted and used authorization as an “other arrangement,” without converting silence into express forum consent. If an asserted condition was not accepted, did not govern the particular search, or bears no meaningful relation to the claim, the exception fails. The 1991 unmodified approval cannot be assigned the 1990 exterior-only term.

**Federal claim and process.** Hafer, which she wrote, rejects a capacity-based escape from §1983 personhood while preserving personal defenses. It does not make a tribal court a generally competent state court. Section 1983's remedial wording supplies no independent tribal forum grant here. That component is reversed. For any remaining tort claim, Iowa Mutual supports consideration of actual tribal-law questions in their forum, but the completed tribal jurisdictional review and Saucier's unchanged immunity law preclude an automatic command to try every personal defense there first. Remand must identify the defense, competent court and ordinary comity basis rather than assume a universal sequence.

**Join limits and separate function.** No join to a categorical inability of a tribe to enforce an accepted search condition, a general all-land rule or sovereign immunity for all personal claims. Can join the federal statutory ruling, rejection of automatic further exhaustion and independently conditioned relief. A separate writing would explain the retained-land/permission distinction and preserve state enforcement and personal defenses without equating them with absence of all tribal authority.

### Scalia

**Committed ground.** His actual Strate opinion makes governing power a prerequisite to nonmember adjudication and rejects jurisdiction created merely by the proposed application of another sovereign's law. Montana's external-relations principle supplies the next step: governing state officers' execution of state process for an off-reservation offense is not an internal tribal relation. The retained trust allotment matters, but does not by itself confer this asserted regulatory authority. No specific federal delegation establishes it. Reverse the tort-jurisdiction judgment.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** The strongest contrary case is the actually accepted 1990 tribal limitation and the direct allegation of exceeding permission; Strate's unrelated subcontract reasoning cannot simply be repeated as though no connection exists. The relationship still arose from cooperation in state criminal enforcement, not entry into a tribal commercial or comparable private regulatory arrangement. Permission to carry out that public task is not sufficient assent to tribal civil regulation of the task. This conclusion does not require express forum consent in every Montana case. Ordinary individual harm and an alleged unlawful search, without the qualifying direct tribal effect, do not establish the second exception. An allegation is not proof that all state enforcement threatens tribal self-government.

**Federal claim and process.** His Hafer join preserves §1983 coverage of personal officers, while Strate and his Neztsosie join require a genuine forum source. Section 1983 supplies none for the reviewed tribal litigation. Iowa Mutual's comity rule, which he joined, does not require completion of personal-defense proceedings where governing authority is absent. No qualified-immunity result is necessary; his Saucier position preserves the separate legal-error defense in the proper forum.

**Join limits and separate function.** Supports a rule resolving this state-enforcement category across retained and fee lands. A universal holding about all private nonmember conduct is unnecessary and not included in this commitment. Will not join a free-floating state-sovereign-immunity bar to personal claims, a misconduct finding or an unconditional injunction. No separate writing is required if the lead ground remains at this level.

### Kennedy

**Committed ground.** His Atkinson concurrence, joined by Souter and Thomas, independently supplies an all-land Montana starting point. Land status remains relevant within the actual source and exception inquiries. The sought power over execution of state process for an off-reservation crime exceeds internal tribal relations; the approval instruments do not establish a consensual commercial or comparable arrangement submitting that state activity to tribal tort regulation. The ordinary injury allegations do not establish the second exception. Reverse.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** A narrower accepted-condition approach has a real nexus and protects retained trust property; it is stronger than an inference from mere reservation presence. His objection is to the character of the relationship, not the absence of a magic forum clause: intergovernmental assistance with state criminal process does not change the governing sovereign for that task. His all-land approach does not erase genuine consensual authority or deem title irrelevant. Conversely, the broadest state argument would exempt personal misconduct from any civil accountability. Hafer and his Saucier dissent preserve liability and immunity as separate questions in competent courts, so he rejects that extension.

**Federal claim, process and joins.** Section 1983 does not create the missing tribal forum power; completed jurisdictional exhaustion cannot supply it. No further immunity exhaustion is required. His full Strate/Atkinson remedial joins require conforming relief under actual parties, immunity and equity. He can join the narrower state-enforcement holding while separately explaining the all-land starting point; the separate proposition needs its own joins and is not automatically controlling. No ruling on immunity's factual application follows.

### Souter

**Committed ground.** His published Atkinson concurrence is his most direct, current individual position: Montana begins the nonmember inquiry regardless of title. His earlier Bourland dissent continues to protect compatible retained sovereignty but does not eliminate the source inquiry he later joined. A state search for evidence of off-reservation crime is an external sovereign function; the actual approval relationship is insufficient to turn it into a tribal civil regulatory arrangement. No supported qualifying tribal effect establishes the second exception. Reverse the tort component.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** His Bourland position and the trust allotment make the accepted-conditions path substantial: the property interest and a direct relationship cannot be dismissed as mere ordinary road safety. The decisive limit is that a requested judicial permission does not itself establish the tribe's power to govern the State's enforcement task. The rule must not convert that conclusion into a universal bar on all nonmember civil claims, nor treat the State's status as proof the search was lawful. His Strate join and National Farmers as qualified by it supply federal review, not categorical distrust of tribal courts.

**Federal claim, process and joins.** His Hafer and Neztsosie joins keep personal liability and statutory forum allocation distinct. The §1983 cause supplies no independent tribal forum authority for these claims. Where none exists, additional exhaustion of a personal defense serves no proper jurisdictional purpose. His own Saucier writing preserves distinct constitutional and qualified-immunity inquiries, but does not justify resolving the searches or inventing a mandatory sequence in this forum case. He supports bounded federal relief and can join the case-specific lead rule, with Kennedy and Thomas's all-land starting proposition separately stated if needed.

### Thomas

**Committed ground.** His Bourland opinion separates actual withdrawal of exclusion-derived authority from supported residual sources; his unanimous Neztsosie opinion separates federal statutory allocation from comity; his Atkinson concurrence supplies the all-land Montana starting point. Neither retained title nor state officers' request for tribal judicial assistance provides the substantive source needed for this asserted tort regulation of off-reservation state enforcement. The accepted warrant does not establish the kind of regulatory relationship the exception requires. Reverse.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** The 1990 modification supplies a stronger direct link than Strate's subcontract, and a tribe has a concrete interest in its member's trust property. But connection alone cannot replace a qualifying relationship; an arrangement implementing the State's criminal authority is not an undertaking to submit that authority to tribal regulation. Ordinary individual injury is insufficient under the second exception without the required tribal threat or direct effect. Conversely, an argument that there can never be tribal power over nonmembers contradicts Montana's preserved exceptions and his actual Bourland remand. He will not join it.

**Federal claim, process and joins.** The express §1983 cause and remedies do not create tribal adjudicatory competence; “proper proceeding” cannot perform that omitted jurisdictional work by itself. No further immunity exhaustion is required where authority is absent. His Saucier dissent supports keeping personal legal-error immunity, not deciding it on facts absent from this review. Join the bounded remedy and case-specific state-enforcement result; an additional all-land Montana explanation can be separate. No damages, refund, permanent injunction or personal-immunity award is inferred.

### Ginsburg

**Committed ground.** Her complete Strate join requires corresponding governing authority, and her complete Atkinson join sustains only its actual fee-land rule. She need not endorse the three-Justice all-land concurrence to conclude that the present tribe lacks authority to regulate state officials executing state process for an off-reservation offense. The public sovereign function, not non-Indian status alone or title alone, controls this category. Reverse.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** The accepted 1990 conditions and retained allotment provide a serious alternative. They establish a permission to execute a particular search and a possible measure of illegality; they do not necessarily establish tribal civil jurisdiction over state enforcement. The narrower rule preserves ordinary retained-land power and genuine consensual transactions while refusing to equate obtaining judicial approval with undertaking to answer the State's enforcement in tribal tort proceedings. She will not join an all-purpose state immunity or a holding that all tribal-land claims must begin with Montana. Her Saucier concurrence's force-specific objection to overlapping reasonableness does not displace Harlow for these search claims.

**Federal claim, process and joins.** Section 1983 independently requires a proper forum; its remedial language supplies no tribal grant for these state-officer claims. Hafer continues to permit personal liability elsewhere subject to proof and personal defenses. National Farmers and Neztsosie permit federal resolution of actual forum limits without a pointless further immunity proceeding. She joins only properly conditioned declaratory/prospective relief, no merits finding and no immunity award. A separate writing is appropriate only if a proposed lead opinion adds the broader all-land proposition she has not committed to join.

### Breyer

**Committed ground.** His actual Strate join requires source and meaningful nexus while preserving retained-land and directly related consensual settings; his Atkinson join does not adopt the all-land extension. The specific requested and accepted tribal authorization supplies an administrable source for adjudicating a claim that the entrants breached its actual conditions on retained trust property. That is different from regulating every state investigation. Vacate the blanket jurisdiction judgment and remand to identify each claim's connection to the authorization and the officer concerned.

**Competing path and strongest objections.** The State's strongest objection is that ordinary cooperation will become an unpredictable surrender of enforcement power. The limited rule answers it by requiring an actual accepted condition and a claim meaningfully arising from its breach, with no implied general forum consent or authority over unrelated state conduct. The contrary risk is that the exception becomes a general territorial tort rule. His Strate join prevents that: title, injury and a member plaintiff alone are insufficient, the two warrants remain distinct, and the Director's own relevant conduct cannot be presumed.

**Federal claim and process.** His full Neztsosie join supports examining actual federal remedial allocation; §1983's language supplies no independent tribal forum grant for the federal claims. Reverse that component, retaining personal liability and ordinary defenses in a competent forum. He does not export the force-specific Saucier concurrence he joined into a general immunity replacement or direct an immunity result here. For a surviving tort claim, ordinary tribal-law comity may matter, but the federal court must assess the particular defense and already completed jurisdictional review rather than demand categorical further exhaustion.

**Join limits and separate function.** Can join a statutory-forum ruling and bounded relief. Cannot join a universal all-land rule or the proposition that actual accepted search restrictions are necessarily irrelevant to tribal tort authority. A separate writing would explain the limited permission relationship and its practical claim-by-claim application without making a new factual finding.

## Remand rules, public-law limits and integrity

For the five complete-reversal commitments, the Ninth Circuit's approval of the reviewed tribal adjudication and its consequent further-exhaustion requirement must be reversed. Federal relief must implement absence of authority but must still fit the actual parties and claims, any limited waiver, applicable immunity and ordinary equitable conditions. No automatic injunction follows from the jurisdictional ruling.

For the three partial-remand commitments, the federal statutory forum component is rejected; the tort component returns for the limited accepted-condition rule. The returned court must distinguish the two instruments, the particular official, the alleged act and its connection to an accepted condition. This does not assume a violation, create a tort, validate every tribal cause of action, enlarge permission into express consent to suit, or eliminate a superior federal-law limit. A claim unsupported by that source and connection cannot proceed in the tribal court. Any surviving personal defense is governed by its actual law and proper procedural channel; no mandatory sequence, automatic tribal trial or judgment of liability is prescribed.

The shared no-§1983-forum conclusion rests on the absence of an independent statutory tribal grant for the reviewed state-officer claims; it does not decide whether Congress can confer such authority, every federal statute's treatment of tribal tribunals, or every form of express litigation consent. Those matters fall outside the question and record. The cause remains available in a competent forum subject to limitations, preservation and other ordinary rules not adjudicated here; this decision does not guarantee a new filing or tolling.
