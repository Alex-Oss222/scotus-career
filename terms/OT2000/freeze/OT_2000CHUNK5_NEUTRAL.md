# Alexander v. Sandoval

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Alexander v. Sandoval
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 275; No. 99-1908
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-24 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether private individuals may sue to enforce disparate-impact regulations promulgated under §602 of Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, affirming an injunction requiring Alabama's Department of Public Safety to accommodate non-English speakers in driver's-license examinations under the disparate-impact regulations.
* **Relief Requested:** Alabama officials seek reversal because the asserted implied private cause of action is unavailable. Sandoval and the class seek preservation of the injunction under Title VI's private enforcement scheme.
* **Material Record:** Alabama's Department of Public Safety accepted federal transportation and justice funds. Following a state constitutional amendment declaring English the official language, the Department administered driver's-license examinations only in English. Sandoval represented non-English speakers and challenged that practice under federal regulations prohibiting methods of administration with discriminatory national-origin effects. The district court found a prohibited effect, enjoined the policy and ordered accommodation; the court of appeals affirmed. The claim at issue invokes the regulatory disparate-impact rule rather than proof of intentional discrimination in the statutory prohibition. [Alexander v. Sandoval](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-275.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review is limited to the existence of the private action. For this question, the regulations' validity and the lower courts' disparate-impact determination are assumed rather than adjudicated. The accepted private action for intentional discrimination under §601 is not challenged. A distinct §1983 theory is not the pleaded and granted route to be supplied by this Court. Federal funding, agency authority, a substantive obligation and a judicial remedy are separate inquiries.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Whether Congress authorized the asserted action is a legal question antecedent to adjudicating the regulatory merits. A request for an injunction does not itself establish a cause of action. Conversely, lack of this cause does not nullify otherwise valid regulations or authorized agency enforcement.

### Issue 1: Private statutory enforcement and regulatory elaboration

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Powell v. Ridge holds that section 602 does not imply a direct private action to enforce Title VI disparate-impact regulations and separately rejects section 1983 enforcement of that effects-based regulatory duty absent an individual statutory right. Its direct-action holding governs the asserted route here unless distinguished or overruled; its separate section 1983 branch is not this granted claim. McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc. requires contextual congressional authorization without a universal express-text requirement. Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. preserves its confined affirmative-assistance action. Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools concerns remedies within an existing action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc. requires contextual analysis of congressional intent while preserving established actions and independently authorized relief. Blessing v. Freestone permits regulatory elaboration of an existing individual federal right in its §1983 setting, but requires identification of that right and does not decide this distinct implied-action question.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title VI §601 states the individual discrimination prohibition. Section 602 authorizes implementing regulations and specified agency enforcement. The 1986 remedies and immunity provision and the Civil Rights Restoration Act are part of the relevant statutory history and structure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Cannon v. University of Chicago recognizes the contextual origin of the Title VI and Title IX private action; Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission and Alexander v. Choate address the statutory and regulatory distinction but do not conclusively settle the precise action now presented.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Powell v. Ridge already rejects the direct regulatory-effects action asserted here. The accepted section 601 intentional-discrimination action and valid administrative enforcement remain available within their own requirements. The broader contextual authorities do not silently displace Powell's specific Title VI holding.
* **Open Point:** No first-impression question remains about whether the existing direct-action rule permits this claim. The merits choice is whether to reaffirm that rule or expressly reconsider it, including whether Title VI's integrated structure warrants a prospective-enforcement exception. Regulatory validity and the unchallenged disparate-impact determination are outside this review.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Title VI's accepted private enforcement and regulatory implementation supported a private equitable action. **Petitioners:** Section 602 addresses agencies and their enforcement duties, and cannot extend the §601 private cause to an independent effects-based wrong; agency language cannot create the missing congressional action. **Respondents:** The action enforces Title VI as lawfully implemented, reflects longstanding judicial treatment and congressional acceptance, and is consistent with the funding scheme. The United States supported that contextual position. Respondents need not show an express action clause, but must demonstrate authorization for this obligation, not merely the §601 action. Petitioners must account for received actions and contextual inference; Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. cannot be cited as categorical rejection of implied liability. Validity of the regulations remains a shared assumption for this review.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reaffirm Powell v. Ridge and reverse the injunction insofar as it rests on the prohibited direct regulatory action, while leaving administrative enforcement intact. Alternatively, expressly reconsider Powell's direct-action branch and affirm if Title VI's integrated structure authorizes prospective enforcement of these valid implementing obligations. That alternative requires a reasoned statutory stare-decisis analysis; it is not mere application of an undecided remedial question. Neither path decides whether the English-only policy is intentional discrimination. A separate preserved action must be identified before it can sustain relief; the Court cannot cure an action defect by inventing §1983 allegations.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Reaffirming Powell can distinguish independent regulatory disparate-impact duties from regulations specifying the content of an existing statutory right, without displacing received securities liability or the §601 action. An affirmative holding must identify the portion of Powell overruled or narrowed and can rest on Title VI's particular history and structure without allowing agencies to create private causes under every implementing grant.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Atwater v. City of Lago Vista

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Atwater v. City of Lago Vista
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 318; No. 99-1408
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-24 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourth Amendment permits a warrantless custodial arrest for a fine-only misdemeanor seatbelt offense based solely on probable cause that the offense occurred in the officer's presence?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit en banc, affirming summary judgment for the City and other defendants because probable cause and the absence of extraordinary arrest execution were sufficient under its adopted rule.
* **Relief Requested:** Atwater and her husband seek reversal and adjudication of their §1983 damages claim. The defendants seek affirmance of the probable-cause rule and retain any independently applicable immunity or municipal-liability defenses.
* **Material Record:** In March 1997, Atwater drove a pickup with her three-year-old son and five-year-old daughter in the front seat; none wore a seatbelt. Officer Turek observed the violations and stopped her. On the plaintiffs' supported summary-judgment account, he announced that she would go to jail and berated her. She lacked license and insurance papers, explaining that her purse had been stolen. A friend took the children. Turek handcuffed Atwater, drove her to the station and had her booked; she removed shoes, jewelry and glasses, emptied pockets, was photographed and spent about an hour in a cell before release on bond. She pleaded no contest to seatbelt misdemeanors and paid a $50 fine; other charges were dismissed. The parties did not dispute probable cause for the observed violations. [Atwater v. City of Lago Vista](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-318.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Probable cause for the initial stop is conceded. The disputed additional custody must not be justified circularly by the search-incident rules that presuppose a lawful arrest. Constitutional reasonableness, the officer's conduct-date immunity and liability of the City are separate. The fine-only civil offense in Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights differs from Texas's criminal seatbelt offense, making extension of its rule a genuine legal question.
* **Relevant Dates:** The arrest occurred in March 1997. Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights became effective May 4, 1998, and Knowles v. Iowa on December 8, 1998; neither can itself supply clearly established notice at the arrest date.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Illinois v. McArthur and any earlier effective OT2000 arrest or execution decision must be checked before decision. Any intervening holding must be considered on its own terms; the historical comparator supplies no additional justification or custody rule.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the summary-judgment record favorably to Atwater, distinguish historical facts from the ultimate reasonableness determination, and address available defenses under their actual standards. Her no-contest seatbelt plea establishes no entitlement to unlawful custody and is not itself challenged by this suit.

### Issue 1: Probable cause and additional restraint

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights holds that probable cause and state enforcement authority do not alone justify station transport and confinement for a fine-only civil licensing violation. Added restraint must reasonably serve an actual enforcement function. Its actual application produced no majority finding that custody was lawful or unlawful. Knowles v. Iowa denies the full incident-search exception for a citation without a lawful custodial arrest.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights recognizes identification, appearance, ongoing violation, protection and necessary processing as possible justifications, without presuming them. It creates neither categorical arrest immunity nor a least-restrictive-means or universal citation requirement.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment's reasonableness requirement and §1983 govern the asserted claim. Texas's misdemeanor arrest authorization supplies state power but does not conclusively answer federal reasonableness.
* **Material Other Authority:** Whren v. United States supplies objective grounds for a traffic stop. Wilson v. Arkansas treats historical practice as relevant to reasonableness, not an automatic substitute for actual constitutional analysis. United States v. Robinson and Gustafson v. Florida presuppose a qualifying lawful arrest for their incident-search rules.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law rejects the automatic-custody premise for a fine-only civil violation. It does not yet resolve the effect of a criminal label or adopt a categorical historical breach-of-peace limit for every misdemeanor arrest.
* **Open Point:** Whether the functional custody inquiry extends to an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor, and whether defendants can establish an independent lawful basis for this custody or a separate damages defense.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Probable cause ordinarily supplies the constitutional balance, with exceptional execution concerns treated separately. **Atwater:** Fine-only offenses do not justify jail and booking absent an actual need; the severe additional restraint bore no reasonable relationship to this seatbelt enforcement. Historical misdemeanor limitations reinforce that position. **Defendants:** Officers require clear arrest rules and cannot adjudicate all punishment classifications or alternatives at roadside; probable cause for an observed criminal offense supplies sufficient authority. Missing documents could bear on identification, but their existence must be evaluated rather than presumed dispositive. The United States supported the categorical arrest rule. Atwater's strongest alternative is an actual-function requirement, not immunity from all temporary detention or every arrest for a minor offense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm by limiting Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights to civil matters and adopting an ordinary probable-cause rule for criminal misdemeanors. Vacate and remand by applying functional review to added custody despite the fine-only criminal label. A conduct-date immunity defense may defeat personal damages even if the constitutional rule favors Atwater; municipal liability has independent policy and causation requirements. Neither reversal nor invalid custody automatically invalidates her uncontested seatbelt conviction.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The proposed alternatives concern additional custodial arrest for a known fine-only criminal offense. Functional review would not require citation in every case or alter probable cause for an initial stop. A categorical rule would establish broad misdemeanor arrest authority but still leave extraordinary execution and separately applicable state constraints distinct.

**Material Uncertainty:** The lower court's categorical rule did not establish every fact relevant to identification, appearance risk or necessary processing. Missing documents are in the record; their actual significance and remaining defenses cannot be invented away.

## E. SOURCES

**Case authorities:** Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights; Knowles v. Iowa; Whren v. United States; Wilson v. Arkansas; United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida. # Sterling v. Borough of Minersville

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Sterling v. Borough of Minersville.

**Citation or Docket:** 232 F.3d 190; Third Circuit No. 99-1768. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; disposition on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** April 24, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term.

**Question Presented:**

1. Whether the constitutional right to informational privacy protects an adult arrestee against a police officer's threat to disclose his suspected sexual orientation to a family member without a legitimate law-enforcement purpose?
2. Whether that right was clearly established in April 1997 sufficiently to defeat the officer's claim of qualified immunity?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses both questions presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit sustained the denial of personal qualified immunity on the reviewed privacy theory.

**Relief Requested:** The officers seek reversal and protection against damages litigation. The estate seeks affirmance and continued proceedings, not a present damages award. Hoban's factual nonparticipation argument raises a separate interlocutory-jurisdiction limitation.

**Material Record:** On April 17, 1997, police investigated eighteen-year-old Marcus Wayman and a seventeen-year-old companion in a parked vehicle near a closed business. There was no evidence of a burglary. They were arrested for underage drinking. Whether they admitted homosexual activity is disputed. At the station, Wilinsky allegedly instructed Wayman to disclose his sexual orientation to his grandfather or the officer would do so. The officer also delivered a biblical admonition. Wayman died by suicide after release. The estate alleged several constitutional and state-law claims; summary judgment eliminated the unlawful-arrest claim while other claims remained. The present appeal concerns legal immunity on the supported plaintiff-favorable privacy account. [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The court cannot decide on an interlocutory appeal whether Hoban participated or whether the threat occurred. Nor does rejecting immunity establish that the threat legally caused the death or establish municipal liability. Actual disclosure by the officer did not occur; the distinction between a threat of future wrongdoing and present compelled self-disclosure is central. The adult's privacy claim must not be decided as though police were notifying a minor's guardian of an arrest.

**Relevant Dates:** The opinion was filed November 6, 2000 and amended November 14, 2000, before the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Denius v. Dunlap is an earlier assigned same-term matter concerning confidential information; no controlling disposition is identified. Later same-term Saucier v. Katz cannot supply current sequencing law.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 24, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier controlling decision in Denius v. Dunlap must be incorporated into the confidentiality analysis. Saucier v. Katz occurs later and supplies no entering sequencing rule.

### Issue 1: Constitutional confidentiality and coercion

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County establishes a seven-Justice holding protecting objectively intimate marital and family information against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination when entrusted for a limited criminal investigation and disclosed without sufficient governmental justification. Consent to investigative use is not consent to publication. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority is also a simulated Supreme Court holding: five Justices assumed without deciding a medical-confidentiality interest and sustained the confined, benefits-related internal use before them. That decision neither requires public dissemination for every possible claim nor authorizes unrelated use. Shahar v. Bowers protects actual intimate association against unsupported employment penalties, on distinct predicates. None squarely decides this coercive family-disclosure ultimatum.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The current intimate-information standard preserves the confined protection established in Sheets v. Salt Lake County. Objectively intimate information and a sufficient official justification matter; confidentiality promises, official possession, subjective preference, or reputation alone do not establish a constitutional claim. No universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means, or seven-factor formula has been adopted. Each claim still requires the relevant governmental intrusion, personal responsibility, causation, and injury. Legitimate investigative use does not itself authorize unrelated disclosure.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment supplies the asserted constitutional confidentiality protection and section 1983 the asserted civil remedy. Whalen v. Roe and Nixon v. Administrator of General Services supply the inherited confidentiality background without resolving every disclosure demand.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp. and Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia supply Third Circuit confidentiality authorities. The earlier circuit opinion in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority remains relevant to what officers could know before this conduct; its broader balancing approach was not adopted by the later Supreme Court decision. Walls v. City of Petersburg supplies contrary Fourth Circuit authority concerning a police employment questionnaire about same-sex relations. Its setting and rationale require consideration. These circuit authorities are discussed in the [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies an actual-disclosure rule involving public dissemination and objectively intimate marital and family material. Extending that protection to an operative ultimatum about suspected orientation, directed toward one family member and without actual officer disclosure, requires an express doctrinal step. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority does not foreclose that step merely because disclosure did not occur, but its assumed-right disposition does not decide it. Neither case establishes liability for every offensive remark or a free-standing autonomy guarantee.

**Open Point:** Whether this official ultimatum constitutes a present invasion of protected confidentiality about sexual orientation, and what governmental justification can authorize it, remains unresolved by the cited precedents.

### Issue 2: Qualified immunity and appellate jurisdiction

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier permit separable legal immunity review on properly assumed facts and exclude evidentiary-sufficiency appeals. Hunter v. Bryant requires judicial resolution where the supported record permits, without replacing disputed facts. Elder v. Holloway requires consideration of all relevant conduct-date precedent. Wilson v. Layne preserves particularized fair notice, without an invariably identical-case requirement.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Notice is measured on April 17, 1997. Sheets v. Salt Lake County had already taken effect on April 29, 1996 and therefore belongs in the notice analysis, with its actual-publication limits intact. The Supreme Court decision in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority took effect on May 5, 1997 and cannot supply earlier notice; its earlier circuit opinion is a distinct conduct-date source. Shahar v. Bowers and Wilson v. Layne likewise supply current instructions rather than earlier notice. Subjective awareness alone is not the objective immunity test. No automatic merits-first sequence governs this case.

**Other Governing Law:** The individual damages claim under section 1983 remains subject to objective qualified immunity. The inherited particularity requirement discussed in Anderson v. Creighton requires analysis of the right in the concrete official circumstances, while the interlocutory posture fixes the supported plaintiff-favorable factual assumptions.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp. and Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia predate the conduct. The Third Circuit's earlier opinion in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority was issued in 1995 and is a relevant local notice source, separately from the simulated Supreme Court's subsequent assumed-right application. Walls v. City of Petersburg also predates the conduct and weighs against treating orientation confidentiality as uniformly settled. The dissent's cited threat cases add a separate concern: a threatened violation is not invariably a completed constitutional injury. Later Gruenke v. Seip, Bartnicki v. Vopper, Bloch v. Ribar, and Powell v. Schriver cannot establish April 1997 notice. [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The objective conduct-date standard and distinction between legal and factual appellate issues are settled. Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies eligible Supreme Court authority, but its recognition of a confined right supplies no automatic notice for every extension. Binding local confidentiality principles and contrary out-of-circuit authority must also be evaluated at their actual scope. A present merits ruling cannot retroactively fill a genuine notice gap.

**Open Point:** Whether the pre-April 1997 authorities made this particular coercive ultimatum, unsupported by any official disclosure purpose, sufficiently apparent as unconstitutional remains contested. Neither a universal threat-liability rule nor an exemption for every threatened compelled disclosure is already established.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The majority treated orientation as intensely personal information, distinguished conduct regulation from disclosure of status, and found no legitimate disclosure purpose. It regarded the threatened loss of confidentiality itself as actionable. United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp., Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia, and Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority supplied the majority's earlier circuit confidentiality foundations; Wilinsky's acknowledgments reinforced its conclusion about absent justification. The majority rejected Walls v. City of Petersburg's transfer of conduct-regulation reasoning to forced disclosure, but the existence of that contrary authority remained material to objective notice. The dissent agreed that coercive self-disclosure could violate privacy but found neither orientation protection nor threat liability sufficiently clear for damages. Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention was outside immediate appellate jurisdiction. [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

**Officers' Strongest Position:** General privacy language does not settle every application. Sheets v. Salt Lake County involved completed public dissemination of entrusted marital and family material, unlike the alleged ultimatum here. Walls v. City of Petersburg had rejected a privacy objection to an employment questionnaire about same-sex relations. That contrary authority, together with decisions refusing to equate every threat with a completed constitutional injury, allowed reasonable legal disagreement about this new application. No officer actually informed the grandfather. The absence of a legitimate policing purpose may establish misconduct without supplying fair notice of a federal damages rule. Each defendant's responsibility must be proved separately.

**Estate's Strongest Position:** The threat was an immediate official demand to surrender an intimate confidence. Sheets v. Salt Lake County had already rejected the premise that official access for a limited investigation authorizes unjustified exposure of intimate information. The government cannot evade the right by making the citizen carry out the disclosure. Established confidentiality principles and the admitted absence of any official need made this application apparent without a factually identical case. An adult arrest cannot become authority for compelled family disclosure or religious instruction.

**Record Limits:** The legal review assumes the supported threat; it does not resolve admissions, participation, causation, damages or other defendants' liability. Later cases cited by the lower court cannot perform conduct-date notice work.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Dismiss factual participation contentions from immediate review while deciding Wilinsky's separable legal defense. If assumptions cannot be identified, remand to specify them without finding historical facts.

**Merits Paths:** The Court can recognize a bounded confidentiality right and deny immunity on contemporaneous notice; recognize the right but grant immunity; or reject the asserted federal right. It may distinguish operative coercion from a threat having no present constitutional effect. A new rule of liability does not automatically defeat immunity.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance permits litigation to continue under the clarified rule. Reversal on immunity protects the individual on this damages theory without deciding all other claims. Neither path establishes a damages amount or that the alleged violation caused the death. Relief against the municipality requires its own predicates.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Adult intimate information, an official disclosure ultimatum, and no legitimate governmental purpose. Broad rules covering criminal investigation, emergency protection or routine parental notification would exceed this record. Any departure from governing objective immunity must be expressly identified as new law.

# Daniels v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Daniels v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 374; No. 99-9136
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-25 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal prisoner may use 28 U.S.C. § 2255 to challenge an enhanced federal sentence on the ground that completed state convictions used as predicates resulted from involuntary counseled pleas or ineffective assistance of counsel?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of Daniels’s § 2255 motion, reasoning that the limitation recognized in Custis v. United States extended to this collateral proceeding unless the predicate conviction involved unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel.
* **Relief Requested:** Daniels seeks consideration of the constitutional validity of two robbery predicates and relief from his enhanced federal sentence if the necessary predicates cannot lawfully support it. The United States seeks affirmance of the denial.
* **Material Record:** Daniels was convicted in 1994 of federal felon-in-possession. Four California convictions, two burglaries and two robberies from 1977 through 1981, supplied the asserted ACCA predicates. After a downward departure he received 176 months, exceeding the otherwise applicable 120-month maximum. His direct appeal challenged the burglary convictions’ statutory classification unsuccessfully. His subsequent § 2255 motion instead alleged that the 1978 and 1981 robbery pleas were not knowing and voluntary and that the latter conviction involved ineffective representation. The sentences for those convictions had expired. His counsel acknowledged no indication that he pursued the constitutional challenges while in custody on those convictions or was externally prevented from doing so. These are allegations of defective counseled proceedings, not a demonstrated complete denial of constitutionally required appointed counsel. [Daniels v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-374.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Custody under the present federal judgment supplies the immediate statutory vehicle; it does not automatically create custody under each expired state judgment. The statutory-predicate objection and the constitutional collateral attack are different claims. No predicate invalidation is established. A genuinely unavailable earlier review channel is not claimed on this record, and the Court should not manufacture such a circumstance.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged predicates date from 1978 and 1981; the federal conviction dates from 1994. Applicable review limits and the actual opportunity to contest the predicates must be assessed historically. This event is April 25, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss presents the related state-prisoner vehicle on the same date and receives its own listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon. The historical disposition of the same-day companion does not govern this case.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Coordinate with Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss. If either decision becomes effective first, its holding must be considered in the other matter with due regard for the distinct statutory vehicle.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 2255 authorizes correction of an unlawful federal sentence subject to its own procedural conditions. A refusal to entertain an indirect attack differs from a finding that the state plea was constitutional. A lawful denial also need not establish that every imaginable collateral claim is unavailable.

### Issue 1: Collateral attacks on completed predicates

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Custis v. United States rejects a general ACCA sentencing forum for ineffective-assistance and involuntary-counseled-plea attacks, while retaining the unconstitutional complete-denial-of-counsel exception. It expressly leaves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases undecided.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Collateral review distinguishes statutory eligibility, complete deprivation of counsel, and an independently authorized review channel. Successful predicate invalidation permits an authorized application concerning the enhancement, without promising reopening or a particular sentence.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Daniels v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-374.pdf). Section 2255’s reference to the current federal sentence must be interpreted independently of the ACCA’s sentencing procedure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Garlotte v. Fordice permits review of an earlier segment of a continuous, unexpired consecutive series when relief would advance release; it distinguishes a wholly expired independent sentence. Maleng v. Cook likewise separates present custody from collateral consequences. Parke v. Raley preserves lawful regularity and proof rules.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Custis v. United States controls the existing sentencing-forum limitation, but extending that limitation into § 2255 requires a new holding. Nichols v. United States addresses a valid fine-only conviction’s particular Guidelines use, not these allegations that the predicates themselves were unconstitutional. Almendarez-Torres v. United States preserves the collateral-review distinction despite its separate element-classification rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether § 2255 offers renewed predicate review after an available original review opportunity has expired; a wholly unavailable review channel would present a distinct case.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Daniels emphasizes that § 2255 expressly reaches a federal sentence imposed in violation of the Constitution and that present incarceration supplies a practical reason to challenge the predicates. He distinguishes collateral review, where a record can be developed, from the immediate sentencing proceeding addressed in Custis v. United States. The government emphasizes the finality of state judgments, stale records, the original jurisdiction’s review opportunities and the risk of avoiding the limits governing those proceedings. Its strongest argument concerns a defendant who allowed available review to lapse. A contention that the ACCA itself resolved every § 2255 question would overstate the holding of Custis v. United States.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could allow a properly presented challenge to the current sentence while applying ordinary procedural and merits restrictions. Alternatively, it could adopt a bounded rule precluding renewed review of these counselled predicates when an earlier meaningful opportunity existed and no external impediment is alleged. A categorical prohibition covering every faultless loss of a review forum would go beyond the facts. Any new review permission must still identify the authorized vehicle and required causal relationship to the present enhancement.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule about reopening expired constitutional attacks through § 2255, limited to the available-review circumstances proved here. It need not alter statutory ACCA classification, complete counsel deprivation, or the consequences of an independently vacated predicate.

**Material Uncertainty:** No evidentiary finding establishes either alleged plea defect. A decision about reviewability must not be described as adjudicating those allegations or declaring the prior judgments constitutionally flawless.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Randell v. Johnson.

**Citation or Docket:** 227 F.3d 300. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; disposition on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Relief Requested:** Randell seeks reversal and access to the civil remedy. The defendants seek to preserve dismissal for absence of a qualifying invalidation.

When he filed this action on June 18, 1999, the disputed confinement had ended and federal habeas relief concerning that confinement was unavailable. He sought $1,000 per excess day. The complaint proceeded in forma pauperis; the district court characterized it as frivolous. The appellate record does not establish that the asserted excess days were actually unlawful or that Randell lacked every other possible review procedure. [Randell v. Johnson](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/227/300/615129/).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify precisely what judgment, administrative calculation or custody decision a favorable civil judgment would contradict. Incorrect execution of a valid sentence is not necessarily invalidation of the sentence itself. Randell's custody status at civil filing does not establish whether he previously had a realistic opportunity to contest the disputed period. The defendant officials' individual responsibility, immunity, limitations defenses and actual injury have not been decided.

**Relevant Dates:** The September 26, 2000 lower decision precedes the assigned date.

**Companion or Related Matters:** The same-term Daniels v. United States and Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss concern distinct review channels; no controlling disposition in either matter is identified.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 25, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Apply any earlier controlling decision in Daniels v. United States or Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss only to the extent its holding bears on this distinct civil-remedy question.

### Issue 1: Favorable invalidation and unavailable custody review

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Heck v. Humphrey imposes a favorable-invalidation prerequisite on an in-custody prisoner's damages claim whose success necessarily establishes the invalidity of an outstanding conviction or sentence, where a lawful challenge opportunity exists. All nine Justices adopted that bounded rule; five supported the statutory-coordination rationale. The decision expressly reserves former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity. It does not establish a universal rule covering every person who once was imprisoned.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Compare the civil claim's elements with the operative conviction and custody determination. A compatible independent injury is outside the prerequisite. Where the prerequisite applies, qualifying reversal, expungement, authorized state invalidation or habeas relief makes the claim cognizable; unsuccessful review or mere expiration is not qualifying invalidation. The barred claim accrues upon invalidation, and dismissal is without prejudice for lack of present cognizability, not a misconduct merits judgment.

**Other Governing Law:** Spencer v. Kemna expressly preserves the former-prisoner and realistic-opportunity reservations. It decides continuing injury in a habeas proceeding, not an affirmative civil exception. Patsy v. Board of Regents remains the rule against converting section 1983 into a general state-remedy exhaustion scheme. Ordinary preclusion and civil-remedy requirements remain independently relevant where their predicates exist.

**Material Other Authority:** Preiser v. Rodriguez and Patsy v. Board of Regents retain the bounded roles identified in the controlling records: custody-relief coordination does not create general exhaustion of state remedies for every civil-rights damages action. Spencer v. Kemna supplies no already-adopted affirmative former-prisoner exception.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Heck v. Humphrey and Spencer v. Kemna neither compel the lower court's universal reading nor already guarantee Randell a damages action. The present case directly presents a category expressly reserved by Heck v. Humphrey.

**Open Point:** The effect of an earlier usable review opportunity, a later absence of habeas jurisdiction, and a claim against sentence administration requires a new resolution or a fact-sensitive remand.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Fifth Circuit read the historical Heck v. Humphrey rule as categorical, considered Spencer v. Kemna's separate opinions insufficient to authorize an exception, and observed that Randell showed only unavailable federal habeas, not absence of every procedural vehicle. It therefore affirmed failure-to-state-a-claim dismissal without deciding overconfinement. Its characterization of Supreme Court authority must be assessed against the narrower holdings of Heck v. Humphrey and Spencer v. Kemna. [Randell v. Johnson](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/227/300/615129/).

**Randell's Strongest Position:** Damages cannot secure release after custody ends. Statutory coordination should not extinguish a civil remedy when the supposedly preferred federal channel cannot be used. A prisoner whose excess confinement is too brief or discovered too late for meaningful review should not lose every federal remedy. Further, crediting time already served can vindicate an existing sentence rather than undermine its validity.

**Defendants' Strongest Position:** Expiration alone does not justify a collateral damages attack on an undisturbed custody determination. A claimant who had an actual opportunity to challenge the calculation cannot manufacture an exception by waiting. Alternative state invalidation procedures may remain. The current allegations do not establish reasonable diligence, lack of opportunity, or that success would leave the controlling sentence determination intact.

**Limits on Both Accounts:** No finding establishes deliberate delay, a previously adjudicated credit issue, the length of the final disputed confinement, or an adequate existing state remedy. An inference about those matters cannot replace the screening record. A requested per-day amount proves no entitlement to that amount.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** First determine whether the claim necessarily contradicts an outstanding judicial or administrative basis of custody. If the complaint challenges only execution contrary to a valid sentence, address whether the favorable-invalidation condition applies at all.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may adopt an exception where no realistic custody-review opportunity existed; permit former-prisoner actions more broadly; or extend the prerequisite to some or all former prisoners. Each extension must be identified as resolving reserved law. The existing record supports rejecting a categorical rule more readily than final adjudication of the exception's factual predicates.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Vacatur and remand can require claim-specific analysis and a fair opportunity to allege the availability and timing of review. Any renewed prerequisite dismissal must be without prejudice and distinguish lack of present cognizability from rejection of the alleged misconduct. No release, sentence recalculation, habeas conversion, damages award or compelled state reopening follows automatically.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Federal habeas unavailability is established for the disputed custody at filing. Earlier realistic opportunity and other qualifying mechanisms are not. A case-specific judgment must not invent findings resolving those matters or impose general exhaustion under another name.

Rogers argues that the State cannot first acknowledge a substantive limit existing when he acted and then erase it to affirm his conviction. Tennessee answers that common-law doctrine develops through adjudication; an obsolete rule mentioned only rarely in dicta, never used as the ground of a Tennessee murder disposition, and widely abandoned elsewhere could foreseeably be discarded. The statutory homicide definition supplied notice that killing another was criminal. The strongest disagreement concerns the level of specificity required for warning: general liability for a fatal stabbing or the continuing legal conditions of the particular murder offense.

The Court could affirm if the pre-existing legal landscape made this common-law development adequately foreseeable under the due-process standard. A conclusion that the 1989 statute already eliminated the rule would contradict the state supreme court’s controlling construction and cannot be used as an alternative federal factual premise. Automatically applying every legislative ex-post-facto category to all judicial decisions would be a broader rule than necessary and would require its own justification.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision can distinguish a permissible prospective correction from an impermissible use of that correction to sustain this conviction, without freezing common-law development or requiring a prior case with identical medical facts.

### Issue 1: Reasonable suspicion from the report and encounter

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Illinois v. Wardlow unanimously preserves objective, particularized suspicion under all circumstances and rejects location alone or a universal flight rule. Its five-Justice application holds the actual look-and-flight record insufficient. Florida v. J. L. unanimously rejects a forcible stop resting on an unaccountable gun accusation corroborated only as to observable identity and location. Ordinary firearm danger supplies no exception. Accountable eyewitness reports and other concrete reliability assurances remain open.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Individualized suspicion of wrongdoing is required for an investigative seizure. Local experience and circumstances may inform that assessment but cannot establish an observation absent from the record. Neither permission to ask consensual questions nor the presence of a gun automatically permits a seizure. A protective search requires its own lawful predicates.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and the inherited Terry v. Ohio framework govern the stop and any protective search. **Material Other Authority:** Ornelas v. United States requires independent review of constitutional sufficiency while protecting historical findings and giving genuine weight to grounded local experience. The historical Third Circuit's categorical account of flight conflicts with the narrower governing holding in Illinois v. Wardlow. The firearm-right standards likewise preclude assuming that all possession is criminal, without themselves deciding the validity of this permitting law.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The settled totality rule governs this encounter. The Court has rejected reliance on identity corroboration, firearm danger, or location alone. Its existing applications do not resolve every recently observed, in-person report by a witness who refuses identification.

**Open Point:** The report's face-to-face delivery supplies a new reliability application, while the particular totality still requires proof of a connection to wrongdoing. The preserved permitting theory remains unadjudicated and may require further factual and legal development.

### Issue 2: Submission before later flight

**Controlling Simulated Law:** California v. Hodari D. preserves the distinction between physical force and actual submission; the cited precedents do not resolve this alleged brief compliance. **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual seizure and the facts known at that moment. Later struggle or gun discovery cannot justify an already completed unsupported seizure. Conduct occurring before any seizure may contribute to its justification. Review of the legal effect of assumed facts does not authorize appellate invention of historical findings.

**Other Governing Law:** California v. Hodari D. supplies the inherited distinction between a show of authority and a seizure through physical force or actual submission. An unheeded command alone does not establish submission.

**Material Other Authority:** The lower court offered an alternative no-submission rationale even assuming Valentine's stop-and-name account, while also stating that the record did not support that account. Both aspects must remain separate. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Force and actual submission are recognized seizure mechanisms. Whether an actual stop and answer amount to submission cannot be decided simply by labeling the interval short; the later act's role depends on whether a seizure had already occurred.

**Open Point:** The categorical significance, if any, of subsequent flight after brief compliance requires resolution. Whether Valentine actually complied is a distinct record question for determination under the proper review allocation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Third Circuit emphasized a recent eyewitness account delivered in person, the opportunity to observe demeanor, proximity to the suspect, late hour, neighborhood shootings and departure. It treated these as sufficient before the command. Alternatively, it concluded that even a brief pause and name did not constitute realistic submission, allowing the later charge toward the officer to support suspicion. It did not rely on the gun's later discovery to justify the initial command. [United States v. Valentine](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/232/232.F3d.350.00-1425.html).

**Government's Strongest Position:** A person who approaches police within sight of a nearby suspect takes more personal risk than a remote caller. Immediate eyewitness knowledge, detailed description, prompt matching and contextual danger warrant a short investigation before violence occurs. Even if initial suspicion was insufficient, an unanswered command did not seize Valentine; his attempt to rush past and push the officer supplied new grounds before physical restraint. A real pause must be distinguished from feigned or equivocal compliance.

**Valentine's Strongest Position:** The witness deliberately retained anonymity, supplied no observed offense beyond possession, and was not shown to be accountable. Late presence and ordinary walking away do not identify criminal behavior. A person who actually yields to a command and answers is seized during that interval; later escape cannot retroactively erase that seizure. A minimum-duration condition would let officers use the results of an unsupported detention to justify its beginning.

**Limits on Both Accounts:** The record does not support inventing a reliable informant history, witnessed threat, unlawful transfer, identifiable permit violation, or a finding of continuous compliance. Conversely, subsequent flight is not legally irrelevant if no seizure preceded it. The informant's possible exposure to retaliation is a contextual inference, not proof of veracity.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Identify credited facts concerning compliance, force and chronology. Apply the correct review allocation rather than allowing appellate sufficiency review to make disputed historical findings.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may find the initial totality sufficient; find those stated grounds insufficient while considering the separately preserved permit argument; or sustain the encounter on conduct occurring before actual seizure. It may clarify that submission requires objectively manifested yielding without a fixed minimum duration. If initial suspicion independently suffices, the second question may be unnecessary to the judgment, but the lower court's alternative legal rule must be treated explicitly.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance permits further prosecution with the suppression reversal intact. Vacatur can require renewed analysis of the initial basis and seizure point. If an unsupported completed seizure is established, analyze the challenged evidence and any preserved independent grounds under ordinary suppression principles; do not automatically order acquittal or dismissal. The firearm's eventual recovery does not establish the antecedent justification.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The disputed stop-and-name account and the unadjudicated permitting argument counsel against a final finding that every lawful path to admission fails. No universal rule about unnamed in-person witnesses or departure from police is warranted.

### Issue 1: Truthful public-concern disclosure and unlawful acquisition

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission controls ordinary protection of editorial speech, classification by what a restriction selects and why, and meaningful judicial examination of the claimed justification. Its later merits decision sustains a particular cable-carriage scheme on a developed record, without making every generally applicable speech restriction valid. Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. unanimously rejects categorical publisher immunity for purposeful, materially effective assistance to murder, while preserving protected advocacy, ordinary publication and the requirement of actual participation. Neither decision adjudicates this chain of unlawful interception followed by lawful receipt.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission requires a substantial governmental interest, actual advancement, adequate fit, and independent review of supported legislative predictions. Its qualification concerning least restrictive means belongs to the intermediate framework. Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. requires the stipulated combination of intent, material assistance and ordinary tort predicates; foreseeability or awareness of another’s wrongdoing alone does not satisfy it.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment governs the federal claim, and its application to state action governs the Pennsylvania claim. The historical federal statute, 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(c), reaches intentional disclosure with knowledge or reason to know that a communication was intercepted unlawfully; the historical civil-remedy provision is 18 U.S.C. § 2520. Pennsylvania’s parallel disclosure prohibition is also challenged. Their text does not exempt an otherwise covered defendant merely because someone else conducted the interception. The public judicial source reproduces the relevant enacted language and explains the contemporaneous remedies.
* **Material Other Authority:** Smith v. Daily Mail Publishing Co., Florida Star v. B. J. F. and Landmark Communications, Inc. v. Virginia supply received protection for lawful acquisition and publication of truthful information of public significance, while leaving the precise unlawful-source problem open. New York Times Co. v. United States concerns prior restraint and does not by itself settle subsequent damages. Cohen v. Cowles Media Co. and Branzburg v. Hayes deny a general press exemption from independently valid law. Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. expressly preserves the difference between that principle and treating the general-law label as sufficient constitutional justification. Sheets v. Salt Lake County recognizes a bounded constitutional protection against unjustified intentional official disclosure of intimate information entrusted for a limited investigation. It establishes the legal significance of that privacy interest while expressly leaving the private author's speech unregulated; it supplies no automatic publication restriction or damages rule against the private speakers here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law requires examination of publication as speech and of the speaker’s own conduct. It permits ordinary interception liability and rejects a blanket professional-press privilege. It does not establish that a stranger’s unlawful acquisition either automatically contaminates every later disclosure or automatically loses all privacy protection. A content-neutral source criterion and a direct prohibition on speech can coexist.
* **Open Point:** Whether the established protection for truthful public-concern publication extends to defendants who lawfully receive information from an unknown unlawful interceptor, and whether privacy or deterrence supplies a sufficient justification for this particular disclosure liability.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Petitioners and United States:** A person who knowingly distributes an unlawful recording compounds an invasion of private conversation even without causing the original tap. The statute protects the ability to speak candidly, not merely reputation. Congress may suppress demand for interceptions and may predict that disclosure liability will discourage them. The rule applies without selecting a viewpoint or bargaining position. General laws can bind publishers, and requiring proof of participation would leave serious privacy harm without an effective remedy when the interceptor is anonymous.

**Respondents:** Their conduct is publication of accurate information about a public dispute, acquired through means lawful for them. They neither arranged nor assisted the interception. Punishing that speech to deter an unidentified stranger shifts responsibility and burdens the public’s receipt of relevant information. The Government must justify the particular burden, not simply identify valuable privacy interests. Yocum’s communication has the same expressive character as a broadcaster’s dissemination.

**Reasoning below:** The panel agreed that the statutes were content neutral and used intermediate scrutiny. The majority regarded the burden on publication as substantially excessive in relation to the asserted privacy protection and ordered judgment for respondents. The dissent accepted the need to reduce incentives for interception and to prevent the separate injury caused by dissemination. The District Court had instead emphasized general applicability and the absence of a prior restraint. The Court must assess those competing rationales on review.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Affirm on an as-applied constitutional ground:** Recognize protection for truthful public-concern disclosure by a nonparticipant who lawfully received the material; affirm the summary-judgment direction on the certified assumptions. This resolves the open question concerning lawful receipt from an unlawful source.

**Reverse on adequate privacy justification:** Hold that independently harmful knowing disclosure may be regulated by this content-neutral statute, with a supported fit sufficient for the applicable standard; remand for the unresolved statutory facts and ordinary defenses. This would not establish damages automatically.

**Require further adjudication:** If an essential premise is genuinely outside the certified issue or cannot properly be treated as fixed for judgment, specify that premise and remand without deciding an abstract publication rule. A remand cannot manufacture participation or a different theory not supported by the record.

**Statutory construction:** The text reaches knowing disclosure by a noninterceptor. Avoidance cannot simply replace that text with an interceptor-only provision. The constitutional question therefore remains necessary on the accepted statutory assumptions.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Any constitutional ruling should identify lawful receipt, nonparticipation, truthfulness, public concern and the particular disclosure claim. It should distinguish liability for interception, procurement or other independently unlawful acquisition from publication liability, and distinguish a defense to damages here from an Act-wide invalidation.

**Material Uncertainty:** Intentional interception is assumed for the certified constitutional issue rather than found after trial. The unknown source does not establish defendants’ involvement or the effectiveness of a market-deterrence theory. Conversely, absence of direct proof of a deterrent effect is not a universal rule that legislative predictions require controlled empirical studies. No intervening controlling decision resolving the interception-publication question is identified.
