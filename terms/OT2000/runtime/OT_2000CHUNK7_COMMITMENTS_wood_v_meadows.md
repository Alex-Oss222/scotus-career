# Wood v. Meadows - frozen non-Stone commitments

**Event:** Merits, June 11, 2001; Fourth Circuit No. 99-1069, 207 F.3d 708.
**Stage:** Independent modeling before comparator and private Stone input; no assignment.
**Inputs:** Frozen C neutral handoff and supplemental receipt; C entering-law slice; full 2000 appellate opinion; the earlier No. 98-333 petition and its appended enforcement orders; only the authorized prior-term public entries for Burdick and Timmons.
**Research cutoff:** June 11, 2001. Later candidacy and election events are not presumed. The assigned date is an organizational premise, not recovered Supreme Court history.

## Jurisdiction, scope and current law

All eight Associates participate. The 1994 election cannot be reopened. Wood challenges the continuing primary-day Senate deadline as a voter and candidate; his earlier petition alleges renewed signature gathering in 1996, and the appended orders establish actual 1996 enforcement proceedings about prospective deadline relief. Those repeated election-related interests and the continuing annual rule support a reasonably recurring controversy that cannot ordinarily be fully reviewed before the ballot closes. This is a bounded inference from the record, not a finding of a specific promised future candidacy. The change in required signatures did not repeal the deadline. No contrary abandonment or end of Wood's voter interest appears in the supplied record. The eight Associates would reach the accepted deadline question on that basis, without reopening contempt or deciding a damages claim.

Norman's election-mootness holding and Moore's recurring-election principle support reachability, with the particular continuing interests identified rather than merely invoking an election label. Norman, Anderson, Burdick and Timmons supply the substantive calibrated inquiry. The actual burden must be compared with the State's precise interests and proportionate fit even when it is not severe. Strict scrutiny applies to a severe burden; ordinary reasonable, nondiscriminatory administration can be supported by important regulatory interests. The lower opinion's contrary suggestion that fit drops out whenever it calls a burden reasonable cannot become the Court's rule.

Cook prohibits official ballot penalties for noncompliance with a substantive political program; Virginia's neutral nomination calendar is not such a penalty. Chen's permissible population base and Brewer's school-transfer classification rule do not alter the election-access test. These sources supply no new general rule requiring the least intrusive deadline.

## Adversarial paths

**Affirm after applying the correct test.** January-to-June circulation includes the spring months; signers may sign several petitions and vote in party primaries; prior party nonaffiliation is not required. Party-primary candidates must file sixty days earlier, and other party nominations must close by June. A common nomination endpoint settles the field when the party contests end, allows an ensuing general campaign, and supports orderly verification and ballot preparation. The 30-day processing evidence does not establish that processing is the State's only permissible interest or that the latest technically possible date is constitutionally required. The strongest objection is that independent alternatives often respond to primary outcomes, so a simultaneous endpoint can burden them differently despite calendar symmetry. On the demonstrated complete system, six Associates conclude that this lost late-response opportunity does not make the burden severe or defeat the stated nomination-stage interests.

**Vacate for meaningful fit review.** A long signature-collection window does not answer the distinct inability to organize after the parties select their nominees. The lower court expressly disclaimed scrutiny of the extent of necessity for a reasonable burden and may therefore have accepted abstract field-closure and candidate-number claims without examining why this burden is proportionate. The strongest objection is that the complete record already establishes accessible routes and a genuine common nomination endpoint; remand must not covertly require strict scrutiny of every ordinary deadline. Stevens and Ginsburg would nevertheless require the lower court to evaluate the demonstrated late-response burden under the correct standard before preserving summary judgment.

**Immediate invalidation and a replacement deadline.** Wood's processing evidence supplies a serious challenge to a purely clerical justification, but does not establish that all nomination-stage interests are illegitimate or that a universal 30-day date follows. Neither group orders immediate invalidation on a least-restrictive-means theory or independently decides every signature requirement. No Justice treats the 1998 numerical amendment as the standard governing the 1994 exclusion.

## Frozen coalitions, remedy and law limits

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support affirmance and the shared bounded application above. Stevens and Ginsburg support vacatur and remand for correct fit analysis; their disagreement is not a holding that the deadline is unconstitutional. All eight preserve the governing Anderson-Burdick calibration and reject a rule that meaningful fit review disappears upon classifying a burden as reasonable.

The affirmance group leaves summary judgment and the challenged deadline in place without independently validating the revised signature requirement or other possible systems. It states that reasonable access and proportionate nomination-stage interests suffice on this record; it neither requires the latest administratively possible filing day nor guarantees an opportunity to respond to every subsequent political event. The remand group would preserve lawful surrounding rules and ask for the proper application to the existing record, with further proceedings only if an actual material issue requires them. Neither disposition places Wood on a completed ballot, orders a new election or resolves the separate enforcement-order litigation.

No source gap is treated as proof of litigant failure. The recurrence basis and the distinction between petition allegations and actual enforcement orders must remain in the internal Record. A genuine contrary jurisdictional fact discovered later would require affected neutral review; none is established now.
