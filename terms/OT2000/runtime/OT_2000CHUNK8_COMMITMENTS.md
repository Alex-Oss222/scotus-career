**D1 — State modifies both forms of review.** The coordinated phrase identifies state postconviction proceedings and other state collateral proceedings. ## Governing law and material alternatives

**F1 — Governing inquiry.** Agostini retains Lemon as one inquiry into actual governmental authority and function; purpose, effects, and institutional relationships are evidence, not independently operative purpose, endorsement, or entanglement prongs. An official message is attributable to the board, but attribution alone is not a violation. Government may not adopt religious doctrine as its own truth, solicit adherence through public authority, or use curricular authority to protect a favored religious account as an official religious task. Objective academic religious study, independent private expression, and genuine nondevotional conscience protection remain distinct. A prohibited governmental function does not require successful persuasion or a completed penalty.

Epperson's and Edwards's surviving curriculum rule denies schools constitutional immunity when they tailor teaching to a religious dogma; those cases do not prohibit every biblical reference or every scientific critique. Settle preserves genuine academic method and rejects categorical religious exclusions. Lee and Santa Fe concern actual school religious organization and representative religious choices; neither turns this nonprayer statement into a prayer. Good News Club protects its actual private forum and does not make the teacher's mandatory message private. Marshfield demands identification of a continuing governmental act; Children's Healthcare permits its limited accommodation and retains examination of the function actually performed.

**F2 — Official protection of the biblical account warrants affirmance.** The board requires science teachers to interpose its own assurance preserving a specifically identified religious explanation whenever evolution is taught. The adoption record and selective curricular placement explain the function of the text: protecting that religious account against the perceived implications of the science lesson, rather than merely informing pupils that education does not compel personal belief. Retention of the biblical reference after the failed creation-science initiative and explicit debate about the reference supports this conclusion in conjunction with the operative resolution; individual motives alone do not establish it. The generic reference to other concepts and the actual OR preserve personal choice but do not remove the board's particular official protective message. The final instruction to examine alternatives is read within that same message, not as an independent command that pupils affirm creationism.

The strongest contrary argument is that the school continues teaching evolution, commands no religious belief, permits independent judgment, and disclaims persuasion about all other concepts as well. F2 answers that the forbidden function need not be a prayer, a ban on science, or an order to believe. The State can preserve pupils' freedom of conscience without making protection of the biblical account part of its own required science presentation. The outcome depends on this complete text, actual directive, selective placement, and supported adoption context together; neither the word Bible alone nor a religious board member's motives is sufficient.

**F3 — Noncompulsion reassurance warrants reversal.** The statement tells pupils that receiving scientific instruction does not determine what they personally must believe. Its OR recognizes both independent judgment and parental belief; other concepts extends the reassurance beyond biblical belief; the invitation to critical examination does not prescribe the result. The board neither teaches the Genesis account as true, requires creation science, removes evolution, nor organizes worship. On this path the government undoubtedly speaks, but its actual function is a permissible assurance about the limit of its instruction, not religious adoption or solicitation. Protecting conscience may take account of a concrete religious concern without itself establishing that faith.

The strongest objection is the deliberately retained biblical reference, the creation-science history, and the restriction of the message to the subject at which that controversy is aimed. F3 accepts their evidentiary relevance but finds that they do not transform this operative noncompulsion statement into a forbidden religious function. The court should not substitute a presumed religious appeal for the actual OR text. This conclusion permits this disclaimer; it does not authorize compulsory creation instruction, religious grading criteria, hostility toward unbelief, or a parental veto over scientific content. No hypothetical new implementation is assumed in either direction.

**F4 — Remedy and severability.** F2 sustains the injunction against the required complete disclaimer while preserving lawful science teaching, objective religious study, private expression, and genuine conscience protection. It supplies no adjudication of the isolated first paragraph or a hypothetical amended resolution. F3 rejects the federal constitutional basis for that injunction and remands for conforming disposition, without ordering a new policy or prejudging any independently lawful issue outside the grant. No Associate votes for an evidentiary remand to obtain proof of conversion, because the existing legal dispute concerns the actual resolution and supported adoption record. No Associate treats the change from the panel's initial AND transcription to the actual OR as irrelevant; they disagree about what legal work it does in the full message.

**I1 — Two distinct clear showings.** Alaska requires clear inclusion of the navigable submerged estate in the reservation and clear intent to defeat the future State's otherwise presumptive title for an appropriate public purpose. The 1873 order's inclusion and congressional notice are conceded here. The contested second showing cannot be inferred from those concessions alone. Shively supplies the federal territorial power; Montana and Utah Division supply the strong state-title presumption and the requirement to identify effective retention rather than a useful general purpose.

**I2 — Timely enacted treatment retains the defined estate.** On this path the enacted 1886, 1888, and 1889 measures have different but cumulative legal work. The 1886 measure addresses cession outside the existing reservation; its ratification condition prevents the negotiated bargain itself from taking effect early. The 1888 railroad statute treats the existing reservation as a tribal estate, conditions the right of way on actual survey approval, consent, and compensation, and forbids the railroad to assist destruction of the Tribe's tenure. The 1889 measure authorizes purchase only of portions the Tribe consents to sell on just and equitable terms, with completion reserved to congressional ratification. Congress acted with notice that the defined reservation included the lake and river beds. Its enacted choice to obtain selected portions by consensual purchase preserves the remaining reservation pending that process, rather than allowing its navigable estate to pass to the State automatically in the interval. The admission instrument accepts the Indian-land disclaimer; that text corroborates retention of this already identified estate but does not independently reserve every bed beneath every Indian reservation.

The strongest objection is that recognizing present occupancy, requiring compensation for a railway, and authorizing negotiations are compatible with later state bed ownership. The ratification clauses expressly deny immediate effect to the agreements, and the 1890 Senate vote is not an enactment. I2 answers by relying on the enacted protection of the existing estate and consent-based cession process, not by treating the proposed bargains as already binding. Retention of this particular watery homeland preserves the subject of Congress's bargain and the uses established by the findings; geography and generalized dependence on fishing are not independent sufficient grounds. A later confirming enactment does not confiscate intervening state title because on I2 the relevant title never vested in Idaho.

**I3 — The enacted measures do not clearly defeat future state title.** On this path their definite legal effects concern railroad access, compensation, present tribal tenure, and negotiation authority. They neither consummate a permanent bed disposition before July 3, 1890 nor state the equivalent of Alaska's operative statehood exclusions. Continuing reservation occupancy and use can coexist with the State's presumptive bed title. The 1889 purchase condition makes the promised agreement contingent, rather than itself a title-preserving congressional segregation against admission. The general Indian-land disclaimer does not decide the antecedent question whether these particular beds had been retained against equal footing.

The strongest objection is that this reading makes Congress's consent-based cession program partially self-defeating and underreads the 1888 tenure-preservation terms. I3 accepts that the instruments have legal effect and that the water matters to the Tribe; it rejects only the further inference that those effects clearly withdraw future state bed title. Alaska's result rests on its identified statutory provisions, not an automatic equation between negotiation protection and title retention. No talismanic phrase is demanded, but the particular enactments must resolve the separate state-title consequence clearly.

**I4 — Later ratification, relation back, and aboriginal title.** A new postadmission reservation cannot itself divest a title already vested at admission. The 1891 ratification may confirm an earlier legally effective reservation, but its date cannot be moved by treating the June 1890 Senate vote as a completed law. The Tribe's distinct relation-back suggestion cites Wismer and Buttz; those decisions have not been separately examined in this model and no Justice is committed to adopting or rejecting that distinct doctrine on its merits. The narrower grant permits resolution of I2/I3 without making relation back a new holding. The preserved unextinguished aboriginal-title theory was not decided by either court below and may require first-instance consideration. If I2 is accepted, neither alternative needs decision. If I3 is accepted, the reservation ground fails but a final opposite title decree does not follow automatically; the unadjudicated alternative must receive lawful lower-court treatment. Agostini and Mitchell eliminate the asserted categorical establishment necessity. Effective secular control and genuinely neutral educational, cost and administrative criteria are less restrictive means than excluding every religious location. This path reverses the Ninth Circuit's constitutional judgment and remands to tailor the declaration and injunction against paragraph (3)'s categorical religious-site exclusion, retaining paragraphs (1), (2) and (4), the statutory conditions and lawful secular delivery choices. It neither orders an onsite specialist nor requires reproof of stipulated adequacy and safety.

**Strongest objection to R:** It converts constitutional permission into a compulsory government service arrangement although the entire statutory benefit is adequate and safely available, no secular onsite recipient exists and the family need not abandon its religious schooling. **Response available to R:** Its affirmative duty is only to decide location without a prohibited religious disability. The district may retain offsite service for genuine neutral reasons and need not extend the program beyond statutory commitments. A rule expressly withholding eligibility differs from a merely hypothetical unequal administration claim. A speculative institutional separation concern cannot alone support the categorical religion-based disability after the actual-control holdings.

**Path A — sustain this delivery arrangement at its proved scope.** The State continues supplying the child's complete adequate secular service without requiring a change of religious school. A publicly financed instructional location is the government's delivery arrangement, not private expression or religious conduct excluded from a forum. Constitutionally permitted onsite teaching need not become a constitutional obligation to make every otherwise permitted site available. With no interference with religious instruction, unsafe journey, inadequate service or actual unequal service recipient proved, the modest separation choice does not impose the actionable religious-exercise or unequal-access burden asserted here. This path affirms the Ninth Circuit judgment and leaves the district decree displaced; it does not prohibit onsite secular services or approve every religion-based benefit exclusion.

**Strongest objection to A:** The regulation's face reserves a legal possibility for nonreligious sites and denies it to religious sites; neither good motives nor a manageable cost cures discrimination. **Response available to A:** The relevant benefit remains complete public instruction, and government can choose a separately administered location without regulating the parents' exercise or financing the religious school's instructional operation. The significance of actual provision and the character of the governmental service distinguish a religious disqualification from benefits altogether. The conclusion must stay confined to these adequate, safe, nearby services; it cannot become a rule that express religious classifications always receive rational-basis review or that practical coercion is necessary to every Religion Clause violation.

These paths disagree about the constitutionally relevant benefit and burden, not about the stipulated facts or whether Agostini is binding. A new hearing to discover hypothetical devotional use is unnecessary on either path. No neutral source gap establishes a party's default.

## Record and lawful channel

The federal action challenges the lawful extent of tribal adjudicatory authority under 28 U.S.C. §1331. National Farmers Union Insurance Cos. v. The tribal trial and appellate courts have already considered jurisdiction. The question about further tribal exhaustion concerns immunity defenses, not an uncompleted opportunity for appellate examination of the previously determined jurisdictional issue.

Hicks's home is on his retained Indian trust allotment. This is neither non-Indian fee land nor a public highway stripped of ordinary exclusion. The State, official-capacity defendants and tribal defendants are no longer defendants on the retained damages claims. ## Entering law and exact legal divisions

- **Montana v. United States, 450 U.S. 544, 557, 564–567 (1981):** The decision recognizes nonmember hunting-and-fishing regulation on retained tribal or trust land while rejecting the reviewed fee-land regulation. Its nonmember principle and two exceptions must be read with those land-specific holdings. Consensual dealings, contracts, leases and other arrangements and conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare remain distinct sources.
- **Strate, April 28, 1997:** The public-highway holding does not itself decide retained trust land. All eight Associates joined the requirement of adequate substantive governing authority, the meaningful-nexus requirement and the separate predicates for coercive federal relief. Express forum consent and contractual privity are not universally necessary, but an unrelated relationship is insufficient.
- **Bourland, June 14, 1993:** Congressional acquisition displaced the identified exclusion-derived regulation on taken land, with residual authority separately preserved. It does not establish state exclusivity throughout a reservation. Souter's published dissent additionally resisted equating every loss of exclusion with loss of compatible sovereignty.
- **Atkinson, May 29, 2001:** The controlling rule concerns the challenged nonmember fee-land transactions. Kennedy, Souter and Thomas separately would begin with Montana for nonmember conduct on every land status; that is their own published position, not the Court's rule.
- **National Farmers Union; Iowa Mutual Insurance Co. v. LaPlante, 480 U.S. 9, 14–20 (1987):** Civil nonmember jurisdiction is not categorically eliminated by the criminal rule in Oliphant. Ordinary tribal examination and appellate exhaustion are prudential, not restrictions on federal subject-matter jurisdiction. Exhaustion does not manufacture substantive authority. National Farmers' exceptions concern bad-faith or harassing assertions of jurisdiction, patent violation of an express jurisdictional prohibition, and futility because no adequate opportunity exists to challenge jurisdiction; none is invented as a fact here.
- **El Paso Natural Gas, May 3, 1999:** All eight Associates joined federal determination of the specifically assigned Price-Anderson coverage task before tribal exhaustion. Neither a universal federal-claim exception nor a new removal power follows.
- **Section 1983:** Liability requires a person acting under color of the law of a State, Territory or the District of Columbia who causes a deprivation of a protected federal right. Its action-at-law, equity and other-proper-proceeding language creates the remedial cause but does not name or confer general jurisdiction on tribal courts. The judicial-officer injunction qualification concerns acts in a judicial capacity and the alternatives of a violated declaratory decree or unavailable declaratory relief. It supplies no warden defense and decides no earlier-act temporal question.
- **Hafer, November 5, 1991; Saucier, June 18, 2001:** Personal capacity, governing authority, federal forum competence and personal immunity are separate. Existing Harlow/Anderson objective immunity remains, without a controlling universal merits-first sequence or an authoritative-reliance replacement. Clearly established law must be assessed at the 1990 and 1991 conduct dates. Uncertainty about the tribal forum in 2001 is not itself uncertainty about whether the searches violated then-established federal rights.

The relevant register entries are **Tribal adjudication of nonmember public-highway claims**, present operation effective Strate, April 28, 1997, and **Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion**, present operation effective El Paso Natural Gas, May 3, 1999. Their actual source, nexus, exhaustion and remedial qualifications govern; neither entry states a general jurisdiction-before-immunity sequence.

## Components and statutory rule

**C1 — Statutory privilege.** Section 201(c) keeps copyright in the separate contribution distinct from copyright in the collective whole. Absent an express transfer of copyright or rights under it, the presumed privilege permits reproducing and distributing the contribution as part of that particular collective work, a revision of that work, or a later collective work in the same series. Section 103(b) gives the compiler rights in the compiler's contribution without enlarging rights in underlying material. Section 106(1) and (3) protect reproduction and distribution. Section 101's media-neutral definition of copies does not itself decide whether the contribution is reproduced as part of the required collective work.

### B. Binding consequences and the source of preclusion law

Section 502(a) deems a proof of claim filed under section 501 allowed unless a party in interest objects; the express party-in-interest inclusion covers a creditor of a general partner of a partnership debtor in Chapter 7. Rule 3001(f) gives a conforming proof prima facie evidentiary force. Section 1327(a) binds debtor and creditor to plan provisions whether or not the creditor's claim is provided for and whether the creditor objected, accepted or rejected. Sections 1327(b) and (c) govern vesting and freedom from covered claims or interests, each subject to contrary plan or confirmation-order provision; subsection (c) concerns property vesting under (b) and a creditor provided for by the plan. None of these consequences is erased by the proposed decision. None establishes, without identification of what occurred, that a particular fact was contested and decided.

Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp., February 27, 2001, is Scalia's unanimous opinion. Its federal common-law rule ordinarily incorporates rendering-state claim-preclusion law for federal diversity judgments on state-law claims unless a specifically demonstrated federal interest conflicts. Its Rule 41(b) holding rejects an automatic universal consequence from a rendering-court merits designation. This case concerns federal bankruptcy and issue preclusion; the rendering-state incorporation rule is not carried over. Semtek supports attention to the particular effect claimed, not an automatic answer here.

Heintz v. Jenkins, April 18, 1995, is Souter's unanimous opinion rejecting a categorical exemption for attorneys regularly collecting covered consumer debts through litigation. It leaves statutory definitions, activity-specific exceptions, conduct elements and defenses operative. The later statutory exception in section 1692e(11) for formal pleadings concerns the specified disclosure requirement; it is not an exception to all of section 1692e. Sections 1692e and 1692f(1) must be applied to the actual alleged representation or unauthorized collection. An unsuccessful collection claim is not by itself an FDCPA violation. The unpresented question whether an FDCPA claim may ever be predicated on a bankruptcy filing is not resolved by the lower opinion or this commitment.

Hartford Underwriters, May 30, 2000, is Thomas's unanimous opinion enforcing the actor and remedy designated in section 506(c), with genuinely separate remedies reserved on their own conditions. Pioneer, March 24, 1993, governs excusable neglect where Rule 9006(b)(1) permits it, attributes counsel's conduct and preserves (b)(2) prohibitions and (b)(3) limitations. Neither authorizes a new equitable exception or conclusively decides the present defense.

### C. Remedy and lawful limits

Vacatur removes the Seventh Circuit's issue-preclusion basis and returns the action for further proceedings. It does not find fraud, determine collateral value, award damages, alter a plan, extend a bankruptcy deadline or determine the full effect of the bankruptcy case's dismissal. The lower courts may address a distinct properly preserved and sufficiently supported defense, including claim preclusion, under the correct rule. No such defense is declared established. A blanket bankruptcy/FDCPA displacement rule, new litigation immunity, sanctions or revocation relief is not selected on an issue the parties did not present. The availability of sanctions and revocation procedures does not establish their predicates or their exclusivity. Reinstating consideration of the complaint is not a liability judgment.

## 1. Components, authority, and decisional paths

### P — Cigarette advertising preemption

This component concerns only cigarette applications of §21.04(5)(a)–(b), asserted by Lorillard, Brown & Williamson, R.J. Reynolds, and Philip Morris. It does not extend the FCLAA to cigars or smokeless tobacco, decide cigarette retail-access preemption, or reopen the unappealed §21.04(6) severance.

**Path P1: express displacement.** The current §1334(b) covers a state prohibition based on smoking and health with respect to advertising or promotion of federally labeled cigarettes. A tobacco-specific geographical or height ban is such a prohibition even though it regulates placement instead of requiring a different warning. The change from required statements to requirements or prohibitions, the separate wording of subsection (a), and the activity phrase defeat a content-only limitation. General signage controls resting on other grounds, actual sales regulation, and independently actionable deception retain their own predicates.

**Strongest objection to P1:** Cipollone preserves independent duties, the enacted scheme principally establishes nationally uniform smoking-health information, and the State traditionally controls locations and children's access. Reading the clause to disable local spatial protection can exceed the warning scheme and its clear displacement. **Answer sufficient for P1 supporters:** the State's reasons for protecting minors from tobacco consumption remain smoking-and-health reasons; the rule directly selects tobacco advertising, rather than incidentally affecting it. Neither a generally worded enabling statute nor the location label changes that operative duty. Express displacement does not require a second showing that a geographical prohibition makes federal compliance impossible.

**Path P2: preserve the location rules.** Read the clause within its warning-and-information architecture and against displacement of traditional state police powers. These rules leave the federally prescribed cigarette message intact and confine its placement near schools and playgrounds; they do not impose a new smoking-health statement or warning-neutralization duty. Cigarette preemption therefore supplies no ground for reversal.

**Strongest objection to P2:** Congress broadened both the covered obligation and its relation to advertising; a positive prohibition on tobacco advertising seems to fit those words directly. **Answer sufficient for P2 supporters:** the expansion reaches more legal forms and negative controls over the federal informational subject, but the smoking-and-health qualification still limits the domain in statutory context. This is not immunity for all measures called zoning, and it does not disturb Cipollone's covered advertising-warning duties.

### O and H — Advertising under the First Amendment

O-C, O-S, and O-G concern outdoor/exterior-visible advertising of cigarettes, smokeless tobacco, and cigars, respectively. H-C, H-S, and H-G concern the corresponding below-five-foot point-of-sale restrictions within the same geographical zones. Cigarette speech positions of P1 supporters are expressly conditional alternatives; preemption is their sufficient cigarette ground. The cigar and smokeless speech questions are independently reached.

**Path A1: uphold these child-protection restrictions.** The existing record's reports, studies, marketing history, and supported inferences establish a material relationship between promotion and underage use, without a required experiment isolating each medium and product. The State may combine access controls with demand controls. Schools and playgrounds identify places where children are likely to encounter advertising; above-five-foot indoor expression, advertising outside the zones, other media, and the actual product-specific exceptions materially limit the burden. A legislature need not criminalize children's possession, await the failure of all existing programs, or prove an exact optimum radius before adopting a reasonable boundary.

**Strongest objection to A1:** the zones combine with existing restrictions to eliminate the ordinary public-facing channel across much of the urban market; existing channels may be inaccessible to a small shop. The rule reaches unobtrusive adult price and brand information rather than only large signs or child-directed appeals. The height distinction poorly corresponds to all minors' sight lines. The missing cigarette/smokeless small-sign permission and the cigar permission's very limited content must be counted. **Answer sufficient for A1 supporters:** the constitutionally relevant relationship is repeated exposure near children's destinations, not whether each sign specifically targets them or can be read from the school itself. Both aggregate urban coverage and remaining communication matter, but neither commercial third-party overbreadth nor a demand for zero adult spillover supplies the governing standard. The height rule reduces immediate visual encounters rather than purporting to prevent any child from looking upward.

**Path A2: invalidate the challenged categorical advertising applications for disproportionate fit.** Even crediting the State's substantial interest and the supported promotion/use relationship, the categorical rules impose an inadequately justified burden on truthful adult commercial communication. One radius covers very different signs, sizes, settings, and retail communications. The actual urban reach and cumulative existing restraints make this a major channel restriction, not merely a small adjustment. The height rule's treatment of any portion of a display, older minors, and upward viewing lacks a sufficiently responsive connection to its substantial adult burden. The Court can decide this proportionality question without declaring that advertising never affects youth use or converting disputed maps into an agreed incremental percentage.

**Strongest objection to A2:** Fox rejects least-restrictive-means review; youth demand is different from direct sales access; the State already uses substantial taxes, education, enforcement, and counter-advertising. **Answer sufficient for A2 supporters:** no exhaustion or alternative-policy mandate is imposed. Those efforts may support a need for additional action, but do not justify this rule's actual breadth. The Court need not prescribe a smaller radius, a replacement sign rule, or an alternative enforcement program to find the present balance deficient.

**Path A3: stronger protection.** Thomas would apply strict scrutiny to this content-based suppression of truthful information about lawful adult transactions: a compelling child-protection objective does not dispense with narrow tailoring and consideration of materially less restrictive ways to preserve adult communication. Commercial subject matter alone supplies no reduced protection. He recognizes the special legitimacy of protecting minors and does not apply his adult-ignorance objection as a bar to every child-specific rule. His sufficient ground here is the broad suppression of lawful adult speech in mixed-audience locations without the tailoring required to accommodate that protected audience. The government's objective may be compelling; the means still fail. No other Justice is committed in this file to a replacement commercial-speech tier.

### R — Physical retail access, with separate products and parties

R-C concerns only Lorillard and Brown & Williamson's First Amendment claims under §21.04(2)(c)–(d). R-S concerns USSTC's corresponding claim. R-G concerns the cigar petitioners' §22.06(2)(c)–(d) claim, including premium-cigars' sensory inspection. R-C is not attributed to R.J. Reynolds or Philip Morris, and no retail preemption holding is proposed.

**Path R1: uphold.** These rules control access to the physical tobacco product, promote clerk-mediated age checks, and inhibit unauthorized acquisition. They leave visible displays, informative empty packages, clerk-assisted transactions and inspection, and adult-only self-service facilities available on their actual terms. Ordinary sealed-package access is conduct, even though its display can communicate. For cigars, assuming protected informational inspection, the rule still proportionately advances access control while preserving assisted inspection and the adult-only option. This is an independent access justification, not a borrowing of disputed advertising causation.

**Strongest objection to R1 / path R2:** packages communicate prices, freshness, and brands; premium-cigar customers compare texture, aroma, weight, and other features. Empty boxes are not a complete substitute, and the adult-only condition excludes accompanied children. **Answer sufficient for R1 supporters:** the State has not prohibited the information or every lawful inspection. Clerk assistance changes physical access, which is the regulatory objective, without forbidding the adult transaction or the manufacturer's message. The burden is not justified simply by telling a shop to exclude children; assisted access itself remains. No record finding that every shoplifted cigar came from a self-service display, or that such theft supplies most youth cigars, is needed to recognize the direct access relationship. The regulation need not eliminate every route of acquisition to make a material contribution.

### S — Cigar sampling presentation and remedy

Section 22.06(1)(a) is named in the cigar brief. The complete submitted briefing develops no distinct sampling merits analysis. No separate cigarette or smokeless sampling challenge is added to the presently identified claims.

If paragraph (1)(C) is invoked, its actual requirements must be decided rather than inferred from unsuccessful foreign arrangements. The record does not establish a formal civil commitment, a special security detention basis, harmlessness, or present acceptance by a receiving country. Any alternative custody route needs its own actual authority, predicates and process. No Justice imports a special national-security regime into these ordinary records.

The current December 21, 2000 section 241.4 replaces the old clear-and-convincing wording with a satisfaction showing by the alien. It still requires the stated nonviolence, safety, compliance and flight findings before discretionary release. It provides district records review and a possible interview, headquarters review with a required personal interview in the specified nonrelease circumstances, written reasons, chosen assistance under its stated limits and periodic review with timing exceptions. No current administrative appeal exists, apart from completion of cases already pending before the Board on December 21 under the transition rule. Travel-document prospects must be considered; cooperation and the rule's distinct revocation grounds remain relevant. The full validated regulatory unit controls those conditions and qualifications. The government's claims of adequate operation are not findings, and the petitioner's November 2000 objections do not establish that the replacement lacks these procedures. The government's historical description of Hendricks is not adopted over actual simulated law.

Foucha's actual controlling propositions require a present lawful basis and reject its indefinite psychiatric dangerousness substitute; they reserve materially narrower preventive schemes and do not prescribe a universal proof burden. Flores concerns temporary juvenile care, a conditions agreement, specified release avenues and preserved delay review. National Center for Immigrants' Rights permits a bounded release condition connected to the immigration scheme. Actual Hendricks invalidated a particular retrospective punitive application; its diagnostic discussion is non-dispositive. Seling, joined by every Associate this term, preserves cumulative punishment inquiry, demanding proof, and the distinction between unlawful custody and deficient conditions. Neither the civil label nor the absence of a fixed endpoint alone decides this case. These commitments do not find punishment on the current record or require the detainees to prove punishment before contesting statutory detention authority.

## Components and distinct rules

**J — Review.** All eight accept jurisdiction over the actual habeas legality claims under section 2241 and the controlling channel decisions. Effective relief is available if custody is unlawful, without disturbing the removal order or converting habeas into ordinary appellate review of removal merits.

**A — Purpose-bound statutory authority, the primary rule of Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** For the previously admitted persons presented here, paragraph (6) permits detention beyond the removal period for a period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. Once removal has no reasonable prospect within a reasonably foreseeable future, that provision does not authorize continuing imprisonment solely because a former offender may be dangerous or might fail to appear for a removal that has no realistic prospect. The agency retains a reasonable opportunity to arrange removal; uncertainty and ordinary diplomatic delay are not themselves disqualifying. Continuing flight and safety concerns may affect detention while removal remains realistically attainable and the lawful conditions of release. They do not supply a new general civil-commitment power after the removal ground ceases to justify confinement.

This construction rests on the statute's removal setting, discretionary rather than mandatory extension, and express supervised-release alternative, reinforced by the serious liberty problem of potentially lifelong imprisonment without a closely limited independent preventive ground. It uses constitutional avoidance; it does not announce a categorical constitutional ban on every preventive detention regime. The criminal and risk categories retain work in determining who may be detained beyond 90 days while removal remains a genuine prospect. The competing construction fairly invokes those categories, the words permitting detention beyond the period and the absence of an endpoint; it is not dismissed as textually impossible. Its difficulty is converting this removal authority into an unrestricted substitute for a separately justified civil commitment.

**A's implementation.** Ninety days is not a release deadline. A detainee who has remained confined beyond that period must present concrete grounds, considered with elapsed time, showing that the reasonable removal opportunity has run out and removal lacks a realistic foreseeable prospect. The government must answer that supported showing with facts supporting such a prospect, rather than the bare order, assertions of good faith, danger alone, or the theoretical possibility that a country may someday change its policy. The court assesses the actual attempts, refusals, negotiations, likely receiving arrangements, the detainee's conduct and time already spent; it gives appropriate weight to supported executive assessments of diplomatic prospects without treating them as conclusive. Increasing elapsed time makes a nonspecific prediction less persuasive. No requirement of permanent impossibility and no requirement of a formal repatriation agreement applies. A sufficient current countershowing permits continued custody; otherwise the court must provide supervised release under the statute. This is an operative legal standard with factual application left below, not permission to postpone the authority question indefinitely.

No fixed number of months, automatic release date, new periodic-review calendar or universal evidentiary-hearing command is adopted on these supplied sources. A numerical presumption could aid uniform administration, but would be a new judicial rule requiring a reason for its chosen interval. The old statute's six-month removal period and the new regulation's usual transition to headquarters after the 90-day period and an additional three months are real reference points. Neither purports to establish a substantive custody ceiling or proves that the same interval is appropriate for every receiving-country problem. The primary commitments favor the stated individualized standard and do not condition relief on adoption of a number. A new proposed interval cannot be attributed to these Justices without a grounded join test.

**B — Express discretionary detention authority, the primary statutory rule of Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Paragraph (6) permits continued custody of the covered persons without making a judicial prediction of foreseeable removal an additional statutory prerequisite. Its distinct criminal, danger and noncompliance categories and its conditional supervision language do more than extend a fixed transportation window. The authority serves execution of the valid removal order and the statutory protective purposes while the government genuinely pursues removal. Lack of a treaty, unsuccessful attempts or inability to predict a date does not alone terminate that authority. The three reject a court-created duration or foreseeability limitation as statutory construction. Their agreement on B does not establish agreement on the constitutional safeguards or the Fifth Circuit disposition.

**K — Kennedy's additional constitutional limit.** For an admitted person held beyond the ordinary period, the government must supply an individualized, current justification connected to the statutory protective or removal purposes, with a meaningful opportunity to contest it and effective habeas review. A final order and the proposition that removal is not forever impossible do not alone justify prolonged custody. A substantial current individual danger, or a concrete compliance concern connected to a genuine removal process, can justify continued nonpunitive detention even though a removal date cannot reasonably be forecast. The justification must actually persist; stale criminal conduct and a paper review cannot be treated as irrebuttable. The court must assess whether the supported reason remains sufficient in light of the duration and actual circumstances, including the practical ability of lawful conditions to address that reason. This rule does not require an ordinary psychiatric diagnosis, does not transplant a particular civil-commitment burden or a universal least-restrictive-means test, and does not make every unsuccessful negotiation require release. The new regulation supplies meaningful mechanisms and possible evidence of compliance; it is not facially invalidated. Its alien satisfaction wording cannot insulate a prolonged detention from habeas determination of the government's actual current reason. This resolves the substantive need for a continuing reason and the necessary effectiveness of review; remand supplies the individual facts and any tailored procedural remedy. Zadvydas's intervening years at liberty particularly require a present decision rather than assumed persistence of the old custody rationale.

The Fifth Circuit's actual rule did require danger or flight, continuing good-faith efforts and reasonable parole and periodic-review procedures; it was not a bare-order authorization. Kennedy's narrower refusal concerns its very high impossibility threshold and the sufficiency of that framework to resolve the physical-liberty claim. Under K, inability to prove harmlessness plus a theoretically possible future removal cannot make an executive danger conclusion conclusive. The habeas court must independently assess the supported current justification with the duration and effective conditions in view, even though it does not substitute its own general immigration policy. This is the additional legal instruction supporting his vacatur, rather than a fictitious omission of all review below or the mere later adoption of a new regulation.

**S/T — Scalia and Thomas's narrower constitutional disposition.** The presented general challenge does not establish that continued detention under B, with genuine removal efforts, a reasonable individual administrative foundation and available release review, is unconstitutional merely because its endpoint cannot be predicted. Their statutory conclusion does not validate punitive confinement, sham removal efforts, arbitrary administration, denial of access to review, or a refusal ever to reconsider release. They do not impose K's additional government-justification formulation or A's foreseeability trigger. Actual Seling remains binding if a supported punishment claim is made; it supplies no finding of punishment here. The current general duration claim is rejected, rather than remanded for an unspecified constitutional theory.

**RZ/RM — Companion remedies.** Under A, vacate the Fifth Circuit judgment concerning Zadvydas and remand because permanent impossibility plus executive good faith and review is too permissive. Vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment concerning Ma and remand because the absence of an agreement cannot replace an individualized assessment of actual foreseeable removal. Neither vacatur itself orders release or renewed detention. The habeas courts apply A and order or preserve supervised release when detention lacks its required ground; if a genuine foreseeable removal prospect supports custody, they may deny the release claim, without the Supreme Court itself directing a new detention. Zadvydas's effective release order and the stayed appellate mandate must be handled in their actual posture; the judgment does not recast his years at liberty as custody or require an unnecessary new release event. Properly presented cooperation allegations require findings and application of the distinct statutory provision. Under K, Kennedy also vacates both judgments, but for a distinct individualized-justification inquiry: danger or concrete noncompliance may sustain custody even without A's foreseeable-removal showing. Under S/T, affirm the Fifth Circuit against the presented general duration challenge; reverse the Ninth Circuit's categorical release basis and remand under B and the retained limits. Even S/T would require the agency to decide any renewed Zadvydas custody under current standards rather than order his automatic return. No position infers that Ma must automatically be rearrested or that an existing release condition or stay changes without a proper order.

**P — Review-process issue and completeness.** A's five supporters decide that the new administrative machinery does not cure the statutory defect if it permits detention after A's removal-purpose basis ends. The current rule may consider travel prospects and other circumstances, but consideration alone is not A's required substantive consequence. The satisfaction showing and safety criteria cannot operate as an additional obstacle to relief from detention already unauthorized under A. No independent facial procedural invalidation, automatic hearing schedule or universal official-compliance finding is needed for that complete statutory resolution. Kennedy gives the current review machinery full weight but requires its actual use to satisfy K; he finds no categorical failure solely because the first review is by an immigration official, and mandates no replacement court hearing absent a material defect. Scalia and Thomas regard the present regulatory safeguards and retained habeas as sufficient to defeat the general duration/process objection presented, without declaring every application compliant. None of the eight uses superseded clear-and-convincing wording to decide the current regulatory question.

**I — Consequences shared by all eight.** Supervision preserves the order and the prospect of later removal. Paragraph (3)'s periodic identification, necessary government-funded medical or psychiatric examination, sworn information and reasonable written restrictions apply on release. Conditions must remain within lawful authority. Release does not grant admission anew, lawful permanent status, asylum, a waiver, cancellation or immunity from removal. A later detention or alternative custody decision requires an actual lawful source and satisfied predicates; neither an unlimited recommitment power nor immunity from otherwise lawful custody is announced. No contempt, new crime, obstruction or dangerousness finding is made.

## Individual commitments and adversarial join tests

### Stevens

**Components and remedy:** J; A with its concrete-showing implementation; RZ and RM vacatur and individualized remand under A; I. He would join the removal-purpose construction and its avoidance support. He does not require a numerical presumption or a new universal hearing schedule.

**Own date-eligible grounds:** His National Center for Immigrants' Rights opinion (December 16, 1991) ties delegated discretion to the statutory scheme and preserves actual individual safeguards. His Flores dissent (March 23, 1993) treats unnecessary institutional confinement as the relevant liberty deprivation and rejects administrative convenience as an independent statutory purpose. His joins of Foucha's Parts II-A, II-B and II-C (May 18, 1992) require continuing grounds, distinguish substantive authority from hearings and reject that indefinite dangerousness substitute. His actual Hendricks Parts II–III join and Seling join support scrutiny of what custody does, without converting duration alone into punishment. His present-term St. Cyr opinion treats a legality judgment as requiring effective relief within the governing channel.

**Strongest competing path:** B gives genuine effect to Congress's criminal and danger categories, and his own National Center opinion rejects a claim that every immigration restriction serves appearance alone. He nevertheless distinguishes a supervised-release work condition from potentially lifelong imprisonment. A permits extended removal detention and risk-sensitive lawful conditions; B would let the removal provision carry a much larger independent commitment function without corresponding substantive limits.

**Barriers and conditional joins:** He will not join an impossibility test, a civil-label answer, or a process-only response after statutory authority ends. He can join a narrower fact-specific disposition if it applies A to both records and requires effective relief when removal is not realistically foreseeable. He declines an automatic release rule based solely on 90 days, treaty absence or prior admission, and does not adjudicate initial-entry custody. A broader as-applied due-process ground is available if the statute is held to unmistakably command the contested indefinite authority, but is unnecessary to his primary statutory disposition. Separate-writing function, if needed: explain why procedure and theoretical diplomatic possibility do not supply a missing custody ground.

### O'Connor

**Components and remedy:** J; A; RZ/RM vacatur under A; I. Her concurrence-level reservation concerns carefully delimited preventive regimes, not an exception that swallows A for anyone deemed risky. She would directly join A's application to these admitted persons and its bounded remedy.

**Own date-eligible grounds:** Her Foucha concurrence and Parts II joins reserve narrower detention while rejecting the indefinite regime actually before the Court. Her Flores concurrence, joined by Souter, recognizes a substantial liberty interest and rests on genuine protective care, the conditions agreement and temporary custody. Her actual Hendricks dissent (June 23, 1997) regards indefinite duration alone as insufficient proof of punishment but emphasizes current qualifying conditions and real release routes; it does not approve every preventive confinement statute. Her Seling join keeps those predicates and the separate unlawful-custody remedy in view. Her Calcano Court opinion supplies the actual review channel.

**Strongest competing path:** B plus K could preserve express congressional protective authority through individualized, effective review. Her Hendricks dissent makes that a substantial alternative: difficult treatment and uncertain duration do not nullify genuine civil custody. The new personal-interview, headquarters and reason-giving requirements strengthen that path and cannot be treated as nonexistent. Here, however, a broad predicted danger or criminal category lacks the more confined current-condition predicate she considered in Hendricks. In a removal statute, hearings about danger do not by themselves answer why detention continues when removal lacks a realistic prospect.

**Barriers and conditional joins:** She refuses a universal rule that preventive custody always needs psychiatric illness, an assumption that civil confinement with an unknown endpoint is punishment, or a ruling on every inadmissible person. She can join A without a fixed-month device and can agree to case-specific timing tied to actual prospects. A new numerical presumption needs an independent justification and cannot be a mandatory release ceiling. She would require a materially narrower, actually established statutory ground before considering different safety detention; no such ground is supplied here. Separate-writing function, if needed: preserve those narrower regimes and distinguish statutory authority, punishment and conditions.

**Own date-eligible grounds:** His Flores Court opinion (March 23, 1993) defines the actual liberty claim and respects the statute's discretionary custody arrangement while preserving concrete unlawful-delay challenges. His join of Thomas's Foucha dissent treats the legal custody channel and its review as significant and rejects automatic transfer of ordinary commitment rules. His actual Hendricks Part I and O'Connor-dissent joins reject a vocabulary rule and the inference that indefinite duration alone proves punishment, while preserving current lawful predicates. His Seling join accepts proof of actual punitive operation rather than categorical immunity. Calcano's separate writing and St. Cyr's Part I join distinguish his original reading from the binding jurisdictional result now applied.

**Strongest competing path:** A invokes serious liberty concerns and an ordinary removal purpose, with Foucha supporting the need for a continuing basis. He regards the new statute itself as the relevant continuing authority: it expressly permits detention beyond the removal period for covered criminal and protective categories and makes supervision conditional on release. A foreseeable-removal prerequisite would narrow the permission by a condition Congress did not state. The same detention phrase should not receive an unexplained different meaning simply because one statutory category raises different constitutional questions.

**Barriers and conditional joins:** He can join rejection of permanent-impossibility and treaty proxies as universal factual measures without accepting A's legal trigger, and can join a remand confined to a proved defect in review or exercise of discretion. He will not join a judicial month limit, a freestanding right of everyone ordered removed to release into this country, or a theory requiring the full psychiatric-commitment template. His position does not deem admitted residents unadmitted, excuse actual punishment or invent absent obstruction. He declines K's newly specified government justification as a general requirement on this record; existing actual review and habeas remain legally consequential. Separate-writing function: identify the textual and constitutional objections to A and distinguish jurisdiction agreement from merits dissent.

### Kennedy

**Components and remedy:** J under controlling precedent with the Calcano reservation; B, coupled with K rather than S/T; vacate both judgments under K; I. **Strongest competing paths:** A supplies a direct purpose-and-duration safeguard for serious physical liberty and avoids making an ordinary removal statute general preventive authority. He accepts B's textual point and the legal significance of these real procedural requirements but rejects the proposition that good faith, an imaginable eventual removal and available procedures automatically settle a prolonged restraint. The government must identify the continuing personal justification; a formal review finding cannot be its own conclusive proof. **Barriers and conditional joins:** He will not join A as statutory meaning, a numerical presumption derived without record support, or a declaration that current danger can never justify this post-order custody. **Barriers and conditional joins:** He refuses to equate unsuccessful diplomacy with bad faith or to make lack of a treaty conclusive; a genuine government factual response matters. **Barriers and conditional joins:** He rejects an automatic importation of ordinary commitment requirements, a numerical period supplied by judicial preference, and the equation of indefinite with punitive. ### Breyer

**Strongest competing path:** B plus a demanding individual-review requirement can protect safety and leave diplomatic assessments to officials with superior information. **Barriers and conditional joins:** He refuses both a formal-agreement prerequisite and a good-faith-only answer.
