# Alexander v. Sandoval

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 5-4, April 24, 2001.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Justice Stevens dissented with Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
* **Historical Holding:** No private right of action exists to enforce the disparate-impact regulations promulgated under §602. The validity of those regulations was assumed.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical majority's treatment of private actions does not displace the governing holding of Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A.

* **Historical Sources:** [Alexander v. Sandoval](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-275.pdf). The historical disposition is comparison material only and does not govern this case.

# Sterling v. Borough of Minersville

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified. The Third Circuit sustained denial of qualified immunity in its November 6, 2000 opinion, amended November 14, 2000, with a dissent on clearly established law.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Mansmann wrote the majority opinion, joined by Judge Rendell. Judge Stapleton dissented from denial of immunity while accepting that the alleged coercion could violate privacy. The panel separately treated Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention as outside immediate appellate jurisdiction.

**Historical Holding:** The majority treated sexual orientation as protected intimate information and the threat as a compromise of protected security without legitimate official justification. It regarded the earlier confidentiality authorities as sufficiently clear. The dissent found inadequate notice of both orientation protection and liability for a threat. The historical majority's threat analysis is broader than the proposed supplement's operative-coercion rationale.

**Historical Authority:** The lower judgment is a historical anchor and is and does not establish controlling Supreme Court precedent. The governing decisions discussed in Section I do not purport to describe the authorities applied by the historical panel.

**Historical Sources:** [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/).

# Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court reversed and remanded, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor’s Parts I, II, III-A and IV had Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Part III-B, discussing possible additional exceptions, had only Rehnquist and Kennedy; Part III-C, finding no actual sentencing effect, also had Thomas. Souter dissented with Stevens and Ginsburg; Breyer separately dissented.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court extended the general restriction on attacks through enhanced sentences to § 2254 and preserved the complete-denial-of-counsel exception.
* **Historical Authority:** The faultless-unavailability discussion and the factual no-effect rationale lacked five direct joins. They must not be represented as unanimous or as governing law.

* **Historical Sources:** [Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-394.pdf). The historical disposition is comparison material only and does not govern this case.

# Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court vacated and remanded, 8-1.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Thomas also concurred separately; Scalia concurred in the judgment. Ginsburg dissented.
* **Historical Holding:** Courts of appeals independently review the constitutional excessiveness of punitive awards; the Court held that this legal review did not impermissibly reexamine a jury finding of fact. It identified potentially flawed applications of the guideposts without itself fixing the lawful amount.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical opinion extended the review reasoning of Ornelas v. United States and United States v. Bajakajian and distinguished compensatory review under Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc. These historical relationships are comparison material only.

# Rogers v. Tennessee

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court affirmed, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote for Rehnquist, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Stevens dissented separately. Scalia dissented, joined by Stevens and Thomas and, as to Part II, Breyer. Breyer also wrote separately.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical majority treated the due-process restriction on judicial change as distinct from automatic incorporation of every legislative ex-post-facto category, and concluded that abolition here was not unexpected and indefensible given the common-law rule’s history and widespread abandonment.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinion distinguished Bouie v. City of Columbia and considered the fair-warning line while declining to equate all judicial development with legislative retroactivity. That historical assessment is quarantined and does not establish the proposed disposition.

* **Historical Sources:** [Rogers v. Tennessee](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-451.pdf). The historical disposition is comparison material only and does not govern this case.

# United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court reversed and remanded, 8-0 among participating Justices. Breyer did not participate.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Stevens concurred in the judgment, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, emphasizing the limits of the distribution question and objecting to broader discussion.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court rejected a medical-necessity exception for manufacture and distribution under the Controlled Substances Act. The majority also rejected the proposed equitable route to that exemption while recognizing ordinary discretion concerning injunctions. It did not decide the independent constitutional challenges.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical analysis addressed statutory structure, United States v. Bailey and United States v. Rutherford, and the traditional-equity cases. Its broader language about other prohibitions was contested in the concurrence and does not establish a governing holding.

# Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** On May 14, 2001, the Supreme Court reversed and remanded, 8-1.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** The Court issued a per curiam opinion. Justice Ginsburg concurred in part and in the judgment, agreeing that the arbitrator’s award should not have been disturbed and declining additional remedial discussion. Justice Stevens dissented, questioning both the substantive clarity and the use of summary reversal without plenary briefing and argument.
* **Historical Holding:** The court of appeals improperly replaced the arbitrator’s factfinding and credibility determination, then compounded that error by directing an award for Garvey. The per curiam opinion also stated that even a properly vacated award ordinarily calls for further arbitration rather than judicial resolution of the contractual merits.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical majority relied on the received labor-arbitration cases and on the historical decision in Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America. That historical reliance is confined to the comparator and supplies no additional governing decision. Ginsburg’s limited concurrence is not a join in every remedial statement.

* **Historical Sources:** [Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-504.pdf). The historical disposition is comparison material only and does not govern this case.

# Bartnicki v. Vopper / United States v. Vopper

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** On May 21, 2001, the historical Supreme Court affirmed the Third Circuit, 6-3.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court treated the statutes as content neutral yet directly restrictive of speech. On the accepted facts, a stranger’s unlawful interception did not remove protection from respondents’ accurate publication of information of public concern, lawfully acquired by defendants who had not participated in the interception. It found the proposed deterrence justification inadequate and acknowledged a stronger independent privacy interest, which did not prevail in this setting. The decision did not license unlawful newsgathering or determine publication rules for purely private material.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for Justices O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Breyer, joined by Justice O’Connor, also concurred, emphasizing the unusually strong public interest in the recorded threat of harm and a fact-specific accommodation of privacy and speech. Chief Justice Rehnquist dissented with Justices Scalia and Thomas, stressing conversational privacy, general applicability and legislative judgment about deterrence. The concurrence joined the Court’s opinion rather than supplying two votes only for the judgment.
* **Historical Authority:** The proposed disposition agrees with the historical judgment and extends the governing publication cases to lawful receipt from an unlawful source. Its boundary turns on the accepted conduct and subject matter, not on imputing a historical vote or approval to Stone. The historical result remains solely a comparator.

* **Historical Sources:** [Bartnicki v. Vopper / United States v. Vopper](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-514.pdf). The historical disposition is comparison material only and does not govern this case.

# United States v. Hatter

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-21. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. Medicare taxation was upheld, 5-2; the discriminatory Social Security application was rejected, 6-1. Stevens and O'Connor did not participate.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Breyer wrote for Rehnquist, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; Scalia joined Parts I, II, and V and wrote separately in partial concurrence and dissent. Thomas concurred in the judgment in part and dissented in part.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court allowed nondiscriminatory taxation, rejected discriminatory Social Security transition treatment, rejected the claimed conclusive effect of its earlier no-quorum action, and held that later pay increases did not cure the violation. Evans v. Gore was overruled insofar as inconsistent. These are historical comparator propositions only.

**Historical Sources:** [United States v. Hatter](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-557.pdf).

**Historical Authority:** The historical majority and separate opinions are comparison material only. Their propositions, votes, and opinion alignments do not govern this case.
