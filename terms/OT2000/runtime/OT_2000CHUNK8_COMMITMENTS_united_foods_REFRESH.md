# United States v. United Foods — independent non-Stone commitments

**Stage:** Independent modeling, before comparator reconciliation and before the present matter's Stone position.
**Event:** Merits, No. 00-276, June 25, 2001.
**Research cutoff:** Immediately before the June 25 event. No uncoordinated same-day decision is treated as earlier law. The fresh neutral validation has screened the final June 18 public law and found no material governing change for this matter.
**Participation:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; no supplied nonparticipation. No author is selected. These commitments do not complete the Court's judgment.

## Reach, record and entering law

The Sixth Circuit reversed the consolidated judgment sustaining the advertising assessment and government enforcement. United Foods used the administrative petition/review channel. The government's brief, page 32 note 19, expressly states that the government-speech argument was neither raised nor addressed below and invokes Lebron's included-argument rule. This is an actual preservation fact, not an inference from an operator's missing document.

The program principally funds generic mushroom promotion. Producers remain competitors without comparable collective price, output and sales restraints or an antitrust exemption. United Foods wishes to promote its mushrooms as superior; an assessment financing the generic message therefore burdens a real commercial expressive choice even though it does not compel the producer to utter that message or identify it publicly as the speaker. The record does not establish an objection to every research or industry-information expenditure.

Section 6104 establishes four to nine Council members appointed by the Secretary through producer/importer nominations, regional production/import representation and the stated nine-member limit. No more than one member may be appointed from any single producer's or importer's nominations; failure to nominate permits appointments on the basis provided in the order. Appointment is statutory control, not a finding of ordinary popular election or unconstrained industry selection. Plans, projects and budgets require the Secretary's approval before implementation; implementing contracts require an approved plan and accounting. These controls supply a substantial governmental-speech argument. They do not establish that a federal official actually selected or controlled each advertisement's message.

Assessments are collected from first handlers for domestic fresh-market production, paid directly by importers, and remitted directly by producers selling their own mushrooms to consumers. The successive statutory ceilings are one-quarter, one-third, one-half and then one cent per pound. Certified mushrooms to be exported as mushrooms may not be assessed. Authorized expenses and a reasonable reserve are distinct from the challenged advertising obligation. Section 6104(h)'s prohibition on influencing legislation or governmental action or policy separately permits recommendations to the Secretary for order amendments and recommended voluntary grade and quality standards. None of those permitted recommendations is converted into an exception to a different provision.

Section 6105 requires the stated voting majority and its majority share of average annual volume; the mandatory five-year referendum differs from the discretionary referendum available after three years on a request from at least thirty percent of producers/importers. Industry approval is neither constitutional waiver nor sufficient justification for compelled speech. Section 6106 supplies petition, hearing, a ruling lawful to be final, timely district review and a mandatory lawful administrative remand when the ruling is contrary to law. Pending petition proceedings do not themselves impede §6107 enforcement. Constitutional invalidity, statutory procedure, collection liability and the amount or availability of monetary relief remain separate.

**Controlling law.** Actual Glickman (June 25, 1997) permits germane, nonideological promotion within a comprehensive collective agricultural marketing arrangement that already substantially displaces independent competitive decisions, with no personal attribution and freedom to advertise and criticize. It expressly leaves advertising-only independent-market programs outside its holding. Common benefit and majority approval alone are insufficient. The register **Generic promotion assessments in collective agricultural marketing**, present operation effective Glickman, June 25, 1997, carries that limited rule.

Actual Southworth (March 22, 2000) permits a distinct broad, objective and viewpoint-neutral private-expression program justified by the university's educational function. It does not convert each recipient's message into government speech or establish a universal compulsory-subsidy permission. Actual Rosenberger distinguishes independent editorial speech from official speech in its controlled private-publication program; it did not decide compulsory fees. Actual Lebron (February 21, 1995) classifies a specially created, permanently federally governed corporation as governmental for constitutional responsibility. It does not decide that every message funded by a public institution is governmental expression exempt from an objector's subsidy claim.

Keller, 496 U.S. 1, 10–14 (1990), rejects treating a state agency label and public purpose alone as an answer to compulsory professional dues. It distinguishes the Bar's member-funded, advisory professional structure from ordinary representative government and taxation. It supplies an analogy, not a holding that every industry council is private or every earmarked assessment unconstitutional. Abood and Keller's germaneness analysis requires the independent purpose justifying the compelled association; a program cannot establish that purpose merely by declaring its chosen advertising germane to itself.

The preservation register **Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review**, present operation effective Lebron, February 21, 1995 for the disavowal component, retains Yee's argument/claim and question-presented distinctions. All eight accept that the government-speech justification is fairly included as a legal argument concerning the same challenged assessment and First Amendment controversy. That does not compel its adjudication, establish all needed facts or enlarge the relief requested. Their actual exercise of discretion differs below.

## Refreshed alternative-ground analysis

This independent refresh replaces the original United Foods commitments for subsequent reconciliation. The original freeze remains unchanged. The material addition is the government's express alternative Central Hudson argument and denial of waiver, United Foods' contrary preservation and merits arguments, and the exact status of the reported effectiveness study. No current-matter comparator, reconciled commitment or Stone material entered this context. No author is selected.

**Three separate routes.** Actual Glickman does not decide this advertising-only arrangement: distinguishing its collective-program permission establishes neither automatic invalidity nor automatic availability of economic-regulation deference. The independent compulsory-private-subsidy question, a proposed commercial-speech justification under Central Hudson, and the government-speech justification must each receive a legal answer at the scope a Justice reaches. The existence of one sufficient answer can make the empirical application of an inapplicable alternative unnecessary; it cannot silently eliminate an alternative the Justice accepts as potentially sufficient.

**Central Hudson preservation.** United Foods says the government waived this argument by omitting a separate presentation in its opening merits brief. The government expressly denies waiver in reply page 9 note 7, says its understanding of Glickman made that presentation unnecessary, and argues the program's validity under less deferential review. This is not the government's admission that government speech was unpresented below. The reply's new argument and any new evidentiary proffer also are not identical. All eight Associates elect to consider the legal availability and sufficiency of the commercial-speech justification: it concerns the same assessment, preserved First Amendment controversy and requested relief, and United Foods itself presents a substantial answer to it. This is a bounded application of actual Yee/Lebron discretion, not a rule that replies invariably cure omissions or that an included legal argument admits new evidence. No affirmative surrender of the alternative is established by the supplied record. The government's mistaken description of Glickman's breadth does not itself establish waiver or the correct merits rule.

**Exact alternative and burden.** If Central Hudson applies, the lawful, nonmisleading commercial-speech threshold, substantial governmental interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit are distinct. No illegality or misleading-message finding is supplied here. The government must justify the compelled burden under the selected scrutiny; invocation of legislative economic policy does not shift the entire burden to United Foods to disprove a presumed valid regulation. Reasonable fit is not a least-restrictive-means requirement. Neither the framework nor current law invariably requires an econometric study, proof that each payer's sales increased, individual-advertising credits or uniform treatment of every commodity. Those proposed requirements must not be invented to obtain invalidation.

**The strongest governmental case.** Congress identified maintaining and expanding mushroom markets, developing uses and supporting the national agricultural economy, with concern about market instability, competition from canned imports, low consumption relative to other countries and smaller producers' promotional capacity. The reply reports testimony that voluntary generic efforts failed when growers declined to pay. It invokes experience in other commodity programs and the 1998 continuation referendum, and reports a February 2000 review with a favorable demand conclusion and copies lodged with the Court. It defends the definition excluding producers/importers at or below 500,000 pounds annually by collection and enforcement costs, distinguishes brand-share advertising from market-wide promotion when rejecting credits, and defends a national program against a demand for geographic matching. Under the reply's stated quarter-cent rate, 500,000 pounds produces a $1,250 charge; that arithmetic is not proof of a particular enforcement cost. The current statutory rate ceilings and certified-export exclusion remain as stated above. Industry referendum requirements concern numbers and volume, not a vote of every citizen or a waiver by objectors.

**The strongest contrary case.** United Foods argues that its branded promotion already supplies the claimed benefit, small growers and shifting processed-market suppliers can receive benefits without equivalent charges, and a national program may principally aid regions outside its three markets. It asserts elastic supply, intermediary capture, preprogram industry growth, limited budgets and ineffective targeting. It invokes a separate 1996 Donald B. Mills administrative ruling and economic literature; the government answers that the earlier ruling concerned too little data and points to the later reported review. No claimed inefficacy, no-benefit proposition, deliberate regional diversion, legislative motive or market response becomes a finding by being asserted in a brief. Conversely, legislative authorization, industry approval and reported success do not automatically satisfy meaningful scrutiny.

**Study and record limit.** The February 2000 review is a reported and quoted proffer, with lodging asserted by the government. Its full text, methodology, procedural admissibility, lower-court record status and legal use on review have not been established in the supplied handoff. This is not a finding that the Court lacks it, that the government failed to submit it, that no evidence supports the program or that United Foods conceded it. The commitments below make no decisive empirical finding from that quotation. The five affirming Associates adopt a legal compulsory-subsidy ground for which success in increasing demand would not suffice. The other three would require application of the proper legal standard to the usable record on remand, preserving ordinary rules about the study and any supplementation. Their remand is for a materially undecided alternative in litigation resolved below on a different rationale, not a research-file production exercise or a guaranteed opportunity to cure failed proof.

### Stevens

**Sources and present conclusion.** His actual Glickman join supports its combined collective-program rationale and explicit reservation, not an automatic rule for every reserved arrangement. His Southworth concurrence examines the complete program, its real purpose and payer-message relation instead of declaring that all permissible compulsion must be incidental to a nonspeech regulatory association. His Lebron reach and remedy joins distinguish a reviewable included argument from the findings necessary to decide its application. The newly complete presentation makes a separate commercial-speech justification an actual alternative requiring treatment. He changes the original remand premise that every private industry message here necessarily fails once Glickman is distinguished.

**Two lawful paths and strongest objections.** The compulsory-subsidy route would affirm because the message is the object of the association and the growers remain independent. Its strongest point is that an asserted common benefit can be made for almost any compelled sales campaign. He does not accept that absence of comprehensive marketing restraints conclusively disposes of an independently justified commercial regulation. On the competing route, the economic aims may be substantial, but government must show direct advancement and a reasonable fit with the burden. The regional and supplier-shift objections must likewise be assessed without requiring a guaranteed gain to this producer or treating academic forecasts as facts.

His prior government-speech remand remains available as a different sufficient route, but need not be reached if another lawful ground completely resolves the claim.

**Join limits and separate-writing function.** He can join the actual Glickman limits, the rejection of automatic economic-regulation deference and the availability of an appropriately supported alternative. ## Scope, disposition and affected changes

**Five-Associate affirmance ground.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas independently adopt a compelled-private-subsidy rule for this arrangement: government cannot require otherwise independent competitors to finance a selected generic private advertising message merely because promoting that product is economically beneficial and a mandatory contribution scheme may advance the promotion effectively. The complete absence of a sufficient independent common duty in this program, rather than the bare absence of a comprehensive marketing order as a label, supplies their objection. They have considered the government's asserted market and collective-action objectives, presumed no waiver, and do not deny that lawful official advocacy and genuinely justified collective programs may be financed. Central Hudson's reduced review of commercial restrictions does not substitute for their governing protection against this compulsory private-message relationship. Accordingly, efficacy and the economic reasonableness of particular exemption/credit/geographic lines cannot cure the violation they find. They make no empirical adverse holding under that alternative.
