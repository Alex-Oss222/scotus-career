# Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's approval of the federal declaration and injunction on the asserted absence of process, and remand for proceedings consistent with the adequate-contract-remedy rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the constitutional declaration and dependent injunction. G & G receives no federal payment award on this claim; its contract remedies and defenses to the wage determination remain available. If an actually preserved, supported barrier to those remedies is established, the lower court must address that barrier rather than treat this judgment as a factual finding of universal access.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A disputed payment claim and its adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach adequacy while assuming the asserted contract interest. State officials ordered the withholding, so private-action analysis cannot dispose of it.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the existing ordinary judicial route can supply a hearing for the claimed contractual entitlement without a special administrative proceeding.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing interest-specific process principles and adopt the unresolved application that adequate ordinary contract adjudication suffices here. Do not convert American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan's disputed-benefit holding into absence of every property interest.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may contest a public-contract payment without a separate administrative hearing when effective judicial contract proceedings remain available to determine and enforce the claimant's entitlement. The identified contractor-or-assignee and ordinary contractual routes defeat the categorical no-process claim on this record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule includes withholding based on alleged noncompliance with the contract's governing wage terms. It excludes loss of an independently established possessory or occupational entitlement and claims for which the State forecloses effective adjudication.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The protected asset is the enforceable claim, whose amount and conditions are contested. A neutral court can resolve the same dispute and order the resulting payment. Delay inherent in ordinary litigation is not itself destruction of that claim. Dependence on an assignment matters if it actually leaves no workable route; its hypothetical refusal does not establish a universal defect in this statutory scheme. The constitutional source, contract merits and remedial machinery must remain separate. The distinction from Good is the interest actually withheld: disputed contractual payment rather than present possession. Mathews requires attention to the usable process for that interest, not a categorical administrative-hearing entitlement. Logan prevents treating a destroyed or inaccessible claim as an adequate remedy. The available contract route is decisive on this record; documents, practical barriers, and the actual availability of assignment may rebut adequacy in a properly supported application. The rule does not presume that every person can finance years of litigation or excuse a State that makes its promised judicial remedy illusory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual wage and penalty amounts are outside the question presented; whether a specific refusal of assignment or other remedial barrier defeats all available routes requires facts the record lacks.
