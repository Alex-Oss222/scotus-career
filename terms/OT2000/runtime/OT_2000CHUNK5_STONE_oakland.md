# OT_2000CHUNK5

# United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment recognizing and directing consideration of a medical-necessity exception to the Cooperative’s prohibited manufacture and distribution, and remand for proceedings under the statute’s actual terms.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The existing injunction may not be modified on the ground that a judge may authorize the statutory violation because its medical benefits outweigh the legislative policy. Preserve lawful discretion over the necessity, scope and means of equitable enforcement, and permit ordinary consideration of independently preserved constitutional objections. Do not decide the mooted contempt appeal, impose criminal punishment, or select findings about individual patients.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Legislative classification and bounded remedial discretion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory construction first and then the Cooperative’s independently offered equitable ground for affirmance. A necessity-based reading is unavailable if it contradicts the enacted scheme. The constitutional objections have not been adjudicated below and are unnecessary to decide this statutory dispute; preserve their ordinary procedural treatment rather than announce a constitutional ruling.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Controlled Substances Act permits this distributor to manufacture and supply Schedule I marijuana on a medical-necessity theory, and whether equity independently authorizes that permission.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing source-and-remedy separation in Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc. and Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc.; adopt the new case-specific statutory conclusion that this scheme forecloses the Cooperative’s medical-distribution exemption. Stone adopts this construction of the present statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The applicable federal scheme does not authorize the Cooperative’s manufacture and distribution under the adopted medical-necessity exception. A court cannot supply that same substantive exception by labeling it injunction modification, although ordinary lawful discretion over equitable enforcement remains.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the actual distribution activities and related intended-distribution possession addressed in the injunction. It does not equate denial of a statutory exemption with a finding that the patients are insincere or that treatment is medically worthless.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The strongest case for the Cooperative is concrete patient need after alternatives fail. That need must receive accurate description, but judicial sympathy cannot itself identify a legal exception. The statute regulates substances according to specified categories, authorizations and channels. Its treatment of Schedule I marijuana cannot be reconciled with an ordinary dispensing exemption created by a court from the same medical considerations Congress organized the scheme to address. The absence of the words “necessity defense” is not decisive by itself; text and structure together must do the work. Equity is flexible within the legal relationship Congress created. A court can examine whether an injunction is needed, whether its language reaches beyond a violation, and how lawful compliance should be ordered. It cannot declare ongoing prohibited distribution permissible because it would have selected a different medical policy. That would collapse the source of an obligation into remedial preference. The narrow judgment protects statutory fidelity while leaving any independently established constitutional limit to genuine adjudication under its own requirements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual possession without distribution is outside the question presented by this Cooperative’s injunction. Whether necessity may ever justify conduct under a materially different federal statute is also outside the question; neither issue is decided.
