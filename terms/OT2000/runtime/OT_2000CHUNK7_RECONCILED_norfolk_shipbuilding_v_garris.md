## Entering-law comparison

Moragne supplies the maritime death remedy; its unseaworthiness facts leave this negligence application to be decided candidly. American Dredging preserves statutory remedial boundaries without converting state forum administration into substantive immunity. Yamaha's nonseafarer state-remedy holding is not transplanted to this maritime employee. Dooley preserves Congress's actual high-seas compensation choice, not universal statutory occupation. Lewis concerns protection of limitation and forum access, not the existence of this death action. None changes a premise that supported the historical common judgment.

The complete historical writings confirm the provisional common rule but reveal a material join barrier not stated in its last paragraph. Ginsburg, Souter and Breyer do not accept a general suggestion that, even outside an applicable statutory limit, new maritime claims should ordinarily await Congress. They regard federal maritime development as continuing alongside legislation. That qualification does not prevent their joining an opinion confined to this established duty, Moragne's remedy and the actual statutory boundaries. No broad prospective caution is necessary to resolve this case.

## Proposed opinion scope and separate-writing function

The common rule is that general maritime law supplies an action when negligent breach of an established maritime duty causes death, unless an applicable enactment precludes that action. Here the Jones Act's seaman regime does not govern Garris; DOHSA does not extend into this territorial-water setting; and the LHWCA preserves third-party claims against Norfolk, which is neither employer nor vessel owner. Removing specified unseaworthiness claims is not abolition of third-party negligence. Respect for enacted boundaries and the continuing maritime judicial role are compatible propositions.

A Court opinion limited to those grounds can receive all eight joins without reproducing the historical partial concurrence. If a broader prospective caution is proposed, Ginsburg, Souter and Breyer withhold that portion; their separate position must explain that Moragne's remedial logic and the absence of a contrary legislative direction support continuing judicial development in harmony with Congress. It cannot be summarized as merely a concurrence. Historical authorship is not an assignment instruction.
