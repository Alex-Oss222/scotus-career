## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 504; No. 00-1210
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-14 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a reviewing court may set aside a labor-arbitration award because it rejects the arbitrator’s assessment of witness credibility and then direct entry of the claimant’s requested monetary award instead of leaving the contractual merits to arbitration.
* **Question Granted:** The question above identifies the merits issue presented for disposition on the petition. No separate limited-question merits grant has been authenticated; the contemporary proceeding used the petition record rather than a separately scheduled argued merits hearing. Exact petition wording is not represented as verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit judgment of December 7, 2000 directing the district court to return the matter to arbitration with instructions to award Garvey the amount he claimed. This second appellate judgment followed an earlier decision vacating the arbitral denial and a district-court remand for further arbitration.
* **Relief Requested:** The Players Association seeks reversal of the directed award and restoration of the limited judicial role in reviewing the arbitral denial. Garvey seeks preservation of the approximately $3 million award ordered by the court of appeals.
* **Material Record:** Following arbitral findings of club collusion in the free-agent markets after the 1985, 1986, and 1987 seasons, the Association and clubs settled through a $280 million fund. The Association’s Framework permitted individual review limited to whether its distribution criteria had been properly applied. A lost-extension claim required evidence of a specific extension offer made before collusion and later withdrawn because of it. Garvey alleged that the Padres withdrew an offer covering the 1988 and 1989 seasons. After the Association rejected his claim for lack of evidence of an offer, he testified at arbitration and presented a June 1996 letter from former club president Ballard Smith supporting his account. The arbitrator requested additional documentation and rejected the claim. He found substantial credibility problems in Smith’s letter because it conflicted with Smith’s prior testimony denying collusion and interest in extending Garvey’s contract; he also identified absent corroboration and Garvey’s age and injury history. The first appellate panel thought refusal to credit the letter inexplicable because prior arbitrators had disbelieved club witnesses about collusion generally. The second appellate panel held that its earlier decision permitted only a monetary award for Garvey. No dishonesty by the arbitrator is alleged in the described record. [Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-504.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The parties accept that the dispute arises under the Labor Management Relations Act because the settlement machinery remedies breach of the collective-bargaining agreement. Garvey contests review of matters resolved in the first appellate judgment because more than ninety days elapsed before the petition challenging the second judgment. A timely petition from the later judgment permits review of antecedent rulings in the same litigation; the earlier panel’s law-of-the-case instructions bind the district court but do not immunize them from Supreme Court review. This matter is briefed as merits review, without a petition addendum.
* **Relevant Dates:** Underlying collusion seasons: 1985 through 1987. Association rejection: February 1996; Smith’s letter: June 1996. Second appellate judgment: December 7, 2000. Merits event: May 14, 2001.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Any earlier effective holding in Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America concerning review of labor-arbitration awards must be considered before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective holding in Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America may affect the scope of review, public policy, or remedy. Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams and C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma do not by their placement alone determine this accepted collective-bargaining submission.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The cause concerns enforcement and review of contractual labor arbitration under the Labor Management Relations Act. Courts retain authority to determine what was submitted, whether the arbitrator acted within the agreement, and whether a recognized ground defeats enforcement. That role is distinct from deciding whether the arbitrator correctly found the facts. The remedy for a properly vacated award must respect the agreement’s remaining allocation of decisionmaking.

### Issue 1: Judicial review of contractual labor arbitration

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan unanimously requires independent judicial determination of antecedent assent absent clear delegation and preserves ordinary appellate review of that judicial determination. Its eight-Justice actual-delegation proposition permits only limited review once delegation exists. It authorizes no new merits-vacatur ground or retrial of an agreed arbitration. Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp. unanimously distinguishes collective contractual rights from independent statutory discrimination claims and refuses to supply a missing clear statutory-forum waiver. Here the claim is under the accepted contractual distribution machinery, not an independent statutory claim inadequately submitted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan preserves the separation between deciding whether parties agreed and reviewing the merits of what they agreed to arbitrate. Neither independent review of a judicial legal conclusion nor the absence of extra deference to a district judge creates independent review of the arbitrator’s credibility findings.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Labor Management Relations Act supplies the accepted statutory route. The Framework confines the arbitrator to applying the approved distribution criteria, including the requirement of a specific extension offer subsequently withdrawn because of collusion. Its limits are legally relevant; whether Smith persuasively proved the required facts is a different question.
* **Material Other Authority:** United Steelworkers of America v. Enterprise Wheel & Car Corp., United Steelworkers of America v. American Manufacturing Co., and United Paperworkers International Union v. Misco, Inc. supply received preterm limits on reviewing contractual labor awards. Judicial disagreement with facts or contract interpretation is insufficient when the arbitrator is arguably applying the contract within his authority. Mercer v. Theriot and Hamilton-Brown Shoe Co. v. Wolf Brothers & Co. support review of an earlier ruling through the later judgment in the same case.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law protects genuine contractual arbitration against a judicial merits substitution while preserving enforcement boundaries. The assent cases do not require fresh consent to each unfavorable finding after an accepted submission. Nor does the limited-review rule enforce an arbitrator’s exercise of powers the agreement did not confer.
* **Open Point:** Whether the described credibility reasoning demonstrates abandonment of the Framework or instead constitutes its application, and whether the appellate court could order the exact monetary recovery even if vacatur were justified. No controlling decision makes an appellate assessment of irrationality a freestanding factual appeal from an arbitral award.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit reasoned that the arbitrator’s reliance on Smith’s earlier denial conflicted with the prior collusion findings, and that Garvey’s account and the later letter left no permissible alternative to an award. It characterized the defect as dispensing independent industrial justice rather than simply making a factual mistake. Garvey’s strongest position is that deference cannot protect an arbitrator who contradicts the governing framework or his own established premises, and that further proceedings need not repeat a logically foreclosed denial. The Association responds that the arbitrator expressly applied the requirement of an actual withdrawn offer, sought supporting material, and made a credibility judgment. A general finding that clubs colluded does not establish this player’s individual contract offer. The Association seeks enforcement of the decisionmaker chosen for those disputed facts. The National Academy of Arbitrators and the Commissioner supported limited judicial review; their institutional interests do not replace the instrument-specific inquiry. Even a court accepting Garvey’s vacatur theory must separately justify replacing arbitration with a court-selected award.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the timing objection permits review of both the directed monetary award and its predicate. If the arbitrator was applying the Framework, the Court can reverse and restore the district court’s denial of vacatur. If an actual departure from the contractual submission is established, it can vacate without deciding disputed individual entitlement and allow further proceedings authorized by the agreement. The Court could reverse only the directed-award component if the defect in that relief is sufficient and the validity of vacatur remains unresolved. Upholding a court-selected award would require a supported legal basis for taking the merits from the agreed tribunal, not simply a strong disagreement with its factfinding.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A merits holding may distinguish failure to exercise contractual authority from an erroneous exercise of that authority. A remedial holding may prohibit a reviewing court from resolving disputed contractual facts and ordering the claimed sum merely because it considers the arbitrator’s explanation irrational. Neither rule requires enforcement of corruption or an award beyond the actual submission.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.
