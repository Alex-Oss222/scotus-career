# TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 23; No. 99-1571
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-20 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether an expired utility patent establishing the useful operation of a product feature bears on functionality under federal trade-dress law, and whether a showing of alternative designs can nevertheless support protection for that feature.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit reversed summary judgment for TrafFix, requiring further consideration of alternative designs and competitive necessity despite the utilitarian role of the claimed dual-spring design.
* **Relief Requested:** TrafFix seeks reinstatement of summary judgment on functionality; Marketing Displays seeks further proceedings on protectable trade dress and confusion.
* **Material Record:** Marketing Displays marketed wind-resistant roadside sign stands using a dual-spring mechanism disclosed in expired utility patents. TrafFix copied the mechanism after expiration. Marketing Displays asserted trade-dress rights in the visible dual springs. The district court found the feature functional and also rejected the asserted secondary-meaning showing. The appellate court relied in part on possible alternative spring arrangements or coverings. The patent and record describe the springs’ contribution to resisting wind and stabilizing the sign. No ornamental feature distinct from that claimed mechanism is established as the basis of this action. [TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-23.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Functionality and acquired source significance are independent requirements. A valid functionality ground can dispose of the claim without resolving the district court’s alternate secondary-meaning ground. Patent expiration does not itself adjudicate every possible trademark claim.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically established as necessary to resolve this matter. Any actual intervening simulation decision must be checked before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The case reviews summary judgment. Statutory burden and actual undisputed design evidence must be distinguished from a categorical patent-based bar.

### Issue 1: Functional product design

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co. excludes functional features from trademark protection. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Samara Brothers, Inc. requires acquired source significance for product design and leaves functionality a separate barrier.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Functionality independently limits trade-dress protection. The 1999 amendment to 15 U.S.C. §1125(a)(3) assigns the plaintiff the nonfunctionality burden for unregistered trade dress, with temporal application requiring care where relevant.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Lanham Act’s source-identification protection and the limited patent grant operate together. Useful features cannot gain perpetual exclusion through source significance alone.
* **Material Other Authority:** Inwood Laboratories v. Ives Laboratories states the traditional useful-function criterion. Qualitex also addresses non-reputation-related competitive disadvantage, without replacing the ordinary utilitarian inquiry.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The patent is relevant evidence of utility; established law already forbids protection of a proven functional feature. Secondary meaning does not cure that defect.
* **Open Point:** How much weight the expired utility patent receives and whether the existence of alternative designs defeats an otherwise demonstrated utilitarian function.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The appellate court emphasized alternative ways to compete and the possibility that the claimed appearance could be separated from useful operation. The district court instead treated the springs themselves as functional.
* **TrafFix:** The patents and record establish that the claimed feature does useful work. Requiring rivals to hide or redesign it creates the very perpetual monopoly functionality prevents.
* **Marketing Displays:** Patent claims and trade-dress appearance are not necessarily coterminous; equivalent alternatives may show the appearance is unnecessary to competition and serves as an identifier.
* **Limits:** A patent on some element does not prove that every ornamental aspect is functional. Conversely, copying, consumer recognition and available alternatives do not negate evidence that the specific claimed feature affects operation or cost.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse on the established functionality ground and terminate the asserted dual-spring trade-dress claim. Alternatively, remand if a genuinely distinct nonfunctional appearance feature was preserved and cannot be resolved on the record. The Court need not decide a constitutional absolute bar on trade dress associated with any expired patent.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A feature-specific rule would treat patent disclosures as strong evidence of utility while allowing proof that a separately claimed appearance is ornamental. Once utilitarian functionality is established, alternative designs would not supply an escape. A patent-wide categorical bar would reach beyond the evidence needed here.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Buford v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Buford v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 59; No. 99-9073
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-20 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether an appellate court should defer to a district court’s fact-bound determination that prior convictions were not functionally consolidated for purposes of the Sentencing Guidelines’ career-offender provisions.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed the district court’s decision that Buford’s drug conviction was not functionally consolidated with her robbery convictions, applying deferential review.
* **Relief Requested:** Buford seeks independent appellate reconsideration of related-case status and relief from the resulting career-offender sentence. The United States seeks affirmance of the review standard and judgment.
* **Material Record:** Buford pleaded guilty to armed bank robbery. Four earlier robbery convictions were conceded to be related for criminal-history purposes; the disputed prior drug conviction was handled in connection with the same sentencing occasion but retained procedural distinctions. One judge imposed concurrent sentences. There was no formal consolidation order establishing the answer. The district court examined the underlying sequence and local sentencing practice and found no functional consolidation. The dispute here concerns the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a new finding about how the state court organized the cases. [Buford v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-59.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court can decide the review standard without announcing a comprehensive definition of functional consolidation. Deference to factual application does not license deference to an erroneous interpretation of the Guidelines.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The historically applicable 18 U.S.C. §3742(e) directs due deference to application of the Guidelines to facts. Legal interpretation, historical facts and context-sensitive application must be separated.

### Issue 1: Mixed sentencing applications

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Koon v. United States and Powell v. United States require correction of legal error and unsupported sentencing grounds while affording substantial deference to supported case-specific sentencing judgments. Ornelas v. United States independently reviews ultimate search thresholds for functional institutional reasons, not every mixed question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Guidelines interpretation is reviewed independently, historical findings for clear error, and application under the standard appropriate to the particular determination. No universal mixed-question label selects a standard.
* **Other Governing Law:** U.S.S.G. §§4B1.1, 4B1.2 and 4A1.2, including the applicable commentary on related cases, determine the substantive classification. Section 3742(e) governs review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative commentary subject to superior law and the guideline text. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan preserves distinct legal and factual review.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law supplies the functional allocation method and requires legal correction. It does not prescribe independent review of every related-conviction application.
* **Open Point:** Whether this record-specific functional-consolidation determination is sufficiently dependent on local sentencing context to warrant deference.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The district judge was better placed to assess the significance of the separate proceedings, shared sentencing occasion and local practice; the appellate court deferred to that supported application.
* **Buford:** Career-offender classification produces substantial consequences and should receive consistent independent appellate review, as other ultimate mixed legal conclusions do.
* **United States:** The operative legal criteria remain reviewable, but the consolidation application is intensely factual and depends on practical familiarity with sentencing administration.
* **Limits:** Concurrent sentences alone do not automatically prove consolidation, and local familiarity cannot substitute for record evidence. Deference must not shelter a categorical legal misunderstanding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm deferential review of this functional application while preserving independent legal interpretation. A contrary allocation would require the appellate court to reconsider the existing record independently, not automatically award a lower sentence. A demonstrated legal error in the consolidation criterion would independently require correction under either standard.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule can cover fact-bound functional-consolidation judgments under the applicable Guidelines without determining review for every criminal-history issue or every mixed constitutional question. It leaves the substantive consolidation definition to the issue actually litigated.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 105; No. 99-1379
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-21 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Arbitration Act excludes every employment contract from its coverage, or only employment contracts of transportation workers within the residual clause of Section 1.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed a federal district-court order enjoining the employee’s state action and compelling arbitration, because it treated Section 1 as excluding employment contracts generally.
* **Relief Requested:** Circuit City seeks reversal of the categorical employment exclusion and consideration of its arbitration request under the Act. Adams seeks to retain a judicial forum for his discrimination claims.
* **Material Record:** Adams signed an employment application containing a provision requiring arbitration of employment disputes, became a retail employee, and later brought a state-law employment-discrimination action. Circuit City invoked the agreement in a separate federal proceeding. The appellate disposition turned on statutory coverage rather than a completed determination of every ordinary contract defense. The employment was retail work; no finding places Adams in a transportation-worker class. [Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-105.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Statutory coverage is antecedent to contractual enforcement. A covered transaction does not establish valid assent, delegation, enforceability of every term, subject-matter jurisdiction, or authority to enjoin every state proceeding. Preserved independent defenses remain distinct from the ground actually decided below.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph occurs earlier on the calendar. No effective decision in that matter has been established. Any intervening holding concerning prohibitive-cost burdens or arbitration appealability must be applied before adjudication; its historical disposition does not establish governing law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Interpret the enacted coverage and exclusion together. Review the legal construction independently. Any implementing order must comply with the Act’s own requirements and applicable federal jurisdiction and remedial limits.

### Issue 1: Employment exclusion and covered agreement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson makes Section 2 operative in state and federal courts and reads actual commerce broadly, but expressly leaves the employment exclusion unresolved. Doctor’s Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto preserves genuinely general contract defenses. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan requires independent determination of assent absent established delegation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The Federal Arbitration Act requires a transaction within its statutory coverage and preserves ordinary contract-formation, scope, and revocation rules. Federal coverage does not establish consent.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-105.pdf) distinguish “involving commerce” from employment of seamen, railroad employees, or another class “engaged in” interstate or foreign commerce.
* **Material Other Authority:** Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp. concerns arbitration of statutory employment claims through a securities-registration agreement, and does not itself resolve this employment-contract exclusion. The statutory list, residual wording, contemporary usage and enacted structure all matter.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No entering simulation holding makes every employment agreement covered or excluded. Ordinary anti-discrimination law remains enforceable through a validly selected forum.
* **Open Point:** Whether the residual employment phrase identifies workers sharing the transportation character of the named classes, and what follows for this request rather than every arbitration term.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Employer:** The named occupations constrain the residual clause to transportation work. Reading every employment contract out would deny effect to the specific enumeration and confuse the Act’s different commerce formulations. **Employee:** The words reflected the commerce jurisdiction understood at enactment, and the labor exclusion protected employment arrangements generally. Legislative circumstances and the distinction between commercial exchange and employment support that reading. Adams also has an interest in preserving ordinary objections to this actual agreement. **Decision-material response:** Neither a general preference for arbitration nor hostility to compulsory employment arbitration can replace the statutory exclusion’s text and context.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A transportation-class reading permits reversal of the categorical exclusion and remand for remaining enforcement questions. A general-employment reading permits affirmance on coverage without deciding contract defenses. A narrower remand is available if a material classification or independently dispositive ground remains unresolved; the Court should not manufacture such a dispute on this retail record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Construction of the residual employment exclusion and its application to this retail employment agreement, without deciding every boundary of transportation work or every enforceability defense.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Egelhoff v. Egelhoff

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Egelhoff v. Egelhoff
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 141; No. 99-1529
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-21 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether ERISA preempts a state statute that automatically revokes a former spouse’s designation as beneficiary of an ERISA-governed life-insurance policy or pension plan upon divorce.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Washington Supreme Court affirmed reversal of summary judgments for the named former-spouse beneficiary, concluding that Washington’s automatic-revocation statute was not preempted.
* **Relief Requested:** The designated beneficiary seeks reversal and enforcement of the plan designations. The decedent’s children seek the benefits under Washington’s divorce-revocation and intestacy rules.
* **Material Record:** While married, David Egelhoff named his wife as beneficiary of employer-provided life insurance and a pension plan governed by ERISA. They divorced; he died intestate shortly afterward without changing those designations. Children of a previous marriage brought separate state suits for the insurance proceeds and pension benefits. Washington’s statute revokes a spouse’s designation on divorce for covered nonprobate assets, including employee benefit plans. The trial courts favored the designated beneficiary; the state appellate courts favored application of the revocation statute. [Egelhoff v. Egelhoff](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-141.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a federal-preemption defense in state litigation concerning identified plan benefits. The record does not establish a qualifying domestic-relations order, actual beneficiary redesignation, independent postdistribution bargain, or slayer claim. Receipt of proceeds does not itself answer whether the asserted right impermissibly displaces a plan beneficiary.

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically established as necessary to resolve this matter. Any actual intervening simulation decision must be checked before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the statutory preemption question independently. Identify what the state rule actually commands and whether the asserted entitlement concerns plan administration or a legally independent subsequent transaction.

### Issue 1: Beneficiary administration and state domestic-relations rules

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Boggs v. Boggs protects federally required survivor benefits and rejects state succession claims that depend on impermissible antecedent transfers outside the federal scheme. California Division of Labor Standards Enforcement v. Dillingham Construction and De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund distinguish general background regulation and ordinary economic effects from commands governing plan benefits or administration.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** ERISA preemption turns on the challenged law’s connection to plan administration and the federal statutory protections. General state regulation is not displaced merely because it affects a plan economically; direct replacement of federally structured rights requires a different inquiry.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Egelhoff v. Egelhoff](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-141.pdf), address plan administration according to governing documents and state laws relating to covered plans.
* **Material Other Authority:** New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co. resists unlimited preemption from remote effects. UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward preserves a genuine insurance rule while distinguishing an administration-dependent agency rule.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The controlling decisions do not resolve every postdivorce default rule for every asset. Nor does Boggs v. Boggs create categorical immunity for distributed property against independently arising obligations.
* **Open Point:** Whether this automatic revocation directly substitutes a beneficiary-selection rule for the plan’s designation rather than regulating an independent transaction or merely affecting costs.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Former spouse:** The statute changes whom the plans must pay despite their beneficiary records, creates different administration in different States, and defeats predictable document-based payment. **Children:** Divorce and succession are traditional state fields; the law applies to many nonprobate assets, supplies an ordinary intent-presuming default, and allows arrangements to avoid its effect. Uniform national administration should not require treating every beneficiary designation as permanently insulated from ordinary legal rules. **Dispositive distinction:** Traditional subject matter is relevant context but does not decide whether this particular rule directly reallocates the benefits of a covered plan.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Preemption supports reversal and remand to apply the governing beneficiary rules. A finding that the statute is merely a general background default would support affirmance, subject to the federal survivor and transfer restrictions. A judgment limited to these benefits can reserve different rules concerning unlawful killing or an independently enforceable agreement arising after distribution.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The automatic divorce-revocation rule as applied to these ERISA benefit designations, with separate treatment of an actually established federal exception or independent obligation.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Ferguson v. City of Charleston

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ferguson v. City of Charleston
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 67; No. 99-936
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review for decision.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-21 (historical decision-calendar date, used only to schedule this simulation).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a public hospital may conduct nonconsensual diagnostic drug tests of pregnant patients without a warrant under the special-needs doctrine when the program is designed and operated with police to obtain evidence supporting criminal prosecution.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed judgment for the defendants on special-needs grounds without deciding the patients’ challenge to the sufficiency of evidence supporting the jury’s consent finding.
* **Relief Requested:** The patients seek reversal of the warrant-exception ruling and further adjudication of their constitutional claims. The hospital and municipal defendants seek affirmance based on consent or the asserted health-related special need.
* **Material Record:** MUSC personnel concerned about prenatal cocaine use coordinated with police and local officials. Their policy identified patients for urine testing, prescribed a chain of custody, provided education and treatment referrals, and specified arrest and prosecution criteria. Patients who tested positive were arrested. The district court instructed the jury to find for the patients unless they had consented; the jury found for defendants. The patients challenged the consent evidence on appeal. The appellate court instead held the searches reasonable under special-needs doctrine. The policy’s treatment provisions did not eliminate its express evidence-gathering and arrest machinery. [Ferguson v. City of Charleston](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-67.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Public-hospital conduct is governmental action. Consent remains unresolved at the appellate level and cannot be inferred from accepting prenatal treatment. For the special-needs question, assume absence of legally sufficient consent. Individual liability, municipal attribution, qualified immunity, causation and prospective relief must be separately adjudicated.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified for this issue.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** City of Indianapolis v. Edmond is earlier on the calendar and directly concerns the law-enforcement boundary of suspicionless searches. No effective decision in that matter has been established. Any intervening holding concerning program purpose must be applied before adjudication; its historical disposition does not establish governing law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Identify the challenged search and its operative programmatic purpose before balancing a claimed special need. Review preserved consent findings under their proper evidentiary and legal standards. A merits exception cannot be created by shifting to a general ultimate aspiration such as healthier pregnancies.

### Issue 1: Special needs and patient consent

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Chandler v. Miller requires a demonstrated special need beyond ordinary law enforcement and rejects symbolic suspicionless drug testing. Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton sustains a confined school-athletics program on its actual safety, privacy and collection safeguards; it does not authorize police evidence collection from patients.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A special-needs search requires a justification distinct from ordinary law enforcement, with the intrusion reasonably related to that need. Participation in a public program is not automatic waiver of a Fourth Amendment right.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Ferguson v. City of Charleston](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-67.pdf). The claim proceeds through the applicable civil-rights cause, with its independent liability requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives’ Association and National Treasury Employees Union v. Von Raab address their particular safety and public-service settings. Ordinary medical care and incidental reporting of discovered evidence are distinct from a coordinated program designed to gather prosecution evidence.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing doctrine does not make every medical test a warrantless police search. Its application depends on the test’s governmental purpose, consent and any separately established exception.
* **Open Point:** Whether police-directed evidence collection can qualify as a special need because prosecution pressure is meant to induce treatment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Patients:** Prenatal care did not authorize collection for police use. Chain-of-custody requirements, agreed arrest criteria and prosecutions show ordinary criminal investigation. The program exposed intimate medical information and used access to treatment as an evidence source. **Defendants:** Protecting mothers and infants and encouraging treatment are substantial health objectives; testing occurred in clinical care, and some patients consented. Police participation served those objectives rather than replacing them. **Necessary separation:** A legitimate health objective does not by itself establish consent or a constitutional exception for the chosen enforcement instrument.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the special-needs justification requires reversal and remand to address the preserved consent question and remaining claims. An independently valid consent determination may sustain particular searches. Upholding special needs would require a principled explanation why this program’s designed criminal evidence collection remains beyond ordinary enforcement, consistent with Chandler v. Miller; general beneficence alone cannot supply it.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Nonconsensual testing under this coordinated prosecution program, with ordinary treatment, incidental reporting, valid consent and other independently lawful exceptions reserved.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Gibbs v. Babbitt

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-03-21. **Date status:** organizationally assigned.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Gibbs v. Babbitt.

**Citation or Docket:** 214 F.3d 483, Fourth Circuit. No Supreme Court docket number is identified.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review from the Fourth Circuit. The June 6, 2000 judgment predates the assigned merits event; review is limited to the question presented.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-03-21 (organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar).

**Question Presented:** Whether Congress's Commerce Clause authority permits application of the Endangered Species Act's prohibition on taking red wolves on private property when the regulated taking is intrastate and noncommercial?

**Question Granted:** Review is limited to the question stated above.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of North Carolina entered summary judgment for federal officials on cross-motions. The Fourth Circuit affirmed. Landowners Charles Gibbs and Richard Mann and Hyde and Washington Counties seek a declaration and injunction against enforcement of the experimental red-wolf taking regulation on private land in eastern North Carolina. Their challenge concerns federal regulatory power, not title to the land or a damages claim for an already established taking. [Gibbs v. Babbitt](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/214/483/533418/).

**Relief Requested:** The landowners and counties seek a declaration and injunction against enforcement of the experimental red-wolf taking regulation on private land in eastern North Carolina. Federal officials seek preservation of summary judgment sustaining that application.

**Material Record:** The Service reintroduced captive-bred red wolves to federal refuges; wolves subsequently occupied private land. The opinion reports that roughly 41 of about 75 wild wolves were on private land in February 1998. Mann pleaded guilty after shooting a wolf in 1990 out of concern for his cattle. The regulation adopted under the ESA's experimental-population provisions contains specified exceptions for protection of human life, ongoing attacks on livestock or pets, noninjurious harassment, and authorized removal after unsuccessful capture efforts, with reporting conditions. North Carolina subsequently allowed broader killing in designated counties; the federal officials reported no contested prosecution conflict since that enactment. [Gibbs v. Babbitt](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/214/483/533418/).

The record includes interstate visitors to refuge howling programs, interstate volunteers, scientific work, contested projections of potential tourism revenues, and a possible future pelt trade. These matters are evidence and arguments of different strength, not interchangeable findings of a presently operating interstate market or a noninternalizable interstate ecological effect. ### Issue 1: The interstate mechanism supporting the challenged private-land taking rule

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Lopez requires identification of the enacted regulated class and a constitutionally eligible interstate mechanism before aggregation or magnitude. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt applies that sequence to ESA takings, including habitat injury: a project's commercial character and biodiversity's general importance do not supply the missing connection. Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. United States v. Morrison preserves both the regime-protection and cross-border-externality routes while rejecting downstream economic loss as a substitute.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The Commerce-power standard proceeds through class, connection, mechanism, eligibility, magnitude, supported prediction, fit, and an enforceable boundary. Regime protection requires a reasonably necessary anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship to an identified valid interstate regime. The externality route requires concrete substantial cross-border effects that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize. Courts independently determine eligibility while allowing supported predictions within the power; formal findings, a numerical threshold, individual border crossing, and a universal jurisdictional element are not mandatory.

**Other Governing Law:** The governing enactment and then-operative regulation are the ESA taking, threatened-species, and experimental-population provisions described in the lower opinion. The experimental designation does not determine constitutional power, and a court may not insert an unprovided commerce element. [Gibbs v. Babbitt](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/214/483/533418/).

**Material Other Authority:** The Fourth Circuit majority and dissent identify competing readings of the tourism, research, commercial-motive, and comprehensive-program evidence. Their reasoning is under review, not controlling Supreme Court law. United States v. Morrison's dismissal also limits the assumption that an inadequate theory always warrants a new record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The district court relied on the wolves' prior interstate movement and associated tourism. The appellate majority emphasized commercial concerns motivating some takings, tourism, research, future trade, and integration into a comprehensive conservation program. Judge Luttig's dissent disputed the economic characterization and the sufficiency of those connections. Historical lower-court reasoning is the challenged reasoning, and has no controlling precedential force here.

**Landowners' strongest rule:** Once wolves are living on private North Carolina land, a prohibition on noncommercial killing cannot rest on their remote transportation, the owner's other commercial activity, or the possibility that people elsewhere would value survival. The government must identify the relationship required by Lopez and National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt. State disagreements over conservation do not themselves establish incapacity to internalize an interstate effect.

**Federal officials' strongest rule:** The protected population supplies a real interstate tourism and research resource. Killing a mobile animal on nearby private property can destroy the resource supporting activity on federal lands, and a refuge-only rule may materially impair a valid interstate regime. Species survival also can produce effects that one State cannot contain. The Service's exceptions preserve substantial private use and support fit. The government must show why these propositions establish a constitutionally eligible mechanism on this record, rather than merely repeat their magnitude.

There is no concession establishing an interstate-externality theory or the validity of every ESA application. Nor does challenging the revenue study erase the conceded interstate visits.
