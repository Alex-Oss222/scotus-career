# OT_2000CHUNK5

# Alexander v. Sandoval

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment on the granted private-action question.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the Eleventh Circuit's underlying prospective judgment undisturbed. The injunction requiring accommodation of non-English-speaking applicants in Alabama's driver's-license examinations is not vacated for absence of a private cause of action; it remains subject to ordinary lawful modification or independent review of issues outside the grant. No new factual inquiry, no damages, and no remand merely to relitigate the private action. The regulation's validity and the lower courts' findings of actual discriminatory effects were not independently presented and are not transformed into newly adjudicated findings of this Court.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A regulatory duty beyond the received statutory action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the private-enforcement question actually presented. Powell v. Ridge already recognized this direct prospective action; deciding against Sandoval therefore requires reconsidering that controlling statutory holding. Regulatory validity, the correctness of the impact finding, and a separate section 1983 action are not before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Title VI authorizes a beneficiary to obtain prospective judicial enforcement of a valid section 602 regulation prohibiting unjustified discriminatory effects in the recipient's administration of a federally assisted program.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law. Stone wrote the Court's opinion in Powell v. Ridge last Term and adheres to it; its direct-action holding decides this claim, and he would apply it rather than reconsider it. The existence of the private action is the granted question, and Powell answers it for the claimants. The regulation's validity and the lower courts' findings of actual discriminatory effects were not independently presented and are not transformed into newly adjudicated findings. Retain Powell's section 1983 holding, which is outside the grant. Preserve the section 601 intentional-discrimination cause, the authority and validity limits on section 602 regulations, and Cannon and Guardians at the scope Powell states. Proceed by direct application of controlling statutory precedent; announce no new balancing test or constitutional rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Persons actually subjected to a prohibited national-origin effect in a covered federally assisted program may seek prospective compliance with the Department of Justice regulation through Title VI's bounded direct equitable action. Under Powell v. Ridge, a person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects; Powell decided this question, and the Court applies it. The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the granted private-action question, and the injunction is not vacated for absence of a private cause of action.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding applies Powell to every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within its bounded prospective scope; it is not an English-language-testing exception, and it does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. The action requires what Powell requires: an applicable section 602 regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred; a defendant subject to it through the legally required federal-assistance relationship; a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits, or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation's actual terms, attributable to an identified recipient policy or method of administration; an actual or imminent continuing effect that prospective relief can meaningfully correct; and an appropriate defendant and a remedy compatible with applicable immunity, statutory and equitable limitations. A numerical disparity alone does not conclusively establish a violation, and the recipient may invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. The action creates no damages for unintended disparate impact, no punitive damages, no retrospective compensation without an independent legal source, and no private order terminating or withholding federal grants; section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination restrictions continue to govern the agencies' own powers. Section 601 intentional-discrimination actions keep Cannon's established private action, and Franklin governs relief within a valid action without supplying damages for effects alone.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Direct application of controlling statutory precedent. Powell held last Term, by contextual statutory interpretation informed by established precedent, that Title VI supplies this bounded action: section 601's beneficiary-focused protection, section 602's authorized implementation, Cannon's recognized private judicial enforcement of the scheme, Guardians' distinction between prospective compliance and retrospective compensation, and Congress's legislation where private Title VI enforcement and effects regulations were familiar. The action rests on that statutory context, not on the regulation's own force. The distinction between equitable compliance and retrospective compensation carries decisive remedial weight, and the funding-termination procedure limits the agencies' powers without becoming the exclusive route to prospective compliance. Petitioners' argument that section 602 addresses only agencies and that an agency's wording cannot create a private cause is the argument Powell considered and answered. Neither a different agency's regulation, Alabama's English-only examination policy, nor a district-court finding rather than a pleaded allegation of effects distinguishes Powell. No amendment of Title VI and no intervening decision shows its construction to be wrong, and statutory precedent carries special force. On this record the Department receives federal assistance and the lower courts found prohibited national-origin effects; the regulation's validity and those findings were not independently presented and are not readjudicated. The private action's existence is the only granted question, and Powell answers it for Sandoval and the class. No discriminatory purpose is found, and none is inferred from the effects finding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The regulation's validity and the lower courts' effects findings lie outside the question presented and are not adjudicated anew; the injunction remains subject to ordinary lawful modification and to independent review of issues outside the grant. Powell's section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.
