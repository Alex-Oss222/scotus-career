# OT_2000CHUNK5

# Alexander v. Sandoval

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit's judgment sustaining the private prospective action to enforce the Title VI section 602 disparate-impact regulation, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the injunction requiring accommodation of non-English speakers in Alabama's driver's-license examinations insofar as it rests on that private action. Remand for ordinary proceedings on genuinely preserved, independently authorized claims and defenses: the lower courts dismiss the section 602 disparate-impact cause of action and enter a conforming judgment, and they may not manufacture a new section 601 intentional-discrimination claim from the accepted effects finding. No factual remand on the private-action question is warranted, because the action's legal source is missing even on the assumed facts. Award no damages, invalidate no regulation, find no discriminatory intent, and make no finding that the Department complies with the Title VI regulations. Federal agencies retain the remedies Congress authorized, subject to section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, report and funding-termination conditions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A regulatory duty beyond the received statutory action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the private-enforcement question actually presented. Powell v. Ridge already rejected this direct regulatory action; deciding for Sandoval therefore requires reconsidering that controlling statutory holding. Regulatory validity, the correctness of the impact finding, and a separate section 1983 action are not before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Title VI authorizes a beneficiary to obtain prospective judicial enforcement of a valid section 602 regulation prohibiting unjustified discriminatory effects in the recipient's administration of a federally assisted program.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law. Stone joined Powell v. Ridge last Term and adheres to it; its direct-action holding decides this claim, and he would follow it rather than reconsider it. Assume, without deciding, that the effects regulation is a valid exercise of section 602 authority, and invalidate no regulation. Preserve Powell's distinction between a regulation's validity and a remedy's source, and do not reach its separate section 1983 holding, which remains controlling at its own scope. Rest the decision on Title VI's own text and structure: adopt no universal requirement of an express action clause, rely on no funding-notice or Spending Clause rule, and draw on no general retreat from contextual statutory inference. McClellan and Central Bank stand as decided.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress has not authorized a private cause of action to enforce the additional disparate-impact prohibitions of Title VI section 602 regulations where the challenged conduct does not violate section 601, and a regulation cannot create that action by its own force. Section 601 prohibits intentional discrimination and supports its established private action. Section 602 authorizes federal agencies to issue implementing regulations and to enforce them through the procedures Congress prescribed; assuming without deciding that the effects regulation here is a valid exercise of that authority, Congress has not given private plaintiffs a cause of action to enforce its additional prohibition. Administrative enforcement through section 602's procedures and private actions alleging actual intentional discrimination under section 601 remain available. Powell v. Ridge decided this question, and the Court follows it. Sandoval and the class have no private action to enforce the effects regulation; the judgment sustaining that action is reversed, the injunction is vacated insofar as it rests on that action, and the case is remanded.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs private enforcement of Title VI section 602 disparate-impact regulations generally, because the statutory source is the same for every covered recipient. It does not hold that agency regulations can never elaborate privately enforceable statutory duties under any federal law, and it does not reach private causes under materially different statutory schemes. A regulation that permissibly elaborates an identifiable individual entitlement created by the statute itself remains governed by Blessing, Wright and Wilder. Section 601 intentional-discrimination actions keep Cannon's established private action; Gebser and Davis continue to govern recipient responsibility in their intentional-discrimination settings; and Franklin governs available remedies once a valid private action exists, without itself creating one.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Statutory text and structure carry the decisive weight; established private-action precedent supplies the framework, and administrative-law principles explain why an agency regulation cannot by itself enlarge private judicial enforcement. Private judicial enforcement is a legislative choice, so the provisions must be examined for the rights and enforcement mechanisms Congress actually established. Section 601's established private action concerns its own substantive prohibition of intentional discrimination. Section 602 gives implementing authority to specified agencies and supplies a distinct public enforcement structure: notice, an effort to secure voluntary compliance, a hearing and express finding, a report to the appropriate congressional committees, and funding termination confined to the noncompliant program. Congress's creation of an agency duty does not establish a parallel entitlement for private plaintiffs to compel compliance in federal court. The rule does not make the regulation meaningless: its violation may justify agency action under the enacted procedures, and a regulation may inform how an existing statutory right is implemented where the plaintiff has such a right. Describing sections 601 and 602 as an integrated scheme does not convert the additional effects obligation into a private judicial claim, and confining relief to prospective injunctions limits the consequences of a cause of action without supplying its source. Nor does the usefulness of private enforcement supply that source. Cannon is an implied-action precedent for the statutory right it recognized, not for every agency obligation. Guardians and Choate distinguish intentional statutory discrimination from effects regulation; their fragmented or context-specific discussions are not an unequivocal congressional authorization of this cause. Powell decided the same statutory question last Term, and Stone joined it. No amendment of Title VI and no intervening decision shows its construction to be wrong, and statutory precedent carries special force; stare decisis therefore confirms the answer the text and structure supply. On this record the national-origin effects finding is unchallenged and the regulation's validity is assumed. Those premises remain, but they do not answer the decisive question whether these respondents possess a private action to enforce the effects requirement. They do not. No discriminatory purpose is found in the English-only examination practice, and none is inferred from the effects finding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The independent validity and scope of every section 602 disparate-impact regulation, the remedies available to federal agencies and particular administrative enforcement disputes, adequately pleaded section 601 intentional-discrimination actions, and private causes under materially different federal statutory schemes lie outside the question presented; regulatory validity is assumed for this review rather than decided. Section 1983 enforcement was neither pleaded nor granted and is not decided; Powell's section 1983 holding remains controlling at its own scope and is not silently abrogated.
