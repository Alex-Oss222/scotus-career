# Daniels v. United States — independent non-Stone commitments

Event: merits, April 25, 2001, No. 99-9136. Stage: independent modeling; not a Court disposition. Eight Associate Justices participate. Authorized inputs: frozen April 24–25 neutral packet, preparation receipt, and Daniels entering-law slice. No matter-specific comparator or controlled position supplement was received. Research cutoff: April 25, 2001; Coss and Randell are not entering precedents merely through calendar order. No additional historical source was retrieved.

## Components and alternatives

Daniels is in custody under the federal judgment. This is not custody under each independently expired state sentence, but the motion challenges the current enhanced federal sentence. The legal question is whether section 2255 permits the old constitutional defects to be adjudicated through that present-sentence challenge. His direct appeal's statutory classification objection is different. No complete denial of appointed counsel, actual predicate invalidation or externally frustrated earlier review is asserted.

Path A affirms by extending Custis's finality and channel reasoning to this section 2255 motion: when an operative, counseled predicate has become unreviewable after an available meaningful original opportunity was not used, the present enhancement does not renew collateral review of that predicate. The special unconstitutional complete-denial-of-counsel exception and a later application after independent invalidation remain distinct. This is a new, bounded section 2255 holding; Custis itself expressly reserved expired-custody cases. It does not find the pleas valid or resolve faultless absence of review.

The strongest objection to Path A is section 2255's express present-sentence remedy. The absence of an ACCA sentencing procedure was central in Custis, whereas Congress has supplied a collateral procedure here, capable of record development. The response is that authority to review the federal sentence does not itself reopen every old state judgment whose own authorized review was available and allowed to lapse. Parke supports regularity, not an irrebuttable merits presumption; Garlotte's continuous aggregate does not change the independently expired predicates.

Path B reverses the categorical channel denial and permits the current-sentence challenge subject to the actual section 2255 and ordinary collateral-review conditions. Custis's sentencing forum and statutory silence are materially different from the explicit current-sentence procedure. Its strongest objection is evasion of old state-review restrictions and stale factual reconstruction. The response preserves applicable default, timeliness, prior-adjudication and proof requirements rather than assuming those requirements are satisfied or creating a categorical additional bar. The motion still must establish constitutional invalidity and an actual unlawful enhancement; reopening does not follow merely because a defect is alleged.

## Compatibility and remedy

Four Associates support affirmance on the bounded new section 2255 rule; four support reversal of the categorical denial. All accept the special status of complete unconstitutional counsel deprivation, separate statutory classification, the independent-expiration distinction, and the absence of an adjudicated plea defect. No final Court majority, opinion assignment or historical outcome is supplied.

The affirming group can join an opinion limited to a meaningful earlier opportunity not used, with no external impediment claimed. They do not require an affirmative declaration that every faultless inability to obtain original review is barred. The reversing group can join express-channel reasoning without automatically erasing genuine procedural defaults. The two grounds cannot be blended into a universal rule merely because both preserve ordinary review requirements.

Affirmance leaves the 176-month sentence operative against this motion and makes no constitutional-validity finding about the robbery pleas. Reversal supplies no automatic evidentiary hearing, sentence reduction, release or predicate vacatur: the lower court must determine the available procedural conditions, actual constitutional error and effect on the required predicates and lawful federal sentence. A successful attack on one predicate does not mechanically establish that the ACCA lacks enough remaining qualifying convictions. No additional material source is required for these legal channel commitments.
