# Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-02-21.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of categorical state immunity for these expressly authorized Title I actions. Article I independently sustains the action, and Title I is also a valid preventive and remedial exercise of Section 5 at the employment scope presented.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand as needed for ordinary adjudication of coverage, defenses, liability, and lawful remedies. Do not find discrimination or award back pay, compensatory damages, fees, or other relief.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Article I enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The immunity dismissal presents the authority to maintain these Title I damages actions. The valid statutory duty, express authorization, and constitutional enforcement power must be established before the employees can obtain adjudication of liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the authorized employment-discrimination action may proceed against these state employers.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the controlling Article I rule. Congress’s regulation of covered employment and its ordinary private enforcement measure support the presented action, and §12202 supplies unmistakable authorization.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may be subjected to the expressly authorized Title I federal employment action where Congress validly regulates the employment obligation and provides an enforcement measure within Article I authority.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding defeats the categorical immunity defense, not any merits defense or award-specific limitation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents already rejects the proposed categorical barrier in the employment context. Disability coverage and accommodation retain their statutory qualifications; the immunity inquiry cannot be used to replace those questions with an invented requirement of an individual constitutional violation. The controlling combination is constitutional structure and statutory text, with the already adopted Article I enforcement rule carrying decisive weight. Congress regulates state employment as employment and supplies an ordinary remedy for breach of that valid duty; Section 12202 leaves no doubt about the named defendant. I reaffirm Florida Prepaid and Kimel at that scope. Requiring these employees to prove a separate constitutional tort merely to enter court would erase Congress's valid statutory protection. The general rule concerns amenability to this authorized action, while the evidence of discrimination and the amount of any award remain for adjudication.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Section 5

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Section 5 is expressly presented as the constitutional basis for the abrogation. Answer that ground as well as the independently adequate Article I ground, while confining the inquiry to Title I employment duties and the remedies before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Title I constitutes congruent and proportional preventive and remedial legislation against unconstitutional disability discrimination by state employers.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Boerne's congruence-and-proportionality rule and sustain this statutory application. Cleburne supplies the prohibition on irrational disability prejudice; this case need not establish a new universal scrutiny tier or make every failure to accommodate independently unconstitutional.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title I validly enforces the Fourteenth Amendment against state employers by prohibiting disability-based exclusion of qualified workers and requiring reasonable accommodation subject to the enacted qualifications and defenses. Congress may prevent unconstitutional stereotyping through these bounded duties without requiring each statutory plaintiff to prove an independent constitutional violation. Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes the presented actions.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns Title I employment protection and its authorized enforcement. It does not decide Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, or whether a particular plaintiff is qualified, suffered discrimination, or is entitled to a particular damages award.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Boerne permits prevention as well as compensation for a completed constitutional wrong. Congress had testimony, documented examples, and a history of public exclusion and disability stereotyping before it; those sources are not legally inferior to statistical proof merely because they describe particular experiences. State-employment evidence must be identified rather than replaced with private discrimination, but neither adjudicated violations nor a fixed incident count are prerequisites to reasonable legislative inference. Cleburne forbids the irrational prejudice the employment provisions address. Qualified-worker limits, job-related standards, reasonable accommodation, and undue-hardship defenses connect the statutory response to that problem without judicially redefining every accommodation as a constitutional entitlement. The decisive combination is Boerne's legal fit inquiry and the demonstrated exclusion Congress addressed, not deference to Congress to rewrite the Constitution. The defendants retain their statutory defenses; categorical immunity would disable the authorized means of testing them.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability, and award-specific limits lie outside the Title I abrogation question. No part of the presented Section 5 validity question remains undecided.

# Buckman Co. v. Plaintiffs' Legal Committee

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-02-21.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment permitting the fraud-on-the-FDA theories to proceed, limited to claims whose duty and causation depend on privately enforcing the federal agency relationship.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for disposition of those theories consistently with the federal enforcement allocation. Leave genuinely independent preserved injury claims to their own pleadings, defenses, and requirements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The source and object of the tort duty

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach whether the pleaded fraud-on-the-agency duty conflicts with the federal enforcement allocation; a general immunity from patient-injury suits would exceed the claim whose dismissal is under review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this private agency-fraud theory conflicts with the enacted federal enforcement arrangement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Treat the specific theory as displaced because its claimed wrong consists of violating duties to the FDA and its causal premise substitutes a private counterfactual agency determination for the assigned regulatory process.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The FDCA preempts the presented state claim existing solely by virtue of alleged fraud on the FDA’s clearance process, where Congress assigned enforcement of that federal relationship to the government.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding creates no general device immunity, no presumption that clearance establishes safety, and no exclusion of a genuinely independent traditional state duty merely because federal regulation is relevant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The conflict arises from the enacted enforcement allocation, not from a prediction that litigation is inconvenient or from compliance with a federal minimum. The pleaded theory targets a duty owed to the regulator and would have juries decide the clearance the agency supposedly should have denied. That differs from comparing the conduct owed to an injured patient under independent state law. Section 337(a)'s allocation of enforcement is decisive; the prediction that state litigation might discourage useful devices is not. I distinguish Lohr's independently existing patient-protection duties and apply Geier only after identifying this concrete conflict. Fraudulent submissions do not become lawful, and FDA clearance does not establish safety. The rule is confined by the source of the asserted duty, so a manufacturer cannot defeat an ordinary negligence or warning claim merely because the same evidence also proves a federal violation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially different claim resting on an authoritative agency fraud determination, or on a separate traditional duty, is not established in this record and is not decided.

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-02-21.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the project-affiliation immunity dismissal and remand for a water-specific inquiry.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require factual development concerning the damaging waters and the purposes behind their release, followed by application of §702c and any independently preserved FTCA defenses. No damages or negligence finding follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Water-specific immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The United States obtained judgment on the pleadings solely from the canal's project affiliation. The immunity question is therefore reached without deciding negligence or resolving the disputed character of the damaging water.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether membership in a flood-control project alone establishes the statutory defense.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reject project affiliation as a sufficient condition and clarify the received rule by returning to the statutory category of the waters causing damage.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 702c immunity depends on the character of the waters causing the relevant damage and the purposes behind their release; a facility’s inclusion in a larger flood-control project alone is insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This clarification governs multipurpose facilities and preserves immunity where the established damage is from qualifying flood waters. It does not supply a mechanical causation rule for every mixed-flow case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute names floods and flood waters. Extending it to every ordinary irrigation flow would make that language depend on administrative project boundaries. The pleading-stage record establishes the project connection but not the necessary water-specific facts. The statutory words carry decisive weight. I preserve James's holding for flood-control releases from flood-stage reservoirs and decline to extend its broader project-related language beyond what that judgment required. That language cannot convert ordinary irrigation water into flood water by administrative association. The resulting rule must govern other multipurpose facilities because the same statutory distinction recurs; the actual classification and causal contribution of this water require a record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The particular flows, release purposes, and allocation of any mixed causation require facts the record lacks.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach dissolution of the injunction because the district court retained the limitation proceeding and the claimant supplied protective stipulations. Liability and the ultimate right to limitation are not before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this adequately protected limitation interest requires exclusive federal liability litigation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the saving-to-suitors and limitation authorities to permit the state remedy when the actual federal limitation interest is secured.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A district court may dissolve a Rule F injunction where the claimant’s stipulations, adequate fund, and retained federal jurisdiction protect limitation; a state jury demand is not a necessary condition, and the Act supplies no separate exclusive federal exoneration right.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers an adequately protected limitation proceeding. It does not eliminate federal control over limitation or guarantee dissolution where competing claims or inadequate stipulations threaten that right.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute protects a limited-liability entitlement rather than a preferred forum for every defense. The district court kept the federal proceeding available and required arrangements directed at that actual entitlement. I reconcile the two statutes through the interests each actually protects, applying Langnes and Lake Tankers rather than creating a new exception. Text is decisive: saving all other remedies is broader than saving a jury demand, and limiting liability does not confer exclusive federal adjudication of every defense. The protective stipulations show why both commands can operate here. They establish a sufficient arrangement for this record without exhausting all ways other claimants may protect limitation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual liability, privity or knowledge, and a materially changed claimant or fund situation are outside the presented forum dispute.

# United States v. Tank

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it sustains admission of the chat-room logs and the convictions against the authentication challenge. Leave intact its independent remand for resentencing; do not reinstate the original 235-month sentence.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Tank receives the resentencing already required for the grouping and role-adjustment errors, but no new trial or exclusion on this authentication record. The district court must separately consider grouping under the permitted subsections and resolve the supported role adjustment and inconsistent calculation. Those sentencing directions derive from the surviving lower-court judgment, not from a new holding on the granted authentication question.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A sufficient foundation does not require technological impossibility of alteration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved objection directly challenges the evidentiary ruling sustaining the convictions and is reached. The record contains an actual foundation hearing, knowledgeable-witness testimony, and corroborating attribution; no absence of screening is inferred. The unrelated search, substantive sufficiency, and sentencing questions are unnecessary to resolve this grant.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the governing authentication Rule requires exclusion of partial Internet logs despite testimony explaining their creation and retained accuracy and evidence connecting the recorded screen name to the accused.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Rule 901’s existing sufficient-foundation standard and adopt its specific application to electronic conversation logs, an unresolved application of that rule. Do not announce a new constitutional reliability test or change the proponent’s threshold into proof that tampering was impossible.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Participant testimony explaining the recording process and identifying the retained record as accurate, combined with circumstances sufficiently connecting a screen name to the defendant, can satisfy Rule 901 even though unrelated passages were deleted and electronic alteration was possible. The combined proof here satisfies that threshold. The rule is an application of existing evidentiary law, with this particular digital-record holding newly adopted; it does not make authentication conclusive or excuse a foundation incapable of supporting the claimed identity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the offered retained conversations and Tank’s alleged participation under the identified screen name. It does not treat the printouts as an unedited archive, establish every conversation’s truth, shift the criminal burden, or foreclose a specific objection under another evidentiary Rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Riva had direct knowledge of the recording and described the deletions rather than concealing that the files were partial. His accuracy testimony supplied evidence a juror could credit; Tank’s admission and witnesses’ identification of the meeting participant supplied independent grounds for attribution. An ability to alter text is a reason to investigate and cross-examine, not alone a reason to require an unavailable degree of certainty. A different offer consisting only of an unexplained printout and an unsupported screen name would lack the combination found here. The defense remained free to contest Riva’s credibility and the significance of missing context. The resulting jury assessment does not replace the judge’s initial threshold, and the crimes’ seriousness neither relaxes that threshold nor adds a special presumption against the accused. The decisive inquiry is Rule 901's evidentiary threshold, applied to the total foundation rather than to the medium's novelty. Testimony, Tank's admission, and corroborating circumstances are evidence even without a technical audit. Their combined force matters; no one item is made an irrebuttable presumption. Hale's public-record application and Daubert's expert-screening function are distinguishable. This is a sufficient-foundation holding, not a safe harbor for every electronic log: concrete evidence of alteration or materially misleading omission remains subject to the governing Rules and judicial screening.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Outside the question presented: the validity of the automobile search, independent hearsay objections, and the substantive sentencing determinations. No separate constitutional confrontation claim about these logs is resolved because none is presented in the question granted.

# Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-02-27.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the automatic-preclusion judgment and remand for application of the appropriate federal rule incorporating California preclusion law.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require Maryland’s courts to determine the effect California would accord the comparable judgment, subject to any specifically demonstrated incompatible federal interest. Do not reopen the California dismissal or decide the underlying tort claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal judgment effect and state-law incorporation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Maryland treated the federal limitations dismissal as automatically dispositive. The source and extent of that judgment's preclusive effect must therefore be decided; the tort merits need not be.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which rule governs this diversity limitations judgment’s effect elsewhere.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a federal judgment-effect rule that ordinarily incorporates the rendering State’s claim-preclusion law. This resolves an open application without creating general federal substantive tort law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Federal common law governs the claim-preclusive effect of a federal diversity judgment and ordinarily adopts the rule of the State where the rendering court sits, except where that rule is incompatible with a specific federal interest; Rule 41(b)’s merits label alone does not dictate universal claim preclusion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns diversity judgments on state-law claims. It does not determine every federal-question judgment, every sanction dismissal, or the application of California law not decided below.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The federal judicial act requires a federal source, but state-law incorporation avoids a removal-driven enlargement of substantive consequences and respects Erie’s allocation. Rule 41(b) can perform its internal dismissal function without silently determining every subsequent forum’s treatment. Finality remains effective at its lawful scope. Federal judicial authority determines the source of the rule, while Erie supplies the decisive reason for ordinarily borrowing state law. Those structural considerations are reconciled rather than balanced case by case against a preferred result. O'Melveny and Atherton do not forbid a rule governing the effect of a federal judgment, but their insistence on an identified federal interest defeats an undifferentiated national substantive rule. Rule 41(b) governs the dismissal's effect in the rendering court; reading it to enlarge substantive claim extinguishment everywhere would also strain the Rules Enabling Act.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Federal-question judgments and materially different sanction dismissals are outside the presented diversity limitations dispute.

# Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc. / American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Whitman

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-02-27.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In the cross-petition, affirm exclusion of implementation costs from §109 standard setting. In EPA’s petition, reverse the nondelegation ruling, sustain reviewability, and reject the asserted implementation interpretation that nullifies applicable ozone-specific constraints.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for a lawful implementation construction and any preserved administrative-review questions. Do not order cost balancing, set numerical air-quality standards, or approve every aspect of the 1997 rules.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Cost criterion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The cross-petition requires construction of the standard-setting provision before assessing the constitutional sufficiency of the power it delegates.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress authorized implementation-cost balancing in §109.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted health-standard criterion and the statute’s separation of functions; exclude implementation costs from this standard-setting decision.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 109’s national health-standard command does not authorize EPA to moderate the required standard through implementation-cost balancing.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This statutory holding does not exclude costs from other provisions that authorize them or settle every indirect health-effects question.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress used a health-protection criterion here and treated implementation considerations separately. Adding a general cost-benefit permission would alter the assigned decision rather than interpret an ambiguity within it. Text and the division between standard setting and implementation decide this question. Union Electric is reaffirmed within that division; MCI and Brown & Williamson reinforce respect for the assigned statutory function, not a presumption against consequential regulation. The exclusion of costs is categorical for this particular decision because Congress chose a health criterion; it is not a general judicial preference against balancing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Adequate congressional guidance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Having identified the statutory assignment, reach the nondelegation ground on which the court of appeals displaced the standards; no adequate nonconstitutional ground disposes of that challenge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the health-protection assignment is constitutionally bounded.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing guided-delegation standard and sustain this assignment. Reject the proposition that technical judgment requires Congress to state a numerical pollution threshold.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress supplies adequate guidance by confining the task to standards requisite to protect public health with an adequate safety margin under the specified scientific framework; EPA’s application of that criterion is constitutionally permissible.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns this enacted, subject-specific assignment. It does not permit agency self-limitation to cure a truly standardless statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress selected the protected interest, the regulatory subject, the degree-of-protection criterion, and the evidentiary framework. Remaining uncertainty concerns application to scientific evidence, not permission to select any social objective. The constitutional structure requires Congress to choose the governing policy, not to legislate every scientific estimate. Loving's distinction between delegated execution and missing legislative authority applies. This combination of structural reasoning and precedent gives decisive weight to the boundaries Congress enacted. Courts can review whether EPA follows them without choosing pollution levels themselves; the agency could not cure a genuinely standardless delegation by voluntarily restraining itself.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 3: Reviewability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve finality and ripeness before adjudicating the implementation interpretation, because an unreviewable agency view would supply no occasion for that statutory holding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the implementation framework may be reviewed now.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply completed-action and present-consequence principles to the settled legal framework accompanying the final ozone rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The settled implementation interpretation presented with the final ozone action is reviewable under §307 where it determines the governing planning framework and presents a fit legal issue with present consequences.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not make tentative guidance or every prediction about future permits final agency action.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Further individual implementation proceedings would not clarify the central statutory question, while the chosen framework already structures the States’ required planning. The specific final action and review statute supply the necessary basis. Bennett supplies the completed-action requirement. Ohio Forestry is distinguished because no later site-specific decision must make this legal framework operative. The present planning consequences establish why review is timely; their practical importance alone would not turn an unsettled proposal into final action.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 4: Implementation constraints

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The final and ripe interpretation is properly before the Court. Rejecting its removal of applicable ozone constraints requires no judicial replacement of EPA's implementation program.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether EPA can use revision of the standard to remove the specific ozone subpart’s constraints.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing statutory-boundary rule and reject the asserted wholesale displacement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** EPA may reconcile genuine ambiguity between the general and ozone-specific nonattainment provisions, but may not interpret revision of a standard as authority to nullify applicable constraints Congress expressly retained.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding identifies the impermissible interpretation and leaves EPA the first opportunity to develop a lawful reconciliation within the statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The specific subpart would lose its operative function under EPA’s asserted theory. Administrative flexibility must operate within the enacted arrangement, and the Court lacks a basis to choose a new implementation program itself. The textually retained limits are decisive even under Chevron. I therefore apply the existing boundary on deference and do not use this case to overrule that doctrine. A choice within an authorized range remains EPA's; a choice that erases Congress's range is not delegated. Remand is warranted because the defect identifies an unlawful interpretation without making the Court the first author of the replacement program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The details of a replacement interpretation and particular State implementation plans are outside the agency action and record under review.

# Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-02-27. **Date status:** organizationally assigned.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Seventh Circuit judgment vacating summary judgment for the City and Fund and remanding for corrective proceedings, while specifying the attribution-and-function inquiry under Agostini v. Felton for the remand.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the genuine conveyance and require effective termination of municipal sponsorship through operational and intelligible separation. The lower court must identify the continuing City conduct and an effective lawful cure; private destruction or relocation is not authorized merely by possible passerby confusion.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Ending municipal sponsorship while protecting the private display

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Identify the City's continuing conduct and the challengers' live injury. The genuine conveyance is constitutionally relevant, but the retained public arrangement remains reviewable and does not moot that challenge.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a formally private parcel integrated into the municipal park has actually ended the challenged governmental religious function.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Agostini v. Felton and the bounded private-expression protection of Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette to the open divestiture application. Do not revive superseded independent Lemon v. Kurtzman prongs.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A bona fide sale ends governmental ownership, but cures the establishment violation only when continuing municipal acts no longer sponsor or present the religious message as the City's own. Here the retained public dedication and indistinguishable display preserve the problem. Effective attribution and operational separation can cure it while leaving the private statue in place. This is an adopted application of the existing unified inquiry.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches invisible divestitures leaving an official religious function intact. It does not equate private religious land adjacent to a park with municipal speech, and it creates no categorical requirement of sale, removal, or a permanent wall for every monument.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The layout, retained use, historical presentation, and ineffective ownership communication operate together. A complete cure must terminate the identified City conduct while preserving the Fund's private speech and property. Courts may require accurate boundaries and clear attribution on municipal land, without selecting theology or prescribing a fence where an equally effective lawful separation works. Agostini's functional attribution inquiry controls; the deed's formal transfer is important evidence but not conclusive. The stipulated end of municipal maintenance and electricity must be credited alongside the covenant, site arrangement, and history. Pinette protects genuinely private expression and therefore limits the cure. A disclaimer is effective only when the City's actual presentation matches it. The controlling distinction is continued governmental sponsorship, not whether a particular observer dislikes or misunderstands a private religious display.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final design, placement, and covenant implementation require property and site details not conclusively supplied here. Those missing implementation facts belong on remand under the stated rule.

# Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez / United States v. Velazquez

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-02-28.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment invalidating the challenged welfare-law advocacy restriction in both consolidated dockets.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the limited relief and uncontested severance of that restriction. Permit otherwise authorized client representation to include relevant statutory and constitutional challenges; order no new funding entitlement or invalidation of unrelated conditions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Independent client representation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the restriction governing otherwise authorized welfare representation; the challenged proviso suppresses a class of legal arguments within that service, and its uncontested severance permits effective relief without redesigning the program.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the funding condition permissibly defines the funded service or impermissibly selects the client’s legal position within it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the private-speech distinction in the entering subsidy cases to client-directed legal advocacy and invalidate this selective restriction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Where Congress funds independent representation of eligible welfare claimants, it may not condition that representation on counsel’s withholding otherwise relevant challenges to the validity of the law governing the client’s claim.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The application covers this selective restriction within authorized representation. It does not require funding every subject, every client, every form of litigation, or the government’s own opposing message.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The lawyer’s function is to provide the client legally competent advocacy, including arguments against government. A condition suppressing only the challenge to governing law changes that function at the point it matters, while the program’s opposing government speaker remains unrestricted. The opportunity to withdraw does not supply the omitted advocacy to the indigent client. Rosenberger and Southworth carry decisive weight because this program purchases independent advocacy for the client. I distinguish Rust's defined governmental program from representation whose assigned role includes contesting government. The effect on judicial decisionmaking confirms the First Amendment defect rather than supplying an independent appropriation power. The categorical bar is on selecting the permissible viewpoint inside this funded representation, not on Congress defining which subject matters it will support. The resulting rule preserves a usable legal service without creating a universal civil-counsel entitlement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unrelated LSC restrictions and the independent validity of the uncontested severability ruling are outside the questions presented.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Privilege follows exposure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the federal premise because it is an operative reason for the state judgment and because the alternative-perpetrator theory creates real incriminating exposure.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a witness’s denial of wrongdoing eliminates an otherwise reasonable risk of self-incrimination.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply surviving privilege law, without changing the compelled-production rule in United States v. Hubbell.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fifth Amendment protects a witness who reasonably faces incriminating exposure even when she denies wrongdoing. A court may not infer absence of privilege from that denial. Here the defense’s accusation and disputed injury chronology create a reasonable risk, requiring correction of the contrary premise.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This rule covers incriminating testimonial links as well as direct admissions; it does not make an unsupported assertion of fear conclusive.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A witness can honestly believe herself innocent while her testimony supplies evidence that prosecutors may use against her. The privilege’s legal operation cannot depend on a court first deciding the ultimate crime. The actual record supplies more than an abstract risk, and resolving that point does not excuse the state court from examining the defendant’s own trial protections. Hoffman and Grunewald are reaffirmed: protection follows a reasonable risk of incrimination, not an admission of guilt. The denial of guilt therefore cannot operate as a categorical forfeiture. The competing expert chronology and accusation supply concrete exposure without requiring the witness to prove she committed the offense. This narrow correction protects the witness while leaving the defendant's distinct challenge to the use and presentation of immunity for ordinary review; recognizing one person's privilege does not establish another person's guilt.

## A. STATUS

* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the First Circuit judgment reversing dismissal of Hilario's indictment.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the prosecution for ordinary proceedings under the valid appointment. No determination of guilt or invalidation of other prosecutions follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The statutory vacancy condition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve duration first. Rejecting the asserted cap leaves the preserved constitutional alternatives necessary to sustaining the appointment; an institutional preference for a presidential appointment cannot substitute for interpreting the enacted route.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Section 546(d) terminates a district-court-appointed United States Attorney after four years despite the vacancy remaining unfilled by presidential appointment.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enactment's distinct appointment routes and adopt the construction of their unresolved interaction. No controlling holding establishes the imported four-year limit.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A district-court appointee serves until the vacancy is filled under the statutory condition, without an imported four-year limit. The ordinary appointment term does not erase the express vacancy condition. Gil's long service alone therefore does not terminate his appointment.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the contemporaneous judicial appointment route and its unfilled-vacancy condition. It does not extend the Attorney General's separate 120-day authority or eliminate the President's ordinary nomination and removal powers.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress stated different durations for different appointment mechanisms. Importing the general term into the judicial mechanism would displace its explicit stopping condition. Extended vacancy may create an accountability concern, but that concern does not amend the text. The express endpoint in Section 546(d) carries decisive weight over an inferred four-year restriction drawn from Section 541. The general presidential appointment route and the vacancy route can both retain their work. I adopt this construction for repeated vacancies because it follows the enacted condition, not because six years of service is inherently desirable.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Later statutory amendments fall outside the event-date law and the question presented.

### Issue 2: Inferior status and permissible interbranch selection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both preserved constitutional challenges after sustaining statutory duration. No actual judicial direction of charging, bias, or improper prosecution is established; the challenge concerns the powers the arrangement confers.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this executive office is inferior and whether Congress may vest its appointment in the district court before which it prosecutes.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Edmond v. United States to the actual executive hierarchy and the Appointments Clause's express court-appointment option. Adopt this office-specific application while preserving the prohibition on judicial assumption of prosecution.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** United States Attorneys remain inferior officers where executive superiors retain legally effective direction and supervision and the President retains removal. Congress may authorize courts to select them without giving those courts power to direct prosecutions. The arrangement is valid on this statutory structure and record.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers appointment to this subordinate executive office and excludes any inference that appointment carries charging, policy, litigation-supervision, or judicial removal authority. The duration conclusion does not depend on calling the office temporary.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Significant prosecutorial responsibilities do not establish autonomy from executive superiors. Actual control and removal, rather than labels, establish the hierarchy. Judicial selection alone does not transfer executive enforcement to judges. A long vacancy changes neither those powers nor the statute's expiration condition. The constitutional text permits Congress to choose courts of law for inferior-officer appointments. Edmond supplies the structural inquiry into real supervision; its particular combination is not a checklist copied from military adjudication. Here executive direction and presidential removal prevent the selecting court from becoming the prosecutor's superior. Functional examination of those powers confirms that textual conclusion and is not a free-standing balance of interbranch convenience. Morrison's incompatibility concern remains: selection cannot carry control over charges or partisan responsibility for prosecution.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual judicial participation in prosecutorial choices or a concrete bias claim requires different facts absent from this record. No collateral reopening of completed prosecutions is presented.

# Shafer v. South Carolina

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical rejection of the Simmons v. South Carolina claim and remand for resolution of the remaining predicates.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the state court to decide whether future dangerousness was placed in issue and, if so, apply the direct-review harmlessness requirement with the State bearing the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Harmful denial requires a new penalty proceeding if death is sought, or a lawful noncapital sentence; guilt remains intact.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The actual penalty choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the structural issue while leaving the fact-dependent dangerousness finding to ordinary state appellate adjudication on the existing record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a preliminary possibility of a lesser judicial sentence defeats accurate parole information at a later binary jury choice.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Simmons v. South Carolina to the revised procedure; this is a new application, not a replacement of its predicates.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When future dangerousness is in issue and the jury, having found the necessary aggravator, can choose only death or life without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to convey that ineligibility. Earlier conditional alternatives available to a judge do not defeat the rule. A generic definition of life is inadequate if it leaves the material parole misunderstanding unresolved.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs this staged capital scheme and materially equivalent jury choices. It excludes proceedings lacking the dangerousness predicate or a legally accurate ineligibility premise.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The information answers the prosecution’s claim that continued life exposes others to future risk outside custody. Its relevance arises at the actual selection point. The State cannot defeat meaningful rebuttal by pointing to a sentence the jury can no longer select, but neither may the Court invent the predicate the state court did not decide. I reaffirm Simmons's opportunity to answer a material death-sentencing premise and extend it to the equivalent choice in this staged procedure. The operative jury alternatives, rather than the statute's abstract list, are decisive. On remand the State's evidence, the purpose for which it was offered, counsel's argument, and the jury's questions must be assessed together; future danger need not be announced through a prescribed phrase. The preserved predicate inquiry does not permit treating circumstantial or qualitative proof as no evidence. A general rule is warranted because the rebuttal right would otherwise depend on rearranging the same sentencing choices.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the trial presentation actually raised future dangerousness requires the unresolved record determination; no broader parole-instruction right is decided.
