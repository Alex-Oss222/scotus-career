# OT_2000CHUNK1

# Bush v. Gore

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, plus any actually effective intervening decision before December 12, 2000. Stone’s Palm Beach position is intervening precedent only to the extent adopted by the Court.
* **Conditions:** Apply intervening controlling decisions and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate defective procedures and remand for correction.

**Stone's order:**

1. Vacate the recount procedures insofar as they permit materially arbitrary treatment of comparable ballots.
2. Remand immediately to the Florida Supreme Court.
3. Direct that court to establish constitutionally adequate procedures implementing Florida's existing voter-intent standard.
4. Require consistent treatment of comparable ballots, including reconsideration of previously counted ballots where necessary to eliminate material inequality.
5. Direct the Florida judiciary to determine whether state law authorizes completion of the corrected recount and whether completion is practically feasible.
6. Hold that December 12 is not, by force of 3 U.S.C. §5 alone, a mandatory federal cutoff for continued counting.
7. Do not award Florida's electoral votes to either candidate.

Correction may proceed only through lawful contest authority and a feasible process. Neither feasibility nor impossibility is established merely by invoking the safe-harbor date. This disposition does not independently resolve an unpresented stay application.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Stone's Holding:** The Equal Protection Clause requires materially consistent treatment of legally comparable votes. A correctable constitutional defect in recount administration warrants corrective relief, not automatic termination of the electoral contest.

**Entering law and application:** This is an open application of equal protection to statewide judicial counting. The districting decisions reject territorial design alone as a constitutional deprivation; they do not permit arbitrary valuation of comparable ballots. The injury here is unequal administration of the vote actually cast. No prior holding makes §5's safe-harbor benefit a universal counting deadline.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Materially inconsistent treatment of comparable ballots violates equal protection. An abstract voter-intent standard is capable of lawful implementation, but the recount needs sufficiently definite instructions and consistent treatment in operation. Uniformity does not require mechanically identical treatment of ballots with legally relevant differences. It requires that equivalent voting marks receive equivalent consideration, including correction of earlier inclusions where that is necessary to remove the established inequality.

The constitutional defect does not automatically invalidate the whole contest. The Florida court must distinguish interpretation implementing existing election law from a materially different post-election rule, identify any state-law deadline, and determine whether a lawful corrective count can actually be completed. Section 5 alone supplies no federal command to terminate the count on December 12. An unfavorable forecast for one candidate supplies no legal reason either to continue or to stop.

**Boundary:** The order determines neither a winner nor how Congress must ultimately treat competing electoral submissions. It neither guarantees completion nor permits counting beyond independently applicable law.

## D. DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT AND FUTURE APPLICATION

Equal protection requires consistent treatment of comparable votes and effective correction of material inequality. The rule applies beyond these candidates and this election. Differences reflecting legally relevant ballot characteristics remain permissible; ordinary administrative variation without material unequal treatment does not establish a constitutional violation.
