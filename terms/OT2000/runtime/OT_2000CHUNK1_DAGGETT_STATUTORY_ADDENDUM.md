## Matching and participation provisions

The complete original section 1125 appears in 1995 Initiated Bill chapter 1, section 17, effective January 2, 1997. The official section history records no amendment to section 1125 before 2001. The source therefore establishes the terms applicable to the presented 2000 system.

Section 1125(9)'s trigger is broader than the lower opinion's shorthand about receipts: a campaign, finance, or election report must show that the greater of expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, alone or together with independent expenditures reported under section 1019, exceeds the distribution under subsection 8. The Commission must immediately issue an opposing certified candidate the reported excess, subject to matching limited to twice the original distribution under subsection 8(A) or (C), as applicable. The independently validated regulatory deduction and no-uncontested-general-election qualification remain applicable.

Section 1125(6) forbids post-certification contributions unless specifically authorized by the Commission. Section 1125(13) prohibits disbursement beyond available fund revenue. If revenue cannot meet subsection 8 or 9 distributions, the Commission may permit certified candidates to accept and spend private contributions, reduced by seed money, up to the applicable public-funding amounts, subject to a $250 per-donor-per-election legislative ceiling or $500 gubernatorial ceiling and Commission rules. This permission is not a mandatory release, an unlimited private-fundraising right, or an assumption that the fund is insufficient.

The contribution limits except candidate and spouse contributions under section 1015(1). Public Law 1999 chapter 729, section 2, effective August 11, 2000, deletes obsolete pre-1999 language but preserves the operative $250 legislative ceiling and this exception. It changes no presented dollar amount. This is a verified noncase statutory chronology fact for operator consideration; the addendum itself enters no Admitted Source Record.

## Severability

The Act contains no special severability clause in its complete nineteen sections. The general rule, 1 MRSA section 71(8), as amended by Public Law 1991 chapter 332, section 1, applies to statutes and session laws. Invalid provisions or applications do not affect other provisions or applications that can operate without them. Repeal of a severability clause is treated as removal of surplus language unless the law indicates otherwise. The introductory construction rule yields when inconsistent with the enactment's plain meaning. These terms supply the severability inquiry; they do not themselves select which portion of the challenged scheme is invalid or dictate a substitute financing design.
