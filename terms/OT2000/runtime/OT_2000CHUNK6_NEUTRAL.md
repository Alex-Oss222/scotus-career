# Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc.

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-21. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc.

**Citation or Docket:** No. 00-347; 532 U.S. 588.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a civil jury judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-21 (historical decision-calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether selling an oral option to acquire stock while secretly intending never to honor it is deception in connection with a securities purchase or sale under Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5. This is a faithful formulation; exact granted wording has not been independently verified.

**Question Granted:** The question presented above defines the merits issue under review.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit judgment affirming the verdict.

**Relief Requested:** Wharf seeks reversal of federal securities liability; United seeks affirmance. The reviewed federal issue does not independently reopen every state-law ground or damages issue.

**Material Record:** United supplied technical, financial, and business work for Wharf's bid to operate a Hong Kong cable system. A jury found that Wharf orally granted an option to purchase ten percent of the future system, subject to financing and exercise conditions. No final writing was signed. United raised financing and sought to exercise after the franchise award; Wharf refused. Internal memoranda supported the finding that Wharf never intended to honor the option, rather than merely changing its plans later. The jury awarded $67 million in compensatory damages and $58.5 million in punitive damages on state-law claims. These findings are taken favorably to the verdict winner; no independent factfinding is authorized on review. [Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-588.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The relevant purchase is the option, not an unrealized later purchase of shares. Whether the oral agreement existed and whether the initial fraudulent intent was proved were litigated below. The legal question cannot be recast as a finding that every breached promise is fraudulent.

**Relevant Dates:** Work and option negotiations in 1992; franchise and attempted exercise in 1993; event May 21, 2001.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No companion consolidation or participation limitation identified.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.

### Issue 1: Deception in selling a presently acquired option

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. preserves a confined affirmative-assistance theory, contrary to the historical categorical rejection of private aiding-and-abetting liability. It requires underlying fraud, culpability, reliance, causation, and loss; neither ordinary services nor breach alone becomes fraud. Wharf's own sale presents primary liability.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The securities standards distinguish culpable affirmative participation and transactional deception from negligence, status, or ordinary contractual nonperformance. The full elements of an available private action remain necessary.

**Other Governing Law:** Section 10(b), Rule 10b-5, and the statutory definition of a security are the enacted sources. The definition includes an option; the immediate purchase alleged here is that option, not merely the prospective shares.

**Material Other Authority:** United States v. O'Hagan concerns deception connected with a securities transaction and does not make every commercial or fiduciary wrong securities fraud. Blue Chip Stamps v. Manor Drug Stores addresses purchaser-seller standing and proof difficulties. Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green requires deception beyond corporate unfairness.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** These authorities preserve purchase, deception, culpability, causation, and loss requirements. None makes oral form a categorical exclusion of an otherwise established option. The secondary-liability rule supplies no shortcut to primary liability.

**Open Point:** Whether concealed contemporaneous intent never to honor an option defeats what is sold at the time of its sale and satisfies the required connection despite the option's oral form.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Tenth Circuit accepted the jury's finding of an actual oral option and treated the deliberately false promise as securities deception, not merely failure to perform a contract.

**Wharf:** Federal securities law should not absorb ordinary contract litigation. An unwritten promise to sell future shares invites the evidentiary concerns behind purchaser-seller limitations, and nondelivery alone cannot establish a deception about the security.

**United:** It bought a presently valuable option with services. The seller's secret decision never to honor that option made the security illusory when sold. The contemporaneous documents establish the required initial intent independently of later breach. The SEC supported affirmance on that transactional theory. The concession central to the dispute is that an honest promise followed by nonperformance would not alone suffice.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Merits paths:** Affirm because the jury found an actual security sale and deception at that sale; reverse if the statute excludes the transaction or the necessary connection is legally absent; or remand if the submitted theory permitted liability on breach alone. The last path requires an identified instruction or preservation defect, not speculative distrust of oral contracts.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding covering knowingly sham options extends to oral and written securities alike while retaining proof of purchase, contemporaneous deception, reliance, causation, and loss. It does not federalize every later contractual dispute. Affirmance resolves the reviewed securities issue; any state punitive award remains grounded in its own law. The Court should not award fresh damages or alter unreviewed state-law claims.

It identifies the Tenth Circuit judgment and the SEC's position. Internal documents are described as trial evidence credited by the jury, not independently authenticated additional findings.

**Case authorities:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A.; United States v. O'Hagan; Blue Chip Stamps v. Manor Drug Stores; Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green.

# Chen v. City of Houston

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Chen v. City of Houston.

**Citation or Docket:** 206 F.3d 502; Fifth Circuit No. 98-20440. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; disposition on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** May 21, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term.

**Question Presented:** Whether the Equal Protection Clause permits a municipality to apportion city-council districts using total population when substantial differences in the distribution of noncitizens produce materially unequal citizen voting-age populations among districts?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses the population-base question presented. The separate racial-gerrymandering claim litigated below is not included in that question.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Houston on the population-equality and racial-gerrymandering claims.

**Relief Requested:** The voters seek reversal on the asserted constitutional requirement to use citizen voting-age population. The city seeks to preserve its choice of total population and the judgment. [Chen v. City of Houston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.502.98-20440.html).

**Material Record:** Houston's fifteen-member council includes the mayor and five other members elected at large and nine members elected from single-member districts. Annexation of Kingwood, approximately 40,000 residents, required adjustments for the 1997 plan. The council adopted that plan on April 9, 1997. Its maximum total-population deviation was 8.63 percent. Two districts had African-American population majorities and two had Hispanic population majorities. The Department of Justice precleared the plan on July 7. Plaintiffs filed on the day of adoption; a preliminary injunction was denied, and the district court granted the city's summary-judgment motions on May 7, 1998. Their evidence indicated that citizen voting-age population deviations exceeded ten percent and that noncitizens were distributed unevenly. The appellate court did not reject the demographic disparity as imaginary; it rejected the claimed obligation to select that denominator. [Chen v. City of Houston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.502.98-20440.html).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Personal exposure to the alleged voting inequality must remain distinct from success on the constitutional theory. The existence of a political choice does not itself make the constitutional challenge nonjusticiable. Review must distinguish choosing a population base from permissible departures within that base. The record supports resolving the categorical demand for citizen voting-age apportionment without selecting an exact alternative map. Preclearance does not adjudicate all equal-protection claims.

**Relevant Dates:** The March 9, 2000 lower judgment precedes the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Easley v. Cromartie and Smallwood v. Cromartie are earlier same-term matters; no controlling disposition in either matter is identified.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before May 21, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Apply any earlier controlling decision in Easley v. Cromartie or Smallwood v. Cromartie only to the extent its holding affects the separate denominator question.

### Issue 1: The permissible population denominator

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Voinovich v. Quilter unanimously requires genuine justification of a state legislative plan's maximum total-population deviation above ten percent. A possible rational county-preservation policy is not automatic justification, and departures below ten percent are generally minor rather than categorically immune. That rule does not decide which population denominator must govern when citizenship distribution differs substantially.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess the challenged electoral injury, the selected constitutional measure and any justification separately. United States Department of Commerce v. Montana unanimously permits review of a constitutional apportionment challenge even when several choices may be constitutional. Its approval of equal proportions depends on interstate allocation of whole seats, state boundaries and constitutional minimums; it expressly preserves distinct intrastate equality rules. It does not resolve Houston's denominator.

**Other Governing Law:** Reynolds v. Sims and Avery v. Midland County supply the inherited population-equality principle for state and local representative bodies. Burns v. Richardson supplies relevant antecedent treatment of a permissible population base, but permission in that setting does not by itself command citizen voting-age apportionment here. The primary opinion sets out those antecedent authorities and the parties' competing representational and electoral-equality theories. [Chen v. City of Houston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.502.98-20440.html).

**Material Other Authority:** The racial-design standards in Shaw v. Reno and Miller v. Johnson require a distinct constitutional deprivation rather than treating race-conscious geometry alone as injury. Those standards concern a separate claim litigated below and do not answer the accepted denominator claim.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing equality law governs justification of departures within an applicable population measure and permits judicial review despite a range of permissible legislative choices. No controlling precedent requires citizen voting-age population to replace total population.

**Open Point:** The Court must newly resolve whether materially unequal citizen voting-age populations make otherwise equal total-population municipal districts impermissible under the accepted question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Fifth Circuit viewed the issue as a choice between representational equality and equality among potential voters. It rejected a requirement of citizen voting-age apportionment and also declined to read the Constitution as invariably commanding total population. It relied on the latitude described in Burns and the absence of a definitive contrary command. On the separate claim, it concluded that the plaintiffs' evidence did not establish predominant racial motivation under the historical framework it applied. That separate disposition does not supply the answer to the accepted question. [Chen v. City of Houston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.502.98-20440.html).

**Voters' Strongest Position:** Voting is exercised by eligible citizens. When two equally populous districts contain materially different numbers of potential voters, an eligible voter in the smaller electorate has greater numerical influence over the choice of a representative. Total population is not merely an imperfect proxy in a case where the city knows of large differences. A requirement of equal voting power should be applied to the people capable of casting votes, rather than dismissed as an unreviewable policy preference.

**City's Strongest Position:** Council members represent and provide access to public decision-making for all residents, including children and noncitizens. Equal resident populations distribute constituent demand and representation on an intelligible basis. Citizenship measures also omit represented persons and introduce questions about eligibility categories and data. The Constitution need not demand simultaneous numerical equality among resident populations and eligible electorates when the two cannot both be achieved. The city's selected denominator does not restrict anyone's franchise or count some ballots less within a district.

**Limits on Both Accounts:** Voting equality remains a constitutional constraint; municipal preference cannot immunize intentional exclusion or an independently established dilution mechanism. Conversely, the mathematical difference between eligible electorates does not itself prove that equal representation of residents is constitutionally forbidden. The ten-percent rule cannot decide which denominator should be selected before that legal issue has been addressed.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Treat the alleged personal representational injury as judicially reviewable and identify the accepted claim. The Court can decide the legal permissibility of total population without reopening the distinct gerrymandering claim.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may hold total population permissible on this record, require a different denominator or a further justification for its use, or remand for material demographic findings if the chosen constitutional rule makes those findings necessary. Holding that one choice is permissible does not require declaring every alternative permissible or a single denominator universally mandatory.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance on the accepted question leaves the city's population-base choice intact. Reversal could require lawful reconsideration and an appropriately supported districting remedy, while respecting the city's primary responsibility to draw a plan. No particular lines, new election date or cancellation of completed elections follows automatically. The unrelated claims' procedural status must remain explicit.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The reviewed material establishes the challenged disparity and the lower court's legal treatment; it does not supply findings that all demographic estimates were exact or that every possible map could equalize both measures. A holding should not invent those findings or transform preclearance into plenary constitutional approval.

[Chen v. City of Houston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.502.98-20440.html).

**Authorities:** Voinovich v. Quilter; United States Department of Commerce v. Montana; Shaw v. Reno; Miller v. Johnson.

**Case Name:** Booth v. Churner.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits decision following argument March 20, 2001.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-29 (historical decision-calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether the Prison Litigation Reform Act requires exhaustion of an available prison grievance process that can take responsive action but cannot award the money damages the prisoner seeks. This is a faithful formulation; exact granted wording has not been independently verified.

**Question Granted:** The question presented above defines the merits issue under review.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit judgment affirming dismissal without prejudice for failure to exhaust.

**Relief Requested:** Booth seeks permission to proceed directly with his damages action; prison officials defend the exhaustion requirement.

**Material Record:** Booth alleged assault and denial of medical attention by Pennsylvania correctional officers. He initially requested injunctive relief and damages, but the dispute on review concerned damages. The grievance system could investigate and respond to misconduct, although it could not award money. Booth initiated a grievance but did not pursue all available review steps. The allegations of force and medical neglect have not been established through a merits trial. [Booth v. Churner](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-731.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The scope of administrative availability is the decisive statutory threshold. The Court need not decide the alleged constitutional wrong or the amount of damages. There is no established claim that the process was inaccessible or incapable of taking any action.

**Relevant Dates:** The PLRA took effect April 26, 1996; this claim is governed by the amended text; event May 29, 2001.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No consolidated companion or participation limitation identified.

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Other prisoner cases do not automatically answer the meaning of available administrative remedies.

### Issue 1: Available administrative remedies under the amended PLRA

**Controlling Simulated Law:** McCarthy v. Madigan preserves its damages-action rule where no statute commands exhaustion; the PLRA subsequently imposed an express exhaustion requirement. The earlier discretion cannot override the amended statute.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Statutory exhaustion differs from judicially created exhaustion. Administrative competence, delay, forfeiture, and relief capacity mattered to the former regime; they do not automatically establish exceptions to the later text.

**Other Governing Law:** Amended Section 1997e(a) requires exhaustion of available administrative remedies before a prisoner brings a covered federal prison-conditions action. Removal of the former adequacy-and-effectiveness qualification matters. Specified screening dismissals without prior exhaustion create no general entitlement to merits adjudication here.

**Material Other Authority:** McCarthy v. Madigan performs work by identifying the prior regime and its express statutory displacement. It does not decide whether a responsive nonmonetary process remains available under the amended language.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The command is established; its application depends on an available process. The amended statute leaves the meaning of availability open rather than adopting a damages-demand exception or a categorical futility rule.

**Open Point:** Whether an accessible grievance process capable of responsive nonmonetary action is available when the prisoner seeks only money damages.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Third Circuit treated the grievance process as available despite its inability to pay damages and required completion before the federal suit.

**Booth:** A remedy should redress the injury through the relief sought. Requiring a purely damages claimant to pursue a forum unable to award damages creates delay without effective compensation and conflicts with the rationale of McCarthy v. Madigan.

**Officials:** The amended statute requires exhaustion of administrative processes, not a match between every requested judicial remedy and agency power. Investigation and correction remain useful, and Congress deliberately removed the former discretionary structure. The United States supported this reading. Both positions must preserve the distinction between an imperfect remedy and a process that cannot provide any responsive action at all.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Merits paths:** Affirm if available refers to an accessible process empowered to respond to the complaint; reverse if the enacted phrase requires power to grant the requested compensation; or remand if actual accessibility or remedial authority is unresolved. The existing record presents the first two legal alternatives without an established obstruction.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An exhaustion holding reaches damages-only claims where the process can respond in another meaningful way. It does not settle a process thwarted by officials, nonexistent review, or an institution with no authority to take any responsive action. Affirmance preserves dismissal without prejudice and leaves the underlying civil-rights merits undecided. No prospective compliance order or retrospective damages determination is appropriate.

**Case authorities:** McCarthy v. Madigan.

# Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-29. **Date status:** historical.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources.

**Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1848; 532 U.S. 598.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a fee denial.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-29 (historical decision-calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether a plaintiff may be a prevailing party for statutory attorney fees when its nonfrivolous lawsuit causes the defendant voluntarily to provide the substantive relief sought, without a merits judgment or court-ordered consent decree. This is a faithful formulation; exact granted wording has not been independently verified.

**Question Granted:** The question presented above defines the merits issue under review.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit affirmance of the district court's refusal to award fees under the Fair Housing Amendments Act and the ADA.

**Relief Requested:** Petitioners seek eligibility under the catalyst theory and remand for the required factual and fee determinations; the State seeks categorical exclusion.

**Material Record:** West Virginia required residents of assisted-living homes to be capable of self-preservation. After the operator received closure orders, the homes and residents sued under federal disability protections. During the litigation the legislature repealed the challenged requirement. The merits action was dismissed as moot, and petitioners requested fees on the ground their suit produced the change. Causation and reasonable amount were not finally established by a judgment awarding fees. [Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-598.pdf).

**Relevant Dates:** Suit filed October 28, 1997; repeal during litigation; event May 29, 2001.

**Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion or participation restriction identified.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.

### Issue 1: Prevailing-party eligibility and causally effective litigation

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Farrar v. Hobby holds that an enforceable nominal judgment conferring a direct benefit establishes prevailing status. Its separate amount rule requires explained assessment of actual success rather than mechanically imposing zero fees or a damages ratio.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Farrar v. Hobby separates statutory eligibility from reasonable amount and requires meaningful assessment of success. The sufficient judicial relief recognized in Farrar v. Hobby is not stated as the exclusive route in every setting.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fair Housing Amendments Act and ADA authorize reasonable attorney fees to a prevailing party. Eligibility must derive from those enactments; actual causation, legal merit, obtained relief, and reasonable amount remain distinct.

**Material Other Authority:** Hewitt v. Helms distinguishes favorable reasoning without relief. Rhodes v. Stewart addresses ineffective declaratory relief. Texas State Teachers Association v. Garland Independent School District separates prevailing status from degree of success. Their specific holdings do not conclusively decide a causally effective suit lacking a decree.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law identifies sufficient and insufficient relief in its prior settings. It does not adopt catalyst eligibility or expressly make a judicial decree indispensable in every case. Neither proposition follows from those decisions.

**Open Point:** Whether a legally substantial lawsuit materially causing the defendant to provide the concrete relief sought establishes prevailing status despite the absence of a merits judgment or consent decree.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Fourth Circuit precedent rejected catalyst fees absent a judicially sanctioned change, and the courts applied that exclusion without resolving the requested factual showing.

**Petitioners:** They obtained the concrete relief sued for because the lawsuit caused the repeal. The term prevailing should include that success when legally grounded and causally proved. Otherwise a defendant may avoid fees by granting relief only after substantial litigation.

**State:** Prevailing party has an established judicial meaning, and a decree provides a reliable alteration of legal relations. Catalyst inquiries require speculative causation proceedings and may reward weak claims or deter voluntary reform. The United States supported the petitioners' broader eligibility reading. The dispute is about statutory success, not a freestanding equitable power to reimburse worthwhile litigation.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Decision paths:** Affirm a decree requirement and deny eligibility; recognize a confined catalyst route and remand; or decide the particular statutes differently if their text and structure justify that distinction. An accepted catalyst rule must require a concrete benefit sought in the suit, a material causal contribution, and a legally substantial claim rather than temporal coincidence or nuisance settlement.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Remand for eligibility facts does not award fees. Reasonableness requires segregation of unrelated work and assessment of actual success under Farrar v. Hobby. A categorical denial resolves only these fee claims; it does not validate the former residency requirement or alter the mootness judgment.

# NLRB v. Kentucky River Community Care, Inc.

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-29. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** NLRB v. Kentucky River Community Care, Inc.

**Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1815.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review of refusal to enforce an unfair-labor-practice bargaining order, May 29, 2001.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-29 (historical decision-calendar date).

**Question Presented:** The petition presents three questions: whether ordinary professional or technical direction of less-skilled employees within employer-prescribed standards necessarily involves independent supervisory judgment; whether the party asserting supervisory status bears the proof burden; and whether the Board reasonably found these six nurses were employees rather than supervisors. These are paraphrases of the verified petition questions, not a verbatim transcription. [Petition for certiorari](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/nlrb-v-kentucky-river-community-care-inc-petition).

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses all three petition questions. The September 26, 2000 grant contains no question limitation. [530 U.S. 1304](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep530/usrep530orders/usrep530orders.pdf).

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit refused enforcement insofar as the bargaining unit included six registered nurses. It rejected both the employer's burden of proving supervisory status and the Board's treatment of professional judgment. The Board requests reversal and enforcement.

**Relief Requested:** The Board seeks reversal and enforcement of its bargaining order. The employer seeks to preserve the refusal of enforcement concerning the six nurses.

**Material Record:** Caney Creek, a Kentucky residential facility serving people with mental illness and intellectual disabilities, employed approximately 110 potentially eligible employees and about a dozen conceded managers or supervisors. In 1997 a union sought a combined professional and nonprofessional unit. The regional director placed the supervisory-exclusion burden on the employer and found it unmet for six nurses. The findings addressed medication and treatment duties, direction of less-skilled staff within prescribed practices, and management availability, including an on-call supervisor when management was off-site. Nurses sometimes described as building supervisors did not acquire statutory status from that title alone. The Board declined review, the union was certified, and the employer refused bargaining to obtain review of the representation ruling. The resulting proceeding did not reopen that record absent newly developed evidence. [Petition and appendices](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/nlrb-v-kentucky-river-community-care-inc-petition).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The petition places both the legal criteria and their application to the six nurses before the Court. The court must review the agency's actual rationale and supported findings. The parties dispute whether the Board distinguished ordinary professional direction under prescribed standards from genuine supervisory independence or categorically exempted all professional judgment. Judicial replacement findings are unavailable; remand is required only if the actual rationale or necessary findings are inadequate.

**Relevant Dates:** Representation proceedings in 1997; appellate judgment in 1999.

**Companion or Related Matters:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America bears directly on the proposed distinction.

**Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier assigned case resolves this statutory supervisory definition. Any intervening effective agency-deference disposition must nevertheless be checked before use.

### Issue 1: Supervisory exclusion and proof

**Controlling Simulated Law:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America permits a functional distinction between skilled patient-care direction and genuine supervisory authority. One enumerated power can suffice; neither hiring and firing authority nor frequent exercise is indispensable. Professional credentials neither establish nor categorically defeat supervisory status.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The professional-direction standard preserves that functional distinction and requires actual supervisory authority. It does not exclude every professional judgment merely because its source is clinical or technical training.

**Other Governing Law:** The National Labor Relations Act excludes supervisors from employee status. Supervisory status requires an enumerated function, exercise in the employer's interest, and independent judgment rather than merely routine or clerical action. Those statutory words do not expressly allocate the disputed burden.

**Material Other Authority:** The agency's representation decision and the Sixth Circuit's review identify the actual classification and burden dispute. Neither establishes a new controlling occupational rule; the direct prior authority is NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America preserves the distinction between ordinary skilled collaboration and genuine supervisory responsibility. It does not automatically make every nurse a supervisor or exempt nurses who possess one qualifying statutory power.

**Open Point:** Whether the Board's present rule applies that functional distinction or instead excludes an entire class of independent professional judgments, and which party must prove supervisory exclusion.

### Issue 2: Agency rationale and the enforcement remedy

**Controlling Simulated Law:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America requires review of the Board's actual rationale. A reviewing court cannot sustain the order by inventing a replacement rationale absent from the agency decision.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Delegation-based review distinguishes discretion entrusted to the Board from a construction contradicting the Act. Supported agency factfinding remains separate from judicial interpretation of the governing legal standard.

**Other Governing Law:** The representation finding becomes reviewable through the refusal-to-bargain unfair-labor-practice proceeding. Enforcement requires a legally adequate agency determination; judicial review does not authorize replacement findings about each nurse's actual authority.

**Material Other Authority:** NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America supplies the relevant existing construction and its rationale-review limitation. The actual regional-director findings, rather than a newly supplied judicial explanation, determine whether this order can stand.

**Question Presented:** Whether 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(e)'s physical-injury requirement continues to bar an action for mental or emotional injury filed during incarceration when the plaintiffs are released before judgment?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses the question presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit, sitting en banc, held that confinement at filing controls the statutory restriction. It affirmed the district court except insofar as that court dismissed with prejudice the monetary claims of six plaintiffs released before judgment; it remanded for dismissal without prejudice of claims barred solely by this provision.

**Relief Requested:** Those plaintiffs seek continuation of their existing action. The officials seek to preserve the filing-date rule and resulting dismissal. [Harris v. Garner](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/216/216.F3d.970.98-8899.html).

Their claims for monetary relief lacked the statutory showing of physical injury. The parties disputed whether a later filing was an amendment or supplement and whether its procedural requirements had been met. The en banc court assumed a proper pleading recording release because it considered that event legally irrelevant to the existing action. This posture establishes neither the truth of every alleged prison abuse nor an entitlement to damages. [Harris v. Garner](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/216/216.F3d.970.98-8899.html).

The accepted question concerns release before judgment and an action commenced during confinement. Whether a separate action filed after release is timely or precluded is not established here. Rule 15 cannot be analyzed without first deciding whether the statute makes filing status or current status controlling.

### Issue 1: Confinement at filing and release before judgment

**Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling precedent resolves whether release before judgment changes the coverage of the enacted physical-injury restriction for an action filed during confinement.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The enacted 1996 physical-injury restriction provides that a federal civil action for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody may not be brought by a confined prisoner without a prior showing of physical injury. The standard distinguishes this restriction on relief from the underlying constitutional merits. It does not establish a temporal exception on release, a universal constitutional-injury valuation rule, or the injury required for every form of relief.

**Other Governing Law:** The statutory definition of prisoner covers the specified persons incarcerated or detained for criminal-law reasons. The temporal question turns on the relationship between that definition and the phrase describing an action brought by a confined prisoner. The historical opinion reproduces the operative provision and analyzes Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(d). Its construction remains the judgment under review, not a simulated Court holding. [Harris v. Garner](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/216/216.F3d.970.98-8899.html).

**Material Other Authority:** The lower majority's commencement-language analysis and the dissent's reliance on supplemental-pleading practice provide the competing constructions. **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing remedies standards require claim-specific attention to injury and lawful relief. They do not permit treating failure of the physical-injury showing as a finding that the officials acted constitutionally.

**Open Point:** Whether release cures the filing-time disability in this provision, including through a supplemental pleading, requires a new temporal construction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The majority construed “brought” as commenced and treated confinement at filing as an enduring historical fact. It considered similar filing-language provisions and Congress's effort to discourage claims filed while the opportunity costs of litigation were low. Release did not change the status at commencement; an amended or supplemental pleading noting release could not change that statutory fact. Its requirement of dismissal without prejudice preserved the possibility of a new post-release action. The dissent accepted that the original filing violated the restriction but argued that ordinary supplemental-pleading practice could cure the disability after release. Requiring dismissal and refiling could duplicate litigation and increase expense. [Harris v. Garner](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/216/216.F3d.970.98-8899.html).

**Prisoners' Strongest Position:** The statute governs prisoners, and released plaintiffs no longer meet that definition. A liberal supplementation rule normally permits later events to cure defects. Nothing expressly commands perpetual dismissal of an action that has become permissible for the same plaintiffs, claims and defendants. When a new suit could be brought immediately, requiring two suits burdens both the parties and the courts without screening the merits of either. The restriction should not be enlarged into a permanent forfeiture.

**Officials' Strongest Position:** The statute identifies the relevant event precisely: bringing the action. Congress could have conditioned continued litigation on current confinement but used commencement language. Post-filing release cannot retroactively supply the required injury or change who brought the suit. The pleading rules regulate procedure and cannot alter the substantive restriction. Dismissal without prejudice addresses permanent-forfeiture concerns without rewriting the temporal trigger.

**Limits on Both Accounts:** The statutory text supplies a stronger basis for a filing-date rule than speculative estimates of which former prisoners will refile. Conversely, administrative convenience cannot alone defeat an enacted filing condition. Neither side's general characterization resolves nominal relief, punitive relief, prospective relief, independent economic injury, or an action newly filed after release.

**Threshold Path:** Confirm that the disputed requests actually seek relief for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody and that the plaintiffs were confined prisoners at commencement. Avoid turning this limited temporal review into a general ruling on every claim in the shakedown litigation.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may adopt the filing-date rule; hold that release permits continuation, subject to appropriate supplementation; or distinguish a statutory commencement defect from other curable procedural omissions. The first two would resolve an open question of statutory construction.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Affirmance preserves the lower court's modification to dismissal without prejudice. Reversal could permit the released plaintiffs' covered claims to proceed on a properly supplemented pleading, while leaving merits and defenses open. Neither disposition entitles the five plaintiffs still confined at district-court judgment to damages, establishes a new injury showing, or decides the constitutional misconduct allegations.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A filing-date holding concerns the action already brought. It need not decide the limitations period, tolling, preclusion, or disposition of a later action. The form and procedural sufficiency of the actual supplemental filing remain disputed; assuming adequacy for the legal question does not find that Rule 15's requirements were satisfied.

### Issue 1: Civil protective orders and the personal arms guarantee

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack recognizes a personal arms right without organized-militia membership, applies it to the States, and protects acquisition necessary to protected keeping; these arms propositions have six direct votes. United States v. Hale unanimously rejects affiliation as a prerequisite and distinguishes constitutional coverage from permissible regulation. United States v. Wright's five-Justice majority establishes Core-Preservation Review. Its first stage determines coverage from text, history, and precedent. Its second stage measures justification against actual practical burden: strict scrutiny for a core deprivation, intermediate scrutiny for a substantial regulation, and reasonableness for an incidental restriction. A general abolition of meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home is independently forbidden. Fraternal Order of Police v. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Love v. Pepersack, United States v. Hale, and United States v. Wright establish personal coverage, incorporation, necessary acquisition, and scrutiny proportionate to the burden. The governmental objective must substantially fit a substantial restriction; describing a prohibition as a safety measure cannot reduce the burden classification. A personal right does not confer immunity on every person or use. Fraternal Order of Police v. * **Other Governing Law:** The Second Amendment and the then-operative federal possession provision. Napier](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/233/233.F3d.394.00-5290.html).
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Miller remains relevant to the distinction between weapon coverage and regulatory justification. The collective-only circuit authorities invoked below cannot override Love v. Pepersack, United States v. Hale, or United States v. Wright. Fraternal Order of Police v. United States is the closest governing comparison, with a criminal-conviction predicate and official-use posture materially different from this case.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law settles personal invocation and the framework for evaluating burdens. It does not settle the constitutional sufficiency of a civil protective-order predicate, the consequences of its less demanding procedural safeguards, or the tier for this application merely by labeling the restriction a status disability.
* **Open Point:** Whether the adjudicated domestic-violence findings and hearing protections justify this possession disability without a criminal conviction, and how its individualized, order-dependent operation fits the established framework.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Sixth Circuit treated the Second Amendment as a collective militia right and found that Napier asserted no interference with that right. It separately rejected his commerce, fair-warning, and state-constitutional objections. The collective premise conflicts with Love v. Pepersack, United States v. Hale, and United States v. Wright; the judgment therefore needs a different adequate basis if sustained. The circuit's other constitutional conclusions remain historical procedural background, and do not establish new controlling precedent. [United States v. Napier](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/233/233.F3d.394.00-5290.html).
* **Napier's Strongest Position:** A total prohibition of both arms and necessary ammunition substantially impairs a personal right. Civil domestic proceedings do not provide criminal proof, counsel, or jury guarantees. Fraternal Order of Police v. United States expressly depended on conviction safeguards and did not decide private possession. A broadly worded order and a prediction that violence may recur should not automatically substitute for demonstrated dangerousness sufficient to suspend a constitutional right. He seeks a rule requiring more than the existence of a qualifying civil order, whether a criminal conviction or a constitutionally adequate individualized necessity finding.
* **United States' Strongest Position:** A criminal conviction is not the only permissible basis for a preventive safety restriction. These orders followed notice and participation and findings of actual domestic violence with a prospect of recurrence. Preventing renewed violence with readily available arms is closely connected to those findings. The disability tracks the operative order rather than imposing a general prohibition on ordinary people. The government seeks a rule permitting this conduct-based civil restraint when meaningful adjudication and the required domestic-danger predicates exist.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limits:** A personal right exists, and the pistol and ammunition cannot be excluded solely for lack of militia connection. The restraint is serious, not incidental paperwork. Conversely, the question does not concern an unadjudicated accusation. Neither side may treat the arrest report as a criminal conviction. No separate amicus position is identified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Decide the preserved Second Amendment challenge on the admitted facts. The plea's limited reservation blocks using this question to reopen the separate evidentiary ruling. No identified jurisdictional obstacle requires dismissal.
* **Merits Paths and Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court could classify a conduct-based, order-dependent restriction following notice and adjudication as substantial and uphold it under intermediate scrutiny, extending existing law from conviction-based official use to the presented civil-order possession application. Such a holding would not dispense with adjudicated facts or statutory qualification. It could instead require stricter justification for a full possession ban and conclude that the existing civil findings are insufficient, invalidating this application without necessarily requiring a conviction in every preventive case. A rule making a criminal conviction categorically indispensable would reach substantially beyond a finding that these safeguards or findings are insufficient and would need independent constitutional support.
* **Judgment and Remedy Consequences:** Affirmance leaves both convictions standing against this objection without endorsing the militia-only rationale. Reversal on the application would require vacating the affected convictions and implementing the conditional plea's lawful consequences. If the legal framework is settled but necessary findings are missing, vacatur and a bounded remand could permit its application; no automatic acquittal or return of weapons follows merely from error in the reasoning below.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The published record does not give the complete hearing transcripts, burden-of-proof instructions, or detailed duration and modification terms. These omissions are not findings that the hearings were defective. A rationale dependent on additional individualized findings requires their verification. The new order-based application must not be represented as already decided by Fraternal Order of Police v. United States.

### Issue 2: An effective mitigation vehicle and sentence correction

**State:** Defense counsel commissioned the earlier examination, and the defense expert relied on it while placing mental condition at issue. Existing law did not dictate a Fifth Amendment exclusion. The psychiatric claim cannot bypass AEDPA by appealing to a preferable new rule. Conversely, statutory deference cannot make express permission to consider evidence equivalent to permission to give it effect. ### Issue 1: Classifying reinstatement-related front pay

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products treats the added compensation provision as supplementing the preexisting Title VII equitable remedies and preserves backpay, interest, and other authorized equitable relief outside the new damages category. Its holding concerns temporal reach, not front pay classification. Kolstad v. American Dental Association separates ordinary liability, punitive eligibility, attribution, submission, award, and amount without deciding front pay.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The statute preserves the enacted separation between existing equitable relief and newly authorized compensatory and punitive damages. The punitive culpability rules remain confined to that category; no controlling decision conclusively classifies front pay under the monetary cap.

**Other Governing Law:** Title VII's equitable-remedy provision authorizes appropriate affirmative action, including reinstatement, backpay, and other equitable relief. The 1991 damages provision excludes backpay, interest, and other relief authorized by that existing provision from its compensatory category, and caps the newly authorized damages by employer size. [Pollard v. E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-843.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** The 1972 expansion of equitable relief and pre-1991 front-pay practice bear on the category Congress preserved. Hudson v. Reno supplied the circuit rule followed below, but is challenged lower-court authority rather than a controlling Supreme Court classification. The Government and EEOC's position is persuasive only to the extent supported by the statutory text and structure.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Monetary form does not determine the source of a remedy. Existing law preserves appropriate equitable employment restoration separately from the new damages categories, but does not establish that every future monetary loss is equitable.

**Open Point:** Whether compensation bridging delayed reinstatement or substituting for inappropriate reinstatement is relief already authorized by the equitable provision, and therefore excluded from the newly capped compensatory damages.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The circuit's controlling precedent put front pay within future pecuniary losses. The reviewing panel considered the contrary statutory argument persuasive but followed that precedent.

**Pollard:** Front pay performs reinstatement's restorative function when immediate reinstatement is delayed or unsuitable. Congress expressly excluded every §706(g) remedy from the added compensatory category. A cap created while expanding relief should not restrict relief already authorized. The distinction should encompass both delayed reinstatement and a payment substituting for reinstatement.

**DuPont:** Front pay compensates future earnings and therefore fits the ordinary meaning of future pecuniary losses in subsection (b)(3). Calling it equitable should not remove a major monetary award from the legislative limit; projecting earnings far into the future may become ordinary compensatory damages.

**Solicitor General:** The United States and the EEOC supported Pollard's exclusion of front pay from the cap, a material view concerning the statutory scheme they administer but not a replacement for judicial interpretation.

**Limits and alternatives:** Pollard must establish appropriate equitable relief, causal employment loss, and a supportable amount with mitigation and avoidance of duplication. DuPont can dispute those matters without treating all future wages as capped damages. An award for emotional injury or punitive deterrence remains in its statutory category even when the plaintiff also seeks front pay.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold path:** Decide the preserved statutory classification without revisiting settled liability or reaching constitutional damages questions.

**Merits paths and plausible scope:** Reverse if §706(g) authorizes front pay as a reinstatement-related remedy and §1981a(b)(2) therefore excludes it from the cap. Such a rule governs interim front pay and front pay in lieu of reinstatement, while preserving factual and equitable limits. Affirm if Congress placed all postjudgment earnings losses within capped compensation. A distinction between delayed and unavailable reinstatement is textually possible only if the equitable authorization supports it; the consequences include different recovery for employees whose workplace is too hostile for a return.

**Judgment and remedy:** Reversal permits an uncapped equitable front-pay determination on remand, not an immediate sum fixed by this Court. Existing damages, backpay, and fees retain their independent status; overlapping loss cannot be recovered twice. Affirmance preserves the cap-based denial. **Uncertainty:** No full evidentiary record about reinstatement feasibility, mitigation, duration, or discounting is supplied. The Court can resolve classification without selecting those facts or announcing a universal front-pay entitlement.
