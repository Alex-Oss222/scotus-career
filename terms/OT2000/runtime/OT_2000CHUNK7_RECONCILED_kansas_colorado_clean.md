## Components, types and dates

**A — Sovereign ownership and immunity.** Kansas sues on its own Compact right and controls recovery. Measuring the State's loss partly by injuries to individual farmers does not turn it into an assigned private-debt action or deprive the Court of jurisdiction. Kansas may determine the use of a recovery; no private entitlement or distribution command is created.

**B — Additional prejudgment interest.** Three amounts must remain distinct: economic losses in historical dollars, an inflation adjustment Colorado concedes is appropriate, and disputed additional interest intended to compensate lost investment opportunities. The report's approximately $9.2 million, $12 million and $41 million amounts illustrate Kansas's claim, not an award or an approved formula. Denial of the additional component is not denial of the conceded inflation correction. Ordinary postjudgment interest is a different issue.

**C — Rate method if additional interest is allowed.** The Master's supported year-specific individual-loss rates measure the economic injury rather than treating the sovereign plaintiff's lower borrowing rate as mandatory. The method must not duplicate inflation already included separately, adopt speculative returns, or presume every farmer would have made a particular investment. No numerical schedule or compounding interval is supplied.

**D — Commencement of the additional component.** Loss-date accrual beginning in 1950, the Master's 1969 notice cutoff, commencement of the initiating request on December 16, 1985, and denial of all additional interest are distinct positions. The date source verifies filing or lodging of the motion, proposed complaint and supporting brief on December 16, 1985. It does not independently date the later grant of leave, and that later event is not the litigation trigger adopted here. No interest from January 1, 1985 is inferred from the shorthand year. The precise administration of the Master's 1969 cutoff remains a calculation matter; no unsupported day is invented. An eligible loss occurring after a chosen cutoff does not acquire interest for a period before it occurred.

**E — Crop-loss evidence.** The specified objections concern actual support for the loss model, not perfection or a permanent future accounting formula. Kansas retains its burden. Colorado's abandonment of an alternative is not affirmative proof. Published crop-specific water/yield studies, acreage and price evidence, the accepted shortage connection and the 25-percent adjustment for ordinary farming stresses supply affirmative support. Capital constraints, credit access, debt aversion and incomplete cost information answer the inference that farmers' noninvestment necessarily disproves profitable use of additional seasonal water. Studies involving additional capital can describe a different transaction from the short-run use being measured.

**F — Remedy.** Return to the Master for calculation and preparation of a judgment under the ultimately adopted component rules. No final amount, private distribution, future water-delivery rule, revised Compact allocation or closing decree is made here.

## Why the uniform loss-date commitment requires revision

The refreshed independent model favored additional interest from supported losses beginning in 1950 for every Associate. It expressly distinguished inflation, recognized all the available commencement alternatives and declined to treat Milwaukee as already controlling interstate relief. Its grounds therefore received an independent assessment on the corrected neutral record; this reconciliation does not simply repair ambiguous terminology.

The historical writings supply materially stronger case-specific evidence than those provisional predictions. The 1949 Compact and the legal expectations surrounding that contract have not changed. The unanimous simulated 1995 Kansas judgment reserved remedy and rejected an established laches bar; it did not promise automatic loss-date interest. The historical Court confronted the same prior rejection of inexcusable Kansas delay. Milwaukee's compensatory principle is still maritime law and supplies an analogy; it does not eliminate the compact-specific inquiry. Texas v. New Mexico supports a money remedy and postjudgment interest without already deciding this additional prejudgment component. West Virginia requires attention to the governmental interests; its compensatory result is not an automatic answer for every old interstate water compact.

The historical majority rejected a categorical bar merely because damages were unliquidated. Funkhouser and the contemporary discretionary authorities meant that prejudgment interest was possible when the Compact was made. But possibility was different from notice of automatic interest from every injury. The state of law in 1949, the lack of awareness of a violation in the early years, the extraordinary interval and the effect of compounding over that interval supported a later commencement. These are the actual grounds of the historical positions, not an unexplained appeal to fairness, an inference of culpable Kansas delay or a newly invented punitive purpose.

O'Connor's contrary opinion, joined by Scalia and Thomas, treated the compact as a contract interpreted against its negotiation-era law. The relevant distinction was between an accepted modern compensatory principle and an extraordinary additional return on unliquidated, difficult-to-ascertain interstate water losses under a bargain made decades earlier. She accepted that interest can be compensatory yet found this contractual exposure outside the parties' reasonably contemplated remedial setting. The uncertainty of damages and the historical remedial background were not generic State immunity or hostility to compensation. Her historical discussion also recited the development of the pleadings, but no unverified date of a monetary-demand amendment or additional pleading premise is needed or adopted in this reconciliation.

No changed legal, factual, procedural or remedial premise justifies carrying the eight provisional 1950 votes past that evidence. The neutral type correction sharpens the disagreement by preserving inflation even for the three opposing additional return; it does not itself dictate an outcome. The date correction makes the litigation alternative precise without compelling its selection. Each affected vote is therefore corrected below.

## Component coalitions and conditional accommodation

All eight Associates overrule the sovereign-ownership/immunity exception and the specified crop-model exceptions, and support return to the Master within their respective substantive rules. All preserve the conceded appropriate inflation adjustment. The historical-dollar principal and that correction must not be conflated with the additional return on which they disagree.

Five Associates — Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer — reject the proposed categorical bar based on unliquidated damages and allow some additional prejudgment interest. They can join the common statement that damages' uncertainty alone does not exclude compensatory interest, with the actual compact equities controlling its extent. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas deny that additional component on the narrower contractual-expectations analysis. Their vote does not declare every unliquidated interest award unavailable.

Those same five support rejection of a mandatory State-borrowing-rate substitution and the year-specific individual-loss approach within a lawful additional-interest award. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not presently join that award's rate holding because their sufficient ground denies the component. Their agreement that Kansas owns the underlying claim is not a rate vote. Their frozen provisional support for the method can explain compatibility with a genuinely separate conditional accounting proposition if one is proposed; it cannot substitute for their actual decision whether to join such a passage. No eight-Justice additional-interest-rate holding is frozen.

On D, **Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer prefer 1969; Kennedy prefers the December 16, 1985 initiating request; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas prefer no additional interest.** No Associate retains 1950 as a first choice. Four votes for 1969 are not a Court majority, and Kennedy's filing preference is not five votes merely because four historically accommodated it. Each of the four recognizes filing as a reasoned lesser alternative capable of producing an interest judgment if the preferred 1969 rule lacks a majority. Whether that contingency actually arises depends on the ninth Justice and the actual proposed judgment; assembly must record it rather than assume it.

The periods and rationales must also remain distinct. A Justice opposing all additional interest does not thereby join an affirmative opinion authorizing it after filing. Agreement in result for an earlier period supplies no automatic common rationale for a later period. A Justice accepting filing only as an accommodation need not represent it as the independently best equitable date. No logical-subset or historical-count shortcut resolves this topology.

## Remedy, source limits and departure accounting

The Master must calculate only the components and periods the final judgment authorizes, maintaining historical principal, conceded inflation and any additional return separately enough to prevent duplicate recovery. An individual-loss rate is a method of measuring the State's injury, not a distribution order or a finding that every farmer incurred a specified loan. The Court need not select an annual table, numerical total, compounding frequency or unprovided inflation subtraction formula in this event. No new evidence, culpable-delay finding, automatic investment success or permanent future calculation rule is reconstructed.

The precise December 16 initiating date replaces the earlier uncertainty only for that event. The year 1986 in the procedural account concerns a different procedural stage and is not silently substituted for initiation. Neither the catalogue's amended-complaint label nor the historical dissent supplies a new, independently verified monetary-demand date in this handoff. The historical shorthand of beginning in 1985 is implemented, if selected, as the supported filing trigger rather than unsupported extra months.

There is **no retained substantive historical departure** at the stated individual first-choice and conditional scopes. The eight loss-date predictions are corrected because the historical case-specific reasoning fits the unchanged legal and factual setting. The historically reported final aggregate date is deliberately not frozen as the present Court's date. That is the required separation between evidence of individual judicial choices and an assembled judgment, not a new historical departure.

The material neutral type omission was corrected through a fresh independent model before this reconciliation. The refreshed model retained its provisional preferences; historical comparison then supplies the reasons for revision stated Justice by Justice above. No source gap creates a litigant default. Assembly receives the complete first-choice and conditional commitments and may test an actual proposed opinion within them; it may not flatten the dates, types or rate nonjoins merely to produce a convenient common outcome.
