# Texas v. Cobb

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals' judgment reversing the capital-murder conviction and remanding for a new trial, on the narrower ground that the murder interrogation concerned the same criminal episode already under prosecution and representation.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The challenged police-elicited confession may not be used in the new prosecution on the asserted ground that the murders were outside the attached counsel protection. Preserve ordinary adjudication of independent evidence and the State's ability to retry; this is neither acquittal nor a bar to investigation through lawful means.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Offense specificity with an anti-circumvention boundary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the disputed scope of the attached right. A valid Miranda waiver answers the Fifth Amendment question but does not, without more, answer the attached Sixth Amendment protection and its existing waiver rules. The State's categorical scope argument cannot avoid that distinction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether appointed counsel for the charged burglary protects the accused against uncounseled police interrogation about murders constituting an inseparable part of that same known episode, although the statutory elements differ.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Moulton's protection against circumvention to deliberately elicited admissions about an inseparable part of the represented episode. Preserve McNeil's offense specificity for unrelated investigations, but reject a new rule importing Blockburger as the exclusive counsel boundary. This same-episode protection is a proposed doctrinal development, not a claim that Moulton already decided it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Once prosecution and representation have begun, the counsel protection extends to police questioning about additional offenses so integrated with the charged occurrence that interrogation necessarily reconstructs that same represented event. Different statutory elements alone do not remove that protection. The murders during this burglary satisfy the rule, so the confession is not admissible on the State's different-offense theory.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the same known criminal episode and preserves investigation of genuinely separate offenses. Shared participants, a similar method, or a general criminal relationship alone is insufficient. Ordinary attachment and Sixth Amendment waiver rules remain applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Sixth Amendment protects assistance in the criminal prosecution, not merely advice about the first label chosen by the prosecutor. Its defense function carries decisive weight in defining the doctrinal term offense. Blockburger serves the different double-jeopardy inquiry into legislatively distinct offenses; transferring that test would let sequencing of closely integrated charges dismantle representation. McNeil involved a distinct investigation and does not compel that result. Moulton establishes why the State cannot bypass counsel by describing the interrogation as another investigation, though extending protection to the integrated additional charge must be acknowledged. Here the inquiry into the deaths necessarily returned to what happened during the charged burglary, and the earlier requests for counsel's permission confirm the relationship was apparent. The rule is bounded by the represented occurrence rather than by sympathy for the accused or every factual association between crimes. I preserve Michigan v. Jackson's applicable protection instead of treating Miranda compliance as its repeal.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unrelated crimes, a defendant-initiated encounter, and a genuinely valid Sixth Amendment waiver present different circumstances. No general prohibition on speaking to represented persons as witnesses is adopted.
