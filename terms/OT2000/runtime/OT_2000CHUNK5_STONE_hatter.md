# OT_2000CHUNK5

# United States v. Hatter

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-21. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit judgment insofar as it rejects the discriminatory Social Security burden on the covered incumbent judges; reverse insofar as it establishes a categorical Medicare exemption; remand for proceedings consistent with those rulings.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The judges may recover timely, proven unconstitutional Social Security deductions, subject to claim-specific limitations and computation. Generally applicable Medicare liability remains valid. A general salary increase does not cleanse an unlawful deduction; no numerical award is entered here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Compensation protected against discriminatory tax diminution

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the separate Medicare and Social Security classifications because their constitutional validity determines liability. Accept the established statutory coverage and offset structure without finding subjective congressional hostility.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which of these taxes impermissibly diminish the compensation of incumbent Article III judges?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Jefferson County v. Acker's operation-based analysis while resolving the constitutional question it reserved. Overrule Evans v. Gore to the extent it categorically bars generally shared taxes enacted after appointment. Replace that proposition with protection against discriminatory diminution while retaining protection against disguised salary reductions.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ordinary nondiscriminatory taxes remain payable by judges. Congress may not accomplish a prohibited diminution by materially singling out incumbent judges through tax or pension classifications. Medicare falls in the former category; the Social Security transition's offset structure places these judges in the latter. This resolves Acker’s reserved constitutional question and overrules Evans’s contrary categorical tax immunity to that extent.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers incumbent Article III judges subjected to these tax extensions and protects against materially equivalent disguised reductions. It does not exempt judges from common tax obligations or require subjective legislative animus.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Judicial independence warrants an effective anti-circumvention rule, not insulation from common obligations. Existing employees' opt-outs and contribution offsets define the comparison. The noncontributory judicial pension creates an unmatched new burden under the Social Security transition; Medicare's broader extension does not single out the office. Constitutional structure and the actual tax classifications work together, with protection of judicial independence carrying decisive weight. Evans's categorical exemption confuses a general civic burden with a diminution directed at the judicial office; O'Malley's acceptance of common taxation exposes that rule's overbreadth. The replacement protects the institutional interest without a personal tax immunity. Reliance on stipulated compensation and protection against selective reduction remains protected, while an expectation of permanent exemption from generally applicable taxes does not justify retaining Evans's broader rule.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Newly appointed judges and materially different pension classifications are outside the questions presented on this record.

### Issue 2: Present review and the remedy for accrued deductions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Determine the statutory effect of the no-quorum disposition before constitutional review. Enforce the earlier judgment within its actual mandate, but reject treating the absence of a quorum as an adjudicated constitutional rule foreclosing the present review.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the earlier statutory affirmance bars the present legal determination, and whether later general salary increases eliminate liability for unlawful withholding.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the no-quorum statute and existing judgment-scope principles to permit present review without erasing the earlier judgment. Resolve the remedial question by keeping accrued lawful compensation distinct from later statutory increases.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The earlier no-quorum action does not supply a conclusive merits determination of the constitutional question now properly before the Court. A later general salary increase does not cure unlawful deductions from compensation otherwise owed. Liability and recoverable periods must be adjudicated separately.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The disposition reaches the prior no-quorum objection and the asserted 1984-raise defense in these compensation claims. It preserves ordinary limitations rules and requires legally matched treatment of any genuine offset rather than an undifferentiated net-pay comparison.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A statutory affirmance issued because the Court could not act does not establish a constitutional rationale it never considered. An increase determines the lawful salary thereafter due; it does not retrospectively authorize prior withholding or permit deduction from the new entitlement. Recoverable amounts still depend on each claimant's timely claim. The earlier disposition retains the same case-specific force as the governing no-quorum statute gives it; the absence of precedential reasoning cannot be used to reopen whatever that judgment actually settled. The present claims and asserted later salary offset must therefore be considered within the surviving mandate.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Claim-specific limitations periods and arithmetic require facts and calculations not conclusively supplied here; those determinations remain for remand.
