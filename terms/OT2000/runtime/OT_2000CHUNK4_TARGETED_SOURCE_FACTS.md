## Egelhoff: benefits and the recovery actions

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-1529.ZO.html ; Part I factual recitation. Saved full source: sources/chunk4/Egelhoff-ZO.txt.

Petitioner Donna Rae Egelhoff was married to David A. Egelhoff.  Mr. Egelhoff was employed by the Boeing Company, which provided him with a life insurance policy and a pension plan.  Both plans were governed by ERISA, and Mr. Egelhoff designated his wife as the beneficiary under both.  In April 1994, the Egelhoffs divorced.  Just over two months later, Mr. Egelhoff died intestate following an automobile accident.  At that time, Mrs. Egelhoff remained the listed beneficiary under both the life insurance policy and the pension plan.  The life insurance proceeds, totaling $46,000, were paid to her.



    Respondents Samantha and David Egelhoff, Mr. Egelhoff’s children by a previous marriage, are his statutory heirs under state law.  They sued petitioner in Washington state court to recover the life insurance proceeds.

In a separate action, respondents also sued to recover the pension plan benefits.  Respondents again argued that the Washington statute disqualified Mrs. Egelhoff as a beneficiary and they were thus entitled to the benefits under the plan.

Source limit: the text establishes that the life-insurance proceeds had been paid to the former spouse before the recovery action. It describes a separate action to recover pension benefits but does not expressly say whether those benefits had been paid. Do not infer an unpaid-pension finding from that silence. The children are not both established as adults; the caption expressly identifies Samantha as a minor.
