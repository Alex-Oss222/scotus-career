## Changed law and the two supported paths

**F1, all eight:** Simulated Agostini modifies Lemon into a single inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Purpose, effects, and institutional relationships are evidence; independent purpose, endorsement, and entanglement prongs do not survive. The Court must identify the governmental act and ask whether it performs a prohibited religious function or fits a preserved permissible relationship. The required teacher message is the board's speech, but attribution alone does not establish a violation. Academic religious study, private expression, and genuine nondevotional conscience protection remain lawful. Once a prohibited function is shown, successful persuasion or a completed penalty is unnecessary.

This is a concrete changed legal premise relative to the lower-court comparator. The panel's independent effect/endorsement reasoning cannot be adopted as governing law simply because five Associates reach the same injunction result. Conversely, the changed method does not itself require reversal: official curricular protection of a favored religious account may satisfy the governing prohibition on public religious activity. Epperson and Edwards retain their curriculum boundaries within the current method; they neither immunize curricular choices nor forbid every biblical mention.

**F2, five Associates:** The mandatory whole statement, its selective placement immediately before evolution instruction, and the supported adoption context establish an official function of protecting the biblical creation account from the implications of public scientific instruction. The maintained biblical clause after the creation-science controversy is evidence of that function together with the operative text, not a dispositive count of religious motives. The OR and reference to other concepts preserve personal choice but do not eliminate the board's own religious-protection task. Government may protect conscience without making defense of that religious account part of its science presentation. No finding of prayer, religious assent, student conversion, or punishment is necessary or made.

The strongest objection is that evolution remains taught, no doctrinal truth is prescribed, students may form independent beliefs, and the reassurance reaches other concepts. The five answer that the constitutional problem is the school's official protective activity identified in the complete text and context; lack of compelled belief does not resolve that question. Their rule does not treat a biblical word alone, offense, religious motivation, or all conscience notices as sufficient.

**F3, three Associates:** The complete statement explains that learning the scientific account does not compel a student's personal belief. The actual OR, the reference to other concepts, and the invitation to critical examination do not prescribe a religious conclusion. The speaker is official, but the function is noncompulsion reassurance, not doctrinal adoption, religious instruction, or solicitation. The selectively retained biblical example and adoption history are substantial contrary evidence, but do not transform the operative reassurance into that forbidden function in these Justices' view. Their rule does not authorize compelled creation teaching, a parental curriculum veto, religious grading criteria, or immunity whenever legal coercion is absent.

**F4, all eight:** Judge the complete adopted resolution. Its introductory paragraph directs teachers; the following two paragraphs are spoken to pupils. The actual OR governs. The first spoken paragraph standing alone was not adjudicated below and is not separately decided here. No severability finding, rewritten disclaimer, replacement curriculum, private forum entitlement, or evidentiary remand to seek proof of conversion is supported. The verified question concerns constitutionality, not fee calculation. Affirmance leaves the existing fee judgment undisturbed; reversal leaves dependent consequences to ordinary proceedings without setting an amount or fee rule.
