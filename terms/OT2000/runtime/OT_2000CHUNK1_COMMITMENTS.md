## Artuz v. Bennett — November 7, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight affirm the Second Circuit on the granted filing-condition question and permit further habeas proceedings under its remand. No writ, definitive tolling duration, finding of receipt or nonreceipt of a written order, or final timeliness determination follows.

**Rule and ground:** A state collateral application is properly filed for section 2244(d)(2) when its delivery and acceptance satisfy the rules governing filing the application. New York Criminal Procedure Law sections 440.10(2)(a) and (c), as supplied here, bar relief on previously resolved claims or claims unjustifiably omitted from direct review; they govern disposition of claims rather than the filing act. Their presence in an application therefore does not make that application improperly filed. Clerk acceptance alone is not a universal sufficient condition if an applicable filing requirement was violated.

The statute distinguishes the application whose filing and pendency matter from the claims on which relief may be denied. Treating any mandatory claim bar as a filing defect would collapse those separate inquiries without textual support. The State's concern about repeated futile collateral litigation does not permit adding a relief-eligibility condition to the enacted filing requirement. The construction does not pronounce every fee, place, timing, jurisdictional, or authorization rule a mere merits bar; those issues require their actual terms and are outside this grant.

## Cleveland v. United States — November 7, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight reject both asserted statutory routes for treating the charged acquisition and renewals of these video-poker licenses as mail fraud. Reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the two mail-fraud convictions on this theory and remand for the necessary consequences to other convictions actually dependent on those predicates. Do not vacate unaffected convictions by category.

### Independently addressed statutory components

1. **The State's licensing interest.** Louisiana's authority to issue, withhold, renew, regulate, and revoke these licenses is sovereign regulatory authority, not a proprietary asset held by the State and obtained from it through the charged deception. The license's value to an operator, the State's exclusion power, and the State's interest in fees and gaming revenue do not transform that regulatory authority into the statutory property that this prosecution alleged was taken. McNally retains the money-or-property boundary; Carpenter establishes that genuine intangible property qualifies, not that every valuable governmental decision is property.

2. **The acquisition clause.** Section 1341's language addressing acquisition by false pretenses describes a means of property fraud within the statute as a whole. It does not create an independent offense whenever a successful applicant gains something valuable in its own hands, even though the deceived issuer lost no property. The government's disjunctive-text argument and omission of an express requirement that the object come from another are substantial textual points, but severing this clause from McNally's statute-wide property construction would erase the very boundary McNally recognized whenever governmental approval creates private economic value. The clause's separate enactment does not establish that Congress authorized that result. This holding does not require proof of completed economic loss or actual reliance, which Neder does not require.

3. **Renewals.** Issuance may create a valuable interest in the licensee's hands. It does not thereby make the State a co-owner of that interest. Continued regulatory control and revenue rights do not transform the charged renewals into an acquisition of State property. A renewal obtained through deception therefore does not cure the absent State-property object under the theory charged here. An actual scheme to take government money, evade payments owed, or acquire a genuine government commercial asset presents a different object and is not decided.

4. **Consequences and reach.** Materiality cannot replace the omitted property element. The Court does not review the separately ungranted materiality issue or decide every government franchise, private license interest, or honest-services theory. The case is returned for count-specific treatment of any racketeering or laundering convictions actually dependent on the invalid mail-fraud predicates and of surviving independent bases. No new holding on tax liability, laundering elements, or racketeering requirements is adopted.

## Sinkfield v. Kelley — November 27, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight vacate the three-judge court's injunction and remand with instructions to dismiss the asserted claims for lack of Article III standing. This is a jurisdictional disposition, not judgment against the plaintiffs on the design-only merits theory.

**Rule and application:** A voter must establish personal exposure to the challenged electoral assignment, causation, and redressability. Residence in a majority-white district adjoining a majority-minority district, together with a statewide objective to maximize majority-minority districts, does not by itself establish that the particular voter was personally assigned by the challenged racial treatment. Unusual district shapes can support an inference when linked to the plaintiff's own treatment; they are not a substitute for that link. The supplied record identifies no allegation or evidence establishing such individual treatment beyond effects attributed to neighboring district design. It identifies no independent exclusion, dilution, unequal voting weight, or legally inferior membership. The plaintiffs therefore have not established a personal injury supporting this injunction.

Johnson v. Miller's unanimous threshold holding separates personal exposure from success on the merits and rejects residence as an automatic standing rule. Its unanimous shape-evidence holding likewise makes geography evidence rather than conclusive proof. Steel Co.'s jurisdictional principle and Ruhrgas's bounded nonmerits sequencing do not permit assuming Article III injury to enter a contested merits judgment. Sections 1253 and 2284 authorize review of the three-judge court's merits injunction; the reviewing Court's disposition does not turn that injunction into a nonmerits lower order.

## Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College — November 27, 2000

### Proposed disposition and sufficient ground

All eight Associates affirm dismissal of the individual-capacity damages action on qualified immunity, reserving whether punishment for mistakenly attributed protected speech violates the targeted employee's own First Amendment right. The Ninth Circuit's no-actual-speech rationale is not adopted. The complaint identifies individual officials, and the pleaded period in which the disciplinary notice could be revived expired October 28, 2000. Reinstatement does not eliminate the past damages controversy, but no continuing prospective threat or new municipal claim is supplied.

The particular right was not clearly established when the officials acted in January 1997. Pickering and Connick identify protected employee expression and permissible employment interests; Mt. Healthy separates protected conduct, substantial motivation, and a same-decision defense. Waters established limited propositions about a mistaken account of speech actually made, with materially different rationales and unresolved boundaries. It did not settle mistaken speaker identity. McIntyre protects actual anonymous advocacy against a specified identification demand; it does not answer this employment-liability extension. Harlow and Anderson therefore protect these defendants from damages without deciding that their alleged conduct was proper. Elder requires examination of all relevant conduct-date legal authority, not a mandatory merits-first decision. Crawford-El supplies the present procedural permission to resolve an adequate legal defense first, but its June 1997 decision cannot establish January 1997 notice.

## City of Indianapolis v. Edmond — November 28, 2000

### Proposed disposition by coalition

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the Seventh Circuit's prospective preliminary-injunction disposition. They adopt the rule that a checkpoint whose primary program purpose is ordinary narcotics investigation cannot justify the seizure of motorists without individualized suspicion merely because the stops are brief, systematic, and accompanied by license or sobriety checks. Scalia and Thomas would reverse the constitutional ruling on the described mixed-function program: its systematic license and sobriety checks provide an independently legitimate roadway basis, and the additional drug objective and exterior sniff do not invalidate a stop whose objective operation remains within that lawful basis. These are distinct rationale commitments, not a blended balancing test.

### Proposition scope for the six-Justice position

1. The vehicle stop is a seizure before the exterior dog sniff occurs. Whether that sniff independently counts as a search does not supply authority for the preceding stop.
2. A primary program purpose of obtaining evidence of ordinary crime requires a lawful exception beyond generalized enforcement benefits and a minimally intrusive method. No such special need or emergency is shown here. Program purpose concerns the category of authority invoked, not an individual officer's hidden motive where probable cause already authorizes the stop.
3. Sitz's immediate highway-safety program and Martinez-Fuerte's border-control context remain available within their own predicates. Prouse does not authorize suspicionless stops for every law-enforcement purpose merely because selection is systematic. Whren's objective probable-cause rule and its purpose-limited administrative exceptions both remain intact. Chandler confirms that reduced intrusion cannot replace an adequate actual need; it does not supply a universal checklist.
4. Ancillary roadway functions cannot sustain a program whose admitted principal object is drug detection. The holding does not prohibit lawful observation or action on evidence encountered during a genuinely justified checkpoint, nor determine emergency roadblocks, imminent threats, border authority, searches based on individualized suspicion, suppression, or damages.

## 4. Federal components kept distinct

### Article II

McPherson establishes the constitutional setting of legislative direction for appointing presidential electors. Smiley recognizes the relevance of a State's lawmaking process in its different congressional-election setting. Neither supplies a ready answer that every state judicial construction is forbidden or that every state constitutional restriction necessarily governs the legislature's Article II function. Ordinary statutory interpretation is not eliminated merely because Article II uses the term legislature. Whether the particular judgment displaced a legislative instruction is the actual dispute, and its resolution requires the state court's own account of the remaining materially ambiguous grounds.

The Court therefore reaches no final Article II merits holding at this stage. It does not use a generalized preference for deference to dismiss the federal question, nor an abstract legislative-power theory to presume that the state court has already overridden legislative law. The remand addresses the antecedent uncertainty necessary to that merits determination.

### Section 5

Then-effective section 5 gives conclusive treatment in the federal electoral count to a qualifying final determination made under pre-election laws by the date six days before electors meet. The supplied dates are December 12 and December 18. The benefit's conditions are distinct from an affirmative federal prohibition on all counting after December 12. An asserted independent cutoff must have its own legal source.

Foster's unanimous holding regulates premature final congressional selection under the congressional election-day statutes. It expressly leaves presidential timing outside its holding and cannot convert section 5 into a universal stopping rule. The remand may seek the state court's actual understanding of federal timing without declaring that the legislature selected December 12 as an absolute state-law end date, that safe-harbor qualification is guaranteed, or that every post-election judicial construction forfeits the benefit. These are not conclusions established by the state opinion's generic citation to federal election statutes.

### Due process

The due-process question remains in the case and must not disappear into the Article II discussion. Every participating Associate joined Yoshina's rule that a neutral authoritative construction of an ambiguous election provision does not become fundamentally unfair merely because an official previously advised otherwise. The actual guidance, dissemination, inducement, reliance, and voting circumstances matter to a supported fairness claim; those subjects are not a new rigid element list.

The source here establishes post-election conflicting advice to officials and subsequent judicial construction. It does not establish a particular pre-election assurance to voters or detrimental reliance. That source limitation proves neither that no such contention was raised elsewhere nor that a litigant failed to produce evidence in Court. A factual finding of deceptive inducement or unfair reliance therefore cannot be invented. The distinct legal contention that the court newly displaced a clear election rule rather than construed ambiguity depends in part on the unresolved legal basis of the judgment. Clarification is relevant to that contention as well as Article II.

This remand does not reject the entire due-process question merely because individualized reliance facts are absent from the supplied source. It also does not hold that a change in an advisory interpretation, standing alone, is sufficient for federal relief. The governing Yoshina distinction is preserved for any eventual merits determination. No affirmative factual reconstruction or new record-production duty is imposed.

## Operative record and enacted qualifications

The contribution limit is per election and applies to the relevant individual and group contributions to legislative candidates. The individual's own and spouse's contributions retain the section 1015(1) exception. That exception does not displace the independent restrictions accepted under section 1125 by a certified public-funding participant. The constitutional question concerns private contributions to legislative candidates, not an independent facial review of the governor's different ceiling, every reporting requirement, or a distinct unsupported party-specific burden. The publicly funded alternative does not excuse an invalid restriction on people choosing private financing.

The participating legislative candidate files a declaration, obtains the necessary ballot qualification and compliant seed financing, and demonstrates support through qualifying contributions from at least 150 registered district voters for a Senate race or 50 for a House race. A qualifying contribution is $5 payable to the fund by check or money order during the applicable qualifying period, with the candidate's knowledge and approval and a receipt identifying the donor; no payment or other value may be exchanged for it. Seed contributions are no more than $100 per individual, including candidate or family funds, with aggregate legislative amounts of $1,500 for Senate and $500 for House subject to the Commission's rule authority. Unspent seed money transfers to the fund upon certification. Ordinary private contributions are prohibited after certification unless the Commission specifically authorizes them.

The initial contested-election grant uses the average expenditures in the corresponding office's contested races in the prior two applicable elections, with the initial 2000 computation reduced by 25 percent. Insufficient data permits use of the most recent applicable elections. An uncontested primary uses the corresponding uncontested-race average or contested-race average if lower; no distribution is made for an uncontested general election. These qualification and amount rules describe the presented public option; their unchallenged procedural details receive no separate constitutional ruling. The public program is legally optional, but actual suppression of a meaningful private alternative remains constitutionally reviewable.

The enacted matching trigger looks to the greater of campaign expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, alone or with the independent expenditures reported under section 1019. When that reported measure exceeds the applicable initial distribution, an opposing certified candidate receives the reported excess. Supplements are capped at twice the original distribution under the relevant contested-primary or contested-general provision, so the ordinary initial-plus-matching total cannot exceed three times that distribution. Mere shorthand about a receipts-only trigger would omit enacted alternatives and is not adopted.

The regulation described in the lower opinion deducts independent expenditures expressly advocating election of the certified participant or defeat of the private opponent from matching disbursements after the private opponent has exceeded the initial distribution. It does not reduce the initial grant. The Court does not infer a formula for unpresented multiple-opponent or timing scenarios from this description. Nor does matching an independently made expenditure transform it into a coordinated contribution or attribute the speaker's message to a candidate.

Section 1125(13) also forbids spending more public money than the fund contains. If the Commission determines that revenue is insufficient for the applicable initial or matching distributions, it may authorize private contributions and expenditures by certified candidates up to the applicable funding amounts, reduced by seed money, and subject to the stated per-donor-per-election ceilings and Commission rules. This is conditional permission, not an automatic or unlimited release. No actual shortage is assumed. The existence of this exception prevents describing the candidate's surrender of private fundraising as absolute under every circumstance; it does not establish a defect in the ordinary program now challenged.

## I. Contribution limit: rule, application, and competing conclusions

All eight accept the governing rule they adopted in Russell Part I: a direct candidate-contribution limit requires an objectively grounded sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest and close fit preserving meaningful political association and effective advocacy. Completed bribery is unnecessary; equality of political influence, ordinary policy agreement, and ordinary access alone are insufficient. Campaign scale, source channel, actual fundraising burdens, per-election operation, and cumulative restraints matter. Neither Buckley's nominal figure nor its inflation-adjusted equivalent supplies a constitutional floor. Shrink Missouri applies, rather than replaces, that standard.

**The five sustaining the limit.** Maine's evidence connects potentially dominant contributions in small legislative campaigns to pressure for official accommodation. The reported effort to obtain a legislator's change of position through threatened withdrawal supplies more than a generic dislike of wealthy speakers; the public concern evidence corroborates the risk but does not give referendum majorities authority to settle constitutional rights. The modest scale of the legislative campaigns, actual fundraising achieved under the new ceiling, per-election operation, and continued usable private channels together justify this restriction on direct candidate transfers. This is the State's affirmative showing, not a requirement that challengers prove it impossible to campaign.

The opposing burden evidence is serious. Retrospective calculations project significant losses, particularly for Senate challengers and competitive races, and the special election differs from an ordinary election in competition for donors. The five therefore do not treat one successful campaign, average expenses, or the percentage of donors below the ceiling as conclusive. Donor percentages do not measure funds lost. The historical-receipt projections, however, freeze the donor base and donation pattern; they establish a risk of impaired fundraising, not a finding that the new ceiling will cause every projected loss or preclude workable recruitment in these relatively small districts. The State's combined evidence of campaign scale, actual capacity, and direct-transfer risk meets the required fit on this presented challenge despite that contrary evidence. Materially different suppressive applications remain reviewable.

**The three invalidating the limit.** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept that the influence evidence justifies some prophylaxis, but find the State's answer to the substantial projected fundraising loss inadequate at this amount. Their Russell and Shrink Missouri separate grounds require a practical answer to the actual burden; no static inflation floor, proof of completed bribe, or expenditure-level scrutiny is substituted. A special election free of the usual competition for donors and average spending figures do not adequately establish capacity in affected competitive races. The possibility of recruiting more contributors is relevant, but cannot become an unsupported finding that the affected campaigns can replace large shares of their funding. The protected association at issue is support for the chosen candidate; public participation, self-financing, or spending through other speakers does not by itself answer the loss of that route.

The strongest objection to their position is that the same reasoning risks invalidating any limit on the basis of inevitable static loss calculations. They answer by relying on the size and concentration of the projected burdens and the inadequacy of the supplied operational answer, not the mere existence of some affected donations. The five's strongest reply is that these forecasts are especially unreliable where the prior limit allowed a few donors to fund a substantial fraction of an inexpensive campaign and the actual new-limit experience shows practicable fundraising. The disagreement concerns the sufficiency of this record under the shared test; neither side invents a conclusive causal finding.

## II. Responsive grants: different proposition coalitions

**The four sustaining the mechanism.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find no constitutionally sufficient burden established merely because private fundraising or protected independent advocacy causes a capped additional payment supporting an opponent's speech. Private speakers remain free to choose their expenditure and fundraising levels and communicate their own messages. The State does not transfer their money, compel their endorsement, attribute the participant's views to them, or impose an expenditure ceiling on them. The mechanism supplies public resources to a participant who otherwise accepted constrained financing, directs limited funds toward an actual contested campaign, caps that response, and adjusts later matching payments for specified independent spending already favoring the participant. Those concrete features support the public-funding connection; they are not treated as magic labels.

The strongest objection is that the government attaches an adverse consequence directly to the exercise of protected activity, predictably encouraging a speaker to remain below the trigger. The four distinguish that competitive effect from a legal restraint or established substantial suppression of the private alternative. Reform Party denies a constitutional right to a particular funding parity or insulation from a rival's public resources; Buckley permits a voluntary financed option with conditions on the participant. Those principles do not make every attractive subsidy valid, but they support this particular bounded response. Day's five-Justice judgment remains effective for its own mechanism; its four-Justice expressive-burden rationale is not controlling law, and no combination of nonidentical opinions supplies a broader rule. The present caps, qualification, financing obligations and adjustment are evaluated directly. The four do not adopt a categorical rule that additional public speech can never burden protected activity.

**The four invalidating the mechanism.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain the expressive-burden ground they joined in Day. The law makes an opponent's extra public grant turn on private candidates' protected fundraising or independent speakers' advocacy. That state-created consequence differs from an opponent's ordinary voluntary response; it imposes a reason to refrain from speech or fundraising before the cap is reached. The cap limits the duration and amount of the burden but does not eliminate it. The regulatory offset improves fit to the campaign's actual resources, yet does not remove the adverse legal trigger imposed on the initiating speaker.

Maine's strongest answer is that a public participant gives up ordinary fundraising, and a limited response protects the viability of that option without burdening the private speaker's own resources. The four accept the legitimacy of voluntary initial grants but conclude that making the program attractive cannot justify attaching this consequence to outsiders' protected activity. An interest in countering the private opponent's financial advantage is not an independent sufficient reason to burden that expression. Preventing dependence on private contributions may support the participant's funding conditions, but the State has not shown why a grant determined by its opponent's protected activity is required for that interest. This conclusion does not invalidate ordinary grants, every public-finance condition, or every adjustment unrelated to the exercise of another speaker's rights.

The candidate-financing and independent-expenditure triggers are separately covered by these commitments. No opinion may silently validate one while invalidating the other on the assumption that contributions and expenditures have identical protection. The four's present objection is the legal consequence attached to protected fundraising or spending, not a wholesale rejection of the direct-contribution distinction governing Issue I. The four sustaining the statute likewise do not convert independent expenditures into candidate contributions.

## III. Combined operation and remedy

The five addressing the additional systemwide theory conclude that the record does not establish that the initial funding option and its participation obligations, combined with the independently analyzed contribution rules, eliminate meaningful private campaigning. The grant carries qualification, spending, and private-funding restrictions; private candidates retain their own fundraising and spending route. The incentive to take public money is not enough. This conclusion rests on the actual program and operational record, including the funding cap and conditional shortage rule, not merely the word voluntary.

O'Connor's join is limited: she rejects an additional basis for invalidating the entire option while maintaining her conclusion that the responsive trigger independently burdens protected activity. A statute's overall optional form cannot cure that invalid feature. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer sustain the three presented components together. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remove both independent restraints and need not decide whether an additional whole-system coercion holding would follow if those restraints were valid. They do not propose eliminating all public grants or forcing existing participants into an invented alternative system.

If component invalidation becomes the Court's judgment, 1 MRSA section 71(8), as amended in 1991, governs severability: unaffected provisions and applications survive when they can operate without the invalid provision or application, subject to the general construction rule's plain-meaning qualification. Initial qualification, fixed grant calculation, public-fund administration, and ordinary disclosure perform independent work. The precise conforming decree and any genuinely linked special enforcement provision should be determined on remand under that rule. The current handoff does not adjudicate every reporting provision or announce that every special obligation necessarily survives or necessarily falls. It does not import Minnesota's statute-specific Day severability disposition into Maine law.

The narrower remedy is a declaration of the constitutional component result and appropriate prospective restraint on the invalid operation, with implementation on remand. There is no instruction to refund past contributions or grants, reopen completed elections, impose retrospective liability, replace the legislative amount, restore a repealed ceiling automatically, or rewrite a matching formula. An invalid trigger creates no general prohibition against public funding. A decision sustaining a component supplies no permanent immunity from an actually different suppressive application.

## Threshold resolution and preservation

The eight Justices would take jurisdiction over the substantial, predecision challenge that the state judgment commands a form of recount and inclusion of returns that Article II forbids because it displaces the legislature's rules. This is a limited Cox fourth-category conclusion, not an election-importance exception. The state court conclusively authorized the contested relief despite the federal objection; Bush could prevail on nonfederal counting grounds and thereby lose a live need for review; acceptance of the asserted federal bar would foreclose the challenged contest relief rather than merely govern an evidentiary ruling; and delayed review during the legally bounded presidential selection process could leave the asserted constitutional protection incapable of effective vindication. Cox permits assessment of substantiality and the effect of a reversal without deciding in advance that reversal is warranted. Goodyear Atomic supplies the particularly useful distinction between accepting jurisdiction over an asserted complete federal bar and ultimately rejecting that bar on the merits.

The strongest contrary path is dismissal: the trial court still must count, resolve the Palm Beach figure, and select further relief, and an objection seeking only better counting procedures ordinarily fails Cox's requirement that reversal foreclose the relevant cause. That objection is substantial. The present jurisdictional conclusion rests on the preserved claim of an impermissible legislative displacement barring the commanded relief, not merely on an appeal from individual ballot rulings. In that claim's substantial version, the alleged substantive counting-rule change and departure from the lawful certification/contest period make the remaining commanded relief unavailable; the two other contest claims have already been rejected. The jurisdictional conclusion does not rest on the distinct, late blanket claim that presidential elections can never be contested under §102.168. It permits review of the constitutionally challenged operative decree. It does not decide premature objections to hypothetical future orders, every individual ballot, or every unpresented election claim. None of these Justices would use agreement by the parties to supply jurisdiction if the Cox predicates were absent.

The November 30 answer and December 6 state brief establish prior presentation of equality and due-process categories involving inconsistent standards and selective counties or precincts. The bounded December 6 extract separately expressly invokes Article II against the alleged new dimple standard and altered certification/contest periods. That presentation is distinct from the December 7 blanket claim that the contest statute does not apply to presidential elections, for which leave was denied. The eight commitments do not reach that blanket claim as a necessary ground. They also do not adopt unverified duplication, every overvote theory, or an independent facial attack on the entire pre-election code under the guise of these preserved objections. The original statute's service and answer requirements supply no invented procedural default.

The state court's general clear-intent rule and its affirmative inclusion/examination command are the objects of review. Post-decree implementation allegations are not supplied as new adjudicated facts. ## Questions, statutory terms, and record limits carried into every commitment

The two corporate questions are analytically distinct: categorical invalidity of the direct corporate treasury candidate-contribution prohibition, and invalidity of that prohibition in the demonstrated soft-money setting. The separate §441f question challenges false-source regulation on the asserted unequal treatment of allegedly comparable soft-money influence. None authorizes deciding every corporate expenditure, party contribution, or criminal count.

Section 441b(a) distinguishes national banks and congressionally organized corporations, whose election coverage is broader, from corporations generally and labor organizations subject to the enumerated federal-election and nomination coverage. It separately addresses knowing receipt and specified officers' or directors' consent. The question decided here concerns direct treasury contributions by the presented business-corporation class to federal candidates; no ruling about every other class or expenditure follows.

Section 441b(b)(2) covers direct and indirect money, services and value, with the ordinary-course bank-loan exception subject to applicable banking law and regulations. Its separate exclusions protect corporate communications to stockholders, executive or administrative personnel and their families, and labor communications to members and their families; nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote campaigns to the corresponding groups; and establishment, administration and solicitation for a separate segregated political fund. These are separate from §431's membership-communication exclusion and reporting proviso preserved by Akins.

The voluntary fund is a specific statutory channel, not a blanket exception for treasury-funded candidate donations. Its contributions cannot use value obtained by force, job discrimination, financial retaliation or threats, compulsory employment or membership payments, or commercial transactions. Any person soliciting an employee must disclose the fund's political purpose and the employee's right to refuse without reprisal. Ordinary corporate solicitation reaches stockholders, executive or administrative personnel and their families; ordinary labor solicitation reaches members and their families. The statute additionally permits two written residential-mail solicitations per calendar year from the specified corporate stockholder, personnel, employee and family groups, designed to conceal from the solicitor the identity of persons giving $50 or less and of noncontributors. Membership organizations, cooperatives and nonstock corporations retain their member-solicitation route. The trade-association route requires separate and specific member-corporation approval, limited to one trade association per corporation per calendar year. Lawful voluntary solicitation methods available to corporations are also available to labor organizations for their members; corporations, including subsidiaries, branches, divisions and affiliates, must provide the methods to a representing labor organization on written request at reimbursement cost. Executive or administrative personnel must be salaried and have the specified policymaking, managerial, professional or supervisory responsibilities. Statutory availability establishes neither an existing fund nor these companies' eligible pool, resources or practical fund capacity.

Section 441f has three clauses: making a contribution in another person's name; knowingly allowing one's name to be used for that contribution; and knowingly accepting a contribution made by one person in another's name. The first contains no express knowledge word; the latter two do. No commitment rewrites that difference, makes the first clause a strict criminal-liability rule, or determines the enforcement provision's elements. Section 441f contains no internal blanket exception. Constitutional protection for demonstrated associational harms requires its own supported application and does not itself authorize substitution of a false donor.

The reimbursement scheme, $80,000 Dole episode, timing relative to waste legislation, and source reporting are indictment allegations. None proves guilt, official exchange, knowledge or actual influence. The record's agreed candidate fundraising, party support, donor access and occasional coordinated advertising are relevant to the fit objection; they do not establish the legal identity of all direct and party transactions. Disputed editorial-based cynicism and legal conclusions are not independent facts. ## Proposed legal propositions and remedy paths

### Corporate sustaining path

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reject both corporate constitutional challenges and affirm that part of the en banc judgment. The source restriction is closely drawn to preventing candidate obligation and its appearance from direct transfers of commercially accumulated corporate resources, with a statutory political fund that uses voluntary political contributions and may receive corporate administrative support. It is not justified by equalizing influence, treating ordinary access as corruption, or assuming every corporation has abundant wealth. National Right to Work Committee supplies the specific corporate-channel precedent; Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett require that its structural rationale be tested against the actual statutory burden and soft-money record.

The principal contrary argument has real force: a zero treasury allowance is severe, voluntary fundraising is not the same asset pool, and parties obtain large corporate gifts while candidates participate in fundraising and benefit from party support. The five would find those facts insufficient to defeat this particular corporate-source rule. Direct control of a treasury transfer by the recipient candidate, and conversion of commercially assembled funds into a candidate obligation, are the regulated connection. Party issue spending and donor access do not make every transaction identical to that direct transfer. Proven coordination remains subject to its actual legal definition; ordinary agreement is not coordination. Underinclusiveness can expose a failed justification, but the existence of another influence channel does not remove the function of this one. The five do not use a finding that these companies successfully operated a fund, or place on Mariani a burden to prove political participation impossible.

Their narrower boundary is essential: reject these two presented constitutional grounds, preserve supported future claims involving materially different organizations, fund burdens or disclosure injuries, and decide no independent-expenditure application. Day's nonprofit protection and Akins's precise transaction and membership rules remain operative. The holding does not validate every corporate restraint or adjudicate the legality of soft money.

### Corporate invalidating path

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the corporate answer to the extent it permits the complete treasury-to-federal-candidate exclusion for the presented business-corporation contributions, and remand for conforming declaratory relief. They accept Russell's contribution standard and the power to impose sufficiently justified contribution limits; they do not adopt expenditure scrutiny for all contributions, demand a completed bribe, or establish an unlimited candidate-giving right.

Their ground is the difference between a justified restriction on candidate-directed value and complete exclusion of the selected funding source. The corporate label and commercially accumulated funds support attention to possible obligation, but do not sufficiently explain why every direct treasury gift, including a modest gift subject to otherwise valid constraints, must be prohibited while the supported candidate-connected party practices remain available. The voluntary fund uses other voluntarily supplied money; administrative support and lawful solicitation do not by themselves answer exclusion of the treasury resources through which this association seeks to act. Their Russell, Shrink Missouri and Daggett dissents insist that a usable substitute or practical answer cannot simply be presumed.

The strongest opposing argument is National Right to Work Committee's acceptance of a prophylactic corporate system and the direct-recipient distinction. These three accept that precedent's actual solicitation holding and the legitimacy of preventing circumvention. They would decline to make its broad deference language conclusive of this later, complete-source challenge under Russell's operative fit rule and this developed soft-money record. That record is evidence against sufficient fit, not an automatic rule that any underinclusiveness invalidates a statute. Their conclusion is confined to the presented direct corporate candidate-contribution prohibition; it does not overturn all corporate-expenditure regulation, displace Day's reserved questions, set a replacement amount, or validate concealed contributions.

Austin is an additional serious contrary authority, not an omitted premise. Its controlling expenditure judgment permits a treasury restraint for the corporation there, and its majority accepts regulation of business-corporation contributions. But it expressly adjudicates only the expenditure prohibition. These three do not claim that their older Austin expenditure objections compel this contribution result. The selected contribution conclusion rests instead on the later actual Russell fit rule, their consistently published application of that rule, and the precise complete-source burden now presented. They leave Austin's independent-expenditure holding undisturbed within its surviving scope. The five sustaining Justices likewise do not turn Austin's expenditure judgment into an automatic answer to the two corporate questions.

A remand merely to collect more facts about these corporations' fund resources is a lawful narrower alternative but is not their selected remedy. The decisive burden is the statutory exclusion of the treasury source, and the government has defended that rule on its structure and this record. The absence of particular fund facts supplies no adverse finding; it means the fund's success cannot be presumed to answer that exclusion. The existing developed record permits their constitutional answer without inventing an actual failed fundraising attempt. Limits and accurate attribution remain available on their own terms.

### False-source sustaining path, independent of corporate result

All eight Associates would affirm rejection of the §441f challenge. For covered candidate contributions, forbidding a contribution in another person's name, knowing lending of a name, and knowing acceptance of such a falsely attributed contribution serves accurate attribution and enforcement of otherwise valid contribution restrictions. Each clause addresses a distinct participant in the same false-source transaction. The constitutional rejection preserves their different express knowledge terms and leaves all actual criminal elements and proof unresolved.

The opposing path would invalidate or remand the false-name restriction because soft money can yield comparable access without the same disclosure, or because anonymous political association is protected. Its strongest support is the actual soft-money evidence together with McIntyre and Day's refusal to accept disclosure burdens by label alone. It fails here because truthful independent anonymity and affirmative substitution of a different candidate donor are materially different. The rule does not impose a dollar ceiling or prevent a lawful accurately attributed contribution. Accurate source information continues to serve valid candidate limits even for the three Justices who reject the complete corporate-source prohibition. Party-financing underinclusiveness does not create a constitutional entitlement to make candidate receipts appear to come from someone else.

This is not a finding of fraud by Mariani or a holding that every disclosure demand is permissible. Genuine coercion, retaliation or other associational injury would require a supported claim; none is supplied as a factual basis for this challenge. No judicially invented blanket exception excuses false naming. The Court need not decide mens rea, whether a particular contribution is covered, whether an alleged reimbursement occurred, sufficiency of evidence, or the constitutionality of every hypothetical enforcement application to answer the presented question.
