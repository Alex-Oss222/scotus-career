## Reconciliation of the constitutional question

The historical opinion sustained timely paternal recognition under demanding intermediate scrutiny on two grounds: reliable proof of parentage and an opportunity for a parent-child tie during childhood. It distinguished that opportunity from a guaranteed enduring relationship, relied on the legal acts' administrability and modest burden, and did not make least restrictive means the standard. The historical dissent challenged both the actual-purpose showing and substantial fit: reliable later proof can establish paternity, a timely formality need not create a real opportunity, and actual care does not excuse the father from a sex-specific formality imposed on no mother.

Those arguments and the material record are present here. Virginia's operative scrutiny remains substantially the same. The father is a proper copetitioner, just as he was historically. Miller's fractured judgment gives no controlling merits or remedy answer, also as historically. Most importantly, Kennedy's earlier conditional criticism in Miller was present in the historical timeline: his join in that criticism alone is not a changed premise justifying rejection of his later, case-specific Nguyen reasoning. His provisional invalidation is revised after that direct comparison.

## Rule scope and remedial positions for assembly

The sustaining merits position permits Congress to demand a legally documented paternal link during childhood as a substantial means of assuring biological parenthood and an opportunity for ties to the citizen parent. Birth ordinarily supplies maternal identification and an initial contact opportunity; conception alone does not guarantee paternal knowledge or contact. The position does not require a lasting bond in every case, make later claims of already acquired citizenship untimely, or approve every father-only requirement. It applies demanding intermediate scrutiny; the demanding justification language neither changes that scrutiny to rational review nor requires least restrictive means. The strongest contrary argument remains the mismatch between an unexcused legal deadline and undisputed actual upbringing; the sustaining position answers that Congress chose an administrable opportunity requirement rather than individual proof of a realized bond.

The four invalidating Associates permit proof safeguards responsive to actual biological differences but reject the additional sex-specific temporal condition because it does not substantially fit either asserted objective. They do not demand one scientifically preferred proof method or the best imaginable statute. Their proposed remedy removes the discriminatory age barrier on the available former-law route, preserving legitimation, nationality, applicable presence and any other valid requirements. The general section 406 severability provision, benefit-conferring structure and Westcott support preserving the benefit; Mathews requires considering withdrawal but does not command it. Pangilinan's prohibition on equitable naturalization contrary to valid law is distinct from recognition under the statute remaining after constitutional correction. Congress remains free to legislate a constitutional scheme.

Scalia and Thomas reject that enlargement because sections 1401 and 1409 form an integrated conditional grant, foreign-birth citizenship is statutory under Wong Kim Ark and Bellei, and Pangilinan and section 1421(d) constrain judicial relief. They regard that citizenship-specific limitation as sufficient despite section 406's general separability language. That ground does not withdraw anyone else's status or establish a universal bar to constitutional review.

Any invalidating judgment must resolve remedy expressly. The corrective position remands for ordinary implementation of the authorized election and genuinely unresolved remaining requirements; it neither orders immediate naturalization nor creates a new support promise, imports the maternal one-year rule, grants citizenship on DNA alone, vacates convictions, or orders removal regardless of citizenship. If the valid remaining statute establishes citizenship at birth, removal as an alien cannot stand. No new factual premise is needed for these bounded commitments.
