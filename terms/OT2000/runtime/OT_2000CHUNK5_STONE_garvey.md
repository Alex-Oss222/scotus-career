# OT_2000CHUNK5

# Major League Baseball Players Assn. v. Garvey

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s December 7, 2000 judgment and remand with instructions to restore the district court’s denial of Garvey’s motion to vacate the arbitral award. Reject the earlier appellate ruling’s merits-substitution premise insofar as necessary to that disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The approximately $3 million directed award is set aside. The arbitrator’s denial remains effective, and no new evidentiary hearing is required on the judicially perceived credibility error. Further contractual proceedings, if independently authorized by the agreement, are not created or prohibited by a judicial redetermination of Garvey’s entitlement.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Contractual authority and credibility findings

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the review standard and its application because the timely challenge to the second judgment presents the first ruling’s necessary predicate. The accepted statutory and contractual route removes any need to decide whether an independent statutory claim could be forced into arbitration. The reported arbitral reasoning supplies enough to decide whether the court replaced a credibility judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the arbitrator exceeded the accepted submission by rejecting the evidence of a withdrawn extension offer, and whether that disagreement entitled the court to order Garvey’s requested award.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received contractual-arbitration limits and the distinction between antecedent authority and merits review. The application to this award is newly adopted, but no replacement standard for collective-bargaining arbitration or broader waiver of independent statutory rights is adopted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An arbitrator applying an agreed compensation framework does not exceed the submission merely because a court regards his assessment of conflicting witness accounts as unreasonable. The reviewing court must identify a departure from contractual authority or another legally recognized enforcement defect, not rename factual disagreement as independent industrial justice. This arbitrator applied the requirement of a specific offer withdrawn because of collusion. The award therefore remains enforceable, and the court cannot replace it with the claimant’s requested monetary recovery.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs contractual compensation claims within an accepted labor-arbitration submission and protects factfinding performed to apply that submission. It leaves intact judicial examination of actual contractual authority and does not convert independent statutory claims into contract claims. A court that lawfully vacates an award must respect any remaining agreement to arbitrate rather than settle disputed contractual entitlement for itself.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Framework did not make proof of league-wide collusion sufficient for every player’s recovery. It required proof of an individual offer and withdrawal. Smith’s inconsistent accounts could make him unreliable even if earlier club testimony about collusion generally had been rejected; rejection of the earlier aggregate account does not compel acceptance of every later individual assertion. The arbitrator’s request for documents and express use of the offer criterion show a contractual inquiry, not substitution of unrelated preferences. Stone need not endorse the factual conclusion to recognize who was authorized to make it. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan protects the judicial gateway; it supplies no route to redo this conceded submission. Because no ground for vacatur is established here, restoration of the denial is the exact remedy, not an unnecessary second arbitration or a judicial damages award.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Outside the question presented: enforcement of a separate statutory discrimination claim and the consequences of actual arbitral corruption or fraud, neither asserted in this record. No boundary is reserved for the submitted contract claim’s review or the resulting denial of judicial monetary relief.
