## Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College — November 27, 2000

### Proposed disposition and sufficient ground

All eight Associates affirm dismissal of the individual-capacity damages action on qualified immunity, reserving whether punishment for mistakenly attributed protected speech violates the targeted employee's own First Amendment right. The Ninth Circuit's no-actual-speech rationale is not adopted. The complaint identifies individual officials, and the pleaded period in which the disciplinary notice could be revived expired October 28, 2000. Reinstatement does not eliminate the past damages controversy, but no continuing prospective threat or new municipal claim is supplied.

The particular right was not clearly established when the officials acted in January 1997. Pickering and Connick identify protected employee expression and permissible employment interests; Mt. Healthy separates protected conduct, substantial motivation, and a same-decision defense. Waters established limited propositions about a mistaken account of speech actually made, with materially different rationales and unresolved boundaries. It did not settle mistaken speaker identity. McIntyre protects actual anonymous advocacy against a specified identification demand; it does not answer this employment-liability extension. Harlow and Anderson therefore protect these defendants from damages without deciding that their alleged conduct was proper. Elder requires examination of all relevant conduct-date legal authority, not a mandatory merits-first decision. Crawford-El supplies the present procedural permission to resolve an adequate legal defense first, but its June 1997 decision cannot establish January 1997 notice.

## City of Indianapolis v. Edmond — November 28, 2000

### Proposed disposition by coalition

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the Seventh Circuit's prospective preliminary-injunction disposition. They adopt the rule that a checkpoint whose primary program purpose is ordinary narcotics investigation cannot justify the seizure of motorists without individualized suspicion merely because the stops are brief, systematic, and accompanied by license or sobriety checks. Scalia and Thomas would reverse the constitutional ruling on the described mixed-function program: its systematic license and sobriety checks provide an independently legitimate roadway basis, and the additional drug objective and exterior sniff do not invalidate a stop whose objective operation remains within that lawful basis. These are distinct rationale commitments, not a blended balancing test.

### Proposition scope for the six-Justice position

1. The vehicle stop is a seizure before the exterior dog sniff occurs. Whether that sniff independently counts as a search does not supply authority for the preceding stop.
2. A primary program purpose of obtaining evidence of ordinary crime requires a lawful exception beyond generalized enforcement benefits and a minimally intrusive method. No such special need or emergency is shown here. Program purpose concerns the category of authority invoked, not an individual officer's hidden motive where probable cause already authorizes the stop.
3. Sitz's immediate highway-safety program and Martinez-Fuerte's border-control context remain available within their own predicates. Prouse does not authorize suspicionless stops for every law-enforcement purpose merely because selection is systematic. Whren's objective probable-cause rule and its purpose-limited administrative exceptions both remain intact. Chandler confirms that reduced intrusion cannot replace an adequate actual need; it does not supply a universal checklist.
4. Ancillary roadway functions cannot sustain a program whose admitted principal object is drug detection. The holding does not prohibit lawful observation or action on evidence encountered during a genuinely justified checkpoint, nor determine emergency roadblocks, imminent threats, border authority, searches based on individualized suspicion, suppression, or damages.

### Adversarial paths and Justice-specific comparison

The strongest city argument is that lawful systematic license and impairment checks do not become unconstitutional merely because administrators also seek narcotics, especially when dog use does not extend the stop. The six-Justice position answers that this assumes the very suspicionless-seizure authority requiring justification: these stops lack Whren's antecedent probable cause, and the admitted program purpose is no independent traffic or border exception. The strongest motorists' argument against the two-Justice position is that administrators could add nominal license checks to any evidence-gathering program. Scalia and Thomas answer by requiring actual, independently supportable checkpoint functions and execution within their permissible scope; they do not recognize an unlimited narcotics-roadblock power. The dispute is whether genuine secondary functions are enough despite the primary criminal-investigation purpose.

### Remedy and limits

The six affirm appropriate prospective restraint at the preliminary-injunction stage on this program; they do not adjudicate damages or suppression. The two would reverse and return for proceedings consistent with their objective mixed-function rule, not enter a universal final judgment approving every execution detail. No purpose-finding remand is needed under the six-Justice rule because the principal objective is admitted. No Justice proposes assigning a new program purpose or inventing an emergency. ## Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph — December 11, 2000

### Common finality and statutory-right commitments

All eight Associates agree that the district court's order compelling arbitration and dismissing every claim with prejudice is final and appealable under FAA section 16(a)(3). Catlin supplies ordinary finality: nothing remains for the district court to adjudicate in this action. A later independent proceeding concerning an award does not convert the completed action into a pending one. The label that arbitration arose within a suit containing substantive claims supplies no statutory exception. Digital Equipment concerns a collateral-order attempt to appeal before final judgment and does not defeat an actual final judgment here. This holding neither decides whether dismissal rather than a stay was proper nor approves dismissal in every arbitration case.

All eight reject categorical invalidity based only on silence about arbitral costs. All eight accept that sufficiently prohibitive arbitration costs can prevent effective assertion of federal statutory rights and that a concrete, supported cost obstacle cannot be dismissed as mere hostility to arbitration. Mitsubishi and Gilmer permit a change of forum while preserving substantive protections; Allied-Bruce preserves actual enforceability questions; First Options preserves independent judicial decision of the antecedent question within its actual consent/delegation scope. None supplies a presumption that every silent agreement is either inexpensive or prohibitively expensive.

### Distinct cost-disposition commitments

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas conclude that Randolph, as the party resisting an otherwise covered agreement on this ground, must initially demonstrate a likelihood of prohibitive expense. The present record identifies no likely forum, actual charges, or amount chargeable to her. Her inability-to-pay assertion and contractual silence do not establish that likelihood. They reverse the cost-based unenforceability ruling and remand for unresolved properly presented alternatives, including the class-action contention identified in the neutral supplement. They do not order unconditional arbitration or decide that alternative. They do not require exact proof of a bill not yet issued, impose an invariably unaffordable-fee test, or resolve what further showing would shift a burden to the proponent. Their commitment rejects a new mandatory cost-development proceeding on this record; ordinary preserved procedural grounds for further proceedings remain for the lower court.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also reject the categorical rule, but would vacate the cost disposition and require a limited opportunity to develop likely forum, fee allocation, and financial access before enforcing arbitration on the cost objection. The record's silence proves neither excess nor affordability. A newly clarified demand for concrete proof should operate through a focused factual inquiry, particularly when the mechanism for choosing a forum and allocating costs determines what the claimant can reasonably establish. Randolph retains an initial responsibility to substantiate the obstacle; these four do not shift the entire burden merely because the contract is silent. A legally binding payment undertaking may remove a demonstrated obstacle; a nonbinding appellate assurance cannot be treated as an amended contract. They also preserve the unadjudicated class-action alternative. No lower-court statutory merits are decided.
