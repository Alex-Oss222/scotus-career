# Saucier v. Katz - frozen non-Stone commitments

**Event:** Merits, June 18, 2001; No. 99-1977.
**Stage:** Independent modeling before the current-matter comparator and before private Stone material. No assignment is made.
**Inputs:** Frozen C neutral handoff, including the source supplementation; C entering-law slice; the complete Government petition and appendices containing the lower opinions. The news video and unproduced deposition pages were not independently inspected.
**Research cutoff:** June 18, 2001 for governing doctrine; September 24, 1994 for the officer's legal notice. No current-matter Supreme Court opinion was consulted. Later cases cannot supply earlier notice, and same-day uncoordinated events supply no entering law.

## Reachability, facts and distinct components

All eight Associates participate. Immediate review reaches the separate legal immunity question using the supported plaintiff-favorable assumptions. Johnson, Behrens and Sterling exclude review of a purely factual sufficiency challenge concerning Saucier's actual participation or the true resistance account. The district judge reviewed the video and still identified material disputes; this Court cannot substitute the petition's Parker-only characterization as an established fact.

The assumed account is a sudden, unwarned two-officer seizure of an unresisting banner demonstrator, hurried removal and a forceful entry into the van that Katz says nearly caused his head to strike its interior. He avoided physical injury. A leg brace and lack of a shoe were supported; the fact of wearing a brace does not conclusively establish Saucier's actual awareness. The display occurred near the Vice President at the security-controlled ceremony. Neither accepting Katz's lack of resistance nor accepting the security setting permits invention of a weapon, flight or continued physical threat.

The questions must remain distinct: whether the force actually violated the Fourth Amendment; whether the 1994 law made the particular illegality sufficiently clear for personal damages; whether Saucier personally participated; and what facts are properly assumed on this appeal. The existing federal-officer damages channel is not challenged by the grant. Prior First Amendment, probable-cause, supervisory and prospective-relief rulings are not reopened.

## Governing rule and competing paths

**Separate immunity.** Graham's merits rule considers the complete circumstances, including the severity of the suspected conduct, actual threat, resistance and the practical setting, from the reasonable officer's perspective. Reasonable factual perceptions belong in that inquiry. Harlow and Anderson independently protect an officer from damages for certain reasonable legal mistakes about what those circumstances constitutionally permit. Merely using reasonableness in both inquiries does not merge them. Hunter supplies proper factual assumptions and judicial legal resolution, Elder supplies all relevant preconduct law, and Lanier rejects both abstract-right sufficiency and an indispensable factual twin. Denius expressly retains this defense and rejects an actual-reliance-only replacement. Every Associate supports those propositions.

**Grant immunity on the assumed account.** Graham itself recognizes some force incidental to effecting a seizure and warns against hindsight treating every unnecessary push as a constitutional violation. On these facts the officers were moving a demonstrator away from a live protected event and into a vehicle, rather than imposing a separate beating after detention. A sudden carrying movement and one forceful van entry can be unreasonable in degree without every reasonable officer in 1994 having sufficiently clear notice that this particular amount crossed the constitutional line. Seven Associates select that legal conclusion without finding the force constitutionally proper. Their strongest counterargument is the account of a restrained, nonthreatening older person nearly driven headfirst into the van; the absence of actual injury cannot answer it. They nonetheless regard the boundary of force in this immediate removal as insufficiently settled for damages, not as an obvious gratuitous assault detached from the seizure.

**Deny immunity while rejecting merger.** Stevens treats the supported assumption of a violent shove of an unresisting, controlled person, after the officers had removed him from the crowd, as an obvious application of Graham's existing restriction. Security explains taking control and prompt removal; it does not make any further force reasonably believed lawful. The strongest objection is that this formulation abstracts away the uncertainty of moving an arrestee into a vehicle and the short continuous event. Stevens answers that the facts must be assumed for Katz and that a jury may later reject them; immunity cannot be obtained by replacing the adverse assumed account with a modest routine push. He would affirm denial of immunity on the distinct notice ground, without adopting the Ninth Circuit's merger rule or finding liability after trial.

**Resolve force as constitutional.** Plakas permits merits summary judgment when the supported facts admit no contrary material account and establish justified force. Here the dispute about force, threat and resistance makes a conclusive no-violation holding an unnecessary and poorly supported basis for the disposition. The immunity-grant group does not join that alternative. No physical-injury prerequisite is introduced.

## Sequence and opinion compatibility

All eight preserve the distinction between current merits doctrine and conduct-date notice. No entering precedent requires a universal merits-first sequence: Elder and Conn expressly leave it open, and Wilson's chosen sequence does not create an across-the-board command. This case can identify the governing Fourth Amendment protection and assume a violation before resolving the distinct notice question. These commitments do not adopt a new categorical rule compelling courts to decide every uncertain constitutional question before granting immunity.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas may explain the value of addressing the asserted right before its clarity so that law can develop; that does not supply a frozen join to an exceptionless universal sequence. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer require room to avoid an unnecessary constitutional holding where the factual posture or dispositive notice question makes one unhelpful. Stevens's notice disposition rests on the supported application of existing Graham protection, not a universal sequencing rule. Any proposed broader mandatory rule needs an explicit new compatibility determination and its own supported justification; it cannot be attributed to these commitments as already agreed.

## Frozen result, remedy and limits

Seven Associates - O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer - support reversal of the immunity denial and judgment recognizing Saucier's personal immunity on this reviewed excessive-force damages theory. They assume, rather than finally decide, a constitutional violation. Stevens opposes that result and supports affirming denial under the separate clear-law inquiry. All eight reject legal merger, preserve the factual-appellate boundary and retain the existing objective defense.

The immunity disposition establishes neither that the force was lawful nor that Saucier did not participate. It does not adjudicate an unrelated damages claim, revive disposed claims, award damages, immunize a municipality or alter the existing cause of action. Under Stevens's alternative, actual responsibility, resistance, force, injury and damages remain for ordinary proof. Under the seven-Justice path the legal defense disposes of this claim even on the supported adverse assumptions, so those factual disputes do not require appellate resolution.

Atwater and Sullivan require separate justification for distinct intrusions but do not retrospectively establish a 1994 immunity boundary; their present custody holdings are not a force-specific answer. McArthur's confined preservation restraint and Wallis's municipal liability do not alter this defense. No material neutral-source blocker remains after the appended lower orders clarify the assumed facts. The independent source review does not certify the uninspected video or every deposition page.
