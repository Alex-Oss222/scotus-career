## Components and statutory rule

**C1 — Statutory privilege.** Section 201(c) keeps copyright in the separate contribution distinct from copyright in the collective whole. Absent an express transfer of copyright or rights under it, the presumed privilege permits reproducing and distributing the contribution as part of that particular collective work, a revision of that work, or a later collective work in the same series. Section 103(b) gives the compiler rights in the compiler's contribution without enlarging rights in underlying material. Section 106(1) and (3) protect reproduction and distribution. Section 101's media-neutral definition of copies does not itself decide whether the contribution is reproduced as part of the required collective work.

**C2 — NEXIS.** The searchable text is presented as individually retrieved articles, with source information but without the original issue's surrounding contents and presentation. Whether that presentation remains part of a revision is a substantive statutory question, not a question of physical medium.

**C3 — New York Times OnDisc.** The same individual-text method applies. Single-publisher provenance does not itself identify each article as currently part of a particular revised issue.

**C4 — General Periodicals OnDisc.** The actual source-page images retain more original appearance than NEXIS or NYTO, but the product does not present the complete original issue. The question is whether that retained page context is sufficient under the privilege, not whether an image can ever form an electronic revision.

**C5 — Review boundary, not a contract holding.** Newsday and Time's unpressed contract-authorization defenses remain as treated below. Whitford's first-publication clause, fifty-percent-of-net-proceeds condition for the nonexclusive republication license, and prevailing-rates condition for Time-publication republication are not reinterpreted here. The Court's opinion may state the material procedural background, but adopts no new contract rule and directs no reopening of either defense.

**C6 — Judgment and residual work.** On the contextual-revision construction, affirm the appellate judgment on the presented statutory ground and leave appropriate damages and equitable relief for ordinary individualized adjudication. On the archival-revision construction, reverse the appellate §201(c) construction and vacate the dependent liability disposition for proceedings under that corrected statutory rule, without reopening either unpressed contract defense. The separate §201(d) transferability contention was not reached below; it is distinct from contractual authorization and cannot be silently decided by relabeling the contract issue. Neither proposed disposition directs a final monetary award, compulsory license or blanket deletion.

## Two-path comparison, including the corrected reach

**Contextual-revision path.** A revision may change presentation, medium and contents; the statute demands neither an exact facsimile nor preservation of every original item. It does require reproduction of the contribution as part of the particular collective work or a recognizable revision. Individually offered text articles outside their issue context fail that relationship. GPO's page image is materially different from bare text but still reproduces the contribution detached from the collective issue rather than as a revised version of that issue. Source tags establish provenance, and a user's selected gathering of articles does not change the reproduction and distribution the publishers supplied. Ownership of the new database does not supply the contributor's separately retained rights.

**Strongest contrary argument.** The original editorial selection may survive in a searchable archive despite a user's separate retrieval, just as a reader can consult one paper or microform article. Revision permits omission and changed appearance, and digital archival continuity has substantial public value. The contextual response is that neither search technology nor an omitted picture is the defect: the particular products distribute individual contributions outside a recognizable source collective work. Archive value cannot itself enlarge the enacted privilege. Actual licensing remains possible, but no general license or new contract conclusion is presumed.

**Archival-revision path.** Publisher-originated collections retain the selected content's source-and-issue association even when retrieval displays one article. Revision can describe that continuing archival publication despite a changed arrangement. NEXIS aggregates source collections; NYTO preserves a Times archive; GPO's source-page images provide additional continuity. The limiting principle is the actual archival relationship to the source editions, not a bare provenance label for an unrelated anthology or independently sold article. This construction gives the statutory permission its own operation without treating the publisher as owner of every underlying copyright.

**Strongest contrary argument.** Nearly any reuse can include a source label, and a new database is not each source issue. The broader construction risks emptying the words particular collective work of limiting force. Its proponents distinguish the preserved publisher-originated editorial and issue relationships from a merely bibliographic acknowledgment. The disagreement remains whether those features are sufficient in these actual products; it is not resolved by postulating an unchanged future interface or by asserting that every possible user search recreates an edition.

**Effect of the reach correction on both paths.** Neither side may cure a perceived weakness by adjudicating Whitford's contract afresh. The contextual path already defeats the statutory defense and supports affirmance without any added contract holding. The archival path supplies a different §201(c) answer, not resurrection of an unpressed license defense. Any genuinely remaining statutory transferability question must be kept distinct and addressed only within its lawful presentation and need after the statutory correction; there is no instruction to reopen Time's or Newsday's contract defense, and no automatic final judgment for every publisher on all conceivable defenses.
