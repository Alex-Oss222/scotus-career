## Section 441b in its statutory setting

Subsection (a) distinguishes national banks and congressionally organized corporations, whose prohibition reaches elections to any political office, from corporations generally and labor organizations, whose prohibition reaches the listed federal elections and the specified nomination processes. It separately forbids knowing acceptance or receipt of a prohibited contribution and consent by the listed officers or directors to the organization's prohibited contribution or expenditure. These textual classes are preserved; the presented constitutional dispute concerns direct corporate treasury contributions to federal candidates. No independent holding about every other class or independent expenditure follows merely because it shares the section.

Subsection (b)(2) reaches the specified direct or indirect transfers, services and things of value connected to covered elections. It excepts ordinary-course national or State bank loans made under applicable banking law and regulations. Three distinct exclusions concern: corporate communications on any subject to stockholders and executive or administrative personnel and their families, or union communications to members and their families; nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote campaigns directed to the corresponding specified groups; and establishment, administration and solicitation of a political separate segregated fund by the listed organizations. These are not interchangeable with §431's distinct membership-communication reporting proviso preserved in Akins.

The separate-fund route is a voluntary political-financing route with operative restrictions. Subsection (b)(3) bars fund use of value obtained by force, employment discrimination, financial retaliation or threats; compulsory membership or employment payments; and commercial transactions. Employee solicitors must state the fund's political purpose and the employee's right to refuse without reprisal. A corporation's ability to finance fund establishment and administration does not authorize corporate treasury money to become the fund's candidate-contribution money.

Subsection (b)(4)(A)'s ordinary solicitation classes are corporate stockholders and executive or administrative personnel and their families, and union members and their families. Subparagraph (B) additionally permits two written solicitations per calendar year from the specified corporate stockholder, executive, administrative, employee and family groups. Those solicitations must be mailed to residences and designed so that the solicitor cannot identify those contributing $50 or less or those who do not contribute. Subparagraph (C) preserves solicitation of members by membership organizations, cooperatives, nonstock corporations and their funds. Subparagraph (D) permits the specified trade-association solicitation of member corporations' stockholders, executive or administrative personnel and families only with the member corporation's separate and specific approval; that corporation may approve only one such trade association in a calendar year. A summary confined to the ordinary solicitation class must retain these additional routes.

Subsections (b)(5) and (6) preserve the statutory equal access to lawful voluntary solicitation methods for labor organizations and require a corporation using such methods, on written request, to provide them to a labor organization representing its workers at reimbursement of expenses only. Subsection (b)(7) defines executive or administrative personnel by salaried rather than hourly compensation and policymaking, managerial, professional or supervisory responsibilities. The text establishes these rules without finding the particular companies' structure, the number or resources of eligible solicitees, an existing fund, or any actual impediment to creating one. Personal contributions remain distinct from corporate reimbursements; statutory availability of a route is not a record finding about its operation for this litigant.

## Section 441f and posture

Section 441f contains three clauses: making a contribution in another person's name; knowingly allowing one's name to be used for that contribution; and knowingly accepting a contribution made by one person in another's name. Its first clause does not contain the express knowledge language used by the latter two. This textual observation neither determines the criminal enforcement provision's requirements nor establishes guilt or liability. No statutory exception appears within §441f itself; applicable definitions, independent disclosure protections and actual criminal elements must not be silently replaced with invented blanket exceptions or strict liability.

Section 437h permits the Commission, a national party committee or an individual eligible to vote for President to bring the described constitutional action in district court, with certification to the circuit court sitting en banc. The official history records repeal in 1988 of the former direct Supreme Court appeal provision. The present Supreme Court route is the supplied §1254(1) certiorari posture. The inherited Reform Party threshold rules remain distinct from statutory eligibility and Article III standing. The [date-eligible government opposition](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/mariani-v-united-states-opposition) independently states §1254(1) as the invoked jurisdiction; it is advocacy, not adjudication or an additional factual finding.
