## 1. Review channel and exact scope

The November 21 judgment is reviewable under 28 U.S.C. section 1257(a). It reverses the two trial orders and gives a complete binding directive for certification, allows no rehearing, and orders no further merits adjudication in that proceeding. County implementation and a possible separate election contest do not make that judgment interlocutory. The competing candidates have an adverse concrete interest. No extraordinary original federal election proceeding is needed or assumed.

The submitted federal questions concern alleged post-election alteration under due process, the effect of then-operative 3 U.S.C. section 5, and displacement of the legislature's Article II authority. There is no independent equal-protection claim in this matter's supplied scope. State-law grounds do not insulate a state judgment from a claim that the judgment itself violates federal law, but section 1257 supplies no authority to correct state statutory construction merely because this Court would read the code differently. Footnote 10's statement that no party challenged the constitutionality of Florida's election laws does not establish waiver of an objection to the judicial interpretation or remedy later announced.

All eight support that threshold conclusion. Their common joins in Foster establish enforcement of an actual federal electoral constraint with relief confined to the demonstrated conflict; their unanimous Yoshina fundamental-fairness joins distinguish authoritative state interpretation from the separate federal protection. Neither earlier decision adjudicated the present Article II issue, and neither is offered as a substitute for the section 1257 finality analysis.

A claim that the state court simply abandoned the election code is not an accurate description of that opinion.

## 3. Clarification requested and exact procedural consequence

Minnesota v. National Tea supports vacatur and remand where a state judgment's unresolved legal basis prevents sound disposition of the federal challenge. Section 1257 supplies review jurisdiction; the ordinary appellate remand authority in 28 U.S.C. section 2106 supplies the selected corrective form. The modeled order vacates the November 21 judgment and remands for an authoritative statement addressing only the material dependence described here, followed by such proceedings as the state court lawfully finds appropriate.

The clarification should identify:

1. Whether the late-return discretion limits and the selected acceptance date rest on construction of the pre-election code independently of a state constitutional power to displace legislative directions, and what role the constitutional principles actually perform for those features.
2. Whether the state court understood a particular federal electoral requirement, including section 5, to require its chosen date or its rule concerning full participation, and the role that understanding played in the remedy.

The Court does not require Florida to adopt a particular answer, make the Secretary's prior interpretation controlling, prohibit state constitutional interpretation categorically, or decide new state-law questions unrelated to the federal challenge. The legal consequences of any actual later act must be examined on its own supported posture. No unrecorded federal stay or intervening state response is supplied.

This is a vacatur-and-remand commitment, not a jurisdiction-retaining request. ## 4. Federal components kept distinct

### Article II

McPherson establishes the constitutional setting of legislative direction for appointing presidential electors. Smiley recognizes the relevance of a State's lawmaking process in its different congressional-election setting. Neither supplies a ready answer that every state judicial construction is forbidden or that every state constitutional restriction necessarily governs the legislature's Article II function. Ordinary statutory interpretation is not eliminated merely because Article II uses the term legislature. Whether the particular judgment displaced a legislative instruction is the actual dispute, and its resolution requires the state court's own account of the remaining materially ambiguous grounds.

The Court therefore reaches no final Article II merits holding at this stage. It does not use a generalized preference for deference to dismiss the federal question, nor an abstract legislative-power theory to presume that the state court has already overridden legislative law. The remand addresses the antecedent uncertainty necessary to that merits determination.

### Section 5

Then-effective section 5 gives conclusive treatment in the federal electoral count to a qualifying final determination made under pre-election laws by the date six days before electors meet. The supplied dates are December 12 and December 18. The benefit's conditions are distinct from an affirmative federal prohibition on all counting after December 12. An asserted independent cutoff must have its own legal source.

Foster's unanimous holding regulates premature final congressional selection under the congressional election-day statutes. It expressly leaves presidential timing outside its holding and cannot convert section 5 into a universal stopping rule. The remand may seek the state court's actual understanding of federal timing without declaring that the legislature selected December 12 as an absolute state-law end date, that safe-harbor qualification is guaranteed, or that every post-election judicial construction forfeits the benefit. These are not conclusions established by the state opinion's generic citation to federal election statutes.

### Due process

The due-process question remains in the case and must not disappear into the Article II discussion. Every participating Associate joined Yoshina's rule that a neutral authoritative construction of an ambiguous election provision does not become fundamentally unfair merely because an official previously advised otherwise. The actual guidance, dissemination, inducement, reliance, and voting circumstances matter to a supported fairness claim; those subjects are not a new rigid element list.

The source here establishes post-election conflicting advice to officials and subsequent judicial construction. It does not establish a particular pre-election assurance to voters or detrimental reliance. That source limitation proves neither that no such contention was raised elsewhere nor that a litigant failed to produce evidence in Court. A factual finding of deceptive inducement or unfair reliance therefore cannot be invented. The distinct legal contention that the court newly displaced a clear election rule rather than construed ambiguity depends in part on the unresolved legal basis of the judgment. Clarification is relevant to that contention as well as Article II.

This remand does not reject the entire due-process question merely because individualized reliance facts are absent from the supplied source. It also does not hold that a change in an advisory interpretation, standing alone, is sufficient for federal relief. The governing Yoshina distinction is preserved for any eventual merits determination. No affirmative factual reconstruction or new record-production duty is imposed.

## 6. Adversarial path assessment and completion boundary

**Sustain now:** The strongest case for affirmance is the genuine conflict between mandatory and permissive provisions, the code's pre-existing manual process, and established state interpretive principles. Yoshina also defeats a claim that a post-vote authoritative construction is automatically unfair. The counterargument is specific: the state court's two-condition restriction and equitable date may depend on a constitutional source or federal-timing assumption rather than merely those statutory ambiguities. Affirmance without resolving that dependency could decide the federal claim on a state-law premise the state court has not fixed.

**Invalidate now:** The strongest case for merits reversal is that a judicially selected date and a newly confined discretion displaced the legislature's pre-election arrangement. The counterargument is likewise specific: section 102.112 already permits late returns to be accepted, section 102.166 provides a recount route, and the state court did not erase the statutory board deadline. A categorical invalidation would assume the relevant displacement without distinguishing construction from override. The source supplies no affirmative factual due-process finding that independently removes the need to know the legal basis.

**Clarify:** A limited remand avoids neither the statutory text nor the federal questions. It obtains an authoritative answer to the genuine antecedent issue identified by National Tea before the Court reaches their merits. The strongest objection is that remand may merely delay a decision where the state opinion is already detailed. The answer is that the remand must identify only the two remaining dependencies above and acknowledge the analysis that is already clear. It must not ask a general question about the basis of a 40-page legal opinion or demand a new record for the operator's benefit. This commitment selects vacatur and remand because it gives the state court an ordinary appellate vehicle to clarify and, if appropriate, revise the judgment; it does not silently assume a certification procedure or retain federal jurisdiction while treating the state judgment as vacated.

**Complete-resolution boundary:** The question decided is whether the present explanation permits sound resolution of the submitted federal challenge, and the remedy is complete for that antecedent determination: a defined vacatur and remand. The Court does not purport to decide half of an Article II, due-process, or section 5 merits question. Those merits await the authoritative state-law predicates that the judgment itself leaves materially uncertain. Neighboring questions outside this certification case include a later contest's ballot standards, recount feasibility, elector totals, and any election winner. Any further application of Yoshina must keep actual factual work separate from unresolved legal scope.
