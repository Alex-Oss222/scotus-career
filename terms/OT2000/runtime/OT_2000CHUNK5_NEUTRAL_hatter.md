# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Hatter.

**Citation or Docket:** No. 99-1978; 532 U.S. 557.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Merits decision after argument on February 20, 2001.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-21 (historical decision-calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether applying Medicare and Social Security taxes to Article III judges appointed before those taxes were extended to federal employees diminishes their protected compensation; whether the earlier no-quorum disposition forecloses review; and whether later salary increases cure an otherwise unlawful diminution. This is a faithful formulation; exact granted wording has not been independently verified.

**Question Granted:** The question presented above defines the merits issue under review.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit judgment reversing the Court of Federal Claims and treating both taxes as impermissible reductions not cured by the 1984 salary increase.

**Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of that liability ruling. The judges seek compensation for unlawful deductions, subject to the claim-specific limitations issues.

**Material Record:** Congress extended Medicare coverage and its payroll tax to federal employees generally in 1982, effective January 1983. The later Social Security transition treated existing employees differently: most could remain outside the system; mandatory participants with contributory pensions could offset their contributions; judges' noncontributory retirement system did not provide that offset. The actual classification and financial incidence, rather than a finding of legislators' hostility, ground the discrimination contention. A subsequent judicial salary increase is undisputed, but its legal curative effect is contested. [United States v. Hatter](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-557.pdf).

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** An earlier petition ended without a Supreme Court quorum, with the statutory effect of an equally divided affirmance. That procedural fact must be distinguished from a reasoned merits determination. The Claims Court also ruled the Medicare claims untimely; resolving constitutional liability does not itself calculate recoverable periods.

**Relevant Dates:** Tax extensions in 1982 and 1983; earlier no-quorum disposition in 1996; present event May 21, 2001.

**Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring consolidation here.

**Participation Matter:** Stevens and O'Connor were disqualified in the historical proceeding. No additional participation order is identified.

### Issue 1: Judicial compensation and tax incidence

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Jefferson County v. Acker permits a nondiscriminatory tax on compensation under the applicable federal consent statute, while expressly reserving the independent Compensation Clause question. Its holding supplies neither an automatic judicial exemption nor an answer to these transition rules.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The taxation rule examines actual operation and discrimination against federal-source compensation rather than statutory labels. It does not convert the independent constitutional question expressly reserved in Jefferson County v. Acker into settled law.

**Other Governing Law:** The Compensation Clause protects Article III judicial compensation against diminution during tenure. The Medicare extension and Social Security transition provisions must be examined separately because their coverage and offsets differ.

**Material Other Authority:** Evans v. Gore, O'Malley v. Woodrough, and United States v. Will are pre-event authorities whose reconciliation remains necessary. Their treatment must be express; a change cannot be derived from the reservation in Jefferson County v. Acker.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law supports an operation-based inquiry into nondiscriminatory taxation under the federal consent statute. It leaves independent Compensation Clause protection, including the effect of pension-based transition classifications, unresolved.

**Open Point:** Whether generally shared taxes and a transition materially disadvantaging incumbent judges are constitutionally different, and whether appointment before enactment creates a categorical exemption.

### Issue 2: Prior no-quorum disposition and compensation remedy

**Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision conclusively determines the present no-quorum question or makes a later salary increase a cure for an accrued unconstitutional deduction. Existing finality principles require identifying the judgment and mandate actually entered.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Finality and law-of-the-case analysis preserve the operative scope of a judgment without treating every unexplained disposition as a reasoned constitutional holding. No controlling decision makes an unrelated later raise extinguish an accrued pay injury.

**Other Governing Law:** The statutory no-quorum mechanism gives the earlier disposition the effect of an affirmance by an equally divided Court. Compensation entitlement, claim limitations, and the calculation of sums legally owed remain separate.

**Material Other Authority:** The earlier proceeding is relevant as a procedural act under the no-quorum statute, not as a reasoned merits holding. United States v. Will bears on compensation protection but does not eliminate the need to determine this prior mandate's scope.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** A lower court cannot disregard an operative mandate merely because it lacks reasons. That principle does not itself establish that a Court unable to assemble a quorum finally decided every constitutional proposition raised in a later review.

**Open Point:** What the prior statutory affirmance forecloses in this proceeding, and whether a lawful subsequent raise offsets, cures, or leaves intact previously unlawful deductions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit read protected compensation to bar the challenged deductions and rejected a later raise as a cure. The Claims Court's contrary cure reasoning would measure overall compensation after the raise, rather than the legality of each withheld sum.

**United States:** Judges should bear generally shared taxes; retirement transition rules respond to pension structures rather than judicial independence. The earlier no-quorum action should not freeze a constitutional question the Court never considered. A later raise may eliminate any net reduction.

**Judges:** The constitutional protection attaches during tenure, and the Social Security transition leaves sitting judges uniquely exposed to a new charge. An increase in lawful salary cannot authorize withholding part of that new salary. The earlier affirmance and mandate protect their established entitlement. These arguments require separate treatment of Medicare's broad coverage and Social Security's narrower transition, not a single payroll-tax conclusion.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold path:** Enforce the earlier judgment to the extent it actually settled a binding issue, or hold that the no-quorum proceeding leaves present legal review open. Either ruling must identify what remains reviewable.

**Merits paths:** Uphold nondiscriminatory Medicare taxation while invalidating materially discriminatory treatment of incumbent judges; uphold both if the Social Security distinction is constitutionally justified; or apply a broader tenure-based prohibition, candidly explaining the necessary precedent treatment. A rule forbidding special judicial burdens can reach other disguised reductions without exempting judges from ordinary taxes. The Court need not decide taxation of newly appointed judges on different terms.

**Judgment and remedy:** A divided disposition can affirm one liability component and reverse another, remanding for limitations and calculation. Invalid deductions are not automatically cured by unrelated salary increases; any genuine offset must be legally and temporally matched. No numerical award or exemption for every federal judge follows without claimant-specific entitlement.
