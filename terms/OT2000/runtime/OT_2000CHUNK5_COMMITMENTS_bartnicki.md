## Reachability, common predicates, and components

All eight Associate Justices participate on the supplied institutional facts: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal is established. The two petitions challenge the same Third Circuit judgment; the United States' defense of its statute does not create a separate private damages claim. The accepted certified questions permit adjudication of the legal First Amendment defense on their stated assumptions.

For that legal question, intentional unlawful interception, defendants' knowledge or reason to know, defendants' nonparticipation and lawful receipt are assumed. The unresolved interception-intent dispute is not adjudicated as a trial fact. The recording is accurate. Yocum's communications to board members and Vopper, Vopper's broadcasts and the other challenged media disclosures receive the same constitutional rule; professional status supplies neither immunity nor disqualification. Nothing establishes that a defendant procured the recording or acted as the interceptor's agent.

Each Justice accepts that the federal disclosure text reaches knowing disclosure by a noninterceptor. Constitutional avoidance cannot rewrite that coverage into an interceptor-only prohibition. The parallel Pennsylvania disclosure claim presents the same relevant speech burden on the supplied issue. Neither the civil-remedy provision nor the use of damages makes the First Amendment irrelevant. There is no final damages adjudication to affirm or reverse.

The separable questions are: (1) whether these disclosure claims are constitutionally barred on the certified assumptions; (2) the adequate justification and operative scope of that answer; and (3) the resulting treatment of the summary-judgment direction. The predicates and remedy require express treatment, but they do not manufacture different judgments for the companion dockets or for nonmedia speakers.

## Entering-law work and limits

- **Turner (1994):** The content inquiry examines what the restriction selects and why. Protection of editorial speech and meaningful review remain even where a law is neutral. Its controlling intermediate standard requires actual advancement and an adequately fitted burden without demanding the least restrictive conceivable means. Kennedy and Souter adopted that framework; Stevens expressly accepted the limited deferential-but-independent predictive-review proposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg rejected the particular must-carry classification, while joining the general content-classification rule.
- **Turner (1997):** Kennedy, Stevens, Souter and Breyer sustained the particular full-power duties on the developed preservation-and-diversity record. That disposition supplies neither a universal presumption against regulation nor conclusive deference to a claimed interest. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg's dissent from the merits affirmance does not decide the classification of this distinct law.
- **Rice (1998):** All eight Associates joined Kennedy's distinction between protected publication and intentional, materially effective operational assistance to murder. Awareness, foreseeability and another person's wrongdoing do not themselves establish purposeful participation. The case rejects a blanket publisher privilege, but also rejects using a general-law label as the entire constitutional analysis. Its participation rule is not a universal prerequisite for every otherwise justified speech regulation.
- **Sheets (1996):** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined protection against unjustified official dissemination of objectively intimate marital and family information. Scalia and Thomas rejected the asserted constitutional extension. The holding expressly left the private author's speech unregulated. None of those positions decides a legislature's power to protect conversational privacy through a private disclosure claim.
- **Denius (2001):** The same six Associates supported the bounded protection against unjustified official demands for intimate information, with coverage grounded in actual intimate content rather than a confidentiality label. Scalia and Thomas again objected to the constitutional extension. Denius neither regulates these private recipients nor decides that a collective-bargaining conversation is objectively intimate. Its requirement of actual justification informs the six Justices' assessment without importing a public-employment test.
- **Daily Mail, Florida Star and Landmark:** As described in the neutral packet, these pre-divergence authorities protect truthful information of public significance lawfully acquired by its publisher, subject to a sufficiently weighty and fitted justification. They leave the unlawful-source issue open. Their cited principles support an extension or a distinction; they do not supply a predetermined answer here.
- **Cohen and Branzburg:** Their rejection of a general press exemption remains relevant. The defendants' speech status does not immunize their independent misconduct. The distinction between independently binding duties and the direct disclosure restriction still must be addressed. New York Times v. United States concerns prior restraint and does not itself decide subsequent damages.

## Enhanced comparison of supported paths

### Path A — affirm the as-applied speech defense

The receiver's own lawful conduct matters. These defendants neither intercepted nor procured the call and are being charged for communicating accurate information about a contested public-school bargaining dispute. The recording's discussion of pressure on board members and possible violence supplies substantial public significance. After-settlement publication remains relevant to the public's evaluation of the dispute and its participants; the settlement does not make that information private in the constitutional sense merely by ending the immediate bargaining impasse.

The strongest objection is that dissemination causes a separate privacy injury and may reward an anonymous interceptor. Nonparticipation does not itself answer that objection. The affirmative path therefore must explain why the particular burden on these disclosures exceeds the justified protection here, rather than declare all innocent recipients immune from general law. Rice supplies no finding of participation; Turner requires examination of the actual fit; Sheets and Denius preserve serious intimate interests but do not constitutionalize all secret conversation or authorize these publication damages.

The breadth of the resulting rule is contested within this path. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg support protection defined by the certified conditions of truthful public-concern disclosure, lawful receipt and nonparticipation, limited to the statutory application before the Court. O'Connor and Breyer require the actual context and privacy interest to do substantive work and do not treat the public-concern description as automatically decisive in every conflicting privacy case.

The remedy is affirmance of the Third Circuit judgment in both dockets, including its direction to enter summary judgment for the disclosure defendants on the assumed facts. The statutory fact dispute does not require a trial on a claim defeated by this constitutional defense. No Act-wide invalidation, general injunction against interception regulation, adjudication of the interceptor's liability, or damages award follows.

### Path B — reverse because knowing dissemination itself may be regulated

The law selects the unlawful source and the recipient's knowledge, not a viewpoint, the bargaining position, or a particular message. Protection of candid private communication is substantial; deliberate later dissemination enlarges the original intrusion. Congress may reasonably prevent that injury and reduce the reward for interception through a source-based rule applicable to all knowing disclosers. Publication status does not make the recipient free of an otherwise adequately justified duty.

The strongest objection is that this imposes responsibility for an unknown stranger's wrongdoing on a person whose own acquisition was lawful, and suppresses political information rather than unlawful interception. Scalia and Thomas answer by treating the recipient's knowing disclosure as the separately regulated conduct: the injury the rule seeks to prevent continues at disclosure. They do not treat awareness as proof of aiding the original interception. They would uphold this application under content-neutral review, with prevention of dissemination injury sufficient in this setting and the reasonable incentive judgment additionally supportive. They make no empirical finding that the statute actually reduced interception or that anonymous interceptions would otherwise increase by a measurable amount.

The remedy is reversal of the constitutional ruling and summary-judgment direction in both dockets, followed by ordinary proceedings on statutory elements and preserved defenses. This path restores the possibility of liability; it does not find intentional interception, impose damages, or decide the amount of any award.

### Further-factfinding path

No Justice requires a factfinding remand solely to determine whether the unknown interceptor was a defendant's agent: the accepted question supplies nonparticipation. Nor does the absence of an empirical deterrence study itself require a new trial. The supported choices are the constitutional answer and its ordinary procedural consequence. A new material factual premise, if later authenticated, must return through neutral preparation rather than become a speculation supporting remand.

## Justice-specific commitments

### Stevens

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in both dockets for every challenged disclosure defendant. Truthful public-concern speech lawfully received by a person who neither participated in nor procured the unlawful interception cannot be subjected to these damages merely because the person knew or had reason to know the source was unlawful. The State must justify this direct publication burden; source illegality alone does not do so.

**Own authority and application:** His joins in Rice and the limited Turner predictive-review proposition support individual responsibility and actual judicial review without categorical hostility to legislative judgments. His Sheets opinion recognizes intimate privacy on its supported facts while expressly excluding regulation of the private author's speech; his Denius join retains the actual-content boundary. Those decisions permit recognition of privacy's importance while distinguishing the bargaining and safety-related information here from compelled exposure of objectively intimate life.

**Competing path and join limits:** The dissemination-injury argument is serious, but damages here suppress the very public information rather than remedy the defendants' participation in interception. He will not join an opinion asserting that privacy has no constitutional or legislative weight, that press status confers an exemption, or that no recipient can ever be liable after lawful receipt. He can join a fact-confined formulation that retains the four certified conditions and resolves these claims completely. No mandatory multifactor test or empirical-study requirement is needed.

### O'Connor

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in both dockets on this record. The justified public interest in disclosure is unusually concrete: the parties were directing a contentious dispute over public-school bargaining, and the recorded statement addressed coercive pressure and apparent violence against board members. Defendants lawfully received the accurate recording and did not cause the interception. In that setting, the particular damages burden is excessive relative to the privacy protection advanced.

**Own authority and application:** Her Turner general-classification join and disagreement with the cable application require an actual examination of what this restriction burdens, rather than either automatic deference or automatic condemnation of content-neutral laws. Her Rice join distinguishes the recipients from purposeful assistance. Her Sheets and Denius joins give confidentiality real legal significance; they are inconsistent with dismissing conversational privacy simply because disclosure is interesting to the public.

**Competing path and join limits:** Prevention of additional privacy injury is a valid justification in principle, and anonymous interception makes upstream enforcement incomplete. It is insufficient on this particular balance. She does not require a criminal true-threat finding, and does not treat the words as adjudicated unprotected speech. She will join a common opinion expressly grounded in these circumstances and recognizing the weight of ordinary conversational privacy. She withholds assent from a categorical rule that lawful acquisition plus any public-concern characterization defeats privacy law, and from a claim that deterrence never matters unless the recipient participated. If the lead opinion adopts that broader premise, her support is for the judgment and the compatible severable propositions only.

### Scalia

**Judgment and ground:** Reverse in both dockets and remand for statutory adjudication. A content-neutral prohibition on intentional disclosure by one who knows or has reason to know of an unlawful interception may protect against the distinct harm of dissemination, even when the discloser was not responsible for interception. The asserted defense does not defeat the claims as a matter of law.

**Own authority and application:** His Turner general-classification join distinguishes regulation based on a message from a rule keyed to unlawful acquisition. His disagreement with the must-carry application supplies no contrary conclusion about this source criterion. Rice preserves constitutional review but does not make purposeful aid to a prior crime necessary for every generally applicable restriction on publication. His Sheets and Denius dissents concern whether a particular privacy interest is constitutionally guaranteed; rejection of that constitutional extension does not disable legislative privacy protection.

**Competing path and join limits:** Lawful receipt and public significance supply the strongest speech objection. He nevertheless regards knowing disclosure as the defendant's own legally relevant conduct and would distinguish the lawful-source publication authorities on that basis. He joins neither an assertion that general applicability eliminates all First Amendment inquiry nor a finding that these defendants aided interception. The porch statement cannot establish an exception for unprotected threats without the required adjudication, and public relevance does not eliminate privacy as a matter of law. Liability, mental state, causation and damages remain unadjudicated.

### Kennedy

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in both dockets. Apply the strong protection of truthful public-concern publication to recipients whose own acquisition was lawful and who took no part in the unlawful interception. Protecting candid communication is legitimate, but the State has not justified imposing this direct speech burden on these nonparticipants simply to discourage a stranger's wrong or suppress its later communicative consequences.

**Own authority and application:** His Turner opinions require both respect for supported legislative predictions and independent attention to advancement and fit. His Rice opinion places the constitutional boundary at purposeful and actual assistance on the stipulated facts, while preserving ordinary publication. His Sheets and Denius joins protect genuine intimate interests without deriving secrecy from a label. The present public bargaining and safety-related communication presents neither those official practices nor a supported basis for transferring interception responsibility.

**Competing path and join limits:** He recognizes that dissemination can inflict additional injury and does not describe the law as content based merely because disclosure is speech. His objection concerns the actual application, not absence of legislative power to protect private conversation. He can join a single as-applied opinion stating the certified conditions and the concrete public interest. He rejects a general exemption for journalistic acquisition offenses, an assertion that all private speech interests disappear on receipt, and a demand for controlled empirical proof as a universal condition of intermediate scrutiny.

### Souter

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in both dockets on the lawful-receipt and nonparticipation extension of the truthful-publication principle. The content-neutral source criterion does not by itself justify damages for the accurate public information these respondents disclosed. The Government's interest must be assessed against this particular expressive burden.

**Own authority and application:** His Turner joins combine actual judicial examination with permissible predictive deference; his Rice join distinguishes purposeful assistance from awareness of possible illegality. His Sheets and Denius joins preserve a confined privacy interest rather than a right to control all information one has kept confidential. Those distinctions prevent a categorical privacy preference while recognizing that an intrusion already committed may carry continuing harm.

**Competing path and join limits:** Congress's demand-reduction reasoning is not irrational, but possibility of an incentive does not resolve whether the suppression of this public-concern speech is justified. Nor may a court invent a finding that these recipients encouraged the interception. He can join the certified-conditions rule and an opinion grounding its application in the bargaining context. He will not adopt an Act-wide rule, a press-only privilege, or a holding that Rice is an exhaustive list of every valid publication restriction. No separate writing is required if those limits are honored.

### Thomas

**Judgment and ground:** Reverse in both dockets and restore ordinary proceedings. The statutory condition regulates knowing dissemination of unlawfully intercepted material without choosing its substance. Prevention of the distinct disclosure injury and the reasonable incentive for interception support this bounded application under content-neutral review.

**Own authority and application:** His Turner general-classification join requires examination of the law's actual selection, and his opposition to that cable scheme does not transform every source-based rule into content discrimination. His Rice join rejects publisher immunity while retaining the difference between publication and purposeful criminal participation. His Sheets and Denius positions insist on a demonstrated constitutional source before recognizing new informational rights; they do not deny the legislature authority to protect privacy under a properly fitted enactment.

**Competing path and join limits:** The lack of unlawful acquisition by the respondents is important, but does not make their subsequent knowing disclosure lawful for every constitutional purpose. He would distinguish rather than overrule the lawful-publication precedents. He rejects an unexplained general-law exemption from constitutional scrutiny, an inferred participation finding, or a conclusion that privacy must prevail over every possible publication interest. The contemplated judgment holds only that the supplied First Amendment defense does not require judgment for these defendants and leaves ordinary liability questions open.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in both dockets for media and nonmedia defendants alike. The disclosed information concerns the conduct of a public dispute; the recipients lawfully acquired it and neither procured nor assisted interception. Knowledge of the stranger's unlawful conduct is insufficient justification for imposing these publication damages on this record.

**Own authority and application:** Her Turner general-classification join and dissent from the cable merits disposition preserve careful scrutiny of an actual editorial burden. Her Rice join rejects responsibility based solely on publication or awareness of another's misconduct. Her Sheets and Denius joins distinguish objectively intimate information and unjustified official action from a general secrecy preference. They support taking privacy seriously without making it a universal veto over accurate speech about public affairs.

**Competing path and join limits:** Dissemination is a further intrusion, but the law here reaches the public's access to relevant information through persons unconnected with the tap. She will not treat a missing empirical record as proof that interception incentives do not exist; the conclusion concerns the insufficient justification for this application. She can join the certified-conditions rule with its actual context and the equal treatment of Yocum. She refuses a categorical professional-press immunity, protection for procurement, or erasure of intimate privacy law.

### Breyer

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in both dockets on a contextual balance that gives actual force to both speech and privacy. These recipients were unconnected with the interception, and the accurate conversation concerns a major public-school bargaining dispute and language suggesting threatened violence against participants. The public interest in this disclosure outweighs the asserted private interest sufficiently to defeat these particular damages claims.

**Own authority and application:** His Turner (1997) join recognizes supported legislative predictions and rejects least-restrictive-means review, while still testing the particular burden. His Rice join preserves responsibility for purposeful assistance without treating awareness as participation. His Sheets and Denius joins protect meaningful confidentiality and actual intimate interests. Together they support a contextual resolution rather than a rule that one constitutional interest always prevails whenever information is described as public concern.

**Competing path and join limits:** Privacy fosters candid communication and the exchange of ideas; enforcement against dissemination can prevent an independent injury and rationally reduce interception incentives. Those interests remain valid even though insufficient here. He does not require proof of a criminal true threat or declare the recorded words unprotected. He will join an opinion that makes this setting and the relative interests operative and preserves the possible adequacy of privacy justifications elsewhere. He withholds assent from a universal public-concern trump, a categorical rejection of deterrence without recipient complicity, or an invented strict-scrutiny or mandatory-factor test. A separate explanation is necessary only if the final common opinion does not preserve those substantive qualifications.
