# Atwater v. City of Lago Vista — independent non-Stone commitments

Event: merits, April 24, 2001, No. 99-1408. Stage: independent modeling; not a Court disposition. All eight Associates participate. Sources: frozen April 24–25 neutral packet, neutral preparation receipt and Atwater entering-law slice. No matter-specific comparator or controlled position supplement was received. Research cutoff: April 24, 2001; same-date decisions are not entering precedent.

## Components and alternatives

Probable cause for the initial seatbelt stop is conceded. The question is the added handcuffed transport, booking and confinement for the known fine-only criminal offense. Ricci controls civil licensing custody and requires an actual enforcement connection; its application divided the Court. Knowles preserves the lawful-arrest predicate and cannot bootstrap the present arrest. Whren validates the ordinary stop while preserving scope and duration limits. McArthur requires justification for its additional residential restraint and attention to gravity, but is not a misdemeanor-booking decision.

One lawful path extends Ricci's actual-function requirement to a fine-only criminal offense and sets aside the categorical summary judgment for case-specific assessment. The strongest objection is administrability: officers must enforce observed crimes without first adjudicating every possible lesser measure. The extension answers that the offense is known to be fine-only and that reasonable practical processing may suffice; it requires neither citation whenever possible nor the least restrictive alternative.

A second path affirms on the supported ordinary processing account, without adopting unlimited custody from probable cause and state permission. Missing license and insurance documents, transport for criminal processing, booking and release after approximately an hour can establish a reasonable connection to identifying and processing the observed violation under the practical standard endorsed by the Ricci dissent. Its strongest objection is that custody-generated booking tasks cannot justify every custody automatically and the lower categorical standard did not establish whether station processing actually served a need. The affirming commitments accept practical enforcement processing as sufficient here without a finding that roadside identification was impossible, that Atwater posed flight or danger, or that citation could not work. They do not convert a disputed identification necessity into fact.

The broader alternative of making any observed criminal misdemeanor categorically sufficient for custody would be a separate extension, not application of Ricci. These commitments do not adopt that broader proposition. The criminal character and actual processing strengthen the record application but do not erase independent reasonableness review.

## Common limits and remedies

The eight agree that the initial stop is valid, that search-incident doctrine presupposes lawful arrest and that Ricci's actual scope remains binding. They disagree about whether the supported processing circumstances permit affirmance here. The four affirming Associates would join a bounded practical-application opinion, not the lower court's unlimited categorical formula. The four remanding Associates would join an extension and application-reservation opinion, not a present holding that the entire custody was unconstitutional. No majority or opinion assignment is supplied.

Personal immunity is independently measured in March 1997. Ricci, Knowles, McArthur and Wallis cannot themselves give retrospective notice. The remanding Justices preserve any actually available immunity defense rather than deny it by the current extension; the affirming Justices need not reach it. Municipal liability requires its own policy, fault and causation under Wallis and cannot borrow personal immunity. Neither group invalidates Atwater's uncontested seatbelt conviction, directs a damages award, finds a municipal policy, orders a new evidentiary hearing as such, or adjudicates an unpresented extraordinary-force theory. The exact identification and processing evidence would be required for a more categorical factual conclusion; the supplied record supports these bounded legal and application positions without inventing it.
