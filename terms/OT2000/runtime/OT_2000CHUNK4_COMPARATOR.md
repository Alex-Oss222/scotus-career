# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Ninth Circuit reversed and remanded summary judgment in an amended decision filed February 7, 2000. The panel divided 2-1 on the examination theory; no historical Supreme Court merits disposition occurred in this matter.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Reinhardt wrote the amended majority opinion joined by Judge Bright. Judge Rymer dissented, disputing the presentation and evidentiary foundation of the examination theory against the city while accepting further proceedings on removal.

# Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified. The historical lower-court anchor is the Eighth Circuit's May 1, 2000 affirmance, by a divided panel.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Judge Wollman wrote the majority opinion, joined by Judge Loken. Judge Lay dissented, objecting to the religious qualification for benefits and the religious institutions' role in coverage decisions.

**Historical Holding:** The majority upheld the revised Medicare and Medicaid program as a permissible accommodation of religious objections to medical treatment and found retained federal control sufficient. The dissent regarded the religiously exclusive benefit and delegated authority as constitutionally defective.

**Historical Authority:** This is the historical lower-court merits disposition under review, and does not establish controlling Supreme Court authority. Its accommodation and delegation reasoning does not displace Agostini v. Felton, Mitchell v. Helms, or Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet.

**Historical Sources:** [Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/212/1084/632477/).
