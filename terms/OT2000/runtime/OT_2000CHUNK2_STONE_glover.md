# Glover v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved; renewed 2026-11-08 by express user approval of this corrected-scope version.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-01-09.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the lower courts to decide the unresolved assistance claim without a minimum-duration or minimum-offense-level prejudice threshold. Resentencing follows only if deficient performance, a meritorious underlying objection, and the required causal probability are established. No immediate sentence reduction or release is ordered.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Every unlawful increment matters

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both questions presented in the petition. The Court of Appeals assumed that counsel performed deficiently, took as alleged that the grouping error raised Glover's offense level by two levels and his sentence by 6 to 21 months, and affirmed solely because that increase was not significant enough to establish prejudice. That ruling is the only ground of the judgment under review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an additional 6 to 21 months of imprisonment allegedly caused by counsel's Sentencing Guidelines error can be held insufficient to establish Strickland prejudice because it is not significant, and whether a two-level offense-level error is insufficient per se to establish prejudice even when it produces that additional imprisonment. Both questions assume, without deciding, deficient performance and the calculation error; neither presents the whole assistance claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Strickland v. Washington as preserved in Lockhart v. Fretwell and Williams v. Taylor.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The prejudice element contains no additional minimum-duration requirement for an attorney-caused increase in imprisonment, and none measured in offense levels; the ordinary reasonable-probability test applies.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Noncapital sentencing errors producing increased custody, while retaining performance and causal requirements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower rule confuses the magnitude of injury with whether a lawful outcome was probably lost. Lockhart v. Fretwell concerned an unavailable legal benefit, not judicial permission to disregard a shorter prison increment. The Court reaffirms the existing reasonable-probability standard and rejects the added significance condition. A threshold stated in offense levels fails for the same reason: the number of levels does not change whether there is a reasonable probability that a lawful shorter sentence was lost. Sentencing calculations, the contemporaneous record, counsel’s explanation, and competent testimony may establish the claim; no statistical account of sentencing outcomes is necessary. Whether the disputed grouping objection succeeds and whether counsel’s conduct was deficient remain application questions for remand, not unresolved legal standards. A generally applicable no-minimum-duration rule is required because even a short unlawful loss of liberty is the injury the Sixth Amendment protects against.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the grouping objection was correct and whether counsel's performance was deficient lie outside the questions presented, which assume them, and neither court in these §2255 proceedings decided them. Those matters, the full lawful sentencing calculation, and any properly preserved independent defense require application to the unadjudicated sentencing record. They remain for lower-court determination under the stated Strickland standard; they are not governed by a minimum-duration rule.
