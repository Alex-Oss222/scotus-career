# United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's refund judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the 1994 wage bases and rates to the challenged backpay. Reject the requested refund insofar as it rests on allocation to 1986 and 1987; resolve any purely computational implementation below without reopening the settled payment facts.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual payment as the statutory tax event

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory tax allocation. A broader constitutional theory of unfair delay is neither necessary to the judgment nor a substitute for the enacted tax structure. No general ruling about the independent binding force of every revenue ruling is necessary because the construction here rests on the operative statutes, regulations and their established administration.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which tax year's rates and wage bases apply to these wages, and whether the asserted administrative construction is within the governing law.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing statutory and administrative rules to a new allocation question. Preserve Auer v. Robbins and Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala as governing law even where an independent textual construction can decide the result; do not announce a new general deference regime.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For these FICA and FUTA provisions, back wages are attributed to the year actually paid. The regulations' calendar-year treatment and settled administrative construction confirm that reading; the 1994 payment therefore uses 1994 tax rates and limits.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers this employer's delayed compensation and the tax provisions presented. It does not require matching attribution for every benefit program or every statute that uses the word wages.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text identifies payment and annual receipts as operative events. Benefit eligibility serves a separate statutory function, including credit for the worker's service history. A single remuneration can accordingly have different legally specified timing for tax collection and benefit computation. Administrative consistency strengthens a construction already fitted to the text and permits predictable payroll administration; it cannot alone cure contrary text. The Company's avoidance of extra tax in the earlier years is a consequence of its preferred interpretation, not a statutory entitlement. Nierotko is distinguished by the statutory function of benefits credit, not ignored because it used similar words. The combination is text read in its tax setting and the established regulatory implementation; the tax provisions' actual-payment structure is decisive. That makes it unnecessary to alter Auer or Thomas Jefferson University in a case where the result does not depend on treating an unsupported agency preference as law. Administrative consistency confirms, rather than replaces, the enacted timing rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual benefit calculations are outside the question presented and remain for their own claims and records.
