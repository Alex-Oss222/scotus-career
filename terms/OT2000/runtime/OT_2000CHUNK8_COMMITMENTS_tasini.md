## Statute and separable components

**C1 — Section 201(c)'s default privilege.** The copyright in a contribution is distinct from copyright in the collective whole. Absent an express transfer of copyright or rights under it, the collective-work owner is presumed to obtain only the privilege to reproduce and distribute the contribution as part of that particular collective work, any revision of that work, or a later collective work in the same series. Section 103(b) confines compilation copyright to the compiler's contribution without enlarging rights in preexisting material; §106(1) and (3) identify the reproduction and distribution rights implicated here. Section 101 treats a collective work as separately copyrightable contributions assembled into a whole and defines copies without a limitation to existing technology. Media neutrality answers the medium question; it does not determine whether the contribution is reproduced as part of the specified whole.

**C2 — NEXIS.** Text articles are individually searchable and retrieved with source identifiers, without the surrounding issue's contents and presentation. Historical source information and a user's ability to gather articles from an issue are different from the publisher reproducing the contribution as part of that issue or a recognizable revision.

**C3 — New York Times OnDisc.** The product similarly offers searchable text from the Times. Its single-publisher provenance does not itself resolve whether the individual article remains part of a particular edition's revision.

**C4 — General Periodicals OnDisc.** Images preserve particular printed pages, including more original presentation than the text products, but the product does not present the complete periodical issue. The actual page image must be considered separately; neither the image label nor the use of a CD-ROM decides the privilege.

**C5 — Whitford.** His express contract is distinct from the general arrangements. First-publication rights do not themselves authorize the subsequent database license. The separate nonexclusive republication-license clause requires payment of fifty percent of net proceeds; the Time-publication republication clause requires prevailing rates. Time did not compensate him and did not rely on those conditional clauses as its defense. An infringement claimant alleging an unauthorized use need not add a contract count merely because the asserted defense concerns a license. The actual relationship between the contract and any surviving statutory privilege is a different question from rewriting either conditional clause into an unconditional license.

**C6 — Consequences and remedy.** Failure of the privilege permits affirmance of the authors' judgment on the challenged uses, with individualized monetary and equitable implementation still to occur. It does not mandate erasure of every freelance contribution from every electronic archive. If the statutory privilege is construed broadly instead, the resulting reversal must still address the unadjudicated §201(d) transferability argument and the express contract posture; a broad reading does not by itself justify final judgment for every publisher on every defense.

Campbell's actual fair-use rule and Quality King's actual first-sale rule remain distinct. There is no fair-use adjudication here merely because databases aid research, and resale of a particular lawfully owned copy does not authorize making new copies. Stewart preserves separate rights in underlying material and the need for a valid permission, but its renewal/derivative-work question does not decide the meaning of revision in §201(c).

## The two live statutory paths

**Contextual-revision path.** A revision may change layout, medium or content; no facsimile or identical-format requirement follows. But the contribution must still be reproduced and distributed as part of the original collective work or its recognizable revision. None of these products meets that condition on the proved presentation. NEXIS and NYTO deliver freestanding text articles. GPO's preserved page does not restore the contribution's membership in the complete issue; page fidelity and edition identity are different. Source labels identify where an article once appeared, and user-generated groupings do not change what the publishers themselves have reproduced and distributed. Treating the entire database as a revision of each source issue would consume much of the author's expressly reserved right to license independent reuse.

**Strongest contrary case.** A permissible revision need not preserve all original content or visual placement. A database can preserve the publisher's original selection, source identity and organized record even while a search retrieves one item; digital retrieval is comparable to a reader locating one article in a paper or microform collection. A rule tied too closely to display threatens statutory media neutrality and valuable historical completeness. The contextual path answers that this objection establishes neither a fixed-page requirement nor the privilege for the products actually shown. The relevant legal difference is reproduction of the article as a separately available work outside a recognizable collective issue, not the use of digital storage or search tools. Archive policy cannot supply a permission Congress withheld, and the relief question remains separate.

**Archival-revision path.** The original publisher's selected content can remain part of a revised archival collection despite altered storage and individual retrieval. On this reading, identifiable source and issue associations within the publishers' continuous archival products preserve the relationship that §201(c) requires. NEXIS aggregates such collections; NYTO retains a single publisher's collection; GPO's source-page images provide an additional continuity feature. The privilege would not encompass an unrelated new anthology or an author's work sold independently without that archival relationship. The rule arises from a broader reading of revision, not a free-floating public-benefit exemption or elimination of contribution ownership.

**Strongest contrary case.** Source provenance alone can accompany almost any unauthorized republication, and the new database collection is not each original issue. The archival construction risks depriving the statutory words particular collective work of limiting force. Its proponents regard the publisher-originated archival aggregation, original editorial selection and continuing issue association as more than a bibliographic label. They accept that a bare individual resale or unrelated compilation is different and that an actual restrictive agreement remains enforceable. Whether those limits sufficiently distinguish the three products is the central disagreement; it is not resolved by counting electronically stored articles or presuming that every possible search recreates an issue.

That rule allows genuinely revised electronic editions and does not impose a page-for-page requirement.
