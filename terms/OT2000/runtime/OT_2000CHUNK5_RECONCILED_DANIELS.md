## Reconciliation and actual entering law

The actual Custis decision limits the ACCA sentencing forum, preserves complete unconstitutional counsel deprivation, and expressly reserves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review circumstances. Section 2255 therefore requires its own holding. Maleng and Garlotte distinguish present federal custody from custody under a wholly expired independent judgment. Parke supplies rebuttable regularity and lawful proof allocations, not a universal validity finding. Nichols concerns an originally valid fine-only conviction and does not decide this channel. Fiore and Glover preserve distinct constitutional, procedural and causal inquiries without creating an expired-predicate review forum.

These distinctions do not materially displace the historical premises of the four affirming Associates. Daniels presents involuntary counseled pleas and ineffective representation, no established invalidation, and no claimed external obstruction to timely original review. The record does not establish the alleged defects. The explicit current-sentence remedy is a serious statutory counterargument, but that counterargument was also before the historical Court; it does not by itself justify a different result.

The provisional judgment positions survive. Two material rationale qualifications are restored after full comparator review. Scalia's additional statutory objection is stronger than a neutral reservation of all hypothetical exceptions. Breyer's statutory and practical objection seeks reconsideration of Custis's original sentencing restriction; it is not interchangeable with an unqualified join in Souter's construction of section 2255. No departure is claimed for those refinements. The narrower Court holding available on the actual facts does not require every affirming Justice to embrace another Justice's additional theory.
