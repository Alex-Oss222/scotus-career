# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 394; No. 99-1884
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-25 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a state prisoner may challenge his current sentence under 28 U.S.C. § 2254 on the ground that completed prior state convictions were unconstitutional, including when a timely state postconviction challenge to those convictions has never been adjudicated?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Third Circuit accepted jurisdiction over Coss’s challenge to his current sentence and remanded after finding prejudice from ineffective assistance at the earlier trial. The district court had found ineffective performance but denied relief for lack of prejudice.
* **Relief Requested:** The district attorney seeks reversal of the decision permitting the indirect challenge. Coss seeks review of the old convictions as a basis for correcting the present sentence, with appropriate relief if constitutional invalidity and sentencing effect are established.
* **Material Record:** Coss’s 1986 Pennsylvania convictions for simple assault, institutional vandalism and criminal mischief produced two consecutive terms of six months to one year. He filed a state postconviction petition in June 1987 asserting ineffective assistance. Counsel was appointed and the prosecutor answered, but no ruling followed, apparently for almost fourteen years. The parties could not explain the lapse. After completing those sentences, Coss was convicted of aggravated assault in 1990 and ultimately resentenced to six to twelve years. The sentencing judge accepted his objection that the 1986 misdemeanors formed one transaction, removing their effect on the numerical prior-record score. The judge nevertheless referred to his criminal record in selecting a sentence within the range. The federal district court and Third Circuit found consideration of the earlier convictions; dissenting appellate judges disputed whether they affected the actual sentence. [Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-394.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The petition must challenge present custody rather than seek a freestanding writ against an independently expired sentence. Present custody, reviewability of the old conviction, exhaustion or an applicable statutory excuse, constitutional invalidity and effect on present confinement are distinct. The unresolved state petition bears directly on realistic availability and responsibility for delay. It cannot be treated either as adjudicated on the merits or as necessarily abandoned by Coss.
* **Relevant Dates:** The original petition was filed in 1987; the federal petition was filed in September 1994. Apply the historically applicable habeas framework, including the significance of its pre-AEDPA filing, rather than indiscriminately applying later procedural amendments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Daniels v. United States addresses the federal-prisoner vehicle on the same historical date and has its own listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Daniels v. United States is a material same-day dependency. Any earlier effective holding must be considered with due regard for the different review history and statutory vehicle.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The petition’s current-sentence construction must be addressed before the merits of the old trial. An exhaustion exception requires the applicable statute and actual ineffective or unavailable state process. Findings concerning actual sentencing influence require proper appellate review rather than fresh assumptions about how a state judge would have sentenced.

### Issue 1: Present custody and the unresolved review channel

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Garlotte v. Fordice addresses a continuous unexpired consecutive series; these independently completed sentences are materially different. Custis v. United States leaves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases undecided while preserving a complete-denial-of-counsel exception in its ACCA setting.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Custody and collateral-review principles distinguish jurisdiction over present restraint from the validity and permissible use of earlier judgments. Regularity is not a finding that a pending constitutional challenge has received a meaningful decision.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-394.pdf). The applicable statute recognizes the significance of unavailable or ineffective state corrective process; invocation requires the actual circumstances.
* **Material Other Authority:** Maleng v. Cook permits a current-sentence construction while leaving indirect review’s extent open. Strickland v. Washington supplies separate deficient-performance and prejudice requirements. United States v. Tucker identifies the need to connect an invalid prior conviction with the present sentence; it does not authorize speculative relief.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No controlling decision transforms Custis v. United States into a universal bar against this § 2254 petition. Conversely, dissatisfaction with an earlier result or a decision to forgo available review does not automatically justify another forum.
* **Open Point:** The availability of current-sentence review where state inaction frustrated a properly presented earlier challenge, and the required finding that the predicate actually adversely affected current custody.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The prosecutor invokes finality, expired custody and the separate sentencing calculus, emphasizing that the 1986 convictions contributed no points to the ultimate range. Coss invokes his existing confinement, the unadjudicated 1987 filing and the sentencing judge’s reference to his prior criminal record. His strongest vehicle argument concerns an apparently pursued but ineffective corrective process. His weakest inference is that any mention of the record necessarily increased the sentence. The prosecutor’s corresponding overstatement would be that removing points necessarily proves no effect on discretionary selection within the range.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A categorical extension of the ACCA sentencing rule would require a new holding. A narrower course could recognize a current-sentence avenue where meaningful original review was defeated without the prisoner’s fault, require the remaining procedural conditions, and then examine both constitutional error and actual sentencing effect. The Court could dispose of the case on a properly established absence of effect; it cannot simply convert disputed appellate inferences into uncontested record facts.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Permit a bounded present-custody challenge when a properly pursued original constitutional challenge received no meaningful adjudication through no fault of the prisoner. Require a constitutionally invalid predicate and an adverse effect on the current sentence before granting relief.

**Material Uncertainty:** The reason for the state court’s long inaction is unresolved. Actual sentencing influence is contested despite the settled elimination of points. These gaps materially affect both access to the proposed review channel and entitlement to relief.
