## Law-entering snapshot and reach

Jefferson v. City of Tarrant, December 9, 1997, supplies the controlling section 1257 rule. A settled federal proposition is insufficient while meaningful state proceedings remain unless a preserved Cox practical-finality route applies: practical completion; necessary survival of the question through every further outcome; effective later review unavailable whatever that outcome; or litigation-preclusive reversal coupled with serious threatened erosion of federal policy. Ritchie's practical treatment does not reduce to absolute impossibility of another appeal, but neither creates general review of every state law-of-the-case ruling.

The Fourth Amendment merits remain governed by the surviving Belton and Chimel rules, Knowles's actual-custody predicate, Whren's independent justification for later intrusions, and the subsequent actual-enforcement holdings in Ricci, Atwater and Sullivan. ## Supported competing paths

**Dismiss for lack of jurisdiction.** Florida may still obtain admission under Chimel. The remaining findings therefore decide a real admissibility question, rather than implement a foreordained result. If Florida loses, its available pretrial appeal permits later presentation before an acquittal could foreclose review. Reversal of the Belton limitation would allow evidence in the prosecution, not preclude the prosecution itself. Ordinary delay supplies no separate threatened federal-policy destruction. None of the four Cox routes is established.

The strongest objection is that the Florida Supreme Court has conclusively settled the Belton question and repeated appeals cannot change that answer. The response concerns the judgment being reviewed: the State has not yet lost admissibility, may never need its Belton theory, and retains an effective pretrial route if the separate ground fails. This is substantive remaining adjudication, not mere repetition of a final suppression result.

**Exercise practical-finality jurisdiction, then decide the search question.** The best case invokes Ritchie and the danger that criminal suppression questions evade later review. That path fails on this procedural record because later pretrial review is available and no conclusive suppression order has yet emerged. No party concession establishes the State's defeat under Chimel. It would be improper to manufacture that concession or the arrestee's access to the car to obtain jurisdiction. Because this path does not clear the threshold, no provisional merits vote is supplied.
