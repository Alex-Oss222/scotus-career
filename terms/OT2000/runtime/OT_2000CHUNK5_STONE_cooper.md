# OT_2000CHUNK5

# Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the limited question of appellate review: abuse-of-discretion review of the trial court's record-based punitive-amount assessment is permissible, with independent correction of legal error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not vacate solely to require a fresh appellate valuation of the punitive award. Leave the reviewed judgment operative, including the undisturbed $50,000 compensation. The Court neither fixes a constitutional maximum nor reinstates the functional-design injunction; its decision concerns the appellate allocation actually granted.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Independent legal review with preserved factual functions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The grant is expressly limited to the standard of review of a trial court's ruling on a constitutional challenge to punitive damages. Resolve that allocation, including the civil jury's role. The grant does not require reopening Gore's substantive rule or deciding a new dollar limit.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What standard governs appellate review of the trial court's ruling on the constitutionality of a punitive-damages award?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gore as the existing substantive premise while declining to extend Bajakajian's de novo forfeiture assessment to this jury award. Extend Gasperini's allocation of record-based amount review to the presented punitive setting, preserving independent review of governing law and constitutional procedure. Stone continues to reject a freestanding judicial power to choose a preferred punitive number; he does not purport to overrule Gore through this limited grant.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An appellate court independently determines the governing constitutional rule, corrects reliance on legally impermissible grounds, and reviews the trial court's assessment of the punitive amount for abuse of discretion in light of that rule and the supported record. Meaningful review does not require a second independent appellate valuation of the same award. The Ninth Circuit did not err merely by using abuse-of-discretion review.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns appellate review of a civil jury's punitive award after actual trial-court review. It preserves review of constitutional legal errors, unsupported premises, and failures to examine amount; it does not immunize an award merely because a judge approved it. It leaves Gore's governing substantive boundary and Oberg's meaningful-review requirement in force.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The institutional allocation of civil factfinding and punishment assessment carries decisive weight. Selecting a punitive amount combines judgments about culpability, deterrence, the course of the misconduct, and the evidence heard by the jury and trial judge. An appellate court can enforce legal boundaries without treating that assessment as a number it must calculate afresh. Gasperini's reason for respecting the trial court's supported valuation extends to this allocation even though its particular substantive standard concerned compensation. Bajakajian concerned a government-imposed forfeiture under the Excessive Fines Clause; its review rule does not itself answer the distinct civil-jury allocation. Ornelas likewise supplies an analogy about appellate legal supervision, not a universal command for de novo treatment of every mixed question. Oberg requires real examination of amount and lawful correction, which the trial and appellate review here supplied; it did not prescribe repeated independent valuations. Gore remains controlling as to substantive excessiveness despite Stone's disagreement. Disregarding its guideposts, punishing lawful copying as such, inventing threatened losses, or relying on wealth without connection to the wrong would be legal or record errors reviewable under this approach. But a court need not replace a supported assessment of intentional deceptive promotion simply because the ratio is ninety to one. The limited grant asks which institution reviews and how; it neither establishes those supposed errors as facts nor authorizes a new determination of the maximum award. This rule preserves meaningful protection against arbitrary punishment while respecting the jury and the trial court's actual work.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether Gore's substantive excessiveness rule should be overruled, the precise permissible amount, and Oregon's allocation of part of the award lie outside this expressly limited review-standard grant.
