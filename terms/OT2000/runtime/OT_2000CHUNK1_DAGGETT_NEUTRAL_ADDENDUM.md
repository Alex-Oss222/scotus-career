# Daggett: additional neutral source detail

Source: First Circuit opinion, March 7, 2000, 205 F.3d 445, paragraphs 9–12, 87–94 and footnotes 4, 29–30, read in full including all footnotes at https://openjurist.org/205/f3d/445/beverly-daggett-v-commission-on-governmental-ethics-and-election-practices . Its record recitations establish the following additional detail; its legal conclusions are not controlling law.

Participating legislative candidates qualify through seed-money and district-voter contributions, then forgo ordinary private contributions and spend public disbursements. The matching mechanism responds to private opponents’ receipts above the initial public grant and relevant independent expenditures favoring those opponents or opposing the participant. Supplements stop at twice the initial grant, so total public funding is capped at three times that grant.

Footnote 29 reports a regulatory adjustment: independent expenditures expressly favoring the participant or opposing the private opponent are deducted from matching disbursements under chapter 3, section 6.3.B(2). That adjustment operates after the opponent’s receipts exceed the participant’s initial grant; it does not diminish the initial grant. Thus the challenged scheme does not simply ignore independent spending favorable to the participant. No statutory equivalence between independent spending and coordinated contributions follows.

The opinion supplies no severability adjudication. A proposed partial invalidation must reserve the implementation of actual severability law pending its identification; it cannot invent a replacement trigger, ceiling, or grant.
