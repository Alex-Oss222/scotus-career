## Record recitations to add

1. Atwater was a longstanding Lago Vista resident. The published source reports sixteen years of residence and identifies Record 376 as its support. Turek had encountered her before and knew from that encounter that she was a local resident. The source separately describes her as known and established in the community. No conclusion about actual flight risk follows merely from residence.
2. Although she lacked the physical driver's license and insurance papers and said her purse had been stolen, she supplied her driver's-license number and address. The source identifies Record 386. Missing papers therefore must not be converted into a finding that she supplied no identifying information or could not be identified. The actual verification or processing significance remains a separate inquiry.
3. Approximately three months earlier, Turek had stopped Atwater because he believed her son was not wearing a seatbelt. He then realized the child was belted, although seated on the vehicle's armrest. Atwater acknowledged that seating position was unsafe, and Turek gave a verbal warning. The source cites Record 379, 420 and 703. The previous encounter does not establish a previous seatbelt violation or a prior conviction.
4. The plaintiff-favorable recitation describes approximately fifteen-mile-per-hour driving, in daylight, on a residential street without other traffic; it cites Record 380. It also reports that her only prior traffic citation was more than ten years earlier, for a lane-change signal, citing Record 378. These facts do not decide the danger associated with the observed unbelted occupants or eliminate the conceded offense.
5. The recitation reports that Atwater promptly accepted responsibility for the present seatbelt violation and apologized, citing Record 381, 384 and 420. This is not an adjudicated prediction of how she would have acted after citation or release.
6. The City's identified case-specific justifications were enforcement of child-safety laws and encouraging appearance for trial, with its merits brief at page 15 cited as the source. These are litigating positions, not findings that either purpose required custody. They do not themselves establish that additional grounds were preserved or waived.
