# Texas v. Cobb

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Texas v. Cobb
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 162; No. 99-1702
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-02 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether attachment of the Sixth Amendment right to counsel for a charged burglary prohibits police questioning, after a valid Miranda waiver, about factually related but separately defined murders not yet charged.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Texas Court of Criminal Appeals reversed a capital-murder conviction and remanded because it extended the attached burglary counsel right to offenses very closely related factually.
* **Relief Requested:** Texas seeks reversal of that extension and rejection of the Sixth Amendment suppression ground. Cobb seeks to retain the judgment setting aside his conviction.
* **Material Record:** Cobb confessed to a home burglary while denying knowledge of the disappearance of a woman and child. He was indicted for burglary and appointed counsel. Later he confessed the killings to his father, who informed police. Officers obtained a Miranda waiver and a confession to the murders, which became evidence in the capital prosecution. The state appellate court treated the factual relationship between the burglary and murders as sufficient to extend the counsel right. [Texas v. Cobb](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-162.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The counsel right had attached to the burglary. The murders were not then charged. A valid waiver of custodial-interrogation rights is a stated premise of the question; it is not an answer to a distinct attached Sixth Amendment protection. The question concerns admissibility in the murder case, not use of statements against Cobb on the charged burglary.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Identify attachment, the legally defined offense, deliberate elicitation and any valid waiver through their separate doctrines. Legal interpretation is independent; the case does not authorize a new factual finding about an unpreserved coercion allegation.

### Issue 1: The charged-offense boundary

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** McNeil v. Wisconsin distinguishes the offense-specific Sixth Amendment right from the Fifth Amendment protection governing custodial interrogation. Maine v. Moulton protects the charged prosecution against deliberate circumvention even when police investigate other offenses. Brewer v. Williams concerns elicitation after the counsel right attached.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Attachment, scope, and waiver present distinct Sixth Amendment questions. Related facts alone do not automatically establish identical legal offenses; investigating an uncharged offense supplies no license to use deliberately elicited charged-case evidence contrary to the attached right.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Texas v. Cobb](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-162.pdf). Miranda v. Arizona supplies an independent interrogation protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Blockburger v. United States and Brown v. Ohio identify same-offense and lesser-included relationships in the double-jeopardy setting. Importing that elements definition into counsel scope is an additional legal step requiring express justification.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing offense-specific rule does not itself resolve every choice between factual-transaction and elements-based definitions. Charged-case protections remain real even when an officer invokes a separate investigative subject.
* **Open Point:** Whether the same-offense boundary should include legally identical and lesser-included offenses but exclude the separately defined burglary and murders despite their shared episode.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**State:** Counsel appointment for burglary cannot immunize a suspect from questioning about separate murders; the distinct elements and valid custodial waiver permit investigation. **Cobb:** A police investigation of the same episode can circumvent counsel on the existing charge. Factual integration makes a narrow elements rule vulnerable to charging manipulation and strategic questioning. **Material response:** The Court must distinguish extending attachment to another prosecution from enforcing the already attached right within the charged prosecution. Neither concern eliminates the other.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

An elements-based rule supports reversing the closely-related-facts holding, while preserving other properly presented grounds. A transactional rule could sustain the state judgment but would require a clear new definition and an administrable boundary. A purpose-only approach must confront the offense-specific premise and cannot equate all investigation with use in an existing prosecution.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A right attached to a charged offense also covers legally the same offense, including lesser-included counterparts, but factual relationship alone does not extend it to another offense. Apply only to the offenses and confession at issue.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# Wallis v. Spencer

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-04-02. **Date status:** organizationally assigned.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Wallis v. Spencer. **Citation or Docket:** Ninth Circuit No. 97-55579; 202 F.3d 1126. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of summary judgment for a municipality in a family-removal and medical-examination civil-rights action. **Simulated Event Date:** April 2, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term.

**Question Presented:**

1. Whether the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments permit officials to remove children from parental custody without judicial authorization absent specific, articulable evidence of imminent serious bodily harm and a removal reasonably limited to averting that harm?
2. Whether officials may subject children in protective custody to invasive examinations for evidence of sexual abuse without parental consent or prior judicial authorization when neither urgent medical need nor imminent loss of evidence requires immediate examination?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses both questions presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment entered by the Southern District of California for the City of Escondido. Its amended opinion was filed February 7, 2000. The federal claims concerned removal, prolonged separation, and invasive examinations; related California claims also survived the panel's disposition. **Relief Requested:** The city seeks reinstatement of judgment; the parents and children seek adjudication of their constitutional and related claims against the city. The individual officers were not defendants in the operative municipal appeal.

**Material Record:** Bill and Becky Wallis's children, Lauren, five, and Jessie, nearly three, were removed late September 21 or early September 22, 1991. A report originating with an aunt hospitalized for serious mental illness alleged that their father intended a ritual sacrifice at the autumn equinox. The referral disclosed the informant's delusions. The family had broken contact with her after an earlier false report. Officers asserted that they had been told a pickup order existed, but no court had issued one, and the child-protection investigator denied authorizing removal. Evidence described a departmental practice of accepting telephone assertions of orders without inspecting them. These are contested liability facts, not a finding that every officer knew no order existed. [Wallis v. Spencer](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/202/202.F3d.1126.html).

Three days later the children underwent invasive anal and vaginal examinations and photographs for evidence of abuse, without parental consent, notice, or judicial authorization. Evidence identified the city as the requesting agency; the institutional responsibility and sufficiency of the municipal-policy theory are disputed. A juvenile court subsequently rejected the occult-sacrifice explanation but relied on a physician's abuse report to continue separation. Later specialist review undermined that report; the children returned after approximately two and one-half months. Liability for each period requires its own causation analysis, including the intervening judicial proceeding. Neither later exoneration alone nor an earlier emergency automatically settles every stage.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Municipal attribution is separate from a constitutional deprivation; the city cannot borrow personal qualified immunity. The dissent argued that the examination-policy theory had not been pleaded, argued, or developed against this defendant. The grant does not cure that preservation problem. Summary judgment requires favorable supported facts without resolving credibility. The later California examination statute cannot establish what law governed the 1991 conduct. **Relevant Dates:** Conduct September 1991; amended lower decision February 7, 2000. **Companion or Related Matters:** Settled claims against county participants and dismissed claims against the physician supply no additional listed case. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 2, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Denius v. Dunlap, organizationally placed January 17, concerns confidential records; Ferguson v. City of Charleston, historically placed March 21, concerns medical searches; and Illinois v. McArthur, historically placed February 20, concerns the justification and duration of a temporary seizure. Their distinct settings do not predetermine child removal or bodily examinations. Any intervening holding on confidential records, medical searches, or temporary seizures must be applied within its factual and legal scope.

### Issue 1: Removal, custody, and municipal responsibility

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Troxel v. Granville recognizes parents' protected authority over care and upbringing and rejects displacement merely because a judge prefers another arrangement. Its fractured portions establish no general strict-scrutiny, unfitness, or universal harm rule. Collins v. Harker Heights separates deprivation from municipal attribution and distinguishes government custody from ordinary voluntary employment. Carter v. City of Philadelphia requires an identified policy, custom, or sufficiently culpable training omission with actual causal connection; personal immunity does not protect the city. Plakas v. Drinski preserves separate underlying-right and municipal-causation inquiries.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The municipal-liability standard requires institutional responsibility, not respondeat superior. The summary-judgment and family-liberty standards do not resolve a genuine dispute about whether an order existed, what officials knew, or what a policy caused.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments, § 1983, and applicable California remedial law provide distinct claims and defenses. A state discretionary immunity cannot extinguish a federal right of action.

**Material Other Authority:** Wilson v. Layne distinguishes what an existing warrant authorizes from unrelated intrusions. Graham v. Connor requires circumstances-specific reasonableness where the Fourth Amendment governs. Their actual holdings do not supply a complete child-removal emergency rule.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Protected family interests and municipal attribution are established. Government's protective purpose does not itself establish a valid seizure or adjudicate municipal causation.

**Open Point:** The precise imminent-harm threshold, time available for a judge, permissible duration, and relationship between the children's seizure claims and parents' family-integrity claims require a new application.

### Issue 2: Examinations and preservation

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Troxel v. Granville and Collins v. Harker Heights preserve the distinct family and custodial interests. Carter v. City of Philadelphia requires proof tying the challenged examination to this city's policy or actionable omission.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** A lawful initial seizure does not automatically adjudicate the lawfulness of another bodily intrusion; claim preservation, attribution, and causation remain necessary.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments govern the claimed bodily examination and family interference; the precise claim and right holder must be identified.

**Material Other Authority:** Conn v. Gabbert rejects conversion of a temporary occupational interruption into an occupational-exclusion claim without eliminating independently available search claims. It requires separate analysis of the occupational-liberty and search claims and does not decide the lawfulness of a bodily examination.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The cited precedents do not confer unrestricted examination authority on protective-custody officials.

**Open Point:** Whether the examination issue was preserved against the city, and the complete policy evidence, must be resolved before a binding merits disposition on that theory.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**City's Position:** The alarming report justified immediate protection, officers reasonably relied on reported judicial authority, and neither removal nor examination was caused by a constitutionally actionable municipal policy. The city challenges extending this appeal to an undeveloped examination theory and contests responsibility for separation continued by juvenile-court order.

**Family's Position:** A visibly unreliable accusation, no actual order, and no specific evidence of imminent harm cannot justify nighttime removal. Investigative bodily examinations needed consent or judicial authorization because neither treatment nor preservation of transient evidence required immediate action. Evidence of accepting unverified order reports and requesting examinations warrants factual testing of municipal responsibility.

**Reasoning Below and Counterposition:** The panel treated the record as permitting both constitutional violations and municipal attribution, and rejected reliance on officers' personal immunities. The dissent accepted that the removal-policy theory could proceed but disputed the examination theory's preservation and evidentiary basis. That disagreement is a vehicle issue, not proof that the examinations were constitutionally proper.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Available Merits Paths:** Affirm the reversal on removal and remand fact questions under a specified emergency standard; reinstate judgment if the properly viewed evidence cannot establish a deprivation or municipal connection; or separate the two claims, resolving removal while limiting the examination remand to preserved issues. If the examination claim is properly presented, the Court may require consent or a judicial order subject to genuine urgency, with independent consideration of parental participation.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** Survival of summary judgment establishes no damages amount or final finding of abuse of authority. Remand must address each actor, policy, intrusive act, and period of separation. State claims require their own immunity analysis. Neither dismissal of the physician nor failure of an individual damages defense determines city liability. A court cannot use an abstract constitutional ruling to rescue an unpreserved municipal theory.

# Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 189; No. 00-152
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-17 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether California deprives a public-works subcontractor of property without due process by directing withholding of disputed contract payments without an administrative hearing when ordinary judicial contract remedies remain available?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reinstating its judgment affirming summary judgment, a declaration of unconstitutionality and an injunction for G & G against California's withholding provisions.
* **Relief Requested:** The state officials seek reversal of the federal declaration and injunction. G & G seeks an enforceable opportunity to contest the wage violations and withholding, rather than an adjudication of the underlying wage account by this Court.
* **Material Record:** California required prevailing wages on public projects and allowed awarding bodies to withhold wages and statutory penalties from prime contractors when a subcontractor violated the requirements. On three projects, state labor officials directed withholding for alleged violations by G & G; the primes consequently withheld corresponding amounts from it. G & G disputed the violations and brought this §1983 suit. The statutory contract action belonged to the contractor or its assignee. Assignment could permit the subcontractor to sue the awarding body; an ordinary suit against the prime also appeared available. The record does not establish that every available contract route was closed, nor that an assignment was actually refused. The dispute concerns payment allegedly owing, not seizure of money already possessed or cancellation of an occupational license. [Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-189.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** State-directed withholding supplies governmental involvement, unlike a private insurer's independent choice. The existence of a contractual property interest can be assumed in deciding what process is due. Availability and adequacy of a remedy are elements of that inquiry, not a general exhaustion prerequisite for §1983. The prior reconsideration order establishes no additional merits holding.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment permits no adverse factual inferences against the subcontractor. The constitutional question concerns the process available for this kind of interest; it does not determine the state-law balance, penalty liability or merits of the asserted wage violations.

### Issue 1: Property and an available contract adjudication

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan distinguishes a general statutory entitlement from an unconditional entitlement to payment of a disputed claim. Its separate state-action ruling does not immunize actual state commands; its remedial limits preserve proper adjudication of an existing claim.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Procedural due process requires an identified property interest, an attributable deprivation, and adequate process appropriate to that interest. They do not constitutionalize every unpaid invoice or eliminate a protected claim because payment is disputed.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and §1983 govern the asserted federal claim; the historically applicable California Labor Code supplies prevailing-wage, withholding and contractor-or-assignee recovery mechanisms.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mathews v. Eldridge supplies contextual procedural analysis. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property concerns deprivation of existing possessory control. Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co. cautions against destroying a claim through the State's own deficient adjudicative machinery.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An alleged contract entitlement is analytically distinct from immediate possession, licensing or an established benefit award. A judicial remedy must be genuinely available and capable of determining the underlying dispute.
* **Open Point:** Whether ordinary breach-of-contract adjudication fully supplies the process constitutionally due for this particular government-directed withholding, without a separate administrative hearing.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The absence of a hearing within the labor statute was treated as a constitutionally missing opportunity to contest deprivation. **State officials:** Ordinary contract litigation can adjudicate both entitlement and withholding; due process does not require duplicative administrative machinery before disputing an invoice. **G & G:** It lacks the prime contractor's automatic direct action, may depend on assignment, and suffers state-imposed loss before an opportunity to challenge the assessment. A theoretical remedy cannot replace an actual hearing. The State must distinguish adequate adjudication from a damages action that cannot reach the merits; G & G must distinguish loss of present possession from delayed resolution of contested debt. Neither position resolves whether particular workers were underpaid.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse if effective ordinary contract adjudication is available, leaving the wage and payment dispute to that process. It may affirm if the withholding extinguishes the relevant interest without any adequate route to challenge the official determination. A remand may establish a genuinely disputed remedial obstacle, rather than presume either assignment or its impossibility. A jurisdictional or immunity limitation on a particular defendant would require its own basis and would not answer the substantive process question.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An adequate-contract-process holding would cover disputed governmental payment obligations for which ordinary adjudication can determine and enforce entitlement. It would not authorize confiscation of possessed assets, eliminate established benefit hearings or validate a statute that blocks every meaningful claim. A contrary holding could require an additional hearing where the identified contract remedies are inaccessible or cannot adjudicate the dispositive wage determination.

# United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co.
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 200; No. 00-203
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-17 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether back wages are subject to FICA and FUTA employment taxes using the tax rates and wage bases in effect when the wages are actually paid or those applicable to the years when they should have been paid?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, affirming a district-court refund judgment for Cleveland Indians Baseball Company under circuit precedent allocating the back wages to the earlier earning years.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of the refund judgment and application of the payment-year tax rules. The Company seeks retention of the refund based on allocation to the years of the underlying services.
* **Material Record:** A grievance settlement required backpay to eight players for wages due in 1986 and fourteen players for wages due in 1987. The Company paid in 1994 and paid its employer employment taxes using 1994 rates and wage bases. After administrative refund denial, it sued. Allocating the payments to 1986 and 1987, when the applicable wage limits had already been reached, would generate no additional employment tax; treating them as wages paid in 1994 produces additional liability. The timing question therefore changes both the base and the rates, rather than merely bookkeeping. No dispute over the settlement's characterization as wages needs resolution. [United States v. Cleveland Indians Baseball Co.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-200.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is the employer's tax refund action following administrative denial. Allocation of wages for Social Security benefit calculations is related but not identical to allocation for tax collection. The tax consequence for these payments is presented; individual players' benefit awards are not.
* **Relevant Dates:** Underlying wages were due in 1986 and 1987; actual payment and challenged taxes occurred in 1994. Those statutory and regulatory versions control, not a later tax year's provisions.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** United States v. Mead Corp. is later on the term calendar and supplies no entering rule. Any intervening decision changing the governing deference rules must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The payment facts are settled. Statutory interpretation is a legal question, followed by any applicable rule for an authoritative agency interpretation and the ordinary refund consequences.

### Issue 1: Payment-year allocation and administrative interpretation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Auer v. Robbins give a responsible agency's fair, textually permissible interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight. Christensen v. Harris County rejects automatic controlling statutory force for the advisory letter there and preserves persuasive consideration; it also forbids changing clear regulatory permission by interpretation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The own-regulation and advisory-interpretation standards distinguish authority, actual agency grounds and textual fit. Administrative continuity can support interpretation but cannot supply a delegation, change clear text or establish missing facts.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable FICA and FUTA provisions impose taxes tied to wages paid and annual wage limits; Treasury's implementing regulations address remuneration received or paid during the calendar year for services rendered at another time.
* **Material Other Authority:** Social Security Board v. Nierotko addressed allocation of backpay for benefit eligibility. Its recognition of backpay as remuneration does not by itself decide a distinct tax timing provision. Cottage Savings Association v. Commissioner supplies the relevance of a longstanding permissible Treasury construction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Binding deference precedents govern the interpretive method; they do not already decide which year controls these back wages. A revenue ruling's status must not be assumed merely from the agency's name.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory payment language, regulatory text and longstanding application require allocation to the actual payment year notwithstanding the benefit-calculation analogy.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The Sixth Circuit followed its existing allocation precedent, treating backpay as belonging to the periods when ordinarily payable. **United States:** The tax statutes and longstanding regulations use actual payment, allowing employers to calculate a year's withholding and limits without reconstructing prior years. Its asserted interpretation reflects decades of consistent administration. **Company:** Earned-period allocation avoids artificial tax increases caused by delayed payment and better harmonizes tax and benefit treatment under Nierotko. Payment language can describe a collection event without fixing the allocation year. The Government need not show taxes and benefits always use the same accounting convention; the Company must locate authority for displacing payment-year language rather than rely only on equitable symmetry.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the statutes, properly construed with their regulations, assign the wages to 1994; the refund premise then fails. Affirm if the statutory scheme requires earning-year attribution. If a materially different agency interpretation or unsupported regulatory premise were essential, the Court could address its legal force without adopting a universal rule for all revenue rulings. No factual remand is needed merely to repeat the agreed payment dates.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A payment-year holding would govern employer FICA and FUTA taxation of delayed wage payments under the applicable provisions, with benefits remaining governed by their own statutes. An earning-year holding would require lawful attribution across the earlier limits and rates; it would not itself award different Social Security benefits.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.

# United States v. Weston

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-04-17. **Date status:** organizationally assigned.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Weston. **Citation or Docket:** D.C. Circuit No. 99-3119; 206 F.3d 9. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Simulated merits review of an interlocutory order permitting involuntary antipsychotic medication of an incompetent pretrial defendant. **Simulated Event Date:** April 17, 2001, organizationally assigned.

**Question Presented:** Whether a court authorizing involuntary antipsychotic medication of an incompetent pretrial defendant must address the medication's potential effects on the defendant's Sixth Amendment trial rights before treatment begins, when restoring competency is one of the government's stated purposes?

**Question Granted:** Review encompasses the question presented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** On March 24, 2000, the D.C. Circuit reversed the district court's order authorizing involuntary medication and remanded for evaluation of the Government's asserted grounds and the potential effect on Weston's fair-trial rights. **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks authority to administer treatment under the existing justification; Weston seeks preservation of the reversal and a judicial assessment before unwanted treatment begins. [United States v. Weston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.9.99-3119.html).

**Material Record:** Weston was charged after the July 24, 1998 Capitol shootings that killed two police officers. He was diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia and found incompetent to stand trial. His delusions concerned a time-reversing system and a need to enter the Capitol to avert catastrophe. The diagnosis and competency finding matter; the truth of the delusions is not an adjudicative premise.

The district court committed him for treatment under the federal competency statute and required notice to counsel before an administrative medication hearing and written judicial approval before medication. An initial Bureau of Prisons hearing proceeded without the required notice. After remand, a second administrative hearing received a defense psychiatrist's written views and counsel's suggestions, though attorneys did not attend. The Bureau relied on asserted dangerousness, grave disability, medical appropriateness, and restoration of competency. The district court authorized medication on a medical-and-safety rationale while postponing consideration of trial effects until competency was restored.

The medical testimony included both possible danger during staff encounters and the treating psychiatrist's acknowledgment that Weston's immediate isolated and continuously observed conditions adequately controlled danger. The appellate dispute therefore concerns the sufficiency of the particular safety finding and the legal importance of an admitted restoration purpose. It is not a finding that medication could never benefit him or that detention without medication is harmless. The record did not contain an adequate assessment of the proposed drugs' actual likely effect on communication with counsel, testimony, demeanor, and presentation of an insanity defense.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The order concerns an impending bodily intrusion and is reviewed before trial. The liberty and trial-rights objections were raised. No conviction exists, so appellate harmless-error review of a completed trial cannot substitute for the prospective inquiry. Competency to proceed and a fair trial after treatment are related but distinct. Whether a new factual showing could establish an independently sufficient urgent safety need remains different from whether this record supports the order.

**Relevant Dates:** Shootings July 24, 1998; incompetency finding April 22, 1999; district authorization September 9, 1999; controlling lower appellate opinion March 24, 2000. No later appellate opinion or post-April 17, 2001 event is imported. **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion. **Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 17, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No identified earlier effective same-term holding resolves this medication question. Any intervening controlling decision concerning custody, bodily intervention, or pretrial rights must be applied as of April 17, 2001.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** The trial court retains responsibility for the constitutional conditions of the proposed prosecution. A medical administrative determination does not itself decide the legal requirements of competence, counsel, and a fair trial.

### Issue 1: Premedication consideration of trial rights

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Riggins v. Nevada recognizes a protected interest in refusing antipsychotic medication and requires an adequate governmental justification, including medical appropriateness, need, and consideration of reasonable less intrusive alternatives. Its trial-prejudice holding recognizes risks to comprehension, communication, testimony, and demeanor that cannot necessarily be reconstructed after the fact. It did not adopt a complete standard for forcibly restoring an incompetent defendant or hold that every issue follows the convicted-prisoner framework.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The medication standard preserves the distinct pretrial liberty and defense interests. Treatment that may be clinically appropriate is not automatically constitutionally justified. Expert explanation of changed appearance does not necessarily remedy impaired understanding or communication. The separate views expressed in Riggins v. Nevada concerning extraordinary circumstances or a particular harmless-error formulation are not controlling Court rules.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fifth Amendment protects this federal defendant's liberty interest; the Sixth Amendment protects trial and assistance-of-counsel interests. The federal competency provisions authorize evaluation and treatment commitment but do not, merely by committing a defendant, resolve all constitutional conditions of involuntary medication. [United States v. Weston](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/206/206.F3d.9.99-3119.html).

**Material Other Authority:** Washington v. Harper supplies the convicted-prisoner safety context recognized by Riggins v. Nevada. Its administrative procedure and justification do not automatically settle treatment aimed at bringing an unconvicted defendant to trial.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Unwanted medication requires justification, and impairment of trial participation is legally material. Riggins v. Nevada establishes neither a categorical right to remain incompetent nor a categorical prosecution interest overriding refusal.

**Open Point:** Whether the court may postpone all consideration of trial effects until after medication when restoration is an actual government purpose, and what prospective assessment is necessary on this record, are unresolved applications requiring decision.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The district court accepted medical appropriateness and safety necessity, found no prosecutorial pretext, and treated the future trial-rights questions as premature. The appellate panel found the safety showing inadequate in light of the actual conditions of confinement and required consideration of both governmental justifications and fair-trial effects before authorization. The judges disagreed in separate writings about the proper depth of review and the implications of medication's intended and unintended effects.

**Government's Position:** Medical judgment supports treatment of serious illness and control of possible danger. The court should not require a complete prediction of a trial that cannot occur unless competency returns. Some side effects may be managed by dose changes or additional treatment, and later judicial safeguards can address what actually occurs.

**Weston's Position:** The Government expressly seeks competency restoration, so the foreseeable effects on the defense cannot be excluded from the authorization decision. Present safety can be controlled by less intrusive conditions. Medication may alter capacity to consult, testify, display relevant symptoms, or present an insanity defense; the Government cannot first cause those changes and then declare their significance ripe.

**Necessary Limits and Alternative Grounds:** Protection of trial rights does not require a perfect trial or permanent display of every symptom. The record must determine whether testimony, existing recordings, medication adjustments, or other measures can protect an effective defense. Clinical benefit alone does not answer necessity. Ethical and capital-sentencing objections were raised below, but the question presented does not require a categorical ruling on medication undertaken solely to make execution possible.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Paths:** Treat the proposed medication's foreseeable trial effects as ripe because they bear on present authorization. If an actually independent safety justification suffices, the court must identify its evidence and alternatives; the mere use of a safety label cannot bypass the challenged restoration purpose.

**Merits Paths and Plausible Holding Scope:** Require a preliminary judicial assessment of likely trial effects, with subsequent review of actual effects if lawful treatment occurs; permit initial treatment on an independently adequate safety basis while specifying the limits of any later prosecution; or uphold deferral if the applicable constitutional rule and an adequate independent record justify it. This record supports a rule concerning treatment with an admitted competency-restoration purpose, not a universal prohibition on antipsychotic treatment of detainees.

**Judgment Consequences and Remedy:** Affirming the appellate remand leaves the challenged authorization unavailable and requires findings on necessity, reasonable alternatives, and likely effects before any renewed order. Reinstating authorization permits treatment but supplies no finding that Weston is competent, criminally responsible, or fit for a capital trial. A new regimen or materially changed confinement circumstances requires its own factual assessment. Neither route dismisses the indictment or determines criminal guilt.

**Uncertainty:** The full clinical file and an adequate individualized trial-effects assessment are absent. They could change whether a renewed, properly supported treatment order is lawful; their absence does not eliminate the present legal question about the required sequence.

# Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle

* **Material Record:** Earlier statutes expressly accommodated Christian Science sanitoria. After those provisions were held unconstitutional in separate litigation, Congress enacted section 4454 of the Balanced Budget Act of 1997, substituting religious nonmedical health-care institutions and eligibility based on religious objections to medical treatment. Covered institutions provide physical care, including assistance with eating and dressing, without medical treatment. Payment is restricted to patients whose condition would qualify for benefits in an ordinary hospital or skilled nursing facility, and to the nonmedical subset of covered services. The statute does not authorize payment for spiritual healing. The institutional committee makes an initial coverage recommendation. The Secretary retains final review, can request nonmedical information, and can deny payment when necessary information is withheld. The Secretary cannot compel an examination inconsistent with the beneficiary's religious objection. Plaintiffs dispute whether these safeguards actually prevent preferential custodial care and meaningful deference to religious decisions; their descriptions are not additional findings of diversion.

Their facial and application challenges require distinguishing defects inherent in the statutory criteria from unsupported assumptions about every recipient's operation. The earlier judgment concerning named-denomination preferences does not adjudicate the revised statute. Summary judgment permits legal review but does not authorize resolving genuinely disputed operational facts against the nonmovant.

### Issue 1: Accommodation and the publicly financed function

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon v. Kurtzman through a unified inquiry identifying governmental religious authority and function. Specific prohibitions and established permissible relationships control at their actual scope. Mitchell v. Helms bars proved public financing of religious instruction under religious control but rejects affiliation, theoretical diversion, and fungibility as substitutes for proof.

Religious motivation does not itself convert physical services into devotion. ### Issue 2: Coverage recommendations and governmental authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet prohibits selection of a denomination to exercise general governmental authority; Agostini v. Felton preserves that limit.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Formal labels do not settle who exercises public power. Genuine public administration of a secular benefit differs from religious control over eligibility or public obligations.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Secretary's statutory review authority and the prohibition on compelled religiously objectionable examinations.

The Eighth Circuit treated the program as a sect-neutral accommodation of access to a subset of benefits already available to ordinary patients. Its separate-prong Establishment Clause analysis must be assessed under the unified inquiry prescribed by Agostini v. Felton.

Plaintiffs' strongest rule would prohibit financial preferences confined to religious refusal when comparable secular refusal receives no equivalent assistance, unless the preference removes an identifiable governmental burden without financing religion or shifting substantial costs to outsiders. They contend that the statutory qualification substitutes religious choice for medical eligibility and shields religious committees from the information necessary for genuine review. The government and intervenor would permit a tailored adjustment enabling otherwise eligible persons to receive the secular portion of a public benefit without violating their faith. A threshold disposition would require an identified jurisdictional defect; taxpayer standing should not be rejected by assumption. Reversal could identify a forbidden religious preference, an actual delegation, or both. The remedy would have to address the defective criterion or function and consider severability rather than automatically terminate every permissible nonmedical service. A remand could require examination of supported disputes over coverage parity or effective review. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The record supports a rule concerning a defined public-benefit accommodation and the independence of coverage review. No finding establishes that the government paid for prayer, that reviewers lacked all useful evidence, or that a particular beneficiary received an otherwise uncovered level of care.
