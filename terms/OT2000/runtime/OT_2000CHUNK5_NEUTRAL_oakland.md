# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 483; No. 00-151
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-05-14 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether medical necessity permits the manufacture and distribution of marijuana otherwise prohibited by the Controlled Substances Act, and whether a court may create that exemption through modification of an injunction enforcing the Act.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the district court’s denial of the Cooperative’s motion to modify a federal injunction, recognized medical necessity as a legally cognizable defense, and directed reconsideration of an exemption’s criteria. The separate contempt appeal had become moot after the Cooperative promised compliance. The live judgment concerns modification of the injunction.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of the medical-necessity ruling. The Cooperative seeks an exemption for distribution to seriously ill patients lacking effective alternatives, either as a statutory defense or through equitable discretion.
* **Material Record:** California’s 1996 Compassionate Use Act removed specified state criminal prohibitions for patients and primary caregivers possessing or cultivating marijuana on a physician’s recommendation or approval. The Oakland Cooperative operated a dispensary with a medical director, nurses, physician statements and screening interviews. Members received identification allowing them to obtain marijuana. The United States sued in January 1998 under federal law and obtained a preliminary injunction against manufacture, distribution and related possession with intent. The Cooperative did not appeal that initial order and distributed marijuana despite it. At the contempt stage, the district court found inadequate proof that every recipient faced imminent harm without marijuana. It later denied a motion to modify the injunction. After the appellate decision, the district court fashioned a four-part medical-necessity exception; a separate government appeal followed. The record does not establish that every patient satisfies those conditions. State authorization, medical recommendations and federal statutory authorization remain distinct. [United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-483.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The motion to modify presents a live injunction controversy reviewable under the applicable interlocutory provision. Mootness of the purged contempt proceeding does not eliminate that separate controversy. The question is not whether marijuana can benefit any patient as a medical fact. Constitutional objections concerning federal power and patients’ interests were advanced but not resolved by the court of appeals; they cannot be treated as adjudicated facts or silently rejected merely through statutory construction. The statutory issue and asserted alternative equitable ground are squarely presented.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provisions and conduct are evaluated in their historically applicable versions; the event date does not retroactively supply notice or alter the record.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified. Any intervening controlling decision must be considered before adjudication.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Controlled Substances Act’s §882(a) authorizes district courts to enjoin violations. Authorization to issue equitable relief does not alone command an injunction in every circumstance; lawful discretion concerning relief must be distinguished from a substantive exemption from the conduct prohibition. The current proceeding does not adjudicate a criminal conviction or automatically revive the mooted contempt dispute.

### Issue 1: Statutory necessity and the lawful scope of equity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc. requires attention to Congress’s actual remedial scheme and preserves traditional equity within its statutory limits; emergency circumstances do not supply every omitted remedy. Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc. likewise distinguishes an established equitable power from a desired result and rejects general equity as a substitute for a missing source of authority. Neither case specifically adjudicates a necessity defense under the Controlled Substances Act. Both preserve authorized statutory injunctions and established equitable discretion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Statutory abatement and equitable relief require separate consideration of legal authority, the authorized remedy, and case-specific discretion. They establish no universal prohibition on injunction modification, no automatic remedy from serious need, and no rule importing the detailed procedures of one statutory scheme into another.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable §841(a)(1) prohibits knowing or intentional manufacture, distribution, dispensing and related possession except as authorized by the subchapter. Marijuana is listed in Schedule I; the statutory framework connects that classification to lack of currently accepted medical use and provides an authorized research route. The Cooperative claims no authorization under that route. California’s exception to its own law is not a federal authorization. These provisions are examined in the historical statutory version quoted in the judicial record.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Bailey discussed common-law necessity in construing a criminal statute while requiring an available lawful alternative to be considered. It does not establish an unlimited judicial power to rebalance a policy Congress resolved. United States v. Rutherford distinguishes statutory medical regulation from judicial creation of a treatment exemption. Hecht Co. v. Bowles and Weinberger v. Romero-Barcelo preserve equitable discretion without making a court free to contradict an enacted priority.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existence of discretion over an injunction’s necessity and form does not necessarily permit a declaration that forbidden distributions are substantively lawful. Conversely, rejecting a necessity exemption does not by itself decide every constitutional objection, compel the broadest possible injunction, or establish criminal liability without its elements.
* **Open Point:** Whether the federal scheme permits this medical-distribution exemption, and whether the same exemption can independently be sustained as an exercise of equitable discretion.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Government relies on the express conduct prohibition, Schedule I placement, authorized research pathway and Congress’s distinct treatment of substances that can be medically prescribed. It argues that the proposed exception would alter that scheme rather than interpret an ambiguity. The Cooperative invokes the common-law necessity background, the extraordinary suffering alleged, the absence of reasonable alternatives, and a narrow exception tied to physician-supported need. It also argues that the district court misunderstood its equitable discretion: even if no criminal defense exists, a civil court should retain flexibility in defining the injunction. The Government’s answer is that genuine remedial discretion cannot be used to grant the very substantive permission that the statute withholds. Neither side can equate California’s choice with a change to federal law.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the claimed statutory defense and the same exemption under general equity, reverse the appellate judgment, and remand without deciding the full constitutional challenge. It can recognize a necessity defense only by explaining why the statutory prohibition and classification leave room for it, then require actual proof of each condition rather than assume all members qualify. It can also distinguish lack of a substantive defense from discretion whether and how to issue an injunction; that distinction supports careful remedial treatment but not automatically the Cooperative’s requested exemption. A separate constitutional limitation, if properly presented and established, would require its own source, analysis and remedy.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can address manufacture, distribution and possession with that intent by this Cooperative. It need not adjudicate every individual patient’s separate possession case or the general existence of necessity in all federal criminal law.

**Material Uncertainty:** The record contains competing descriptions of medical need, not adjudicated universal necessity for all recipients. The published material does not supply a lower-court disposition of the independent constitutional objections. The litigation is not a scientific determination that marijuana can never provide therapeutic benefit.
