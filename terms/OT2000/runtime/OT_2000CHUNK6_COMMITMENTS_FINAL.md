## Common disposition and complete bounded rule

All eight Associates affirm on the accepted population-base question. A municipality may apportion ordinary representative council districts using total resident population. Material differences in citizen voting-age populations do not, by themselves, make that population base unconstitutional: representatives govern and respond to all residents, including people who cannot vote, and equal resident districts serve equal representation of those constituents. That permission does not require every jurisdiction to use total population, validate every alternative denominator or immunize a separate intentional exclusion or independently established dilution mechanism.

Voting equality is a genuine constitutional constraint. Counting all residents for district populations neither grants noncitizens a ballot nor assigns unequal weight to ballots cast within a district. An electoral-headcount disparity identifies the competing measure, but it does not establish that the Constitution forbids the representational measure. Reynolds and Avery's population-equality principles govern state and local representative institutions; Burns shows that the population-base issue cannot be resolved by treating one numerical formulation as a universal command. The Court need not decide a case using another denominator or a plan that excludes represented residents.

Voinovich requires actual justification of deviations under the applicable measure and treats less-than-ten-percent departures as generally minor, not immune. Its threshold cannot be transferred to citizen voting-age figures before deciding whether that measure is constitutionally compulsory. Department of Commerce's interstate holding does not authorize intrastate inequality, but its unanimous rejection of nonjusticiability preserves judicial review of this accepted question. The distinct racial-gerrymandering claim is outside the grant; Easley/Smallwood supplies no denominator rule. Preclearance does not decide equal protection.

## Enhanced alternative-path test

Path A permits total-population districts on this record and affirms. Its strongest objection is concrete: two equal resident districts can contain substantially different eligible electorates, giving a voter in the smaller electorate more numerical influence over the election. The answer is not that the disparity is imaginary or immune from review. Equal protection permits this representational measure because the offices represent all residents, and the Constitution does not require simultaneous numerical equality of every distinct relevant population. No finding that all possible maps could or could not equalize both is needed.

Path B requires citizen voting-age equalization, or at least a special justification whenever that deviation exceeds ten percent, and remands for a new plan or demographic findings. Its strongest support is Reynolds's protection against dilution of the individual vote and the directness of eligible-elector counts as a measure of electoral influence. Its decisive weakness is treating those observations as selecting the exclusive denominator while discounting the represented interests of children and noncitizens. Neither the constitutional text, Burns nor the current Court's denominator-neutral deviation cases supplies that categorical choice. There is no particular map or supported alternative metric to order merely by importing Voinovich's number.

## Enhanced two-path comparison

### Finality and review route

Path A sustains review under Bennett and the earlier Whitman holding. The final national command plus EPA's statutory objection authority establishes more than influential advice. Path B requires regulated parties to wait for an individual permit because the guidance disclaims enforceable rights and permits involve further judgments. The agency concedes the central position is settled. The Court therefore need not treat all influential guidance as final.

### Interpretation, amendment and remedy

Path A rejects the guidance because the enacted regulatory condition cannot be erased by an assurance purpose. Christensen's regulatory-text holding and Thomas Jefferson's permissible-interpretation rule both require textual fit. The 1992 explanation promising rulemaking to repair deficient federal standards reinforces the conditional structure; it is not a separate estoppel or universally immutable-interpretation rule.

Path B sustains the directive as a reasonable interpretation of monitoring adequate to assure compliance. Its strongest argument is that recurring but useless measurement would frustrate the permit program, and the explanation acknowledged additional requirements. But the broader assurance language is expressly linked to the conditional rule. The text distinguishes the prerequisite monitoring/testing from the gap-filling periodic-monitoring obligation. Reading them as the same universal adequacy instruction eliminates that distinction. The stated problem can support prospective rulemaking; it cannot expand the present rule through an asserted purpose alone.

For remedy, partial vacatur is ordinarily possible when a supported independent remainder can function as intended. Here the lower opinion describes an intertwined directive organized around the challenged sufficiency review, and the supplied record establishes no independently adopted replacement. Whole-document vacatur is therefore supported without turning every interpretation error into total invalidation. The Court does not import a rule requiring notice and comment for every interpretive change, and does not independently adopt every circuit doctrine cited below.

## Proposed action and bounded common rule

All eight Associates commit to granting the petition, vacating the Arkansas judgment on its stated federal rationale and remanding for proceedings under the controlling federal rules. They support a reasoned summary merits correction of that rationale, not an unexplained disposition that decides nothing and not a finding that every challenged intrusion was lawful. An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by probable cause does not violate the Fourth Amendment solely because the officer also seeks evidence of a different offense. A state court cannot supply greater protection by assigning the Federal Constitution a meaning inconsistent with this Court's controlling interpretation. It may apply an adequately supported independent state-law protection, but none is invented to preserve this expressly federal rationale.

The stop does not authorize every later restraint or search. Atwater now extends Ricci's requirement of an actual enforcement function to added station transport and confinement for a known fine-only criminal offense. Such custody must reasonably serve supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence-protection or necessary-processing needs; the examples are not exhaustive, no such need is presumed, and neither least restrictive means nor a universal citation duty is required. The actual punishment and legal basis for Sullivan's asserted offenses must be addressed where material. Impoundment and an asserted inventory remain separately justified. Genuine inventory authority retains caretaking and standardized-practice limits under Bertine and Wells, as preserved in Whren. Investigative interest does not invalidate every otherwise lawful act, but evidence of an investigatory ruse can show that the inventory exception's own conditions are missing. Calling a policy an inventory does not prove its constitutional application. Knowles preserves the antecedent lawful-custody requirement and does not allow search fruits or the prospect of an incident search to establish arrest authority.

The remand permits adjudication of supported, preserved custody, impoundment, inventory and independent state-law grounds under the proper standards. ## Common disposition and proposed controlling propositions

All eight Associates reverse the Tenth Circuit's approval of this tax and return for conforming relief within lawful party, immunity and equity limits.

Montana's nonmember framework governs this tax on nonmember guests' occupancy transactions at a nonmember hotel on non-Indian fee land. No treaty or statute specifically supplies the asserted power. The sovereign character of taxation does not create unlimited territorial power over every nonmember transaction; Merrion preserves sovereign taxation distinct from private exclusion, but its tribal-land lease and consensual commercial relationship do not establish this fee-land tax. Reservation boundaries and fee status must both retain their actual significance. The parcel remains within the reservation; the Tribe's authority nevertheless requires the applicable nonmember source and nexus.

Neither Montana exception is established for these transactions. General police, fire and medical benefits do not themselves demonstrate a consensual relationship authorizing the occupancy tax. Otherwise essentially every nonmember activity would become consensual merely because government supplies ordinary public services. The federal Indian-trader license reflects federal permission, not a tribal bargain or guest agreement covering the tax. Even a distinct commercial relationship cannot support this tax without a meaningful relation between that relationship and the taxed nonmember guest activity. No universal requirement of express tax consent or contractual privity is adopted.

The second exception preserves power over conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The general need for revenue and provision of services cannot alone establish the required direct tribal effect for every fee-land guest transaction. The record supplies no additional concrete threat of the required kind; the Court does not deny that fiscal and service consequences are real or demand a new quantified formula. The tax cannot be sustained through the lower court's free-ranging territorial balance in place of these source and exception inquiries.

The remedy concerns collection of this tax from the challenged nonmember transactions. The substantive ruling does not establish a monetary-refund cause of action, unrestricted tribal waiver or automatic entitlement to every injunction. The federal court must provide appropriate conforming relief against lawful parties within immunity and ordinary equity. C & L's particular integrated contract waiver supplies no missing agreement here. No tax on members, tribal leases, trust-land dealings, qualifying consensual transactions or expressly authorized activity is invalidated.

## Enhanced two-path comparison

Path A applies Montana, separately tests both exceptions and reverses. Its strongest objection is that government taxing power is more than landowner exclusion and public services impose real expense; an inherent territorial revenue power might support a guest levy independently. This objection prevents describing Merrion as merely a property-right case. It does not explain why non-Indian fee-land dealings escape Montana's governing-power boundary. Strate preserves the sovereign-taxation distinction while requiring an actual source and meaningful relation. The present record lacks Merrion's tribal land and lease relationship.

Path B affirms through independent territorial taxing sovereignty or a service-based consensual relationship. Its strongest justification is reciprocal support for government in the place where business occurs. Its decisive difficulty is the absence of a limiting connection to these guest transactions: residence or location plus ordinary service benefits would make the nonmember framework nearly universal. The federal license is no tribal agreement. On the second exception, ordinary economic impact cannot become the required direct effect simply because a tax would raise money. # Frozen independent non-Stone commitments: Buckhannon Board & Care Home

## Reach and entering law

The question concerns prevailing-party eligibility under the Fair Housing Amendments Act and ADA after the legislature repealed the challenged residency requirement and the merits case became moot. The factual proposition that this lawsuit caused repeal remains to be found, as do legal substance, actual benefit and a reasonable amount. The Court may resolve eligibility without deciding the prior residency rule's validity or awarding fees.

Farrar unanimously makes an enforceable nominal judgment sufficient; its five-Justice amount holding requires an explained assessment of success, with possible zero but no automatic proportion to damages. Neither supplies an exclusive judicial-decree rule. Hewitt rejects favorable judicial language without benefit to the plaintiff; its opinion did not decide whether an actually beneficial causally induced change could support fees. Rhodes rejects a declaration unable to benefit either plaintiff. Garland requires a material change and some requested benefit rather than success on the central issue, and separates degree of success from eligibility. These holdings must be applied at their actual scope.

## Two supported constructions

**Judicial-relief path.** Affirm categorical ineligibility here. A prevailing party must obtain a judicially sanctioned material alteration of the parties' legal relationship, as through an enforceable merits judgment or a court-approved consent decree. A voluntary change caused by litigation may be valuable and may moot the merits, but it does not supply that judicial alteration. The necessary approval or enforceability distinguishes a consent decree from an ordinary private resolution. The holding need not classify every possible settlement device or interlocutory order.

The strongest objection is that the plaintiff can obtain everything sought through a meritorious suit and still lose reimbursement solely because the defendant acts before judgment. Existing cases identify sufficient judicial relief without making it exclusive, and Hewitt discussed voluntary change without resolving eligibility. The answer for this path is that Congress used a litigation term that connects fee eligibility to an adjudicative change; causal success alone need not establish legal entitlement. The categorical rule gives an administrable threshold and avoids trying the causation and legal merits of a controversy the parties have already ended. Those administrative consequences reinforce the text; they are not a claim that every catalyst inquiry is impossible or that every catalyst lawsuit is weak.

**Confined catalyst path.** Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand. A plaintiff may prevail when a legally substantial, nonfrivolous suit materially causes the defendant to provide concrete relief the plaintiff sought, even without judgment or consent decree. Causation must be proved; temporal sequence is insufficient. The change must actually benefit the plaintiff, and a gratuitous payment or response merely buying peace from a groundless claim does not establish the required legally grounded success. This is a statutory construction of prevailing party, not an inherent power to reward socially useful litigation.

Its strongest objection is that fees would rest on a causal hypothesis rather than enforceable judicial relief, invite secondary merits litigation and deter voluntary correction. The answer is that courts regularly assess factual causation and legal substance, and the fee statute's reasonable-amount requirement independently controls overclaiming. Hewitt's absence-of-benefit holding, Rhodes's ineffective declaration and Farrar's payment judgment turn on meaningful relief rather than a categorical decree requirement. The litigation must change the defendant's conduct in the respect for which the claimant sued; mere favorable language does not suffice. A defendant may voluntarily comply without conceding liability, but that freedom does not necessarily erase the plaintiff's statutory success.

Neither route is already compelled by Farrar. The different positions therefore remain a genuine statutory disagreement, not a purported application of nonexistent controlling catalyst law. Laidlaw's mootness and redressability holdings do not determine fees and are not extended to make a collateral fee demand preserve the merits controversy.

## Component I: burden of proving supervisory exclusion

All eight provisionally uphold the Board's allocation to the party asserting supervisory status, here the employer, and reject the Sixth Circuit's contrary requirement that the Board prove the negative in this representation dispute. The NLRA expressly excludes supervisors from its broad employee category but does not prescribe the disputed allocation. The Board may reasonably require the proponent of that exception to establish the qualifying actual authority. The rule is not inherently employer-specific: a union or the General Counsel asserting supervisory status bears that same affirmative burden in the corresponding setting.

The contrary path treats protected employee status as part of the General Counsel's case and thus demands proof that each nurse is not a supervisor. That objection correctly preserves the General Counsel's burden to prove unfair labor practices. ## Component II: permissible distinction and forbidden shortcut

All eight accept this common operative boundary: the Board may distinguish ordinary skilled collaboration or routine implementation of prescribed care from actual authority to perform a listed supervisory function with independent judgment in the employer's interest. It may not exclude a direction from independent judgment merely because it rests on professional or technical skill, nor demand hiring or disciplinary power when assignment or responsible direction independently qualifies. Conversely, professional skill, higher rank, the building-supervisor title, patient-care responsibility or physical absence of managers does not automatically establish statutory supervision.

The supported alternatives are thus application of the permitted functional distinction and correction of a professional-category shortcut. Neither side may repeat the historical Health Care account in the petition as present law. The prior actual holding already permits functional distinction; it did not sustain every later classification, make the construction mandatory or validate every conceivable professional-judgment rationale. No reconsideration of Health Care is proposed by these commitments.

## Component III: agency rationale, record and remedy

**Enforcement path.** Reverse the nurses' exclusion from the unit and direct enforcement of the Board's existing order as to those employees. The regional director evaluated actual authority: prescribed staffing, voluntary retention and local calls for coverage, lack of power to compel attendance, ordinary incident reporting independently investigated by management, no effective recommendation of personnel action and continuing management availability. It considered the conflicting building-supervisor memoranda and found the demonstrated additional role to be obtaining coverage. Its final description of remaining work as medical services sits within those actual functional findings, not a categorical rule that professional judgment can never be independent.

The strongest objection is the contrary employer evidence about interunit reassignment, directions to write up noncompliance and authority to send an employee home. The agency cannot dismiss genuinely conferred authority merely because it has rarely been exercised. The enforcement path treats the director's supported findings as evaluating whether those paper descriptions reflected real delegated authority: any employee could report problems, reports were independently investigated, an actual report did not produce the claimed consequence, and established staffing procedures constrained discretion. Lack of firing power is a supporting fact within that broader inquiry, not a mandatory element. Allentown requires consideration of contrary evidence but does not permit replacing the agency's supported choice with the appellate court's preferred view. Chenery allows the rationale the director actually gave, without inventing an alternative.

**Agency-reconsideration path.** Reject the appellate burden ruling and its conclusive judicial declaration that the six nurses are supervisors, but withhold enforcement of the disputed nurse portion and remand for application of the correct criteria to the actual authority. The director's routine-coverage findings can support some conclusions; its treatment of other medical services and emphasis on absent discipline or nonexercise do not adequately resolve the particular evidence of assignment and responsible direction. The disputed memoranda and send-home testimony make it necessary to distinguish lack of authority from lack of recorded use and to determine whether clinical direction also exercises a listed power independently. Courts may not answer that question through a new agency rationale or their own classification.

The strongest objection is that this demands a pointless second proceeding where the agency already assessed the documents and actual roles. The response is confined to the unaddressed legal significance of possessed assignment or responsible-direction authority, not a demand that every agency decision recite a checklist or prove the negative. Health Care expressly preserves those independent routes. Allentown requires the real standard, and Chenery prevents the court from repairing a material omission by attributing a new rationale. The employer still bears its burden; remand is not a judicial finding that its evidence wins or that clinical responsibility inherently entails statutory supervision.

The two paths disagree about the adequacy and supported scope of the explanation actually given. They do not disagree about an automatic professional exemption, and they do not treat a court's characterization of Board policy as dispositive evidence of what the director did.
