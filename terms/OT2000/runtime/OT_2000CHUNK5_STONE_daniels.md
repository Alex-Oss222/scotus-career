# OT_2000CHUNK5

# Daniels v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment denying this § 2255 motion on the narrower ground that Daniels identifies no unconstitutional complete counsel deprivation and no loss of a meaningful earlier review opportunity through an external impediment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the existing federal sentence undisturbed against these claims. Order no fresh sentencing hearing or state-conviction invalidation. An independently available original-conviction challenge or later authorized application remains subject to its own prerequisites.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Available review and finality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory review question and necessary constitutional counsel exception. Do not decide whether either plea was actually invalid; those merits do not become adjudicable merely because an enhanced sentence exists.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** When may section 2255 review of a current enhanced federal sentence reach a constitutional challenge to an expired predicate conviction, and do Daniels's allegations meet that standard?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new, bounded extension of the entering channel rule into § 2255. Apply Custis v. United States to the distinction between complete counsel deprivation and ineffective representation, but do not portray its expired-custody reservation as already resolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2255 does not reopen an expired state conviction merely because it enhanced a current federal sentence. Review remains available for unconstitutional complete denial of required counsel and where, through no fault of the prisoner, meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated, subject to the applicable procedural requirements and proof of adverse effect on current custody. A genuinely available earlier opportunity lost through unexcused failure is not an unavailable remedy. Daniels identifies neither complete counsel deprivation nor an external impediment to meaningful original review, so his involuntary-plea and ineffective-assistance claims cannot proceed through this motion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the presented involuntary-plea and ineffective-assistance claims on their actual review history. It supplies no rule for a different statutory predicate objection or a predicate already invalidated through proper proceedings.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The current-custody language identifies the sentence a federal court may correct, but it does not erase the completed judgment’s lawful review structure. Daniels’s reason to challenge the old convictions may have strengthened after the new offense; that change does not establish that the earlier forum was unavailable. Nor should administrative inconvenience alone make a demonstrably unlawful present sentence immune from every authorized challenge. The rule therefore rests on the available opportunity and the absence of an asserted external barrier, not on an irrebuttable factual conclusion that every final conviction is valid. This statutory channel rule preserves a used or genuinely available first opportunity; it does not equate every attorney failure with the prisoner's deliberate choice. Daniels identifies no impediment or failed first opportunity here. The reasoning does not decide a jury claim about a new sentencing fact: Almendarez-Torres and Apprendi cannot supply that unpleaded claim. The defined faultless-loss exception follows the distinction between meaningful first review and repetitive litigation; it is adopted here as part of the governing access rule, not borrowed from a later decision in Coss. Daniels alleges no facts placing him within it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual constitutional validity of the old pleas is not reached because Daniels fails the governing review-access rule. A predicate already invalidated through proper proceedings would present a different reopening question absent from this record; no new sentencing-fact jury claim is presented.
