# Florida v. Thomas

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the Florida judgment and its Chimel v. California remand in place without endorsing either search analysis. No federal merits declaration or remedial order should accompany the dismissal.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: State-judgment finality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide §1257 finality first and stop the federal merits inquiry upon finding it absent. The fact that this is a listed merits matter does not waive a jurisdictional defect or turn it into a certiorari recommendation.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this unresolved admissibility remand, with a later pretrial appeal route, is a final state judgment within the Court's statutory appellate jurisdiction.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Jefferson v. City of Tarrant and its preserved practical-finality routes without changing them.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A remand that may admit the challenged evidence under another Fourth Amendment ground and that permits later pretrial appellate review is not practically final merely because one search-incident theory was rejected. None of the preserved Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn routes is established.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs a substantive state remand where the State can still prevail on admissibility and can seek effective pretrial review if it loses. It does not categorically deny review of every suppression ruling.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The threshold issue is the jurisdiction Congress conferred. The State's feared inability to appeal after acquittal matters only after a conclusive suppression ruling makes trial without the evidence necessary. It does not describe the present order. Conversely, it would be inadequate simply to point to a theoretically possible later appeal; the specific Florida pretrial route and the unresolved admissibility question make later correction effective here. The ruling should explain both features rather than announce a universal bar on review of suppression judgments. Stone retains the existing finality doctrine. New York v. Belton's extension is not reached because jurisdiction is absent, not because its constitutional stakes are too difficult. The merits question must await a case within the Court’s jurisdiction. The jurisdictional dismissal expresses no merits vote.

  Reaffirm the practical-finality framework rather than create a suppression-case exception: the unresolved admissibility ground and effective later pretrial review are the material distinctions. The statutory jurisdictional boundary is decisive. That boundary requires a narrow disposition even though the proposed extension of Belton has consequences for people stopped beside their cars.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The factual predicate for the Chimel v. California remand remains missing from the current record. The Court expresses no conclusion on that application. The unnecessary New York v. Belton merits ruling is addressed under Threshold and Merits Reach, not treated as a freestanding doctrinal reservation.

# Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit's reversal of the categorical dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Recognize the maritime wrongful-death action for negligent breach of an otherwise applicable maritime duty by this third party. Leave preserved duty, breach, causation, statutory defenses, and damage questions to the lower court on the actual record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A death remedy for an established maritime duty

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the availability of the cause of action and the asserted statutory preclusion. Those are necessary antecedents to allowing this complaint to proceed. Do not adjudicate allegations as proven facts.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a nonseaman's estate may pursue general maritime wrongful death for a third party's negligent breach of an applicable maritime duty in territorial waters when no governing statutory rule excludes that action.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing maritime remedial and statutory-boundary framework and expressly extend Moragne v. States Marine Lines's death remedy to this negligence setting. Its original facts concerned unseaworthiness. The extension changes no applicable statutory limit and overrules no controlling holding.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** General maritime law permits an estate to recover for death caused by negligent breach of an otherwise applicable maritime duty where the actual statutory regime does not preclude that third-party action. The complaint cannot be dismissed solely because the alleged negligent injury was fatal. Duty, breach, causation, defenses, and lawful damages remain subject to proof.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers an established maritime negligence duty in this nonseaman, territorial-water, third-party setting. It neither creates a duty where none exists nor alters exclusive employer compensation or an applicable statutory damage limit.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The same maritime duty should not become unenforceable merely because its breach causes death, unless the governing legal regime requires that outcome. Here Congress's classifications matter: Garris is not a Jones Act seaman, the accident is not within DOHSA's geographic field, and Norfolk is a third party rather than the compensation employer or vessel defendant. A general judicial preference for more recovery would be inadequate, but the established maritime duty, Moragne v. States Marine Lines's principle, and preservation of third-party claims supply the legal basis. Preserve Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co.'s refusal to supplement Congress's actual high-seas compensation choices and Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun's narrower treatment of state remedies. Do not convert this decision into a presumption favoring any newly proposed maritime claim. The maritime duty and statutory savings structure support this extension. Reinstating the categorical dismissal is not acceptable merely because Congress has occupied adjacent remedial fields.

  Moragne's extension rests on continuity of an existing maritime duty and the statutory preservation of third-party responsibility, with the enacted remedial boundaries controlling over a general preference for uniformity. The new rule accordingly governs fatal negligence within this legal setting; it does not authorize courts to supplement compensation limits that Congress actually selected.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific duty and breach are unresolved because the complaint was dismissed before their adjudication. Damage categories and survival are outside the question presented and not resolved on the record. No additional reservation is needed.

### Issue 1: The NOL the consolidated regulations actually define

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which legally defined NOL enters the product-liability-loss comparison for a consolidated group under the operative tax-year regulations?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the consolidated definitions actually enacted and promulgated. Resolve this open accounting application without overruling a controlling holding or supplying a restriction absent from the governing rules.

Separate taxable income is a component with exclusions, not a disguised stand-alone NOL. The separate loss allocation used for carrybacks to a member's nonconsolidated year cannot simply be repurposed as an initial group-level ceiling.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the operative consolidated-return regime and product liability losses for the litigated years. Treasury's authority to make a different rule within delegation remains distinct, with ordinary limits on its temporal operation. Stone would not accept the separate-member result based only on anticipated abuse or a general maxim against favorable tax consequences.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The governing statute and regulations control the consolidated calculation. Congress authorized consolidated accounting, and Treasury chose which items to consolidate. The resulting interaction can differ from filing separate returns without being an illicit double benefit. United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. protects lawful delegated choices; it does not authorize a court to supply a missing restriction because the Government now prefers it. No controlling tax precedent is overruled.

  Text and regulatory structure carry decisive weight. The comparison between consolidated and separate returns explains the definitions, but a prediction of favorable tax treatment cannot override them. This accounting rule must govern like returns for the litigated years; there is no reason to decide later regulatory regimes or an unproved refund amount.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact refund computation is not established by the available record. Application of later revised regulations and other liability-loss categories is outside the presented question. There is no reservation of the calculation method itself.

# In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 4, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply intervening controlling decisions and any separately dispositive ground established by the full record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining summary judgment on the affected monopolization claims and remand because intellectual-property ownership cannot supply the categorical bar used below.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require assessment of the claims under the distinct market-power, exclusionary-conduct, justification, causation, and antitrust-injury requirements, giving the patent and copyright interests substantive weight. Further summary judgment remains available on an adequately established ground. No damages, compulsory parts supply, software license, or infringement disposition is ordered by this judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory exclusivity has real force but does not create categorical immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved legal defense responsible for summary judgment. The reported sales restrictions and alleged service-market consequences make the interaction material. No fraud, sham-litigation, or tying claim is created to evade the presented issue.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether valid intellectual-property rights necessarily defeat a monopolization claim arising from refusal to provide protected inputs to independent service rivals, even when the plaintiff offers a distinct theory of exclusionary maintenance in a service aftermarket.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc.'s separate power and exclusion requirements and adopt a new holding resolving the intellectual-property defense it did not decide. No existing controlling precedent establishes categorical patent or copyright immunity.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A valid patent or copyright and a unilateral refusal to supply or license are neither sufficient proof of monopolization nor a categorical defense to it. A plaintiff must prove relevant-market power, exclusionary acquisition or maintenance, competitive injury, and causation. The statutory interest in controlling protected production and expression is a substantive justification; the mere exercise of exclusion or desire to profit from it cannot be condemned. But an objectively supported course of conduct using control of protected inputs to maintain monopoly power in a distinct service market may be examined for competitive harm and legitimate justification without an additional prerequisite of procurement fraud, sham litigation, or tying. The categorical limitation below therefore cannot sustain this summary judgment.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This rule governs patented replacement parts and copyrighted diagnostic software implicated in a supported monopolization claim. It does not presume that every aftermarket is distinct, that every protected input is indispensable, or that excluding a rival is unlawful. Genuine superiority, lawful returns on invention and expression, workable product support, and other established efficiency explanations remain material. Subjective dislike of competitors is insufficient; objective conduct and competitive consequences must do the work.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The legal entitlement to exclude copying or unauthorized exploitation does not itself define every antitrust market or excuse all conduct maintaining power over customers locked into a service relationship. Equally, antitrust cannot convert the value of a patented product into an obligation to share it whenever a rival would benefit. The statutes can operate together by requiring a demonstrated competitive wrong beyond the ordinary consequences of enjoying exclusivity. Xerox's end-user sales, selective verification, restrictions on alternate supply, and the alleged service consequences warrant assessment under that inquiry; they do not conclusively establish a wrong. The court below foreclosed that assessment through a categorical legal limit and a refusal to examine any relevant exclusion beyond its enumerated exceptions. Removing that limit restores the required inquiry while leaving CSU its full evidentiary burdens. A future remedy must target proved unlawful maintenance and preserve protected returns insofar as compatible with curing that wrong; it cannot simply award CSU access as a preferred competitor.

  The decisive move is to reconcile the Sherman Act's prohibition of exclusionary monopoly maintenance with the intellectual-property statutes' grant of exclusivity, rather than place either statute outside the other's reach. Kodak supplies the market inquiry and material analogy; it did not decide the full patent defense. The new rule removes the categorical defense because that defense blocks examination of a legally cognizable wrong, while keeping the plaintiff's burdens and the substantial justification supplied by exclusivity. Documents, customer and service-provider testimony, the sequence of supply restrictions, and other competent proof may establish or defeat those elements; no universal demand for an econometric study is imposed.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final monopoly power, exclusion, causal injury, damages, and any compulsory-access terms require facts not determined in this summary-judgment record. Patent validity, infringement, and copyright ownership are not separately presented for adjudication by the question presented. The merits rule on categorical protection is fully resolved; factual liability and relief return under it.

# Cedric Kushner Promotions, Ltd. v. King

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-11. **Date status:** historical.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit's dismissal judgment on the distinctness issue.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate consideration of the § 1962(c) claim, requiring proof of the remaining statutory elements and allowing preserved defenses. Award no damages, fees, or injunction at this stage.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Legal separateness under § 1962(c)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The undisputed corporate structure and dismissal posture squarely present statutory identity. The necessary issue is whether King's corporate role negates distinctness before evidence on racketeering is heard.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does an individual become legally identical to the corporation he owns merely because his alleged racketeering conduct was authorized corporate activity?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing element-by-element statutory interpretation and adopt a new application holding on distinctness, which entering law has not expressly resolved.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A natural person and a legally separate corporation satisfy the person-enterprise distinction under § 1962(c), including where that person owns the corporation and acts within his authority. The plaintiff must still prove that the person directed enterprise affairs through the required pattern and caused compensable statutory injury. King's ownership and in-scope conduct therefore cannot sustain this dismissal.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers natural owners and employees conducting a corporation's affairs. Corporate existence supplies distinct identity, not liability for ownership. It creates no respondeat superior rule and does not determine a different enterprise configuration.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress chose individual and corporate categories whose legal distinction normally matters precisely when individuals act for companies. Treating authorized operation as identity would exempt the conduct that the direction element otherwise reaches. The proposed functional-independence limitation has no support in these enacted words. Preserving all other elements answers the concern that every corporate dispute becomes a successful RICO action; inventing an identity exemption does not.

  A formal distinction is appropriate here because Congress used legally constituted persons and enterprises as separate statutory categories. That textual choice, rather than an assessment of King's economic independence from his company, is decisive. Bestfoods supports respect for corporate identity by analogy; Reves still requires actual participation in directing enterprise affairs. The categorical identity rule answers the recurring legal question without deciding the unproved racketeering allegations.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Corporate-person/association-in-fact identity and vicarious corporate liability lie outside the question presented. Whether the alleged offenses, pattern, and causal injury are proved requires facts this dismissal record lacks.

# Good News Club v. Milford Central School

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse summary judgment for Milford insofar as it validates exclusion of this otherwise comparable private program because its character teaching includes religious instruction, prayer, and worship.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct equal access for the Club under the same genuine neutral operational rules as comparable groups, with declaratory and access relief implementing that entitlement. On the stated record, independent adult control, parental authorization, and after-school use defeat the asserted establishment defense; youth, location, and devotional content do not justify withholding relief. Any remaining damages or fees require their own determinations.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Access and the governmental relationship

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The admitted community subjects and stated religious basis for denial present a speech claim. Because the school invokes its constitutional establishment duty, the permissible governmental relationship must also be decided; it cannot remain an unexamined defense to the remedy. Other independent claims and monetary relief are not reached because the speech ground supplies the judgment.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a public school exclude comparable private moral instruction because its means are devotional, and does permission-based private use itself establish religion?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply entering Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 and Agostini v. Felton. This position does not represent Stone's earlier separate disagreement as controlling or adopt silently replacing the Court's access rule.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Within this broad private community forum, religious instruction and devotional form cannot disqualify otherwise comparable moral and character instruction. Independent adult sponsorship, parental authorization, and after-school operation constitute permissible private access absent actual official adoption or substantial government-created religious pressure. Location, youth, and prayer alone do not establish those prohibited governmental acts.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the requested private Club use on the same valid terms as comparable groups. It does not authorize teacher-led official devotions, compulsory religious participation, or preferential school recruitment. Parental permission is material evidence of voluntary private choice, not an automatic answer to every institutional-pressure claim.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The school has opened moral instruction to outsiders. Its religious/secular distinction removes a viewpoint and its expressive method from an admitted subject. Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10 rejects that distinction for comparable worship. The separate establishment inquiry asks who organizes and controls the religious activity and how the school engages participants. A private meeting on school premises is not converted into governmental religious teaching by the content of its story or closing prayer.

  Stone distinguishes equal private-forum access from governmental financing or sponsorship of devotion. His earlier separate disagreement in Bronx Household does not displace that decision's controlling access rule, which he now applies to the admitted subject. Young age and immediate after-school timing warrant attention to actual official recruitment or compulsion; they do not justify demanding proof that private religious expression can never influence a child. A remand must resolve a supported material issue. Funding and compulsory-school-devotion cases retain their distinct constitutional questions.

  Stone reaffirms the controlling forum rule and distinguishes official devotion by the school's actual role. The decisive speech principle is viewpoint neutrality within the subjects admitted; the functional inquiry into sponsorship and practical pressure separately answers the school's establishment defense. Age and timing are legally relevant circumstances, not automatic disqualifications or a requirement that parents prove religious influence impossible. The stated record establishes private use and identifies no material finding of official adoption or compulsion that defeats access. Remand implements equal access rather than reopens the rejected establishment defense.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Official school devotion and materially different compulsory programs are outside this private-forum question. Any damages or fee amount requires proceedings not completed on this record; the Club's access entitlement is resolved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The thermal search and its consequences

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Kyllo preserved the deliberate home-surveillance challenge. The record identifies the sensor, target, heat pattern, inference, and lack of a scan warrant; no speculative future technology is needed.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Did agents search the home by technologically acquiring concealed domestic-operation information, and what must be corrected when the conviction was affirmed on a no-search premise?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Pinson and United States v. Myers; reject a general-public-use limitation.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Targeted technological reception of a closed home's differential heat pattern is a search when it reveals concealed domestic operation otherwise unavailable to ordinary observation. This scan disclosed the use of indoor cultivation equipment and required a warrant or recognized exception. None justified the scan itself. The later warrant cannot cure it retroactively, while admissibility requires its own preserved, fact-supported determination.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects concealed domestic information regardless of whether it is expressed as an image, numerical pattern, or inference. It does not convert ordinary external observation into a search merely because it aids investigation. Widespread purchase of a sensor does not itself waive home privacy.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Escape of physical energy is not public disclosure of the domestic facts encoded in it. The distinction between passive and penetrating equipment describes mechanism, not the privacy interest. The lower findings confirm an inference about hidden operation even while denying identifiable interior images. Applying the entering rule therefore requires correction, followed by a genuine causal and remedial inquiry rather than either automatic exclusion or automatic good faith.

  Pinson and Myers control the search characterization. Their functional treatment of concealed domestic information is decisive, supported by the home's constitutional protection; the physical mechanism and commercial availability do not supply competing exceptions. Reaffirming that rule protects future comparable thermal examinations without announcing a rule for every sensor. The distinct remedy inquiry cannot dilute the search holding or excuse the deliberate scan simply because an eventual warrant issued.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The sufficiency of the untainted warrant and particular reliance facts require lower-record findings not established here. Other surveillance technologies and open commercial premises lie outside the question presented.

# Tuan Anh Nguyen v. INS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse rejection of the equal-protection claim and vacate the removal affirmance insofar as it rests on the unconstitutional paternal-recognition condition.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the male-only timely-recognition barrier from the applicable transmission scheme and remand for confirmation of all remaining valid prerequisites under Nguyen's available statutory election. If established, recognize statutory citizenship and terminate removal on alienage grounds. If an independent valid prerequisite fails, decide its consequence through the ordinary process. Do not vacate criminal convictions or award damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equality and statutory corrective relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The citizen father personally challenges unequal transmission; biological parentage and an actual relationship are established in the described record. Because alienage supports removal, the constitutional condition and its remedial consequence both require decision.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Congress add a male-only minority recognition deadline to otherwise established parentage, and can the resulting statutory inequality be cured without withdrawing citizenship from children of mothers?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing demanding sex-classification scrutiny and adopt a new application and severability holding. Miller v. Albright left these points unresolved; this position does not claim its separate opinions already established them.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A sex-specific citizenship condition must substantially advance its genuine objective. The additional paternal-recognition deadline fails where the statute already requires reliable proof of biological parentage and its formal acts do not ensure an actual relationship: it excludes an established caregiving father while accepting bare timely acknowledgment. Sever the discriminatory barrier, leaving Congress's underlying citizenship transmission and lawful remaining qualifications in force.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This reaches the challenged male-only recognition requirement and its application to Nguyen's available statutory regime. It leaves legitimate parentage proof, citizen-parent status, and applicable physical presence intact. It does not constitutionalize all parent-child transmission or excuse unrelated valid qualifications.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Birth makes maternal identification easier; it does not justify a permanent male-only loss after parentage is reliably proved. Opportunity for a relationship is important, but a mother can relinquish the child immediately and a father can meet the statutory act without sustained contact. Nguyen's upbringing exposes the mismatch without making an individual hardship exception the rule. Corrective extension preserves the general transmission scheme and settled citizenship interests more faithfully than withdrawing maternal transmission. This is enforcement of a statute after constitutional severance, not free-floating equitable naturalization contrary to a valid statute.

  The Government's strongest distinction is real: birth ordinarily establishes maternal identity and an initial opportunity for contact more directly than it establishes paternity. Intermediate scrutiny permits reasonable generalization from actual differences and does not demand a perfect proxy or the least restrictive measure. Stone nevertheless would reject this additional sex-specific recognition deadline because reliable parentage is already separately required and the formal act can admit a father with no sustained relationship while excluding the established relationship here. Those features bear on substantial fit; underinclusiveness alone is not the rule of invalidity. Removing that barrier does not erase other unreviewed support or residence conditions.

  Demanding intermediate scrutiny supplies the standard. Comparison of the statute's treatment of mothers and fathers and its own recognition requirements tests substantial fit; the statutory design and established parentage supply the decisive reasons without requiring a demographic study. This is a general rejection of the additional sex-based deadline, not a sympathetic exception for one family. Severability implements that conclusion while preserving valid transmission requirements.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Separate support and physical-presence classifications are outside this reviewed recognition question. Definitive citizenship depends on any remaining qualification findings absent from the record.

# Alabama v. Bozeman

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-11. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 11, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Alabama Supreme Court's enforcement of Article IV(e).

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require dismissal with prejudice of the covered charges. Any ministerial implementation belongs to the state court; no further prejudice hearing or discretionary balancing is necessary on the established unwaived return.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Effect of the premature return

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved direct-review claim and the identity of the receiving sovereign establish the governing remedial channel. Statutory interpretation resolves the case; alternative constitutional theories are unnecessary.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a court disregard Article IV(e) because the return lasted only a short time and allegedly helped the prisoner?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted command and preserve New York v. Hill's actual, limited waiver rule. This is a new application to antishuttling, not a new judicial exception to the agreement.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a receiving state obtains a prisoner under Article IV and returns him to the original place of imprisonment before trial, Article IV(e) requires dismissal with prejudice unless a legally valid exception or waiver actually applies. Brief duration and absence of prejudice do not themselves supply either.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches this pretrial return to federal prison from Alabama custody. It does not equate every scheduling discussion with a waiver of custody protections or eliminate special statutory provisions applicable to a federal receiving jurisdiction.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agreement chooses a concrete event and consequence. The overnight detention and next-day arraignment occurred; trial had not occurred before the return. Rehabilitation can be served through lawful arrangements and an actual waiver where permitted, but a court cannot infer consent merely because officials believe the prisoner benefited. Nor may a later trial erase a violation that the agreement makes dispositive. The severity of dismissal reflects the enacted incentive to organize custody correctly; it does not license a court to insert harmless-error language.

  The enacted event-and-remedy rule is categorical because Article IV(e) makes the premature return, not a later showing of prejudice, dispositive. Text carries decisive weight. Hill concerns an actual authorized scheduling waiver and is distinguished from officials' assumption that their chosen return benefited Bozeman. This general application supplies a usable custody rule without inventing a de minimis exception that would remove the agreement's protection.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The requirements for a different prisoner's actual antishuttling waiver remain outside this record, which supplies no such waiver. No other issue is reserved.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 15, 2001.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment denying review and remand.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct the lower court to set aside the challenged order and require a fresh hearing before a constitutionally appointed adjudicator, followed by a fresh Board decision. Treat the FDIC's abandonment of its alternative valid-appointment defense as controlling in this case; do not decide the constitutional status of every independent agency. The FDIC may pursue the same charges through lawful proceedings; Landry receives no merits exoneration or damages award.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The timely preserved objection reaches an adjudicator who exercised authority in Landry's own case. Determine office status and the consequences of the abandoned validity defense. No independent ruling on whether the FDIC is a constitutional department is needed.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether statutory hearing authority becomes employee assistance solely because the Board retains final decisional power.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the functional and hierarchical principles of Edmond v. United States and adopt their extension to this administrative hearing office. The extension is new; the military-office holding did not itself decide FDIC ALJ status.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An ALJ occupying a continuing office established by law and exercising substantial discretion in conducting required evidentiary hearings, ruling on evidence, enforcing procedural orders, and issuing findings and recommendations is an inferior officer even though the Board reviews the record de novo and issues the final order. On the agency's litigated position here, no valid appointment defense remains.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding answers officer status for this office and permits the Court to reach cure on the conceded appointment premise. It imposes no final-decision prerequisite for inferior officers.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** These are delegated exercises of governmental adjudicatory authority, not private suggestions that acquire significance only when adopted. Superior review explains subordination; it does not erase the power exercised during the hearing. The Board's accountability for its own order cannot supply the missing constitutional appointment of another officer.

  Freytag's treatment of significant discretionary hearing authority supplies a closer analogy than a final-order requirement. Edmond explains inferior status through supervision rather than denying office altogether. The combination is structural and functional: the constitutional allocation of appointment power controls, while the legally assigned duties identify whether that allocation applies. This office's consequential evidentiary powers meet the standard despite the Board's final review.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The agency abandoned its department-head appointment defense, so that validity ground is not preserved for decision here. The instrument for appointing a future ALJ and classification of other administrative roles are outside the questions presented.

### Issue 2

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The agency preserved cure, so decide it. The banking merits and unrelated discovery grounds need not be adjudicated to order lawful proceedings.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether independent Board review of the existing administrative record substitutes for a lawful hearing.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Ryder v. United States to this timely challenge and adopt a new resolution of its expressly reserved different-review-structure question.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** De novo Board review of the record created by an unconstitutionally appointed ALJ does not cure that officer's conduct of the required evidentiary hearing. A timely challenger is entitled to a fresh lawful hearing and decision without demonstrating that appointment error changed the ultimate result.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Reject cure through this form of record review. Do not announce that a genuinely fresh adjudication by a lawfully appointed decisionmaker can never remedy a prior appointment defect.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Board reconsidered evidence and recommendations; it did not replace the two-week hearing through which evidence was admitted, excluded, developed, and evaluated. Requiring proof of a different outcome would make lawful appointment effectively optional whenever a superior approved the resulting record. Strong admissions may support a new order, but they do not eliminate the timely claimant's entitlement to the lawful adjudication the government selected as its enforcement mechanism. The Appointments Clause allocates public authority. Relief must correct an unconstitutional appointment while preserving lawful enforcement. No alternative disposition is approved.

  Ryder's protection for a timely direct challenge is extended to this actual review mechanism. Record review cannot retroactively provide lawful appointment to the officer who controlled the evidentiary hearing. The appointment requirement is decisive; the concrete role of hearing rulings explains why this is more than a harmless labeling error. Landry's admissions may make the agency's eventual substantive case strong, but do not eliminate the constitutional entitlement to the lawful adjudication through which that case must be established.

# Saucier v. Katz

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening controlling law before June 18, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply material corrections to the record and any effective intervening decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand under the replacement personal-immunity rule stated below. Reject both the Ninth Circuit's account of existing precedent and a judgment granting Saucier immunity on this disputed record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Determine the force claim on facts properly assumed at this interlocutory stage, then decide whether Saucier proves the separate authoritative-reliance defense. Remand genuine disputes about the acts, perceived danger, resistance, and personal participation for lawful factfinding; permit bounded submissions on the new defense. A finding that the defense fails does not establish damages, a constitutional violation, institutional liability, or a new federal cause of action.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Constitutional reasonableness and a distinct reliance defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved interlocutory appeal permits decision of the legal relationship between excessive force and immunity on the facts the court must assume. It does not authorize reweighing disputed historical facts. The question directly presents the asserted second allowance for otherwise unconstitutional force; resolving that defense requires confronting the precedents sustaining it.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a finding of constitutionally unreasonable force necessarily defeats personal immunity, and whether the record permits judgment for this officer under the proper distinction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose overruling Harlow and Anderson to the extent that they, as carried forward in Hunter, Elder, and Wilson, recognize a personal defense merely because the violated right was insufficiently clearly established. Preserve their compatible requirements of particularized constitutional analysis and lawful summary-judgment review. Replace the displaced defense with a defined protection for actual reasonable reliance on directly authorizing law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** First apply the complete substantive constitutional rule, including its allowances for urgency, reasonably perceived danger, reasonable factual mistakes, and operational discretion. If that rule establishes a violation, an individual official bears the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, genuine reliance on it, and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately conveying that authority may implement reliance. Legal silence, the absence of a materially identical case, department custom, a supervisor's unsupported assurance, and general authority to perform the job do not establish the defense. Thus an unreasonable-force finding does not invariably eliminate every immunity defense, but the same factual reasonableness allowances cannot excuse the violation a second time.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the personal legal-uncertainty defense asserted against this existing Fourth Amendment damages claim and materially equivalent claims. It supplies a safe harbor only when all authoritative-reliance requirements are met; it neither establishes liability outside the harbor nor expands the underlying cause of action. Function-specific absolute immunities, sovereign immunity, proper defendants, causation, and compensable injury retain their separate requirements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Graham already tests force against the circumstances reasonably confronting the officer. A second defense based on the absence of sufficiently similar precedent can defeat an otherwise available remedy even after that complete standard establishes wrongful force. The legitimate interest in protecting officials against unexpected legal change is narrower: it concerns conduct reasonably undertaken under affirmative authoritative permission. The replacement protects that reliance without making judicial silence permission to violate a right. This is a deliberate change in judge-made remedial doctrine, not a claim that the existing cases already say it. The prior rule's predictability and litigation-control benefits count in its favor, but they do not justify withholding an otherwise authorized remedy from every first claimant establishing a particular application of a settled protection. The fit between the defense and justified legal reliance is decisive; the functional concern about duplicate reasonableness supports that conclusion. This case calls for a reusable replacement because accepting or rejecting this officer's legal-uncertainty defense cannot be explained consistently without identifying what the defense protects.

  Applied here, vice-presidential security, what Katz did, whether he resisted, and the force needed to move him concern the merits. Katz's testimony and other admissible accounts of the shove are evidence; a lack of serious injury does not establish that all force was justified. Conversely, protest activity does not prevent necessary proportionate security measures. Taking Katz's supported account as true, an unnecessary shove of an already controlled, nonresisting demonstrator violates Graham's proportional-force requirement even without physical injury. Necessary movement away from a protected official remains lawful; disputed resistance and the officers' perceived security needs bear on whether the shove was actually unnecessary. This resolves the legal sufficiency of the competing factual accounts without choosing whose account is true. The present record identifies no binding precedent or enactment directly authorizing the allegedly unnecessary shove. General authority to arrest or protect the Vice President cannot supply that missing permission. The court must resolve the claim and defense using the facts properly available at the relevant procedural stage, without assuming that denial of immunity proves the claim.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Disputed force, resistance, perceptions, participation, and actual reliance require findings not available in this interlocutory record. The First Amendment and false-arrest dispositions are outside this granted excessive-force inquiry. New federal-officer causes of action and public compensation schemes are not presented.

### Issue 2: Application of the revised defense to pending litigation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** A change in the defense requires an express transition rule because Saucier seeks protection for past conduct in a pending case.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the revised defense applies here and how actual legal reliance and completed judgments are protected.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the new personal-defense rule to this pending case while preserving final judgments and ordinary rules of preservation and preclusion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The replacement defense governs this case. Actual reasonable reliance on directly authorizing law remains protected. An expectation that unclear law would defeat damages is not reliance on legal permission for the challenged conduct and does not preserve the displaced defense. Final judgments are not reopened.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This pending immunity appeal and ordinary subsequent application of the effective rule; no automatic reopening of concluded litigation or elimination of independent litigation defenses.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Withholding the new rule from the claimant who properly presents the defect would retain the remedial barrier without protecting a legally authorized act. At the same time, a fair chance to address genuinely new defense elements avoids deciding unlitigated reliance facts by assumption. Bounded further proceedings provide that opportunity without restarting matters already conclusively resolved. The transition therefore implements the same distinction between concrete permission and a general expectation of immunity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No final judgment is before the Court, and the treatment of another pending case's forfeited argument depends on that case's procedural record.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Government presents both the effect of the classification letter and the correctness of the classification. The authorized tariff appeal permits resolution of each, and the undisputed description of the merchandise permits independent construction rather than an open-ended classification remand.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this Customs ruling letter receives controlling deference and, if not, what judicial consideration its explanation warrants.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Haggar's requirement of actual delegated authority and Christensen's distinction between controlling and persuasive interpretation. Extend those principles to the particular Customs mechanism rather than overrule Chevron or attribute to the earlier decisions an answer they expressly left unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** General responsibility for administering a statute does not itself delegate power to bind reviewing courts through every agency interpretation. This transaction-specific, revisable ruling letter does not exercise a demonstrated congressional delegation to make controlling law through that mechanism. The court independently determines tariff meaning, considering the letter's reasoning to the extent its thoroughness, expertise, logic, and consistency warrant under Skidmore.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This classification mechanism and materially equivalent ruling letters. A valid regulation exercising an actual delegation remains governed by Haggar; genuinely delegated bounded policy choices remain distinct from an agency's assertion of controlling statutory meaning.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The letters' limited reach, issuance by numerous offices, transaction-specific operation, and revisability explain their administrative function. They bind the agency's handling of particular entries without thereby establishing law for independent judicial review. Those features are evidence of legal function, not a numerical rule or a categorical requirement of notice and comment for every valid delegation. The statutory allocation of authority carries decisive weight; institutional practice identifies how that allocation operates. Conversely, denying controlling force does not erase Customs' knowledge or explanation. Its changed treatment weakens a claim to settled consistency, but the dictionary and commercial reasoning can still persuade on their merits. Neither expertise alone nor the bare absence of a regulation resolves both questions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Interpretive mechanisms under other statutory delegations and the separate question of deference to an agency's own regulations lie outside this grant.

### Issue 2: Classification of the imported planners

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The second petition question presents the tariff coverage of these planners. The product features material to that legal question are identified and undisputed; no missing fact prevents a decision.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the tariff provision for bound diaries includes these ring-bound day planners with calendar and memorandum pages.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Independently construe the tariff text and accept the persuasive portions of Customs' explanation. The agency prevails because its reading is correct, not because a merely reasonable interpretation controls the court.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A diary within the relevant subheading includes a volume organized for dated entries concerning appointments and daily affairs; it is not confined to retrospective narrative. Pages secured together in a ring binder form a bound volume even though individual pages can be removed. These imported planners therefore fall within the bound-diary provision rather than the residual duty-free category.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The ring-bound planners described in this record and equivalent dated-entry volumes. Loose unattached sheets do not become bound merely because they can later be assembled; the holding concerns the imported article as presented for classification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The tariff's ordinary words do the decisive work. Nothing in diary limits entries to events already completed, and nothing in bound requires sewing, glue, or permanently irremovable pages. A binder's fastening function supplies the relevant distinction from loose paper. Calendars and spaces for notes, appointments, and related daily information make these articles diaries despite their prospective uses and auxiliary telephone pages. Customs' commercial experience and explanation reinforce that reading but supply no independent power to enlarge the enacted category. The Federal Circuit's narrower treatment substitutes restrictions the tariff does not contain. A direct decision is warranted because both the interpretive issue and the merchandise features are before the Court; remand is needed only to implement the legal answer through the entry-specific judgment and calculations.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The monetary consequences for particular entries require implementation of the resolved classification; unrelated products and later tariff amendments are outside the question presented.
