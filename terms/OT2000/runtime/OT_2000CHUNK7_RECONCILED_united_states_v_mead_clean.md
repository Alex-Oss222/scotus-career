## Verified comparator and corrected premise

Historically the Court vacated and remanded, eight to one. Souter wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented, would give the official agency interpretation controlling reasonable-choice treatment, sustain the classification and reverse. The historical majority denied controlling Chevron weight to this mechanism while retaining Skidmore consideration; it left classification undecided. These are materially distinct grounds and remedies, even though all rejected the importer's complete appellate victory.

The original neutral packet omitted a material public litigation-position fact. The official report at page 258 states that the Solicitor General filed a brief cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel representing the classification in the ruling letter as Customs' official position. The letter itself was signed by the Director of the Commercial Rulings Branch of the headquarters regulations-and-rulings office. The source-only extraction was returned to fresh affected neutral validation and modeling, which accepted the public representation and refreshed every Associate's ground before this reconciliation resumed.

This is a verified published litigation position, not a recovered internal approval instrument, an original Secretary or Commissioner signature, or a new regulation. Both the historical majority's note 19 and Scalia's pages 258-259 discussion engage the legal significance of that public endorsement. Neither its absence nor conjectural hidden approval supplies the present disposition. The seven-Associate ground accepts the endorsement but denies its legal sufficiency to establish this mechanism's controlling interpretive authority; Scalia treats it as concrete satisfaction of his official-position condition.

## Proposition-level rules and coalition

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply seven direct commitments to the following rule and remand. Controlling Chevron treatment requires a congressional conferral of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. The conferral may be express or evident from the statutory scheme. Formal rulemaking or adjudication is important evidence, but neither notice and comment nor a particular document label is an exclusive prerequisite. An agency's expertise, administrative responsibility or official endorsement alone does not establish that the particular action exercises the required authority.

The Customs mechanism here does not satisfy that test. Its individualized operation, modifiability, treatment of third-party reliance, numerous issuing offices, and place in the protest/judicial-review scheme together distinguish it from Haggar's actual delegated regulation. Independent judicial factfinding does not itself defeat deference to lawfully delegated interpretation; Haggar remains intact. Headquarters' more developed reasoning and the public institutional endorsement merit attention but do not establish a separate congressional conferral that gives this letter controlling legal force. Neither the signer's rank nor the absence of public procedures is independently dispositive.

Lack of controlling force does not make the reasons irrelevant. Under Skidmore the court considers actual thoroughness, quality of reasoning, specialized experience, consistency, commercial usage and other sources of persuasive force. There is no predetermined amount of weight, and the departure from prior classification must be evaluated rather than denied. The Federal Circuit's wholesale refusal to consider Customs' reasoning therefore cannot stand; classification returns for the proper judicial inquiry.

Scalia supplies no eighth join to those threshold or Skidmore propositions. His agreement that the Federal Circuit wrongly discarded the agency's position rests on a different controlling-deference rule. He regards congressional assignment of classification, the genuine tariff ambiguity and the publicly confirmed official agency position as sufficient for reasonable-choice treatment, absent a contrary statutory limit. His Christensen concurrence supplies his resistance to a form-based exclusion; the present representation supplies the official-position fact that could not be presumed in that earlier case. He does not claim that every view expressed by any employee receives controlling deference.

## Classification, competing paths and scope

Mead's strongest direct textual argument is that a planner with removable pages is not an ordinarily bound diary and that the residual category must retain work. Customs answers that a ring mechanism binds the pages and that a diary may provide dated space for prospective scheduling and memoranda rather than only retrospective narrative. Its explanatory letter supplies dictionary and commercial reasons addressing those actual goods. Those competing arguments remain distinct from the agency-authority question.

The seven remand Justices conclude that useful interpretive assistance was wrongly excluded and leave the tariff answer for the corrected judicial inquiry. They do not hold either that Customs must prevail or that its interpretation is necessarily unreasonable. They do not infer a failure of proof from the unrecovered administrative file. They accept the possibility that genuinely clear tariff text governs independently of agency weight, but do not select that direct disposition here.

Scalia considers the ring-bound planner with calendar and note pages reasonably within the disputed bound-diary category and would sustain that classification under the delegated-choice standard. The residual provision does not compel his opposite answer. His judgment is an actual classification result, not merely an additional vote to reopen the question. He recognizes the strongest contrary point that administrative implementation does not invariably create law controlling courts, but regards the statutory responsibility and now concretely supported institutional interpretation as sufficient here. No monetary assessment or refund amount follows merely from choosing his interpretive ground.

The 1993 classification is governed by its actual legal regime. Later statutory procedures concerning modification of rulings cannot retroactively supply process or authority for this action. The public litigation endorsement similarly does not fabricate original notice and comment, an agency-head signature or a new regulation. The decision does not resolve every later ruling under every changed procedure.

## Precedent treatment and separate-position function

Haggar retains controlling respect for a lawful exercise of actual delegated regulatory authority and rejects an automatic no-deference inference from judicial factfinding. Its reserved informal-letter application is resolved here at this mechanism's scope. Christensen retains the advisory-letter disposition and independent Skidmore role; its actual holding is not enlarged into a categorical exclusion of every informal administrative act. Appalachian Power retains reviewability based on completed legal consequences and its separate prohibition on interpretive amendment of a regulation. Reviewability does not itself establish judicially controlling interpretive authority. Chevron and Skidmore perform their distinct delegated-choice and persuasive-reasoning functions.

Scalia's separate position must explain his official-position criterion, statutory delegation, reasonableness conclusion and different remedy. His objection to replacing a presumptive authoritative-agency rule with a less determinate delegation inquiry remains substantive. Predictions that future courts or agencies will behave in a particular way are not findings or present law. Historical authorship and joinders are comparison evidence only; assembly chooses actual authorship and final joins.

## Remedies, alternatives and unresolved matters

The seven-Associate disposition vacates the Federal Circuit judgment and remands for independent classification informed by the letter's persuasive force; the appropriate lower court may perform that work within ordinary jurisdiction and procedure. It does not command a particular tariff category, duty amount, refund, new agency proceeding or future rulemaking. No search for an original agency-head signature is a condition of remand.

Scalia would reverse the contrary appellate classification and sustain Customs' construction of the disputed subheading; only ordinary consequential assessment matters remain. He does not alternatively support an open classification remand as an interchangeable judgment. Agreement that the appellate reasoning fails cannot erase that remedial difference.
