# OT_2000CHUNK5

# Bartnicki v. Vopper / United States v. Vopper

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Third Circuit judgment in both consolidated petitions, on the limited as-applied ground stated below, leaving its direction to enter summary judgment for these respondents operative on the certified record and assumptions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enter judgment against the federal and parallel Pennsylvania disclosure claims within the accepted case. Award no damages to either side and issue no general injunction against the wiretap statutes. Do not reopen the case to seek a new theory that the respondents procured or participated in interception when the certified premise is the opposite. Any genuinely distinct preserved claim must rest on its own pleaded elements and record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The publisher’s conduct and the public-concern disclosure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory coverage question first and reject an interceptor-only reading; then decide the as-applied constitutional defense for all respondents. Public concern, accurate transcription, lawful receipt and nonparticipation are the premises of that decision. A professional-media classification is unnecessary because Yocum’s communication is itself speech. The assessment concerns publication of the recorded discussion, not whether the participants could be convicted of making threats.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State may impose disclosure damages on these speakers solely because a stranger illegally intercepted the public-concern conversation they lawfully obtained.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt an extension of existing publication protection to this specific unlawful-source setting. Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission and Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. supply governing distinctions and scrutiny obligations; they do not already decide this result. Accept a serious interest in conversational privacy, including its support for candid private speech, while requiring justification responsive to the present defendants and expression.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The First Amendment bars the federal and parallel state disclosure damages at issue where defendants played no part in the unlawful interception, acquired the recording lawfully, and disclosed accurate information about this public-school labor controversy. Knowledge or reason to know that a stranger intercepted the conversation unlawfully does not alone justify suppressing those disclosures.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Resolve both petitions and the nonmedia conduit’s claim. The rule covers the disclosed bargaining discussion and its coercive-sounding language because those contents bear on the public controversy. It does not declare all private statements by public participants available for publication or create a categorical press exemption from valid acquisition laws.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Privacy injury can arise upon dissemination and is not answered completely by saying that only the interceptor acted wrongly. Nonetheless, these defendants did not induce the invasion, and publication supplies information directly relevant to a contested public negotiation. The Government’s proposed chain from punishing these disclosures to deterring anonymous interception does not sufficiently justify this burden on that public discussion. Independent review remains compatible with supported legislative prediction; the conclusion does not demand a particular study in every case. The communicated words heighten the public interest in understanding the controversy without requiring a judicial finding that criminal violence was actually planned. Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. confirms that purposeful participation would be a materially different basis of responsibility, but knowledge of the tape’s unlawful origin is not purposeful assistance to the completed interception. The same reasoning applies to Yocum’s delivery and Vopper’s broadcast.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Disclosures of purely private matters, trade secrets, and recordings procured or unlawfully obtained by a defendant are outside the accepted question and its nonparticipation and public-concern premises. Their constitutional treatment is reserved on that scope ground. No unsupported factual finding of criminal-threat liability is made; that separate claim is not presented.
