# District of Columbia v. Tri County Industries, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved; renewed 2026-11-08 by express user approval of this Tri County version on the corrected first-trial record.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision before 2001-01-17.
* **Conditions:** Subject to material changes in the record or intervening controlling law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment reinstating the first verdict, on ordinary abuse-of-discretion review with independent correction of the identified legal errors.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the $5 million verdict and the appellate interest amendment, without ordering another trial or creating a general damages presumption for permit suspensions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Review and actual loss

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the question and would not dismiss the writ. The Court of Appeals expressly stated its "more searching inquiry" formulation, drawn from binding circuit precedent (Langevine), as part of the standard it applied to this new-trial order, so the question was passed upon below and may be reviewed (United States v. Williams). Respondent's objection that the District first challenged that formulation on rehearing, and the District's reply that it sought deferential review in its appellate brief, remain competing submissions; the rehearing denial gives no reason, and Stone makes no waiver finding. The order's several grounds can be resolved on the existing first-trial record under ordinary abuse-of-discretion review, without new facts: the Court can state the governing standard and apply it to each ground. Under that standard the judgment stands, so Tri County's unreached second-trial challenges require no decision.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the new-trial order can stand on its stated legal and record grounds.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Review legal premises independently and discretionary evaluations deferentially. A trial judge ruling on a new-trial motion may reconsider an earlier discretionary exclusion, but a reconsideration that supports a new trial needs a lawful relevance and evidentiary foundation. On the first-trial record, including the excluded letters, health-and-safety testimony and closure proffer, three things do not sustain this order: the erroneous categorical mitigation premise, a reconsideration without that foundation, and the unsupported displacement of the jury's supported assessment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An appellate court may set aside a remittitur or new-trial order for identified legal error and unsupported discretionary grounds without conducting its own initial damages valuation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the grounds actually used and the record then available. It supplies neither a general heightened appellate standard nor automatic lost-profit recovery whenever a permit is suspended without adequate process.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** At the first trial the District sought to prove that community opposition and further regulatory action would have ended the operation. It offered the October 15 and December 6, 1993 letters, health-and-safety testimony and the expert testimony of Alfred Siess, and it pointed to the remaining certificate-of-occupancy inspections and a repeat stack test. The trial judge repeatedly asked the District to identify the lawful mechanism by which it could have stopped an operation it had already permitted, and invited it to make that showing. He assumed that the equipment would pass the required tests at Bryant Street, which the permit conditions themselves required; the contrary suggestions came from counsel and the excluded Siess proffer. He found no proper evidentiary basis on which the jury could find that the District ultimately would have refused permission, struck the letters and the health-and-safety testimony, and excluded Siess. He left to the jury the District's defenses based on the unpaid fine and the failure to appeal the stop-work order. The July 23 order reconsidered those exclusions. A trial judge may do that, and the permissibility of the original exclusion does not by itself make the reconsideration an abuse of discretion. Stone does not rest on that proposition. He rests on the absence of a lawful foundation for the reconsidered proof, which the Court of Appeals also found. The order identifies no lawful mechanism and relies on no evidence beyond the proffer already excluded for want of one; it speaks only of a realistic prospect of community resistance and a very real likelihood of closure. Community opposition is not itself lawful regulation. The order does not rely on the occupancy process or on any citizen challenge or misrepresentation proceeding; the judge noted at trial that no such proceeding had occurred, and Stone does not decide whether 11 DCMR §3203.1 or Woodley Park could supply a mechanism on a different record. The order's excessiveness reason relies particularly on that same excluded prospect, and its speculation reason relies on it in part. Its other doubts about the profit model (the plaintiff's inexperience, the unanalyzed Virginia failure and falling prices per ton) go to the weight of disputed valuation evidence. The jury was instructed to weigh those very considerations, including new-business uncertainty, inexperience, competition and the failure of like businesses, and it awarded less than half of the projection. On the first record those doubts do not support displacing its assessment. The order's categorical mitigation premise is legal error: mitigation was a jury question on which the District bore the burden, and the jury was instructed on the defenses actually tried. The order does not rest expressly on the October 15 letter as an unanswered invitation to be heard; to the extent its mitigation ground rests on Tri County's failure to seek reinstatement, it is that same categorical premise. The District otherwise had the first trial to contest the expert's premises. Evidence first offered at the second trial cannot justify the earlier order. The jury awarded materially less than the expert’s projection, and the appellate record identifies concrete legal defects in the trial judge’s reasons. The statutory interest consequences follow the surviving judgment. Stone reaffirms Gasperini’s abuse-of-discretion allocation and distinguishes its state-law diversity measure. Legal error receives independent correction; disputed valuation is not retried on appeal. Expert testimony and its factual premises, contemporaneous business records, and competent evidence of mitigation and causation are the relevant proof; the absence of a separate statistical study is immaterial. The first record, including the excluded proffers, and the identified errors supply the decisive application, so the judgment announces no new amount-of-damages formula.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether a different, properly supported new-trial order or a materially different first-trial record would require deference is outside this record.
* **Approved Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the Court dismisses the writ, Stone dissents from the dismissal and states that he would reach the question and affirm on the grounds above.
