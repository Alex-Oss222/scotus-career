# Duncan v. Walker

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit's ruling that the first federal habeas petition tolled the period under § 2244(d)(2), and remand.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower court must calculate timeliness without excluding the first federal proceeding under this subsection. It may address any independently preserved, legally available basis for a different commencement date or equitable relief under the applicable law and actual record. If none exists, the petition must be dismissed as untimely. This judgment grants no writ, release, resentencing, or automatic extension, and it does not convert the first dismissal into a merits adjudication.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A federal filing is not state collateral review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the meaning of § 2244(d)(2), because that interpretation supplied the appellate court's reason for reinstatement. The underlying constitutional claims and the ultimate entitlement to habeas relief are unnecessary to decide that issue. The stated transitional deadline can be used as the review premise without resolving every possible AEDPA commencement dispute.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress excluded the duration of a prior federal habeas application from the one-year period by excluding pending applications for state postconviction or other collateral review.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statute and entering temporal and successive-application rules. Adopt a new holding on the unresolved grammatical question, without modifying Lindh v. Murphy or Slack v. McDaniel. Their existing protections do not authorize a separate judicial extension of the statutory exclusion.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** “State” modifies both coordinated forms of review in § 2244(d)(2). A federal habeas petition therefore is not a qualifying state postconviction or other collateral application, and its pendency does not stop the limitation period under that subsection. This applies even when the earlier federal petition was timely and dismissed without prejudice for exhaustion. Walker's appellate reinstatement cannot rest on the exclusion claimed here.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs applications for federal collateral review and distinguishes them from collateral proceedings conducted in a state system. It does not make every state filing qualifying: the application's proper filing, connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency remain statutory conditions. A prisoner's civil-rights pleading does not become state collateral review simply by sharing a document with a habeas application.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** In ordinary coordinated usage the initial modifier reaches both categories. The alternative makes “State” largely pointless, whereas the adopted construction leaves “other collateral review” meaningful for varying state procedures and custody outside an ordinary criminal judgment. This textual account also fits the distinct statutory jobs: state litigation can complete exhaustion while federal habeas invokes the forum for which Congress imposed the filing period. A rule that a return petition is not successive does not erase the time condition.

The unfairness alleged for petitioners whose first federal application remains pending beyond the deadline deserves accurate treatment, but it does not change the enacted category. Walker's own first dismissal left roughly nine months under the appellate calculation, and the source records no intervening state filing. The Court cannot invent an impediment or identical-claim finding to make the example his case. Equally, it should not announce that this statutory interpretation extinguishes every independently lawful equitable remedy. Those matters require their own legal source and an actual preserved factual basis.

  Artuz concerns what makes a state application properly filed; it cannot answer whether a federal filing counts as state review. If Artuz has become an effective same-term holding, apply it within that scope. Slack's protection from successive-petition treatment also supplies no new limitations period. Stone leaves an actually preserved equitable claim for lawful adjudication, but neither promises equitable tolling nor decides a stay-and-abeyance regime absent the presented issue.

  The decisive basis is the text read in its statutory structure, not a preference for finality over an otherwise available remedy. Slack is reaffirmed within its successive-petition scope and does not settle limitations. A dismissal on remand must follow an actual timeliness calculation and adjudication of any properly available alternative; the court may not treat today's rejection of statutory tolling as rejection of equitable tolling on facts never examined. A general stay procedure is outside the granted tolling question and is not silently imposed here.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether court misdirection or an extraordinary external impediment permits equitable tolling is outside the statutory question reviewed and lacks supporting findings here. The legal requirements for staying a mixed federal petition likewise lie outside this question. No other issue is reserved merely because it is difficult.

# Idaho v. United States

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment sustaining federal trust title to the bed and banks within the present reservation.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the district court's geographically bounded quiet-title decree recognizing the United States as trustee and the Tribe as beneficiary. Do not expand the decree to the ceded northern lake, other aboriginal territory, or newly described coordinates. No damages, forced conveyance, or general regulatory injunction follows merely from recognition of title. Any implementation must conform to the adjudicated reservation boundaries and actual decree.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Congress retained the defined unceded estate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the title question in the United States' own suit. The prior immunity litigation did not adjudicate ownership or disable this claimant. The historical findings and Idaho's concessions permit application of the existing clear-intent standard without an evidentiary remand.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress's legally operative treatment of this known reservation before Idaho's admission sufficiently retained its remaining submerged lands against the State's otherwise presumptive title.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Alaska's two independent required showings to a different documentary record. Do not lower the requirement to mere geographical inclusion, substitute a tribal-policy preference for federal law, or make the later ratification the original divesting act. This is an application of existing law rather than revision of the equal-footing standard.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Where executive inclusion of submerged lands is conceded, and Congress before statehood knowingly legislates concerning the identified reservation as an estate requiring tribal consent and compensation for authorized access and acquisition of selected portions, the enacted record can clearly manifest retention of the unceded submerged estate. The 1888 and 1889 measures, read with the known boundaries and admission instruments here, meet that requirement. Idaho therefore did not acquire the beds and banks remaining within the reservation on July 3, 1890.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the presently disputed reservation bed and banks and depends on the combination of actual prestatehood enactments, identified property, congressional knowledge, and limited consensual acquisition. It does not make a Senate vote law, automatically ratify an agreement, or reserve every navigable bed enclosed by an executive boundary. It also does not treat every general Indian-land disclaimer as an independently sufficient reservation of submerged title.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Alaska requires clarity about both the included property and the decision to retain it. The first is conceded. For the second, Congress did more than remain silent after a presidential description. It legislated concerning crossings of the known reservation and required tribal consent and payment, then authorized acquisition of only those portions the Tribe agreed to sell. The defined subject included the lake and river; the partial cession deliberately left the lower lake and river in the remaining estate. Reading admission as silently transferring that remainder would contradict the property arrangement Congress had already authorized and maintained.

The State's objection to the unratified agreements is correct as far as it goes. Neither negotiations nor Senate passage completed congressional ratification, and the later statute could not reclaim an estate that had already vested in Idaho. The conclusion instead rests on the earlier enacted treatment of the known reservation and the admission context. Later ratification confirms that arrangement; it does not perform retroactive divestiture. The Tribe's water-dependent uses help identify the reservation's understood subject, but they cannot supply missing legislative power or make equity override title. The combination is sufficiently clear without requiring a particular verbal formula.

  This combines historical interpretation with the governing clear-intent rule. The legally enacted prestatehood treatment of the identified estate carries decisive weight; tribal uses, negotiations, surveys, and admission context explain its object without substituting cultural importance for congressional action. Reaffirming Alaska's two showings preserves the State's equal-footing protection while recognizing the retained property that the actual enactments establish.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Regulatory jurisdiction over nonmembers, taxes, fishing restrictions, environmental regulation, and title outside the present reservation are outside the question presented. Any dispute requiring a new geographical line would also require the actual survey evidence absent from the available record. No other issue is reserved.

### Issue 1: Governmental religious protection within instruction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the live challenge to the official resolution; no coercion finding is necessary to the sponsorship question.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the board uses its instructional authority to shelter a religious creation account from the effect of scientific instruction.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the controlling functional establishment method to a new curricular application, without changing that method or reviving independent Lemon prongs.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A school may distinguish academic instruction from compulsory personal belief, but may not require teachers to assure students, as part of a targeted curricular statement, that instruction will preserve a specified religious account. The actual resolution performs that prohibited function and cannot be sustained by its references to critical thinking and other concepts.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Covers this required biblical-creation disclaimer, including the parental-belief and critical-thinking language read in context. It does not prohibit teaching about creation beliefs, academic criticism, private statements, or evenhanded protection against compelled religious professions.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The message belongs to the board. Its selected religious account, mandatory placement, and expressed assurance give it a function beyond explaining the scientific curriculum. The State need not demand students reject parental religion, but it cannot undertake to preserve that religion through official instruction. The absence of prayer avoids one specific prohibition; it does not resolve the separate use of public authority to protect religious adherence. The supported adoption history confirms rather than substitutes for the text's function.

  The decisive inquiry is the function of the State's own mandatory message under the governing establishment rule. Its text, placement, adoption history, and official delivery supply legally relevant proof without a survey of students' religious reactions. A categorical prohibition on government adoption of religious truth resolves this disclaimer, while leaving genuine academic study and private belief intact.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially different, general conscience notice is outside the question presented and the challenged resolution.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Judicial access to a legal eligibility claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve channel allocation on the admitted criminal-removability predicates. Do not reach the merits of each underlying immigration claim.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the withdrawal of ordinary appellate review also eliminate the independent judicial remedy for allegedly unlawful executive restraint or removal based on a legal eligibility error?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the habeas principles of Kolster v. INS and Reno v. Goncalves to the permanent provisions. This extends an existing distinction to the statutory configuration here, rather than announcing that all review restrictions are invalid.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Where the conceded predicates of §1252(a)(2)(C) withdraw direct appellate review, a court must enforce that limitation. General language allocating or restricting judicial review does not, without a sufficiently clear displacement of §2241, extinguish a properly brought habeas claim that executive action rests on an unlawful rule of statutory eligibility. Such habeas review determines legal authority and leaves permissible factual and discretionary judgments to the responsible agency.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This disposes of the forum question for these petitioners without creating universal appellate review, universal district-court jurisdiction, or a right to a favorable waiver.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A legal disability imposed by the Board is capable of judicial resolution before any exercise of discretion. Kolster v. INS and Reno v. Goncalves already prohibit conflating those questions. The conceded aggravated felonies establish the direct-review limitation; they do not establish the lawfulness of every consequence the Executive attaches to those convictions. Maintaining the independent habeas channel respects both Congress's chosen ordinary-review restriction and the remaining judicial remedy.

  The enacted allocation of forums is decisive. Kolster and Goncalves are extended to the permanent statutory provisions, preserving a real channel for legal eligibility while respecting the specific withdrawal of direct review. This statutory resolution makes a broader Suspension Clause holding unnecessary; it does not treat the existence of a named habeas action as sufficient if that action cannot actually determine the presented legal question.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual habeas predicates require facts not established here. Disputed criminal-removability classifications, review of actual discretionary denials, and a statute clearly withdrawing every effective legality-review channel are outside the conceded-predicate statutory question.

# FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the facial invalidation and remand for rejection of the party’s categorical exemption claim.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the earlier judgment protecting the independent advertisements. Return any remaining enforcement questions for application of the actual coordination and contribution rules; impose no unadjudicated civil penalty or liability for a specific communication absent the necessary findings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual coordination and the contribution channel

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved facial challenge because the challenged category contains actual coordinated spending, not merely expenditures by an affiliated political organization.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a party's associational role makes genuinely candidate-connected expenditures categorically immune from a justified contribution safeguard.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering contribution framework and resolve the previously reserved party-specific question. This is a new application and holding, not a claim that Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC already decided it.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may limit expenditures actually coordinated with a federal candidate when the government demonstrates an objectively grounded risk that party financing will circumvent valid candidate-contribution limits and the restriction is closely drawn while preserving meaningful party advocacy. Party identity alone supplies neither coordination nor corruption. On this record, the larger party-donation channel, fundraising credits, and tacit allocation practices sufficiently establish the circumvention mechanism; the facial challenge therefore fails.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers genuine candidate-connected expenditures and leaves independent party spending protected. It does not convert shared ideology, endorsement, or ordinary affiliation into coordinated spending, authorize a particular dollar ceiling without fit, or declare every future application valid.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Joint expression has real value, but a candidate's practical control or request can also turn a purchase into campaign support equivalent to money. Enforcing only explicit earmarks would leave the demonstrated general-treasury and tacit-credit mechanisms intact. The party retains independent expression and substantial coordinated activity. Those facts support the challenged category without relying on equalization or ordinary political access as independent reasons to suppress speech.

  The operative standard is closely drawn contribution scrutiny. Functional analysis identifies a genuinely candidate-connected transfer; the contribution rule and supported anti-circumvention justification then do the decisive work. Testimony about fundraising credits, documentary arrangements, tacit understandings reasonably inferred from conduct, and the financing structure are relevant evidence alongside any quantitative information. Party association alone cannot substitute for proof of the required connection. The facial disposition rejects categorical immunity without prejudging unsupported applications.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific applications lacking an actual statutory candidate connection, materially different ceiling levels, and penalties for particular conduct are outside this facial record.

# INS v. St. Cyr

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment permitting habeas consideration and requiring access to former §212(c) consideration.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand through the appropriate agency process for a lawful discretionary determination. Do not grant the waiver, retry the criminal case, or adjudicate unavailable factual equities.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Independent legality review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide jurisdiction under §2241 as applied to the legal ineligibility claim. Because no clear repeal is established, the statutory ground supplies jurisdiction without deciding the complete constitutional minimum under the Suspension Clause.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the cited amendments clearly displaced that independent judicial remedy.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and extend the existing preserved-habeas doctrine to the permanent provisions.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** General restrictions on immigration judicial review, and repeal of a separate immigration-specific habeas provision, do not clearly repeal §2241 jurisdiction over a properly brought claim that executive action rests on a legally erroneous eligibility rule. The court may determine eligibility law without exercising immigration discretion.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The particular legal claim and jurisdictional source.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** St. Cyr asks whether the Attorney General has authority to consider relief, a legal issue already distinguished from discretion by Reno v. Goncalves. Neither the comprehensive nature of a new system nor the importance of immigration administration supplies omitted repeal language.

  The statutory clear-statement rule and the already recognized separation of legality review from discretion are decisive. The Suspension Clause and the historical function of habeas reinforce that reading; they are not used to invent a repealed jurisdictional grant. A narrow statutory resolution supplies an effective legal forum without deciding every possible restriction on judicial review.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Factual or discretionary review beyond this legal eligibility claim is outside the question presented.

### Issue 2: The completed plea transaction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the temporal construction after recognizing jurisdiction.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether eliminating eligibility changes the legal consequences of St. Cyr's earlier eligible plea.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** An application of Landgraf v. USI Film Products to a question left open in Reno v. Goncalves.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent a sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, repeal of former §212(c) eligibility does not apply to a resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a preenactment guilty plea and who was eligible to seek that relief under the law then governing the plea. The protected consequence is access to lawful consideration, not a guaranteed favorable exercise of discretion.

* **Reach Within the Question:** No universal exemption for every preenactment conviction.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The plea is a completed exchange of legal rights. The government received the trial-right waiver while an established avenue for relief remained available. Converting possible removal into mandatory ineligibility alters that transaction. A requirement of proof that a particular official promised a waiver would confuse protected eligibility with an impossible guarantee of discretionary success.

  Landgraf's legal-consequence inquiry controls. The completed waiver of trial rights and then-existing eligibility are the material circumstances, not an empirical prediction that every pleader would have gone to trial. Preserve the opportunity for discretion because that was the legal position attached to the completed plea; do not turn that opportunity into a promised immigration outcome.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Trial convictions, ineligible pleaders, postchange pleas, and unrelated later grounds of removal are outside the presented preenactment eligible-plea question.

# Nevada v. Hicks

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening controlling law before June 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply any effective intervening holding on nonmember jurisdiction or personal immunity within its actual scope.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the recognition of tribal jurisdiction over the personal-capacity tribal tort claims arising directly from execution of the accepted tribal warrants; reverse recognition of tribal jurisdiction over the §1983 claims. Reject both land ownership alone and state employment alone as dispositive.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct dismissal of the §1983 claims from the tribal proceeding, without deciding their availability in a competent forum. Permit the connected tribal-law claims to proceed, including initial adjudication of applicable immunity defenses in the competent tribal forum under the governing effective law. The remand concerns liability, defenses, and relief, not an unanswered jurisdictional test. No damages, unlawful-search finding, or waiver of the State's immunity is established.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Tribal tort jurisdiction arising from the accepted warrants

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The federal action presents adjudicatory authority over these nonmember officers. The record establishes that they obtained and executed tribal warrants on retained tribal land. That relationship can be evaluated without deciding whether the alleged damage or excess actually occurred.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Montana's nonmember framework applies on this tribal land and permits jurisdiction over claims directly connected to the officers' voluntary use of tribal judicial authorization.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Montana and Strate's source-and-nexus framework to this retained-land setting. Adopt a limited consensual-relationship rule for officers who obtain and execute tribal warrants; do not characterize that precise application as already decided by Strate.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Tribal land ownership is material but does not alone settle nonmember adjudicatory authority. The Montana framework applies, and its consensual-relationship exception includes a state officer's voluntary invocation and use of a tribal warrant for a search of a member's home on retained tribal land. Tribal tort claims alleging damage or excess in executing that very authorization bear the required direct nexus. The officers' state enforcement purpose does not eliminate this jurisdiction, and an express consent-to-suit clause is unnecessary to establish the connected regulatory relationship. These claims satisfy that limited jurisdictional rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Claims against the officers personally concerning execution of these searches. The relationship does not authorize adjudication of unrelated official conduct, confer general jurisdiction over all state officers on reservations, or waive state sovereign immunity. No reliance on Montana's second exception is required.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A member's home on retained tribal land differs materially from Strate's public right-of-way, where ordinary public use displaced exclusion. Yet land alone need not carry the decision: these officers sought the tribe's judicial authorization and used it to conduct the challenged searches. The first state warrant even conditioned execution on tribal approval, and the later search also proceeded under state and tribal warrants. Alleged destruction and excess in executing those warrants concern the same conduct and relationship, not a remote consequence of entering the reservation. The consensual exception should turn on the actual undertaking and direct connection, rather than a requirement that every arrangement include the words consent to civil suit. It does not matter that state authority might independently have permitted entry: the officers actually invoked and used an additional sovereign's process. Seeking that authorization was cooperation, but cooperation does not strip the process of every legal consequence.

  Structural allocation of governing authority supplies the framework; the established consensual relationship and direct nexus carry the decisive weight. This extends Montana's reference to other arrangements beyond purely commercial dealings because the pertinent reason is accountable participation in a specific legally governed undertaking, not the commercial identity of the participant. State enforcement of off-reservation law remains possible, while tort adjudication concerning the accepted warrants protects the tribe's authority over the process used at a member's home. Personal jurisdictional accountability and a substantive immunity defense remain separate. The rule is narrow because the actual warrant relationship fully resolves the case; deciding jurisdiction over officers who never invoked tribal process would require another source of authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the searches exceeded authorization, caused compensable injury, or satisfy an applicable personal immunity requires facts and merits adjudication absent from this jurisdictional record. Authority over an officer who did not accept tribal process lies outside this holding's necessary application.

### Issue 2: Federal claims and exhaustion of immunity defenses

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide each source of forum authority before requiring merits litigation. Having resolved the tribal tort jurisdiction, decide rather than postpone the resulting exhaustion obligation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1983 authorizes the tribal court to hear the federal claims, and whether the officers must first present immunity defenses to the tribal tribunal for the claims within its jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the source-specific distinction between a federal cause of action and adjudicatory power; distinguish Neztsosie's specially assigned federal statutory channel from the tribal tort claims properly within tribal authority.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1983 does not itself confer the general concurrent jurisdiction of state courts on tribal tribunals. These §1983 claims therefore cannot proceed there, and exhaustion cannot supply the missing authority. The directly connected tribal tort claims stand differently: their jurisdictional basis is established, and the officers must present applicable personal immunity defenses there in the ordinary course. State employment does not itself create an exhaustion exception. A distinct, established exception to exhaustion must rest on its own predicates, not the rejected assertion that no tribal authority can reach a state officer.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The federal statutory claims and the immunity-exhaustion demand in this case. The tribal jurisdictional process has already addressed jurisdiction; the remaining initial presentation concerns defenses to the competent tribunal's merits adjudication. Neither this forum ruling nor exhaustion enlarges §1983 or determines the content of immunity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A substantive federal right and a tribunal's power to adjudicate it require separate legal sources. State-court concurrent jurisdiction cannot be attributed automatically to every sovereign's courts. Conversely, a legally grounded tribal tort action does not become unauthorized because its defendant invokes a merits defense. Requiring that competent court to address the defense respects ordinary adjudicative sequence and the direct relationship already established. Neztsosie gives priority to the specific federal statutory assignment it construed; no comparable assignment removes these tribal tort questions. The governing source of jurisdiction is decisive, with comity operating only after that source exists.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The fact-dependent merits and immunity application require proceedings under effective law; no substantive immunity judgment is presented by this jurisdiction-only record.

# New York Times Co. v. Tasini

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment that the proved database uses exceed §201(c).

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the district court to determine damages and equitable relief under the applicable statutory and equitable requirements. Do not mandate wholesale deletion, create a compulsory license, or adjudicate unpresented articles and agreements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Separate articles and the statutory revision privilege

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved statutory defense on the undisputed absence of contractual consent and the described products.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these publishers reproduce the articles as parts of revisions of the original collective works.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve an open statutory construction, preserving the Act's separate ownership and limited privilege without displacing an established holding.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 201(c) permits reproduction and distribution of a contribution in the original collective work, a recognizable revision, or the permitted later work in the same series. It does not permit a publisher to distribute the contribution as a separately retrieved article in a new general compendium merely by attaching its original publication data. The actual presentation to users and the retained collective context determine the distinction, independently of whether the medium is paper, microform, or electronic. Each challenged product exceeds the privilege on this record.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers these unauthorized uses of freelance contributions. It preserves actual licenses and electronic versions that present the contributions as parts of the original edition or a genuine revision. It does not require immutability of layout or prohibit search functionality itself.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress preserved an author's copyright when the article entered a collective work and gave the publisher a defined, limited ability to reuse it. Identifying the source cannot expand that privilege into an unrestricted secondary market. The text-only products detach the article; the page-image product retains a page but not the original issue as the collective whole. A user's capacity to reconstruct a collection does not establish that the publisher distributed that collection. Archival utility can inform relief within the statute but cannot rewrite the ownership allocation.

  The statutory division of ownership carries decisive weight. Examination of how the products present the articles is functional application of the revision privilege, not a technology-specific copyright exception. The same principle reaches paper and digital redistribution alike, while archival utility belongs in the lawful remedial inquiry rather than an invented compulsory license.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remedies need facts and proceedings not completed here. Other interfaces and contractual transfers are outside the presented uses.

# United States v. United Foods, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the challenged compulsory advertising assessment.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return unresolved accounting, severability, or monetary-relief questions for application of governing remedial law. Do not create a general refund entitlement or invalidate an independently lawful research assessment without deciding its actual operation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The compelled advertising subsidy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved First Amendment claim; decline to decide the unpreserved government-speech defense.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether common economic benefit alone justifies compelling independently competing producers to finance generic promotional expression.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's stated boundary and announce the new holding for the expressly reserved advertising-centered arrangement. Do not overrule Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's comprehensive-market result.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A mandatory industry assessment principally financing private generic promotion cannot be justified merely by the promotion's asserted benefit to the assessed industry, majority approval, or the payer's ability to advertise separately. Where no independently sufficient collective regulatory purpose requires the association, the compelled advertising cannot supply its own justification. On this record, the assessment infringes United Foods' freedom against compelled promotional support.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs this compelled advertising in an otherwise independent competitive market. It does not prohibit taxes financing actual government speech, factual commercial disclosures meeting their native requirements, or germane promotion within the comprehensive program sustained in Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The government has organized a speech subsidy without the market integration that explained the earlier collective obligation. United Foods' objection concerns the commercial message it must pay for, not only whether its name appears on an advertisement. Allowing speech to be justified as germane to the speech program would turn a limiting inquiry into a tautology. The government's unpreserved characterization cannot substitute for litigation over actual control and attribution.

  Distinguish Glickman's integrated economic regulation by the independent regulatory obligation that made its ancillary speech assessment germane. The compelled-association boundary is decisive; general economic benefit cannot perform that missing legal function. The requirement of actual litigation concerns an adjudicative event. A forfeited opportunity to create that event is not its occurrence. Bankruptcy statutes can assign decisive legal effects to silence, but the Court should identify and enforce those particular effects rather than imply that an adversarial determination occurred. # Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate both appellate judgments and remand for prompt application of a common purpose-bound detention standard to the current records.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require supervised release if the government cannot establish continuing statutory authority under that standard. Preserve lawful supervision and the removal orders. Do not authorize detention under an uninvoked alternative regime.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Habeas and continued detention

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Exercise review over the legal authority for custody without reopening removal merits.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether removal authority continues when removal lacks a significant prospect in the reasonably foreseeable future.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the preserved habeas and substantive-custody principles and announce a new construction of this detention provision.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Post-removal-period detention must remain reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. A final removal order, past criminality, or ongoing diplomatic effort alone does not authorize indefinite custody when removal lacks a significant likelihood in the reasonably foreseeable future. Habeas courts must independently examine the authority and the current factual prospect while giving appropriate weight to supported executive judgments about negotiations and practical arrangements. When continued custody is challenged after the removal period with concrete evidence of repeated refusals or comparable impediments, the government must identify facts supporting continued removal-purpose detention. A speculative possibility of eventual removal is insufficient.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Post-removal-period detention of the admitted residents presented here. The rule adopts no six-month safe harbor or mandatory deadline. Duration, progress, receiving-country decisions, and obstruction matter to individualized necessity. Relief is lawful supervision when custody authority ends, without cancellation of removal.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute's detention-and-supervision structure addresses carrying out removal. It does not clearly create a permanent preventive regime for everyone with a criminal removal order. Zadvydas's repeated receiving-country refusals require more than the assertion that removal is not impossible; Ma's lack of a repatriation agreement requires examination of realistic alternatives and negotiations. Individual danger may inform custody within a live removal process, but cannot preserve that source after its purpose ceases to support confinement.

  Detention requires a live substantive basis, not merely periodic paperwork. The prolonged detention and concrete removal obstacles require prompt examination: the detainee identifies the obstacle, and the Government must support a reasonably foreseeable removal prospect rather than recite ongoing negotiations. The detainee must have a meaningful opportunity to answer that account. Stone adopts a purpose-based limit without an automatic day-91 release or a universal six-month safe harbor because the operative question is whether custody can still serve lawful removal. If that authority is absent and no independent custody basis applies, supervised release must follow. Other preventive-detention regimes require their own authority and safeguards.

  Statutory purpose and structure carry decisive weight in construing the duration of this custody authority; substantive liberty and the avoidance of indefinite executive confinement reinforce the available reading. The standard uses a justified burden shift once concrete removal obstacles are shown, rather than demanding that a detained person prove diplomatic impossibility. Country refusals, communications, travel-document history, the detainee's conduct, and other reliable information can establish the actual prospect. A universal six-month safe harbor could authorize custody after its purpose has already failed, while a day-91 deadline could disregard a genuinely imminent removal. The purpose-based rule therefore governs future cases without making a calendar replace the statutory justification.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Previously unadmitted entrants, distinct national-security authorities, and genuinely independent civil-commitment regimes are outside the presented statutory custody questions.
