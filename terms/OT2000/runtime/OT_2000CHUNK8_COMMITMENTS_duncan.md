## Material alternative paths

**D1 — State modifies both forms of review.** The coordinated phrase identifies state postconviction proceedings and other state collateral proceedings. The word State otherwise has little work because the broader collateral category already includes ordinary postconviction collateral review. Other collateral review accommodates different state procedural forms and review of state custody outside conventional criminal postconviction procedure. Congress's separate state and federal references in adjacent provisions and chapter 154 reinforce attention to the sovereign distinction. This reading also supports state-first exhaustion rather than treating a premature federal filing as a statutory clock-stopping substitute.

The strongest objection is that federal habeas naturally is collateral review and that excluding a timely federal proceeding can exhaust the filing period while a court considers exhaustion. The D1 conclusion is that natural category membership does not answer the modifier's scope. That consequence does not justify deleting State from the coordinated phrase. Properly presented equitable protection remains a distinct inquiry; its possible availability is not a premise establishing that every affected prisoner will obtain relief.

**D2 — Other collateral review is the independent broader category.** The disjunction can separate state postconviction review from other collateral review, including federal habeas. This reading gives the broader category a practical role and harmonizes timely federal filing with exhaustion without forfeiting a first merits opportunity merely through time spent awaiting a nonmerits order. The chapter 154 reference to final State-court disposition shows that Congress could specify a state endpoint expressly when desired.

The strongest objection is substantial redundancy in State and the risk that repeated unexhausted federal petitions postpone finality. D2 accepts some overlap in a comprehensive description of review and does not make every civil-rights suit collateral review. Proper filing, the pertinent-judgment-or-claim requirement, actual pendency, exhaustion, abuse rules on their actual predicates, and the new application's other defenses remain. This is a statutory exclusion, not an equitable reward conditioned on the particular applicant being especially diligent.

**D3 — The initial timely filing permanently satisfies subsection (d)(1).** Rejected as an independent general rule by all eight. Timeliness of the first application does not itself make a distinct later application timely or erase subsection (d)(2)'s specified exclusion. Souter and Breyer obtain the pendency exclusion through D2, not a permanently satisfied limitations requirement. American Pipe and Burnett, as explained in Irwin, support defined judicial tolling in appropriate circumstances; they do not establish that every later application inherits the first filing date.

**D4 — Equitable or procedural protection.** A reversal of D2 must leave the actually preserved equitable alternative for lawful consideration. Irwin supplies a rebuttable equitable-tolling background, sparing relief, diligence, and the distinction between a timely defective pleading and ordinary neglect; Brockamp demonstrates that the particular statute can rebut the background and that reversal of one ground does not decide a preserved independent alternative. Its integrated tax filing/allowance/payment-window holding is not an AEDPA holding. No Associate finds Walker entitled to equitable tolling on this record. None votes for a universal mandatory notice, election, dismissal deadline, or stay procedure as part of this statutory decision. Rose's choice to abandon unexhausted claims or pursue exhaustion and section 2254(b)(2)'s permission to deny unexhausted claims do not create those duties. No stay request or still-pending original application is before this Court for relief.

## Proposition and remedy commitments for reconciliation

- **D1 statutory exclusion:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg — six Associates support reversal on the same sufficient text-and-structure rule. They exclude a federal habeas application's pendency from subsection (d)(2), including a timely application dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. - **D2 statutory inclusion:** Souter, Breyer — two Associates would count qualifying federal collateral pendency and affirm the statutory reinstatement. They preserve all other actual requirements and defenses, rather than award a writ.
- **D3 rejection:** All eight; a timely first filing is not a permanent satisfaction of the limitations requirement for every later application.
- **Preserved equitable alternative after a D1 judgment:** All eight accept ordinary adjudication of the actual preserved contention, conditionally for the two D2 supporters. That common remand does not establish general AEDPA equitable availability or Walker's entitlement. Stevens supports an affirmative, separate explanation that ordinary equitable protection remains available where warranted; the six-Associate D1 coalition must not be enlarged into an unrecorded affirmative equity coalition.
- **Procedure request:** All eight decline to impose the requested universal prompt-dismissal, warning/election, or stay obligations in this decision. The statutory permission to deny an unexhausted application is not a duty. No application is granted, stayed, reopened automatically, or declared exhausted.
