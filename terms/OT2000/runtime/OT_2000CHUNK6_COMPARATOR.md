# Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc.

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-21. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed unanimously, 9-0.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Breyer delivered the opinion for the Court; no separate opinion.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court held that selling an option while secretly intending never to honor it is actionable securities deception. Oral form did not exclude the option, and breach alone was expressly distinguished. The opinion discussed purchaser-seller limits and statutory securities definitions; it did not decide the separate affirmative-assistance rule of Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A..

**Historical Sources:** [Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-588.pdf).

**Historical Authority:** The historical majority and separate opinions are comparison material only. Their propositions, votes, and opinion alignments do not govern this case.

# Harris v. Garner

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified. On June 27, 2000, the twelve-member Eleventh Circuit sitting en banc upheld the filing-date restriction and vacated the prejudicial aspect of the six released plaintiffs' monetary-claim dismissals.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Carnes wrote the majority opinion. Judge Tjoflat concurred in part and dissented in part, joined by Judges Birch, Barkett and Wilson. The separate opinion's partial agreement should not be represented as a single undifferentiated vote on every component.

**Historical Holding:** The physical-injury restriction applied to actions filed during incarceration despite release before judgment. The released plaintiffs' affected damages claims were dismissed without prejudice, preserving the distinction between the original action and a later action after release.

**Historical Authority:** This is the historical en banc lower-court anchor. Its temporal reasoning and disposition are comparison material; the disposition in Section II remains proposed and unapproved.

**Historical Sources:** [Harris v. Garner](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/216/216.F3d.970.98-8899.html).
