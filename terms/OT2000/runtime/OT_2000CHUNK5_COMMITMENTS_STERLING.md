# Sterling v. Borough of Minersville — independent non-Stone commitments

Event: merits, April 24, 2001; Third Circuit No. 99-1768; no additional docket supplied. Stage: independent modeling; not a Court disposition. All eight Associate Justices participate. Sources: frozen April 24–25 neutral packet, preparation receipt and Sterling entering-law slice. No matter-specific comparator or controlled position supplement was received. Research cutoff: April 24, 2001. Current constitutional law includes Denius of January 17, 2001; personal damages notice is separately fixed at April 17, 1997.

## Components and lawful alternatives

The supported plaintiff-favorable account is an officer's operative ultimatum to an adult arrestee: reveal suspected sexual orientation to his grandfather or the officer will do so. It is not an adjudicated factual finding, a minor's guardian notification, an actual officer disclosure, or proof of causation of death. Hoban's factual participation challenge is outside interlocutory jurisdiction under Johnson and Behrens; all eight preserve that boundary and any separable properly assumed legal defense.

On present merits, one path treats the ultimatum as present official compulsion to surrender objectively intimate information. Sheets protects intimate material against unjustified dissemination; Denius now establishes that completed publication is not indispensable when government imposes a concrete disclosure condition. No supported drinking-enforcement purpose connects adult orientation disclosure to the grandfather with this arrest. The best objection is the difference between employment loss and an unexecuted threat: offensive speech or a future possible violation is not itself deprivation. The bounded response rests on an operative coercive disclosure demand supported by official arrest authority, not on every insult or threatened future misconduct. Actual responsibility, effect and causation still must be shown.

The competing merits path refuses to extend the confined disclosure and acquisition holdings to this unexecuted family-notification demand without an independently established constitutional source. Sheets binds at its actual scope; Denius binds at its compulsory employment-access scope. Moral offensiveness and intimate subject matter do not by themselves establish every further substantive-due-process right. Its strongest objection is that official coercion cannot evade an existing confidentiality protection by ordering the individual to do the revealing.

On immunity, the strongest denial ground combines then-effective Sheets, Whalen and the local Westinghouse, Fraternal Order of Police and 1995 circuit Doe authorities: established confidential intimate content cannot be exposed without a genuine official purpose. No identical case is necessary. Walls concerned employment inquiry into conduct and does not authorize an arrest-backed disclosure to a family member. The contrary grant ground is that Sheets involved completed public dissemination of entrusted marital material; orientation and an unexecuted ultimatum introduce additional boundaries. Walls and threat cases reinforce objective uncertainty even if no policing purpose excuses the conduct on today's merits. Denius does not supply retrospective notice and explicitly distinguished legal-consultation, financial and medical notice records.

## Compatibility, scope and remedy

Six Associates support the bounded present merits proposition; Scalia and Thomas reject it. Four Associates deny the legal immunity defense on the assumed no-purpose ultimatum; O'Connor and Kennedy grant it on notice grounds, and Scalia and Thomas independently grant it. The six merits supporters can join an opinion expressly identifying operative compulsion and actual intimate content, rejecting publication as an indispensable criterion, and retaining ordinary objective immunity. They cannot join universal threat liability or an automatic damages consequence. O'Connor and Kennedy require separate treatment of merits and notice; Scalia and Thomas cannot supply a merits join merely through their shared immunity judgment.

All eight exclude fact-sufficiency review of Hoban's participation while allowing any properly separable legal question. No commitment resolves whether the threat occurred, what either officer actually knew or did, whether coercion caused the suicide, the amount or measure of damages, municipal policy or causation, other constitutional claims, or state-law liability. An immunity reversal concerns the reviewed individual damages theory only; it is not a general judgment for the Borough or all defendants. Affirmance permits further litigation under ordinary proof and defenses and awards nothing. No additional source is required for these bounded legal commitments; final actor-specific or causal findings would require the evidence expressly reserved by the neutral packet.
