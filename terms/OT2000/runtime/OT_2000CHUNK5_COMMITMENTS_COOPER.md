## Enhanced comparison: legal boundary and jury protection

**Independent-review path.** BMW supplies a binding substantive limit separate from the procedural adequacy upheld in TXO and required in Oberg. Applying that limit is a legal judgment informed by facts, not a measurement of compensation or a new finding of wrongdoing. Bajakajian supplies a close but noncontrolling analogy concerning punishment; Ornelas supports independent constitutional evaluation without a universal mixed-question rule. The Seventh Amendment does not require deference to a jury's ultimate legal power to impose a particular punishment. Supported underlying findings remain protected; an appellate court cannot invent losses, disbelieve witnesses anew, or substitute its own compensation award.

The strongest objection is that punitive punishment expresses the jury's fact-bound moral appraisal, so fresh appellate judgment displaces the verdict and Gasperini's institutional protection. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer answer that constitutional limits already constrain that appraisal independently of the state's authorized discretion; independent legal review does not redetermine what occurred. Scalia and Thomas accept this allocation within BMW's binding premise while retaining their source objection. Their actual Gasperini position expressly separates prohibited factual reexamination from legal-error correction. The opinion must not characterize their limited agreement as adoption of BMW's constitutional foundation.

**Deferential-review path.** Ginsburg would preserve meaningful trial and appellate review, independent correction of erroneous legal criteria, and the ability to set aside an abuse, while giving substantial weight to the trial assessment of culpability and proper punishment. The strongest contrary argument is that a federal legal maximum cannot depend upon district-court tolerance. Her answer is functional: an open-textured constitutional outer boundary can be enforced through review for abuse of discretion, including correction of legal mistakes, without making every application a fresh appellate punishment decision. Bajakajian and Ornelas do not decide this jury-award setting. Her position preserves BMW's controlling force and Oberg's meaningful amount review rather than treating adequate procedure as categorical immunity from a substantive challenge.

## Scope, remedy and proposition compatibility

The seven vacatur votes support independent appellate constitutional review and protection of supported historical facts. Five additionally affirm the substantive constitutional justification already adopted in BMW; Scalia and Thomas's agreement must not be inflated into new support for that justification. Ginsburg opposes making independent application mandatory but accepts independent legal-error correction and the continued force of existing law.

The vacatur remedy returns the punitive judgment to the Ninth Circuit to use the proper allocation. It supplies no replacement amount, constitutional ratio, mandatory remittitur, new punitive trial, automatic invalidation of $4.5 million, or finding of an unproved sale or loss. The lawful-copying ruling remains distinct from passing off and false advertising. A reviewing court may assess only supported actual or potential harm and the wrong adjudicated; enterprise wealth cannot supply a missing relationship to the wrong. These are existing BMW and TrafFix boundaries, not a separate final amount holding under the limited grant. Oregon's receipt of part of the award is not presented for decision.

No precedent is overruled. Gasperini retains state-compensation and federal trial-allocation force; Oberg does not itself require a specified number of reviewing courts; TXO supplies no controlling numerical formula; Bajakajian and Ornelas remain task-specific. The full trial record is unnecessary to select the legal review standard and would be necessary before fixing or invalidating a sum on additional factual grounds.
