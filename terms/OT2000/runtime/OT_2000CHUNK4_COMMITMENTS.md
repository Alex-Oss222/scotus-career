## 1. Buford v. United States — March 20, 2001

### Question, record and two paths

The question is appellate review of the district court's fact-bound application of functional consolidation to the drug conviction and four concededly related robbery convictions. It is not a comprehensive definition of consolidation or a fresh sentencing classification. The common hearing, single presentence report, counsel's consent letter and concurrent sentences favor treating the proceedings together. Separate filing, drug-branch assignment, prosecutors, pleas and plea judges, two judgments and distinct sentences point the other way. No formal order appears, but its absence cannot be decisive under a doctrine permitting functional consolidation. The claimed addiction connection is an argument, not a finding of a common scheme.

Path A affirms the deferential review allocation for this application while preserving independent review of guideline text, authoritative commentary and legal criteria. Koon/Powell requires legal correction and deference to supported context-sensitive sentencing judgment. Section 3742(e)'s due-deference instruction fits a classification whose significance depends on practical sentencing arrangements and whose particular facts have limited value as a general appellate rule. Stinson makes valid commentary authoritative; it does not turn every application of commentary into independent appellate judgment.

The strongest contrary argument is that a classification increasing the reported range from 84–105 months to 188–235 months needs uniform appellate enforcement. That importance requires correct legal criteria, but does not erase the institutional distinction between defining those criteria and assessing a particular local proceeding. The 188-month sentence is not sustained by unexamined older convictions or a general characterization of the defendant.

Path B would independently apply the legal standard to established facts and return the appeal for that review, without awarding a new sentence. Ornelas and Fields explain why ultimate constitutional search thresholds require independent review while protecting facts and giving due weight to experience. Their functional approach is relevant, but their constitutional standard-setting task does not compel the same allocation for this consolidation determination. First Options likewise protects legal review without deciding all mixed questions. No separately supported legal error in the consolidation criterion is established in the authorized record; a demonstrated error would require correction under either path.

## Enhanced comparison of the actual paths

### A. State replacement of beneficiary entitlement

This path reverses nonpreemption for both demands. Section 514's connection inquiry addresses the rule's operation: the divorce event changes the beneficiary entitlement and creates notice-dependent duties irrespective of what the designation itself says. A contingent provision for invalid designations cannot make every state declaration of invalidity binding on an ERISA plan. Nor does allowing a plan to opt out necessarily remove a default administrative command. Travelers and Dillingham reject unlimited effects-based preemption but distinguish direct benefit and administration rules; UNUM preserves that distinction for an administrative reassignment dressed in ordinary legal language.

The strongest objection is that this assumes the very validity it must establish. Family and property law ordinarily helps determine the meaning and effect of instruments, and this plan expressly contemplates invalidity. The response is narrower than universal federal validity: the Court need not deny ordinary rules of capacity, execution or interpretation, none of which is litigated here, to hold that this particular automatic postdesignation divorce rule is a substitute beneficiary rule. The actual designation is unchanged; no text makes marital status a continuing condition or adopts this state-law rule. The objection rests on a subsequent legal event imposed by the state default, rather than an established defect in making or expressing the designation. The federal conflict lies in state-specific beneficiary substitution and its administrative operation, not in a presumed federal prohibition of all background law.

For insurance, this path must additionally answer the completed recipient payment. Boggs's governing pension holdings reject the antecedent-transfer claims there and leave independent later obligations outside their scope. They do not decide life insurance and do not transfer pension anti-alienation into welfare benefits. The insurance holding proposed here instead applies section 514's beneficiary-entitlement principle to a claim whose legal source is the same asserted revocation. Moving that claim to the recipient does not supply a new legal source. This is a new application of the administration/entitlement principle, with Boggs's source-of-right reasoning informative rather than mechanically dispositive.

### B. Permissible background validity law

This path affirms rejection of preemption for the challenged default, leaving actual entitlement, exceptions and ordinary remedy to the state proceedings. On this view, the statute determines the continued validity of a relational designation in an area the actual documents do not settle. The investment plan then operates according to its own fallback; the silent insurance plan is not treated as federalizing every unaddressed family-law question. Travelers and Dillingham demand a concrete conflict, and no protected survivor annuity or mandatory federal designation rule for this former spouse is established. The law is defeasible and generally regulates nonprobate dispositions. None of those characteristics alone decides preemption; together with the unresolved document-validity issue, they support treating the law as background construction.

The strongest objection is that the statute expressly changes who receives plan benefits and imposes duties after notice, producing the administrative variation ERISA seeks to avoid. The response offered by this path is that applying a lawful validity rule is not the same as contradicting an operative designation: benefit documents necessarily function against some background law, and national administration does not itself identify which ordinary validity rules federal law excludes. The plan's fallback shows that interpretation may require information beyond a name on a form. That does not prove the children win; it answers the asserted categorical preemption defense. This path cannot claim the plan expressly incorporates Washington law, that the designation automatically canceled itself, or that separate payor penalties are already adjudicated.

### C. Recipient-only nonpreemption without general approval of the default

This narrower path preserves recovery only from an already paid recipient while rejecting direct changes to future plan payment or responsibility. It rests on Breyer's published Boggs writing joined by O'Connor and Ginsburg, which distinguishes an ordinary recipient accounting from creation of a new plan beneficiary. For the insurance, the completed payment is an established predicate and pension anti-alienation does not apply by analogy. For the pension, an outcome that depends on distribution requires its actual status and the precise relief sought; they are not established here.

The strongest objection is that the children's right still originated in revocation of the beneficiary designation, the problem identified by Boggs's controlling antecedent-transfer analysis. This path does not fabricate a later independent obligation to evade that objection. Its supporters maintain the legal distinction they actually advanced in Boggs and identify insurance's different statutory category. To the extent they would permit an analogous pension accounting, that remains disagreement with Boggs, not an existing exception or a conclusion that the controlling pension law is unresolved.

## 6. Gibbs v. Babbitt — March 21, 2001

### The statutory class and genuinely different routes

The challenge concerns the private-land application of the experimental red-wolf taking rule. Under controlling Home Builders, the regulated class is wildlife taking without a commercial element. Concern for cattle and the commercial character of an owner's operations do not rewrite that class. Mann's earlier guilty plea is background; no conviction is reviewed here. The supplied numbers, interstate howling visitors, volunteers and scientific work are actual reported record evidence; projected revenues and a possible future pelt trade are distinct predictive or speculative material. Prior interstate transportation of captive animals does not itself establish the current necessary connection.

**Route A — necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime.** The government must identify the particular valid interstate regulation and support why excluding private-land taking permits evasion or materially impairs that regulation. Species-wide conservation, the Act's comprehensiveness and federal refuge ownership do not themselves establish the commerce anchor. A relationship between the protected population and an operating interstate tourism or research resource is materially more concrete than national concern, but a court must still identify the actual federal interstate regime and the necessary-inclusion relationship. The present lower rationale did not resolve those elements under Home Builders. The strongest contrary argument to a remand is that the government has merely renamed conservation a regime without showing its commerce validity; that would require confined relief if the existing record supplies nothing more.

**Route B — independent substantial interstate externality.** The court must identify a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable effect generated by the taking class, materially supported substantiality, inability of affected States reasonably to contain or internalize that effect through ordinary territorial authority, and fit of the prohibition. Movement from federal refuge to neighboring private land shows local mobility and possible program vulnerability, not state-border movement or territorial incapacity. Interstate visits show actual interstate use, but do not alone prove that North Carolina cannot internalize the conservation and tourism consequences. The record's potential functional relationship deserves examination; no crossing, migration pattern, interstate population mechanism, quantified effect or failed agreement is invented. Solid Waste's four-Justice separate position involved a shared mobile interstate bird population and loss of habitat beyond another State's control; it does not supply those facts for red wolves.

**Path C — the broader proposed class-effects rule.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Morrison dissent would permit a materially supported rational substantial-class-effects relationship without the controlling connection prerequisite. Actual interstate visits, organized study and the irreplaceable live population can support a reasoned class-wide relationship more directly than a conjectural future pelt market. ## Issues and competing paths

**A — offense boundary and use in the murder prosecution.** An elements boundary extends an attached right to legally identical and greater/lesser included offenses, while withholding attachment from separately defined crimes merely arising from one episode. McNeil supplies offense specificity; Blockburger and Brown provide an administrable definition of the legally same prosecution rather than a rule based on which charge the prosecutor happens to announce. Moulton continues to exclude unlawfully elicited charged-offense evidence in that charged prosecution. Burglary and the actual capital count, murder of multiple persons in one criminal transaction under Texas Penal Code §19.03(a)(7)(A), each require a distinct element. This route reverses the factual-relatedness ground and remands without deciding unilateral waiver of an attached right.

The strongest objection is that charging one aspect of an episode lets investigators obtain evidence about the represented transaction by asking about another label. The answer on this path is legally same-offense coverage plus full protection of use in the charged prosecution, not an assertion that Miranda waivers extinguish Sixth Amendment rights. The remaining objection is real: that boundary leaves some closely integrated investigation outside the attached right.

**B — closely connected offense and circumvention.** Counsel protection includes investigation of an uncharged offense so closely connected with the represented charge that questioning necessarily investigates the same underlying criminal episode and predictably elicits the represented offense's facts. Stevens's McNeil dissent and Moulton identify why double-jeopardy classification need not define the counsel relationship. Here the murders occurred in the burglary transaction and the challenged confession explains that transaction. This route sustains the lower attachment conclusion. Its strongest objection is indeterminacy and the need to preserve McNeil's offense-specific premise; the limiting rule concerns an inseparable investigated criminal episode, not all crimes attributed to a represented person. A free-standing subjective-purpose test is rejected.

**C — waiver is separate.** Prior counsel permission covered earlier interviews, not this interrogation. The state court found an assertion through acceptance of appointed counsel and held Jackson applicable. A valid Miranda waiver alone does not displace that finding or resolve Sixth Amendment unilateral waiver. No Justice below adopts an alternative holding overruling Jackson. The attachment dissenters sustain suppression and the new-trial disposition under the state court's actual assertion and nonharmlessness determinations, rather than inventing a new invocation fact. The attachment reversers leave Jackson and the alternative waiver contention undecided because no right attached to these murders on their rule.

## I. Removal and scope: two lawful paths

**Path R1 — emergency justification and supported factual disputes.** Removing a child from parental custody without prior judicial authorization requires specific, articulable information giving objectively reasonable grounds to believe the child faces imminent serious bodily harm requiring action before authorization can reasonably be obtained. The scope and duration of the intrusion must be reasonably directed to averting that danger. The children's Fourth Amendment seizure interests and the parents' Fourteenth Amendment family-association interests are distinct rights; on this record both require justification for this unilateral physical separation. The Court need not declare the two Amendments coextensive in every setting, adopt a complete family-law scrutiny framework or convert Troxel's separate proposals into controlling law.

The supported record presents genuine disputes under that rule. No actual removal order existed. What CPS told the officers, what information they possessed and the accounts of the therapist's assurances remain contested. The tip's disclosed psychiatric setting and prior unfounded report bear on reliability, but mental illness alone does not categorically disqualify an informant. The changing testimony, absence of substantial investigation into the underlying allegation, the children's observed condition and time reasonably available for inquiry matter. The report threatened Jessie on a specified day; it did not allege a plan to harm Lauren or participation by Becky. Those distinctions bear on each child's removal, separation from the unimplicated mother and duration. They cannot be erased by treating the family as one undifferentiated object of protection.

The rule does not require officers to wait for an injury, exhaust every conceivable alternative, obtain a fixed category of corroboration, use a prescribed verification technology, or satisfy a newly invented proof quantum. It requires an actual objective emergency and fitted action. Reasonably available inquiry or protection with the unimplicated parent can matter to that assessment without becoming a universal checklist. Consent to police entry was not consent to removing the children. Later exoneration does not retroactively prove the original seizure unreasonable; the contemporaneous information and actual intervention control.

**Strongest opposing path R2 — reinstate judgment on objective protection.** A specific report of a young child's imminent killing, transmitted by a mandated reporter and protective-services personnel, can justify prompt intervention even if it later proves false. Police need not make a final abuse finding before preventing catastrophic harm; the reliability of professional transmission and the approaching date give the City a substantial argument. A reasonable protective response may require arrangements that are imperfect in hindsight.

R2 nevertheless cannot sustain defense summary judgment here without resolving material disputes in the City's favor. Professional transmission does not erase the source's known limitations or settle the conflicting account of what the therapist said. A danger to Jessie does not automatically establish the same danger to Lauren or the need to separate both from Becky. The asserted equinox danger does not by itself authorize indefinite continuation. The City did not independently preserve a contention that mistaken belief in an order alone constituted lawful authority, and the lower court reserved that question. The Court therefore neither creates that alternative defense nor decides it against the City on invented facts.

**Holding-level commitment:** R1 is a minimum emergency and fit rule sufficient to reject the challenged summary judgment. It does not find an actual constitutional violation. It also does not adopt the lower opinion's broad suggestion that every reasonable-cause question is exclusively factual for a jury: courts decide governing law and may resolve legal sufficiency on undisputed facts, while genuinely disputed material facts and credibility cannot be resolved against the nonmovants here. No universal custody deadline or automatic right to every alternative placement follows.

## II. Municipal responsibility and immunity: distinct paths

**Path M1 — survival under the correct municipal elements.** Carter, Collins, Monell and Canton require an underlying federal deprivation, the City's own attributable policy or legally effective custom, the fault appropriate to that theory and actual causation. The officers' testimony about an established practice of accepting telephone representations of orders without verification, including testimony by the designated knowledgeable officer and a supervisor, permits a genuine issue about an institutional practice rather than merely one employee's mistake. Their testimony connecting reliance on the supposed order to removal and failure to investigate also supports a causal question. These are grounds to reject categorical judgment that there is no policy evidence, not findings that the City had an unconstitutional policy or caused every subsequent injury.

An alleged affirmative municipal policy and an omission theory remain different. The remand must identify the actual theory and the applicable fault requirement. If the claim rests on a training, supervision or safeguarding omission, Carter requires deliberate indifference, notice through a supported pattern or sufficiently obvious recurring need, an identified deficiency and actual causal connection. A longstanding practice does not automatically prove all those elements. Nor may a court require prior identical incidents in every case or impose heightened pleading about internal operations. The City's cooperation with county personnel does not make them or the physician City employees.

**Strongest opposing path M2 — no institutional liability from a mistaken act.** A telephone report can be a reasonable means of interagency communication, and negligence by an officer or inaccurate outside information does not make the municipality a guarantor of every removal. A general observation that more verification might have prevented harm is not deliberate indifference or proof of final municipal authority. That is a real limit, and the mere existence of a hospital contract cannot establish the entire separate examination policy.

M2 cannot support the categorical removal-policy summary judgment on this record. The evidence concerns a described settled practice and its actual role, not only hindsight or a bare employment relationship. The proper response is application of the existing municipal elements to that supported theory, not a finding of City liability or dismissal based on an employee's potential personal defense. No universal obligation to inspect a paper order physically is adopted; the lawfulness of relying on an actual supported communication remains distinct from the disputed facts and preserved grounds here.

**Immunity:** The City cannot borrow personal qualified or absolute immunity or invoke state immunity against the federal claim. This does not establish that any individual officer lacks immunity. Denius preserves conduct-date notice for personal damages, and neither Ferguson nor another later decision supplies September 1991 notice. No individual defendant is restored and no personal immunity judgment is entered. State-law respondeat superior and derivative immunity remain separate legal mechanisms, not reasons to import federal employer liability or state immunity into section 1983.

## III. Independent examinations, parental participation and preservation

**Path E1 — reach a properly presented independent examination claim.** The examinations were highly invasive bodily intrusions and reportedly occurred about three days after removal without parental consent, notice or prior judicial authorization. No supported fact supplies urgent medical treatment, imminent loss of material evidence, parental waiver or a valid substitute-consent determination. Ferguson requires an actual justification for the intrusion and use; custody or a protective label is not automatic permission. Troxel preserves parental direction but does not itself supply the lower court's complete notice, judicial-approval and parental-presence framework. Denius's information-acquisition rule is not a physical-examination authorization.

If an independent claim is properly presented, its legal assessment must distinguish the children's Fourth Amendment bodily interests from the parents' decision-making, notice, opportunity-to-object and physical-presence interests. Even the lower court's presence rule permits exclusion for a valid reason and nearby presence instead; it is not an unconditional right to remain in the examination room. Consent, an actual court authorization, an emergency and a justified substitute decisionmaker have distinct predicates. A lawful initial seizure would not by itself establish all of them.

**Strongest justification for reaching E1 now:** The district court addressed the examinations, the appellate majority identified the requesting-agency form and contract testimony, and the grant expressly includes the constitutional question. The challenged acts are concrete rather than hypothetical. The majority's inference of a customary examination practice could support a claim if the actual pleadings and proceedings preserve it. Avoiding the issue unnecessarily could leave the family without a clear rule for a serious intrusion.

**Path E2 — first resolve the independent theory's presentation and scope.** Rymer specifically disputes that an examination-policy claim was ever alleged, argued or supported against the City, the only remaining defendant. The appellate majority's evidentiary inference does not itself resolve that procedural objection. A grant identifies a question but does not amend pleadings or cure forfeiture. A constitutional ruling on an independent claim cannot be made necessary merely by treating it as preserved when the source establishes a real dispute about that premise.

All eight choose E2 as the present disposition. They would vacate the independent examination-policy and associated constitutional analysis insofar as it conclusively governs the remand, and require ordinary determination of which claim and theory, if any, were properly presented against the City. They do not find the theory waived or hold the examinations lawful. If it survives, the lower courts apply the actual governing constitutional and municipal rules to that claim, including Ferguson at its confined scope; a new comprehensive Supreme Court examination rule is not adopted in this event. The Court does not require an automatic new evidentiary hearing or authorize an otherwise unavailable amendment.

**Why the contrary merits path does not control the present vote:** Examination-related harm may remain part of the removal case without a new independent constitutional examination claim. Lower note 15 expressly preserves that possible causal route. Its availability defeats the argument that refusing to decide every new examination rule necessarily extinguishes all recovery for those injuries. Conversely, preserving consequential damages does not make the City responsible for every act occurring after removal. The judgment returns those distinct issues without inferring either preservation or default from an unavailable original filing.

## Separable questions and alternatives

**A. Present liberty justification.** Affirm the appellate judgment setting aside the authorization and requiring further findings. Riggins requires medical appropriateness, overriding need and consideration of reasonable less intrusive means upon objection. Harper recognizes justified safety treatment but does not make a diagnostic label or clinical benefit sufficient. The current record includes possible danger in staff encounters, but also the treating psychiatrist's concession that isolation and continuous observation controlled immediate danger. The district court cannot approve treatment on a safety label without confronting that actual alternative and its adequacy, burdens and duration. This is not a finding that indefinite isolation is preferable or harmless, or that future circumstances cannot justify medication.

**Opposing safety path:** Reinstate authorization on medically appropriate management of a seriously ill, potentially dangerous confined person, leaving trial effects for later. Its strongest case is the real danger during unavoidable contact and the cost of maintaining extreme isolation; Harper counsels respect for informed clinical administration. Its obstacle here is the failure to supply an independently sufficient safety justification in light of the evidence actually contradicting immediate necessity. Lack of prosecutorial pretext does not establish such a justification, because restoration is an acknowledged purpose. A revised safety showing may succeed; the present order does not.

**B. Prospective trial-effects inquiry.** Where restoration is an actual purpose and this record supplies no independently sufficient safety basis, the judge must assess the proposed treatment's likely effects on the defendant's ability to understand, consult, testify and present his defense before authorizing it. Riggins identifies risks that a completed-trial transcript may not reconstruct; a court cannot defer every trial consideration until after imposing the intervention. This is a preliminary, individualized likely-effects assessment, not a guarantee against every side effect or a complete competency-restoration code. If treatment is later lawfully authorized, actual effects and competence remain subject to reassessment.

**Strongest contrary argument:** No one can know precise trial effects until treatment occurs, so an advance requirement can prevent restoration even when treatment will improve participation. The bounded answer is to require available clinical evidence and realistic protective measures rather than perfect prediction. Demeanor is not the only interest: recordings or psychiatric explanation may preserve evidence of prior symptoms but cannot necessarily cure impaired communication. Conversely, an insanity defense does not invariably entitle a defendant to display untreated delusions to the jury. The adequacy of recordings, testimony, dose adjustment or other protections must be assessed, not assumed.

**C. Distinct approach to B.** A Justice can concur in setting aside this order solely because the present refusal-interest justification is deficient, while declining to make anticipated Sixth Amendment effects a necessary part of every initial treatment decision. That narrower judgment preserves direct prospective liberty relief and avoids deciding a completed-trial prejudice question. No position here permits prosecution of an incompetent person or a trial shown to be unfair.

### Component E: facial religious eligibility and accommodation

**E1 — permissible accommodation in its stated terms.** The religious refusal condition can accommodate access to a preexisting health benefit where a patient otherwise meets the condition-based standard and public payment buys only the independently identifiable physical-care component. ## Proposed confined summary grounds

**1. Opposition to the October 1994 incident.** Assume, without deciding the entire opposition doctrine, that an employee's objectively reasonable good-faith belief that Title VII was violated receives protection even if no violation is ultimately proved. Harris asks whether conditions are objectively and subjectively hostile under all the circumstances, with severity or pervasiveness and no psychological-injury requirement. This incident occurred while employees performed assigned review of an applicant's psychological report. Breeden did not object to reading its quoted remark itself; the objection concerns the supervisor's response, a coworker's offer to explain and their laughter. On this context, the isolated exchange is not sufficiently threatening, humiliating or severe to support an objectively reasonable belief that it created the asserted unlawful working environment. This is no universal single-incident exclusion: an extremely serious incident can qualify, and reasonable opposition can precede proof of a completed violation. The Court need not decide every anticipatory complaint or a materially different pattern.

**Strongest opposing route:** Remand because an employee need not wait for repeated abuse before complaining and a supervisor's participation may reasonably signal a developing hostile environment. That proposition is sound generally, but the record supplies this particular exchange, not a supported pattern, threatened continuation or extremely serious act. Neither Harris's contextual rule nor Faragher's actual-authority attribution rule turns the mere presence of a supervisor into an objectively reasonable environment violation. No lack of psychological injury is used against Breeden.

**2. Later transfer causation.** Assume the formal administrative and litigation activity is protected. The April 10 contemplation of transfer preceded the April 11 service, and Rice's unchallenged affidavit states she learned of the action only after April 11. Implementation in May alone does not show that a previously contemplated transfer was retaliatory. The district presentation relied wholly on chronology. A changed or accelerated plan could be retaliatory if supported; no such evidence is supplied. The August 1995 charge is too remote, without other causal evidence, to support this May 1997 inference.

**Strongest opposing route:** A recent right-to-sue letter might mark employer knowledge and shorten the apparent interval. That theory first appeared in the appellate reply, not below or in the opening brief, and the record does not establish Rice's knowledge of it. It cannot sustain reversal of summary judgment on speculation. The ten-day employer-notice requirement is a statutory/regulatory command, not proof of actual receipt by Rice or her predecessor. Neither actual receipt nor nonreceipt is found by inference from that command. No general numerical timing cutoff or later retaliation/causation standard is adopted.

**3. Disposition and institutional alternative.** Grant and summarily reverse the appellate judgment, remanding consistently with the failure of the two presented retaliation theories. No damages or new hostile-environment liability issue is adjudicated. The strongest institutional alternative is denial as fact-specific correction, or plenary briefing to clarify reasonable opposition. Each grant supporter accepts that concern but chooses the summary route because the legal boundary can be expressly assumed for Breeden and the causation record presents no genuine supported dispute. This choice announces no general duty to correct every fact-bound appellate error.
