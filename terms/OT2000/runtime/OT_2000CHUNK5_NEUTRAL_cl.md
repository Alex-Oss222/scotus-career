# C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 411; No. 00-292
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-30 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federally recognized tribe clearly waives immunity from a state-court action to enforce an arbitral award by agreeing to binding arbitration, specified arbitration rules, the law of the project’s location and judicial entry of judgment on the award?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** On remand following earlier review, the Oklahoma Court of Civil Appeals directed dismissal for tribal sovereign immunity. It concluded that the arbitration and judicial-enforcement provisions suggested willingness to submit to suit but did not unequivocally waive immunity. The Oklahoma Supreme Court denied review.
* **Relief Requested:** C & L seeks reversal of the immunity dismissal and enforcement of its award. The Tribe seeks preservation of immunity despite its contractual dispute-resolution undertaking.
* **Material Record:** The Tribe proposed a standard construction contract for a roof on a commercial building on off-reservation, nontrust land in Oklahoma. The contract required arbitration of claims arising out of or relating to it under American Arbitration Association construction rules and provided that an award would be final and judgment could be entered in accordance with applicable law in any court having jurisdiction. A choice-of-law provision selected the project location’s law. Oklahoma’s arbitration statute provided for enforcement in competent state courts. Before performance began, the Tribe changed roofing material and hired another contractor. C & L demanded arbitration. The Tribe asserted immunity, declined participation, and informed the arbitrator of substantive defenses. An award granted $25,400, plus fees and costs. The Oklahoma trial court confirmed it. No congressional abrogation is claimed. [C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-411.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The present issue is consent to the judicial confirmation proceeding, not whether commercial activity alone defeats immunity. The court must read the actual agreement and incorporated prescriptions as a whole. Consent cannot create otherwise absent federal subject-matter jurisdiction, and immunity from execution against particular assets is distinct from amenability to this confirmation suit.
* **Relevant Dates:** The contract was executed in 1993. The lower judgment was reconsidered after Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. The merits event is April 30, 2001; incorporated contract terms must be evaluated as actually applicable, not silently replaced with later forms.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires an additional judgment in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

A merits decision about breach, award validity or execution requires the ordinary separate legal predicates after that threshold is resolved.

### Issue 1: Clear contractual consent to judicial enforcement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. retains immunity for governmental and commercial contracts on and off reservation land unless the Tribe clearly waives it or Congress unequivocally authorizes suit. Signing a contract or defaulting alone is insufficient.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Tribal immunity permits suit upon an unequivocal waiver and remains distinct from the underlying substantive obligation. Tribal recognition, land status, regulatory applicability and a particular remedial forum must be analyzed separately.
* **Other Governing Law:** [C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-411.pdf) identify the agreed decision process and the judicial enforcement contemplated by the parties.
* **Material Other Authority:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma preserves the separation of immunity and substantive obligation. Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez requires clear relinquishment. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan reinforces the need to identify actual consent and its scope; agreement by one person does not automatically bind another.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering immunity rule forecloses reliance solely on the commercial location or perceived fairness of enforcing a debt. It permits a waiver actually expressed through an agreement to an identified adjudicative process.
* **Open Point:** Whether this combination of binding arbitration and express judicial confirmation states sufficiently clear consent despite omitting the particular words sovereign immunity.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

C & L argues that final arbitration plus express entry of judgment gives the dispute process a judicial endpoint and would be deprived of its stated operation if immunity barred the very confirmation promised. The Tribe argues that immunity may be relinquished only clearly and that courts having jurisdiction may mean courts possessing jurisdiction independent of any waiver. It characterizes ordinary arbitration language as insufficient to show surrender of a sovereign protection. The competing positions concern what this agreement actually says, not an implied waiver merely from doing business with a nontribal party.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find a clear waiver confined to arbitration and contemplated confirmation, leaving all other procedural defenses intact. It may instead conclude that the agreement preserves immunity because the judicial language is too conditional. Either path must honor Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc.; creating a commercial-contract exception would alter governing law unnecessarily. Recognizing a clear waiver does not require reinstating every part of the award without examining any still-preserved independent objection.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A tribe that expressly accepts binding arbitration and judicial entry of judgment under the specified enforcement regime consents to the contemplated confirmation action. The decision need not decide immunity for unrelated claims, unrestricted damages suits, or particular execution measures.

**Material Uncertainty:** No additional merits finding is inferred from the existence of this appeal. Ordinary preserved defenses and remedial prerequisites remain separate from the legal issue presented.
