## Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District — 212 F.3d 738

**Provisional judgment and component positions:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the vacatur of the mandatory preliminary-admission order and remand under Adarand and Eisenberg, with the qualifications below. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would join the affirmative proposition that supported educational benefits of meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation can justify a voluntary program without a prior de jure finding. Kennedy accepts that existing holding as governing law but does not add his own affirmative join to it. Scalia and Thomas would instead vacate the appellate judgment and remand with the merits prerequisite for preliminary protection against this racial exclusion treated as established on the existing record; they would require ordinary current equitable and remedial determinations rather than automatically direct present admission.

**Common-standard remand supported by six Associates:** Race restricted Haak's actual opportunity: written acceptance was withdrawn after verification that she was white. This is not Sinkfield's claim of injury based only on neighboring electoral design. Government must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and this racial restriction, including coverage, operation, actual individual burdens and a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Brown's prohibition on state-imposed racial caste remains, while Adarand displaces categorical strict-scrutiny formulations. Eisenberg expressly permits the supported K–12 educational objective but gives no presumption to demographic balance or a program's integrative label.

The preliminary record neither establishes the complete justification nor conclusively disproves the program's claimed educational benefits. A directional interdistrict program may operate differently from a restriction based only on county percentages. Its limited scale, changing demographics and long operation are relevant evidence, not categorical proof of futility. Conversely, the existence of a program and the possibility of a legitimate objective cannot justify this child's exclusion without examining actual educational conditions, operation and alternatives. The state funding text calls for a qualifying program designed to reduce racial isolation and annual demonstration; the court of appeals' account of inevitable funding loss is not itself an express statutory ban on every outward transfer by a white city pupil. That distinction matters to the claimed necessity of the categorical racial line.

The correct disposition preserves the appellate result setting aside mandatory admission while replacing its displaced constitutional framework. It does not finally sustain the program. A changed legal standard creates no entitlement to reopen evidence: existing supported findings and concessions retain their scope, and further proceedings require an identified unresolved material issue under ordinary rules. The mandatory-injunction standard and other equitable requirements remain independent; the preliminary merits assessment is not a final validity judgment or an automatic seat entitlement. Current enrollment, grade, seat availability and continuing injury must be established where material to prospective relief. The conditional protection for completion of 1999–2000 is not an operative January 2001 placement order. The unappealed rejection of contract and estoppel grounds and other claims outside this equal-protection grant remain outside the decision.

Section 2254(b)'s exhaustion alternatives and requirement of express waiver retain their force. A constitutional error determination does not silently resolve those questions. If the independent gates permit relief, the remedy must remove the two invalid no-permit convictions and their attributable sentence portions while preserving independent convictions and lawful custody.

## Gitlitz v. Commissioner — No. 99-1295

**Provisional judgment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg would reverse and remand for application of the shareholder pass-through and basis increase before the required corporate attribute reduction. Breyer would affirm on the corporation-level, integrated exclusion-and-attribute reading described below. No Justice's position relies on a later amendment or determines an unsupported final tax amount.

**Tax-year control:** The verified 1991 section 108(d)(7)(A) directs application of subsections (a), (b) and (g) at the corporate level. The later addition of subsection (c), and the later real-property sentence in (d)(7)(B), do not enter this year. The mechanically restored statutory copy controls those version details over a generic later-version paraphrase. Reported discharge, insolvency and pro rata amounts remain record facts; they do not replace application of every ordinary deduction limit.

**Seven-Associate statutory position:** Debt-discharge income excluded from gross income under section 108(a)'s insolvency rule remains an item of income for section 1366(a)(1)(A). That provision includes tax-exempt income and is not limited to items currently taxable in the shareholder's hands. Section 1367(a)(1)(A) increases stock basis for those income items, and section 1366(d)(1) computes the loss limit with that increase. Section 108(b)(4)(A) then places attribute reduction after determination of the discharge-year tax. The sequence therefore permits the recognized basis increase and otherwise allowable loss deductions before reduction of the corporate attributes that remain.

Section 108(d)(7)(A) identifies the level at which exclusion and reduction occur; it does not say the excluded income ceases to be an item under section 1366 or reverse the enacted timing rule. Section 108(d)(7)(B) treats losses actually disallowed under section 1366(d)(1) for this year as a corporate net operating loss for attribute reduction. Determining which losses remain disallowed requires the basis calculation that section 1366(d)(1) itself directs. The provision still operates where losses exceed properly adjusted basis; it need not erase a basis increase to retain work. Section 1367(b)(1)'s return-inclusion rule applies to amounts required to be included in gross income, and therefore does not silently prohibit an increase for an excluded item that section 1366 recognizes. Section 108's insolvency ceiling and ordinary limits continue to apply; the rule does not create unlimited basis or free-standing deduction entitlement.

The strongest objection is that exclusion combined with released losses gives a shareholder a benefit without a new cash outlay and frustrates the corporate attribute bargain. The seven conclude that Congress's operative classifications and timing control despite that effect. Hill recognizes distinct tax accounts and supplies no general ban on multiple benefits; Bufferd confirms that an S corporation's corporate and shareholder tax consequences retain their different statutory functions. Atlantic Mutual permits administrative construction within genuine interpretive room, not contradiction of a clear direction or a general revenue-protection override. No applicable authorized regulation supplied here independently changes the enacted sequence.

## Glover v. United States — No. 99-8576

**Provisional judgment:** All eight Associates would reverse and remand on both presented prejudice questions. Neither an asserted additional six to twenty-one months nor the two-level character of the alleged Guidelines error may be rejected categorically as too small to constitute Strickland prejudice. None would find actual ineffective assistance or order a particular sentence on this record.

**Supported common propositions:** A defendant claiming ineffective assistance at sentencing must establish deficient performance and a reasonable probability that competent representation would have produced a shorter lawful sentence, sufficient to undermine confidence in the result. That inquiry contains no additional minimum-duration or minimum-offense-level requirement. An actual increment of imprisonment is a legally cognizable adverse consequence; this principle does not presume prejudice from every attorney error, a changed range alone or an unsupported calculation. Fretwell concerns a lost benefit the governing substantive law does not recognize. It does not demand additional unfairness when the alleged lost benefit is a lawful sentencing protection. Williams Part I, joined by all eight Associates, preserves that ordinary prejudice inquiry.

The petition presents two prejudice questions and assumes deficiency and calculation error for those questions. It does not ask the Court to decide the entire assistance claim. The presentence recommendation, the government's objection, counsel's omissions and the 84-month sentence are source facts; they do not establish that grouping was legally required or that representation was constitutionally deficient. Actual performance, correct grouping, the full lawful sentencing consequences and any properly available procedural defense remain for ordinary proceedings on remand. The government disputed performance below at the district level, so the Court must not label every alternative never raised; but neither the court of appeals' unadjudicated record nor new merits submissions support first-instance findings by this Court.

## Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — No. 99-1178

**Provisional judgment positions:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on statutory authority and remand for relief against the challenged jurisdictional assertion. Stevens and Souter would affirm statutory coverage and this economic landfill application on the economic-application ground they retained in Home Builders. Ginsburg and Breyer would reject categorical statutory exclusion but vacate and remand for application of the controlling Commerce framework to the existing record. Those are three distinct judgment positions among the eight Associates, not an equal division or a fabricated majority. No Justice would revive the abandoned separate permit-merits challenge, grant a landfill permit or displace independent state law.

### Statutory exclusion supported by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas

Section 404 does not authorize jurisdiction over these isolated, nonnavigable, intrastate ponds solely because birds crossing state lines use them as habitat. Section 1362(7) extends the Act beyond waters navigable in the traditional sense, but it does not make the retained navigable-waters terminology or the particular water-regulation structure immaterial. Riverside Bayview supports adjacent wetlands on their statutory and ecological relationship to covered waters and expressly reserves nonadjacent wetlands. These ponds are not wetlands under the Corps' definition and do not acquire the missing relationship to covered waters merely because migratory birds use them.

Section 404(g)(1)'s provision for state administration of waters other than those reserved to federal administration confirms coverage beyond traditional navigation; it does not independently define every isolated pond as covered. Section 101(a)'s protection of biological integrity and wildlife supplies statutory purpose, while section 101(b)'s preservation of state responsibilities informs the same enacted structure. Neither clause alone settles jurisdiction. The four would reject this bird-only application without holding that every nonnavigable water is excluded, every water must actually carry commerce, every wetland is unprotected, or local land use is constitutionally immune from federal regulation.

MCI and FDA require authority in the connected statutory scheme before deference; Sweet Home's broader habitat construction rests on a different enacted definition and its actual-injury limits. The Corps' change of position is not an automatic bar, and the preamble's lack of notice and comment is not treated as an independently preserved procedural claim. Because the asserted reach exceeds the statute as these four construe it, they need not settle whether every statement of this form receives Chevron, Skidmore or own-regulation deference. Christensen does not establish a universal rule against all informal interpretations. The regulation's other categories, cropland exclusion and qualified treatment-system exclusion are not adjudicated by invalidating this isolated bird-only application.

### Statutory inclusion supported by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

The definition of navigable waters as waters of the United States, section 101(a)'s protection of biological integrity and aquatic wildlife functions, and section 404(g)(1)'s express treatment of waters beyond the traditional navigation category permit coverage of intrastate ponds whose destruction bears the required interstate relationship. The statute states no categorical requirement of physical adjacency or hydrological connection for every covered water. Riverside Bayview leaves the present question open; it does not transform its own adjacent-wetland facts into an exhaustive statutory limit. The regulation's commerce condition remains operative, and migratory-bird use must be assessed within it rather than as a free-standing grant of general land-use power.

These four find the Corps' biological-function interpretation within the statutory coverage language, subject to the independent constitutional and regulatory conditions. Sweet Home and PUD No. 1 support giving broad environmental terms their actual statutory operation while preserving distinct triggers and limits. MCI's requirement of enacted authority remains; unlike removal of mandatory filing, recognizing qualifying waters here does not replace an express statutory command. FDA's majority supplies no universal rule barring consequential agency coverage, and the four retain their published distinction between broad coverage terms and the independent constraints on permissible administration. This statutory conclusion does not establish that every pond used by a bird automatically satisfies Commerce authority.

### Commerce positions and the actual consequence of entering Home Builders

The governing Lopez/Home Builders/Morrison framework cannot be replaced with aggregation of bird-related recreation spending, a species count, national ecological importance or the commercial character of this one landfill. On the controlling enacted-class approach, the relevant prohibition reaches discharges of dredged or fill material into the claimed waters; its statutory scope is not limited to commercial landfill construction. An interstate mechanism must connect that regulated class before aggregate magnitude can do constitutional work.

The two available routes retain their full terms. For necessary inclusion, the Government must identify a valid interstate regime and materially support why excluding the challenged local class permits evasion or materially impairs that regime; the Clean Water Act's comprehensiveness alone does not establish necessity. For the independent externality route, it must identify a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable effect generated by the regulated class, materially support its substantial character, explain why individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize it through territorial authority, and show a reasonable fit between the prohibition and that spillover. There is no requirement of a particular species count, dollar figure, scientific technique, formal congressional finding, clear-and-convincing proof or separately substantial effect from this individual fill.

The admitted bird use, agency species count and denial reasons remain at their actual source status. They are not this Court's findings of substantial population loss, a particular interstate ecological pathway or inability of States to internalize that effect. Conversely, the absence of such findings in the lower judgment does not establish that the existing record cannot support them. The court of appeals' bird-related-commerce rationale has not performed the governing connection, territorial-control and fit inquiries. Ginsburg and Breyer therefore support a bounded existing-record remand under Home Builders, preserving Congress's predictive latitude after an eligible connection is identified. Their remand corrects the legal inquiry; it is not an evidentiary order issued to cure a research gap or an automatic new chance to manufacture facts.

Stevens and Souter retain their different Home Builders position: the physical habitat-altering construction and conversion at issue are themselves economic conduct, rather than merely a landowner's unrelated purchases. A municipal landfill's construction and operation provide the actual activity connection here. They would assess this economic application in its real market setting under Wickard, Perez and Hodel and affirm, reserving other noneconomic applications and any independent externality theory. That is a disagreement with the controlling enacted-class approach, not an assertion that they join Home Builders Parts I–IV. They do not purport to establish the missing externality predicates from bird use alone.
