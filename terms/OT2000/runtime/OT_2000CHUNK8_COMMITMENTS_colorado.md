## Operative components and applicable law

**C1 — Classification and standard.** Actual coordinated party spending can deliver a candidate the practical equivalent of campaign resources. Whether that connection permits contribution treatment, and whether it encompasses the party's own jointly developed message, are distinct constitutional questions. Buckley's surviving contribution/expenditure distinction controls; the actual Colorado holding prohibits presuming cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion merely from party affiliation. Contribution regulation remains subject to the government's affirmative burden under Russell and Shrink Missouri: an objectively supported, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest and close fit preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy. Daggett and Mariani retain that burden; neither independently decides this party-expression question.

**C2 — Facial validity of the presented Senate-election ceiling.** The relevant provisions permit the greater of two cents per voting-age resident or $20,000 for the Senate and single-Representative-State category, increased under §441a(c). The separate $10,000 indexed provision governs other House, Delegate and Resident Commissioner races. Section (d)(1)'s notwithstanding clause provides the party expenditure allowance subject to its specified limits; it does not itself determine constitutional validity. The present counterclaim supports no independent constitutional judgment on the presidential allowance or every other office category.

**C3 — Remedy and preserved independence.** Reversing the facial invalidation rejects the requested categorical exemption and returns the counterclaim for conforming disposition and any genuinely remaining proceedings. It establishes no coordination, liability or penalty for the already protected advertisement. Protecting the party's own coordinated expression instead would require bounded declaratory relief and any necessary severability treatment on remand, preserving ordinary candidate contributions and genuine conduit applications.

**Distinct statutory predicates.** Section (a)(4) exempts transfers among national, State, district, local and subordinate committees of the same party from (a)(1)–(2). Section (a)(7)(B)(i) requires actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion involving a candidate, authorized committee or agent. Section (a)(7)(B)(ii)'s candidate-material dissemination, distribution or republication rule is separately triggered. Section (a)(8) attributes directly or indirectly directed or earmarked contributions to the original source and requires intermediary reporting of that source and intended recipient. Neither interparty transfer permission nor an absence of a formal earmark negates every actual-coordination application. Nor does the anti-circumvention interest prove these predicates in a particular enforcement case.

## Supported alternatives and record assessment

**Contribution-safeguard path.** Reject facial invalidation because the candidate-connected category can be subject to contribution scrutiny and the record supports a closely fitted preventive ceiling. Testimony describes candidate fundraising credits, tally systems and donor understandings that permit larger party gifts to benefit a candidate without a traceable express earmark. These supported mechanisms supply more than a generalized claim that parties influence their candidates. Earmark enforcement alone is not an adequate answer to the described fungibility and tacit arrangements. The allowance, separate national and State participation, and the protected independent-expression channel bear on practical operation, while none alone proves fit. The party's serious evidence about impaired joint communication and inefficient independent spending must be weighed as a burden on the chosen association, not dismissed because it could speak elsewhere.

**Strongest contrary case.** The ceiling limits the party's central collaborative political message even when funded by lawful, restricted and disclosed receipts. Paying for a single statewide communication may exceed the allowance. Party members' agreement with their candidate is protected association, and testimony denying actual corruption plus existing contribution and earmark restrictions weakens a categorical burden. The sustaining path answers that the Constitution does not require proof of completed corruption or transaction-by-transaction express earmarks, and that hard-money source compliance does not prevent the cumulative candidate-directed benefit the record describes. This is a conclusion about the presented facial claim and this operation, not a presumption validating every amount or coordinated-communication application.

**Party-expression path.** A party's own political communication remains its expression when it cooperates with its candidate. Protect that expression while preserving limits on direct cash, candidate-controlled financial benefits and genuine donor conduits. The strongest objection is that such a line could allow the party to buy everything the campaign would otherwise buy, defeating contribution limits. The proponents answer that candidate-directed purchases and actual conduits can still be regulated on their own predicates; an expenditure ceiling cannot use those examples to eliminate protection of a party's independently owned, jointly developed political message. The packet does not establish that every coordinated expenditure falls on one side of that line, so this path cannot support universal immunity for all party spending.

## Compatibility, remedy and separate-writing functions

The five sustaining Justices can join a common opinion that expressly applies the government's burden and rejects facial invalidation of the presented Senate coordinated-spending ceiling. It must explain the donor-understanding/tally mechanism, the insufficiency of earmarks alone, and why the serious association burden does not establish facial invalidity on this record. It must retain the original independence determination. An opinion merely reciting that coordination is a contribution and therefore valid would omit their material fit commitment.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas share a contrary party-expression ground and a substantive dissenting function: distinguish a party's own jointly developed message from a contribution substitute, explain why the government's evidence does not support the broader expressive ceiling, and preserve targeted cash/conduit regulation. Their proposed remedial disposition is bounded protection of that expression, with any overbroad lower-court declaration conformed on remand. No author is assigned. A future draft could secure their agreement on regulation of demonstrated candidate-directed financial substitutes, but that narrower agreement cannot be counted as assent to the complete sustaining framework.

No final penalty, donation-attribution finding, retrospective enforcement liability, exact replacement ceiling, compelled new trial, refund or damages award is authorized. No independent or coordinated fact about the original advertisements is reopened. The presidential and other distinct office allowances receive no new holding. A question about a particular later communication or a materially different amount is ordinary application or a separate challenge, not a silently resolved category here.
