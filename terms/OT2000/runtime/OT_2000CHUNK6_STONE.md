**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-21. **Date status:** historical.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment on the reviewed federal securities-liability issue.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the existing judgment within the scope reviewed, without a new damages award or reopening unreviewed state-law grounds. Compensation under the proved federal claim and punitive relief resting on state law retain their distinct legal sources.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Contemporaneously sham option sale

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Treat United as the purchaser of the option found by the jury. Review statutory coverage independently while accepting supported verdict facts; do not convert the case into an unproved promise to purchase shares later.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether deliberate deception about the seller's present intention to honor the option occurs in connection with selling that security.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the securities statute and the governing fraud requirements to a new transactional setting. No change to Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. is necessary because the seller's own conduct supplies the alleged deception.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A seller who obtains value by selling an option it contemporaneously intends never to honor employs a deceptive device in connection with that security's sale. Oral form does not erase a security the statute otherwise covers. A subsequently broken promise, standing alone, establishes neither initial deception nor culpability. This is the adopted application of existing elements to the option sale.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers sham options regardless of drafting form, with all elements of the available private action still required. Regulatory breadth and the seriousness of misconduct do not eliminate reliance, transaction causation, loss, or ordinary proof requirements.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An option is the right to choose a later purchase; a concealed intention to deny that choice affects what is sold now. The memoranda and jury findings distinguish the transaction from an honest commercial disagreement. Ordinary evidence rules address proof difficulties without adding a writing requirement Congress did not enact.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unpresented foreign-transaction limitations and the independent state-law punitive award lie outside the granted federal coverage question. Other disputed oral agreements would require their own facts; no new factual holding about them is made.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment on the population-base question presented.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the challenged total-population choice operative. Require no new map or election on the sole ground of unequal citizen voting-age populations.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Total-population districting

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the constitutional merits of this population base. Do not characterize the issue as categorically beyond judicial review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a city's equal allocation of resident representation becomes unconstitutional solely because noncitizens are concentrated unevenly.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing principles of reviewability and population equality and adopt a new, bounded holding permitting total-population apportionment here.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality may apportion representative council districts by total resident population despite substantial differences in citizen voting-age population. The numerical disparity between eligible electorates, standing alone, does not make that representational choice unconstitutional.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Total population measures people represented by the council. This holding does not approve every deviation, intentional exclusion, vote-denial practice or discriminatory manipulation merely because a plan uses that denominator.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The equal-protection injury must be identified rather than inferred from a slogan. Equal resident representation serves persons whose interests and requests the council must address, although some cannot vote. Counting those persons is not counting their nonexistent ballots. The plaintiffs have shown a real numerical distinction, but not why the Constitution forbids a city to equalize the people each council member represents. The 8.63 percent total-population variation is not automatically immune; it simply does not establish this asserted denominator defect.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether a city could use a citizen-only or registered-voter denominator is outside the question presented. The separate racial-gerrymandering claim and any distinct intentional exclusion or dilution theory require their own preserved grounds and are outside this accepted population-base review.

# Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-29. **Date status:** historical.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Fourth Circuit judgment affirming categorical denial of fees and remand for application of the stated prevailing-party standard.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower court must determine legal substantiality, a material causal contribution to the concrete relief sought, and then a reasonable fee if eligibility is proved. No fee is awarded here, and the underlying mootness dismissal remains intact.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory success obtained through voluntary compliance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide fee eligibility under the identified statutes while leaving the moot merits action closed. Require a lawful fee source and evidence; good intentions do not prove prevailing status.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these statutes exclude an otherwise prevailing plaintiff solely because the defendant supplies the requested relief before a decree.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the statutory question by adopting a confined catalyst route. Neither that route nor a decree-exclusive requirement is established by existing precedent. Preserve Farrar v. Hobby's distinction between status and amount.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A plaintiff prevails when a legally substantial action materially causes the defendant to supply an enduring, concrete benefit sought in that action. The plaintiff bears the causal showing. Chronology alone, favorable judicial language, or unrelated policy change is insufficient. This is the new statutory holding Stone adopts for the open question.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the housing and disability fee claims presented and similarly situated claims under the statutory prevailing-party language to the extent their text and structure support that application. It creates no automatic fee entitlement in every moot action or private settlement.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Voluntary compliance can accomplish the success litigation seeks. The ordinary statutory term does not expressly require a judgment, and courts can adjudicate causation through evidence. Defendants remain free to reform; fees follow proved litigation success under Congress's rule. Neither expenditure nor asserted public importance proves eligibility. Any amount must segregate unrelated work and reflect actual success. The statutory meaning of prevailing is decisive, read against the actual success the fee provisions reward. A decree-only safe harbor for defendants would let unilateral delivery of the requested relief defeat fees for the very success Congress sought to make attainable. That consequence confirms the textual reading rather than independently authorizing fees. Contemporaneous communications, the legislative record of repeal, testimony, admissions, and the course of the litigation may establish causation; neither a statistical study nor temporal sequence alone is required or sufficient. Hewitt and Rhodes are distinguished because favorable words without concrete benefit differ from the proved relief required here.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether this lawsuit actually caused repeal, whether the claims were legally substantial, and the reasonable amount require findings absent from the existing categorical fee denial. Those factual issues remain for remand.

# NLRB v. Kentucky River Community Care, Inc.

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-05-29. **Date status:** historical.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's judgment insofar as it rejects the Board's supervisory-status burden and invalidates the inclusion of the six registered nurses; remand for enforcement consistent with the Board's supported employee-status determination.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the Board's treatment of the six nurses as employees within the bargaining unit and give effect to the bargaining order on that basis. Address only genuinely preserved remaining enforcement matters. Do not order a new classification hearing merely because the employer disagrees with the Board's permissible construction, or award unadjudicated backpay.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Proving and assessing genuine supervisory authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The petition presents the meaning of independent judgment, the burden of proving exclusion, and their application to these six nurses. Decide all three on the agency's actual rationale and record. The representation ruling is properly reviewed through the refusal-to-bargain proceeding.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Board may distinguish ordinary professional direction within employer-prescribed standards from independent supervisory judgment, assign the supervisory-exclusion burden to the employer, and find these six nurses to be protected employees.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reaffirm and apply NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America's permissible functional construction. Adopt the burden rule placing proof on the party asserting the supervisory exclusion. Reject the claim that the Board's actual rule creates a blanket professional exemption; no change to the earlier controlling construction is necessary.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The party asserting supervisory exclusion must prove a statutory supervisory function exercised with the required independent authority. The Board may treat ordinary professional or technical direction within employer-specified standards as insufficient without separate proof of the supervisory independence the Act requires. This is a permissible distinction between kinds and degrees of authority, not immunity for all professional judgment. On the supported findings the employer did not establish that the six nurses possessed the required supervisory authority, and their inclusion in the unit must be sustained.

* **Reach Within the Question:** One genuinely supervisory statutory function can suffice, including responsible direction; neither hiring-and-firing power nor frequent exercise is indispensable. Professional credentials do not establish or defeat exclusion. Nurses who exercise genuine independent supervisory power may be excluded, but clinical responsibility and the title building supervisor alone do not settle that question.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Act protects professional employees whose ordinary work itself requires judgment while separately excluding supervisors. Treating any skilled direction of an assistant as independent supervisory judgment would allow the exclusion to consume that protection. The decisive ground is the Board's permissible reconciliation of the statutory provisions, already sustained in Health Care & Retirement; its practical application confirms that reading. The Board considered treatment plans, employer standards, the nurses' duties, management's continuing availability, and the asserted direction of other staff. Its rule concerns ordinary professional direction under prescribed standards, not every judgment made by a professional. Professional competence can coexist with genuine supervisory power, and the employer must demonstrate the latter rather than obtain exclusion by occupational label. The underlying testimony and institutional arrangements can establish that allocation without a statistical study. The burden properly rests on the party invoking the exclusion and possessing the relevant information about delegated authority. The reviewing court may correct a legal error or unsupported finding, but it may not replace the agency's discernible permissible judgment with its own preferred definition. Sustaining this determination therefore protects the statutory bargaining right without creating a special occupational entitlement or rewriting section 2(11).

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The supervisory status of other employees under materially different delegations requires their own records. No unpresented occupational exemption or restriction on every future Board interpretation is decided.

### Issue 1: Covered services and the necessary cart modification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Both Title III coverage and the claimed fundamental-alteration defense are preserved and necessary. Martin's disability and need are established, and the supported trial findings must be respected under the governing review standard.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this golf-course operator must remove a disability barrier to its offered competitive service when doing so preserves the competition's essential character.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A golf-course operator offering qualifying and tournament participation supplies covered services to competitors as well as spectators. It must make necessary reasonable modifications unless it establishes alteration of an essential feature or an individualized change conferring a material competitive advantage. On these findings Martin's cart is required.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects access to covered facilities and offered competitive services while preserving essential skill requirements and ordinary qualification. It does not declare every sports rule dispensable. A competitor need not be an employee to receive this Title III protection.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute expressly covers golf courses and broadly enumerates services and privileges. Calling participants the product does not remove those offered opportunities from coverage. Walking's role must be established by actual competitive function. Related cart use and trial findings show that Martin's accommodation preserves shot-making competition. His substantial fatigue even with a cart defeats the claimed advantage on this record. The statutory modification duty and its assigned defense are decisive; functional examination identifies whether the requested change is fundamental. Medical testimony, the actual competition rules, existing cart practices, and supported findings about Martin's fatigue supply the proof. The operator bears its statutory fundamental-alteration burden. This application gives effect to individualized access while preserving essential competitive requirements.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Employment status is outside the Title III questions presented. Different disabilities and contests with essential physical tests require facts absent from this individual accommodation record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Arkansas Supreme Court's judgment and remand. Reject both its asserted authority to enlarge federal constitutional doctrine and its treatment of motive alone as sufficient to invalidate otherwise authorized enforcement.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require separate determination of lawful custody, lawful impoundment, and a genuine standardized inventory. Suppression remains available if a preserved independent violation requires it. The Court does not direct admission of the evidence or a conviction; it identifies the legal inquiries the remand must resolve.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Objective authority must cover the actual intrusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The State's federal claim is reviewable in this suppression posture. Reject the lower court's asserted power to enlarge federal constitutional law, then identify the restraint and search exception actually at issue. Consider any earlier effective holding in Atwater v. City of Lago Vista before resolving the arrest question.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether investigative motive defeats otherwise lawful enforcement, and whether that principle supplies missing objective authority for additional custody, impoundment, or an inventory.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Whren v. United States and Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights within their actual holdings and preserve Knowles v. Iowa's lawful-custody predicate. Extend Ricci to fine-only criminal custody for the reasons below; do not treat every minor offense as equivalent regardless of its authorized punishment.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An ordinary objectively justified traffic stop is not invalid because the officer also has an investigative purpose. The same principle does not eliminate independent constitutional predicates for additional custody or a search. A claimed inventory must perform its authorized caretaking function under genuine standards. State courts may develop state-law protection but may not override controlling federal holdings while purporting to interpret federal law.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The inquiry covers the complete stop-to-search sequence without treating probable cause for the initial traffic infraction as conclusive authorization for every later step. Separate objective grounds may sustain custody or impoundment if they are legally sufficient and supported.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The hatchet and documentation circumstances may matter, but the available account does not establish all required custody and impoundment findings or the operative inventory standards. The lower court's motive-only reasoning and the State's blanket objective-probable-cause theory each omit necessary inquiries. Remand permits resolution of preserved independent grounds without using the discovered drugs as an antecedent justification. Ricci should extend to fine-only criminal custody because the criminal label cannot itself justify a restraint beyond the actual needs of enforcing that violation. If these traffic predicates are fine-only, the court must examine identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence, and necessary-processing grounds. The objective justification for each intrusion is decisive; the hatchet or any independently adequate offense must be assessed on its own legal elements. This extends the custody principle without turning every minor offense into a mandatory-citation case.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate inventory validity and the independent weapons ground require findings from the suppression record not supplied here. No unsupported finding concerning either ground is made.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether EPA may prevent review of this completed, operative permitting directive through a disclaimer.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Bennett v. Spear's requirements and adopt their specific application to this guidance; do not replace legal consequences with influence alone.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Guidance is final when the agency has completed its determination and applies the announced requirement as controlling through a permitting process that determines legal permission to operate. EPA's settled directive has those consequences notwithstanding its disclaimer. Tentative advice that leaves decisionmakers genuinely free to decide does not meet this holding merely because regulated parties listen to it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the challenged mandatory sufficiency policy, not every technical recommendation in every agency publication.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The legal consequence is the permit constraint backed by EPA's veto, not a claim that the guidance validly enacted law. * **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual permit obligations require facts absent here; other review statutes lie outside the question presented.

### Issue 2: A substantive addition requires lawful adoption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the regulatory-text and required-procedure grounds. The alternative challenge to the 1992 regulation and the ultimate limits of EPA's statutory authority are unnecessary to this disposition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this guidance may expand conditional gap-filling into mandatory reassessment of already prescribed periodic testing.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing limits on regulatory interpretation. The specific construction of this monitoring regulation and associated severability determination are new applications.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An agency cannot use guidance to impose substantive monitoring duties beyond a regulation that conditions supplementation on the absence of periodic testing or monitoring. The 1998 directive adds precisely that duty and is invalid without the required rulemaking. Interpretation may explain the existing trigger and the monitoring it authorizes; it cannot erase the trigger.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Sources with no periodic testing, unspecified frequency, or a one-time test remain subject to lawful supplementation. Sources with existing recurring requirements cannot be subjected to the additional mandate on this guidance's asserted authority alone. Independent lawful authority remains available.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reliable monitoring is a legitimate objective, but the method for adding legal duties matters. The regulatory condition and the original promise to revise deficient federal standards through rulemaking defeat the claim that the same universal reassessment duty was already enacted. Interpretive deference cannot supply missing adoption authority. Integrated invalid requirements justify vacating the guidance while leaving lawful pollution controls operative.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether a particular stricter state requirement rests on independent state authority requires a permit and legal record absent here. The validity of any future regulation lies outside the question presented.

### Issue 1: Filing-time coverage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the filing-status question and the effect of a supplement merely recording release. No alternative constitutional ruling is needed to decide that question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether later release changes the statutory status of an action brought by a confined prisoner for covered injury.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the preserved statutory text and resolve the temporal issue through a new holding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Confinement when the federal civil action is brought determines the application of this physical-injury restriction to covered mental or emotional injury claims. Subsequent release and a pleading noting that release do not cure the original action's covered deficiency. Dismissal resting solely on that restriction must not operate as an adjudication of the underlying misconduct or permanently bar a permissible post-release action.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns this action and this type of injury. It does not make every monetary claim in a prison case subject to a single undifferentiated bar.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The operative language selects commencement. The definition identifies who is a prisoner; it does not move the selected time to judgment. Supplemental pleading can establish the new event, but it cannot change the legal consequence Congress attached to the earlier one. These six plaintiffs were confined at commencement. # United States v. Napier

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to effective intervening law before June 1, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply intervening controlling decisions and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining both firearm and ammunition convictions against the accepted Second Amendment challenge. Reject the collective-right rationale below. A qualifying civil protective order can justify the possession prohibition; a criminal conviction is not required.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No remand on this constitutional question. The conditional plea and both convictions remain effective against this objection. Preserve the plea's admissions and the limits of its reservation; the distinct, unpreserved evidentiary attack on the orders is not reopened. This disposition grants no release, firearm return, or restoration of possession rights.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Entering law:** Love and Hale establish personal invocation and the separation between coverage and permissible regulation. Wright makes practical severity determine scrutiny, subject to its categorical protection against general abolition of ordinary defensive home possession. Fraternal Order of Police upheld a conviction-based disability in an official-use controversy; it expressly left materially different private-possession applications open.

**Stone's Holding:** A qualifying domestic-violence protective order, entered after actual notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest the evidence, can justify a prohibition on firearm and ammunition possession when supported by findings establishing domestic violence and a risk of recurrence or a credible threat of such violence. The Constitution does not require a criminal conviction before this preventive protection can operate. Proven domestic violence with a supported risk of recurrence is a sufficient danger predicate; the Government need not await another assault or prove that the next assault is inevitable. Napier's admitted orders justify the prohibition while they remain operative.

**Core-Preservation Review:** The pistol and its necessary ammunition fall within the personal right. A complete possession disability is severe; describing it as a safety regulation does not reduce its practical burden. Stone would nevertheless find strict scrutiny satisfied on this admitted record. Preventing armed domestic violence against a partner or child is compelling. The restriction addresses an identified person whose violence and risk of recurrence were judicially established after notice and an opportunity to participate, and it operates only while the qualifying protective order remains in effect. It does not abolish ordinary defensive arms for the general population.

The prohibition directly removes access to the means of armed escalation. A prohibition limited to carrying in particular public places would leave that access in the domestic setting; merely repeating a command not to assault or threaten would not provide the same preventive protection. Those measures are not comparably effective substitutes for disarmament on these findings. The Constitution does not require proof that the adjudicated violence already involved a firearm. Necessary ammunition may be restricted with firearms because exempting either would undermine the restraint. The existing adjudication and the restriction's dependence on the operative order supply the required fit here; no additional criminal proceeding or freestanding second dangerousness adjudication is required solely because the predicate proceeding was civil.

**Record application:** The December 1996 and September 1998 orders found that domestic violence had occurred and might recur. Napier had actual notice and an opportunity to participate, reportedly attended the hearings, and admitted knowing possession while both orders remained operative. These adjudicated findings support affirmance. The later assault report is not itself an adjudication and does no independent work in this holding. The absence of complete hearing transcripts from the supplied summary does not establish defective process or require reopening an evidentiary challenge outside the plea's reservation.

**Boundary:** A protective-order label, an unadjudicated allegation, or speculation that violence might someday occur does not itself establish the necessary danger. An evidence-backed judicial finding of a credible threat is different from that speculation; this record also establishes completed domestic violence, so the precise evidentiary boundary of a threat-only case need not be settled here. Ex parte orders and unrelated civil orders present different questions. Separate commerce, fair-warning, and state-constitutional issues remain outside this grant.

## D. DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT AND FUTURE APPLICATION

Danger-based disqualification is compatible with the personal arms right when supported by adequate adjudication and a restriction fitted to the established danger. Stone adopts this civil-order application while respecting Wright's scrutiny of the actual burden. FOP did not already decide it. Protection of the individual right does not immunize adjudicated domestic violence from preventive disarmament.

**Later restoration:** Stone accepts that a person may later establish a basis for lawful restoration. Where modification or restoration is provided, it must be genuinely available and may require evidence that the danger has sufficiently abated; eligibility to seek relief is not an entitlement to immediate firearm return. No restoration claim or particular waiting period is decided here. Section 922(g)(8)'s disability operates while the qualifying order remains in force: when that predicate ends, this provision supplies no continuing prohibition, although another independently lawful disability may apply. A longer or permanent restriction would require its own legal basis and constitutional justification.

# Penry v. Johnson

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's rejection of the psychiatric habeas claim; reverse its rejection of the mitigation claim and remand for sentence correction under the stated standard.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply State-borne harmless-error review under Chapman v. California to the preserved original-charge restriction on capital mitigation. If the State cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the full penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death, issue a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. Preserve guilt and make no unsupported whole-record harmlessness finding.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Defense-generated psychiatric material under the habeas statute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved claim through the statutory habeas restriction and keep the certificate inquiry distinct. The independent direct-review scope of a privilege objection is unnecessary once the state court's treatment survives the statutory gateway.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether established examination law required exclusion of the earlier defense-requested report when the defense mental-health expert was cross-examined.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Williams v. Taylor to a materially distinguishable examination setting. Decline to derive a new universal waiver or exclusion rule from the limited ground sufficient to resolve this habeas claim.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The state court's rejection of the psychiatric claim is neither contrary to nor objectively unreasonable under the established compelled-examination holding. A defense-requested earlier examination used in cross-examining a defense mental-condition expert differs materially from the compelled, uncounseled capital examination in Estelle v. Smith.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding resolves eligibility for federal habeas relief on this record. It does not declare that all mental-health testimony waives every privilege objection or that all reports become admissible once mental condition is discussed.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The report originated with a defense request in an unrelated competency proceeding, and the defense expert reviewed it while testifying to mental condition. Those circumstances support the distinction made by the state court. The statutory standard cannot be displaced merely because a broader exclusion rule could be argued on direct review. The statutory restriction on relief is decisive on this claim; a preference for a broader direct-review privilege does not make the state court's materially distinct treatment unreasonable. This application leaves the Fifth Amendment's protection against a genuinely compelled capital examination intact.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The unnecessary broader direct-review inquiry is identified under Threshold and Merits Reach rather than treated as a reserved question within this statutory disposition.

### Issue 2: Coherent mitigation instructions and a bounded remedial extension

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved claim concerns supported mitigation and the actual original charge. Decide the statutory gateway, the constitutional impairment, and the separate effect inquiry in that sequence. No categorical disability exemption or prescribed evidentiary weight is necessary.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the charge gave the jury an effective lawful route to choose life because of reduced culpability, and what consequence follows if the contradictory factual-answer mechanism denied that route.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established functional mitigation rule. Adopt expressly extending the State-borne harmless-error standard of Chapman v. California to this defined preserved original-charge restriction, rather than representing Calderon v. Coleman as an already universal habeas rule.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A capital jury must be able to give independent culpability mitigation effect through an authorized lawful verdict. Requiring a negative answer to an otherwise true factual proposition while retaining instructions to answer those propositions does not supply a coherent vehicle. The state court's acceptance falls outside the established rule's reasonable range. For this preserved original-charge category, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the restriction did not contribute to death; that remedial extension is adopted as new.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs materially equivalent contradictory special-issue mechanisms on a supported mitigation record. It requires neither a particular fourth question nor favorable weight for the evidence. Guilt remains outside the penalty claim.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Intellectual impairment and childhood abuse can reduce culpability while leaving future dangerousness intact or increasing it. The jury must be able to express that permissible judgment without disguising it as a false factual finding. The supplemental instruction and unchanged form preserve the conflict. Allocating the uncertainty from an established capital-selection error to the State follows the rationale of the existing narrower remedy, while the extension is candidly identified. The functional requirement that mitigation can affect the lawful verdict is decisive. The contradictory charge cannot be saved by admitting the evidence while withholding a coherent route to act on it. Graham and Johnson supplied that merits command before the relevant state adjudication; the newly adopted harmless-error extension does not become an earlier section 2254(d)(1) benchmark. The State may contest harmlessness through the entire record, including testimony and instructions, but the affirmative special-issue answers do not themselves show what an unconflicted jury would have done. The defined extension of Chapman addresses the reliability of selecting death and does not silently change harmlessness for every habeas claim.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual whole-record harmlessness requires the complete penalty record, which is not supplied. The lower court must perform that inquiry rather than infer absence of effect from the affirmative special-issue answers.

# Pollard v. E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co.

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's front-pay cap ruling and remand for a determination under the proper statutory classification.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the district court to determine appropriate front pay under the existing equitable-remedy provision without counting it against the added damages cap. Require support for substitution or delay of reinstatement, causal earnings loss, duration, mitigation, and avoidance of overlapping recovery. Do not reopen liability or prescribe an unsupported amount.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Front pay follows its employment-restoration source

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved classification question determines whether the trial court mistakenly curtailed its remedial authority. Decide it without reopening liability or reaching a constitutional damages question.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether front pay restoring compensation lost because reinstatement is delayed or inappropriate is authorized equitable employment relief and therefore outside the added damages cap.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted structure and Landgraf v. USI Film Products's preserved-category distinction to a classification not previously resolved. The front-pay classification is the adopted additional holding; existing temporal and punitive decisions are not recast as having already decided it.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Front pay serving as a substitute for or bridge to reinstatement is appropriate affirmative equitable relief under the existing Title VII provision. The 1991 provision excludes that relief from its new compensatory category, so its damages cap does not apply. This classification covers eventual reinstatement and circumstances in which reinstatement cannot appropriately occur.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers a proved employment-restoration remedy. Emotional distress, separate consequential loss, and punitive damages retain their own statutory sources and limits; a label cannot convert them into front pay.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The exclusion turns on the source of remedial authorization, not on whether wages accrue before or after judgment. Congress added damages while preserving the earlier remedies. Prospective compensation may be necessary to restore an employment position when immediate return is unavailable, including because the established hostile environment makes return inappropriate. Pollard's findings support reconsideration, but do not themselves fix the duration or amount.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual feasibility of reinstatement and the calculation require facts not supplied. Broader jury allocation and punitive-damages questions are outside the grant.
