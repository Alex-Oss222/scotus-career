# Daniels v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Daniels v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 532 U.S. 374; No. 99-9136
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-04-25 (historical decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal prisoner may use 28 U.S.C. § 2255 to challenge an enhanced federal sentence on the ground that completed state convictions used as predicates resulted from involuntary counseled pleas or ineffective assistance of counsel?
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a faithful formulation of the issue accepted for merits review; exact granted wording was not independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of Daniels’s § 2255 motion, reasoning that the limitation recognized in Custis v. United States extended to this collateral proceeding unless the predicate conviction involved unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel.
* **Relief Requested:** Daniels seeks consideration of the constitutional validity of two robbery predicates and relief from his enhanced federal sentence if the necessary predicates cannot lawfully support it. The United States seeks affirmance of the denial.
* **Material Record:** Daniels was convicted in 1994 of federal felon-in-possession. Four California convictions, two burglaries and two robberies from 1977 through 1981, supplied the asserted ACCA predicates. After a downward departure he received 176 months, exceeding the otherwise applicable 120-month maximum. His direct appeal challenged the burglary convictions’ statutory classification unsuccessfully. His subsequent § 2255 motion instead alleged that the 1978 and 1981 robbery pleas were not knowing and voluntary and that the latter conviction involved ineffective representation. The sentences for those convictions had expired. His counsel acknowledged no indication that he pursued the constitutional challenges while in custody on those convictions or was externally prevented from doing so. These are allegations of defective counseled proceedings, not a demonstrated complete denial of constitutionally required appointed counsel. [Daniels v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-374.pdf).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Custody under the present federal judgment supplies the immediate statutory vehicle; it does not automatically create custody under each expired state judgment. The statutory-predicate objection and the constitutional collateral attack are different claims. No predicate invalidation is established. A genuinely unavailable earlier review channel is not claimed on this record, and the Court should not manufacture such a circumstance.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged predicates date from 1978 and 1981; the federal conviction dates from 1994. Applicable review limits and the actual opportunity to contest the predicates must be assessed historically. This event is April 25, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss presents the related state-prisoner vehicle on the same date and receives its own listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional same-term decision is relied upon. The historical disposition of the same-day companion does not govern this case.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Coordinate with Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss. If either decision becomes effective first, its holding must be considered in the other matter with due regard for the distinct statutory vehicle.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 2255 authorizes correction of an unlawful federal sentence subject to its own procedural conditions. A refusal to entertain an indirect attack differs from a finding that the state plea was constitutional. A lawful denial also need not establish that every imaginable collateral claim is unavailable.

### Issue 1: Collateral attacks on completed predicates

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Custis v. United States rejects a general ACCA sentencing forum for ineffective-assistance and involuntary-counseled-plea attacks, while retaining the unconstitutional complete-denial-of-counsel exception. It expressly leaves expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases undecided.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Collateral review distinguishes statutory eligibility, complete deprivation of counsel, and an independently authorized review channel. Successful predicate invalidation permits an authorized application concerning the enhancement, without promising reopening or a particular sentence.
* **Other Governing Law:** [Daniels v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-374.pdf). Section 2255’s reference to the current federal sentence must be interpreted independently of the ACCA’s sentencing procedure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Garlotte v. Fordice permits review of an earlier segment of a continuous, unexpired consecutive series when relief would advance release; it distinguishes a wholly expired independent sentence. Maleng v. Cook likewise separates present custody from collateral consequences. Parke v. Raley preserves lawful regularity and proof rules.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Custis v. United States controls the existing sentencing-forum limitation, but extending that limitation into § 2255 requires a new holding. Nichols v. United States addresses a valid fine-only conviction’s particular Guidelines use, not these allegations that the predicates themselves were unconstitutional. Almendarez-Torres v. United States preserves the collateral-review distinction despite its separate element-classification rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether § 2255 offers renewed predicate review after an available original review opportunity has expired; a wholly unavailable review channel would present a distinct case.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Daniels emphasizes that § 2255 expressly reaches a federal sentence imposed in violation of the Constitution and that present incarceration supplies a practical reason to challenge the predicates. He distinguishes collateral review, where a record can be developed, from the immediate sentencing proceeding addressed in Custis v. United States. The government emphasizes the finality of state judgments, stale records, the original jurisdiction’s review opportunities and the risk of avoiding the limits governing those proceedings. Its strongest argument concerns a defendant who allowed available review to lapse. A contention that the ACCA itself resolved every § 2255 question would overstate the holding of Custis v. United States.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could allow a properly presented challenge to the current sentence while applying ordinary procedural and merits restrictions. Alternatively, it could adopt a bounded rule precluding renewed review of these counselled predicates when an earlier meaningful opportunity existed and no external impediment is alleged. A categorical prohibition covering every faultless loss of a review forum would go beyond the facts. Any new review permission must still identify the authorized vehicle and required causal relationship to the present enhancement.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule about reopening expired constitutional attacks through § 2255, limited to the available-review circumstances proved here. It need not alter statutory ACCA classification, complete counsel deprivation, or the consequences of an independently vacated predicate.

**Material Uncertainty:** No evidentiary finding establishes either alleged plea defect. A decision about reviewability must not be described as adjudicating those allegations or declaring the prior judgments constitutionally flawless.
