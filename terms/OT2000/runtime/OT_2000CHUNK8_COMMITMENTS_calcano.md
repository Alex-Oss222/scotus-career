## Two supported constructions and their limits

**Retained-habeas construction.** The permanent scheme withdraws the ordinary statutory review route in this category but does not clearly withdraw the independently enacted legality writ. Section (b)'s introduction addresses review under (a)(1); subsection (b)(9) consolidates questions for that judicial-review process rather than clearly extinguishing every historically separate habeas proceeding. Subsection (c)'s express recognition of petitions for review or habeas reinforces the need to distinguish channels. The notwithstanding clause in (a)(2)(C) has substantial work in eliminating other ordinary review routes without treating every form of legality inquiry as the same proceeding. Felker, Kolster and Goncalves supply that channel-specific method. A legal rule foreclosing consideration of a waiver is reviewable legality even though the lawful ultimate decision would be discretionary.

**Strongest objection.** Section (b)(9) expressly speaks of all questions of law and fact, including constitutional and statutory interpretation, and makes judicial review available only under this section. Read together with (a)(1) and (a)(2)(C), it is a stronger consolidation command than the transitional provisions in Goncalves. Congress need not use a statutory number or a magic word to withdraw jurisdiction. The retained-habeas Justices accept that latter proposition, but find the actual textual context insufficiently clear to eliminate this distinct writ. They do not hold that a missing reference to §2241 always defeats an otherwise unmistakable repeal.

**Exclusive-permanent-channel construction.** The integrated permanent statute directs all removal-related legal review into its final-order process; (a)(2)(C) then limits the claims cognizable there for the conceded criminal category. Under this construction ordinary statutory eligibility review cannot be restored by naming it habeas. Section (c)'s reference retains work for expressly allowed habeas proceedings, including the separately specified expedited-removal procedure, without guaranteeing this broader collateral route. The prior transitional cases remain intact because they did not govern under the permanent consolidation provision.

**Strongest objection and constitutional consequence.** Denial of every forum for a substantial legal authority objection may raise a serious Suspension Clause or other constitutional question, and the petitioners have preserved constitutional claims. The three proponents do not declare this concern answered by a hypothetical future proceeding, a discretionary-relief label, or the agency's concession. Their actual AADC position distinguishes statutory construction from a limited present inquiry into whether applying the exclusion would itself be unconstitutional. They would return for that question and any properly surviving constitutional review, rather than both rejecting habeas and treating every constitutional claim as already adjudicated. This is not an operative holding that statutory eligibility review is constitutionally required or that the asserted equal-protection and due-process theories succeed.

The petitioners' third suggested route—reading the ordinary petition broadly to include all statutory eligibility claims—is not preferred by any Associate on these conceded predicates. The retained-habeas group finds it unnecessary and inconsistent with the direct bar's ordinary operation. The exclusive-channel group finds it contrary to the clear restriction unless a particular constitutional ruling requires a remedy; such a ruling cannot be assumed here.
