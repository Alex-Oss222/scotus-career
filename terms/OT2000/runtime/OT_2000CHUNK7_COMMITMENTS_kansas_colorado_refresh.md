## Entering law, separable components and competing paths

The unanimous 1995 Kansas judgment establishes compact pumping liability and reserves remedy. It rejects the requested pre-1985 laches bar because inexcusable delay was not established, while reserving categorical availability of laches and properly supported remedy equities. No finding of culpable delay can be inferred from the length of this litigation. Texas v. New Mexico enforces past compact obligations despite good-faith uncertainty, permits a suitable money remedy for a sovereign's own compact claim and recognizes that the State may receive and control that remedy even when private users experienced the shortages. It specifically treats postjudgment interest; its discussion does not already decide every prejudgment-interest issue here.

West Virginia v. United States requires consideration of the relevant governmental interests before applying the federal compensatory rule and finds interest necessary to preserve the underlying allocation. It rejects generalized hardship or a borrowed state sovereign-immunity exemption; an actual equitable bar such as laches remains distinct. Milwaukee treats ordinary interest as loss-of-use compensation, rejecting ordinary liability uncertainty or fault already reflected in principal as sufficient exceptions. Its maritime rule must be expressly extended by analogy, not silently treated as an interstate rule already decided.

**Sovereign ownership.** The principal path rejects immunity because Kansas owns and controls the compact claim and recovery. Its strongest objection is that agricultural loss measures conceal a suit for farmers' private debts. The reported ownership and control, and the compact's statewide allocation interest, answer that objection; no assignment or pass-through arrangement is established. A genuinely assigned private-debt suit would require different treatment. Economic evidence alone does not transform the plaintiff's legal right.

**Interest availability and commencement.** One path awards compensatory interest from the losses beginning in 1950, subject to an actual supported exception, because delayed compensation reduces the value of the agreed allocation. Its strongest objection is the extraordinary interval, uncertain violations and cumulative compounding burden. The competing path defers commencement to 1969 because of the Master's finding that Colorado then knew or should have known of the violation, or to the requested 1985 litigation year as a further accommodation to sovereign interests. Its strongest objection is that a compensatory rather than punitive remedy does not arise only after the debtor understands its obligation; the 1995 decision found no established inexcusable claimant delay. No exact filing-year selection is needed for the first path.

The provisional assessment of each Associate favors compensation from the supported losses rather than a notice-based cutoff. This is not a claim that Milwaukee compels that result or that the earlier laches ruling abolishes all remedial discretion. The reported additional grounds identify uncertainty, long elapsed time and aggregate amount, but no distinct finding of duplicative recovery, claimant-created loss of use or legally justified nonperformance. The narrower competing position remains legally available for historical reconciliation; the specific changed-law premise must be assessed there rather than assumed.

**Rates.** The report's year-specific individual-loss approach tracks the loss being valued. A mandatory State borrowing rate could undercompensate that economic loss even though Kansas owns the claim. Conversely, the individual rate must compensate actual loss of use rather than reward speculative investment or duplicate inflation already included elsewhere. The supported disposition overrules the categorical demand for State rates, preserving numerical implementation and nonduplication in the calculation proceeding. It invents no annual table or compounding interval.

**Crop model.** Kansas has affirmative support: accepted shortage attribution, crop acreage and prices; published crop-specific water/yield studies; and a 25-percent reduction for ordinary farming stresses. The noninvestment objection does not itself refute marginal yield where the Master identifies capital limits, credit access, debt aversion and imperfect cost information. Comparison studies involving additional capital can measure a different transaction from extra seasonal water without additional capital. Colorado's abandoned alternative is not affirmative proof for Kansas and does not shift the burden. The contrary path would recommit if those limitations still left the coefficients unsupported; on the reported supporting studies and adjustment, these specified exceptions do not establish that defect. No perfect-model requirement or permanent future accounting formula results.

### Validated supplemental facts and remaining limits

The type supplement separates three elements: direct and indirect economic losses valued in the historical dollars when incurred; an adjustment for inflation that Colorado concedes is appropriate; and additional interest intended to compensate lost investment opportunities. Thus Colorado's disputed interest position must not be described as opposition to every inflation adjustment or every increase above historical-dollar principal. The earlier file's references to a categorical interest objection concern the additional component when read through this refinement.

The reported $62,369,173 total, $9,218,305 historical-dollar principal, approximately $12 million inflation adjustment and approximately $41 million additional interest describe Kansas's pending claim. They are illustrative recitations of the Third Report, not an adjudicated award or independent proof that each item is recoverable. The new evidence supplies neither a verified numerical rate schedule nor a compounding frequency. Any accepted loss-of-use method must separate its inflation and additional-return elements so the same erosion in purchasing power is not recovered twice. The size of the claimed total alone establishes neither penalty nor full compensation.

The date supplement establishes December 16, 1985 as the filing or lodging of the initiating motion, proposed complaint and supporting brief. It does not establish the date of a later judicial grant of leave, and that different event is not needed for the refreshed preferred disposition. This resolves the previously identified source question about commencement of the initiating request, while preserving the procedural distinction. The source also lists a later First Amended Complaint; it does not prove when a particular demand for money was first made or what that pleading requested. No remedial trigger is inferred from that docket label.

### All supported commencement alternatives, kept separate

1. **Loss-date commencement, beginning in 1950.** Additional interest may compensate the lost use of recoverable economic losses from the dates they occurred, beyond the separately identified inflation correction. The strongest objection is that maintaining purchasing power already gives substantial compensation while the additional claim compounds an uncertain investment opportunity over decades. The governing response is that lost purchasing power and lost opportunity to use capital are different injuries. A supported ordinary rate can value the latter without proving that every recipient would have selected a particular profitable investment. This does not justify speculative returns, mechanically adopt the claimed $41 million, or dispense with an actual supported exception.

2. **The Master's 1969 commencement.** Colorado's actual or constructive knowledge, as found by the Master, can support a discretionary cutoff for the additional component without withholding the conceded inflation correction. Its strongest objection is that knowledge of wrongdoing is ordinarily relevant to culpability, while compensatory loss of use occurs independently of culpability. The prior compact ruling's reservation of remedy equities permits consideration of this path; its rejection of established inexcusable Kansas delay does not compel either adoption or rejection of every interest accommodation.

3. **Commencement of the initiating request on December 16, 1985.** The now-supported filing date supplies a precise litigation-notice candidate for the additional component. Its strongest ground is the practical distinction between uncertain accumulated interstate obligations and a formally asserted judicial claim. Its strongest objection is that filing does not itself create the underlying loss or the compact duty, and delay without established inexcusable conduct cannot simply be recast as fault. No automatic whole-calendar-year accrual from January 1, 1985 follows from a December filing. A court choosing the year as an accounting convention would need a reason and a method avoiding unsupported extra months.

4. **No additional lost-investment interest.** The alternative preserves the conceded inflation adjustment but denies the separate return. Its strongest basis is that equitable compensation between States can require restraint where an investment return is insufficiently supported. Its strongest objection is the ordinary compensatory distinction between maintaining principal's purchasing power and compensating the lost opportunity to use it. Unliquidated status alone does not establish that the latter loss is legally nonexistent. A defect in a particular rate or duplication can justify correcting that calculation without adopting a categorical bar.

No independently supported date for first demanding monetary relief, the later grant of leave, or a different judicial milestone is supplied as an additional present trigger. Those possibilities are not treated as proved alternatives. Ordinary postjudgment interest is distinct from the prejudgment accrual questions being decided here.

### Refined disposition and final scope

The independent judgment preferences remain unchanged after the source additions. All eight provisionally reject the categorical bar to additional lost-investment interest and favor loss-date accrual from supported losses beginning in 1950, rather than 1969, December 16, 1985, or no additional return. This assessment expressly extends the compensatory reasoning to this compact remedy; it does not claim an existing case already dictates the precise accrual result. No historical departure is inferred or assessed here.

Remand calculation must distinguish historical-dollar principal, the conceded appropriate inflation correction and any supported additional return. It must not award an inflation-adjusted principal plus a nominal interest measure duplicating the same inflation. Nothing in this refresh orders a particular subtraction formula, rate schedule, compounding frequency or total. The calculation remains limited to recoverable loss and the supported method, with actual exceptions preserved. The original principal, crop and State-rate exception dispositions remain at their stated scope.
