# TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening controlling decision identified above and any material change in the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit on the functionality issue and restore judgment for TrafFix on the asserted dual-spring trade-dress claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct ordinary implementation of that judgment. No liability for copying the functional mechanism follows from this trade-dress claim; no ruling is required on unrelated marks or an unasserted ornamental design.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: No perpetual control of a useful mechanism

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach functionality as the sufficient statutory ground. Do not decide secondary meaning or a freestanding constitutional patent-preemption theory. The temporal application of the 1999 burden amendment is unnecessary because the undisputed functional evidence is sufficient under either allocation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether evidence of useful operation, strongly confirmed by the expired patents, defeats this particular claim despite alternative designs.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing functionality prohibition and specify the patent’s evidentiary role.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An expired utility patent is strong evidence that the feature it identifies as useful is functional. A claimant may distinguish a genuinely ornamental aspect, but cannot protect the same useful feature merely by proving recognition or alternative workable designs. The claimed dual springs perform the stabilizing function identified in the patents and are not protectable trade dress.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the claimed feature’s actual function, not every visual detail of a once-patented product. It covers useful design features regardless of whether competitors could choose a more costly or covered alternative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Trademark law protects source identification without granting continued control over useful engineering. The proposed alternative-design escape would force competitors away from an available functional mechanism and make recognition a substitute for the independent statutory requirement. The record supports functionality even without deciding whether the later burden amendment governs every earlier act. Inwood and Qualitex are reconciled by retaining the independent useful-function bar; competitive necessity does not become an additional element once that bar is satisfied. The patent is strong evidentiary support, not a conclusive presumption attaching to every appearance detail. The decisive legal rule is statutory functionality, and its practical operation is demonstrated by the claimed stabilizing mechanism. That adequate ground makes a broader constitutional rule about all expired patents unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unasserted ornamental features are outside the trade-dress claim presented and are reserved on that scope ground.

### Issue 1: Function-sensitive appellate review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the allocation issue because the same historical record can produce different appellate treatment depending on the governing standard.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the district court’s practical assessment of consolidation receives deference while legal criteria remain independently reviewable.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Koon v. United States’s task-sensitive separation and distinguish Ornelas v. United States rather than weakening it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Appellate courts independently review the meaning of the related-case provisions and defer to supported, record-specific determinations of functional consolidation that depend on the procedural history and practical sentencing context. Concurrent sentencing is relevant but not conclusive. Under that allocation, the challenged determination is sustainable.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs this functional-consolidation application. It does not make Guidelines text discretionary or transfer constitutional search review to a deferential standard.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The district judge must reconstruct what separate state proceedings actually did, not formulate a general constitutional boundary. Local experience helps evaluate those procedural facts, while appellate correction remains available for mistaken legal criteria or unsupported inferences. Section 3742(e) and Koon require deference fitted to the task, rather than deference simply because punishment is involved. The appellate court still corrects a mistaken legal definition. Ornelas is distinguished by the need there for independent elaboration of a constitutional search boundary. The limited rule here leaves constitutional factfinding rights intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The full substantive definition of functional consolidation and constitutional jury allocation were not granted. Apprendi reserved general Guidelines and prior-conviction questions; the controlling Almendarez-Torres judgment did not adopt a general constitutional recidivism exception. # Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment denying enforcement under the Federal Arbitration Act because Section 1 excludes this employment contract.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the federal order compelling arbitration and enjoining the state action reversed. Adams may pursue the state proceeding subject to any independently applicable law and properly preserved defenses. This judgment decides no discrimination liability and does not declare every employment arbitration agreement unenforceable under state law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Coverage without automatic enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The question is statutory and can be decided before constitutional or discrimination merits questions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Section 1 excludes employment contracts within the Act's asserted commerce coverage, including this retail employment agreement, or only contracts of transportation workers.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt the broader employment exclusion as the better construction of the previously unresolved Section 1 question. Preserve Allied-Bruce's Section 2 holding and distinguish Gilmer's securities-registration agreement; neither decided this exclusion. No governing Supreme Court holding must be overruled.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1 excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the Act, rather than confining its residual language to transportation occupations. Adams's employment agreement is excluded, and the FAA supplies no authority to compel arbitration or enjoin his suit.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule answers the employment-contract exclusion generally. It preserves the FAA's application to otherwise covered commercial agreements and does not decide an independent state-law basis for arbitration.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive reading combines the enacted words with their contemporaneous commercial meaning: Congress singled out contracts of employment, then added any other class of workers engaged in commerce. The common category can be workers in commerce; the text does not say transportation. Ejusdem generis offers an inference, not authority to insert that narrower occupation. Reading the reach provision expansively while freezing the accompanying employment exclusion to only the occupations most clearly regulable in 1925 would detach the two parts of the same enactment. The contemporaneous explanation for adding the exclusion confirms the broader labor boundary where the competing linguistic readings leave uncertainty. Concern for genuine employee choice supports that construction but cannot itself create an exemption. I therefore adopt a statutory rule for employment contracts, rather than requiring Adams to prove that this particular arbitration procedure already denied him a remedy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent state-law enforcement, the underlying discrimination claim, and the classification of an independent contractor are outside this employee's presented FAA exclusion question.

Whether an individual patient actually consented requires patient-specific facts absent from a categorical ruling about the program.

# Gibbs v. Babbitt

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Fourth Circuit judgment affirming summary judgment for the federal officials and remand for a confined examination of the existing summary-judgment record under National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require identification of the valid interstate regime and material impairment caused by excluding the challenged private-land takings, or a concrete substantial cross-border externality and reasonable fit. If the existing record supports neither route, direct appropriate party- and application-specific declaratory or injunctive relief. Remand grants no new entitlement to manufacture missing premises, no damages, no categorical ESA invalidation, and no interim permission to kill wolves contrary to otherwise applicable law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A confined existing-record inquiry into federal power

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The record supports a live regulated-party controversy. Reach the Commerce Clause question presented and the necessary regulated-class and interstate-mechanism antecedents. An alternative constitutional power is outside this grant. Remand must be confined to relevant evidence and theories properly before the lower courts.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this challenged application to intrastate, noncommercial taking of red wolves rests on a constitutionally eligible interstate mechanism under the governing Commerce Clause framework.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Lopez, National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, and United States v. Morrison without overruling or replacing their supported externality route with a categorical economic-conduct limitation. This is an adopted application and remedial disposition, not a new Commerce-power test.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The lower courts' accumulation of farmers' economic motives, projected tourism, science, and potential trade does not by itself establish the required interstate connection. The existing record must identify a valid interstate regime materially impaired by excluding the challenged private-land takings, or a concrete substantial cross-border externality beyond reasonable state confinement or internalization, with reasonable fit. If neither route is supported, the challenged application cannot stand.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The inquiry concerns the enacted class of taking protected wildlife, not a substituted class of operating tourist attractions or trading pelts. A supported connection may reach noncommercial conduct. Any relief remains specific to the parties and challenged application rather than invalidating the ESA generally.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Actual interstate refuge visits and the program's dependence on roaming wolves warrant examination; their existence cannot be ignored merely because an individual killing is local. Conversely, ecological usefulness and a comprehensive conservation program do not prove the legally required connection. The lower analysis did not distinguish these mechanisms. A confined remand permits assessment of existing evidence under the correct rule without inventing causal facts or granting an indefinite opportunity for replacement rationales. Affirmance is lawful only upon adequate existing-record support; otherwise appropriate relief must follow. Home Builders and Morrison are reaffirmed; no categorical economic-only limitation is added. The constitutional connection is decisive, while Congress retains latitude on the supported predictive judgments within it. Agency accounts of wolf movement, program records, testimony, the history of eradication and reintroduction, and reasonable circumstantial inference may provide material support; no mandatory statistical study or proof that each animal crosses a state line is required. Remand is confined because the lower judgment used a different theory, not because government deserves repeated opportunities to supply a missing constitutional premise.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Property Clause and any takings-compensation claim are outside the question presented. Facts needed to resolve a particular future enforcement event are absent and are not found in this civil challenge.

# Texas v. Cobb

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals' judgment reversing the capital-murder conviction and remanding for a new trial, on the narrower ground that the murder interrogation concerned the same criminal episode already under prosecution and representation.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The challenged police-elicited confession may not be used in the new prosecution on the asserted ground that the murders were outside the attached counsel protection. Preserve ordinary adjudication of independent evidence and the State's ability to retry; this is neither acquittal nor a bar to investigation through lawful means.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Offense specificity with an anti-circumvention boundary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the disputed scope of the attached right. A valid Miranda waiver answers the Fifth Amendment question but does not, without more, answer the attached Sixth Amendment protection and its existing waiver rules. The State's categorical scope argument cannot avoid that distinction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether appointed counsel for the charged burglary protects the accused against uncounseled police interrogation about murders constituting an inseparable part of that same known episode, although the statutory elements differ.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Moulton's protection against circumvention to deliberately elicited admissions about an inseparable part of the represented episode. Preserve McNeil's offense specificity for unrelated investigations, but reject a new rule importing Blockburger as the exclusive counsel boundary. This same-episode protection is a proposed doctrinal development, not a claim that Moulton already decided it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Once prosecution and representation have begun, the counsel protection extends to police questioning about additional offenses so integrated with the charged occurrence that interrogation necessarily reconstructs that same represented event. Different statutory elements alone do not remove that protection. The murders during this burglary satisfy the rule, so the confession is not admissible on the State's different-offense theory.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the same known criminal episode and preserves investigation of genuinely separate offenses. Shared participants, a similar method, or a general criminal relationship alone is insufficient. Ordinary attachment and Sixth Amendment waiver rules remain applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Sixth Amendment protects assistance in the criminal prosecution, not merely advice about the first label chosen by the prosecutor. Its defense function carries decisive weight in defining the doctrinal term offense. Blockburger serves the different double-jeopardy inquiry into legislatively distinct offenses; transferring that test would let sequencing of closely integrated charges dismantle representation. McNeil involved a distinct investigation and does not compel that result. Moulton establishes why the State cannot bypass counsel by describing the interrogation as another investigation, though extending protection to the integrated additional charge must be acknowledged. Here the inquiry into the deaths necessarily returned to what happened during the charged burglary, and the earlier requests for counsel's permission confirm the relationship was apparent. The rule is bounded by the represented occurrence rather than by sympathy for the accused or every factual association between crimes. I preserve Michigan v. Jackson's applicable protection instead of treating Miranda compliance as its repeal.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unrelated crimes, a defendant-initiated encounter, and a genuinely valid Sixth Amendment waiver present different circumstances. No general prohibition on speaking to represented persons as witnesses is adopted.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved removal claim. Resolve the examination claim only to the extent the lower record establishes that it was properly presented against the city.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for factual adjudication of emergency, scope, duration, policy, and causation. Require the lower court to address the examination theory's preservation and municipal connection expressly. No damages award, finding of every officer's knowledge, or automatic attribution of the entire separation follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Emergency removal

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The children's seizure and the parents' family interference are distinct interests; the claim against the city requires both an actionable deprivation and institutional causation. Decide the legal emergency standard needed to assess the supported facts.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** What permits removal without a judge, and does this record justify final judgment for the municipality?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established family and municipal principles and adopt a specific child-protection emergency rule. A broader family-court framework would require a separate doctrinal extension.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Warrantless removal requires specific, articulable grounds for an imminent threat of serious bodily harm and circumstances leaving insufficient time to obtain judicial authorization. Its scope and duration must be reasonably confined to averting that threat. A municipality's responsibility remains separately dependent on its actionable policy or omission and causation.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule assesses the contemporaneous information, available time, and protective alternatives. It neither requires certainty of abuse nor treats later vindication as conclusive evidence of an earlier violation.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Protective authority must be usable in a real emergency but cannot rest on a bare accusation insulated from reliability review. The report's disclosed source problems, absence of an actual order, and alleged practice of accepting unverified orders create material factual issues. The reported order cannot be manufactured into judicial authority. Each later continuation needs its own basis; the juvenile court's action and its evidentiary source matter to causation. The emergency exception must be established from the facts available to officials, including source reliability, contemporaneous reports, timing, and practical protective alternatives. A mental-health diagnosis alone does not discredit every witness; the particular reliability problems and what officials actually knew matter. The Fourth Amendment's reasonableness rule governs the children's seizure, while the Fourteenth Amendment protects the family relationship. Both support the immediate-danger boundary here, with the absence of time for judicial authorization doing decisive work; neither supplies municipal causation by itself.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final findings about knowledge, genuine urgency, and damages are reserved for missing or disputed record findings. Termination-of-parental-rights rules and appointed-counsel requirements are outside the questions granted.

### Issue 2: Evidentiary examinations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide preservation and attribution before extending relief against this city. If preserved, the examination is an independent intrusion requiring its own justification.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does protective custody alone authorize invasive evidentiary examinations without consent or a judge?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt this bodily-integrity application, without converting an unpreserved theory into an adjudicated claim.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent genuine urgent medical need or imminent loss of evidence, such examinations require valid parental consent or prior judicial authorization through an appropriate process. A justified removal alone supplies neither. Reasonable protective arrangements for participation may reflect a genuine examination need, not a categorical exclusion of parents.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs investigative bodily examinations of these children, with the child's own rights independently protected.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A three-day interval and the described evidentiary purpose require an actual urgency explanation. The city must be connected to the examination through a preserved institutional theory; moral responsibility for the overall episode cannot replace that proof. The bodily intrusion requires separate consent, judicial authority, or established urgency. Extending the ordinary warrant-and-exigency distinction to this examination protects the children as persons, not merely as objects of parental authority. This rule follows from the purpose and intrusiveness of the examination and does not create a universal strict-scrutiny test for family administration. A case-specific objection cannot be dismissed solely because the initial custody was lawful.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the city-specific examination theory was preserved and supported is reserved on the stated preservation and missing-record grounds.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preauthorization question because the bodily intervention and loss of effective defense assistance cannot be reliably reconstructed after treatment. An asserted additional safety purpose does not remove the trial-effects question when restoration is an actual purpose; no immediate emergency is established here.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the court may authorize this treatment without first considering how the very means proposed to enable trial could impair the defendant's trial participation and defense.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Riggins's medical-appropriateness, necessity, and alternatives requirements and extend its protection of effective defense to the timing of a pretrial restoration order. Harper's convicted-prisoner safety setting does not authorize deferring this distinct inquiry. The preauthorization requirement is the proposed extension.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Yes. Where competency restoration is an actual purpose, a court must assess reasonably foreseeable effects on consultation, comprehension, testimony, demeanor, and defense before authorizing unwanted antipsychotic treatment. A government safety rationale does not excuse that review in a mixed-purpose order. The government must establish through the proposed regimen, alternatives, and safeguards that the authorization is compatible with an effective defense; uncertainty cannot be resolved merely by promising later observation or explanatory testimony. This order improperly deferred that assessment and cannot stand.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches pretrial restoration orders, including mixed asserted purposes, and governs the actual proposed medication rather than all psychiatric treatment categorically. Incompetence to stand trial does not itself establish incapacity to refuse treatment. Preauthorization findings do not eliminate continuing review before trial if actual effects differ from the supported prediction.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bodily refusal and a functioning defense protect different interests, so a sufficient medical justification cannot extinguish the independent trial protection. Riggins supplies the decisive principle that medical appropriateness alone is insufficient and that trial effects may be impossible to reconstruct. The extension to preauthorization follows from when the threatened harm occurs: impaired consultation and preparation can precede the courtroom. Expert testimony, treatment observations, records, and supported clinical inference can establish likely effects without perfect prediction. The medical testimony about isolation and observation also prevents accepting a safety label as a reason to bypass examination of this actual order. The judge must consider the regimen, alternatives, and proposed safeguards before treatment, not presume recordings or explanatory testimony can restore an impaired defense afterward. The combination gives the Sixth Amendment's practical protection of the defense decisive weight on timing, while the existing bodily-liberty requirements continue to govern medical justification. The current record warrants setting aside the order and making the required findings, not authorizing the treatment first and litigating its consequences later.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The grant concerns preauthorization review of trial effects. Whether prosecution alone can ever justify involuntary treatment, without another treatment interest, is a distinct substantive-authorization question outside that grant; this judgment does not answer it. A genuine immediate emergency and medication solely to enable execution are likewise outside this nonemergency pretrial order. Specific regimen findings require evidence on remand under the stated rule.

# Easley v. Cromartie / Smallwood v. Cromartie

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment in both dockets and remand to apply the independent-injury rule of Shaw v. Reno, Johnson v. Miller, and Hunt v. Cromartie, with directions that racial predominance alone cannot sustain invalidation.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside relief resting solely on predominance. The district court must identify and decide any actually preserved independent injury using existing evidence and proper burdens; absent one, enter judgment for defendants. No replacement plan, quota, automatic discovery reopening or election delay is ordered by this proposal.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The necessary injury before fact review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the antecedent legal sufficiency of the constitutional theory. Without an independent injury, adjudicating which motive predominated cannot lawfully sustain the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this judgment establishes an injury recognized by entering law, and whether the two appellant groups are entitled to have predominance-only relief set aside.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law, including the mandate in Hunt v. Cromartie. Do not adopt replacing it with historical strict scrutiny or treating every race-conscious district as a separate classification injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A territorial district cannot be invalidated merely because racial considerations predominated. The reviewing court must identify a preserved independent exclusion, dilution, participation or civic-membership injury and apply the corresponding rule. The judgment here requires vacatur because its stated constitutional premise is insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This rule governs both consolidated appeals and analogous territorial districting challenges. It leaves actual racial disadvantage reviewable and does not make partisan purpose a defense to an otherwise proved wrong.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** District lines necessarily collect people with shared political and demographic characteristics. Neither an unusual shape nor racial-political correlation supplies the missing legal injury. The remand in Hunt v. Cromartie required that distinction. Existing findings may matter to an actual claim, but a finding reached under an insufficient legal theory cannot itself cure the theory. Remand preserves adjudication of genuine claims without directing a trial about motive for its own sake. Shaw, Johnson, and Hunt are reaffirmed rather than replaced with the historical predominance rule. Their injury requirement is decisive; Rule 52 deference cannot save findings directed only to a legally insufficient theory. If a preserved cognizable injury exists, the court must consider the full proof, including testimony, drafting communications, maps, electoral experience, and circumstantial evidence. A statistical model or a particular alternative map is not a universal entry requirement. The limited remand follows the absence of a necessary finding, not reluctance to protect a demonstrated participation injury.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether a separately pleaded and preserved dilution or unequal-participation injury is proved requires record findings not established by the reported record; any unpleaded theory lies outside the preserved case.

# Shaw v. Murphy

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the enhanced-protection rule and remand for ordinary Turner v. Safley review of any preserved challenge to this application.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Murphy receives no automatic reversal of discipline or injunction from the legal-assistance label. The lower court must decide any preserved ordinary speech or retaliation claim on the actual record. Relief, if warranted, must correct the proved violation and satisfy applicable prospective-relief requirements; no new access claimant or lost lawsuit is assumed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: No categorical adviser enhancement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the governing standard because the appellate judgment gave legal assistance additional categorical protection. Do not decide the remaining factual application merely from the letter's subject.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether assistance content changes the First Amendment standard and what process remains for the actual discipline challenge.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Turner v. Safley and Lewis v. Casey; adopt the unresolved holding that advice content alone supplies no enhanced constitutional tier. Preserve the separate protections those precedents actually adopted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An inmate's letter does not receive a heightened speech standard merely because it offers legal assistance. Its regulation remains subject to actual reasonable-relationship review, while demonstrated obstruction of meaningful court access or proven retaliation is assessed under those distinct rules.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers mixed advice and criticism in inmate communications. It does not excuse unsupported restrictions, create immunity for fabricated charges or foreclose assistance necessary for an inmate who otherwise cannot obtain meaningful access.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Advice labels are easily attached to a broad range of prison communications and do not themselves determine the security effects. The real inquiry is how this rule and application relate to legitimate prison operation, with available communication alternatives and practical burdens considered. Lewis v. Casey protects effective arrangements rather than a preferred adviser, but its preservation of independent communication rights prevents officials from converting that limitation into plenary censorship. An access violation must be proved through its own actual-obstruction requirement. I retain Turner's contextual standard, including its inquiry into the connection to a legitimate objective, realistic alternatives, effects on others, and readily available alternatives to the restriction. No added tier follows from a legal-advice label, but the content and setting remain evidence of what the restriction actually accomplishes. Testimony, the letter, disciplinary records, and supported inferences must be considered; no numerical security study is required of either side. Criticism cannot become a security threat merely because it criticizes an officer. Lewis limits an access claim without extinguishing Murphy's independent speech claim, which must receive meaningful review on remand.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ordinary as-applied justification and any properly preserved retaliatory-fabrication claim require findings not established here; a separate recipient's lost legal claim is outside this presented theory.

# Clark County School District v. Breeden

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and restore summary judgment for the District on the retaliation theories supported only by this exchange and chronology.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Breeden receives no damages or trial on those insufficient theories. The judgment does not impose a new exhaustion requirement or foreclose another properly preserved theory supported by different evidence; none is invented for remand.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Insufficient opposition and causation evidence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach evidentiary sufficiency, assuming reasonable good-faith opposition can be protected before an underlying violation is judicially established. A broader restriction on opposition protection is unnecessary. The general outer limits of reasonable-belief opposition protection therefore remain unnecessary to the judgment and are not decided.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the contextual exchange and the actual transfer chronology permit a reasonable jury to find unlawful retaliation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current hostile-environment and ordinary causation principles; make the limited new application without changing the rule to require a completed harassment violation before every complaint.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An isolated report-review exchange of this character does not support an objectively reasonable belief that Title VII was violated. Independently, continuation of a transfer already contemplated before notice of suit, without evidence of retaliatory alteration and with a twenty-month gap from the earlier charge, does not establish retaliation by timing alone.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers these evidentiary premises, while permitting good-faith complaints about developing unlawful practices and supported claims that an initially legitimate action was later implemented for retaliatory reasons.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The report's explicit language supplies context but does not show that the employer adopted a continuing abusive condition. Subjective offense is real yet is not the legal threshold. Likewise, an employer does not acquire immunity by claiming a preexisting plan, but established prior contemplation deprives later notice of its assumed causal significance absent additional evidence. Those distinct deficiencies cannot be repaired by merging complaint, offense and timing into one inference. Harris's contextual standard is preserved, and I do not turn its severe-or-pervasive merits rule into a requirement that a worker wait for completed harassment before raising a reasonable concern. The deficiency here concerns this exchange and this causal record. The employee may rely on testimony, admissions, explanations, and circumstantial chronology without statistical proof. A long interval is not an absolute bar, and an employer's preexisting plan is not conclusive if evidence shows retaliatory alteration. Summary judgment follows only because those possibilities lack support here, not because opposition or participation loses protection whenever the underlying claim fails.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different retaliatory implementation would require facts absent from this record; no such finding is supplied by the sequence of dates alone.

# Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 23, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Incorporate any intervening controlling decision and resolve material record disputes before adjudication.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse insofar as the judgment approves withholding the same nonmedical benefit from otherwise comparable sincere nonreligious conscience objectors solely because their objection is nonreligious. Affirm rejection of the categorical religious-delegation claim on the established governmental-review structure. Remand for relief addressing the discriminatory eligibility boundary.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a declaration identifying the unjustified religious distinction and a severability determination governing prospective relief. The court must determine whether lawful equal eligibility can operate within the enacted program and appropriations or whether the discriminatory benefit provision must be withdrawn; it may not invent an appropriation or rewrite the statute. Tailor any transition to the established reliance and care needs of current patients. Order no repayment of lawful past benefits and no injunction against separable secular care merely because a provider is religious.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Limited accommodation of access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the expenditure challenge on the litigated taxpayer posture and the statutory record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether religious accommodation justifies excluding otherwise comparable serious secular conscience objections from the same condition-equivalent nonmedical benefit.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Agostini and Mitchell to permit identifiable secular care, while adopting an equal-consideration rule for the expressly presented religious eligibility distinction. The funding precedents have not already decided this conscience boundary; the rule is a proposed extension of religious neutrality and equal civic treatment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Government may accommodate a sincere objection to medical treatment by funding the otherwise covered nonmedical care for which a patient's condition qualifies. It may not exclude an otherwise comparable serious secular moral or ethical objection solely because the objection is nonreligious, absent a difference relevant to the accommodation's purpose or administration. The challenged criterion makes that unsupported distinction and cannot stand at that scope. Payment for devotional services remains forbidden.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule requires equal consideration of materially comparable conscience claims, not every preference against treatment. Sincerity, an actual conflict, independent medical-condition eligibility, equivalent covered services, and public accountability remain required. It does not authorize prayer reimbursement or a general exemption from medical laws.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The secular character of the purchased care answers the funding objection but does not answer who may receive it. Religious neutrality carries decisive weight on that second question. A sincere secular conscience conflict can prevent acceptance of the same medical treatment without changing the patient's need, the physical service purchased, or the burden of oversight. Calling one refusal religious does not supply the missing functional difference. Accommodation of a distinct religious burden can remain distinct when that difference actually explains the program's scope; here the stated benefit removes a treatment obstacle that the comparable conscience claimant also faces. I distinguish genuine accommodation from religious preference without reviving separate Lemon prongs or inferring diversion from institutional affiliation. The statute's exclusion is the defect; no finding that public funds bought prayer is necessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The lawful severability choice and transition require statutory and remedial consideration on remand. Individual sincerity, comparability, and qualification disputes need their own records; the present ruling establishes no entitlement for every person who dislikes medical care.

### Issue 2: Independent coverage administration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach because the asserted delegation is an independent challenge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether initial institutional recommendations coupled with meaningful nonmedical governmental review confer religious control over public entitlement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the prohibition on religious allocation of civic power; adopt the concrete administrative distinction necessary here.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A provider's initial recommendation does not transfer governmental authority when public officials independently decide statutory eligibility using adequate objective information and may deny payment for failure to supply it. An exemption from a particular examination does not negate review when other sufficient evidence remains available.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Applies to coverage review under this program and rejects both automatic invalidity and nominal-review immunity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The institution does not possess a final veto or conclusive authority. The Secretary can demand information and deny payment. Plaintiffs have not established that the legal examination limit renders every determination unreviewable. If the evidence is inadequate, the proper consequence is denial of the claim, not surrender of the governmental standard. Kiryas Joel and Larkin prohibit religious control of civic authority; they do not bar a private provider from submitting evidence for public officials to assess. The retained governmental power is decisive only if review can operate. Nonmedical observations, records, testimony, and other reliable information may establish eligibility; the absence of a compelled examination does not by itself eliminate evidence. A particular determination unsupported by adequate information must be denied or corrected under the governing program, rather than insulated by the institution's religious judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual interference with a specific review is outside the established record.
