# Duncan v. Walker

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** On June 18, 2001, the historical Court reversed and remanded, 7-2 in the judgment.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Justice Souter also concurred separately. Justice Stevens concurred in part and in the judgment, joined by Justice Souter. Justice Breyer dissented, joined by Justice Ginsburg. The seven votes for the judgment must not be treated as seven full joins in the Court's opinion.

**Historical Holding:** A federal habeas petition is not an application for state postconviction or other collateral review under § 2244(d)(2); its pendency therefore does not trigger that statutory exclusion. Separate opinions discussed stays and equitable tolling as possible responses to other circumstances. Those discussions were not a majority holding establishing an automatic alternative exception.

**Historical Authority:** Quarantined historical Supreme Court comparator only. It does not determine the governing statutory interpretation or Stone's proposed disposition.

**Historical Sources:** [Duncan v. Walker](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-167.pdf).

# Idaho v. United States

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** On June 18, 2001, the historical Court affirmed, 5-4.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter wrote for Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Chief Justice Rehnquist dissented, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

**Historical Holding:** The executive reservation included the submerged lands and Congress intended to defeat Idaho's equal-footing title to the beds within the remaining reservation. The Court affirmed federal trust title, relying on the historical reservation and congressional course of dealing. The dissent considered the prestatehood congressional evidence insufficient to overcome the state-title presumption.

**Historical Authority:** Quarantined historical Supreme Court comparator only. It neither establishes the operative ownership rule nor transforms the earlier immunity case into a title adjudication.

**Historical Sources:** [Idaho v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-262.pdf).

# K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified. The Ninth Circuit reversed the challenged judgment on November 15, 1999, rejecting the constitutional challenge as applied by a two-judge majority.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Senior District Judge Schwarzer, sitting by designation, wrote the majority opinion, joined by Judge Hawkins. Judge Kleinfeld dissented from approval of the religious classification.

**Historical Holding:** The majority emphasized that adequate special-education services and safe access were available at the alternative secular location. **Historical Authority:** This is the historical lower-court anchor. Its as-applied conclusion does not displace the governing rules of Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, Agostini v. Felton, or Mitchell v. Helms.

# Adair v. Sherman

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator applies. The lower-court historical anchor is the Seventh Circuit's affirmance on August 25, 2000. The panel consisted of Judges Bauer, Easterbrook, and Ripple, with no separate opinion identified in the published decision.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Ripple wrote the published panel opinion. It first rejected a procedural objection based on Adair's Chapter 13 status, then affirmed dismissal through issue preclusion, distinguishing a challenge to collection methods from an attack on the underlying allowed debt valuation.

**Historical Holding:** The court treated Adair's notice and opportunity to object before confirmation, coupled with his failure to do so, as precluding a later challenge to the collateral valuation through his FDCPA theory. It limited its account to claims filed sufficiently before confirmation to permit objection and expressly left unaddressed the broader question whether an FDCPA action may ever rest on a bankruptcy filing.

**Historical Authority:** The decision is the historical lower-court disposition and judgment under review. It is not a Supreme Court merits holding or approval of the proposed disposition in Section II.

**Historical Sources:** [Adair v. Sherman](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/230/230.F3d.890.99-1736.html); [Adair v. * **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded, with different majorities for the separate regulatory questions. The cigarette-preemption and geographic-advertising conclusions had five votes; the point-of-sale and sales-practice analysis had six.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O'Connor wrote. Parts I, II-C, and II-D were unanimous. Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas joined Parts III-A, III-C, and III-D. # Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5-4.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Breyer wrote for Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg. Scalia dissented with Thomas. Kennedy dissented with Rehnquist, with Scalia and Thomas joining Part I.

* **Historical Holding:** The statute implicitly limits detention to the period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal, with habeas review. The Court adopted six months as presumptively reasonable; thereafter, a detainee's good reason to believe removal is not significantly likely in the reasonably foreseeable future requires a government rebuttal. The Fifth Circuit demanded too much by effectively requiring impossibility; the Ninth Circuit needed to consider actual prospects beyond the lack of a formal agreement.

* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only. The historical six-month presumption does not govern this case or form part of Stone's proposed rule. [Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-678.pdf).

* **Historical Sources:** [Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-678.pdf).
