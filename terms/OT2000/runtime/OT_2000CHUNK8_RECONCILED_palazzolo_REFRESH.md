Dodd, November 19, 1998, is a genuine changed legal premise in this timeline. All eight Associates joined Parts II–IV; Stevens wrote them. ### Stevens: finality and injury are different components

The refreshed provisional model would affirm on finality. That unconditional finality departure is not sustained. Stevens historically joined Part II-A despite the same State argument and additional-upland dispute. His prior Lucas reservations and Suitum join permit the provisional alternative, but they do not identify a materially different premise from the one on which he actually accepted finality here. Dodd supplies no new finality rule. After this source-specific comparison, his reconciled position supports reaching the existing challenge to the settled ordinary wetlands restriction. It does not adopt an agreed use/value ceiling, a universal prohibition on every access road or a wetlands-only parcel.

That correction does not turn Stevens into a supporter of the present merits remand. His historical opinion, 533 U.S. at 638–645, separately distinguishes the later owner's right to challenge a restriction from compensation for an injury suffered by a prior owner. Danforth and Dow supply the predivergence owner-at-taking distinction. His reading of this particular compensation theory is that the fixed restriction itself removed the claimed filling right, if such a right existed and if a taking occurred; a later decision applying that unchanged restriction does not by itself transfer the same interest again. Ripeness for judicial review and the time of the asserted injury are not necessarily the same event. He reads the claim as seeking compensation for that already imposed burden, not as an established materially new deprivation first imposed upon this owner in 1986.

Stevens would therefore affirm the compensation-denying judgment on the claimant's own-injury ground, while supporting finality of the existing fixed-restriction challenge. This is his legal characterization of the presented claim and the significance of the regulatory acts, not a neutral fact or a Court finding that a taking actually occurred at enactment. He does not infer an assignment, find a completed taking or determine compensation. Petitioner's assertion of a 1986 injury was known historically and does not itself answer his objection. A genuinely new or unforeseeable post-acquisition application would present a different injury theory requiring its own supported scope and finality; that conditional possibility is not an unconditional vote against finality of this claim.

Dodd does require a limit on Stevens's alternative historical rationale. He cannot say that every pre-existing regulation necessarily reduced the acquired interest to a hearing opportunity or that acquisition notice alone defeats compensation. His own Dodd opinion rejects that shortcut. He agrees that a claimant's own later injury may not be rejected merely because acquisition followed enactment, while continuing to deny that title alone transfers a predecessor's completed-injury claim. Dodd's fact-specific dwelling condition and independent contextual adjudication did not decide that latter question.

**Historical departure:** Stevens does not carry forward the alternative suggestion that the mere existence of the restriction before acquisition necessarily confines the acquired property to a discretionary hearing right and ends the compensation inquiry. His own intervening Dodd Part III requires the combined inquiry and rejects acquisition alone as dispositive. This changed legal premise limits that rationale; it does not displace his separate owner-at-injury objection or supply a vote for the current merits remand.

### Scalia: the precise regulatory-context accommodation

Scalia's historical concurrence, 533 U.S. at 636–637, said that the fact a restriction existed when title was acquired should have no bearing, outside genuine background property or nuisance principles. His intervening Dodd Part III join supports a more particular common formulation: the actual regulatory conditions, assurances or lack of assurances and their effect on an objectively reasonable expectation may be considered with economic impact and governmental character. Mere notice neither extinguishes the claim nor independently resolves expectations. This supports his refreshed shared join without adopting an unlimited notice penalty, price-paid cap or presumption against every later purchaser.

**Historical departure:** Scalia joins the bounded contextual treatment of actual pre-existing regulatory conditions because his own intervening Dodd Part III join treated the existing necessary-and-accessory dwelling condition and nonassuring County communications as relevant to reasonable expectations in the combined inquiry. That concrete prior commitment supports departure from his historical concurrence's broader no-bearing formulation. It does not authorize making acquisition alone dispositive or treating enactment as background title law.

O'Connor's historically stated nonexclusive use of regulatory context is compatible with Dodd and the refreshed common formulation. Kennedy's expectations-sensitive Lucas concurrence and Dodd join likewise support it. Thomas's Dodd join supplies direct support. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer preserve their threshold objections and the conditional governing merits rules; conditional agreement does not turn them into supporters of immediate merits remand. No changed finality premise justifies replacing those three historical threshold positions.

## 3. Reconciled component rules and limits

### F — Finality

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support sufficient finality for the existing challenge to the ordinary private wetlands-filling restriction. The governing standard, actual denials and the State's limited wetlands position establish that restriction sufficiently to permit review without another version of the rejected private filling proposal. This conclusion does not fix the maximum development elsewhere on the property. Actual remaining upland use and value remain relevant, and genuinely unsettled application of the challenged restriction is not declared settled by fiction.

The rationale distinguishes the final regulatory refusal under challenge from every fact needed to measure its ultimate burden. Suitum requires a definitive agency use determination and rejects a further act incapable of changing it; Lucas preserves ordinary finality while allowing review of an established prohibition. Applying that distinction here does not make every two permit denials sufficient, require maximization of all other uses as a universal prerequisite, or permit strategic isolation of an unpresented parcel.

Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm on finality. They view the additional upland sites and access possibilities as unresolved questions of actual permissible development of the same parcel, not merely price or off-parcel compensation. The case's whole-parcel premise and the record's litigation scope make that uncertainty material to them. They demand neither endless resubmission of a rejected design nor proof of a precise maximum valuation. Their objection survives because no settled answer to those materially different on-parcel possibilities is established.

### A — Acquisition notice

Acquisition after enactment alone does not bar the claimant's own takings challenge or convert the restriction into an antecedent property limitation. Actual background law and the regulatory context retain their proper work. Dodd supplies direct prior support from all eight Associates. Stevens joins that bounded proposition while rejecting its use as an answer to whose injury is being compensated. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer state express conditional agreement if the Court reaches the question.

The common rule does not transfer an accrued claim, establish injury at denial, extinguish genuine background title limitations or make every expectation reasonable. A restriction does not become such a background limitation for a new owner merely because title passed after enactment.

### L — Complete deprivation and parcel

Meaningful residential upland use defeats complete deprivation on the whole-parcel premise presented. At least one available home and supported substantial retained value suffice for that conclusion; a maximum is unnecessary. Lucas and Dodd Part II preserve contextual partial-taking analysis. Severe percentage diminution is not complete loss, and the asserted $3,150,000 subdivision value is not an award or proved before-value. The wetlands-only denominator was not presented below or in the petition and is not adjudicated. No universal parcel rule, minimum percentage or right to a permit follows.

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support this disposition on reaching the merits. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer expressly agree with the proposition conditionally while maintaining their finality ground.

### T — Injury and ownership of a compensation claim

The acquisition-notice holding does not itself establish the taking date, a prior completed injury, an assignment or the claimant entitled to a prior owner's compensation. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not treat this record as establishing a dispositive accrued-claim defect; they preserve actual timing, injury, title and claim-ownership questions for their necessary application in the existing case. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conditionally preserve those distinctions without reaching them as a basis for the present judgment.

Stevens differs. As stated above, he interprets this compensation claim as resting on a fixed injury, if any, imposed before the present owner acquired title, and would affirm for want of this claimant's own compensable injury. His judgment cannot be counted with the four votes to remand merely because all reject a bare notice rule. His separate position requires substantive explanation of this difference; it is not described as a finality dissent.

### P — Existing-case remand and governing contextual inquiry

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support retaining rejection of the total-loss theory, rejecting the categorical acquisition ground and returning the existing compensation case for proceedings under the correct rules. Their common instruction requires the combined Penn Central inquiry where the existing claim calls for it. Actual economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and governmental character must be considered together. Existing regulatory conditions may bear on expectations; acquisition alone, remaining value alone and public purpose alone are not dispositive. No compensation conclusion follows.

The lower decisions addressed Penn Central, and the acquisition-only premise cannot remain the answer to the federal question they passed upon. The remand concerns that existing case; it does not promise a new trial, revive abandoned factual theories or certify that every later argument was preserved. Ordinary rules governing actual claim presentation, sufficiency, independent lawful grounds and properly preserved defenses retain their operation. The trial nuisance contention, not adopted by the state appellate court according to the supplied account, is not converted into a new final appellate holding or rejected by assumption.

The lower courts must perform any remaining lawful work under these holdings, including actual uses, value, relevant expectations, title limitations, injury and remedy where material. They may not substitute the same categorical acquisition or retained-value shortcut for that work. The opinion must state which established grounds it rejects and which ordinary application or preserved independent issue remains; it must not portray the governing partial-taking standard itself as unresolved.

Stevens opposes this present merits remand on his distinct T ground. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer oppose reaching it on F, while conditionally agreeing that a properly reached existing partial-taking claim receives the combined inquiry and ordinary preservation limits. Those conditional views do not create a remand coalition.
