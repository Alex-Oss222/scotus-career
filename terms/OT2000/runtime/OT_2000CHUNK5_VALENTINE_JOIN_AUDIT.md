## Decisional conclusion

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer each join the proposed limited legal proposition: reliable information of possession must be connected to wrongdoing, and a bare state evidentiary presumption of nonlicensure does not itself supply a particularized factual basis for a seizure. Each retains vacatur and the existing-record remand. This affirmative join resolves the previously reserved bare-presumption question; it is not inferred from their judgment votes.

The join does not decide the Government's complete permitting argument. Actual licensing circumstances, the supported content of the report and other facts known before the first seizure remain available under the ordinary totality standard. A court must evaluate grounded inferences; no officer must invariably check a licensing database, observe a completed offense or acquire a separately prescribed third fact. Nor does the rule establish that Valentine had a permit, that permits were common or rare, that the officers knew any particular licensing fact, or that every possible basis for admission fails.

The source describes strict permit requirements and investigation as part of the Government's separate argument, then expressly declines to decide that argument. Those features must not disappear from the remand. Their actual operation and bearing, to the extent properly supported and available in this record, remain for lower-court evaluation. A legal allocation of evidentiary responsibility cannot substitute for that evaluation. This audit does not independently construe every exception to the New Jersey provisions or decide their validity.

## Why the new proposition does real legal work

The strongest contrary argument is that the legislature's permit requirement and presumption, reinforced by restrictive licensing administration, make reported possession sufficiently indicative of illegality under the modest reasonable-suspicion threshold. The four do not answer by demanding proof of an offense or excluding innocent circumstances. They distinguish the legal presumption from the actual circumstances that might support its investigative inference. The presumption's force as an evidentiary burden rule does not itself establish facts about this person or supply the officers with unreported licensing knowledge. Actual regulatory circumstances may be relevant, but their force must be assessed rather than assumed from the existence of the presumption.

This is a direct legal limit on a preserved ground for admission. The complete permitting theory remains for ordinary application because its additional factual predicates and legal operation were not resolved below and are not established in the supplied record. The Court need not leave the bare-presumption shortcut undecided merely because a materially fuller theory remains available. Conversely, the legal limit cannot be enlarged into a decision that the fuller theory necessarily fails.

J. L. supplies the distinction between credited information and justification, not an already decided licensing rule. Wardlow supplies particularized totality, not a categorical third-factor demand. Ornelas supplies independent legal review with protection for actual historical findings, not permission to create an evidentiary conflict. The arms authorities separate coverage from regulatory validity and expressly leave public-carry licensing questions open; they supply no holding that this permit scheme is unconstitutional. The new proposition follows from the four Justices' Fourth Amendment grounds rather than an inferred arms-law invalidation.

Genuine yielding can be brief; later flight does not erase it. An inquiry about whom the command concerns or momentary hesitation need not amount to yielding.
