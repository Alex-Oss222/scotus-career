## Controlling rule candidates and actual remedies

**D1, supported by six Associates:** Section 2244(d)(2)'s exclusion covers properly filed state postconviction or other state collateral review concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. A federal habeas application's pendency is not excluded by that subsection, including when it was timely filed and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. State modifies the coordinated category; the broader collateral wording accommodates other state procedures and custody judgments. The enacted distinctions between State and Federal proceedings elsewhere, together with the sovereign modifier's operative work, support that construction. The decision does not comprehensively classify every state procedure or filing defect.

The contrary reading correctly recognizes that federal habeas is collateral review in ordinary usage and that a delayed exhaustion dismissal can consume the filing period. The six do not treat ordinary category membership as answering the modifier's scope or those consequences as erasing State. Their statutory conclusion does not depend on a promise that equity will save every prisoner. A narrow text-and-structure opinion can resolve the question without declaring all federal-pendency relief contrary to AEDPA's purposes.

**D2, supported by Ginsburg and Breyer:** Other collateral review is an independently broader category that includes a properly filed federal collateral application concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. The competing grammar and statutory-neighbor arguments do not uniquely resolve the text; ordinary exhaustion administration should not randomly extinguish an otherwise timely first opportunity merely through the duration of the federal court's nonmerits consideration. The rule excludes only qualifying actual pendency, leaves proper filing and pertinent-judgment-or-claim requirements intact, and supplies no writ, exhaustion ruling, or immunity from actual remaining defenses. Under this construction, affirm reinstatement and remand for the petition's remaining requirements.

**D3, no permanent-first-filing rule:** All eight reject the proposition that one timely initial application permanently satisfies the limitation for every distinct later application. D2 is an exclusion of qualifying pendency, not permanent satisfaction. Lindh concerns the identified pre-enactment application; Slack's non-successive classification does not reset a limitation period; Artuz's filing/relief distinction does not decide the sovereign category.

**D4, preserved alternative and procedural limits:** All eight preserve ordinary consideration of the equitable contention actually raised below, conditionally for D2 supporters. Availability, its governing conditions, and Walker's entitlement remain distinct. Approximately nine months remained after the first dismissal, the later claims differed, and Walker did not return to state court. Those supplied facts defeat a claim that judicial delay necessarily caused his late refiling, but do not adjudicate every equitable basis. No universal compulsory warning, election, prompt-dismissal deadline, or stay regime is adopted. Section 2254(b)(2) permits merits denial of unexhausted claims; it does not require it. There is no current stay request or still-pending first application for this Court to act upon.

## Changed law and the two supported paths

**F1, all eight:** Simulated Agostini modifies Lemon into a single inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Purpose, effects, and institutional relationships are evidence; independent purpose, endorsement, and entanglement prongs do not survive. The Court must identify the governmental act and ask whether it performs a prohibited religious function or fits a preserved permissible relationship. The required teacher message is the board's speech, but attribution alone does not establish a violation. Academic religious study, private expression, and genuine nondevotional conscience protection remain lawful. Once a prohibited function is shown, successful persuasion or a completed penalty is unnecessary.

This is a concrete changed legal premise relative to the lower-court comparator. The panel's independent effect/endorsement reasoning cannot be adopted as governing law simply because five Associates reach the same injunction result. Conversely, the changed method does not itself require reversal: official curricular protection of a favored religious account may satisfy the governing prohibition on public religious activity. Epperson and Edwards retain their curriculum boundaries within the current method; they neither immunize curricular choices nor forbid every biblical mention.

**F2, five Associates:** The mandatory whole statement, its selective placement immediately before evolution instruction, and the supported adoption context establish an official function of protecting the biblical creation account from the implications of public scientific instruction. The maintained biblical clause after the creation-science controversy is evidence of that function together with the operative text, not a dispositive count of religious motives. The OR and reference to other concepts preserve personal choice but do not eliminate the board's own religious-protection task. Government may protect conscience without making defense of that religious account part of its science presentation. No finding of prayer, religious assent, student conversion, or punishment is necessary or made.

The strongest objection is that evolution remains taught, no doctrinal truth is prescribed, students may form independent beliefs, and the reassurance reaches other concepts. The five answer that the constitutional problem is the school's official protective activity identified in the complete text and context; lack of compelled belief does not resolve that question. Their rule does not treat a biblical word alone, offense, religious motivation, or all conscience notices as sufficient.

**F3, three Associates:** The complete statement explains that learning the scientific account does not compel a student's personal belief. The actual OR, the reference to other concepts, and the invitation to critical examination do not prescribe a religious conclusion. The speaker is official, but the function is noncompulsion reassurance, not doctrinal adoption, religious instruction, or solicitation. The selectively retained biblical example and adoption history are substantial contrary evidence, but do not transform the operative reassurance into that forbidden function in these Justices' view. Their rule does not authorize compelled creation teaching, a parental curriculum veto, religious grading criteria, or immunity whenever legal coercion is absent.

**F4, all eight:** Judge the complete adopted resolution. Its introductory paragraph directs teachers; the following two paragraphs are spoken to pupils. The actual OR governs. The first spoken paragraph standing alone was not adjudicated below and is not separately decided here. No severability finding, rewritten disclaimer, replacement curriculum, private forum entitlement, or evidentiary remand to seek proof of conversion is supported. The verified question concerns constitutionality, not fee calculation. Affirmance leaves the existing fee judgment undisturbed; reversal leaves dependent consequences to ordinary proceedings without setting an amount or fee rule.

## Reconciled legal and remedial scope

**I1, all eight:** Alaska requires separate clear submerged-land inclusion and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose. Inclusion and congressional notice are conceded; neither independently supplies the second showing. Shively supplies territorial power; Montana and Utah Division supply the strong state-title presumption and separate inquiry. The earlier Coeur d'Alene immunity disposition does not adjudicate title or bar this United States suit. Atkinson's regulation rule does not establish ownership.

**I2, five Associates:** The enacted 1886, 1888, and 1889 measures, with notice of this particular submerged estate, show clear protection of the remaining reservation pending consensual, compensated cession. The 1886 cession negotiations concern land outside the existing reservation. The 1888 right-of-way requires actual approval, consent and compensation and preserves tribal tenure. The 1889 purchase authorization concerns only portions the Tribe consents to sell, on its prescribed terms and subject to ratification. Their combined timely legal treatment protects the identified estate; the unratified agreements themselves are not prematurely binding. Statehood did not transfer the remaining beds during that process. The admission disclaimer corroborates the specific retention; it is not an automatic rule for all Indian reservations. Later ratification may confirm but cannot newly confiscate vested state title.

The strongest I3 objection is that actual access, negotiation, compensation, and tenure obligations can coexist with the future State's bed ownership. The five answer with Congress's enacted choice to obtain identified reservation portions only by consent while preserving the unceded estate, rather than with geography, fishing dependence, legislative sympathy, or an unenacted Senate vote alone. The existing purpose and unchallenged findings explain that specific enacted scheme. Simulated Alaska's legally operative temporary segregation supports examining real legal effect; it does not make all negotiations equivalent to Alaska's statutory wildlife exclusion.

**I3, three Associates:** These statutes protect present use and authorize a contingent transaction but do not clearly accomplish the separate future-state-title consequence. Alaska's operative statehood provisions did that work for its identified estate; general congressional knowledge, temporary tribal use, and a purchase process do not themselves supply it here. No special verbal formula is demanded. Scalia and Thomas's post-1953 Submerged Lands Act objection remains confined to that statute and is not imported into Idaho's 1890 admission. Kennedy may join I3 without adopting that separate statutory position.

**Actual decree:** Affirmance preserves trustee title in the United States, the Tribe's beneficial interest and exclusive use, occupancy and quiet enjoyment, the fitted injunction against Idaho's adverse claim, and denial of its counterclaim within the reservation described by the 1891 and 1894 Acts. The waters and submerged lands Idaho claims within Heyburn State Park remain expressly excluded. No enlarged coordinate description, regulation holding, fishing entitlement, automatic interim injunction, or new trial finding follows.

**I4 and the rejected-reservation remedy:** The preserved aboriginal-title theory was not reached below; the Tribe expressly did not develop its merits in this Court and identified aspects suitable for first-instance adjudication. The historical majority states that limit in footnote 5. The neutral packet and original brief note 12 establish the same facts. They are not newly changed historical premises. If the reservation ground fails, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would return the case for lawful lower-court treatment of the preserved independent ground, without deciding its success or directing unconditional quiet title in Idaho. The historical dissent rejects reservation retention but supplies no separate analysis resolving the aboriginal alternative and no express unconditional contrary-decree direction. The scoped remand therefore should not be described as a demonstrated departure from an adjudicated historical aboriginal-title holding.

The separately suggested ratification/relation-back theory is not adopted. A proposal affirmatively to decide that doctrine against intervening equal-footing title requires the source review identified in the neutral packet; neither affirmance on I2 nor the bounded alternative remand requires that new holding. ## The two's exact contrary statutory rule

Stevens and Breyer may not be summarized as finding provenance alone sufficient. Their historical contrary position requires **both** that each article expressly identify its source collective work **and** that substantially the rest of that collective work be readily accessible to the reader of the individual file at the same time. The publisher-originated editorial selection and issue association must remain available; a detached article carrying only a bibliographic label does not qualify. This gives precise terms to the refreshed model's recorded insistence that the actual archival relationship do real limiting work. It is not a new historical fact, new interface or circulation bargain.

Their analysis separates two questions: whether the collection of electronic files representing one issue is a revision, and whether putting that revision alongside other editions and publications destroys its status. They answer the first yes on the retained issue content and source identification, and the second no where that relationship remains accessible. Individual retrieval and altered layout are consistent with a media-neutral revision; aggregating issues resembles placing periodicals together in a library or on a common microfilm roll. GPO's preserved page images add context but are not the sole basis of their rule. They distinguish an unrelated anthology or a separately sold article without that archival relationship. Author ownership, control of unrelated exploitation and the ban on revising the contribution itself remain intact.

The strongest opposing argument is that the database, as users receive it, sells distinct articles rather than a revised issue. The two regard the remaining accessible editorial selection as enough statutory context; the six require the stronger perceptible collective presentation that these interfaces lack. That is the actual legal disagreement. No party is assigned a newly invented factual concession that every archive has complete contents or that every search produces the original edition.

### A. The five-Justice statutory commitment

For the previously admitted persons presented here, section 1231(a)(6) authorizes detention beyond the removal period only for a period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. Once there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future, continuing detention under this provision is unauthorized. Neither a bare final order nor general danger or flight concerns independently supplies an unlimited civil-commitment power after the removal purpose can no longer reasonably be accomplished.

The statute's removal setting, discretionary extension and express supervised-release alternative support this construction. The serious physical-liberty question reinforces it through constitutional avoidance. The five acknowledge the competing textual argument: Congress specified criminal and risk categories, permitted detention beyond the ordinary period, and supplied no express endpoint. Their answer is that these categories identify persons who may remain detained beyond the initial period while removal remains a genuine prospect; they do not clearly create lifelong preventive custody detached from that purpose. The word permitting detention does not itself confer unbounded discretion.

Foucha and Jackson supply the relationship between custody and a continuing lawful ground; National Center confines delegated discretion to the immigration scheme; Flores concerns temporary juvenile care and preserved individual challenges. Actual Hendricks and Seling require independent attention to punishment and custody authority, but neither supplies a general due-process holding approving the Kansas confinement or requiring a psychiatric diagnosis in every preventive regime. The five assume no proved punitive operation here and do not require a punishment showing to obtain statutory relief. They resolve the statutory claim without an independent constitutional invalidation, universal dangerousness burden, compulsory psychiatric model or ruling on every inadmissible person.

### A6. The implementation rule and its grounds

The five recognize six months as a presumptively reasonable removal-detention period. It is the six-month period in the removal process, not a new six months added after the ordinary 90-day period. It is a presumption, not an absolute statutory ceiling, an order releasing everyone at its expiration, or an immunity from other properly supported unlawful-custody claims during that interval.

After that period, the detainee must provide good reason to believe that there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future. The government must then furnish evidence sufficient to rebut that showing. Passage of six months alone does not compel release; detention may continue until the absence of that significant likelihood has been determined. Conversely, an unanswered supported showing cannot be defeated by indefinite theoretical possibility, good faith alone, the removal order alone, or danger alone. As prior post-removal confinement lengthens, the reasonably foreseeable future must become correspondingly shorter. This preserves both the detainee's initial showing and the government's evidentiary response.

The court assesses supported attempts, foreign responses, negotiations, travel-document prospects, actual receiving arrangements and material detainee conduct. It gives appropriate leeway to executive immigration and foreign-policy expertise, administrative needs and supported assessments, without surrendering the legal-authority determination. No treaty is indispensable, no proof of permanent impossibility is required, and diplomatic uncertainty is not automatically failure. If removal remains reasonably foreseeable, risk of further crimes and flight may help justify detention within the reasonable removal interval. If it does not, the court must provide effective supervised-release relief under the statute, subject to the actual posture and independent lawful custody.

The historical implementation appears at 533 U.S. 699–701. The independent model already accepted the supported-showing and government-response structure, shrinking relevance of vague predictions, and effective relief. Its remaining objection was the lack of a justified interval. The already-validated former six-month removal period supplies a legal reference point; the current regulation's ordinary district-to-headquarters transition after 90 days and up to three additional months supplies an administrative reference point, with all its timing exceptions intact. Uniform judicial administration and a meaningful initial opportunity for executive arrangements justify using that interval as a rebuttable device. Neither reference establishes a statutory maximum or proves how long every deportation takes. The five's historical direct joins are strong additional evidence that this limited device answers, rather than contradicts, their individual concerns on unchanged material premises.

The historical majority also referred to an earlier congressional constitutional concern through a 1956 jurisdictional statement. That additional legislative-intent inference is **not adopted as a newly established neutral fact** here. The present implementation rests on the supplied statutory and regulatory references, the practical reason for a rebuttable rule, and the Justice-specific comparison. No unprovided legislative document, current private preference or mandatory administrative schedule supplies the number.

Zadvydas's time at liberty is not detention counted toward this presumption; his past extended detention and present status are part of the actual remand. The decision does not reset his case to a fictional first day of confinement or order a new six-month detention. Any proposed renewed custody must satisfy current lawful authority and the governing standards. No unpresented universal restart, tolling or aggregation rule is created.

### B. The contrary statutory commitment and the distinct constitutional positions

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas read section 1231(a)(6) to permit continued discretionary detention of its covered persons without a judicial foreseeable-removal prerequisite. Its criminal, danger and noncompliance categories serve protective purposes as well as execution of removal. Foreign refusal, lack of an agreement or inability to predict a date therefore does not alone terminate statutory authority. They reject A's construction and the numerical presumption as conditions Congress did not enact. The same detention phrase's application to both inadmissible and removable categories is a substantial objection to implying different statutory meanings, although the present litigation does not adjudicate all initial-entry cases.

Scalia and Thomas reject the claimed general constitutional entitlement to supervised release into this country based on unforeseeable removal after a valid final removal order. Their precise description of the right, drawn from their Flores and Foucha positions, treats that adjudicated status as relevant; it does not falsely find that these persons never entered. Their historical Mezei analogy concerns the asserted substantive right to release, not a historical finding of nonadmission. They accept the enacted discretionary custody channel and the actual opportunity for release review. They do not adopt Kennedy's distinct affirmative procedural-liberty rationale or the model's K rule. The general challenge fails; a new indefinite remand to invent another constitutional theory is not their disposition. This does not authorize torture, punishment forbidden by governing law, sham exercises of authority or refusal to perform a legally required procedure. No such individual violation is found, and no new remedy for an unpresented violation is prescribed.

Kennedy also rejects the statutory limitation, but recognizes that both removable and inadmissible persons remain protected against arbitrary or capricious detention and unlawful punishment. Genuine danger or flight risk may support detention even when removal cannot be forecast. The current regulation provides meaningful means to contest continued custody, rather than a merely nominal promise of review; its existence does not establish compliant administration in either case. Denial of the prescribed process in an arbitrary or categorical manner can be challenged through habeas. He does not impose a new government burden, a universal hearing calendar or an automatic adversarial judicial release trial.

His historical dissent expressly left for a proper concrete case the severity of danger needed, the adequacy of review when an actual danger or flight finding lacks justification, and the burden's operation. ### Review machinery, statutory exceptions and supervision

The current regulation is the December 21 replacement. It requires the alien's satisfaction showing and the stated findings concerning unavailable or impracticable immediate removal, present and likely future nonviolence, community safety, compliance and no significant flight risk before discretionary release. It provides notice, records review, reasons, a possible district interview, headquarters review and a required personal interview when the stated nonrelease circumstances occur. A two-member panel must agree; a split adds a third member and permits a majority recommendation; the final official is not bound by the panel. Chosen assistance is at no government expense and subject to security and attendance limits; translation follows the stated discretionary determination. Current decisions have no administrative appeal, except completion of Board cases pending on December 21 under the transition rule.

Timing is qualified: the initial pre-expiration review can be waived by requesting additional preparation time; district responsibility can continue for the next three months; headquarters review follows the specified transfer points ordinarily or as soon thereafter as practicable. Subsequent review ordinarily begins approximately annually. Material-change requests may be made no more often than every three months, with a response approximately within 90 days. Shorter discretionary reviews, documented postponement for prompt proper removal or other good cause, unforeseen or emergent circumstances, and the distinct transition schedules remain. The rule is not an absolute six-month deadline or automatic annual release trial.

Travel-document prospects must be considered; ongoing efforts, forthcoming documents, impracticable or public-interest immediate-removal exceptions, and the rule dispensing with review when removal is ready to execute remain distinct. The rule also addresses cooperation, sponsorship or placement where ordered, conditions, discretionary employment authorization and withdrawal of approval before release. Revocation may rest on served release purposes, a condition violation, appropriate enforcement or commencement of removal, or conduct or another circumstance making release inappropriate. It requires reasons and a prompt informal response opportunity; subsequent review includes contested-fact evaluation and an interview ordinarily expected within approximately three months, followed by the stated periodic process. These regulatory provisions do not settle the statutory or constitutional limit of the custody they administer.

For A's five supporters, improved review does not cure detention after its statutory ground ends: considering travel prospects is not the same as requiring release when the requisite prospect fails. They need not invalidate the regulation facially to enforce A/A6. Kennedy finds the prescribed process material to rejecting the general challenge while preserving concrete adequacy questions. Scalia and Thomas likewise do not infer constitutional invalidity from duration or a government's failure to guarantee a removal date. None treats counsel's description of effective operation or statistical releases as a judicial finding about these detainees.

Section 1231(a)(1)(C) separately extends the removal period and permits detention upon the specified failure or refusal to make timely good-faith document applications, or conspiracy or action preventing removal. A foreign country's refusal does not prove those triggers. The government's allegations about Lithuanian applications, reporting appointments and citizenship possibilities, and Zadvydas's asserted renewed application, remain unresolved. Any reliance on that statute or a regulatory cooperation requirement needs its actual predicates; neither obstruction nor complete cooperation is inferred.

The ordinary 90-day period, its latest-of-three commencement rule and the mandatory initial detention categories remain distinct from discretionary paragraph (6). On lawful release, paragraph (3) requires supervision: periodic identification, necessary government-funded medical or psychiatric examination, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A violation can support return to custody under actual lawful authority and process; no unlimited power to recommit on the old rationale is created. Release supplies neither a new admission or immigration status nor a waiver, asylum, cancellation or immunity from removal. A separate criminal, civil-commitment or specialized security regime requires its own established authority, predicates and protections. Terrorism and other special circumstances potentially justifying different preventive authority or heightened deference are not decided on these ordinary records.
