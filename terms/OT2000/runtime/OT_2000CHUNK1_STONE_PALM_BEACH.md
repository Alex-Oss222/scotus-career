# OT_2000CHUNK1

# Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, plus any actually effective intervening decision before December 4, 2000.
* **Conditions:** Apply intervening controlling decisions and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for immediate clarification.

**Stone's order:**

1. Vacate the Florida Supreme Court's judgment.
2. Remand for immediate clarification of the statutory authority supporting its certification deadline.
3. Require the Florida court to explain how the federal safe-harbor provision affects its interpretation of state law.
4. Do not prohibit lawful recounts or direct certification of either presidential candidate.

This merits disposition supplies no independent stay. Any interim restraint requires a separately supported order under the governing law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Stone's Holding:** Article II protects the legislature's authority to establish the manner of appointing electors, but it does not remove state courts from their ordinary role of interpreting enacted election law. The state court must clarify whether its remedy implements existing law or creates a materially different rule after the election. This is a limited remand, not a judgment that Florida's recount procedures are unconstitutional.

**Entering law and application:** Foster v. Love's federal election-date holding does not resolve this presidential certification controversy. The Article II application is open in the governing law. Stone would resolve it through the allocation of authority in Article II and the actual state election statutes, not through an assumption that presidential elections displace ordinary state judicial interpretation.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida's provisions concerning manual recounts, late returns, and certification deadlines present genuine questions of interpretation. A reference to the state constitution does not establish that the court displaced the legislature; an appeal to equity does not establish statutory authority either. The federal Court needs the state court's explanation of the enacted basis and its treatment of the then-effective 3 U.S.C. §5 before determining whether a materially new rule was substituted. Clarification is a legal task, not a demand for a new evidentiary record or a preferred vote total.

**Boundary:** The later election contest, the constitutionality of its particular counting procedures, and the eventual lawful winner are not decided here.

## D. DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT AND FUTURE APPLICATION

Article II permits ordinary state adjudication subject to enforceable federal limits. The distinction is between interpreting enacted election law and replacing it with a materially different rule after voting. This clarification remedy preserves lawful recounts while requiring an identifiable statutory basis for the state court's action.
