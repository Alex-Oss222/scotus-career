# Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-04. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court affirmed on June 4, 2001, unanimously recognizing the maritime negligence wrongful-death action.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote. Parts I, II-A, and II-B-1 were unanimous; Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Thomas joined Part II-B-2. Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justices Souter and Breyer, concurred in part and declined the broader caution about leaving further development to Congress. The vote structure concerns rationale, not disagreement over the action's availability.

**Historical Holding:** Moragne v. States Marine Lines's general maritime death action is available for negligent breach of a maritime duty, and the applicable federal statutes did not preclude the pleaded claim. The judgment did not establish negligence or a damage award.

**Historical Authority:** Quarantined historical Supreme Court comparator only; it does not establish the governing rule.

**Historical Sources:** [Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-811.pdf).

# In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified. The Federal Circuit affirmed on February 17, 2000. No separate opinion is reported. The April 13, 2000 denial of rehearing en banc does not replace the opinion date.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Judge Mayer wrote for a panel including Senior Judge Archer and Judge Plager, analyzing the patent and copyright grounds separately.

**Historical Holding:** The patent analysis protected refusal within the statutory grant absent illegal tying, procurement fraud, or sham litigation and rejected subjective motive inquiry. The copyright analysis adopted a strong presumptive exclusion justification, found no adequate rebuttal, and likewise rejected the proposed subjective-pretext inquiry. The court affirmed antitrust summary judgment without imposing a supply or licensing duty.

**Historical Authority:** This is the historical Federal Circuit anchor only. Its treatment of intellectual-property exclusion is not a simulated Supreme Court holding, and no historical certiorari disposition is used as a merits comparator.

**Historical Sources:** [In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/203/203.F3d.1322.99-1323.html).

# Kansas v. Colorado

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-11. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** On June 11, 2001, the Court rejected Colorado's sovereign-immunity and principal-damages objections, allowed prejudgment interest, and selected 1985 rather than 1969 or 1950 as its commencement date. Further calculation remained. The Court divided 6-3 on the award of prejudgment interest; the immunity and crop-loss portions were unanimous.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens delivered the opinion. Parts I, IV, and V were unanimous. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer joined Parts II and III. Justice O'Connor concurred in part and dissented in part, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court treated interest as available despite unliquidated damages but used the initiation of the original proceeding in 1985 as the equitable starting point. Stone's proposed loss-specific accrual differs and is not attributed to that Court.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; the operative interest commencement date and final damages calculation remain unresolved.

**Historical Sources:** [Kansas v. Colorado](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-1.pdf).

# Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator applies. The D.C. Circuit denied Landry's petition on March 3, 2000. All three judges agreed in the judgment, while the officer-status rationale divided the panel.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Williams wrote the opinion, joined by Chief Judge Edwards. Judge Randolph concurred in part and in the judgment, disputing the employee classification but finding no prejudicial error after de novo Board review.

**Historical Holding:** The majority treated the FDIC administrative law judge as an employee rather than an inferior officer. The separate opinion would classify the adjudicator as an officer but regarded the Board's de novo review as sufficient to avoid reversal. The proposed Stone position agrees with that officer classification but rejects the cure conclusion and requires a fresh lawful hearing.

**Historical Authority:** This is the historical lower-court merits anchor. Neither its result nor later historical developments establish controlling law for this adjudication; a historical refusal of Supreme Court review would answer neither merits question.

**Historical Sources:** [Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/204/204.F3d.1125.99-1230.html).

# Saucier v. Katz

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** On June 18, 2001, the Supreme Court reversed and remanded, with eight Justices supporting the judgment. Justice Souter dissented in part from the application.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas. Justice Souter joined Parts I and II, producing six Justices for those parts. Justice Ginsburg concurred in the judgment joined by Justices Stevens and Breyer. Five Justices supported the opinion's controlling qualified-immunity application and rationale beyond Parts I and II.

**Historical Holding:** The historical majority required a constitutional inquiry before clearly-established-law analysis and distinguished substantive reasonableness from reasonable legal mistakes. It granted immunity on its application of the 1994 circumstances. Justice Ginsburg's group reached the result through the constitutional force analysis rather than the majority's broader immunity approach. Stone's proposed authoritative-reliance defense materially differs from the historical rule and the governing immunity precedents.

**Historical Authority:** Quarantined historical Supreme Court comparator only. The historical majority's approach does not amend the governing immunity precedents or approve Stone's proposed replacement.

**Historical Sources:** [Saucier v. Katz](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-194.pdf).

# United States v. Mead Corp.

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** June 18, 2001: vacated and remanded, 8-1.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented.

**Historical Holding:** The Customs classification letter did not receive deference under Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., but could receive respect proportional to its persuasive power under Skidmore v. Swift & Co. The Court tied controlling deference to congressional delegation of authority to act with the force of law and exercise of that authority; formal rulemaking or adjudication was important evidence, not an exclusive procedural requirement. Remand left persuasive evaluation and the classification result for the lower court.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it does not govern this adjudication or resolve an earlier guidance case.

**Historical Sources:** [United States v. Mead Corp.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-218.pdf).
