# Alexander v. Sandoval

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it recognizes the private action for prospective enforcement of the valid Title VI disparate-impact regulations and sustains the injunction on that basis.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the prospective injunction operative on the accepted findings and within the scope reviewed. Award no damages, invalidate no regulation, and create no section 1983 claim. The validity of the regulations and the unchallenged finding of unjustified disparate impact remain the premises of this review rather than new merits adjudications.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A regulatory duty beyond the received statutory action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the private-enforcement question actually presented. Powell v. Ridge already rejected this direct regulatory action; deciding for Sandoval therefore requires reconsidering that controlling statutory holding. Regulatory validity, the correctness of the impact finding, and a separate section 1983 action are not before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Title VI authorizes a beneficiary to obtain prospective judicial enforcement of a valid section 602 regulation prohibiting unjustified discriminatory effects in the recipient's administration of a federally assisted program.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose overruling Powell v. Ridge's direct-action prohibition to the extent it forecloses this prospective enforcement. Stone joined that prohibition but now rejects its treatment of the integrated statutory scheme. Preserve its distinction between a regulation's validity and a remedy's source, and do not decide its separate section 1983 holding. Reaffirm Cannon's contextual inquiry and distinguish McClellan's unsupported Cable Act damages action.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VI's established private action permits beneficiaries to obtain prospective relief requiring a covered recipient to comply with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 regulations implementing the statutory protection against discrimination. Congress supplies this action through Title VI read as a whole; a regulation cannot independently create an action under an otherwise remediless statute. Powell v. Ridge's contrary direct-action holding should be overruled to this extent. Sandoval may maintain this action, and the reviewed injunction remains supported by that enforcement route.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns prospective enforcement of valid Title VI administration requirements protecting the statutory beneficiaries, including the effects regulation assumed valid here. Plaintiffs must prove the regulation's actual elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation. Statistical imbalance alone is not automatically a violation. Damages for unintentional discrimination and enforcement under section 1983 are not decided.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 601 identifies the protected persons and prohibition; section 602 expressly directs implementation of that protection. The established private action and authorized implementing standards should operate together unless Congress has made the administrative route exclusive. The recipient's obligation does not become privately enforceable merely because an agency wrote a rule, but neither does the agency-facing form of the implementation provision sever every valid regulatory specification from the action Congress accepted. Cannon's interpretive setting, Congress's later express recognition of Title VI suits, and the continued administrative-and-judicial enforcement structure support that conclusion. Those enactments are affirmative context, not an inference from congressional silence alone. The congressional-authorization inquiry carries decisive weight; the practical loss of an effective individual remedy confirms the consequences of the competing readings but does not create jurisdiction or a cause of action. Powell gave insufficient weight to that integrated source and improperly treated the difference between statutory intent liability and valid preventive implementation as dispositive of prospective enforcement. McClellan involved a distinct statute and an asserted damages action without this established remedial background. Central Bank likewise rejects a universal express-text-only rule without itself deciding this claim. Statutory stare decisis deserves substantial weight, and Stone acknowledges his prior join. But Powell's recent channel restriction has not changed the recipients' preexisting duty to obey these regulations; correcting the prospective remedy neither imposes retrospective damages nor disturbs a completed final judgment. Reliance on the absence of a beneficiary's enforcement action cannot outweigh the statute's better-supported remedial arrangement. The present record assumes both valid regulations and a violation. Recognizing this action therefore preserves the remedy for an existing duty rather than judicially prescribing a new language policy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Regulatory validity, a contested disparate-impact merits determination, damages availability, and the separate section 1983 route are outside the granted question. Powell's section 1983 rationale is not silently abrogated.

# Atwater v. City of Lago Vista

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Fifth Circuit judgment and remand because probable cause alone cannot dispose of the challenge to additional custody for this fine-only criminal offense.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require independent evaluation of actual enforcement need under the functional standard. On the plaintiffs' account, berating and a routine booking preference alone cannot justify confinement. Determine whether supported identification or other practical needs justified the additional restraint. Separately decide qualified immunity as of March 1997 and municipal policy and causation; no automatic damages amount, punitive award or invalidation of the seatbelt conviction follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Extend the actual-function rule to fine-only misdemeanors

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal rule because the defense judgment rests on probable cause alone. A categorical common-law breach-of-peace rule is unnecessary once the actual-function approach answers the stated question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a criminal label alone makes station transport and confinement reasonable for an observed fine-only violation, and what adjudication this record requires.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt an extension of Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights to fine-only criminal offenses. Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights presently controls civil licensing custody, so this application is a new holding rather than a claim that the criminal question was already decided.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only misdemeanor does not by itself justify additional station-house transport and confinement. Such custody must reasonably serve its actual enforcement function, with supported identification, appearance, safety, continuing-violation, evidence or necessary-processing grounds considered. Defendants' categorical entitlement to judgment therefore fails.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the added restraints presented here and analogous fine-only criminal enforcement. It preserves initial stops on probable cause and genuine custody needs without requiring the least intrusive imaginable alternative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State's punishment choice and the actual enforcement needs matter to the scale of a seizure. Calling the same modest regulatory violation criminal does not by itself establish that the additional custody reasonably serves its asserted enforcement function. Clear administration is a legitimate interest, but a categorical permission to confine whenever paperwork permits would erase Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights's underlying distinction. Missing papers may justify verification; they do not conclusively establish that every later booking restraint was warranted. Actual facts and practical alternatives must be assessed without hindsight perfection. The rule's merits cannot retroactively establish notice for an earlier officer. The functional inquiry into the seizure's actual enforcement purpose is decisive; the State's choice of a fine-only penalty reinforces the assessment of its intrusiveness. The extension supplies a rule for like cases because a criminal label otherwise defeats the reason for the existing custody limitation. Whren's initial-stop rule remains intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual verification and custody-need findings, conduct-date immunity and municipal policy causation require record determinations absent from the categorical decision; other punishment classes are outside the question presented.

# Sterling v. Borough of Minersville

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 24, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply any earlier controlling decision in Denius v. Dunlap and assess immunity under the authorities effective on the conduct date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of Wilinsky's legal immunity defense; dismiss Hoban's factual-sufficiency appellate contention.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Continue ordinary proceedings on participation, causation and lawful damages. No finding that either officer caused the death and no automatic trial or award.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Compelled intimate disclosure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal privacy question on the supported plaintiff-favorable facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether official leverage can compel an adult to disclose intimate information to family without a legitimate official purpose.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established confidentiality protection in Sheets v. Salt Lake County and adopt its bounded extension to a credible official ultimatum compelling intimate family disclosure. Stone adopts that extension here. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority preserves the distinction between confined justified administration and the unrelated use alleged here without itself deciding the extension.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An officer violates protected intimate confidentiality by imposing a credible official ultimatum requiring an adult to disclose suspected sexual orientation to a family member, or face the officer's disclosure, when no legitimate governmental purpose supports either course.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The present infringement is coerced surrender of confidentiality. Actual communication by the officer is unnecessary; an idle insult without an operative disclosure demand does not fall within the rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Authority acquired through arrest remains connected to lawful public purposes. The supported ultimatum uses that authority to control an adult's private relationship with his family. A religious admonition supplies no policing justification. Disputed admissions do not justify assuming that the information was already public.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Emergency protective disclosure requires facts absent here; a general right to secrecy about evidence of criminal conduct is outside the question presented.

### Issue 2: Notice and individual responsibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply legal immunity separately to each supported role, keeping factual-sufficiency contentions outside this appeal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the particular official ultimatum was sufficiently apparent as unconstitutional in April 1997.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing objective notice test; adopt the conclusion that established confidentiality protection plus the complete absence of an official disclosure purpose provided adequate notice. Stone adopts this notice conclusion on the stated facts despite the contrary reading of the precedents.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An identical orientation-and-threat case is unnecessary where the supported conduct deliberately compels the surrender of clearly protected intimate information for no official purpose. The demand's operative coercion supplies the connection that a bare future-threat theory lacks.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Denial of immunity concerns the supported ultimatum, not every discussion of personal matters. No later opinion supplies retrospective notice, and the officer's subjective awareness alone is insufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County was governing Supreme Court law before the conduct. It protected objectively intimate information against unjustified official exposure after access for a limited investigation, while expressly preserving separate conduct-date immunity analysis. Its completed public dissemination is materially different from a family-directed ultimatum. Stone would nevertheless treat the present use of arrest authority to compel disclosure as an apparent invasion of the same protected confidence: officials cannot make the citizen perform the very surrender they have no official reason to impose. That is a reasoned extension, not a claim that Sheets v. Salt Lake County already resolved threats. The earlier circuit authorities reinforce the required connection to actual official need: United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp. addressed intimate medical information; Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia addressed justified employment inquiries and disclosure safeguards; the 1995 circuit opinion in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority evaluated an actual health-plan monitoring interest. The later Supreme Court ruling in that case supplies no April 1997 notice. Walls v. City of Petersburg presents a substantial contrary argument, but Stone would distinguish its employment-screening inquiry from an arrest-based command to disclose to family for no policing purpose. Ordinary future threats likewise differ from operative compelled self-disclosure. The eligible Supreme Court rule, local confidentiality authority and complete lack of an official purpose support his notice conclusion; neither the newly announced extension nor subjective bad faith alone does so. Personal involvement, causation and compensable injury remain for proof. [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/); Sheets v. Salt Lake County; Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Hoban's actual participation requires fact adjudication unavailable in this interlocutory legal appeal.

# Daniels v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment denying this § 2255 motion on the narrower ground that Daniels identifies no unconstitutional complete counsel deprivation and no loss of a meaningful earlier review opportunity through an external impediment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the existing federal sentence undisturbed against these claims. Order no fresh sentencing hearing or state-conviction invalidation. An independently available original-conviction challenge or later authorized application remains subject to its own prerequisites.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Available review and finality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory review question and necessary constitutional counsel exception. Do not decide whether either plea was actually invalid; those merits do not become adjudicable merely because an enhanced sentence exists.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** When may section 2255 review of a current enhanced federal sentence reach a constitutional challenge to an expired predicate conviction, and do Daniels's allegations meet that standard?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new, bounded extension of the entering channel rule into § 2255. Apply Custis v. United States to the distinction between complete counsel deprivation and ineffective representation, but do not portray its expired-custody reservation as already resolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2255 does not reopen an expired state conviction merely because it enhanced a current federal sentence. Review remains available for unconstitutional complete denial of required counsel and where, through no fault of the prisoner, meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated, subject to the applicable procedural requirements and proof of adverse effect on current custody. A genuinely available earlier opportunity lost through unexcused failure is not an unavailable remedy. Daniels identifies neither complete counsel deprivation nor an external impediment to meaningful original review, so his involuntary-plea and ineffective-assistance claims cannot proceed through this motion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the presented involuntary-plea and ineffective-assistance claims on their actual review history. It supplies no rule for a different statutory predicate objection or a predicate already invalidated through proper proceedings.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The current-custody language identifies the sentence a federal court may correct, but it does not erase the completed judgment’s lawful review structure. Daniels’s reason to challenge the old convictions may have strengthened after the new offense; that change does not establish that the earlier forum was unavailable. Nor should administrative inconvenience alone make a demonstrably unlawful present sentence immune from every authorized challenge. The rule therefore rests on the available opportunity and the absence of an asserted external barrier, not on an irrebuttable factual conclusion that every final conviction is valid. This statutory channel rule preserves a used or genuinely available first opportunity; it does not equate every attorney failure with the prisoner's deliberate choice. Daniels identifies no impediment or failed first opportunity here. The reasoning does not decide a jury claim about a new sentencing fact: Almendarez-Torres and Apprendi cannot supply that unpleaded claim. The defined faultless-loss exception follows the distinction between meaningful first review and repetitive litigation; it is adopted here as part of the governing access rule, not borrowed from a later decision in Coss. Daniels alleges no facts placing him within it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual constitutional validity of the old pleas is not reached because Daniels fails the governing review-access rule. A predicate already invalidated through proper proceedings would present a different reopening question absent from this record; no new sentencing-fact jury claim is presented.

# Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved; renewed 2026-11-08 by express user approval of the corrected-record Coss version, with an authorized alternative judgment added.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Third Circuit judgment and remand under the bounded present-custody review framework, rather than affirming an unrestricted right to relitigate expired convictions or directing denial on a newly assumed absence of sentencing effect.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require determination of responsibility for the failed original review, satisfaction or lawful excuse of procedural conditions, and actual adverse effect on the current sentence. If those conditions and constitutional invalidity are established, provide an appropriately conditioned opportunity for lawful resentencing; order no automatic release or freestanding vacation of an expired judgment.
* **Stone's Authorized Alternative Judgment:** If five votes do not support the remand stated above, Stone may instead vote to vacate the Third Circuit judgment and remand for the Court of Appeals to determine whether any pursued objection that the 1986 convictions did not increase the sentence selected within the range may be heard or was waived and, if it may be heard, to apply proper appellate review; and, only if an identified requirement of former 28 U.S.C. § 2254(b) is shown to remain actually unresolved, to resolve that requirement on the materials bearing on it. The existing deficient-performance, trial-prejudice and consideration determinations keep their lawful scope unless lawfully displaced. Stone maintains his own view that responsibility for the failed original review must be determined in every event and states that view separately; his alternative judgment vote directs no denial on a newly assumed absence of sentencing effect, no automatic release and no freestanding vacation of an expired judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A real review opportunity and present injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Accept the petition’s current-sentence construction, then decide whether the original review process was meaningfully available. Reach constitutional trial error and remedial prejudice only through the proper vehicle and on adequate findings.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can unexplained state failure to decide a properly filed challenge foreclose all review of its alleged effect on current imprisonment?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new limited rule within the entering precedents’ express reservation. This is neither an application of an already adopted Daniels v. United States rule nor an abandonment of the distinct Custis v. United States sentencing limitation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prisoner challenging a current state sentence may obtain consideration of an allegedly unconstitutional expired predicate when meaningful original review was frustrated through no fault of the prisoner, subject to ordinary applicable procedural requirements and proof that the predicate adversely affected current custody.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The pending petition and present-sentence causal issue fall within the granted vehicle question. Neither a missing adjudication nor the existence of old error independently establishes a right to a writ.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Finality properly protects adjudications and review opportunities; it should not manufacture a merits decision that a court never gave. Yet a fourteen-year delay does not itself resolve whether Coss complied with required steps or whether the state process was ineffective throughout. Those findings are necessary. The record also separates the sentencing range from selection within that range. Eliminating prior-record points is substantial evidence about one causal pathway, but it cannot conclusively erase possible discretionary reliance. A lawful remand should require that causal question to be decided rather than assumed in either direction. The statutory availability inquiry is decisive, reinforced by the function of custody review. Former section 2254(b) recognizes an ineffective state corrective process; a nominally pending petition cannot demand indefinite waiting without an effective decision. The remand must examine the actual filings, notices, testimony, and explanation for the delay, not require Coss to produce a statistical demonstration that fourteen years is excessive. Any attribution of the lapse must rest on supported facts. This defined access rule governs comparable failures of first review because finality cannot treat an unprovided adjudication as one already obtained.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate responsibility for the unadjudicated petition and the predicate's adverse sentencing effect require findings on remand under the rule stated above. No legal choice about that rule remains undecided. A new-evidence actual-innocence claim is outside the claims presented.

# Randell v. Johnson

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before April 25, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply intervening controlling decisions; the record must establish any asserted review opportunity or deliberate bypass.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand; reject categorical dismissal solely because no tribunal previously invalidated the disputed confinement.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Compare the credit claim with the actual sentence and custody determination. If it seeks enforcement of the valid sentence, permit the civil action without favorable invalidation. If success necessarily establishes invalidity, decide from supported facts whether a realistic custody-review opportunity existed and whether its loss is legally excused. Proceed without the prerequisite if no meaningful opportunity existed; otherwise enforce the prerequisite and dismiss without prejudice to a qualifying later invalidation. Preserve ordinary defenses and decide liability and injury if the claim may proceed; award no damages now and direct no state-court reopening.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Former-prisoner civil remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the legal universality issue and the standard for the reserved no-realistic-opportunity category. Leave supported factual application to the remand court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether favorable invalidation bars the former prisoner's damages claim when federal habeas relief is unavailable, taking account of whether the claim contradicts a valid sentence and whether a realistic prior opportunity for custody review existed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Heck v. Humphrey's claim-specific prerequisite and expressly adopt a new, narrow exception within its reservation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A claim compatible with the valid sentence is not subject to favorable invalidation. If success necessarily contradicts the operative custody determination, a former prisoner who lacked a realistic lawful opportunity to contest that confinement before custody review became unavailable may proceed without prior invalidation. A claimant who lost a genuinely available opportunity through an unexcused failure to use it remains subject to the prerequisite; expiration alone does not satisfy it. These are the governing rules for Randell's remand, not questions postponed to another case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the formerly confined claimant and the effect of prior availability. It rejects both automatic civil exclusion after release and automatic evasion through deliberate or otherwise unexcused loss of an available review route. It does not require exhaustion of every conceivable state procedure as a universal civil-action condition.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Heck v. Humphrey and Spencer v. Kemna coordinate available statutory remedies without creating an absolute civil disability arising from a conviction. A review route that never realistically existed cannot perform that coordinating function. Randell's allegations establish present federal unavailability but do not settle earlier opportunity or necessary invalidity. The lower court resolved those open matters through an overbroad rule, so remand is required. The decisive reason for the exception is statutory reconciliation: a rule coordinating section 1983 with custody review cannot require a remedy the claimant never realistically could obtain. Patsy's rejection of general state-remedy exhaustion remains in force. Ordinary records of custody, notices, access to the court, and the time available to act can establish realistic opportunity; absence of an earlier successful writ alone proves neither opportunity nor deliberate bypass.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The legal consequences of availability and unexcused bypass are resolved above. Randell's actual review opportunity, the credit calculation, individual responsibility, immunity, limitations, and compensable injury require the undeveloped factual record and ordinary remand adjudication.

# Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the limited question of appellate review: abuse-of-discretion review of the trial court's record-based punitive-amount assessment is permissible, with independent correction of legal error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not vacate solely to require a fresh appellate valuation of the punitive award. Leave the reviewed judgment operative, including the undisturbed $50,000 compensation. The Court neither fixes a constitutional maximum nor reinstates the functional-design injunction; its decision concerns the appellate allocation actually granted.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Independent legal review with preserved factual functions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The grant is expressly limited to the standard of review of a trial court's ruling on a constitutional challenge to punitive damages. Resolve that allocation, including the civil jury's role. The grant does not require reopening Gore's substantive rule or deciding a new dollar limit.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What standard governs appellate review of the trial court's ruling on the constitutionality of a punitive-damages award?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gore as the existing substantive premise while declining to extend Bajakajian's de novo forfeiture assessment to this jury award. Extend Gasperini's allocation of record-based amount review to the presented punitive setting, preserving independent review of governing law and constitutional procedure. Stone continues to reject a freestanding judicial power to choose a preferred punitive number; he does not purport to overrule Gore through this limited grant.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An appellate court independently determines the governing constitutional rule, corrects reliance on legally impermissible grounds, and reviews the trial court's assessment of the punitive amount for abuse of discretion in light of that rule and the supported record. Meaningful review does not require a second independent appellate valuation of the same award. The Ninth Circuit did not err merely by using abuse-of-discretion review.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns appellate review of a civil jury's punitive award after actual trial-court review. It preserves review of constitutional legal errors, unsupported premises, and failures to examine amount; it does not immunize an award merely because a judge approved it. It leaves Gore's governing substantive boundary and Oberg's meaningful-review requirement in force.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The institutional allocation of civil factfinding and punishment assessment carries decisive weight. Selecting a punitive amount combines judgments about culpability, deterrence, the course of the misconduct, and the evidence heard by the jury and trial judge. An appellate court can enforce legal boundaries without treating that assessment as a number it must calculate afresh. Gasperini's reason for respecting the trial court's supported valuation extends to this allocation even though its particular substantive standard concerned compensation. Bajakajian concerned a government-imposed forfeiture under the Excessive Fines Clause; its review rule does not itself answer the distinct civil-jury allocation. Ornelas likewise supplies an analogy about appellate legal supervision, not a universal command for de novo treatment of every mixed question. Oberg requires real examination of amount and lawful correction, which the trial and appellate review here supplied; it did not prescribe repeated independent valuations. Gore remains controlling as to substantive excessiveness despite Stone's disagreement. Disregarding its guideposts, punishing lawful copying as such, inventing threatened losses, or relying on wealth without connection to the wrong would be legal or record errors reviewable under this approach. But a court need not replace a supported assessment of intentional deceptive promotion simply because the ratio is ninety to one. The limited grant asks which institution reviews and how; it neither establishes those supposed errors as facts nor authorizes a new determination of the maximum award. This rule preserves meaningful protection against arbitrary punishment while respecting the jury and the trial court's actual work.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether Gore's substantive excessiveness rule should be overruled, the precise permissible amount, and Oregon's allocation of part of the award lie outside this expressly limited review-standard grant.

# Rogers v. Tennessee

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to any intervening decision effective before the stated event date.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling precedent and material corrections to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Tennessee Supreme Court judgment insofar as it sustains the murder conviction by retroactively abolishing the year-and-a-day limitation, and remand to vacate that conviction and determine any lawful remaining disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Tennessee may abolish the limitation for later conduct. For Rogers, the undisputed interval cannot be cured by a new opportunity to prove that death occurred sooner. The mandate should not decide an unpresented lesser-offense judgment or compel release independently of other lawful custody; the state court must identify any authorized remaining disposition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A surviving offense limitation cannot be removed after the conduct merely because it is obsolete

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved federal due-process question. Accept the Tennessee Supreme Court’s determination that the common-law limitation survived the statute. Separate the constitutional application date from whether medicine and policy justify prospective abolition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a defendant may be convicted of murder through a judicial change that removes a state-law temporal limitation acknowledged to have governed at the time of his act.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the conduct-time protection of United States v. Lanier and adopt its bounded extension to this acknowledged substantive state-law limitation. Stone extends the rule to the year-and-a-day doctrine. Carmell v. Texas reinforces the importance of retaining the earlier legal conditions but is not treated as literally controlling judicial action under the Ex Post Facto Clause.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Where the state’s highest court acknowledges that an offense-limiting rule remained in force when the defendant acted, its later abolition cannot sustain this conviction solely because the rule had become medically outdated or had been abolished elsewhere. Fair warning must come from the prior law’s reasonably apparent reach, not from reasons why the law should change.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding addresses the conceded survival and subsequent abolition shown here. It preserves ordinary judicial interpretation, foreseeable clarification and prospective common-law reform. It does not convert every procedural change into a new offense or demand personal reliance on a legal technicality.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State has a strong reason to abandon an arbitrary interval when reliable proof can establish causation after a longer time. That reason supports lawmaking authority, not necessarily the effective date. Tennessee resolved the critical ambiguity against the State’s first theory: the legislature had not previously abolished the rule. The court therefore cannot substitute the statute’s general homicide language for its own conclusion about the complete law governing homicide. Rogers’s violence was plainly punishable, but notice of serious assault does not erase a surviving limit on murder liability. Nor can a rule’s infrequent practical use establish its absence. Common-law development is legitimate; once a court admits it is removing an existing substantive boundary, the protection against retrospective enlargement must have real content. Here the former rule and undisputed interval supply a concrete basis for relief without questioning the medical examiner’s causation account or giving the defendant a general immunity for his conduct. The conduct-time legal boundary is decisive; modern medicine explains why prospective change is sensible but does not establish prior notice. Bouie and Lanier's fair-warning principle therefore governs this acknowledged removal of an existing limit. The rule protects people against retrospective enlargement without freezing legitimate prospective common-law development.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The availability and form of a lesser-offense disposition are outside the presented federal question and are not resolved on this record. No broader criminal judgment is inferred.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment recognizing and directing consideration of a medical-necessity exception to the Cooperative’s prohibited manufacture and distribution, and remand for proceedings under the statute’s actual terms.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The existing injunction may not be modified on the ground that a judge may authorize the statutory violation because its medical benefits outweigh the legislative policy. ## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Legislative classification and bounded remedial discretion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory construction first and then the Cooperative’s independently offered equitable ground for affirmance. A necessity-based reading is unavailable if it contradicts the enacted scheme. The constitutional objections have not been adjudicated below and are unnecessary to decide this statutory dispute; preserve their ordinary procedural treatment rather than announce a constitutional ruling.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Controlled Substances Act permits this distributor to manufacture and supply Schedule I marijuana on a medical-necessity theory, and whether equity independently authorizes that permission.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing source-and-remedy separation in Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc. and Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc.; adopt the new case-specific statutory conclusion that this scheme forecloses the Cooperative’s medical-distribution exemption. Stone adopts this construction of the present statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The applicable federal scheme does not authorize the Cooperative’s manufacture and distribution under the adopted medical-necessity exception. A court cannot supply that same substantive exception by labeling it injunction modification, although ordinary lawful discretion over equitable enforcement remains.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the actual distribution activities and related intended-distribution possession addressed in the injunction. It does not equate denial of a statutory exemption with a finding that the patients are insincere or that treatment is medically worthless.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The strongest case for the Cooperative is concrete patient need after alternatives fail. That need must receive accurate description, but judicial sympathy cannot itself identify a legal exception. The statute regulates substances according to specified categories, authorizations and channels. Its treatment of Schedule I marijuana cannot be reconciled with an ordinary dispensing exemption created by a court from the same medical considerations Congress organized the scheme to address. The absence of the words “necessity defense” is not decisive by itself; text and structure together must do the work. Equity is flexible within the legal relationship Congress created. A court can examine whether an injunction is needed, whether its language reaches beyond a violation, and how lawful compliance should be ordered. It cannot declare ongoing prohibited distribution permissible because it would have selected a different medical policy. That would collapse the source of an obligation into remedial preference. The narrow judgment protects statutory fidelity while leaving any independently established constitutional limit to genuine adjudication under its own requirements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual possession without distribution is outside the question presented by this Cooperative’s injunction. Whether necessity may ever justify conduct under a materially different federal statute is also outside the question; neither issue is decided.

# United States v. Valentine

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling precedent, subject to intervening decisions effective before May 14, 2001.
* **Conditions:** Apply intervening controlling decisions and determine the sequence of events from the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for reconsideration under individualized suspicion and actual submission standards.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the stated suspicion and submission rules to the supported chronology. The bare permitting presumption cannot itself justify the initial restraint; determine whether officers had additional concrete grounds to suspect unlawful carrying before a seizure. If Valentine actually submitted before any such ground existed, subsequent flight cannot retroactively justify that seizure. If he did not submit and independently suspicious conduct preceded the first seizure, assess that conduct at the correct time. Decide suppression under the resulting findings and ordinary remedial rules; do not order acquittal or infer justification from the discovered gun.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Initial reasonable suspicion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the granted suspicion question, including the Government's preserved permitting rationale, and distinguish the legal sufficiency of its theory from missing findings about what the officers knew.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the lower court's stated combination establishes individualized suspicion of wrongdoing rather than possession and presence.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing totality rule; the clarification that eyewitness reliability and indication of illegality are distinct is an application, not a categorical anonymous-witness prohibition.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** In-person reporting and recent observation may materially support reliability, but corroborated possession, ordinary departure, late hour and generalized neighborhood danger do not without a supported further connection establish individualized suspicion of criminal activity on this record. A state-law presumption that a handgun carrier lacks a permit cannot by itself replace the Fourth Amendment requirement of particularized suspicion. Officers may rely on concrete facts known before seizure supporting unlawful carrying or another crime, but the bare statutory presumption adds no such fact.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No officer must ignore a report. Consensual inquiry and investigation remain available, and a more particularized account may justify immediate restraint.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The report improves knowledge of possession. It does not report a threat or other crime. The actual departure was walking, and Illinois v. Wardlow rejected more substantial flight on an inadequately particularized record. Neither a gun-danger exception nor imagined wrongdoing fills the gap. This is a totality-of-the-circumstances assessment, not a demand that each observation be incriminating in isolation. The report, proximity, conduct, and officers' experience must be considered together; their combination still must support the inference of illegality that justifies restraint. Wardlow and Florida v. J. L. are reaffirmed within their actual requirements, without a categorical firearms exception. New Jersey's burden rule for proving a permit is not itself an observation about this person. Giving that presumption automatic stop-authorizing force would eliminate the required connection to unlawful conduct whenever the reported object requires a license. The Government must identify circumstances that actually support its nonlicensure inference; the court may evaluate those circumstances without deciding the broader validity of the licensing scheme.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Application of the permitting rationale depends on any additional facts actually known before seizure, which the lower court did not determine under this rule. The validity of New Jersey's presumption as a trial-evidence rule and the constitutionality of the licensing scheme itself are outside the presented stop question.

### Issue 2: Submission to authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the legal effect of short duration and remand the disputed historical sequence.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether later flight categorically negates an earlier actual submission.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the inherited force-or-submission framework and newly reject a minimum-duration rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Actual yielding to a police command may constitute seizure even if brief. Later flight does not erase an already completed seizure. A hesitation, inquiry or other equivocal movement that does not objectively manifest yielding remains distinct.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Stopping and giving a name can establish submission in context; the label alone cannot substitute for findings concerning the command and response.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The appellate alternative assumed Valentine's version but rejected it solely as momentary. Duration informs the factual inquiry but does not define a period during which actual restraint has no constitutional status. If no yielding occurred before the charge toward the officer, that conduct may support independent intervention. The force-or-submission boundary supplies the decisive rule. The clarification prevents later flight from retrospectively erasing an earlier seizure, while leaving the disputed chronology to ordinary testimony and factfinding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final application requires missing or disputed findings about the alleged stop and answer; the Court should not invent them.

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit judgment insofar as it rejects the discriminatory Social Security burden on the covered incumbent judges; reverse insofar as it establishes a categorical Medicare exemption; remand for proceedings consistent with those rulings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Compensation protected against discriminatory tax diminution

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the separate Medicare and Social Security classifications because their constitutional validity determines liability. Accept the established statutory coverage and offset structure without finding subjective congressional hostility.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which of these taxes impermissibly diminish the compensation of incumbent Article III judges?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Jefferson County v. Acker's operation-based analysis while resolving the constitutional question it reserved. Overrule Evans v. Gore to the extent it categorically bars generally shared taxes enacted after appointment. Replace that proposition with protection against discriminatory diminution while retaining protection against disguised salary reductions.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ordinary nondiscriminatory taxes remain payable by judges. Congress may not accomplish a prohibited diminution by materially singling out incumbent judges through tax or pension classifications. Medicare falls in the former category; the Social Security transition's offset structure places these judges in the latter. This resolves Acker’s reserved constitutional question and overrules Evans’s contrary categorical tax immunity to that extent.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers incumbent Article III judges subjected to these tax extensions and protects against materially equivalent disguised reductions. It does not exempt judges from common tax obligations or require subjective legislative animus.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Judicial independence warrants an effective anti-circumvention rule, not insulation from common obligations. Existing employees' opt-outs and contribution offsets define the comparison. The noncontributory judicial pension creates an unmatched new burden under the Social Security transition; Medicare's broader extension does not single out the office. Constitutional structure and the actual tax classifications work together, with protection of judicial independence carrying decisive weight. Evans's categorical exemption confuses a general civic burden with a diminution directed at the judicial office; O'Malley's acceptance of common taxation exposes that rule's overbreadth. The replacement protects the institutional interest without a personal tax immunity. Reliance on stipulated compensation and protection against selective reduction remains protected, while an expectation of permanent exemption from generally applicable taxes does not justify retaining Evans's broader rule.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Newly appointed judges and materially different pension classifications are outside the questions presented on this record.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the no-quorum statute and existing judgment-scope principles to permit present review without erasing the earlier judgment. Resolve the remedial question by keeping accrued lawful compensation distinct from later statutory increases.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The earlier no-quorum action does not supply a conclusive merits determination of the constitutional question now properly before the Court. * **Reach Within the Question:** The disposition reaches the prior no-quorum objection and the asserted 1984-raise defense in these compensation claims. * **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A statutory affirmance issued because the Court could not act does not establish a constitutional rationale it never considered. An increase determines the lawful salary thereafter due; it does not retrospectively authorize prior withholding or permit deduction from the new entitlement. Recoverable amounts still depend on each claimant's timely claim.
