## Grounds, competing paths and scope

The seven compare consolidated NOL with aggregate otherwise allowable product liability expenses in the initial computation; product liability loss is the lesser amount. The operating scheme supplies consolidated NOL for this task. A member's STI omits items that ordinarily affect loss and cannot supply a complete separate NOL by assumption. The adjusted measure in section 1.1502-79(a)(3) improves accounting completeness but is confined to a different allocation task; usefulness does not establish applicability. Positive STI therefore does not alone disqualify a member's expenses from the group calculation.

The seven reject the double-deduction claim because STI is an intermediate accounting step, not a separate taxable event: the same expense reduces consolidated income once, and the dispute concerns which carryback period applies. Omission of product liability expenses from a list adopted before the special carryback existed does not establish an intended later member ceiling. Anti-avoidance concerns do not establish abuse here or change the operative provisions; existing section 269 authority and lawful amendment under section 1502 remain available on their own predicates. No future regulation is adjudicated.

Stevens's dissent is substantive, not bare disagreement with a taxpayer advantage. Individual corporations remain taxpayers even when filing together. A consolidated-loss definition does not necessarily answer which taxpayer's loss this special statutory exception uses. He finds the competing textual implications inconclusive, views the adjusted member measure as administrable despite its different direct function, and gives Treasury's expertise about the asserted acquisition-based avoidance risk persuasive weight sufficient to resolve the ambiguity. He neither finds an abusive transaction in this record nor revives the rejected double-deduction contention. His note 3 specifically favors the adjusted member measure over STI and supports affirmance in full.

Thomas's separate position responds to that ambiguity treatment. His historical concurrence invokes Merriam, Bowers, American Net & Twine and Benziger for construing doubtful revenue exactions in the taxpayer's favor. Stevens's first note answers that an exception granting a tax benefit has its own narrower-construction tradition, citing INDOPCO and related authorities. These competing canons remain separate positions; the seven-member operative rule rests on the actual statutory and regulatory account, not on counting those different general theories together.

Reversal and remand require proper refund implementation of the group rule, without inventing a dollar award, reopening agreed expense amounts, or deciding unrelated accounting. The group NOL cap, otherwise allowable-expense requirement, tax-year restrictions and remaining timing conditions survive. No new hearing or future Treasury choice is necessary to resolve the legal issue for these returns.
