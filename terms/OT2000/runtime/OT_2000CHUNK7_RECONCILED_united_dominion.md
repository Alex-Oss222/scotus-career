## Reconciliation of the two provisional discrepancies

**Stevens: revise reverse to affirm.** The provisional position used his Gitlitz and Cleveland Indians joins to infer that the function-specific member allocation provision could not govern the initial loss computation. The full historical dissent supplies substantially more specific evidence. Stevens distinguishes the antecedent question of which taxpayer's loss section 172 requires from the subsequent mechanics for computing consolidated NOL. He finds neither the statute nor the adjacent regulations decisive on that antecedent question, treats the adjusted member loss as an administrable analogue, rejects the unmodified separate-taxable-income proxy, and credits Treasury's concern about tax-motivated acquisition in the remaining ambiguity. He expressly rejects the double-deduction argument.

The entering-law check reveals no concrete changed premise sufficient to sustain the provisional reversal. Gitlitz's specific income classification and express sequence remain binding but do not decide this antecedent taxpayer question. Cleveland Indians preserves a lawful established regulatory construction; it does not forbid persuasive consideration of Treasury's technical concern in an otherwise unresolved statutory construction. Christensen denies automatic controlling statutory force to a nonbinding interpretation but preserves persuasive weight. Stevens's position therefore cannot simply demand binding deference to this litigation view; it can credit its specialized reasoning after his independent conclusion that the text remains ambiguous. This is a correction after full comparison of the grounds, not substitution of a historical vote by tally.

**Breyer: revise affirm to reverse.** His provisional Gitlitz analogy is too general to justify departure from his historical full join. In Gitlitz he identifies a specific corporate exclusion-and-offset mechanism that would be undermined by the proposed shareholder item. Here the operative regulations provide the relevant initial NOL at the consolidated level; the adjusted member figure has an expressly different allocation function. No counterpart of the Gitlitz corporate-only mechanism supplies the missing initial member ceiling. His concern for coordinated statutory operation can support aggregation here while supporting his distinct Gitlitz dissent. Cleveland Indians similarly requires an actual qualifying construction, not any governmental preference. No changed law, record, question, remedy or prior simulated commitment materially displaces the premises of his historical reversal. On full source review he therefore joins the consolidated calculation.

The other six Associates' provisional reversal grounds remain supported. No departure from the historical non-Stone judgment alignment survives this comparison. Current simulated law remains controlling; the comparison does not import the historical majority as earlier precedent.

## Rule, alternatives and full separate-position substance

For these tax years, a consolidated group's initial product liability loss is the lesser of its consolidated NOL and aggregate otherwise allowable product liability expenses. Positive separate taxable income does not independently disqualify a member's expenses. The adjusted member NOL used for allocating a consolidated loss to separate-return years does not impose the asserted ceiling in this different initial computation. The group cap and all other enacted eligibility, timing and amount conditions remain.

The common explanation must address the principal contrary position: the member measure does not simply equal the modified separate taxable income, and its existence elsewhere does not establish its applicability here. Expenses enter the consolidated result through member income once; classifying a portion of that loss for the longer carryback is not a second deduction. Treasury's power to promulgate an appropriate different rule is not proof that it already did. No abuse finding is made.

Stevens's dissent would affirm in full using the adjusted member measure. He accepts the ordinary reason for consolidated treatment but notes that individual corporations remain taxpayers for some purposes. A regulation explaining how to calculate consolidated NOL does not itself determine every occasion on which section 172 requires that figure. The omitted explicit product-liability direction and the workable adjusted member measure leave him unpersuaded by the exclusive group inference. Treasury's concern about buying a profitable affiliate with product liability expenses reinforces his construction within that genuine ambiguity. He agrees that the majority's method does not deduct an expense twice; his position is not a finding of abuse or a categorical taxpayer-unfavorable canon.

If Thomas publishes separately, his function is to reject treating uncertainty in a complex revenue scheme as itself a reason to prefer the Government. The traditional taxpayer-favoring construction principle supplies additional support for the shared result. His statement must remain confined to this interpretive disagreement; he does not reject the settled deference rules he applied in Cleveland Indians. Stevens's contrary distinction between an ordinary revenue imposition and an exception from revenue may be explained without treating either canon as controlling Court law.

## Remedy and limits

The seven support reversal of the Fourth Circuit and remand for the proper refund judgment under the group rule. The uncontested aggregate expenses below consolidated NOL resolve the litigated ceiling; no unsupported dollar refund, novel evidentiary hearing, new Treasury choice for the old years, duplicate deduction, or eligibility above consolidated NOL follows. Stevens would affirm the adjusted-member approach and the judgment below. No material source limitation prevents these bounded dispositions.
