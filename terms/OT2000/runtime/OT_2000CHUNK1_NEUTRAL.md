### Issue 1: Application filing and claim eligibility

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AEDPA governs the limitation period; Lindh v. Murphy governs the temporal application of its amendments. No controlling decision resolves whether these New York relief bars are filing conditions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The one-year state-custody habeas rule excludes time while a properly filed state collateral application concerning the pertinent judgment or claim is pending.

The Second Circuit treated acceptance under the State's filing rules as sufficient despite mandatory claim bars. The warden argues that a state application forbidden from obtaining relief cannot be properly filed, and that a broader rule permits repeated futile filings to delay federal limitations. Bennett distinguishes an application from its constituent claims and rules controlling the filing act from rules controlling a court's disposition. His position does not require treating an untimely or misdirected application as proper. The warden's policy concern must be tied to the language Congress enacted.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Filing-condition construction:** Affirm on the question reviewed; an accepted application complying with rules governing its filing tolls even if individual claims cannot succeed. The rule reaches claim-specific bars, not every timeliness, fee, or jurisdictional restriction. **Relief-eligibility construction:** Reverse only if these particular New York bars constitute conditions on proper filing; do not automatically resolve other independent grounds supporting timeliness. **Uncertainty:** The appellate assumption concerning the written denial is not a Supreme Court finding. Either merits path leaves actual pending time and any unreviewed habeas defenses for appropriate proceedings.

# Cleveland v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Cleveland v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-804; 531 U.S. 12
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2000-11-07 (historical).

Cleveland assisted applications identifying Goodson's children as owners while allegedly concealing Cleveland's and Goodson's interests and financial difficulties. The State did not operate the gaming business. The prosecution alleged deceptive acquisition of licenses, not deprivation of unpaid fees or taxes. Two mail-fraud convictions and other convictions dependent on the charged fraud followed a jury trial.

The honest-services provision supplies no independently charged route in this reviewed theory. The treatment of unaffected counts must follow the actual indictment, verdict, and dependence of predicates.

### Issue 1: Government licensing and property fraud

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Neder v. United States makes materiality an element of charged financial-deception schemes; material deception does not eliminate the separate money-or-property element. No controlling decision converts regulatory authority into property.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The financial-deception rule keeps materiality, mailing, and the elements of the underlying scheme separate.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1341, 1346, 1957 and 1962; Louisiana's licensing provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** McNally v. United States and Carpenter v. United States supply the received money-or-property distinction; they do not resolve this licensing application.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Federal fraud reaches property schemes, including intangible property under Carpenter v. United States. McNally v. United States preserves a distinction between property and an asserted right to honest governmental administration.
* **Open Point:** Whether the State's power to issue, withhold, renew, and revoke a license is property rather than regulatory authority.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The lower court treated the State's control over issuance and the license's value as sufficient property. The United States stresses exclusivity, allocative control, revenue, and analogies to patents or franchises. Cleveland responds that these are sovereign permissions, not assets the State owns and transfers, and that no unpaid revenue is charged. A rule excluding regulatory permission need not exclude fraudulent acquisition of government money, land, or an actual commercial entitlement. A contrary rule must explain why it does not federalize every dishonest licensing application.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Regulatory-permission construction:** Reverse the property-fraud ground and remand for consequences on the linked convictions. The holding can cover comparable licenses held only in a sovereign regulatory capacity without deciding every government franchise. **Government-property construction:** Affirm if the identified interest genuinely constitutes property before issuance; materiality and the remaining elements remain required. **Scope and uncertainty:** Licensee property, licenses transferred as commercial assets, and independently charged revenue fraud are different questions. The record does not support treating fees or taxes as missing property merely to save this prosecution.

# Sinkfield v. Kelley

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sinkfield v. Kelley
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 00-132 and 00-133; 531 U.S. 28
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; the historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2000-11-27 (historical).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the plaintiffs established personal standing to challenge Alabama legislative districts as racial gerrymanders and, if so, whether the adjudicated design-only theory supports an injunction under governing simulated law.
* **Question Granted:** The issues stated above define the scope of review; their wording follows the judicial record rather than a verified verbatim petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Three-judge Middle District of Alabama, 96 F.Supp.2d 1301: invalidated seven majority-white legislative districts and enjoined their use. The private intervenors and state officials take consolidated direct appeals.
* **Relief Requested:** Appellants seek vacatur of the injunction and dismissal or judgment against the asserted claims. Appellees seek preservation of the seven-district relief.
* **Material Record:** The plaintiffs reside in majority-white districts adjoining majority-minority districts created under a plan designed to maximize majority-minority districts. They rely on the unusual shapes and the effects of designing neighboring districts. The historical record supplies no allegation or evidence that a particular plaintiff was personally assigned by a racial classification, beyond the asserted consequence of neighboring district design. It establishes no independent exclusion, dilution, unequal voting weight, or legally inferior membership.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Article III personal injury must precede merits. Direct appellate jurisdiction rests on the three-judge court's merits injunction under §§2284 and 1253. Failure of a design-only substantive theory is not automatically absence of jurisdiction; the standing and merits grounds must remain distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date fixes the decisional cutoff. The lower judgment and all facts relied on must predate that event; later law is excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The associated appeal by Alabama officials is included in this one listed matter; it receives no separate brief.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000. Any intervening decision effective before the stated event date must be incorporated before adjudication.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified. A proposed position is not precedent.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific outcome-dependent earlier OT2000 matter is identified. Apply any intervening effective ruling that bears on the issue before final use.

### Issue 1: Personal stake and design-only claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Johnson v. Miller and Bush v. Vera separate personal assignment and standing from ultimate success. Shaw v. Reno, Johnson v. Miller and Bush v. Vera reject territorial racial design alone as the asserted substantive deprivation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The territorial-design rule requires an independently cognizable constitutional wrong for merits relief; shape is evidence, not a sufficient wrong. Stage-appropriate personal injury, causation and redressability govern standing independently.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; Equal Protection Clause; 28 U.S.C. §§1253 and 2284; 42 U.S.C. §1983.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Actual personal exposure can establish standing even when the constitutional theory loses. Neither living near another district nor the unsupported inference that every neighboring assignment was racial necessarily establishes that exposure.
* **Open Point:** Whether these plaintiffs proved the necessary personal relationship to the challenged conduct. If they did, the design-only merits theory is already foreclosed absent a supported distinct deprivation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court inferred unconstitutional racial design in the seven districts and ordered relief. Appellants challenge personal standing and the legal inference from surrounding racial design. Appellees say their own districts' shapes and the statewide plan demonstrate personal sorting. Under simulated law, appellants also have the independent merits defense that predominant racial design alone is insufficient. Appellees cannot obtain an injunction merely by replacing a rejected design-only theory with an unsupported assertion of status injury; a genuine preserved participation or dilution claim would require its own proof.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**No personal injury:** Vacate and direct jurisdictional dismissal, without a merits judgment. **Standing established:** Reject the design-only merits theory under controlling precedent and dissolve the dependent injunction; do not label this a standing failure. **Materially different preserved injury:** Adjudicate that claim under its own standard, with a bounded remand only if facts actually require it. The first two routes establish different preclusion consequences. Neither creates a right to maximize minority districts or certifies every aspect of the map. The record does not establish an independent injury sufficient to support the third route.

# Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 98-15967; 203 F.3d 659 (9th Cir. 2000)
* **October Term:** 2000
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for decision; expressly listed simulation grant from a lower court.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2000-11-27 (organizationally assigned).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a public employee may maintain a First Amendment retaliation claim when her employer acts on the mistaken belief that she engaged in protected speech, although she did not make the speech attributed to her?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define the scope of merits review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed dismissal of Sylvia Wasson’s amended complaint, holding that she had not stated a First Amendment claim because she denied the attributed speech. The district court had dismissed on qualified-immunity grounds. The operative amended complaint named President Robert Agrella and Vice President John Roberts individually, rather than all entities appearing in the caption.
* **Relief Requested:** Wasson seeks reinstatement of her retaliation claim and damages adjudication. The officials seek affirmance, either because actual protected speech is an element she cannot establish or because the asserted right was not clearly established when they acted.
* **Material Record:** Five anonymous letters and a flyer circulated during 1995 and 1996 criticizing the college president. A document examiner compared material from Wasson’s personnel file and attributed the writings to her. She denies authorship and dissemination. In January 1997 officials recommended dismissal for alleged false and defamatory statements and unfitness for service; the board adopted the recommendation and put her on paid leave pending appeal. It withdrew the notice without prejudice and reinstated her in March 1997. At the pleading stage her denial and properly pleaded retaliatory conduct are accepted, but the writings’ protected status is not established simply by calling them criticism. The complaint alleged that the notice could be reinstated until October 28, 2000; that date precedes this assigned event.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate her own asserted injury from third-party standing to assert an unidentified author’s rights. Past damages claims are not mooted merely by reinstatement. A continuing prospective threat cannot be assumed from a reinstatement deadline that has passed. Qualified immunity is preserved and concerns January 1997 notice. Later circuit decisions may describe the controversy but cannot retroactively supply notice. The record does not establish a free-standing constitutional right to a flawless employment investigation.
* **Relevant Dates:** Writings circulated August 1995 through October 1996. Discipline was recommended January 14, 1997; the notice was withdrawn March 24, 1997. The lower decision issued February 16, 2000. The alleged reinstatement period ended October 28, 2000. The November 27, 2000 event is organizationally assigned.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is addressed in Section III.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000. Any intervening decision effective before the stated event date must be incorporated before adjudication.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No intervening controlling decision is identified. A proposed position is not precedent.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific outcome-dependent earlier OT2000 matter is identified. Apply any intervening effective ruling that bears on the issue before final use.

### Issue 1: Mistaken identity and public-employee retaliation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Waters v. Churchill establishes a seven-Justice sufficient safe harbor for a genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech and a six-Justice limit against using an unreasonable false account to defeat actually protected speech. No controlling opinion adopted its full investigative framework. Crawford-El v. Britton preserves ordinary motive and causation proof, including competent circumstantial evidence, and the Mt. Healthy same-decision defense.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The existing public-employee-speech rule asks whether protected speech substantially motivated an adverse action and permits proof that the same action would have occurred without it. Waters v. Churchill neither creates a universal investigation tort nor resolves a claim in which the employee spoke nothing attributed to her.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments and 42 U.S.C. §1983; Pickering v. Board of Education and Connick v. Myers supply the received public-concern and employer-interest framework. McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission protects anonymous expression within its actual scope.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A content mistake about actual employee speech differs from a mistake about the speaker’s identity. Neither the existing safe harbor nor anonymous-speech protection alone establishes the answer.
* **Open Point:** Whether government action directed at suppressing perceived protected speech infringes the targeted employee’s own First Amendment right despite mistaken attribution, and whether a liability decision is needed in this individual-damages case.

### Issue 2: Clearly established law and permissible decisional order

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Elder v. Holloway requires independent review using all relevant legal authority on the preserved immunity issue, fixed at the time of official conduct, and does not impose a mandatory merits-first sequence. Crawford-El v. Britton permits dispositive legal defenses to be addressed first without imposing a special motive-proof burden.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Clearly established law must give fair, particularized notice of the asserted federal restriction. General speech protection does not automatically settle the materially distinct mistaken-identity issue. The defense does not authorize factfinding against well-pleaded allegations.
* **Other Governing Law:** Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton provide the received objective notice inquiry. The January 1997 conduct date, not the November 2000 event date, controls that inquiry.
* **Material Other Authority:** No additional authority is necessary beyond the sources identified here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A court may preserve an unresolved constitutional question while disposing of the preserved damages claim on an independently adequate immunity ground.
* **Open Point:** Whether preconduct authority already made perceived-speech retaliation unlawful in the particular setting. No identified controlling decision squarely resolves that extension.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Wasson argues that deliberate punishment for attributed criticism chills her own and others’ speech, that government censorship should not gain protection from mistaken identity, and that an unreasonable investigation cannot defeat protection for anonymous speech. The officials argue that a retaliation claim requires actual protected expression, that the Constitution does not correct every mistaken dismissal, and that Waters v. Churchill does not clearly establish this distinct right. The historical dissent treated the threatened punishment as a direct injury to Wasson and would extend protection despite her denial. The majority treated the absence of her speech as dispositive and found no basis for third-party standing.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

One lawful path recognizes a perceived-speech right and remands for protected-status, motive, personal-responsibility, and immunity analysis. Another rejects that extension and affirms on the constitutional element. A narrower path affirms the individual damages dismissal on lack of clearly established law while reserving the constitutional question. The last path must not imply that the officials acted lawfully, that every investigative practice was reasonable, or that immunity disposes of an independently live prospective claim. Here such a separate surviving claim is not established by the amended-complaint record.

### Issue 1: Ordinary crime-control checkpoints

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Whren v. United States sustains an ordinary traffic stop supported by probable cause while preserving purpose-limited administrative and inventory doctrines. Chandler v. Miller requires a concrete special need for its suspicionless testing program. Bond v. United States does not permit lawful presence to supply independent search authority.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The objective traffic-stop rule and special-needs rule operate in their own domains. A brief intrusion alone does not establish authority for suspicionless seizure.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. §1983; preliminary-injunction and prospective-relief principles.
* **Material Other Authority:** Michigan Department of State Police v. Sitz; United States v. Martinez-Fuerte; Delaware v. Prouse, each within its actual checkpoint setting.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Michigan Department of State Police v. Sitz and United States v. Martinez-Fuerte address particular roadway-safety and border objectives; they do not by themselves authorize every crime-control checkpoint.
* **Open Point:** Whether generalized narcotics detection can justify suspicionless checkpoint stops and how primary program purpose bears on that issue.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Seventh Circuit treated generalized drug investigation as outside the limited reasons supporting suspicionless roadblocks. The city and United States emphasize systematic operation, short delays, serious drug harms, lawful exterior observation, and the simultaneous license and sobriety functions. Motorists answer that those features regulate an otherwise unauthorized seizure and cannot create a drug-investigation exception. Their limiting principle preserves supported border or immediate road-safety checkpoints and action on evidence lawfully encountered there.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Crime-control limitation:** Affirm and permit appropriate prospective restraint on the described program. The rule concerns programs principally aimed at ordinary evidence gathering, leaving genuine special needs and emergencies to their own law. **Contextual reasonableness approval:** Reverse only if the Court identifies a lawful category supporting these suspicionless stops and explains why the rationale does not authorize universal crime checkpoints. **Purpose dispute:** A remand could resolve a genuinely contested primary objective, but the present announced drug purpose supplies no reason to invent such a dispute. No suppression or damages issue is before the Court.

### Issue 1: Corporate and conduit contributions

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC preserves meaningful association while allowing adequately justified contribution restrictions. Russell v. Burris and Day v. Holahan prohibit treating any nominal limit as automatically valid. Those holdings do not make every campaign-finance provision rise or fall with equal treatment of all possible influence.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Contribution restrictions require a sufficiently important justification and a fit preserving effective advocacy and meaningful association. Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC, Russell v. Burris, and Day v. Holahan retain their distinct applications to contribution ceilings.
* **Other Governing Law:** The historically applicable 2 U.S.C. §§441b and 441f regulate corporate treasury contributions and contributions made in another person’s name. The First Amendment protects human political association; statutory corporate channels and disclosure serve different functions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Buckley v. Valeo supplies the contribution/expenditure distinction. FEC v. National Right to Work Committee addresses corporate contribution channels. Neither makes the existence of a soft-money channel a categorical defense to concealed candidate contributions.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The established contribution framework permits source and attribution scrutiny but has not decided this alleged soft-money inconsistency. The conduit issue independently concerns truthful attribution.
* **Open Point:** Whether lawful party soft money makes the direct-corporate prohibition inadequately fitted, and whether that contention independently defeats the ban on false-name contributions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Third Circuit distinguished candidate contributions from party soft money and treated accurate identification as independently necessary to contribution limits and disclosure. It rejected the proposition that incomplete regulation makes the challenged rules unconstitutional.
* **Mariani:** Unlimited party contributions can secure comparable candidate access, undermining the asserted corruption rationale for direct corporate restrictions; accurate names cannot justify a scheme whose major influence channels avoid the same discipline. He seeks invalidation based on the system’s operation, not merely disfavored corporate status.
* **United States:** Treasury-to-candidate transfers and concealed reimbursements directly threaten contribution limits and attribution. Congress can regulate these transactions without simultaneously prohibiting every other route to political influence. Separate political committees preserve human participation.
* **Limits:** The government must defend these burdens on their own terms. Mariani’s equal-influence premise does not establish that direct contributions and all party spending are legally or practically identical.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Sustain both provisions and return the pending prosecution for ordinary adjudication; invalidate the corporate ban while preserving the independent false-name prohibition; or invalidate a specific application if its burden lacks the required justification. A favorable constitutional ruling would not decide any surviving count’s elements or authorize broad relief against unchallenged soft-money practices.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rejection of the loophole argument would establish that underinclusiveness is evidence relevant to fit, not automatic invalidity, where distinct direct-transfer and attribution functions remain. It would not approve every corporate expenditure restriction or every contribution amount.
