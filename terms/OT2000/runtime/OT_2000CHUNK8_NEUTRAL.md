# Duncan v. Walker

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Duncan v. Walker. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-121; 533 U.S. 167. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the statutory tolling provision for a state prisoner's federal habeas application. **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether the pendency of a federal habeas petition is time during which an application for “State post-conviction or other collateral review” is pending under 28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)(2), and therefore is excluded from the one-year limitation period for a later federal habeas application. **Question Granted:** Faithful formulation of the statutory question reviewed; the petition's precise wording has not been independently verified.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reversed the district court's dismissal of Walker's second federal habeas petition, reinstated the petition, and remanded. It held that his first federal petition qualified as “other collateral review” and that excluding its pendency made the later petition timely. **Relief Requested:** The superintendent seeks reversal of that statutory ruling. Walker seeks preservation of reinstatement and further consideration of his application. [Duncan v. Walker](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-167.pdf).

**Material Record:** Several New York robbery judgments were entered against Walker in 1992. His last conviction followed a June 1992 guilty plea to first-degree robbery and carried a sentence of seven to fourteen years. That conviction was affirmed on June 12, 1995; leave to appeal was subsequently denied. A separate coram nobis application was denied on March 18, 1996. The last conviction became final in April 1996, before AEDPA's April 24 effective date.

In a single document dated April 10, 1996, Walker submitted a civil-rights complaint and a federal habeas petition. On July 9, the district court dismissed without prejudice. The habeas allegations did not adequately identify the claims presented in the state appellate proceedings, leaving exhaustion unestablished. This was not an adjudication that his constitutional claims lacked merit.

Walker filed another federal habeas petition on May 20, 1997. He had not returned to state court after the first dismissal, and the two federal petitions contained different claims. The district court dismissed the second petition as outside a reasonable period after AEDPA became effective. The court of appeals instead used an April 24, 1997 deadline for convictions already final at enactment. On that premise, the petition was late unless the first federal proceeding's pendency was excluded or another lawful basis applied. Roughly nine months remained after the first dismissal under the appellate court's calculation.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory tolling question is preserved and dispositive of the reason the appellate court reinstated the application. The parties' dispute does not establish that every allegation in the first pleading was properly filed, exhausted, or identical to the later claims. Whether a return petition is second or successive, whether it satisfies the time limitation, and whether its claims may ultimately be heard are distinct inquiries. The appellate court's transitional deadline is a premise of this review, and does not establish every feature of an AEDPA grace period.

**Relevant Dates:** AEDPA effective April 24, 1996; first dismissal July 9, 1996; second federal petition May 20, 1997; district dismissal May 6, 1998; Second Circuit judgment in 2000; argument March 26, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** Artuz v. Bennett concerns a different component of the same tolling provision and requires the chronology check below. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Artuz v. Bennett appears earlier on the calendar and concerns when an application is properly filed. Any operative holding must be applied to tolling eligibility and the distinction between filing requirements and merits defects.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** The immediate review concerns timeliness, not entitlement to release. A conclusion that a federal proceeding does not satisfy this particular statutory exclusion leaves the lower court responsible for any other properly presented basis for timeliness.

### Issue 1: The proceedings covered by statutory tolling

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Lindh v. Murphy treats AEDPA's application provision by provision and excludes the identified chapter 153 changes from the noncapital applications already pending at enactment; it does not establish a blanket exemption for every later application concerning an older conviction. Slack v. McDaniel holds that a return application after dismissal without prejudice solely for nonexhaustion before merits adjudication is not second or successive merely because it follows that dismissal. Slack v. McDaniel expressly preserves independent limitations, exhaustion, and procedural-default requirements. Neither case determines whether federal pendency is state collateral review.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** The one-year-period standard identifies the statutory commencement events and the exclusion for pending state postconviction or other collateral review. The enacted language leaves the contested modifier for construction here. The temporal-application and return-petition standards preserve Lindh v. Murphy's provision-specific analysis and Slack v. McDaniel's separation between successive-application classification and timeliness. A non-successive petition does not receive an automatic new limitations period.

**Other Governing Law:** Section 2244(d)(1) supplies the limitation period and its specified commencement rules. Section 2244(d)(2) states: “The time during which a properly filed application for State post-conviction or other collateral review with respect to the pertinent judgment or claim is pending shall not be counted toward any period of limitation under this subsection.” The exhaustion requirement remains a separate statutory condition. [Duncan v. Walker](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-167.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** The Second Circuit's reasoning that “other collateral review” includes federal proceedings is the judgment under review. Its construction is subject to independent review under the governing statute.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law distinguishes a new merits application from an exhausted return and distinguishes successive-application restrictions from the statute's clock. Properly qualifying state collateral proceedings receive the statutory exclusion. These rules leave the disputed word “State” and the coordinated phrase to be interpreted.

**Open Point:** Whether “State” modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review, or only the first category, is unresolved. The effect of a particular unraised equitable circumstance is not established by deciding that grammatical question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Controlling Reasoning Below:** The court of appeals read “State” as modifying postconviction review alone. It believed a contrary reading would either make “other collateral review” superfluous or produce an awkward phrase, “State other collateral review.” It also considered an early federal filing consistent with AEDPA's goal of prompt litigation.

**Petitioner's Strongest Supported Position and Requested Rule:** The superintendent contends that “State” qualifies the whole coordinated phrase. Otherwise the adjective does virtually no work: unqualified postconviction or collateral review would already encompass state review. State systems use diverse collateral procedures, and state custody can arise from civil commitment or contempt, so “other collateral review” need not mean federal habeas. Congress elsewhere expressly distinguishes state and federal proceedings. The requested rule excludes federal habeas pendency from this statutory tolling provision.

**Respondent's Strongest Supported Position and Requested Rule:** Walker argues that federal habeas is naturally collateral review and that the disjunctive wording creates a second, broader category. The limitation should not penalize a prisoner who timely invokes federal jurisdiction but receives a nonexhaustion dismissal only after the filing period has expired. His requested rule excludes the time any properly filed federal habeas application concerning the pertinent judgment or claim remains pending.

**Necessary Limits and Alternative Grounds:** Walker's fairness example is not identical to his record: substantial time remained after the first dismissal, he did not return to state court, and the applications contained different claims. The superintendent's statutory reading need not resolve the availability of equitable tolling, the handling of a mixed petition by a stay, or every possible later commencement event. No verified Solicitor General position materially alters the issue.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Paths:** A distinction between the two applications or an independent filing defect could affect entitlement to the exclusion even under Walker's reading, but the appellate judgment squarely rests on the legal inclusion of federal proceedings. The present record supports resolution of that statutory question without adjudicating the robbery claims.

**Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Reading “State” across the coordinated phrase requires reversal of the appellate tolling ruling and remand to apply the limitation without subtracting the first federal proceeding. If no other properly presented basis makes the later petition timely, dismissal follows. Reading “other collateral review” as an independently unqualified category supports affirming reinstatement, subject to the statute's other requirements and remaining habeas defenses.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The first interpretation would exclude federal applications from § 2244(d)(2), including timely federal petitions later dismissed for exhaustion, while preserving the separate questions of equitable relief and case-management authority. The second would include properly filed federal collateral applications concerning the pertinent judgment or claim, not all federal litigation or every civil-rights complaint. Neither route necessarily decides whether a pending civil commitment challenge or a differently named state procedure qualifies on its own facts.

**Uncertainty:** The complete first and second petitions and any request for equitable relief are unavailable. The record does not establish government obstruction, court misdirection, or extraordinary circumstances preventing timely refiling. Such facts cannot be assumed to avoid either construction's consequences.

# Idaho v. United States

**Matter type:** MERITS. **October Term:** OT 2000. **Event date:** 2001-06-18. **Date status:** historical.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Idaho v. United States. **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-189; 533 U.S. 262. **October Term:** 2000. **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of quiet title to submerged lands within the Coeur d'Alene Reservation. **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 2001, historical calendar date.

**Question Presented:** Whether the United States reserved the submerged lands within the Coeur d'Alene Reservation for the Tribe and Congress sufficiently manifested an intention to defeat Idaho's otherwise presumptive title upon statehood. **Question Granted:** Faithful formulation of the title question reviewed; the petition's exact wording has not been independently verified.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** After a nine-day trial, the federal district court quieted title in the United States as trustee and recognized the Tribe's beneficial interest in the bed and banks within the present reservation boundaries. The Ninth Circuit affirmed. Idaho did not challenge the district court's historical factual findings on appeal. **Relief Requested:** Idaho seeks reversal and recognition of its equal-footing title; the United States and intervening Tribe seek preservation of the trust-title judgment. [Idaho v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-262.pdf).

**Material Record:** The dispute concerns approximately the lower third of Lake Coeur d'Alene and the relevant St. Joe River segment within the existing reservation, not the entire historical aboriginal territory. The Tribe used the waters and beds for fishing, aquatic plants, transport, and other activities. Those facts bear on the understood object of the reservation but cannot by themselves prove that Congress withheld state title.

A small reservation established in 1867 did not satisfy the Tribe. Negotiators reached a broader agreement in 1873, expressly subject to congressional ratification. President Grant's November 8, 1873 executive order independently set apart a defined reservation including virtually the whole lake and part of the river. An 1883 survey counted submerged acreage. Idaho concedes that the executive reservation included submerged lands and that Congress received notice of that inclusion; the disputed question is Congress's intention concerning later state title.

An 1887 agreement ceded land outside the existing reservation and promised protection of the remaining estate, but it too awaited ratification. In January 1888, the Senate requested information about the reservation's boundaries and navigable waters. Interior's response identified inclusion of virtually the entire lake and part of the river. An enacted May 30, 1888 railroad measure authorized a right of way through the reservation, including water crossings, with tribal consent and compensation to the Tribe. An enacted March 2, 1889 measure authorized negotiations to obtain designated reservation portions that the Tribe consented to sell. The resulting 1889 agreement ceded the northern portion, including roughly two thirds of the lake, and retained a southern boundary crossing the lake. Negotiators expressly described the river and lower lake as remaining with the Tribe.

The Senate passed a ratification bill on June 7, 1890, but the full Congress did not ratify the agreements until March 3, 1891. Idaho entered the Union on July 3, 1890. Its admission accepted a state constitutional disclaimer concerning Indian lands, with federal control over disposition until title was extinguished. The sequence makes the significance of the enacted prestatehood measures central: a Senate bill is not an enacted statute, and later ratification could not simply reclaim title if it had already vested in the State at admission.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The United States brings its own trust-title suit, with the Tribe intervening. This is not merely the earlier private action against Idaho or its officials. No unresolved factual attack on the historical findings prevents legal review. The federal sovereign's claim, the State's title counterclaim, and the geographical limit of the judgment must remain distinct from questions of tribal regulatory jurisdiction over particular persons or activities.

**Relevant Dates:** Reservation order November 8, 1873; railroad enactment May 30, 1888; purchase-negotiation enactment March 2, 1889; Senate ratification vote June 7, 1890; statehood July 3, 1890; congressional ratification March 3, 1891; argument April 23, 2001. **Companion or Related Matters:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho addressed immunity and prospective official relief, without deciding this title. **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No identified intervening decision determines this title. Atkinson Trading Co. v. Shirley concerns tribal taxation and does not supply an inferred land-title holding. Any intervening controlling decision concerning tribal property or reserved submerged lands must be applied.

**Institutional or Procedural Law:** Sovereign immunity, authority to sue, ownership of the submerged estate, and regulatory jurisdiction are separate questions. The judgment sought is geographically limited quiet title, not a universal declaration of regulatory power.

### Issue 1: Reservation of submerged lands against future state title

**Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Alaska requires a clear showing that submerged lands were included in the federal reservation and a clear intention to defeat the future State's title for an appropriate public purpose. Geographic inclusion or a general reservation purpose alone is insufficient. That case found the necessary congressional confirmation in the operative statehood provisions and identified prestatehood reservation action. Its later implementing decree, completed before this term, did not alter the merits test or authorize poststatehood action to retroactively defeat vested state title.

Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho permits the specified prospective claims against connected state officers under its simulated rule, while preserving immunity for the direct private quiet-title claim against the State and agency. It expressly does not decide ownership, navigability, prestatehood disposition, or the separate United States action. The title cannot be inferred from that immunity disposition.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho distinguishes prospective relief from a judgment binding an immune State's property title. The implementing decrees preserve the judgments' express property exceptions; they do not replace United States v. Alaska's required evidence of congressional intent with an automatic rule that every described reservation includes all navigable beds against a future State.

**Other Governing Law:** The equal-footing rule supplies the presumption of state ownership of beds of navigable waters at admission, subject to valid federal reservation or disposition. The 1873 executive order, enacted 1888 and 1889 measures, Idaho admission legislation and accepted disclaimer, and eventual 1891 ratification have distinct legal dates and functions. The 1887 and 1889 agreements were not binding ratified treaties before statehood merely because negotiations were complete. [Idaho v. United States](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-262.pdf).

**Material Other Authority:** The district court's historical findings and the actual reservation instruments establish the relevant factual and legal context. The Ninth Circuit's inference concerning congressional intention is reviewable reasoning, and does not independently establish controlling law. Later governmental transactions can corroborate an earlier understanding but cannot substitute for timely authority.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** United States v. Alaska governs the two required showings and rejects reliance on a later executive order as the original legal withdrawal. Geographic inclusion is conceded here. The unresolved task is application of the second showing to Congress's own treatment of this particular reservation before July 3, 1890.

**Open Point:** Whether enacted authorizations to cross and purchase the defined reservation, read with the federal notice and admission instruments, sufficiently manifest retention of the unceded submerged estate despite the agreements' poststatehood ratification. United States v. Alaska supplies the test; it does not predetermine this different documentary record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Controlling Reasoning Below:** The lower courts treated the executive reservation's inclusion of the waters, Congress's knowledge of that inclusion, and enacted dealings with the reservation as sufficient evidence that the remaining submerged lands were retained for the Tribe. The authorized partial cession presupposed a defined estate from which the Tribe would convey only agreed portions.

**Petitioner's Strongest Supported Position and Requested Rule:** Idaho accepts executive inclusion but argues that Congress did not clearly defeat equal-footing title before admission. A promise in an unratified agreement, passage in only one chamber, and poststatehood ratification cannot satisfy the separate congressional requirement. The railroad and negotiation statutes need not be read as specific dispositions of submerged ownership. Idaho seeks application of the state-title presumption unless timely enacted law clearly does the necessary work.

**Respondents' Strongest Supported Position and Requested Rule:** The United States and Tribe contend that Congress knowingly treated the defined reservation, including its waters, as a protected tribal estate: it required consent and compensation for access and authorized purchase only of portions the Tribe would sell. The partial-cession process and admission disclaimer reinforced retention of what remained. They seek recognition that clear congressional intent can appear through enacted treatment of an identified estate and its contemporaneously understood boundaries without a formula expressly saying “submerged lands.”

**Necessary Limits and Alternative Grounds:** Respondents must establish more than geographic inclusion or the Tribe's dependence on water. Idaho must address the enacted prestatehood measures rather than treating all federal conduct as unratified negotiation. The 1891 ratification may confirm an existing reservation but cannot itself divest state title already acquired in 1890. No additional amicus position is necessary to the source-based dispute.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Paths:** A jurisdictional result based solely on the earlier private immunity litigation would be unsupported; this is the separate federal sovereign's suit. The unchallenged historical findings allow legal resolution without inventing a new trial dispute.

**Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Finding a clear timely federal retention requires affirmance of the trust-title judgment within the present reservation. Finding the enacted record insufficient requires reversal of that title judgment and proceedings consistent with Idaho's presumptive ownership. A remand for additional factfinding would require an identified material gap, not merely disagreement with the legal inference drawn from the existing documents.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance could hold that Congress's knowing, enacted treatment of a specifically defined reservation as property obtainable from the Tribe only by consent is sufficiently clear to retain its unceded navigable beds. It need not hold that every reservation description or Indian-land disclaimer defeats equal-footing title. Reversal could hold that these particular access and negotiation measures do not clearly address the future State's bed title, while preserving United States v. Alaska's actual reservation rules. Both paths must respect the difference between evidentiary confirmation after admission and a new postadmission divestiture.

**Uncertainty:** The complete trial exhibits and maps are unavailable. The reported findings establish inclusion and congressional notice, and Idaho does not challenge them. The judgment's actual reservation boundary controls; no replacement coordinate description should be invented from the general phrase “lower third.” Ownership does not by itself decide present fishing, environmental, taxation, or nonmember jurisdiction questions.

# Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education
* **Citation or Docket:** 185 F.3d 337; Fifth Circuit Nos. 97-30879 and 98-30132.
* **October Term:** 2000.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the lower-court judgment; merits disposition.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 2001, organizationally assigned.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Establishment Clause permits a public school to require an evolution disclaimer that expressly references biblical creation, disclaims any intention to dissuade students from that account, and affirms their freedom to retain beliefs taught by their parents?
* **Question Granted:** Review encompasses the question presented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit affirmance of an Eastern District of Louisiana judgment enjoining the mandatory disclaimer.
* **Relief Requested:** The school board seeks reversal and permission to implement its resolution; the parents seek preservation of the injunction.
* **Material Record:** On April 19, 1994, the board adopted a resolution requiring a statement immediately before evolution instruction in elementary and high-school classes. It described evolution as a scientific theory, said the instruction was not intended to influence or dissuade the biblical version of creation or any other concept, affirmed students' ability to retain parental beliefs, and encouraged critical examination. A previous attempt to place creation science in the curriculum had failed. Debate specifically addressed retaining the biblical reference despite concern that it favored Christianity. The challenged statement was the board's required message delivered through teachers, not a student's private observation. It required no prayer or spoken profession by students. These features must be assessed together rather than replacing the text with either a religious exercise or a purely general notice about freedom of conscience.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Parents of enrolled children sought prospective protection against a continuing curricular policy. The case concerns official speech and curricular use, not exclusion of a private speaker. A particular student's conversion, recitation, or punishment is not an assumed fact. The quoted disclaimer and adoption record permit legal review without deciding the truth of evolution or religious beliefs.
* **Relevant Dates:** Resolution April 19, 1994; appellate judgment August 13, 1999. Both precede the assigned event.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Any earlier controlling Establishment Clause decision must be applied within its scope.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 18, 2001.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Apply any earlier controlling Establishment Clause decision in Good News Club v. Milford Central School or Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle within its scope.

### Issue 1: Official curricular protection of religious belief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton makes purpose, effects, and institutional relationships evidence within the unified Lemon v. Kurtzman inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Government cannot adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit adherence through public authority, or govern theology. Its established permissible relationships and specific prohibitions retain their own limits. Settle v. Dickson County School Board permits genuine academic treatment of religion and curricular method requirements, while rejecting categorical religious exclusions disguised as pedagogy.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the governmental act, attribution, and function. Once prohibited governmental religious conduct is established, proof of successful religious persuasion or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Private expression and objective academic religious study remain distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause and the board's actual resolution. Epperson v. Arkansas and Edwards v. Aguillard supply the surviving distinction between curricular authority and tailoring instruction to religious doctrine; they must be applied consistently with the inquiry prescribed by Agostini v. Felton.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe establish limits on official school religious sponsorship. This disclaimer is not itself a prayer, so those holdings do not decide attribution and function by analogy alone.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Schools may teach scientific subjects and study religious explanations academically. An official message does not become private because it preserves individual freedom of belief. Conversely, mentioning the Bible is not categorically unconstitutional.
* **Open Point:** Whether this mandatory statement functions as protection of religious adherence against the school's scientific instruction or as a permissible explanation of the difference between instruction and compelled belief.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit found that the disclaimer's text and context protected a particular religious account and undermined its asserted purposes of critical thinking and intellectual freedom. It found an establishment defect without treating the statement as compelled participation in a formal religious exercise. Its historical reliance on independent purpose and effect tests cannot replace the unified inquiry prescribed by Agostini v. Felton.

The board's strongest position is that teaching a scientific account need not compel personal assent and that an official reassurance protects conscience without teaching creationism. It asks for a rule allowing nondisparaging acknowledgments of divergent beliefs alongside ordinary science instruction. It must explain the selective biblical reference and its placement only before evolution, and cannot equate governmental protection of a favored doctrine with every disclaimer of compulsory belief.

The parents argue that the State may not use curricular authority to preserve biblical belief against scientific instruction. They emphasize the mandatory speaker, selective subject, and adoption record. Their rule must distinguish objective comparative study, ordinary accommodation, and an evenhanded statement that students need not profess a belief. Motive alone does not determine every disputed educational choice, and hostility to religion is no lawful remedy.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance could identify the statement's actual official religious function and retain an injunction against the required text. A narrower ruling could invalidate the biblical-protection feature while leaving ordinary conscience notices and academic instruction available, with severability addressed under the resolution's structure. Reversal could regard the statement as a disclaimer of compelled belief, permitting this policy without requiring creationism instruction or granting parents control over the science curriculum.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The record supports a rule about mandatory curricular statements that officially protect a religious account from the implications of instruction. It does not support prohibiting references to religious origins, treating every curricular controversy as unconstitutional, or creating a universal viewpoint-neutrality requirement for teaching. Remand would be appropriate only for an identified material dispute or unresolved severability issue, not to solicit evidence of religious conversion. The injunction's exact implementation should preserve lawful teaching and private student expression.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

### Issue 1: Allocation of direct and habeas review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Kolster v. INS permits withdrawal of ordinary appellate review on conceded criminal-removability predicates while preserving independent habeas review of substantial legal or constitutional objections to the government's authority. Reno v. Goncalves confirms that a legal exclusion from eligibility is reviewable even though the eventual grant of relief is discretionary. Reyes-Hernandez v. INS distinguishes the specifically named commencement, adjudication, and execution acts from a universal jurisdictional prohibition covering every completed-order challenge.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The immigration-transition standards require identifying the actual statutory regime and preserving the distinction between direct review, habeas legality review, and discretionary relief. Habeas cannot be assumed available merely because another channel closes, but an independent habeas grant is not silently repealed by general review language.

* **Other Governing Law:** The version of §1252(a)(1) governing final removal orders and §1252(a)(2)(C)'s criminal-removability limitation, together with §2241, must be read as an integrated allocation of remedies. [Calcano-Martinez v. INS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-348.pdf).

* **Material Other Authority:** Felker v. Turpin preserves the importance of the distinct habeas jurisdictional source; it does not resolve all immigration procedure.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Kolster v. INS and Reno v. Goncalves reject treating eligibility as unreviewable discretionary balancing. They do not automatically convert an appellate petition into habeas, establish custody in every individual case, or grant immigration relief.

* **Open Point:** The precise effect of the permanent IIRIRA review provisions on these petitions and the extent of any remaining appellate jurisdiction over other categories of claims.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning Below:** The Second Circuit treated the criminal-removability provision as eliminating direct review of these petitions but not §2241 review of legal claims. It distinguished eligibility from favorable exercise of discretion. That reasoning is reviewable as lower-court analysis, and does not independently establish controlling Supreme Court law.

**Petitioners:** Congress should not be understood to have left an admitted resident without any court capable of correcting an unlawful eligibility rule. General restrictions on review do not clearly eliminate the independent habeas grant. Their preferred forum may be district court; alternatively, if habeas were eliminated, ordinary review must be construed to preserve the legal claims. They need not prevail on waiver equities to show the need for a lawful eligibility determination.

**Government:** The permanent review statute concentrates immigration challenges in a specified system and expressly removes judicial review for the conceded criminal category. A litigant should not circumvent that restriction by relabeling review as habeas. The eligibility issue concerns a discretionary form of relief, and Congress can narrow judicial intervention. Its concessions about threshold predicates and substantial constitutional issues need not concede jurisdiction over every statutory disagreement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold Path:** Determine the governing permanent-rule provisions and the conceded jurisdictional predicates. If some individual petition falls under a different transitional regime, address that petition separately rather than assume uniformity.

**Merits Paths:** The Court may sustain the direct-review bar while recognizing the independent, limited habeas channel; retain direct legal review under a narrower reading of the bar; or conclude that Congress displaced both routes and then confront the constitutional consequence. The third path cannot avoid Kolster v. INS and Reno v. Goncalves by citing discretionary relief in the abstract.

**Judgment and Relief:** Affirming direct dismissal with a preserved habeas route does not grant any waiver, authorize removal notwithstanding a lawful stay, or decide the separately filed habeas cases. Reversal of direct dismissal returns legal review to the appellate court, with eligibility and remedy still to be adjudicated. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Limit the rule to conceded criminal removability and a legal challenge to eligibility; reserve predicate disputes, factual review, and actual discretionary denials. **Material Uncertainty:** The record does not establish each individual habeas predicate; any intervening controlling decision must also be applied.

It expressly reserved advertising-only programs in otherwise independent markets. The record's common benefit, majority support, and absence of attribution were relevant but not independently sufficient.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Compelled financial support can implicate speech even if the payer need not utter the message and the disagreement is commercial rather than ideological. Identify the lawful associational purpose independently of the compelled message before judging germaneness.

* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment and the Mushroom Act provisions reproduced in the primary judicial source.

* **Material Other Authority:** Abood v. Detroit Board of Education and Keller v. State Bar of California concern compelled association for independent purposes; their germaneness reasoning cannot establish that advertising is germane to itself.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott protects the actual comprehensive program it adjudicated.

* **Open Point:** Whether the independent mushroom-market arrangement justifies compelled generic advocacy, and whether another properly preserved justification supports it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The district court treated generic agricultural promotion as governed by Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott. The Sixth Circuit distinguished the absence of comprehensive cooperative restrictions and held the compelled payments unconstitutional.

**Government:** Generic advertising promotes a common economic interest, can avoid free riding, and does not attribute the message to any particular producer or prevent brand-specific counteradvertising. Commercial disagreement should receive correspondingly limited protection. It also invokes government speech, subject to the preservation problem.

**United Foods:** The government cannot force competitors to fund another seller's preferred promotional message merely because officials or an industry majority regard it as beneficial. Its own claim of superior quality is at odds with generic promotion. The comprehensive collective-market justification in Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott is absent. Allowing the advertising program to supply its own associational purpose would remove any meaningful boundary on compelled promotional subsidies.

**Limits and Alternatives:** This is not a misleading-advertisement disclosure requirement imposed on the producer's own speech. Nor does objecting to the advertising establish a constitutional objection to every neutral economic assessment or government-funded message. Any independent government-speech argument must be both properly presented and factually supported rather than assumed from the statute's existence.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Threshold:** Decline the unpreserved government-speech theory or determine whether exceptional review is warranted without inventing control findings. **Merits:** Sustain the assessment by extending Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott to common-benefit promotion; distinguish that holding and invalidate the advertising requirement; or remand if a properly presented alternative genuinely needs further factual determination.

**Judgment and Remedy:** Upholding supports government enforcement within the adjudicated assessment. Invalidating defeats enforcement of the unconstitutional advertising obligation and permits appropriate prospective and monetary proceedings under the applicable remedial law. No automatic nationwide refund, damages award, or invalidation of unrelated council functions follows.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule requiring an independently sufficient collective purpose can preserve Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's actual cooperative program while preventing advertising from bootstrapping its own compelled association. A commercial-speech justification would need to explain why economic benefit and freedom to disagree sufficiently answer the compelled-payment burden. **Uncertainty:** The disposition of severable nonadvertising expenses and any monetary recovery needs the relevant accounting and remedial record.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Adair v. Sherman.

**Citation or Docket:** 230 F.3d 890; Seventh Circuit No. 99-1736. The district-court proceeding was No. 98 C 3946 in the Northern District of Illinois. No Supreme Court docket identified.

**October Term:** OT 2000.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; decision on review of the lower-court judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** June 27, 2001, organizationally assigned within the historical term calendar.

**Question Presented:** Whether issue preclusion bars a debtor's Fair Debt Collection Practices Act suit alleging that a creditor's attorneys fraudulently inflated collateral value in a proof of claim, when the debtor received notice but did not contest that valuation before confirmation of the Chapter 13 plan?

**Question Granted:** The question presented concerning issue preclusion. The grant does not itself resolve every possible limitation on a damages action based on filings in bankruptcy proceedings.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed dismissal for failure to state a claim. The district court relied on claim preclusion; the court of appeals used issue preclusion, reasoning that the debtor's opportunity to object before confirmation prevented a later attack on the valuation underlying an allowed secured claim. [Adair v. Sherman](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/230/230.F3d.890.99-1736.html).

**Relief Requested:** Adair seeks reinstatement of his Fair Debt Collection Practices Act damages action against attorney Michael Sherman and the law firm Sherman & Sherman. The defendants seek dismissal because the action challenges the valuation reflected in a claim that Adair allowed to proceed through confirmation without objection. No request for this Court to determine the vehicle's correct value, amend a bankruptcy plan, or award a specified amount of damages is established.

**Material Record:** In March 1997, Adair obtained a loan of $16,483.22 from First Midwest Bank to finance a 1995 Chevrolet Lumina. He filed a Chapter 13 petition in July. His proposed plan would pay allowed secured claims in full and a percentage of unsecured claims. On September 3, Sherman filed the bank's proof of claim, valuing the car at $19,841.43, an amount exceeding its original purchase price. Adair did not object before confirmation on September 15; the claim was treated as fully secured. These figures and the sequence are reported in the [Adair v. Sherman](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/230/230.F3d.890.99-1736.html).

Adair alleged that the defendants knowingly overstated collateral value to increase the portion of the bank's claim receiving full payment. He commenced an adversary challenge in June 1998, but that proceeding was dismissed when the Chapter 13 case was dismissed. The later FDCPA action alleged fraudulent collection practices. At the pleading stage, the allegations receive the required favorable treatment; neither the complaint nor the appellate account supplies a judicial finding that the defendants committed fraud. Adair's subsequent return to Chapter 13 did not itself prevent him from pursuing his own statutory claim. The distinction between alleged misconduct, actual collateral value, and the finality of a prior bankruptcy determination remains central.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The parties did not dispute that Adair received notice of the proof of claim before confirmation or argue that the interval left inadequate time to object. The appellate opinion expressly limited its reasoning to a claim filed sufficiently before confirmation to permit a timely objection. There is no identified adversarial valuation hearing or express finding resolving a contested valuation. The lower court nevertheless treated the allowed claim and confirmation as sufficient adjudication. That is a legal characterization to be tested, not an additional record fact.

The district court's claim-preclusion rationale and the appellate issue-preclusion rationale must be kept distinct. A conclusion about whether valuation was actually litigated does not automatically decide whether a particular later claim is independently barred by the scope of a final judgment. The court of appeals expressly declined to address whether an FDCPA claim can ever be predicated on a bankruptcy filing because the parties had not presented that issue. No general displacement or categorical litigation immunity may be inferred from its reservation. [Adair v. Sherman](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/230/230.F3d.890.99-1736.html).

### Issue 1: Uncontested claim allowance, confirmation, and issue preclusion

**Controlling Simulated Law:** Dodd v. Hood River County applies issue-specific preclusion where an issue was actually and necessarily determined, the relevant parties participated, and there was a full and fair opportunity to litigate. Its state-judgment and Oregon-law setting matters; it supplies no automatic federal bankruptcy answer. Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz enforces the enacted consequence of a failure to object to a claimed exemption within the prescribed period, even when the exemption lacked a colorable legal basis. That exemption-specific holding is binding within its own statutory and procedural conditions, without converting every unopposed bankruptcy allegation into a litigated factual finding.

Heintz v. Jenkins holds that attorneys regularly engaging in covered debt collection are not exempt from the FDCPA merely because their collection activity includes litigation. Coverage depends on the statutory definitions and does not itself establish a violation, damages, or the defeat of a properly supported defense. Hubbard v. United States preserves that holding while distinguishing the coverage and definitions of a different statute. Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates and Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank preserve the importance of the particular procedural mechanism and statutory entitlement rather than supplying a general equitable exemption from bankruptcy rules.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Dodd v. Hood River County requires actual, necessary, issue-specific adjudication; Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz enforces the prescribed objection deadline. Heintz v. Jenkins requires separate analysis of collector coverage, the alleged prohibited practice, causation or damages where required, and defenses. Neither decision establishes that every factual assertion in an unopposed preconfirmation proof of claim has been actually litigated.

**Other Governing Law:** The historical Bankruptcy Code provides that a proof of claim is deemed allowed unless a party in interest objects. Bankruptcy Rule 3001(f) gives a properly filed proof of claim prima facie evidentiary force. Confirmation binds debtor and creditors under the governing Chapter 13 provisions. Those consequences must be interpreted alongside, rather than treated as identical to, the elements of issue preclusion. The FDCPA separately regulates false or misleading collection representations and collection of amounts not authorized by agreement or law, subject to its definitions and defenses. The [Adair v. Sherman](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/230/230.F3d.890.99-1736.html) identifies the applicable provisions and their operation in this record; later amendments are not substituted for the event-date law.

**Material Other Authority:** The Seventh Circuit reasoned that an attack on valuation is an attack on the debt established through bankruptcy, not merely a challenge to an independent method of collection. It emphasized the available objection process and the finality necessary to administer confirmed plans. That rationale is the position under review, and does not independently establish a controlling holding. The existence of sanctions and statutory revocation procedures establishes possible procedural avenues subject to their own predicates; it does not establish that this debtor successfully invoked them or that any deadline should be extended.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Binding consequences may follow from an unopposed filing where enacted law assigns those consequences. Conversely, an opportunity to litigate is not textually identical to actual litigation. Dodd v. Hood River County and Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz preserve those propositions in distinct settings. Their reconciliation requires identifying which asserted effect is needed here: maintaining the allowed claim and plan, conclusively establishing a historical valuation for a separate action, or barring the later action as a whole.

**Open Point:** Whether claim allowance and confirmation, without an objection or contested valuation determination, satisfy the actual-litigation requirement for issue preclusion in a later FDCPA damages action. The cited precedents do not resolve either the requirement's application to this federal bankruptcy setting or a bankruptcy-specific exception based solely on adequate notice.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Adair's strongest position accepts that confirmation has operative consequences while denying that it adjudicated the truth of the valuation allegation through litigation. A proof of claim is a party's submission. Prima facie force and deemed allowance facilitate administration but do not demonstrate that a judge resolved a disputed factual issue. He seeks damages for allegedly deceptive conduct, requiring proof of the statutory violation; reinstatement would not itself revalue collateral or rewrite the prior plan. The fact that successful proof could contradict the earlier claim's valuation explains the relevance of preclusion, but does not eliminate a required element of that defense.

The defendants' strongest position emphasizes the practical and statutory meaning of confirmation. Adair could object with notice and did not. His complaint's theory depends on proving that the allowed secured valuation was wrong, and thus attempts to obtain through a separate suit what should have been pursued in bankruptcy. If a debtor can accept confirmation and later relitigate collateral value under a different statutory label, the prescribed objection process and reliance on confirmed plans lose force. They can distinguish ordinary default judgments by the integrated allowance and confirmation process and characterize the value as necessarily established by treatment of the claim as fully secured.

Each position has a limiting requirement. Adair cannot avoid genuine issue preclusion simply by alleging fraud or choosing an FDCPA label, nor can he claim that attorney coverage establishes liability. Defendants must explain why adequate opportunity supplies actual litigation, or identify an applicable rule that dispenses with that element. The actual judgment, plan terms, and procedural consequences must do that work; generalized disapproval of collateral litigation cannot silently merge issue preclusion, claim preclusion, and statutory finality.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm by holding that the specified bankruptcy process gives the uncontested valuation conclusive issue-preclusive effect after adequate notice and confirmation. That would require an express explanation of the claimed exception or the basis for treating this issue as actually adjudicated. It would preserve dismissal without adjudicating fraud on the merits.

The Court may vacate because opportunity alone does not satisfy actual litigation, leaving the district court to address the statutory claim and any distinct, properly preserved defense. That path does not order a revised plan, establish an FDCPA violation, or immunize the debtor from consequences that bankruptcy law independently assigns to confirmation. An alternative affirmance on a different ground would require both legal availability and an adequate record; the unpresented broad interaction between bankruptcy filings and the FDCPA cannot be invented as a decided ground below.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule addressing an uncontested collateral valuation in a preconfirmation proof of claim, where notice and an opportunity to object are conceded and no actual contested valuation adjudication is identified. A rule for deficient notice, contested valuation hearings, expressly litigated fraud, or the preclusive consequences of every bankruptcy order exceeds what this record establishes.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective land-use or takings ruling must be checked before finalizing this brief.

### Issue 1: Finality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency requires a definitive account of permitted use, not a futile transaction that cannot alter the agency's decision. Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council permits review of a finally rejected earlier categorical prohibition while preserving actual duration and valuation issues.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Finality and compensation merits are separate.

* **Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause as applied to the State and the actual coastal regulations reproduced in the judicial record.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified in this issue.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An agency must have settled the relevant use limits; an owner need not perform a meaningless act merely to ripen a claim.

* **Open Point:** Whether these denials and the undisputed upland allowance establish sufficient finality despite unsubmitted alternatives.

### Issue 2: Acquisition, complete deprivation, and contextual review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council requires complete deprivation of economically beneficial use of the relevant property for its categorical rule. A genuine background state property or nuisance limitation may establish that the asserted use was never within title; a newly enacted declaration of harm cannot do that alone. Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City remains the framework for partial deprivation.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Economic impact, investment-backed expectations, and the character of governmental action require contextual application. No loss percentage automatically establishes or defeats compensation.

* **Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause as applied to the State and the operative coastal regulations; the actual state-law content of title remains relevant to a properly supported background-law defense.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified in this issue.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The retained meaningful development use matters to the total-deprivation claim; notice of regulation may matter to expectations.

* **Open Point:** Whether notice categorically extinguishes a successor owner's constitutional claim, rather than informing a component of the contextual analysis.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The state court found the claim unripe, treated post-regulation title acquisition as fatal, and rejected total deprivation because of the upland value. It did not conduct an adequate independent Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City inquiry after treating acquisition as a bar.

**Palazzolo's Position:** More applications cannot change the Council's refusal to permit filling for a private purpose. A regulation cannot become an unchallengeable background title limitation merely through transfer of ownership. The rule would impair both successors and original owners trying to sell. Meaningful partial-loss review remains necessary even if the upland prevents categorical recovery. His strongest limited position does not require treating every post-regulation expectation as reasonable or accepting his proposed subdivision value as proved compensation.

**State's Position:** The large proposals do not establish what a smaller project could obtain, and the owner cannot demand compensation before the agency defines the actual burden. The existing restrictions shaped the interest acquired and the reasonableness of his expectations. The upland's value defeats total loss. Environmental protection and the natural condition of the marsh also bear on the asserted right to fill, although a general public-interest claim cannot substitute for an identified background property rule.

**Material Unresolved Matters:** The contextual compensation claim, applicable background limitations, actual valuation, and any duration issues remain distinct from the preserved threshold questions. The unpresented wetlands-only denominator theory is not an alternative route to a total-loss judgment here.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A finality dismissal is available only if a material permitted-use question remains genuinely unsettled. If the record is final, the Court can reject a categorical post-acquisition bar, reject the total-deprivation theory on the whole parcel, and remand for Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City review. That does not establish a taking or reinstate the requested $3,150,000 valuation. Alternatively, a demonstrated background limitation could defeat a claimed use through actual state property law; notice alone must not be silently substituted for that proof. The remedy should preserve valid coastal regulation while determining whether its particular burden requires compensation. The record does not establish the merits of the remaining contextual claim.

# Tyler v. Cain

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Tyler v. Cain

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-5961; 533 U.S. 656.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-28 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the reasonable-doubt rule invoked from Cage v. Louisiana has been made retroactive by the Supreme Court within §2244(b)(2)(A), and the litigated alternative request that the Court make Cage retroactive on collateral review. The first question concerns the statutory gate; the second raises the relationship between a new retroactivity determination and that gate.

* **Question Granted:** The statutory question and alternative retroactivity request are verified in the contemporaneous United States amicus brief and the opinion's discussion. The amicus formulation is not represented as the petition's exact wording or a separately verified grant-order reformulation. [United States amicus brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/media/233476/dl?inline=).

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit, judgment reported at 218 F.3d 744, affirmed denial of habeas relief because the successive-application condition was not established.

* **Relief Requested:** Tyler seeks consideration and relief on his jury-instruction claim; the warden seeks preservation of the denial under the statutory gate.

* **Material Record:** Tyler was convicted of second-degree murder after a 1975 shooting that killed his infant daughter. His conviction was affirmed. He filed several unsuccessful state postconviction petitions and an unsuccessful federal habeas application. After Cage v. Louisiana, he raised a reasonable-doubt challenge through another state petition, asserting a substantively identical instruction. Louisiana denied relief. In early 1997, the Fifth Circuit authorized a second federal application on a prima facie showing. The district court reached the merits, accepted retroactivity under circuit authority, but denied relief under the governing federal habeas standard. The court of appeals concluded that authorization did not relieve the district court of independently enforcing the statutory successive-application requirements.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of the final disposition after a petition was filed, not certiorari review of the circuit's initial authorization decision. The alleged constitutional defect, retroactivity under general doctrine, and satisfaction of the special successive gate are distinct questions. The case does not present new factual innocence evidence as an alternative statutory gateway.

* **Relevant Dates:** Original crime and trial, 1975; second federal filing, 1997, after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring an additional case module.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Check any earlier effective decision on AEDPA, retroactivity, or reasonable-doubt instructions. Neither the historical outcome here nor an unapproved same-term draft establishes retroactivity.

### Issue 1: The successive-application gate

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Felker v. Turpin preserves the statutory distinction between prima facie appellate authorization and actual district-court verification. The new-constitutional-rule exception requires a previously unavailable rule made retroactive on collateral review by this Court. Felker v. Turpin does not settle the precise meaning of “made retroactive.” Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal preserves a narrow exception to successive classification for a previously presented, expressly preserved premature competency claim; those circumstances are absent here.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Authorization is permission to file, not a final entitlement to merits adjudication. The statutory bar on review of authorization decisions does not automatically eliminate review of a later final judgment.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(2)(A), governing successive applications that invoke new constitutional rules made retroactive by the Supreme Court.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified in this issue.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering decisions preserve independent district-court gate verification and the requirement of an unavailable rule already made retroactive by this Court, without settling what Court action establishes that status.

* **Open Point:** Whether “made” requires an express retroactivity holding, permits holdings that necessarily entail retroactivity, or also permits lower courts to apply more general Supreme Court principles to a new rule.

### Issue 2: The asserted relationship between structural error and retroactivity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Sullivan v. Louisiana holds that a reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the jury's governing standard leaves no valid verdict for harmless-error review. It expressly reserves collateral-review gates. Schiro v. Farley treats ordinary nonretroactivity under Teague v. Lane as nonjurisdictional while preserving its substantive framework.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Structural-error treatment and collateral retroactivity answer distinct questions. A rule eliminating harmless-error review does not itself eliminate a separate statutory reopening condition.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2244(b)(2)(A), including its independent new-rule and Supreme Court retroactivity conditions.

* **Material Other Authority:** Cage v. Louisiana identifies the asserted instructional defect; Teague v. Lane provides the governing nonretroactivity framework.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Structural error and retroactivity answer different questions.

* **Open Point:** Whether the relevant existing holdings necessarily establish this rule's retroactive operation, rather than furnishing an argument for extending retroactivity to it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The district court treated the rule as retroactive under circuit precedent, then denied the constitutional claim under AEDPA's merits standard. The Fifth Circuit instead held that the application failed the antecedent statutory requirement, notwithstanding prior authorization.

**Tyler's Position:** Congress used “made,” not an express requirement that a single opinion contain a retroactivity declaration. The combined force of the Court's retroactivity principles and Sullivan v. Louisiana's recognition that the defect destroys the reasonable-doubt jury verdict establishes the necessary status. A contrary reading could make the gate depend on the chance availability of a separate vehicle rather than the Court's substantive law. His strongest limited argument requires a conclusion compelled by existing Supreme Court holdings, not merely agreement by lower courts that retroactivity would be desirable.

**Warden's Position:** Congress confined successive relief to rules whose retroactive effect the Supreme Court itself has established. Circuit precedent and predictions cannot satisfy that assignment. Sullivan v. Louisiana decided harmless error in a direct-review setting and explicitly does not decide collateral gates. The prima facie order cannot override the district court's independent obligation. The general principles of Teague v. Lane leave a further legal classification to be made and therefore do not alone establish the required Supreme Court action.

**Material Unresolved Grounds:** The merits of Tyler's exact charge and his entitlement under the separate federal habeas merits standard do not need resolution if the successive gate fails. Conversely, passing the gate would not itself establish that the instruction warrants a writ.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm for failure to establish the successive exception while allowing either an express holding or multiple holdings that necessarily entail retroactivity to qualify. It may instead conclude that the existing decisions establish retroactivity and remand for proper merits review. Treating the statutory question as dispositive does not authorize an acquittal, retry the case, or decide every structural error's retroactivity. A request that the Court newly make the rule retroactive raises a separate vehicle problem: the statutory question concerns the authority permitting this successive application, and an unnecessary statement about another future application would not be a holding. Any constitutional challenge to an unavailable meaningful review channel would require an actually presented claim rather than inference from the seriousness of the alleged trial error.

# Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma

* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 99-7791 and 00-38; 533 U.S. 678.

* **October Term:** OT2000.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2001-06-28 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether federal law authorizes continued detention of previously admitted noncitizens after the removal period when removal is not reasonably foreseeable, and what judicial review and release consequences follow.

* **Question Granted:** The post-removal detention question reconstructed from the two judicial records.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit, 185 F.3d 279, reversed an order releasing Zadvydas. The Ninth Circuit, 208 F.3d 815, affirmed Ma's release.

* **Relief Requested:** Zadvydas seeks relief from continued confinement; the government seeks authority to detain and reversal of Ma's release. Neither seeks invalidation of the underlying removal order in these custody proceedings.

* **Material Record:** Zadvydas entered the United States as a child and later incurred convictions leading to a removal order. Born in a displaced-persons camp in Germany, he was not accepted by Germany or Lithuania as a citizen. Efforts involving his wife's native country also failed. The government nevertheless continued attempts to arrange removal. Ma, admitted as a Cambodian refugee as a child, was ordered removed after an aggravated-felony conviction arising from a gang-related killing. Cambodia lacked a repatriation agreement with the United States. Both remained detained beyond the statutory 90-day removal period. Administrative review considered danger and flight concerns. The records do not equate difficulty arranging removal with a judicial finding that either person poses no danger.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** These are habeas challenges to custody authority, distinct from review of removal merits. The cases concern people previously admitted to the country, not the initial-entry setting. The relevant statute authorizes additional detention or supervised release after the removal period but supplies no express numerical outside limit in the quoted provision. Whether the executive's efforts show a realistic prospect of removal is record-specific.

* **Relevant Dates:** Zadvydas's habeas proceeding began in 1995; Ma's post-removal-period custody and habeas litigation occurred in 1999.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring an additional case module.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Precedent effective through June 29, 2000, subject to intervening decisions effective before June 28, 2001.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No additional controlling same-term decision is identified; any intervening decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier controlling decision in INS v. St. Cyr, Calcano-Martinez v. INS, or Seling v. Young must be applied within its scope.

### Issue 1: Custody review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. Goncalves and Kolster v. INS preserve meaningful habeas consideration of substantial legal questions within their respective immigration-review settings. Their specific removal provisions are not automatically custody bars. Felker v. Turpin distinguishes a withdrawn appellate route from retained independent habeas authority.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the precise statute, challenged government act, and retained forum before inferring preclusion.

* **Other Governing Law:** The federal habeas statute and the post-removal detention and supervision provisions reproduced in the primary source.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified in this issue.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The prior decisions preserve independent legality review within their actual statutory settings. They do not establish every detainee’s custody, forum, or respondent predicates automatically.

* **Open Point:** Their application to custody continuing after final removal orders.

### Issue 2: Authority, purpose, and duration

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. Flores sustains the particular temporary juvenile custody system while preserving individual unlawful-delay and conditions challenges. INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights sustains a bounded release condition within its statutory context, not limitless detention. Foucha v. Louisiana rejects continuing custody after its substantive basis disappears and rejects an indefinite scheme supported only by failure to prove harmlessness. Kansas v. Hendricks found the particular retrospective commitment punitive under its Ex Post Facto analysis; its non-dispositive discussion supplies no general authorization for preventive confinement.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The nature and duration of custody must remain connected to its lawful ground. Periodic procedures do not supply a missing substantive authority. Different preventive regimes require their own source, predicates, and safeguards.

* **Other Governing Law:** 8 U.S.C. §1231(a)(6), the related removal-period and supervision provisions, 28 U.S.C. §2241, and the Fifth Amendment’s due-process protection.

* **Material Other Authority:** Jackson v. Indiana supplies the purpose-and-duration principle retained in Foucha v. Louisiana.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** None of these precedents establishes a particular number of months for post-removal immigration custody.

* **Open Point:** Whether this removal statute supports indefinite preventive detention or instead contains a purpose-bound reasonable-duration limitation, and how a habeas court should implement that limitation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The Fifth Circuit accepted continuing custody if eventual removal was not impossible, the government acted in good faith, and administrative review continued. The Ninth Circuit required removal within a reasonable time and treated the absence of a Cambodian repatriation agreement as establishing that detention could not continue on Ma's record.

**Detainees' Position:** A removal order cannot become an open-ended substitute for criminal punishment or an independently authorized civil commitment. Once removal lacks a realistic foreseeable prospect, flight concerns cannot explain imprisonment to secure that removal. Administrative review focused on danger does not answer the absence of a removal purpose. Supervision preserves the removal order and permits controls without indefinite confinement. The strongest bounded position permits continued custody where concrete facts show removal is approaching or the detainee obstructs an otherwise available process under applicable law.

**Government's Position:** The statute expressly permits detention after 90 days and entrusts sensitive foreign-relations judgments to the Executive. Prior criminal conduct, danger, flight, and uncertainty about negotiations may justify custody while officials continue good-faith removal efforts. Lack of a formal agreement does not prove that return cannot occur. The strongest limited defense identifies concrete prospects and individual risks rather than treating the bare removal order as permanent authority.

**Unresolved Matters:** Neither impossibility in perpetuity nor a formal treaty is a necessary proxy for practical foreseeability. Any different national-security, terrorism, or specialized civil-commitment authority is outside these ordinary removal records unless actually invoked and established.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can preserve habeas jurisdiction, construe the detention provision in relation to removal, and require individualized factual assessment. The Fifth Circuit's impossibility threshold may demand too much from a detainee; the Ninth Circuit's apparent reliance on lack of an agreement may demand too little inquiry into actual negotiations. Vacating both judgments would permit the same lawful standard to govern both records without ordering unconditional admission or immediate release regardless of circumstances. A new numerical presumption is an additional doctrinal choice, not an existing rule. Relief can require supervised release when removal-purpose detention is no longer authorized, while preserving the removal order and lawful conditions. Independent preventive custody cannot be supplied by judicial invention.
