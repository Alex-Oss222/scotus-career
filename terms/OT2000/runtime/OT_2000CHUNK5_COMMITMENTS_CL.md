## Reach, public form, and common legal scope

All eight non-Stone Justices participate. The supplied state appellate judgment presents the federal tribal-immunity question for merits review. Its reconsideration after Kiowa is lower-court history; no earlier Supreme Court remand is inferred. The March 19, 2001 argument supports an ordinary signed plenary threshold opinion. That form follows the argued question and the need to explain the agreement's legal operation; authorship and opinion counts played no part in selecting it.

The contract's mandatory arbitration, final-award term, express judicial-entry provision, incorporated arbitration rules, and Oklahoma choice of law collectively express consent to the contemplated state confirmation proceeding. Clear waiver requires an unequivocal undertaking, not prescribed sovereign-immunity vocabulary. The phrase about a court having jurisdiction preserves ordinary forum requirements; reading it to preserve immunity against the very enforcement promised would deny the integrated judicial-enforcement undertaking its operation. This is an express agreement interpreted as a whole, not waiver inferred from commercial activity, breach, silence, or participation in an arbitration objection.

The common disposition is reversal of the immunity dismissal and remand for proceedings consistent with this limited waiver. The decision does not itself confirm every item of the award, decide its substantive validity, supply federal subject-matter jurisdiction, or authorize execution against any particular asset. Former 25 U.S.C. section 81 and signatory authority were not aired below and are outside the grant; the waiver construction does not conclusively resolve either. No default is inferred from the Tribe's refusal to participate in arbitration.

## Alternative-path comparison and join barriers

The strongest immunity-preserving path reads courts having jurisdiction as requiring an independent immunity waiver and treats ordinary arbitration language as insufficiently unequivocal. That path properly insists that commercial expectations cannot create jurisdiction. It fails here because the case involves an integrated undertaking expressly providing binding arbitration and judicial entry under an identified enforcement regime, rather than a mere promise to pay or an isolated arbitration obligation. All eight accept that distinction at this level of generality.

The strongest waiver objection is that enforcing this text without a literal immunity clause risks implied waiver. The operative limit answers it: only the express judicial enforcement promised by this actual agreement is recognized, and the result does not decide the effect of arbitration alone, unrelated claims, an unrestricted damages suit, execution, or absent signatory authority. First Options remains an analogy concerning genuine assent, not a new delegation holding based on incorporated rules. Kiowa, Santa Clara Pueblo, and Potawatomi retain their immunity and clear-waiver rules. No prior holding is overruled.

The three Kiowa dissenters need no judgment-only position because they independently accept the same sufficient express-waiver ground. No non-Stone separate writing is required to preserve an operative difference on the supplied question. Any proposed opinion going beyond these limits requires a new compatibility test.
