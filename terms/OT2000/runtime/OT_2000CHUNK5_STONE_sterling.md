## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Compelled intimate disclosure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal privacy question on the supported plaintiff-favorable facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether official leverage can compel an adult to disclose intimate information to family without a legitimate official purpose.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established confidentiality protection in Sheets v. Salt Lake County and adopt its bounded extension to a credible official ultimatum compelling intimate family disclosure. Stone adopts that extension here. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority preserves the distinction between confined justified administration and the unrelated use alleged here without itself deciding the extension.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An officer violates protected intimate confidentiality by imposing a credible official ultimatum requiring an adult to disclose suspected sexual orientation to a family member, or face the officer's disclosure, when no legitimate governmental purpose supports either course.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The present infringement is coerced surrender of confidentiality. Actual communication by the officer is unnecessary; an idle insult without an operative disclosure demand does not fall within the rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Authority acquired through arrest remains connected to lawful public purposes. The supported ultimatum uses that authority to control an adult's private relationship with his family. A religious admonition supplies no policing justification. Disputed admissions do not justify assuming that the information was already public.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Emergency protective disclosure requires facts absent here; a general right to secrecy about evidence of criminal conduct is outside the question presented.

### Issue 2: Notice and individual responsibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply legal immunity separately to each supported role, keeping factual-sufficiency contentions outside this appeal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the particular official ultimatum was sufficiently apparent as unconstitutional in April 1997.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing objective notice test; adopt the conclusion that established confidentiality protection plus the complete absence of an official disclosure purpose provided adequate notice. Stone adopts this notice conclusion on the stated facts despite the contrary reading of the precedents.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An identical orientation-and-threat case is unnecessary where the supported conduct deliberately compels the surrender of clearly protected intimate information for no official purpose. The demand's operative coercion supplies the connection that a bare future-threat theory lacks.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Denial of immunity concerns the supported ultimatum, not every discussion of personal matters. No later opinion supplies retrospective notice, and the officer's subjective awareness alone is insufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County was governing Supreme Court law before the conduct. It protected objectively intimate information against unjustified official exposure after access for a limited investigation, while expressly preserving separate conduct-date immunity analysis. Its completed public dissemination is materially different from a family-directed ultimatum. Stone would nevertheless treat the present use of arrest authority to compel disclosure as an apparent invasion of the same protected confidence: officials cannot make the citizen perform the very surrender they have no official reason to impose. That is a reasoned extension, not a claim that Sheets v. Salt Lake County already resolved threats. The earlier circuit authorities reinforce the required connection to actual official need: United States v. Westinghouse Electric Corp. addressed intimate medical information; Fraternal Order of Police v. City of Philadelphia addressed justified employment inquiries and disclosure safeguards; the 1995 circuit opinion in Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority evaluated an actual health-plan monitoring interest. The later Supreme Court ruling in that case supplies no April 1997 notice. Walls v. City of Petersburg presents a substantial contrary argument, but Stone would distinguish its employment-screening inquiry from an arrest-based command to disclose to family for no policing purpose. Ordinary future threats likewise differ from operative compelled self-disclosure. The eligible Supreme Court rule, local confidentiality authority and complete lack of an official purpose support his notice conclusion; neither the newly announced extension nor subjective bad faith alone does so. Personal involvement, causation and compensable injury remain for proof. [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/232/190/514967/); Sheets v. Salt Lake County; Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Hoban's actual participation requires fact adjudication unavailable in this interlocutory legal appeal.
