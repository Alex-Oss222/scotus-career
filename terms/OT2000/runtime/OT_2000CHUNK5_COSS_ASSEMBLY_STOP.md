## Already-supported alternatives tested

1. **The circulated combined mandate.** Preserving the consideration and trial-error determinations, declining a present contrary no-effect conclusion and providing lower appellate preservation review answers part of the affirmers' objection. It does not supply their assent to suspending the existing conditional relief for a mandatory review-history inquiry. The exact mandate therefore remains unsupported by a majority.
2. **The narrower causal-preservation return.** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg accept an alternative directed to whether a genuinely pursued causal objection may be heard and, if so, to its proper appellate treatment. They permit review-history work only when an identified, actually unresolved ordinary habeas requirement makes it necessary. Removing the unconditional inquiry would remove a fixed part of Stone's approved remedy. Breyer has not agreed to abandon his first-effective-forum inquiry. The three available alternatives are not three cast votes for a newly specified vacatur and do not establish a common mandate.
3. **Combining reversal and vacatur as removal of the lower judgment.** The four reversal positions would defeat the present conditional relief on channel or no-effect grounds; the two vacatur positions reopen specified inquiries. Those are materially different mandates. Agreement that the lower judgment should not remain in its present form supplies no majority for either operative disposition.
4. **Combining affirmance and vacatur as further proceedings.** Continuing the existing conditional direction differs from vacating it pending new mandatory review. Common words about a return to a lower court do not erase that difference.
5. **A partial common proposition as a substitute for judgment.** Present-custody construction, the distinction between consideration and increased punishment, preservation of independent lawful custody, and opposition to present no-effect adjudication do not settle the operative judgment. No partial agreement supplies the absent mandate. O'Connor's and Kennedy's reservations, and Breyer's reservation, are not affirmative exception votes. No controlling indirect-review rule or Marks synthesis follows.

There is no already-supported alternative exact judgment and mandatory remedy that satisfies the fixed core and commands a majority. No missing Stone component is identified: the supplement is complete, but its approved disposition does not presently assemble with the reconciled Associates' positions into a majority judgment. The standing fallback cannot replace an express controlled position.
