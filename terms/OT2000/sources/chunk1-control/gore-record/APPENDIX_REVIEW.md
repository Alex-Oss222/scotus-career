## Verification by live factual issue

**Physical counting standards.** The appendix contains relevant party allegations and judicial characterizations, but no reproduced evidentiary testimony of Burton or Leahy and no county-by-county authenticated ballot-protocol collection. Sauls's ruling, 62a?63a, finds Palm Beach acted within its discretion, complied with its circuit court's order, and used lawful standards; it also says the standards changed from those of 1990. Bush's December 6 brief, 110a/original 45?46, characterizes the 1990 guideline as excluding indentations or dimples, and expressly attacks differing county standards and selected county or precinct recounts under equal protection and due process. That is Bush's characterization of the findings, not the guideline itself.

The Broward third-party complaint supplies considerably more physical detail, expressly as allegations: an initial two-detached-corner rule (paragraph 15); advice on November 19 to determine clear intent rather than retain that rule (17); unresolved standards for set-aside ballots (20?21); light, dimple depth, other marks, and party-pattern tests (22); divergent member practices (24?27); differential treatment of ballots with a dimple and a punched presidential choice (29?30); and a Broward/Palm Beach difference (32). It does not establish these propositions as adjudicated facts. Wells's dissent, 43a?44a, discusses apparent county disagreement on a dimple where other contests were fully punched. Its language is a dissenting assessment, not a majority finding.

**Palm Beach 1990, light test, and subsequent rule.** The appendix does not reproduce the 1990 guideline, trial exhibit P.E.46, or the cited Burton testimony. Its detailed light-test allegation at 113a refers to Broward; it cannot be reassigned to Palm Beach. The quoted chairman's description of Palm Beach in paragraph 15 is an adversary's pleaded report. The full Palm Beach sequence therefore requires testimony or the original rule, rather than merging these distinct passages.

**Undervotes and overvotes.** The December 8 majority's remedy concerns undervotes not already manually tabulated; it does not order inspection of all machine overvotes. Wells, 38a?39a footnote 26, questions why machine error would be possible for undervotes but not overvotes. That question is not proof of a specific recoverable overvote or its number. Harding, 53a footnote 33, describes 177655 statewide no-votes, with 28492 in Miami-Dade and 29366 in Palm Beach, citing state exhibits 40?41. The exhibits themselves are not attached, and this category must not be relabeled as undervotes. The Broward pleading's dimple/punch allegations likewise do not establish the validity of any specific machine overvote.

**Miami-Dade partial 168.** The December 8 majority, 24a?25a, says the county began a manual recount of all ballots, then stopped; roughly 9000 of 10750 undervotes remained unreviewed. Its recitation reports 436 additional legal votes from 20 percent of precincts representing 15 percent of votes cast, yielding a net 168 for Gore. At 29a it specifies 302 Gore and 134 Bush. Sauls's ruling, 59a?60a/original 7?8, describes a three-precinct one-percent sample and 136 additional precincts out of 635, plus the requested examination of separated machine undervotes. These are descriptions of the incomplete recount and requested remedy. The appendix contains no precinct-by-precinct ballot comparison or complete dataset proving how the partial all-ballot recount and remaining undervotes compare.

**Palm Beach 215 or 176.** The majority at 4a footnote 6 expressly declines to determine which figure is accurate and directs the trial court to determine it on remand. Its 29a footnote 19 repeats Bush's 176 assertion. The body uses 215 while preserving that qualification. Neither figure is established here as a finally resolved tally.

**Preservation and clarification.** The November 30 answer, 120a?122a/original 13?16, raises federal equal protection and due process objections to selected counties and later-changed standards. The December 6 brief's reproduced passages, 109a?110a/original 26?27, 43, 45?46, raise different county standards as well as partial geographic coverage. Thus the appendix supports that those categories of objections were presented before the December 8 decision; it does not by itself decide legal sufficiency of preservation or the precise scope of a later claim. The November 21 opinion's footnote 10, 74a, says neither party challenged the constitutionality of the election laws in that earlier appeal; it should not be converted into a statement that no constitutional objection was ever raised in the contest.

The December 7 clarification is present. The December 8 majority's footnote 7, 5a?6a, calls the contest-statute-inapplicability claim a substantial change after argument and contrasts earlier representations, including counsel's agreement at December 7 argument that contest proceedings involved judicial determination and appellate review. The appendix does not include the full argument transcript, the actual leave motion, a separate order granting leave, or a service receipt. Its reproduction and judicial discussion establish the paper's existence and consideration in that stated sense; they do not settle every acceptance, timing, waiver, or preservation issue. Sauls's ruling at 64a also says remaining portions of pending dismissal motions were denied for expedition and legal review.

**Finality.** Appendix B is a December 4 final judgment. Appendix A reverses and remands in part, directs immediate counting, permits further necessary relief, leaves the Palm Beach number for determination, and bars rehearing (33a?34a and footnote 6). These establish procedural posture relevant to a finality analysis. The appendix neither reproduces 28 U.S.C. section 1257 nor adjudicates the federal review-finality question. No later final trial judgment is attached.

**Feasibility and deadlines.** The majority, 33a footnote 22, expresses confidence that officials working over the weekend can complete the remaining undervotes in the required time. Footnote 21, 32a, acknowledges practical difficulty may control. Wells, 47a?48a, contests feasibility once ballot objections and judicial review are included. Harding, 55a?56a, asserts that a statewide no-vote review cannot be adequately completed, and characterizes December 12 as agreed by the parties. The November 30 motion excerpt, 126a/original 44, asserts impossibility of a statewide or substantial recount within section 5's time constraints. These are conflicting judicial assessments or party assertions, not a quantified completion model, signed timing stipulation, or completed-count evidence. This appendix does not establish impossibility or guaranteed completion of a later specified protocol.
