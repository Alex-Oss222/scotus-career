## Testimony with material qualifications

1. Burton identified himself as chair of Palm Beach's three-member canvassing board. He said the statutory clear-intent standard guided the board throughout. He identified exhibit 46 as the November 6, 1990 policy used at the beginning of the one-percent sample. Counsel read its exclusion of a fully attached chad bearing only an indentation; Burton agreed. He said the policy existed on election day, while noting the board had not had much comparable activity since 1990. The original exhibit image was not reviewed here.
2. During the sample, the board briefly counted indentations through which light could be seen. Burton became concerned about multiple presidential impressions with a pinhole of light through only one. The board returned to the 1990 guidelines and redid the first precinct. He was not certain of every date supplied by counsel. The temporary light test was not the full-recount standard.
3. He identified exhibit 39 as Labarga's November 15 order rejecting per-se exclusion and leaving the board discretion to accept or reject ballots according to intent. He agreed that the board abandoned the categorical rule. He identified exhibit 40 as the November 16 policy: two-corner hanging or partial punches may count, while one-corner punches and dimples may also count if clear intent appears. This policy preceded the full manual recount. The exact exhibit images were not inspected during this review.
4. Burton sought consistency with what he understood Broward was doing. Gore's counsel objected to his foundation, and the court allowed cross-examination. This does not independently prove Broward's countywide practices.
5. He described at least two counters, observers from both parties, and referral to the three-member board whenever an observer questioned a ballot. The counters principally displayed ballots. He estimated about 14500 board-reviewed questioned ballots and believed their rulings were announced and transcribed.
6. During the full recount the board examined the entire ballot. It rejected an isolated presidential dimple when all other choices were fully punched; it counted patterns of dimples or pinholes as evidence of intent. His possible explanations for such patterns, including misplaced ballots or punching through numbers, were inferences rather than witnessed voter behavior.
7. The pattern approach was refined during the full recount: every other choice no longer had to be dimpled. Burton accepted an example with fifteen punches and five unpunched dimples. The testimony does not identify the precise time of this refinement, each affected ballot, or a re-review of all previously assessed ballots under the refined approach.
8. A fully punched presidential choice controlled over dimples elsewhere in that presidential column, regardless of dimple prominence. This does not establish that a machine overvote with two fully punched choices was counted or could be salvaged. He also described many ballots with no presidential marks but votes in other races, without supplying a count.
9. He said all board members believed their practice complied with Labarga's November 22 clarification. He did not know every subsequent litigation event. He described a November 24 presentation of witnesses and affidavits. His recollection does not decide waiver or preservation.
10. He estimated completion of the earlier full manual recount ninety minutes to two hours after 5 p.m. Sunday, and acknowledged moving faster near its end. This is evidence about that November recount, not a completion estimate for a later statewide protocol.
11. On cross, he said he had heard the 215 net figure but had not personally seen the final tally and did not know whether he had seen the filed answer. He did not dispute counsel's approximate 3300 disputed-ballot number but had no independent count. Neither exchange resolves the later 215-versus-176 dispute.
12. Cross-examination described segregated objections at each precinct's end. Redirect supplied a material qualification: in the last several precincts, whose number and precise timing he did not remember, counsel's objections were treated as preserved without physically separating every ballot, to save time. Retain this when discussing the identifiable ballot population and the mechanics of further review.
13. Burton initially opposed an immediate full recount without planning and legal advice, but ultimately joined a unanimous decision to proceed. His statement to Klock that machines worked properly relied on the supervisor's opinion rather than his own technical expertise.

## Source limits

The witness slice contains two inaudible markers, two off-microphone markers, seven crosstalk markers, and eight phonetic-name markers. The inaudible gaps occur in an introductory question about party registration and the objection to his Broward knowledge. The off-microphone gaps occur after direct examination and during a proposed observer intervention. The recorded exchanges establish the described counting sequence without filling those gaps.
