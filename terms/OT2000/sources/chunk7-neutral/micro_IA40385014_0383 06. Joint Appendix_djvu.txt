FOR A COMPLAINT, THE PLAINTIFF ALLEGES: 
JURISDICTION 


1. The Good News Club, Andrea Fournier, and 
Darlene Fournier bring a civil rights action for 
injunctive relief pursuant to 42 USC §1983 and 42 
USC §2000BB. 


2. Plaintiffs invoke the jurisdiction of the Court 
pursuant to 28 USC § 1331. 


PARTIES 


3. The Good News Club (hereinafter “GNC”) is an 
unincorporated association with its principal place 
of business located in the County of Otsego, the 
State of New York. 


C2 


4. Andrea Fournier is a member of the GNC and 
resides in the County of Otsego, the State of New 
York. 


5. Darleen Fournier is the mother of Andrea 
Fournier and isin charge of the organization of the 
GNC; Ms. Fournier resides in the County of 
Otsego, the State of New York. 


6. The Defendant, Milford Central School (herein 
after “Milford”), has its principal piace of business 
located in the County of Oneonta, the State of New 
York. 


BACKGROUND 


7. GNC is a youth organization that promotes and 
teaches children Christian values. 


8. GNC is non exclusive and is open to any child 
between the ages 5 and 12. : 


9. GNC is not affiliated with any church and does 
not teach any particular Christian sect’s doctrine 
or theology. 


10. At GNC meetings, the children hear a Bible 
story (in a style that has application to their young 
lives), play games, learn scripture, and sing songs. 


11. On or about September 22, 1996, GNC 


C3 


submitted a request to use the Milford School 
cafeteria every Tuesday during the School year 
between the hours of 3:00 PM to 4:00 p.m. 


12. The request was submitted by Ms. Darleen 
Fournier as the representative in charge of the 
organization. 


13. On or about September 22, 1996, Dr. Robert 
McGruder, superintendent of Milford Central 
School, called Ms. Fournier and informed her that 


_ GNC would not be permitted to use school facilities 


because it was school policy that “religious groups 
could not use the school building.” 


14. In a letter dated October 3, 1996, Dr. 
McGruder formally deny GNC’s request to use 
school facilities because GNC’s acivities were “the 
equivalent of [sic] religious worship,- which is 
prohibited under [Milfcrd School] policy.” 


15. Milford allows the Girls Scouts to use school 
facilities. 


16. Milford allows the Boys Scouts to use school 
facilities. 


17. Milford allows Ostego County 4-H to use 
school facilities. 


18. Milford allows a local little league to use school 


C4 


facilities. 


19. Upon information and belief, in its entire 
history, Milford has never refused the use of school 
facilities to any youth organization except plaintiff. 


First Cause of Action -- Violation of the Freedom 
of Speech 


20. Plaintiff repeats and realleges 41- 419 as if fully 
set forth herein. 


21. By denying use of school facilities to GNC 
because of the content and nature of its speech, 
defendant violated plaintiff's Constitutional right to 
Freedom of Speech. 


Second Cause of Action -- Violation of RFRA 


22. Plaintiff repeats and realleges {1- 419 as if fully 
set forth herein. 


23. By denying use of school facilities to GNC 
because of the content and nature of its speech 
and the religious value which it promotes, 
defendant has discriminated against plaintiff and 
has violated plaintiffs rights under the Religious 
Freedom Restoration Act. 


Third Cause of Action -- Violation of the Equal 
Protection Clause 


CS 


24. Plaintiff repeats and realleges {1- 419 as if fully 
set forth herein. 


25. By denying use of school facilities to GNC 
because of the content and nature of its specch 
and its religious identity, defendant has 
discriminated against plaintiff and has violated 
plaintiffs rights under the Equal Protection clause. 


WHEREFORE, the Good News Club asks this 
honorable court to issue an injunction ordering 
Milford Central School to permit GNC to use its 
facilities to conduct its meetings, to cease and 
desist from further violating GNC’s the 
constitutional rights and rights under the 
Religious Freedom Restoration Act, and to paid 
GNC’s attorney’s fees together with the cost of this 
litigation; GNC also requests further and different 
relief that the court deems just, proper, and 
necessary to remedy the harm that it has suffered.

In general, to obtain a_ preliminary 
injunction, plaintiffs must show that they are likely 
to suffer possible irreparable injury if the 
injunction is not granted and either a likelihood of 
success on the merits of their case or sufficiently 
serious questions going to the merits to make them

Stay governmental action taken in the public 
interest pursuant to a statutory or regulatory 
scheme, the District Court should not apply the 
less rigorous fair-ground-for-litigation standard and 
should not grant the injunction unless the moving 
party establishes, along with irreparable injury, a 
likelihood that he will succeed on the merits of his 


claim. Plaza Health Labs, Inc. v. Perales, 878 Fed

In Deeper Life, a similar factual scenario was 
at issue. A New York school, acting pursuant to 
New York Education Law Section 414 governing the 
use of school facilities, denied after-hours use of 
the school building to a religious organization 
planning to conduct religious activities at the 
facility. Although there are factual issues in this

stay governmental action taken in the public

scheme. Thus, if the Court finds that the 
possibility of irreparable harm exists, it will then 
turn to whether this case presents fair ground for 
litigation.

There's no question that the defendant can 
limit the use of its facilities and that it need not 
permit even those uses provided for in Section 414 
of New York's Education Law. See Lamb's Chapel] 
versus Center Moriches School District, 508 US

In the instant case, it's undisputed that the

namely, the purpose of instruction in any branch

given permission to use the defendant's facilities. 
Clearly, as demonstrated by the exhibits

purpose and/or the content of these groups is to 
teach young people proper habits and values. The 
Supreme Court instructs, quote, "With respect to 
public property that is not a designated public 
forum open for indiscriminate public use for 
communicative purposes, control over access to a 
nonpublic forum can be based on subject matter 
and speaker identity so long as the distinctions 
drawn are reasonable in light of the purposes 
served by the forum and are viewpoint neutral." 
Lamb's Chapel] at 392, 393. However, if the request 
for use by the GNC would not have been denied for 
any reason other than the fact that the meetings

would have been from a religious perspective, the 
denial is impermissible because it is not viewpoint 
neutral. Lamb's Chapel at 393-394. [9] More 
recently, the Supreme Court elaborated when it 
stated, quote, “When the Government targets not 
subject matter but particular views taken by 
speakers on a subject, the violation of the First 
Amendment is all the more blatant. Viewpoint 
discrimination is thus an egregious form of content 
discrimination. The Government must abstain 
from regulating speech when the specific 
motivating ideology or the opinion of the speaker is 
the rationale for the restriction.” Rosenberger at 
2516. Thus, there is a distinction between 
content-based discrimination, which is permissible 
if it preserves the purposes of a limited forum, and 
viewpoint discrimination, which is impermissible 
when directed against speech that otherwise is 


within the purposes of the forum. Rosenberger at 
2517. 


In the instant case, the central dispute

of the plaintiffs’ GNC meetings. Is the content 
religious, as alleged by the defendant? Or is it 
non-religious but presented from a religious 
viewpoint, as alleged by the plaintiffs? Both sides

Chapel and Rosenberger are or are not controlling 
thick. The Court need not decide this issue today 
Rather, the Court holds that Lamb's Chapel ond 
Rosenberger pinpoint the critical issue in this case;

issue presents fair ground for litigation as it can 
only be resolved after full and complete proof of the 
nature of the GNC’s programming. See Jack Kahn 
Music C ; Baldwin Pi ; 


Organ Company, 604 Fed 2d 755, 763.

plaintiffs, although they have not yet used the 
defendant's facilities, will, in the absence of 
injunctive relief, be left without a viable meeting 
place. It has been alleged that the defendant has 
discontinued the practice of busing GNC members 
to the former location. Thus, without 
transportation, it may be impractical for the group 
to meet. In addition, the fear that the defendant 
has expressed of confusion of the young non- 
member students at Milford is sufficiently 
minimized by the fact that the meetings are not 
being held during school hours and in a location 
other than the classrooms. Thus, there is less 
chance that the students will witness the meetings 
or mistakenly perceive the meetings as a part of 
their state sponsored curriculum. See, Lamb's 
Chapel and Deeper Life previously cited.

681. Notably, in Deeper Life, although somewhat 
factually dissimilar from the instant case, the 
Second Circuit found that there was fair ground for 
litigation and the balance of the hardships tipped 
decidedly in favor of the plaintiff such that 
injunctive relief was properly imposed.
