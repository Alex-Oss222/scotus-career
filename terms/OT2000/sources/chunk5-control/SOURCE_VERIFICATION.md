### Daniels v. United States

[Official report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-374.pdf), 532 U.S. 374–393; No. 99-9136; argued January 8, 2001; decided April 25, 2001. All twenty PDF pages, including syllabus, O'Connor's entire opinion and footnotes, Scalia's concurrence in part, Souter's dissent and Breyer's dissent, were read.

The supplied comparator accurately states the 5–4 affirmance. O'Connor, Rehnquist, Kennedy and Thomas joined throughout; Scalia withheld the rare-circumstances discussion. The potential faultless-unavailability exception was expressly left unresolved, not adopted by four or five Justices. The majority preserved a complete-denial-of-required-counsel exception, with ordinary procedural default requirements, generally including presentation at federal sentencing. Scalia's additional ground was that §2255 cannot authorize this non-Gideon collateral inquiry at all; his suggested alternative was a remedy in the rendering jurisdiction, not a §2255 exception. Souter, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, disputed the nontextual finality bar and explained why Custis's forum limitation should not leave no forum. Breyer would reconsider Custis itself because the simpler challenge at sentencing better reflected congressional intent and avoided the later complexity. These are different grounds, not interchangeable votes for a general exception.

The record recites counsel's concession that no external force prevented earlier challenge and that the defendant could have pursued the claims while in custody. The absence of a missing plea transcript does not by itself establish a faultless inability to seek earlier review. Post-divergence Custis and Parke propositions in the historical opinion must be tested against actual entering law, not automatically imported.
