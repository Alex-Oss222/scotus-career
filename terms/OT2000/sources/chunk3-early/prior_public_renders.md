#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents from the Article I holding and resulting remand. They distinguish a valid federal employment duty from a private action against a nonconsenting State's treasury and expressly seek reconsideration of Florida Prepaid's Article I rule and the Union Gas power conclusion to the necessary extent. They do not claim that a patent-only limitation already governs lower courts or that ordinary age-employment regulation lies outside commerce.

They also reject §5 as an independent basis for these private remedies. Gregory permits rational age classifications, including imperfect generalizations, whereas the ADEA generally demands more than constitutional rationality. Its bona fide occupational qualification requires reasonable necessity to normal business operation; a reasonable factor other than age does not authorize treatment because of age. Good-cause discipline remains distinct. A protected seniority system must be bona fide and not intended to evade the Act, and cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement of a protected individual. For each benefit or benefit package under a bona fide employee benefit plan, the actual payment made or cost incurred for an older worker must be no less than that for a younger worker, as permissible under 29 C.F.R. §1625.10 as in effect on June 22, 1989; the separate alternative is a voluntary early-retirement incentive plan consistent with the Act's purposes; neither excuses failure to hire, and benefit plans cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement. The employer bears the stated burden under those seniority and benefit provisions. The foreign-workplace defense requires that compliance violate the foreign country's law and supplies no domestic exception. No defense is found established.

The four assess the identified governmental complaints, federal-employment newspaper reports, private-sector findings and California survey, principally concerning public-safety age limits, as insufficiently connected to unconstitutional state practices to support this remedy's breadth. They do not infer constitutional validity from state-law permission. The identified recitations are not an exhaustive congressional record, and the dissent does not conclude that Congress heard no evidence. Boerne requires substantive fit; their objection does not impose formal findings, a number of violations, geographical evidence, a sunset or an individual constitutional-tort prerequisite. Florida Prepaid's preventive property remedy presents, for them, a different protected interest and remedial gap; permitting prevention beyond constitutional violations does not itself settle this statute's fit.

Justice Thomas, joined by Kennedy, additionally dissents from statutory authorization. They distinguish express State-employer coverage from unmistakable authorization of this private federal action. Section 626(c) can be read to supply the ADEA's own cause while §626(b) imports FLSA remedial and procedural incidents. The original 1967 incorporation, the 1974 amendment of §216(b), the later removal of §255's incorporation and the competent-court language leave insufficient clarity in their view. They require no universal single-section formula, but regard this statutory ambiguity as independently sufficient to affirm; their constitutional objections remain alternatives if authorization is assumed.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The dissenters join Parts I–II, including the rejection of express displacement and the availability of independently established actual conflict. They would reverse and remand for ordinary adjudication of the actual tort claim because the statutory and regulatory scheme does not sufficiently establish protection of these compliance choices against the common-law liability Congress expressly saved.

Cipollone requires the actual statutory language and predicate duty; Myrick requires an affirmative federal premise; Medtronic and the particular Shanklin arrangement require the substantive counterpart claimed to displace the state obligation. The options and phase-in genuinely advance innovation and public acceptance, but permission to satisfy national requirements in several ways does not itself establish immunity. The absence of an airbag-omission command or fixed mix is not an exclusive physical-impossibility test; it bears on whether the asserted protected choice has been shown here. The agency's safety purpose, encouragement of airbags and own reliance on potential liability for deficient systems reinforce the statutory reservation.

The dissent confronts the Court's concern that widespread airbag duties would influence manufacturers and hasten the transition. That practical pressure does not, in its view, demonstrate legally protected exclusivity under this saving provision. It does not deny that damages can impose duties or that an options rule could produce a demonstrated conflict in another application. It would remove this defense without giving it a new evidentiary trial, awarding damages, finding a defect or adding a different vehicle-specific theory. Its preserved-liability conclusion is not the Court's holding.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas, dissents from affirmance while joining Parts I and IV.** Boerne permits prevention, but ordinary patent liability includes innocent infringement and circumstances with adequate process. Patent property and the usefulness of a federal remedy do not establish a sufficiently fitted response to deprivation without due process. National uniformity and equal treatment cannot independently supply §5 authority. The objection concerns substantive fit, not a mandatory quantity of evidence, violation in every jurisdiction, formal findings, geographic limits, or sunsets. The dissent does not find Florida's untested claims-bill or takings routes adequate and does not require exhaustion.

The dissent joins Part IV. On consent, a clear federal declaration states Congress's intent rather than Florida's unequivocal submission. Continuing lawful commerce, even an optional undertaking, differs from accepting a specified federal benefit or affirmatively invoking judicial authority. Actual waiver, properly conditioned grants, and interstate compacts remain separate, including their genuine coercion questions. The dissent does not adopt an automatic exemption for activities serving public purposes or deny substantive federal regulation of the patent conduct.

The dissent would vacate and remand for ordinary treatment of the preserved actual-submission contentions and any other properly available current-law ground before final immunity dismissal. Appearance, transfer, and affirmative relief are not interchangeable or already adjudicated consent. The dissent opposes the Court’s Article I extension: substantive Patent Clause authority and unmistakable statutory intent do not, on its structural view, establish power to impose a nonconsensual direct-State suit. All four would reject that power and overrule Union Gas if the question required decision; their shared limited remand leaves that overruling undecided. The surviving Union Gas conclusion cannot be silently displaced through the mandate; actual submission may resolve the case without that further constitutional decision. Fitzpatrick’s Section 5 power, actual consent, United States enforcement, and proper Young relief remain distinct. The dissent orders no discovery, hearing, new proof, amendment, or constitutional-infringement trial, and decides no patent merits.

Every controlling part has majority support. The concurrences of Stevens and O'Connor have the joins shown; Stone-Zsela writes alone.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join the design, parallel-duty and manufacturing/warning holdings and the formal FDA interpretation. They would leave open whether some other common-law duty may be expressly preempted by section 360k. Rejecting Medtronic's sweeping displacement argument does not require categorical immunity for tort law, but neither does it require affirmative inclusion beyond the duties actually resolved. Cipollone construed different statutory language and does not compel that additional step here. Their reserved position is not the Court's rule because five Justices directly adopt Part IV. Their agreement with the present application does not depend on a prediction that future common-law preemption must be rare.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I, II and IV. Substantial-equivalence clearance supplies no substantive design approval; genuinely equal state duties are not different merely because damages are available. But actual federal manufacturing and warning commands apply to this device even though they cover other devices. In their view, the statute requires applicability, difference or addition and subject connection, not an extra specificity prerequisite. The FDA cannot impose that extra condition against text they find clear. Section 808.1's recognition of court decisions and divergent substantive applications also defeats a categorical exemption based on general state-law form. They would affirm preemption of genuinely divergent manufacturing/warning duties, reverse the parallel-duty bar and remand to identify the actual obligations. They make no finding of compliance, breach or Florida entitlement and do not demand dismissal of an entire count that may contain a viable equal-duty theory.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I through IV and the judgment. Congress's express comparison controls: a damages duty can be a requirement, but clearance and the agency's presence do not supply a missing substantive federal comparator. Equal conduct does not become different merely because damages are available. His conclusion rests on that statutory ground, so he does not join Part V's independent reliance on agency interpretation. He neither rejects that ground as unlawful nor adopts a general tort exemption. The unresolved content of a particular pleaded duty can be identified through ordinary proceedings without requiring a new preliminary hearing in every products case; breach, causation and relief remain unproved.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens concurs in part and in the judgment, joined by no other Justice. He rests on the Jones Act's statutory incorporation and the state-forum principles of Mayfield and Bainbridge. Those sources suffice to permit retention of the combined claims. He does not join the broader Jensen explanation, and his vote supplies no approval of that explanation or a holding overruling Jensen.

Souter concurs, joined by no other Justice, while joining both portions of the Court's opinion. The substance/procedure distinction is a useful practical guide, not an answer produced merely by a label. When classification is close, he would examine whether the state rule unduly interferes with the federal interest in free maritime commerce. That qualification is his separate position, not a new majority balancing test.

Kennedy dissents, joined by Thomas. The federal maritime safeguard need not have originated exclusively in admiralty to be characteristic of maritime law. Its protection against burdensome litigation in an unsuitable forum serves maritime commerce and coherent federal adjudication. Mayfield and Bainbridge preserve ordinary local administration but do not, in their view, justify eliminating this established safeguard for maritime cases. They would reverse and remand for consideration under the federal doctrine, including an available adequate alternative forum and the relevant convenience and justice considerations. They would not automatically reinstate the dismissal or decide the injury claims.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Justice Stevens dissented, joined in full by Justice Breyer.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justice Breyer. In their view, Congress's express extension of §504 to federal programs carries the remedial setting in which that prohibition already operated. Section 505's differentiated enforcement channels codify particular means of enforcement without withdrawing ordinary damages for the newly express federal obligation; the 1986 recognition of legal and equitable remedies reinforces that reading. They accept that consent must come from enacted law and that Franklin alone cannot waive immunity, but find the combined provisions sufficient and distinguish Nordic Village's ambiguous bankruptcy formulation. Treating §505's assistance language as a restriction on the separate federal-program obligation gives the remedial scheme an unwarranted gap. They would reverse the categorical damages bar and remand for litigation of entitlement, amount and any remaining lawful defense. They would neither award damages automatically nor reopen the injunction.
