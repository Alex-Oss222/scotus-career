I. BACKGROUND.

(4/16/98). An extensive governmental review of this project

informed the District, inter alia, that it intended to treat 
approximately 75,000 tons of contaminated soil per year and

Il. THE FIRST TRIAL. 
On September 20, 1994, Tri County filed suit against the 
District of Columbia and its employees seeking damages 
under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 for the violation of Tn County’s due 
process rights. The district court granted the District’s 
motion for summary judgment and rejected Tn County’s 
constitutional claims. J.A. 2, D.E. 47. On appeal, a panel of 
the D.C. Circuit, consisting of Chief Judge Edwards and 
Circuit Judges Williams and Sentelle, held that “Cross’s 
‘procedure’ .. . deprived Tri County of property without due 
process of law.” 104 F.3d at 462. The D.C. Circuit de- 
scribed the actions of Cross as “a classic case of shooting 
from the hip on complex technical matters.” /d. It also 
determined that there was “provision for correction of the 
sort of suspension issued by Cross, but uncertainty as to its 
likely speed.” Jd. at 461. The case was remanded to the 
district court for proof of damages sustained by Tn County, 
as well as consideration of the District’s defenses of moot- 
ness and failure to mitigate damages. J.A. 29-30. 
Prior to trial, Tri County sought summary judgment on its 
claim for damages. R. D.E. 57 (4/15/97). The district court 
denied the motion and stated that lost profits of a relatively 
new business would be speculative. J.A. 33. Its sole author- 
ity for this statement was Country Club Associates Limited 
Partnership v. FDIC, 918 F. Supp. 429 (D.D.C. 1996).* That 
case, however, was governed by Virginia law, which bars 
recovery of lost profits by a new business as a matter of law. 
The district court was advised that under District of Colum- 
bia law, a new business can assert a claim for lost profits. 
See ALPO Petfoods, Inc. v. Ralston Purina Co., 997 F.2d 
949, 951 (D.C. Cir. 1993). At trial, the district court accord- 
ingly instructed the jury, without objection by the District, 
that a new business could recover future profits. J.A. 125.

SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT 
The D.C. Circuit has never explained the meaning or effect 
of conducting a more searching inquiry. The District is 
unable to establish that this phrase affected the abuse of 
discretion standard of appellate review, although it had the 
opportunity to raise this issue with the D.C. Circuit. The writ 
of certiorari should be dismissed as improvidently granted in 
view of the uncertainty surrounding a “more searching 
inquiry.” 
A more searching inquiry does not have any substantive 
connotations. It only indicates that the D.C. Circuit con- 
ducted a more searching review of the record due to the 
conflict between the views of the judge and the jury. Such 
additional review is necessary to make certain that a party 
has not been deprived of its right to a jury trial. 
Even if a more searching inquiry constituted a stricter 
abuse of discretion standard of review, such action was 
appropriate. The jury’s examination of the facts was entitled 
to greater deference than the trial judge’s review of the 
evidence. Such greater deference is afforded by a stricter 
abuse of discretion standard. 
Further, the errors committed by the district court were 
legal in nature. Such erroneous rulings constituted abuse of 
discretion. Regardless of whether a more searching inquiry 
15 
was conducted, the D.C. Circuit was correct in reversing the 
district court’s grant of a new trial under an ordinary abuse of 
discretion standard. 
Lastly, if this Court determines that the D.C. Circuit did 
apply the wrong standard of review, the case should be 
remanded for reconsideration especially because the D.C. 
Circuit never reviewed Tri County’s objections to the second 
trial.

IV. IF THIS COURT DETERMINES THAT THE 
D.C. CIRCUIT EMPLOYED AN ERRONEOUS 
STANDARD OF APPELLATE REVIEW, THE 
CASE SHOULD BE REMANDED FOR THE 
D.C. CIRCUIT TO APPLY THE CORRECT

If this Court should determine that the D.C. Circuit did 
apply the wrong standard of review, the appropriate course of 
action would be to remand for reconsideration in light of the 
correct standard. Contrary to the District’s suggestion that 
this Court should apply any standard in the first instance, Pet. 
Br. 46-49, the usual course is to permit the lower courts to 
engage in that task. See Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 
U.S. 242, 257 (1986); Crawford-El v. Britton, 523 U.S. 574, 
601 (1998); Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Pena, 515 U.S. 
200, 237 (1995). The District has identified no reason not to 
follow that course here. Such action would be particularly 
appropriate in this case because the D.C. Circuit would in 
any event have to consider the issues Tri County raised as 
errors with respect to the second trial.
