QUESTION PRESENTED PARTIES

prison sentence was based on an incorrect calculation under the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines. Sup. Ct. R. 14.1(b). 
While his original case was pending, the Seventh Circuit had issued a decision in another case 
interpreting a Guidelines issue in favor of defendants. Mr. Glover filed the instant action 
pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 2255 to correct his sentence, on the ground that his attorney had 
provided ineffective assistance in failing to raise this binding Seventh Circuit decision. 
The court of appeals affirmed the dismissal of Mr. Glover’s Section 2255 action. In light 
of the dispositive appellate ruling that his counsel never raised, the court of appeals assumed that 
Glover's attorney performed inadequately. It also assumed that the deficient performance led to 
an offense level under the Sentencing Guidelines which was two levels higher than it should 
have been, and that this error caused Mr. Glover to receive a sentence that was 6 to 21 months 
higher than he should have received. Nevertheless, the Seventh Circuit held that such an 
increased sentence due to counsel’s deficient performance "is not sufficiently significant to be 
cognizable on collateral attack." (App. 2). The questions on which review is sought are: 
1. Whether the court of appeals erred in holding that an additional 6 to 21 months in 
prison due to counsel’s error relating to the sentencing guidelines fails to satisfy the “prejudice” | 
prong of Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984). 
2. Whether the court of appeals erred in holding that a 2-level error in the offense 
level under the sentencing guidelines was per se insufficient to satisfy the “prejudice” prong of 
Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984), even where this 2-level error resulted in the 
petitioner being sentenced to an additional 6 to 21 months in prison. 



## Tri-County

=== PDF PAGE 2 ===
QUESTION PRESENTED 
Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., 518 U:S. 415 
(1996), holds that the Seventh Amendment does not preclude 
district courts from exercising substantial discretion in grant- 
ing new trials but does constrain appellate review of such 
orders. The question presented is whether, as some Circuits 
have recognized, Gasperini invalidates the contrary approach 
of other Circuits, including the D.C. Circuit here, which hold 
that the Seventh Amendment strictly circumscribes a district 
court’s discretion to grant a new trial and thereby expands the 
scope of appcllate review.
