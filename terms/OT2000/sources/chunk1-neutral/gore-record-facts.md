# Bounded record addendum: briefs and Miami-Dade testimony

This supplements `gore-appendix-facts.md` and the complete Burton witness extraction. It distinguishes party positions, witness testimony, and judicial recitations. The factual cutoff is December 8, 2000. The December 10 merits briefs supply arguments and citations to earlier proceedings, not later recount events or results. No legal conclusion about equal protection, preservation, finality, or remedy is supplied by this addendum.

## Miami-Dade physical ballot treatment

Thomas Spencer, a Republican lawyer who observed the county proceedings for Bush, testified to different approaches among the three board members. According to his answers to Judge Sauls, Judge King counted any dimple or pregnant chad as a vote irrespective of other ballot markings; Judge Lehr looked for paper separation; Supervisor Leahy looked for separation visible against light plus another indication on the ballot. Spencer said Leahy had used a two-point hanging-chad approach in the initial three-precinct sample, together with examination of other clear punches, and later used the light approach. These details are testimony at trial pages 496-500, not a judicial finding that every ballot received inconsistent treatment. The entire Spencer examination, pages 460-500, was read, including direct, both cross-examinations, judicial questions, and release.

Material qualifications: Spencer acknowledged the board members' honesty, competence, and good motives, while disagreeing with their decisions and methods (487-492). He said many decisions were 2-1, with the majority varying, and some clear-punch decisions were unanimous (498). He did not identify a matched set of otherwise identical ballots, calculate how many outcomes changed because of the differing tests, or supply a completed remaining-precinct count. On cross he agreed that the entire manual recount had not been completed and that its result was unknown (494). His statements about partisan precinct patterns are an observer's testimony, not a completed statistical comparison.

Thomas Spargo, another Bush observer, described a ballot with a clear presidential punch and a dimple for another candidate being counted for the clear punch, rather than rejected as two votes (512-513; follow-up at 519-520). This supports a physical-treatment example; it does not establish a machine overvote with two full punches or a statewide count of recoverable overvotes. His testimony about falling chads estimated at least 1000 during the November 19 machine sorting operation (505-506), while describing fewer during later manual handling (508-509). It does not establish 1000 lost presidential votes per day. He expressly disclaimed purposeful creation of votes (508); on cross he did not know whether the remaining unexamined undervotes had been physically handled (536-537). The complete Spargo examination was read, including the continued cross-examination and release (500-523, 531-540).

Mark Lampkin testified from county tally sheets admitted as defense exhibits 62 and 63 that about 34 precinct envelopes had more undervote ballots on November 29 than the earlier typed tally, while about 120 had fewer (545-550). The approximate 34 refers to precincts, not ballots. His cross-examination showed that he did not know how the initial numerical undervote figure was calculated; he could describe handling but not establish the county's full reconciliation method or the cause of every discrepancy (556-564). He acknowledged the officials' honesty (560) and described some inspection and re-sorting to reconcile counts (558-559). The exhibits themselves have not been obtained for independent recomputation. The complete Lampkin examination, pages 541-564, was read. This evidence supports a sorting-reliability dispute; it does not itself identify a particular vote included twice in the certified tally.

## Partial results and the competing legal arguments

The December 8 state majority's partial Miami-Dade figures are 436 additional votes, 302 for Gore and 134 for Bush, giving Gore a net 168, from 20 percent of precincts representing 15 percent of votes cast. Use those denominators as stated. Bush's merits argument at pages 43-44 says 20 percent of ballots; that argumentative shorthand must not displace the majority's more precise recitation. Neither the briefs nor these witness examinations supply a completed comparison with the remaining undervotes. The unresolved Palm Beach 215-versus-176 dispute and the distinction between statewide no-votes and undervotes remain as stated in `gore-appendix-facts.md`.

Bush argues that including the partial all-ballot Miami-Dade recount while examining only remaining undervotes treats populations differently; he also objects to leaving machine overvotes unexamined and claims that sorting can produce double counting. Those are party arguments, with the testimony limits above. Gore argues that voter intent is the single governing standard, that particular ballots can require different physical evidence to reveal that intent, and that inconsistent applications can be corrected by the circuit court and appellate review. Gore also disputes the factual basis and preservation of Bush's broader claims. His fallback remedy is a uniform standard followed by continued counting, rather than cancellation of all recounts. The exact selected arguments are mechanically copied in `gore-main-brief-excerpts.txt`; none is adopted as a finding.

## Preservation and federal finality

The appendix contains Bush's November 30 answer and December 6 state appellate brief raising equal protection and due process objections to differing county standards and selective county or precinct recounts. Carr's separately filed respondent brief reproduces its November 29 declaratory pleading and December 6 state brief presenting related objections. This establishes that such categories of objection were presented; it does not resolve whether a particular later theory was adequately preserved by a particular party. Gore's merits brief argues that the later claims are materially different and that challenges to pre-contest tabulations belonged in a separate contest.

The official Florida Supreme Court order dated December 8 denies Bush and Cheney leave to file their clarification of argument. This is now verified independently of the competing briefs. Its operative paragraph is copied in `gore-clarification-leave-order-2000-12-08.txt`. The denial establishes the leave ruling, while the majority opinion's discussion of the tendered clarification remains part of that opinion. Neither fact alone decides every federal preservation question.

The application argues for federal review under the fourth category described in Cox Broadcasting: a conclusively determined federal issue, possible success later on nonfederal grounds that could eliminate review, reversal on the federal issue that would preclude further litigation of the relevant cause, and serious erosion of federal policy without immediate review. These are Bush's asserted predicates, not adjudicated jurisdictional findings. Gore's main merits brief expressly states that section 1257 supplies jurisdiction to review the judgment. The state judgment still required further counting, possible further relief, and resolution of the Palm Beach numerical dispute. These procedural features and the party positions must be kept distinct from the legal finality analysis.

## Feasibility and deadlines

The complete core filings contain opposing judicial assessments and party assertions about available time. They do not provide a verified statewide completion model identifying every county's ballot population, staffing, physical test, throughput, objection rate, and judicial-review time. Spargo's cross-examination discusses a November 22 board statement that an undervote review at about 300 ballots per hour could be accomplished by that Sunday's cutoff (532-533), while the afternoon decision expressed contrary practical concerns (514-518). This is evidence of competing assessments in the earlier county process, not proof of the feasibility or impossibility of a later statewide protocol. Burton's earlier Palm Beach completion account has the same limited use. No later count totals, interruption, or result closes that evidentiary gap.
