# Bounded record facts and attributed assertions

Sources: [filed petitioners appendix](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0437) and [December 2 hearing transcript, Burton testimony](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/presidential-documents-archive-guidebook/documents-related-the-2000-election-dispute/1202). Appendix citations use printed pages; PDF page number is printed page plus four. The witness transcript is a republished primary transcript without certified original pagination. Its entire examination is retained in `gore-burton-testimony-2000-12-02.txt` as an exact mechanical extraction. No subsequent count totals supply a fact here.

## Counting practices

Burton testified that Palm Beach began its one-percent sample with the 1990 policy excluding fully attached indentations, briefly used a light-through-indentation test, returned to the 1990 rule, and redid the first precinct. Following Labarga's November 15 order, the board adopted a November 16 policy allowing two-corner punches to count and also permitting one-corner punches and dimples where clear voter intent appeared. These are witness identifications and descriptions of exhibits; the original exhibit images were not inspected for this extract.

The full recount used the whole ballot, not the brief light test. Burton described rejecting an isolated presidential dimple with all other choices fully punched, counting patterns of dimples or pinholes, and later refining the pattern approach so not every other choice had to be dimpled. He accepted a fifteen-punch/five-dimple example. The testimony supplies neither a precise time for that refinement nor a ballot-by-ballot account showing all earlier ballots were reconsidered. A full presidential punch controlled over other presidential dimples regardless of their prominence. His explanations of how voters might have created patterns were inferences, and counsel's displayed example ballots were expressly demonstratives.

The board reviewed ballots questioned by observers from either party. Burton estimated roughly 14500 such ballots and believed the rulings were publicly announced and transcribed. Although he described separate objection envelopes, redirect qualified that practice: in the last several precincts, whose number and timing he did not recall, objections were preserved without physically segregating every ballot to save time. A later account of identifiable disputed ballots must preserve that qualification.

The appendix's detailed Broward account is a third-party complaint, not witness testimony or a finding. Paragraphs 15–32 at 111a–115a allege an initial two-corner rule, later varying tests involving light, dimple depth, other ballot marks, and party patterns, and different treatment of dimples with a full punch. Its light-test allegations concern Broward and must not be reassigned to Palm Beach. Burton's effort to match his understanding of Broward does not independently verify Broward practices; his foundation was challenged.

## Counts, populations, and procedural posture

The December 8 state majority recites that Miami-Dade began a manual count of all ballots but stopped with roughly 9000 of 10750 undervotes unreviewed. It describes 436 additional legal votes from 20 percent of precincts representing 15 percent of votes cast, a net 168 for Gore; at 29a it gives 302 Gore and 134 Bush. Sauls's ruling, 59a–60a, describes the initial three-precinct sample plus 136 additional precincts out of 635. These are judicial recitations of a partial recount. The appendix does not reproduce a complete precinct-level comparison between that partial count and the unreviewed undervotes.

Palm Beach's 215-versus-176 difference remains unresolved in the December 8 majority's footnote 6, 4a, which directs determination on remand; 29a footnote 19 repeats Bush's 176 assertion. Burton had heard 215 but had not personally seen the final tally. His testimony therefore does not independently resolve the discrepancy.

The December 8 remedy concerns undervotes not already manually tabulated. Wells's dissent, 38a–39a footnote 26, questions the exclusion of overvotes; that question is not proof of particular recoverable overvotes. Harding's 53a footnote 33 describes a statewide no-vote total and cites state exhibits 40–41, which are not reproduced. That population must not be relabeled as undervotes. Burton's example with one full punch and other dimples does not establish how ballots with two full presidential punches were treated.

The appendix reproduces the December 4 final judgment and the December 8 partial reversal and remand. Further counting and relief remained to be performed, and the Palm Beach number remained for determination; rehearing was barred. These procedural facts bear on finality but do not decide the federal review question under section 1257, whose text is not reproduced here.

## Filed objections and feasibility assertions

The November 30 answer, 120a–122a/original 13–16, raises federal equal protection and due process objections to selected counties and changed standards. The December 6 state appellate brief, 109a–110a/original 26–27, 43, 45–46, expressly objects both to differing county counting standards and selected counties or portions of counties. This establishes presentation of those categories of objections before December 8, not a legal conclusion about the sufficiency or exact scope of preservation. The earlier November 21 opinion's 74a footnote 10 concerns challenges to the election laws in that different appeal and is not a statement that no constitutional objection was ever presented in this contest.

The December 7 clarification appears at 101a–107a. The December 8 majority, 5a–6a footnote 7, discusses its contest-statute-inapplicability argument as a change after oral argument and contrasts prior representations. No separate leave-grant order, service receipt, or complete argument transcript accompanies it. Its existence and discussion do not settle every acceptance, timing, waiver, or preservation question.

The majority's 33a footnote 22 expresses confidence in weekend completion, while its 32a footnote 21 acknowledges practical difficulty. Wells, 47a–48a, and Harding, 55a–56a, dispute feasibility, including time for objections and review. The November 30 motion excerpt, 126a, asserts impossibility within the federal time constraints. These are judicial assessments and party assertions, not a quantified completion model or completed-count proof. Burton's account of finishing the earlier Palm Beach recount ninety minutes to two hours after its Sunday 5 p.m. cutoff, with faster work near the end, does not establish feasibility of a later statewide protocol.

## Source limitations

The complete appendix contains selected excerpts of the state brief, complaint, answer, and dismissal memorandum, not their full originals. Its section 102.168(2) reproduction at 141a–142a actually omits the alternative five-day post-protest clause in print; the redline at 9a footnote 9 displays both alternatives. Use a complete official statute for exact text. The bench-ruling cover at 58a has an inconsistent date; the December 4 judgment and December 8 recitation identify the ruling differently. Neither printing defect should be silently corrected in a quotation.

Burton's testimony is fully read but has transcription gaps and a mislabeled redirect question. The relevant gaps concern an introductory registration question, an objection to his Broward foundation, and procedural transitions, not the recorded standard-sequence exchanges. No missing words, exhibit images, or original transcript page numbers have been supplied by inference. These source limitations do not establish missing court filings, litigant default, or a failure of proof.
