# Gore — predecision Article II presentation

Source: Appendix F, printed appendix pages 108a–110a, reproducing the cover and selected pages 26–27, 43, and 45–46 of Bush and Cheney's December 6, 2000 amended Florida Supreme Court brief. This complete bounded appendix item is mechanically copied from the saved OCR of the primary document. The complete state brief is not reproduced or independently reviewed here. The public collection item is https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0437 .

The reproduced original page 45 expressly invokes Article II for applying a newly adopted dimple standard in selected counties and for changes to certification and contest periods. That distinct predecision argument is not limited to the December 7 paper's blanket contention that the contest statute cannot apply to presidential elections. The description of trial findings remains the advocate's description, not an additional finding by this review. These excerpts establish presentation of the specified objections; they do not independently adjudicate preservation of every broader legislative-displacement theory, the legal sufficiency of any objection, or the merits.

The separately reviewed November 30 answer, appendix pages 120a–122a/original pages 13–16, expressly invokes equality, due process, and section 5 in its selected defenses. Those selected pages do not expressly name Article II. Their omission cannot establish what every unexcerpted page said. The December 8 denial of leave for the separate clarification remains effective and distinct.

Il. THERE ARE FEDERAL CONSTITUTIONAL 
AND STATUTORY CONSIDERATIONS THAT 
APPLY TO THIS CASE AND THAT REQUIRE 
AFFIRMATION OF THE CIRCUIT COURT’S

The Circuit Court found as a factual matter that the 
standard of vote counting in Palm Beach County that 
existed on November 7, 2000 was that embodied in a 
written 1990 guideline: that indentations or dimples will 
not count as votes. The court further noted that any 
change in that standard may, in turn, be contrary to 3 
U.S.C. § 5. Indeed, for a court in a contest proceeding 
to now apply a standard that counts dimples as votes in 
selective counties would be directly contrary to 3 U.S.C. 
§ 5, and it would also violate Article II, Section I of the 
U.S. Constitution. In addition, a change in the deadline 
for certification for election returns, along with a change 
in the time period for contesting an election, would 
likewise violate Article II, Section 1 of the U.S. Consti- 
tution and 3 U.S.C. § 5. Finally, the application of 
counting standards in different counties as well as the
