ARGUMENT 


POINT I THE PEOPLE’S FAILURE TO PROVIDE 

DETECTIVE’S AFFIDAVIT SUBMITTED IN SUPPORT 

OF TAKE-OUT ORDER PRIOR TO WADE HEARING 
WAS REVERSIBLE ROSARIO VIOLATION 


Prior to the Wade hearing defense counsel requested a 
copy of the affidavit submitted by Detective Redmond in 
support of the order directing defendant to appear in a lineup. 
Prior to the start of trial counsel requested to reopen the Wade 
hearing based on information in the newly discovered 
affidavit. Although counsel obtained the affidavit, it was not 
until after the Wade hearing. Additionally, the court denied 
the defense motion to reopen the Wade hearing. 


A prior statement by a witness who testifies at trial is 
clearly Rosario material. The detective’s affidavit referred 


42a 
Petition Dated May 20, 1997 with Exhibit C 


to aconfidential informant whose information helped launch 
this investigation. The trial court attempted to minimize the 
role of the informant but it is well-settled that defense 
counsel, not the prosecutor or the court “is in a far better 
position to appraise the value of a witness’ pretrial statements 
for impeachment purposes” People v. Rosario, 9 N.Y.2d 286, 
290, 213 N.Y.S.2d 448, 173 N.E.2d 881, cert. denied, 363 
U.S. 866, 82 S.Ct. 117, 17 L.Ed.2d 64. 


This evidence was critical because the alleged informant 
was known to the defendant. It is therefore likely that this 
information would have significantly affected the outcome 
of the trial. People v. Jones, 70 N.Y.2d 547, 550, 523 
N.Y.S.2d 53, 517 N.E.2d 865 (1987) (“evidence must be 
material to the defense and would have in any reasonable 
likelihood, affected the judgment’’). 


The People’s failure to produce the requested Rosario 
material together with the trial court’s failure to reopen the 
Wade hearing, amounted to a complete failure” to turn over 
the requested material. People v. Ranghelle, 69 N.Y.2d 56, 
63, 511 N.Y.S.2d 580, 503 N.E.2d 1011 (1986). As a result 
there was per se error which requires reversal of the 
conviction and the ordering of a new trial. 


POINT If DEFENDANT WAS DENIED HIS RIGHT TO 
COUNSEL AT THE LINEUP WHERE THE POLICE 
FAILED TO CONTACT DEFENDANT’S 
RETAINED COUNSEL 


Defendant acknowledges that the right to counsel does 
not attach until the formal initiation of a prosecution. 


43a 


Petition Dated May 20, 1997 with Exhibit C 


People v. Hawkins, 55 N.Y .2d 474, 483, 450 N.Y.S.2d 159, 
435 N.E.2d 376 (1982). In this case, however, defendant had 
retained counsel on Ind. No. 6542/89. Defendant’s retained 
counsel could not be excluded from the lineup in this case 
unless his arrival caused unreasonable delay. Hawkins, 55 
N.Y.2d at 486, 450 N.Y.S.2d at 166, 435 N.E.2d at _. 
“Pertinent to this consideration is whether the delay would 
result in significant inconvenience to the witnesses or would 
undermine the substantial advantage of a prompt identification 
confrontation” /bid. 


The take-out order in this case was signed March 22, 
1991. The lineup was not held until five days later. Thus 
there was no question of a prompt investigation or witnesses’ 
convenience. The police knew or should have known that 
defendant had an attorney because he was incarcerated. The 
Assistant District Attorney who submitted the order should 
have known the defendant had an attorney because he had 
an open case in Queens Supreme Court. Moreover, it is 
undisputed that the defendant unequivocally requested the 
presence of his retained counsel. 


“Though law enforcement authorities ordinarily are not 
required to notify counsel of an impending investigatory 
lineup absent a specific request to do so, when the police are 
aware that a criminal defendant is represented by counsel 
and the defendant explicitly requests this assistance of his 
attorney, the police may not proceed with the lineup without 
at least apprising the defendant’s lawyer of the situation and 
affording him or her an opportunity to appear” People v. 
Coates, 74 N.Y.2d 244, 249, 544 N.Y.S.2d 992, 994, __ 
N.E.2d (1989). 


44a 
Petition Dated May 20, 1997 with Exhibit C 


The single last-minute telephone call made by the 
detective is insufficient evidence of an acceptable effort to 
contact the attorney under the circumstances of this case. 
Moreover, the police knew that defendant was going to be 
viewed by several robbery victims. As a result the failure to 
promptly notify defendant’ counsel is even more egregious. 


POINT Ill THE DEFENDANT’S APPEARANCE 
MADE THE LINEUP SUGGESTIVE 


The defendant concedes that lineup fillers are not 
required to match his appearance exactly. People v. 
Rodriguez, 124 A.D.2d 611, 507 N.Y.S.2d 757 (2d Dep’t 
1986). Yet “[t]he issues involved in ascertaining the validity 
of a lineup identification concerns “undue suggestiveness” 
which is determined by considering the totality of he 
circumstances surrounding the lineup” (/bid.). 


In this case the defendant’s complexion and his clothing 
made him stand out from the fillers. These features were 
aggravated by the lighting conditions in the viewing area. 
Additionally defendant likely stood out more because of the 
police practice of getting fillers from men’s shelters. The 
trial court simply made a factual error when it determined 
that the lineup photographs showed that the fillers were

AEDPA significantly modified § 2254 for non-death penalty 
cases. Specifically, the AEDPA instituted a one-year statute 
of limitations for habeas petitions:

court. 


28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)(1) (as amended by the AEDPA).

petition, we see no need to accord a full year after the

JOHNSON, District Judge: 


Sherman Walker (“plaintiff’ or “Walker”), appearing 
pro se, brings this action pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983 
alleging that a number of lawyers associated with the Legal 
Aid Society and in private practice violated his right to 
counsel and due process of the law. Plaintiff also seeks 
habeas corpus relief pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 2254. For the 


76a 
Petitioner's Reply Affidavit with Exhibits 


reasons stated below, the motion to proceed in forma 
pauperis is granted, but the complaint is dismissed sua sponte 
in accordance with 28 U.S.C. § 1915(d). 


BACKGROUND 


In three separate state court trials — one in New York 
County, and two in Queens County — Plaintiff was convicted 
on robbery charges. In each case, the Legal Aid Society was 
appointed to represent plaintiff in the appeal of his 
conviction. In the course of representing Walker, however, 

-it became apparent that the Legal Aid Society already 
represented a confidential informant who had been a primary 
source of information leading to plaintiff's arrest. Because 
representing both clients would pose a conflict of interest, 
the Legal Aid Society formally moved to be relieved as 
counsel on plaintiff's appeals. The state appellate courts 
granted this motion and appointed new counsel to prosecute 
plaintiff's appeals. 


Walker now argues that no confidential informant 
existed and thus that there was no conflict of interest 
preventing the Legal Aid Society from representing him. 
Plaintiff contends that, although he notified the Legal Aid 
Society of this fact, they failed to investigate his claim. 
Plaintiff argues that the Legal Aid Society’s improper 
withdrawal denied him of his right to the counsel of his 
choice and due process of the law. 


Accompanying plaintiff's complaint was an application 
to proceed in forma pauperis. 


77a 
Petitioner's Reply Affidavit with Exhibits 
DISCUSSION 


Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915, a district court may dismiss a 
case in which the plaintiff has applied to proceed in forma 
pauperis “if the allegation of poverty is untrue, or if satisfied 
that the action is frivolous or malicious.” 28 U.S.C. 
§ 1915(d). The Second Circuit follows a two-step approach 
in establishing eligibility to proceed in forma pauperis by 
first assessing a plaintiff's financial status, followed by a 
determination as to the frivolousness of the action. 
See Anderson v. Coughlin, 700 F.2d 37, 41 (2d Cir. 1983). 


Applying the two-step approach to the present case, this 
Court finds that plaintiff satisfies the financial requirements 
to proceed in forma pauperis. Based on the allegation of 
poverty stated in his declaration, the Court is convinced that 
Walker is unable to pay the costs and filing fees in connection 
with this suit. Thus, plaintiff's motion for leave to proceed 
in forma pauperis is granted. 


Nevertheless, plaintiff's complaint must be dismissed. 
First, to establish a claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, a plaintiff 
must demonstrate that the conduct complained of was 
committed by a person acting under the color of state law 
and that the conduct deprived the plaintiff of a federal right. 
Bernheim v. Litt, 79 F.3d 318, 321 (2d Cir. 1996). The United 
States Supreme Court has stated unequivocally that court- 
appointed lawyers do not act under color of state law within 
the meaning of § 1983. See Polk County v. Dodson, 454 U.S. 
312 (1981) (public defender does not act under color of state 
law); Cassidento v. Hammer, 1995 WL 722536 (2d Cir. 
1995) (court-appointed attorney does not act under color of 
state law). As such, plaintiff's claims against all of the 


78a 
Petitioner's Reply Affidavit with Exhibits 


individual attorneys as well as the Legal Aid Society must 
be dismissed. See Pino v. Ryan, 49 F.3d 51 (2d Cir. 1995) 
(sua sponte dismissal may be based on an affirmative defense 
that appears on the face of the complaint). 


Plaintiff's claim for habeas corpus relief also must be 
dismissed for failure to adequately set forth his claim. For 
instance, although plaintiff refers to state appellate 
proceedings relating to his robbery convictions, he fails to 
specify the claims litigated in those appeals. Because this 
court cannot determine which issues have been properly 
exhausted in state court, the essential allegations of a habeas 
corpus action have not been presented. See 28 U.S.C. 
§ 2254(b) (writ of habeas corpus “shall not be granted unless 
it appears that the applicant has exhausted the remedies 
available in the courts of the State”). Therefore, this claim 
is dismissed without prejudice. 


CONCLUSION 


For the reasons stated herein, plaintiff's motion to 
proceed in forma pauperis is granted. However, the Court 
finds plaintiff's complaint to be frivolous within the meaning 
of 28 U.S.C. § 1915(d), since it fails to state a cognizable 
claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 or 28 U.S.C. § 2254. Accordingly, 
plaintiff's complaint is dismissed without prejudice.

The voucher for defendant’s eyeglasses taken from him at

Mullin’s testimony, since it did not contain any statements 
or descriptions of the events or participants in the incident, 
was not completed by that detective, and the item vouchered

questioning Favilla with respect to the voucher, cured any 
prejudice caused by any violation of the Rosario rule 
(see, People v Ranghelle, 69 NY2d 56, 63; cf, People vy

the failure to timely turn over two detective’s reports (DD5 ’s)

since the detective who prepared the documents did not 
testify at trial (CPL 240.45 [1][a]) and since the information 
contained therein did not tend to exculpate him or affect the 
credibility of a witness who testified at trial. 


It was not an improvident exercise of discretion for the trial 
court to have denied the defense application to relieve 
counsel prior to and during the pretrial suppression hearing 
nor was good cause demonstrated requiring a hearing on the 
matter (see, People y Smith, 192 AD2d 310, affd 82 NY2d

731; People v Newell, 200 AD2d 451). In light of the conclusory 
assertions proffered in support of the application and the fact 
that the case had been pending before the trial court for 
several months, “the court had a clear picture of the ‘nature 
of the disagreement’ between defendant and counsel and its 
‘potential for resolution’ ”, and was therefore in the best 
position to determine the merit of the application (People v 
Smith, supra, at 312, quoting People v Sides, 75 NY2d 822,

evidence, since there is no indication that the complainant 
was ever questioned concerning the instant offense. 
Therefore, evidence of the “fact” in question was not Brady 
material which the People were obliged to turn over to the 
defense. 


The defendant’s sentence was not excessive (see, People 
v Suitte, 90 AD2d 80).

Both the Federal and New York State Constitutions 
guaranty to the defendant in a criminal proceeding the right 
to the assistance of competent legal Counsel to assist in his 
defense. U.S. Const., Amdt. 6, N.Y. Const., Art. 1 Sec. 6. 
The Court have elucidated this requirement to interpret the 
mere presence of Counsel as being insufficient compliance 
with the Constitutional guarantees, and have instead required 
that a criminal defendant be afforded Counsel who provides 
effect-assistance. McMann v. Richardson, 397 U.S. 759, 90 
S.Ct. 1441, 25 L.Ed.2d 763 (1970). The performance of trial 
Counsel may be evaluated by the appellate courts to ensure 
that Counsel has not failed to render adequate, reasonably 
effective legal assistance. Cuyler v. Sullivan, 446 U.S. 335, 
100 S.Ct. 1708, 64 L.Ed.2d 333 (1980). Analysis of the 
alleged deprivation of his right is based on the Constitution 
as defined in the Sixth Amendment. Powell v. Alabama, 287 
U.S. 45, 53 S.Ct. 55, 77 L.Ed. 158 (1932); Johnson v. Zerbst, 
304 U.S. 458, 58 S.Ct. 1019, 82 L.Ed. 1461 (1938); Gideon 
v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335, 83 S.Ct. 792, 9 L.Ed.2d 799 
(1963). 


The ideals to be applied in assessing the trial 
performance of a defendant’s Counsel have been set forth 
by the Supreme Court in the Opinion of the Court by Justice 
O’Conner in the case of Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 


119a 


Supplement for Appellant with Attached Exhibits 


668, 104 S.Ct. 2052, 80 L.Ed.2d 764 (1984). It was the 
intention of the Court to describe the standards of judging 
the contention that a conviction must be overturned because 
of actual prejudice by way of ineffective assistance of 
Counsel. Id., at 2063. The Strickland opinion provide liitle 
specific guidance or litmus test of the trial Counsel’s 
adequacy, but while deferring to the individual attorney’s 
decisions of Strategy and manner of providing assistance, 
the Court made clear that the purpose of such analysis is not 
to examine with hind-sight whether a particular act or 
omission was unreasonable, but to simply ensure that the 
defendant received a fair trial. The Strickland holding 
requires that for reversal to be required, it must be shown 
that the trial Counsel’s assistance must have been “deficient 
... [with] errors so serious that Counsel was not functioning 
as the “Counsel” guaranteed .. . by the Sixth Amendment 
[and that] the deficient performance prejudiced the defense 
.. . [1.e.,] that Counsel’s errors Were SO serious as to deprive 
the defendant of a fair trial . . . Whose result is unreliable. 
Id., at 687. The Strickland opinion simply requires a showing 
that Counsel’s representation fell below an objective standard 
of reasonableness. 


In New York, the standard for questions of ineffective 
representation derive from the opinion of the Court of 
Appeals in People v. Baldi, 54 N.Y.2d 137, 444 N.Y.S.2d 
893, 429 N.E.2d 400 (1981). Even though this decision 
predates the Strickland opinion, the broader protection 
offered by this decision remain the law in New York. 


The Court of Appeals has varied little from this standard, 
and has continued to assert that hindsight will not benefit an

A Contention of ineffective assistance — of trial Counsel 
require proof of less than meaningful representation, rather

(see People v. Benn, 68 N.Y.2d 941; also People v. Rivera,

Legal Aid Society, however, filed a motion to be relieved of 
the assignment claiming a conflict of interest; the People

Defendant did not oppose the Legal Aid Society’s request 
to be relieved. This Court granted Legal Aid’s Motion on

5. Defendant’s writ of error coram nobis, which claims 
that the Legal Aid Society should not have been relieved as 
assigned counsel on defendant’s direct appeal, should be

163a 


Affirmation in Opposition 


Had defendant wished to oppose the motion to be relieved, 
he could have done so at the time the motion was made. 
Indeed, the People opposed the motion. This Court decided 
the motion after careful consideration of the basis asserted 
by the Legal Aid Society — an alleged conflict of interest 
— and defendant cannot reopen the issue in an application 
for a writ of error coram nobis. 


Defendant misconstrues the purposes and the limitations 
of the writ of error coram nobis. Indeed, defendant’s current 
application makes no clear claim of ineffectiveness of 
appellate counsel. To the degree that defendant asserts that 
a claim should have been made on his direct appeal regarding 
the confidential informant’s role in defendant’s arrest, and 
it is not even clear from his motion papers that he does So, 
the brief filed by Alden T. Lewis, defendant’s appellate 
counsel, properly raised such a claim, as noted by Mr. Lewis 
in his affirmation of J anuary 26, 1996, submitted to this Court 
in response to defendant’s current application. As explained 
by Mr. Lewis, he raised an issue on appeal regarding the 
Prosecution’s purported failure to disclose the informant’s 
identity and the trial court’s refusal to reopen the Wade 
Hearing based upon the discovery of the informant. Notably, 
these claims were made only with respect to indictment 
2120/91. Mr. Lewis properly concluded that the confidential 
informant had no relevancy to defendant’s other conviction, 
under Indictment 65421/89. 


Defendant does not, in any way, contend that counsel’s 
presentation of these issues on direct appeal was defective. 
Indeed, to the extent that his motion can be read as making 
an ineffectiveness of counsel claim, it asserts only that 


164a 
Affirmation in Opposition 


counsel should have argued that there was no basis for the 
Legal Aid Society’s request to be relieved as there was no 
conflict of interest. But this would not have been a proper 
claim to raise on direct appeal. 


A criminal defendant is afforded the constitutional right 
to the effective assistance of counsel on any appeal granted 
him as a matter of right. Douglass v. California, 372 U.S. 
353, 357-358 (1963) (announcing principle, based on equal 
protection clause). Because New York State, by Criminal 
Procedure Law § 450.10(1), does grant a criminal defendant 
an initial appeal as of right to an intermediate appellate court, 
its statute implicates the constitutional principles announced 
in Douglass, and requires that a defendant on that initial 
appeal receive the assistance of counsel. That assistance is 
subject to the usual constitutional scrutiny: the assistance 
afforded a defendant in such instances must be effective 
assistance. Evitts v. Lucey, 469 U.S. 387, 396 (1985) 
(requiring the effective assistance of counsel in any appeal 
granted defendant as a matter of nght); People v. Ramos, 
108 A.D.2d 209, 210-211 (2d Dept. 1985) (accurately stating 
principle, though erroneously delegating trial court as the 
appropriate forum for litigating question of appellate 
counsel’s effectiveness). The Court of Appeals, in People v. 
Bachert, 69 N.Y.2d 593, 599 (1987), ultimately determined 
that the proper forum for such relief lay in the intermediate 
appellate court, when the alleged ineffectiveness had earlier 
occurred in that forum. Therefore, an ineffectiveness of appellate : 
counsel would be properly brought before this Court. 


But appellate counsel need not raise all issues or even 
all meritorious issues — suggested to him by his client, to 


16Sa 
Affirmation in Opposition 


meet the constitutional standard of effective assistance. 
Hence, in Jones y. Barnes, 463 U.S. 745 (1983), the Supreme 
Court held that an appellate attorney did not violate his 
client’s Sixth Amendment rights when counsel refused to 
prepare a brief raising all of the issues that his client had 
directed him to address. 


Nevertheless, to succeed, a claim of ineffective assistance 
of appellate counsel must demonstrate true ineffectiveness, 
rather than mere disagreement. People v. Benn, 68 N.Y.2d 
941 (1986). Defendant has failed to raised any issue to 
demonstrate any true ineffectiveness of appellate counsel. 
In addition, an examination of the appellate brief filed on 
defendant’s behalf reveals counsel’s professional 
-_Tepresentation of defendant did not fall below the minimal 
requirement that counsel provide “meaningful representation,” 
as of the time of the representation, People y. Baldi, 
54.N.Y.2d 137, 147 (1981), to meet the New York standard. 
Furthermore, counsel provided “objectively reasonable 
representation,” to meet the federal Standard. Strickland y. 
Washington, 466 U.S. 668, 688 (1984). 

As noted above, there would have been no legitimate 
basis for appellate counsel to contend on direct appeal that 
the Legal Aid Society had been improperly relieved as 
defendant’s assigned counsel. This Court had already 
considered that issue in that it granted the Legal Aid Society’s 
motion to be relieved, over the People’s Opposition, and 
appellate counsel could not have re-argued that issue, on 
direct appeal. That being the case, defendant has no basis 
now to claim that appellate counsel was ineffective for failing 
to raise the issue on direct appeal. Additionally, as shown 


166a 
Affirmation in Opposition 


above, appellate counsel did raise on direct appeal two 
legitimate issues concerning the confidential informant’s role 
in defendant’s conviction under Indictment 2120/91. 
Defendant makes no showing that appellate counsel’s 
treatment of those claims was defective in any manner. 
Consequently, his current assertion that appellate counsel 
was somehow ineffective has no basis in fact or law. 


Therefore, for the above stated reasons defendant’s 
motion should be denied.

MEMORANDUM OF LAW 


192a 


Affirmation in Opposition Dated December 2, 1994 
POINT ONE 


DEFENDANT?’S 440 CLAIMS WERE PROCE- 
DURALLY BARRED BECAUSE DEFENDANT 
FAILED TO RAISE HIS CURRENT CLAIM IN 
ANY OF HIS TWO PRIOR MOTIONS TO 
VACATE JUDGMENT. ' 


Although defendant claimed in his motion to vacate 
judgment that Terrence Heywood’s affidavit represents new 
evidence that merits a new trial, defendant did not raise these 
claims in any of his two prior motions to vacate judgment. 
Thus, absent a showing that defendant was not in a position 
adequately to raise these claims — a showing that defendant 
did not make — the trial court properly denied defendant’s 
motion to vacate judgment on this ground. See C.P.L. 
§440.10(3)(c). Significantly, Terrence Heywood’s affidavit 
is dated December 26, 1992, a full five months before 
defendant filed his second motion to vacate the conviction 
(see Appendix A). 


1. Defendant’s claim that the trial court improperly denied his 
motion to vacate judgment because the prosecution did not file a 
response in opposition is baseless. First, as shown in the 
accompanying affirmation, the People were not properly served by 
defendant with his motion papers; consequently, they cannot be 
penalized for failing to respond. Second, as shown herein, the claims 
raised by defendant in his motion to vacate judgment were 
procedurally barred. That being the case, the trial court was justified 
in denying defendant’s motion on its face — with, or without 
opposition papers from the People. Criminal Procedure Law 
§ 440.10(3)(a), (b). 


193a 


Affirmation in Opposition Dated December 2, 1994 


Accordingly, this Court should summarily deny 
defendant’s current application. 


POINT TWO . 


DEFENDANT’S 440 CLAIMS WERE PROCE- 
DURALLY BARRED BECAUSE HE UNJUSTI- 
FIABLY FAILED TO PLACE FACTS ON THE 
RECORD THAT WOULD PERMIT APPEL- 
LATE REVIEW OF THE CLAIM. 


Defendant’s motion to vacate his judgment of conviction, 
based upon his new evidence claim, was properly denied by 
the trial court because he could have put facts in support of 
this claim on the record at trial but unjustifiably failed to do 
so. Criminal Procedure Law Section 440.10 (3) (a) provides 
that the court may deny a motion to vacate judgment where 
the defendant could have, with due diligence, put facts on 
the record in support of his claim so as to permit appellate 
review, but unjustifiably failed to do so. Thus, for example, 
in People v. Friedgood, 58 N.Y.2d 467, 471 (1983), the Court 
of Appeals held that the court properly denied the defendant’s 
motion to vacate the conviction because he failed to show 
that he used due diligence in trying to adduce, at trial, facts 
to support his claim that the prosecutor had used allegedly 
coercive tactics. 


Here, although Terrence Heywood’s name is listed in 
the police reports attached to defendant’s motion, defendant 
failed to assert his claim at trial, or in a motion to set aside 
the verdict, that Heywood was not a confidential informant. 


194a 


Affirmation in Opposition Dated December 2, 1994 


In sum, because defendant failed to place facts on the 
record to allow appellate review of his claim, the trial court 
properly denied his claim. 


POINT THREE 


BECAUSE DEFENDANT KNEW ABOUT 
TERRENCE HEYWOOD BEFORE TRIAL, 
HEYWOOD’S AFFIDAVIT DID NOT CONSTI- 
TUTE NEWLY DISCOVERED EVIDENCE. 


Defendant knew about Terrence Heywood’s existence 
before trial and thus, Heywood’s affidavit did not represent 
newly discovered evidence within the meaning of C.P.L. 
§ 440.10(g). First, defendant knew about the evidence before 
trial. Indeed, defendant attached two police reports to his 
August 9, 1993 motion which delineate Heywood’s limited 
involvement in the case. Furthermore, the fact that Heywood 
alleges that he never spoke to the police is irrelevant to 
defendant’s case. Indeed, Heywood never testified at either 
of defendant’s trials. How the police came to regard 
defendant as a suspect had no bearing on his guilt or 
innocence. Once the police had defendant’s name, the 
eyewitnesses selected his photograph from a series of 
pictures, thereby providing the necessary probable cause to 
arrest defendant. Thus, defendant’s alleged newly discovered 
evidence is not material, and there is no possibility that 
defendant’s alleged newly discovered evidence would result 
in a different verdict at a new trial. People v. Salemi, 
309 N.Y. 208 (1955); cert. denied, 350 U.S. 950 (1956). 
Accordingly, defendant’s motion to vacate judgment was 
meritless and properly denied.

INTRODUCTION: 


Point B. 


All criminal defendants are guaranteed the right to 
effective assistance of competent counsel at both the 
pre-trial, trial and on appeal. See, Strickland v Washington, 
466 U.S. 668 (1984); United States vs. Cronic, 466 U.S. 648 
(1984); EVITTS vs. LUCEY, 469 U.S. 387 (1985); People 
vs. Baldi, 54 N.Y.2d 13; People v. Sims, 55 A.D.2d 629.

Affirmation in Opposition Dated December 2, 1994 


The defendant will now address the meaningful 
representation standard of Baldi adopted in People vs. 
VEZZA, 89 A.D.2d 882 to review the attorney’s effectiveness. 
The VEZZA standard is applicable to the instant case to 
warrant a reversal of this conviction due to ineffective 
assistance of counsel at both level of the proceeding(s). 


[Attached hereto for this Court consideration Exhibit 
“A-( yh.

VEZZA, 89 A.D.2d 882, 453 N.Y.S.2d 216 at p. 217 for a 
reversal of his conviction. In VEZZA the panel recognized 
the failure(s) of defense trial counsel to accord meaningful 
representation to the defendants before and at trial. At bar 
defendant here argues that the level of the failure(s) of his 
defense counsel, Mr. Greenfield, are much more unique than 
the failure(s) outlined in VEZZA. However, the standard the 
Court applied to VEZZA case is applicable to the present 
case, against which Mr. Greenfield’s effectiveness can be 
measured before this court. A VEZZA problem is evident in 
the instant case. In both, cases, the facts of Counsel failure(s) 
were serious and several. In both case(s) the identification 
were the primary issue. In both case(s) the cumulative effect 
of the failure(s) reached errors of such a nature to deprive
