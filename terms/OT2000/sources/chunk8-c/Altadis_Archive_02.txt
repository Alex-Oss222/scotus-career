QUESTION PRESENTED 


Whether Massachusetts' content-based prohibition on 
virtually any public display of cigar advertisements violates 
the First Amendment, either under the strict scrutiny 
Standard that Petitioners contend is applicable or under the 
test set forth in Central Hudson Gas & Elec. Corp. v. 
Public Serv. Comm'n, 447 U.S. 557 (1980) 


‘i 
PARTIES TO THE PROCEEDING 


The petitioners in this case are Altadis U.S.A. Inc.; 
General Cigar Co., Inc.; John Middleton, Inc.; Lane Limited; 
L.J. Peretti Co., Inc.; Swedish Match North America, Inc.; 
and Swisher International, Inc. Altadis U.S.A. Inc. is the 
successor to Consolidated Cigar Corporation and 
Havatampa, Inc. Lane Limited is the successor to Tobacco 
Exporters International (USA) Ltd. The respondent is 
Thomas F. Reilly, Attorney General of the Commonwealth 
of Massachusetts. A separate petition for certiorari has 
been filed today by Lorillard Tobacco Company; Brown & 
Williamson Tobacco Corporation; R.J. Reynolds Tobacco 
Company; Philip Morris Incorporated; and United States 
Tobacco Company, who were also parties to the 
consolidated appeals below.

CONSTITUTIONAL AND STATUTORY 
PROVISIONS INVOLVED 


The challenged regulations of the Massachusetts 
Attorney General, which are codified at 940 C.M.R. 22.00, 
are set out verbatim in the Appendix to this Petition. See 
Pet. App. 100a-111a. 

The First Amendment to the United States 
Constitution provides in relevant part: "Congress shall 
make no law . . . abridging the freedom of speech... ." 

The Fourteenth Amendment to the United States 
Constitution provides in relevant part: "No state shall... 
deprive any person of life, liberty, or property, without due 
process of law... ."
