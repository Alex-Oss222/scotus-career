### Arizona v. California — 2000-10-10

The unsigned supplemental decree implements the two settlements approved June 19, 2000, without a new allocation test or adjudication of reserved rights. Its [operative clauses A-G](../records/Arizona_v_California_supplemental-decree_2000-10-10.md#public-action) control the distinct aggregate entitlements and California present-perfected-right entries. Colorado River Reservation's aggregate is the lesser of 719,248 acre-feet or the amount for 107,903 acres and related uses, with the stated reservation-date priorities. Fort Mojave's aggregate is the lesser of 132,789 acre-feet or the amount for 20,544 acres and related uses, retaining September 19, 1890 and February 2, 1911 priorities. The California entries remain distinct: Colorado River's 10,745 acre-feet/1,612 acres (November 22, 1873), 40,241/6,037 (November 16, 1874), and 5,860/879 (May 15, 1876); Fort Mojave's 16,720/2,587 retains September 18, 1890. The inherited September 18/19 distinction is preserved without deciding its source or resolution.

The Fort Mojave increment is the lesser of 3,022 acre-feet or water for 468 acres; its settlement fixes the specified boundary near the Hay and Wood Reserve and bars further reserved Colorado River water claims by the United States and Tribe for Hay and Wood Reserve lands. The Colorado River increment is the lesser of 2,100 acre-feet or water for 315 acres; its settlement bars further such claims by the United States and Tribes for California reservation lands. The respective riverbed-title and jurisdiction claims, Fort Mojave private-land claims, and Colorado River's disputed boundary remain reserved. Absence of objections enlarges neither consent nor releases.

Clause C excepts only the western California boundaries of Fort Mojave and Colorado River reservations from the 1979 condition (5) boundary-adjustment proviso. Its remaining shortage, calculation, use and priority rules survive: when Article II(B)(3) establishes insufficient mainstream water for present perfected rights, the Secretary must first satisfy in full the specified Chemehuevi, Cocopah, Fort Yuma, Colorado River and Fort Mojave rights, then other nonmiscellaneous rights by priority date across state lines; miscellaneous rights 7-21 and 29-80 retain their order. Other boundary adjustments use the Master's December 5, 1960 methods and diversion rates (acre-feet/acre): Cocopah 6.37, Colorado River 6.67, Chemehuevi 5.97, Fort Mojave 6.46, Fort Yuma 6.67. Irrigation measures quantity, not exclusive use: other use may not consume more than the specified irrigation diversions and equivalent supplements for the specified acreage and related uses. No priority is established for unspecified Indian claims.

The decree implements the June 19 judgment, amends the identified 1964 and 1979 provisions, incorporates the stated 1984 Fort Mojave adjustment, and preserves the other decree provisions. Nebraska v. Wyoming's distinction between enforcing an established entitlement and seeking a new allocation remains unchanged. Fort Yuma merits and additional water, the reserved riverbed/private-title/jurisdiction claims, and Colorado River's disputed boundary are not decided. The original action remains open; the implementing event is complete.

### Artuz v. Bennett — 2000-11-07

Ginsburg's unanimous opinion affirms: under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a state collateral application is properly filed when delivery and acceptance comply with the rules governing filing the application. Claim-specific mandatory relief bars do not defeat otherwise proper filing. New York Criminal Procedure Law §440.10(2)(a) requires denial after merits appellate adjudication unless a subsequent retroactively effective change in controlling law occurred; paragraph (c) requires record facts sufficient for adequate appellate review and omitted review caused by an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or raise the issue on a perfected appeal. Both govern claim relief, not delivery and acceptance. Neither provision is found actually applicable to a particular Bennett claim.

Clerk acceptance does not cure a genuine filing defect. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, fees and filing preconditions remain distinct; the Court does not classify every rule without its terms. Whether deadline exceptions can preserve proper filing of a late application remains open. The Court fixes no pending period or tolling duration and makes no finding about receipt or absence of the written order; absence was an assumption below based on Bennett's allegation. Transitional timing, exhaustion, default and habeas entitlement remain outside the grant. Lindh's provision-specific temporal rule survives; it neither exempts this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA nor answers proper filing. The one-year framework and other habeas defenses remain effective; the remand awards no writ or release.

### Cleveland v. United States — 2000-11-07

Thomas's unanimous opinion holds that a State's authority to issue, withhold, renew, regulate or revoke a license held solely as sovereign regulator is not its property under 18 U.S.C. §1341. Louisiana's video-poker licenses retain that character at initial issuance and renewal; value to the recipient and expected licensing or gaming revenue do not supply the charged State-property object. The statute's acquisition-by-false-pretenses clause describes a means of property fraud, not a separate route dispensing with property in the deceived victim's hands. Both holdings independently reject the government's routes supporting these license-renewal convictions.

McNally's money-or-property boundary applies to the whole statute. Carpenter's protection of genuine intangible commercial property remains distinct. Neder's materiality element and rejection of completed-loss or actual-reliance requirements survive; materiality was not granted. Actual public money, unpaid revenue and commercially held assets present different objects; the Court decides neither every government franchise nor an independent honest-services theory. The two mail-fraud convictions are set aside. The remand reconsiders each conviction and sentence actually dependent on those predicates, using the indictment and verdict, and preserves independent convictions and bases. No unreviewed tax, laundering or racketeering count receives an automatic disposition.

### Sinkfield v. Kelley — 2000-11-27

The per curiam opinion requires a districting plaintiff to establish personal exposure to the challenged electoral treatment, causation and redressability. Residence in a majority-white district adjoining majority-minority districts and the neighbors' influence on map design do not alone establish personal racial-assignment injury; this record supplies no additional allegation or evidence connecting these plaintiffs to that treatment. Direct or circumstantial proof may suffice. There is no expert-study requirement, numerical racial threshold, electoral-defeat requirement, universal district-shape bar or residence-based entitlement to standing.

Johnson v. Miller's separate personal-exposure and shape-evidence holdings apply; shape can support an inference linked to actual treatment. Bush v. Vera preserves the personal-stake requirement. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (June 2, 1997) requires jurisdiction before contested merits; Ruhrgas permits bounded sequencing of nonmerits questions, not assumed injury. Shaw's substantive design-only rule remains unchanged. Personal unequal treatment may confer standing despite failure on the merits; independently supported dilution, exclusion or unequal participation is outside this record. Both judgments are vacated and the seven-district injunction dissolved for jurisdictional dismissal, without deciding the design-only merits, certifying a map or ordering a replacement. No tally or individual lineup is published.

### Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College — 2000-11-27

Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices, affirms personal damages dismissal. Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized conduct-date notice; Elder requires all relevant authority and does not make an identical precedent necessary. January 1997 law did not clearly establish the claimed personal First Amendment right against punishment for speech the employee denies making. The Court resolves the adequate preserved immunity defense without recognizing or rejecting that constitutional extension and without adopting an actual-authorship prerequisite.

Pickering, Connick and Mt. Healthy retain their expression, employment-interest, causation and same-decision rules. Waters's actual-speech and mistaken-account holdings, distinct immunity defense and unresolved complete investigation framework do not settle mistaken speaker identity. McIntyre's actual anonymous-advocacy protection is distinguished. Elder imposes no mandatory merits-first sequence; Crawford-El permits an adequate legal defense first and preserves ordinary motive proof, but its June 1997 decision supplies no January notice. The Court finds neither reasonable investigation, unprotected writings nor lawful conduct, and creates no investigation tort, heightened motive-proof rule or liability for every identity mistake. The only established live relief is individual damages; no municipal claim or continuing prospective threat is established. The pleaded revival period expired October 28, 2000. Reinstatement did not moot damages. Stone-Zsela dissents; his constitutional and immunity proposals are noncontrolling.

### City of Indianapolis v. Edmond — 2000-11-28

Souter's seven-Justice opinion (Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer joining) holds that a checkpoint stop is a seizure before exterior canine examination. A program principally directed to gathering evidence of ordinary narcotics offenses requires an independently lawful justification for dispensing with individualized suspicion; brief systematic stops and ancillary license or sobriety checks do not supply it. The admitted primary objective resolves this program's purpose without a fact-finding remand. Program instructions, official statements and actual practices may establish purpose; no statistical study, universal efficacy threshold, catastrophe requirement or exhaustion of every less intrusive alternative is required.

Whren's probable-cause rule does not establish authority for stopping everyone without suspicion. Chandler requires an actual adequate need; Sitz's highway-safety and Martinez-Fuerte's border settings remain distinct. Prouse supplies no general authorization from systematic selection; Bond preserves the distinction between lawful presence and separate intrusion without adding a checkpoint-purpose test. Comparable ordinary crime-detection roadblocks fall within the rule, but genuinely justified programs and lawful action on evidence encountered during them retain their own requirements. The Court reserves expansion of a program principally justified by license or sobriety checking to narcotics detection. Urgent threats, emergency roadblocks, airport or government-building security, other border settings, individualized-suspicion searches and the particular canine examination remain outside the challenge. The antecedent seizure makes the dog-sniff classification unnecessary to decide. The 7-2 affirmance and remand require preliminary prospective restraint while litigation continues; no replacement program, suppression or damages follows.

### Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America — 2000-11-28

Breyer's unanimous opinion enforces the actual conditional award. A reviewing court must identify an explicit, well-defined, dominant public policy grounded in law and legal precedent and a conflict with the performance the award requires. The transportation regime permits rehabilitation and qualified return; a general safety objection to repeated prior drug use cannot justify vacatur when the award preserves the regime's safeguards. Misco and W. R. Grace supply the law-grounded-policy and conflict inquiry; Enterprise Wheel preserves the unchallenged contractual just-cause assignment. First Options does not replace section 301 labor-award doctrine.

The first award imposed thirty days without pay, substance-abuse-program participation and testing at the employer's or approved professional's discretion over five years. After four negative tests and a second positive test, the second award imposed more than three months without pay, reimbursement of both parties' costs of both arbitrations, continued treatment and random testing, and an undated resignation triggered by another positive test within five years. No future trigger is found. Before safety-sensitive work, independent law requires professional evaluation, certification of compliance with any prescribed rehabilitation, a negative return-to-duty test and at least six random tests during the first year after return. Treatment depends on professional assessment and prescription; the award's program condition remains operative. Employment reinstatement does not establish completed compliance or an entitlement to drive now. A positive test is not a finding of driving under the influence.

The full objection to this award is resolved without determining whether every public-policy objection must rest on performance directly forbidden by positive law. That outer question remains open because the actual policy conflict fails on either view. Different awards contrary to governing legal policy remain reviewable. No general right to reinstatement or new public-policy exception is created.

### Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board — 2000-12-04

The per curiam opinion vacates and remands, 9-0, under sections 1257 and 2106 and National Tea. A final state judgment's material unresolved dependence on state law may require authoritative clarification before sound resolution of the federal challenge. The complete November 21 certification directive was final despite implementation and a separate possible contest. The court must clarify (1) whether the late-return discretion restrictions and selected acceptance date independently construe pre-election law and the role of state constitutional power, and (2) whether a particular federal electoral requirement compelled that date or the full-participation condition and how that premise affected relief. The Court acknowledges the substantial statutory explanation already given; it assumes neither legislative displacement nor independent statutory sufficiency. McPherson supplies Article II's legislative-direction setting; ordinary state interpretation remains available. Smiley's congressional setting does not resolve this presidential application.

All nine join Parts I, II and IV. Part II holds that then-effective section 5 offers conclusive federal-count treatment for a qualifying final determination under pre-election law made at least six days before the electors meet; it does not itself prohibit all later counting. December 12 and 18 remain distinct dates. Foster's congressional-election holding supplies no presidential recount cutoff. No final qualification or forfeiture of the section 5 benefit is decided.

Part III has eight Associate joins; Stone-Zsela joins only that component's remand disposition. Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina, January 21, 1999, supplies the separate due-process distinction between authoritative construction of ambiguity and fundamental unfairness. Actual guidance, dissemination, inducement, reliance and voting circumstances matter without a new rigid checklist. Conflicting post-election advice establishes neither pre-election assurance nor detrimental reliance; the source limit is not litigant default. Neither interpretation after voting alone nor the absence of reliance facts resolves the entire submitted challenge. The legal predicates require clarification.

The Record retains each distinct manual-request, sample-error response, late-return, personal-fine, overseas-ballot and November 26/27 contingency in its Holdings table, identifying statutory commands separately from state constructions and judicial relief. It directs no particular answer, tally, rejected ballot, elector or winner. No final Article II or due-process violation is decided; no equal-protection question is presented. Federal jurisdiction is not retained, no independent stay issues, and vacatur does not automatically reverse every administrative act. Any later federal case requires its own supported posture and an actual dependency refresh.

### Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices — 2000-12-11

Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, sustains Maine's $250 per-election legislative contribution ceiling, 6-3. The State retains the burden to demonstrate that a direct candidate-contribution ceiling is closely drawn to an objectively supported, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest while preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy in the actual campaign setting. Russell and Shrink Missouri supply that standard, not a fixed dollar floor, general $250 safe harbor or requirement that challengers prove campaigning impossible. Maine's evidence of contributor pressure for official accommodation, campaign scale, actual fundraising, per-election operation and usable private channels meets its burden on the combined record. Completed bribery and statistical proof are unnecessary. Static receipt projections establish a serious risk, not future causal loss; special-election experience is corroborative rather than conclusive, and donor percentages are not dollar-loss measures. Referendum support cannot settle constitutional rights, and public financing cannot cure an independently invalid private restriction.

Part II has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Both distinct triggers survive 5-4: responsive grants measured by private candidate financing, and grants measured by qualifying independent advocacy. A capped, support-qualified mechanism may respond to either where its demonstrated programmatic connection and actual operation establish neither an independent legal restraint nor substantial practical suppression of opposing lawful activity. The private speaker retains its funds, message, lawful fundraising and spending, without endorsement, attribution or an expenditure ceiling. The qualified recipient accepts restrictions in an actual contest; supplements are finite and adjusted for specified favorable independent spending. The record establishes competitive effects rather than substantial suppression. This is neither unlimited permission to offset speech nor a categorical claim that subsidies cannot burden it. The State cannot avoid examining the consequence by calling enrollment voluntary.

Buckley preserves the contribution/expenditure distinction and voluntary public funding with participant conditions; independent expenditures do not become coordinated contributions because a grant formula measures them. Reform Party rejects an automatic entitlement to parity or insulation from an opponent's public resources while preserving actual suppression review. Day's invalidating judgment and actual vote-supported propositions remain binding for its materially different mechanism. Neither its four-Justice expressive-burden rationale nor Stone-Zsela's separate rationale becomes controlling by aggregation. Daggett's five direct Part II joins require no narrowest-rationale inference.

Part III has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. It rejects an additional whole-system coercion claim because the combined program and sustained ceiling leave a meaningful private campaigning alternative on this record. Lawful components can combine into an unconstitutional burden; nominal voluntariness, attractive incentives or a better-funded opponent do not alone resolve that inquiry. Actual contribution capacity, the grant cap, participant duties and conditional shortage rule support the result. No fixed funding percentage, mandatory study or proof of a disappeared candidate is required. O'Connor's Part III join preserves her matching objection; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not reach this additional theory after their proposed targeted invalidations and supply no sustaining votes. All three rules preserve review of materially different suppressive applications. No component is invalidated and no severability remedy becomes operative.

**Operative statutory qualifications.** The legislative ceiling is per election; the individual candidate's own and spouse's contributions retain §1015(1)'s exception. That exception does not remove separate restrictions accepted by a certified public participant. A legislative participant must file the declaration, obtain ballot qualification and compliant seed funding, and collect qualifying $5 contributions from at least 150 registered district voters for a Senate race or 50 for a House race. Contributions go to the fund by check or money order during the qualifying period with the candidate's knowledge and approval, an identifying receipt, and no exchanged value. Seed money is limited to $100 per individual, including candidate or family funds, and aggregate legislative amounts of $1,500 for Senate or $500 for House, subject to Commission rule authority. Unspent seed money transfers to the fund at certification. Later private contributions require specific Commission authorization.

Contested-election initial grants use the corresponding office's average expenditures in contested races in the prior two applicable elections, with the initial 2000 amount reduced by 25 percent; inadequate data permits use of the most recent applicable elections. An uncontested primary uses the corresponding uncontested average or the contested average if lower. No grant issues for an uncontested general election.

The matching measure is the greater of expenditures or obligations and funds raised or borrowed, alone or with qualifying independent expenditures reported under §1019. Once that measure exceeds the applicable initial distribution, the Commission pays the opposing certified candidate the reported excess. Supplements stop at twice the relevant contested-primary or contested-general initial distribution, making the ordinary combined maximum three times that distribution. The regulatory deduction reduces matching payments for independent express advocacy favoring the participant or opposing the private opponent after that opponent exceeds the initial distribution; it never reduces the initial grant. No unpresented multiple-opponent formula is decided.

Section 1125(13) forbids spending beyond available fund revenue. If revenue cannot meet the initial or matching distributions, the Commission may authorize certified candidates to accept and spend private contributions up to the applicable funding amounts, reduced by seed money and subject to the legislative $250 per-donor-per-election ceiling and Commission rules. This is conditional permission, not a mandatory release or unlimited financing right. No actual shortage is found.

**Scope.** These qualifications describe the mechanism adjudicated; unchallenged administrative details receive no independent constitutional holding. The separate §1019 disclosure challenge, governor ceiling, unsupported party-specific theory, and unpursued primary-allocation and §1017 theories are outside this grant. Statutory participant terminology establishes no government command to stigmatize nonparticipants.

### Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph — 2000-12-11

Part I of Stevens's opinion has all nine joins: compelling arbitration and dismissing every claim ends the action and is final and appealable under 9 U.S.C. §16(a)(3). Catlin's ordinary finality rule applies; a possible later award proceeding is separate. Digital Equipment's restrictions on premature collateral appeals do not bar this final appeal. A stay leaving claims pending is different, and appealability does not approve dismissal instead of a stay.

Part II-A also has all nine joins: contractual silence about arbitral costs alone proves neither unenforceability nor affordability, and genuinely prohibitive required costs may prevent effective assertion of federal statutory rights. Part II-B has five direct joins—Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—and requires focused development of the likely forum, mandatory access costs, allocation and the claimant's financial access on this record. Randolph's assertion of inability and the contract's silence establish no likely fee or allocation, but the absence of those facts does not establish an accessible forum. Substantiation of an actual obstacle is required during the appropriate proceedings. The categorical cost ruling is vacated 5-4; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse it on the deficient showing without the required new cost opportunity.

Mitsubishi and Gilmer preserve effective substantive statutory protection while allowing arbitration; Allied-Bruce preserves actual enforceability defenses after coverage. First Options preserves the relevant antecedent independent inquiry within the agreement's actual consent and delegation limits, without deciding a coverage or consent dispute here. The access inquiry concerns likely mandatory charges and this claimant's ability to pursue these claims, not a general right to costless dispute resolution or a universal lender-payment duty. A binding allocation or undertaking may remove an obstacle; a nonbinding appellate assurance does not amend the agreement. No finding that discovery was requested, denied or exhausted supports or is needed for this remand.

No majority adopts a complete general initial-proof rule for the trigger, consequences of nonproduction and unavailable cost information beyond the supported-obstacle requirement and this focused opportunity. Stone-Zsela's concrete-likelihood formulation includes ordinary-procedure disclosure of material information controlled by the party seeking compulsion and this opportunity to develop the objection. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas use their likelihood requirement to reject this showing without that required opportunity and do not adopt his disclosure qualification. Their shared terminology supplies no identical operative majority rule. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject deriving individual-access allocation merely from categorical congressional exclusion of statutory claims, without supplying a comprehensive replacement; Breyer joins the inquiry but reserves that broader critique. No narrowest-rationale aggregation resolves the division, and the current remand is complete despite the unresolved general allocation.

If the obstacle is unproved or lawfully removed, the cost objection does not prevent arbitration. If established, enforcement must be withheld to the necessary extent, subject to a lawful binding solution. Properly presented independent grounds remain, including the class-action contention not decided below. The Court decides neither TILA or ECOA merits, class relief, unrelated terms, nor whether the district court originally should have stayed the case. No unconditional arbitration mandate follows.

### Bush v. Gore — 2000-12-12

Souter's multipart opinion produces a 5-2-2 judgment: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer choose corrective vacatur and immediate remand; Scalia and Thomas choose independent Article II reversal with only conditional equality correction; Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm. These positions are not seven unconditional votes for one remand.

Part I has eight reasoned joins, all Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees only in taking review. Section 1257(a) permits review within Cox's fourth category where the highest state court conclusively authorizes challenged relief despite a substantial federal objection that would foreclose it, later nonfederal success could eliminate review, and postponement could seriously impair the asserted federal policy. Goodyear distinguishes a substantial complete-bar claim from its ultimate success. The preserved objections to the operative inclusion and examination decree satisfy those predicates; urgency, consent, absent rehearing and unfinished ballot disputes create no general finality exception. The December 6 presentation of particular Article II objections remains distinct from the December 8 refusal of leave to make a blanket claim that §102.168 never applies to presidential elections. Neither hypothetical implementation nor that blanket claim is adjudicated.

Part II has seven direct joins: Souter, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. A statewide judicial vote determination combining recovered votes and further examination may not use materially arbitrary operative criteria for legally comparable ballots. Existing legal-vote requirements need sufficiently definite, consistent and reviewable application. Relevant physical differences and immaterial administrative variation remain permissible; no national physical-mark code results. Spencer's conflicting Miami-Dade thresholds and Burton's successive Palm Beach approaches, without established consistent reconsideration of every affected earlier determination, support the identified criteria conflict. A general intent command and honest officials do not cure it. The rule requires neither bad faith nor proof that the winner changes and rests neither on complaint-only Broward allegations, proved duplicate votes, a matched-ballot study nor an assumption that every overvote contains a recoverable vote. Harper and Reynolds supply equal participation and weight; Burdick's neutral-administration rule and Shaw's rejection of territorial design alone remain effective. The equality rule applies beyond this election.

Part III has six reasoned joins: Souter, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela adds only a vote rejecting independent Article II reversal. Article II preserves legislative choice of elector-appointment rules while permitting ordinary judicial interpretation and enforcement of the enacted code. The contest, rejected-legal-vote, investigative, corrective and existing intent provisions support these directions without proved material legislative displacement. They permit examination before proof of the winner and do not confine a contest judge to appellate review of board discretion. Palm Beach requires actual statutory authority, without conclusive deference to a state label or a presumption that all interpretation changes the scheme. The December 11 statutory clarification supports the construction; general remedial power still cannot erase substantive voting rules, remaining certification functions or institutional allocation. No undervote automatically becomes legal, no new mark requirement or unlimited late amendment is authorized, and the exact state timing consequence remains distinct. Scalia and Thomas dissent on separate institutional-allocation and substantive legal-vote grounds.

Part IV has eight reasoned joins, all Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting separate due-process reversal. Under Yoshina and Palm Beach, neutral construction of an ambiguous election provision does not alone establish fundamental unfairness because it differs from prior practice or advice. Supported official inducement or frustration of electoral choice requires examination of actual directions, dissemination, reliance and voting circumstances. No verified pre-election assurance, dissemination or detrimental reliance establishes the separate deprivation here. Missing proof is not litigant default, and the equality holding does not depend on inducement. Deliberately misleading directions, pervasive frustration and supported reliance claims remain distinct.

All nine join Part V-A: then-operative 3 U.S.C. §5 gives conclusive federal-count treatment to a final elector-appointment determination under state procedures enacted before election day and made pursuant to that law at least six days before the electors meet. #### Statutory scope and operative qualifications

The direct-treasury holding concerns the presented business-corporation-to-federal-candidate class. Section 441b(a) covers national-bank and congressionally organized corporate contributions or expenditures in elections for any political office, including candidate-selection primaries, conventions, and caucuses. For corporations generally and labor organizations, it covers elections for presidential and vice-presidential electors, Senators, Representatives, Delegates, and Resident Commissioners, and the corresponding nomination processes. It separately prohibits knowing acceptance or receipt by candidates, political committees, or other persons, and consent to prohibited organizational contributions or expenditures by corporate or bank officers or directors and labor-organization officers. A labor organization includes an organization, agency, employee committee, or plan with employee participation and at least a partial purpose of dealing with employers over grievances, labor disputes, wages, pay rates, hours, or working conditions. These distinct classes receive no new independent holdings merely because they share the section; independent expenditures and press activity remain outside this decision.

Section 441b(b)(2) reaches direct or indirect payments, distributions, loans, advances, deposits, gifts, services, and value to candidates, campaign committees, political parties, or organizations in connection with covered elections. It excepts ordinary-course national or State bank loans complying with applicable banking law and regulations. Three separate exclusions protect corporate communications on any subject to stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families, or labor communications to members and families; nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote activity directed to the corresponding groups; and establishment, administration, and solicitation for separate segregated political funds by corporations, labor organizations, membership organizations, cooperatives, and nonstock corporations. The distinct membership-communication exclusion and reporting proviso in §431, preserved in Akins, are not substituted for these provisions.

The political fund cannot use value obtained by physical force, job discrimination, financial reprisal or threats; payments required for labor-organization membership or employment; or commercial transactions. Any person soliciting an employee must disclose, at the time of solicitation, the fund's political purpose and the employee's right to refuse without reprisal. Corporate support for establishment, administration, and solicitation does not authorize treasury money as the fund's candidate-contribution money.

Ordinary corporate solicitation reaches stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families; ordinary labor solicitation reaches members and their families. Corporations, labor organizations, and their funds may also make two written solicitations per calendar year to corporate stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, employees, and their families, mailed to residences and designed to conceal from the solicitor who gives $50 or less and who gives nothing. Additional routes preserve member solicitation by membership organizations, cooperatives, nonstock corporations, and their funds, and trade-association or trade-association-fund solicitation with a member corporation's separate, specific approval, limited to one association per corporation per calendar year. That trade-association route reaches the approving member corporation's stockholders, executive or administrative personnel, and their families.

Lawful methods of soliciting or facilitating voluntary fund contributions available to corporations for stockholders and executive or administrative personnel must also be available to labor organizations for members. A corporation using those methods, including its subsidiaries, branches, divisions, and affiliates, must provide them on written request to a labor organization representing members working for those entities at reimbursement of expenses only. Executive or administrative personnel must be salaried rather than hourly and have policymaking, managerial, professional, or supervisory responsibility. These statutory routes establish no finding that the particular companies possess a successful fund, sufficient donor pool, or practical resources.

Section 441f contains no internal blanket exception. The actual contribution definition, true source, attributable reporting, knowledge and enforcement requirements, and supported associational protections retain their separate work. None of the indictment allegations supplies a completed exchange, corrupt intent, knowledge, or actual influence finding.

### Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Brewer_v_West_Irondequoit_Central_School_District_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Brewer_v_West_Irondequoit_Central_School_District_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Brewer_v_West_Irondequoit_Central_School_District_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The actual exclusion requires substantial justification

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-school program that changes a child's transfer opportunity because of race must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and the classification's coverage, operation and actual individual burdens. It must give a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage; unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, demographic imbalance or a beneficial label does not discharge that burden.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has seven direct joins. Part III has the same coalition. Scalia and Thomas accept the governing standard's binding force but reject this application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the common justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications. Eisenberg applies it to school transfers and requires justification of the individual denial. Haak received written acceptance that was withdrawn after officials verified that she was white. Her lost opportunity is an actual racial burden, unlike Sinkfield's neighboring-district injury theory. The program must explain how its directional transfer restriction serves the educational conditions it identifies and why that child's exclusion has the required relationship to the objective. Brown's prohibition of state-imposed racial caste remains intact.

The inquiry does not demand a commissioned study, fixed ratio, statistical demonstration or proof that every conceivable substitute fails. Testimony, program records, historical experience, admissions, comparisons, observed operation and reasonable inferences may support the relationship. Concrete alternatives must receive the consideration the governing standard requires; an abstract preference for race-neutrality cannot replace that inquiry. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and allocation for its own sake remain impermissible.

#### II. Supported voluntary integration is a permissible educational objective

**Holding and operative rule:** Supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation in public elementary and secondary schools can constitute an important lawful objective without a prior finding of de jure segregation. That permission neither requires racial preferences nor validates a numerical target or the particular transfer restriction: the government must still prove its educational claim, substantial fit and justified individual burdens.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion has six direct joins: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy does not join this affirmative objective holding; his acceptance of Eisenberg's binding force is not an additional affirmative vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Eisenberg recognizes the educational objective and rejects a categorical decree prerequisite. Meaningful educational contact and access can matter independently of an existing judicial dismantlement duty. Absence of a decree establishes neither such a duty nor invalidity. The program's interdistrict design may operate differently from a restriction based solely on county percentages. Its longevity, limited scale and changing demographics are relevant evidence, but do not conclusively establish either constitutional adequacy or the absence of every educational benefit. A potentially lawful objective likewise cannot justify Haak's exclusion without evidence about the program's actual operation. The Constitution permits the supported objective within lawful governmental authority; it supplies no presumption validating this program.

#### III. Correct the preliminary-relief inquiry without ordering placement

**Holding and operative rule:** The vacatur of the mandatory preliminary-admission injunction is affirmed, the appellate judgment is vacated insofar as its substantive directions employ the displaced constitutional standard, and the matter is remanded for prompt application of the common standard and ordinary equitable requirements. Existing supported findings and concessions retain their scope; further evidence or proceedings require an identified unresolved material issue, and any placement order requires current enrollment, continuing injury, educational-disruption and other material equitable facts.

**Authority:** Part III carries the seven-Justice coalition identified for Part I; Scalia and Thomas oppose this disposition because they would treat the preliminary merits prerequisite as already established against the exclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand and Eisenberg require correction of a displaced legal framework without automatically reopening proof or awarding individual relief. The preliminary record does not establish the program's complete justification, but neither does it conclusively disprove its claimed interdistrict educational benefits. State funding requires a qualifying program designed to reduce racial isolation and an annual demonstration. The appellate account of inevitable funding loss is not an express statutory prohibition on every outward transfer by a white city pupil. That distinction bears on the asserted necessity of the categorical eligibility line.

The mandatory-injunction merits requirement and other equitable prerequisites remain independent. The existing irreparable-harm determination does not alone establish entitlement to a mandatory seat order. The earlier conditional protection lasted only through 1999–2000 and only if Haak had begun attending; it creates no January 2001 placement right. The original mandatory injunction is not reinstated, and this Court orders neither admission nor removal. Final program findings and current interim relief are ordinary application and remedial work under the stated rules. Unappealed contract and estoppel grounds, and other claims outside the equal-protection question granted, are not decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: applies its common substantial-justification standard and bounded-remand rule unchanged; no displaced strict-scrutiny formulation is restored.
- Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools: applies its school-transfer standard, supported K–12 educational objective and independent equitable-remedy limits to this interdistrict program; its objective holding is reaffirmed by six Justices.
- Brown v. Board of Education: retains its prohibition of state-imposed racial caste; voluntary integration is not a license for racial hierarchy.
- Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County: applies the requirement of a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives without requiring rejection of every conceivable substitute.
- Sinkfield v. Kelley: distinguishes a claim lacking personal exposure from the withdrawal of this child's actual educational opportunity; the personal-injury requirement remains in force.

### Fiore v. White — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The existing missing element defeats both challenged convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not sustain a conviction when its authoritative account of the law in force at the relevant time identifies a necessary element and the State concedes it produced no evidence of that element. Pennsylvania's clarification that actual absence of the required permit was always an element of the owner/operator offense therefore establishes a due-process violation in both of Fiore's challenged no-permit convictions; deviation from a permit's terms cannot substitute for its absence.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both holdings and the bounded reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** In re Winship requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of every element. Jackson v. Virginia enforces that protection against a conviction unsupported on a necessary element. State law identifies the offense; the federal Constitution requires its elements to be proved. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's August 21, 2000 answer establishes that Commonwealth v. Scarpone clarified the statute's existing meaning, including its meaning when Fiore's convictions became final. The Commonwealth does not dispute that Fiore possessed a permit. The asserted departure from its conditions thus cannot establish the different no-permit element.

The Third Circuit's premise that a new state rule was unavailable on collateral review cannot survive the authoritative answer. The Court decides no general obligation to apply genuinely new state constructions retroactively: there is no new construction here. Nor can the seriousness of other environmental misconduct supply the missing element. The distinct authorization, transportation-license and permit-compliance provisions do not become substitute offenses in this dispute. The ruling covers the no-permit conviction from each trial, not the other convictions among Fiore's sixty convictions.

#### II. The constitutional ruling and the conditions for the writ remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's rejection of the constitutional claim is reversed and the case remanded to resolve any properly preserved, actually undecided statutory prerequisite to habeas relief under the applicable post-April 24, 1996 regime. If those prerequisites permit relief, the habeas order must remove both invalid no-permit convictions and end custody attributable to them, preserving independent convictions and lawful custody.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion directly adopts this remand and the following statutory conditions; the Court makes no finding that an unresolved gate is satisfied or violated.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh v. Murphy distinguishes applications filed before enactment from later applications; Fiore's later filing is not exempt from the applicable amendments. Under section 2254(b), relief requires exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Merits denial despite nonexhaustion is permitted, not required; the State waives exhaustion only through counsel's express waiver. The appellate court expressly left exhaustion and state merits adjudication undecided.

If the relevant claim was actually adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, section 2254(d) requires the statutory inquiry. Williams v. Taylor preserves the independent contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses, the objective legal standard and the restriction to this Court's holdings at the actual state merits decision. Winship and Jackson are preexisting rules, not rules originating in today's decision. The separate statutory factual-review provisions retain their own triggers and force. A finding of constitutional error cannot silently resolve those questions; conversely, remand supplies no permission to reopen the conceded permit fact, the state court's authoritative answer or the decided constitutional issue. The court below must resolve a real preserved prerequisite and implement the resulting conviction-specific relief, rather than infer an unconditional all-count writ.

**Precedent treatment:**

- In re Winship: applies its proof-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt requirement to an existing state-law element; the seriousness of other conduct cannot supply the omitted proof.
- Jackson v. Virginia: applies its protection against conviction without evidence sufficient to establish each necessary element.
- Fiore v. White, November 30, 1999: completes the reserved federal inquiry after the authoritative state answer; the earlier certification itself supplied neither state-law meaning nor a federal merits judgment.
- Lindh v. Murphy: preserves its filing-date distinction; this post-enactment application falls outside its pre-enactment exemption.
- Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams): applies its independent statutory clauses and objective, date-bounded Supreme Court-law inquiry if an actual state merits adjudication triggers section 2254(d); no exclusive allocation between the clauses is created.

### Gitlitz v. Commissioner — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Gitlitz_v_Commissioner_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Gitlitz_v_Commissioner_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Gitlitz_v_Commissioner_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. Excluded discharge income passes through and increases basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the Code version governing the 1991 returns, an insolvent S corporation's debt-discharge amount excluded from gross income by section 108(a) remains an item of income under section 1366(a)(1)(A), passes through in each shareholder's pro rata share and increases stock basis under section 1367(a)(1)(A). Losses and deductions are then limited by section 1366(d)(1), using that adjusted stock basis and the shareholder's qualifying debt basis; exclusion remains capped by the corporation's insolvency immediately before discharge and supplies no free-standing deduction entitlement.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those eight directly adopt both holdings and the statutory remand. Breyer dissents from this item's pass-through and resulting basis increase.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 61 identifies discharge of indebtedness as income. Section 108 excludes the qualifying amount from gross income without declaring that the item ceases to be income. Section 1366(a)(1)(A) expressly encompasses tax-exempt income, and section 1367(a)(1)(A) links the basis increase to that category. The Code therefore supplies both the income classification and the adjustment.

Section 108(d)(7)(A), as applicable in 1991, directs that subsections (a), (b) and (g) operate at the corporate level. That direction determines where exclusion and attribute reduction occur; it does not erase the income item or suspend the shareholder provisions. Section 1367(b)(1)'s return-inclusion restriction concerns amounts required to be included in gross income, and does not prohibit this increase for an excluded item that section 1366 recognizes. Bufferd preserves the different statutory functions of corporate and shareholder tax consequences. Hill likewise recognizes distinct statutory accounts, without a general prohibition against multiple tax benefits.

The Commissioner's objection that shareholders receive a benefit without fresh economic outlay cannot add a condition absent from these coordinated provisions. The result is confined to the presented statutory mechanism and version. It neither makes every excluded receipt a basis item nor exempts a claimed deduction from other applicable limits. Here the reported $2,021,296 discharge is less than the corporation's reported $2,181,748 insolvency; the holding determines the legal treatment rather than entering an unsupported final tax amount.

#### II. The discharge-year calculation precedes the prescribed attribute reduction

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 108(b)(4)(A) requires the corporate tax-attribute reductions described in section 108(b)(2) after determination of the tax for the discharge year. For these S corporation returns, that determination includes the pass-through income, basis increase and otherwise allowable shareholder losses; only losses still disallowed under section 1366(d)(1) are treated by section 108(d)(7)(B) as a corporate net operating loss for this attribute-reduction calculation.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Court opinion directly adopts the sequence. Breyer's conditional acceptance of the timing command for an item that properly enters shareholder tax is not a join to this actual item's pass-through, basis or sequence holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Determining discharge-year shareholder tax requires calculating basis and allowable losses. Section 1366(d)(1) expressly uses basis determined with the section 1367(a)(1) income adjustments. Only then can the court identify the losses still disallowed and apply section 108(d)(7)(B). That paragraph retains work when losses exceed properly adjusted basis; it need not suppress the income adjustment to operate.

The Tenth Circuit's reduction-first approach reverses the enacted sequence. Breyer's competing construction instead disputes whether the special corporate exclusion-and-offset mechanism permits this shareholder item at all. The Court rejects that antecedent restriction for the textual reasons in Part I. Once the item enters the calculation, the express after-tax command governs. A perceived double benefit cannot authorize a court to rearrange that command. Atlantic Mutual permits administrative construction within genuine interpretive room, but supplies no power to contradict an express statutory direction or a general revenue-protection override; no applicable authorized regulation identified here independently changes the sequence.

The judgment is reversed and remanded for recomputation of the deficiencies and permitted losses under these rules, followed by the applicable corporate attribute reduction. The reported half shares and claimed losses of $1,010,648 per shareholder are not automatic awards. Ordinary amount, basis and deduction limits remain for application. Later statutory changes, other entities and other tax years lie outside the question presented; no new evidentiary hearing or reopening of settled tax facts is directed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bufferd v. Commissioner: applies the distinction between S corporation and shareholder statutory tax functions; its return-and-limitations holding is not converted into an answer it had already given to debt discharge.
- United States v. Hill: applies its respect for distinct statutory tax accounts and retains its rejection of a general ban on multiple tax benefits; its mineral-basis rule is unchanged.
- Atlantic Mutual Insurance Co. v. Commissioner: preserves deference within actual statutory interpretive room while distinguishing a construction that would contradict the express sequence; no general revenue-protection exception is adopted.
- Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert: preserves the need to give operative statutory and regulatory qualifications independent work; it supplies no authority to replace this Code sequence with a categorical economic-outlay condition.

### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Solid_Waste_Agency_of_Northern_Cook_County_v_US_Army_Corps_of_Engineers_merits_2001-01-09.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The asserted bird-habitat criterion does not supply section 404 authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 404 of the Clean Water Act does not authorize federal jurisdiction over the isolated, nonnavigable intrastate ponds presented solely because migratory birds use them. The Corps must identify authority within the enacted water program; an asserted interstate environmental benefit alone does not supply the delegated jurisdiction, and the challenged jurisdiction and permit-denial ground must be set aside to the extent they depend exclusively on that criterion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Five Justices directly adopt this statutory ground, its limits and reversal with remand. The Court does not reach the contingent Commerce Clause question.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 404(a) authorizes permits for discharge of dredged or fill material into navigable waters. Section 502(7) defines those waters to include waters of the United States and the territorial seas. That expansion reaches beyond classical navigation without making the enacted water limitation meaningless. Riverside Bayview sustained adjacent wetlands because of their statutory and ecological relationship to covered waters and expressly left nonadjacent waters open. These isolated ponds are not wetlands under the Corps' vegetation definition and are not alleged to adjoin navigable waters. Bird use does not itself supply that missing statutory relationship.

Section 404(g)(1) allows state administration outside the waters reserved to federal administration, confirming coverage beyond traditional navigation without defining every remaining pond as covered. Section 101(a)'s biological-integrity and wildlife objectives operate consistently with the Act's provisions and alongside section 101(b)'s protection of state responsibilities. Neither purpose alone settles the disputed jurisdiction. MCI and FDA v. Brown & Williamson require authority in the connected statutory scheme before deference; Sweet Home construed a different enacted habitat-injury definition and supplies no substitute delegation here.

The Corps' change of position is not automatically disqualifying, and the preamble's form is not an independent reason to invalidate it. No universal rule governing Chevron, Skidmore or interpretation of an agency's own regulation is necessary: the asserted reach exceeds the statute on its terms. Christensen's limited advisory-letter holding is not enlarged. Because the statutory answer disposes of this assertion, Congress's possible broader power is not decided. The Court neither imposes present navigability as a universal prerequisite nor determines all isolated waters or materially different supported statutory connections.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes: preserves its adjacent-wetland construction and ecological reasoning, distinguishing the expressly reserved isolated-water application rather than treating adjacency as a universal statutory test.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: applies the requirement that an agency act within the actual statutory grant; an administrative criterion cannot supply omitted authority.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.: applies analysis of the connected statutory scheme before deference; the decision supplies no general rule against consequential agency regulation.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon: distinguishes its habitat-injury construction under a different statutory definition and preserves its actual-injury and causation limits.
- Christensen v. Harris County: leaves intact its specific advisory-letter and own-regulation holdings; it does not establish a categorical rule against every preamble or informal interpretation.
- United States v. Lopez, National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, and United States v. Morrison: leave their actual Commerce rules unchanged because the statutory ground makes the contingent constitutional question unnecessary.

### Lopez v. Davis — 2001-01-10

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Lopez_v_Davis_merits_2001-01-10.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The Bureau may apply this reasoned firearm-conduct category

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3621(e)(2)(B) permits the Bureau of Prisons to withhold a discretionary early-release reduction categorically from otherwise eligible prisoners whose current felony conduct involved carrying, possessing or using a firearm or other dangerous weapon, under the challenged 1997 regulation. A nonviolent conviction and successful completion of qualifying treatment are necessary conditions for a reduction, not an entitlement to individual selection or an award; any reduction remains limited to one year, and the category must remain within the statute and rationally applied to the actual qualifying conduct.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six adopt the entire statutory ground, its public-safety application and affirmance. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Kennedy dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress distinguished treatment qualification, threshold eligibility for a reduction and the Bureau's discretionary choice whether to grant one. The permission to reduce custody does not require the Bureau to decide each recurring safety issue anew. Heckler v. Campbell permits valid rules within a statutory delegation; it supplies no authority to disregard that delegation. Here firearm involvement in felony conduct rationally bears on safe early release. Congress's nonviolent-conviction threshold does not direct the Bureau to disregard that conduct at the separate selection stage. The 1997 rule does not reclassify the conviction as violent.

Yang preserves statutory limits, reasoned administration and the distinction between eligibility and relief. Its immigration-waiver rule forbids using qualifying fraud automatically to defeat every eligible applicant; this subset does not extinguish the release incentive for all eligible prisoners. Koray likewise requires the statutory categories to do their work and supplies no independent deference shortcut. The Court's conclusion rests on this delegation and reasoned safety criterion, not administrative convenience alone.

The Bureau's separate duty to make appropriate treatment available to prisoners it determines have a treatable addiction or abuse condition remains. Residential treatment and aftercare are required for eligible willing prisoners with a substance-abuse problem, subject to available appropriations and release-date priority. After the Director finds successful residential treatment, the prisoner remains in custody under appropriate conditions; if those conditions differ because of completion, periodic testing is required and the altered conditions must end when abuse recurs. None of those commands makes early release mandatory.

Lopez's firearm enhancement presents the challenged category. The record establishes treatment admission eligibility, not successful completion or entitlement to a reduction. Ordinary objections to a misapplied category, disregard of binding rules or supported arbitrary administration remain distinct. The Court does not validate every possible exclusion or automatic denial to all eligible prisoners. The amici rulemaking-procedure objection was not raised or decided below or presented in the petition and is not reached; no constitutional liberty-interest question is decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Heckler v. Campbell: applies the permission to resolve recurring issues by rules within the actual delegation; it does not make every categorical restriction lawful.
- INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang: preserves eligibility/discretion separation and reasoned, consistent administration; distinguishes its prohibition of automatically defeating every eligible waiver application from this reasoned subset under a different statute.
- Reno v. Koray: preserves contextual statutory construction and its detention-credit holding; it supplies neither the present release rule nor an independently sufficient administrative-deference ground.

### City News & Novelty, Inc. v. City of Waukesha — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/City_News_and_Novelty_v_City_of_Waukesha_mootness-dismissal_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/City_News_and_Novelty_v_City_of_Waukesha_mootness-dismissal_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/City_News_and_Novelty_v_City_of_Waukesha_mootness-dismissal_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. Voluntary abandonment leaves no continuing controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A former licensee who withdraws renewal, abandons the regulated business and establishes no concrete plan to return or surviving claim has no continuing personal stake in prospective review of the licensing procedure. Refusal to promise permanent nonreturn does not restore that stake; this writ must therefore be dismissed as moot without deciding the granted First Amendment question.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt both the mootness and equitable-vacatur holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans requires a personal controversy throughout review after the claimant voluntarily leaves the regulated setting. City News withdrew its renewal request, closed the adult business and asserts no present plan to obtain another license. No surviving damages claim is established within the grant. The disabilities from the 1995 violations expired in 2000. The proposed through-2005 disability depends on an additional violation finding the council never made; the council instead permitted operation during state review.

Laidlaw's demanding defendant-cessation rule protects a plaintiff's continuing stake against a defendant's strategic cessation. It does not require Waukesha to prove that this applicant will never change its mind after voluntarily abandoning the activity. Neither a short-lived action reasonably likely to recur for this applicant nor a presently imposed surviving disability is shown. A concrete licensing plan or surviving claim would present different facts, but speculation is not such a showing. The importance of the prompt-review issue and the First Amendment supply no exception to Article III. The Court accordingly decides neither prompt access versus prompt determination nor the distinct question of interim operation during review.

#### II. The unfavorable state judgment remains undisturbed

**Holding and operative rule:** Dismissal for mootness does not automatically require vacatur of the judgment under review; the party seeking that equitable relief must justify it in the actual circumstances. City News voluntarily abandoned the activity and application that made review useful and establishes no further sufficient equity, so the Wisconsin judgment is left intact without affirmance of its federal merits reasoning.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion directly adopts the equitable disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** U.S. Bancorp preserves authority to dispose of an unreviewable judgment equitably while rejecting automatic judgment clearing after voluntary loss of review. City News is the reviewing loser that chose to abandon the renewal and business. No further sufficient equitable circumstance is established. Arizonans involved a prevailing claimant's departure followed by lower federal merits adjudication and justified corrective vacatur on those distinct equities. No comparable lower federal jurisdictional error is present here. Article III governs this Court's ability to decide the federal controversy; it does not retroactively govern the Wisconsin court's jurisdiction. Leaving that judgment in place decides neither its correctness nor the licensing rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: applies its continuing-personal-stake rule; distinguishes the equities supporting corrective vacatur of lower federal judgments in that case.
- Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw: preserves its demanding defendant-cessation burden while distinguishing this claimant's abandonment of its own regulated activity.
- U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership: applies equitable rather than automatic vacatur after voluntary loss of review; no exhaustive list of exceptional equities or new proof quantum is adopted.
- Freedman v. Maryland and FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas: their prompt-review requirements are not construed because this applicant's case is moot; the Wisconsin court's interpretation is not affirmed.

### Seling v. Young — 2001-01-17

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. Civil designation does not foreclose proof of actual punishment

**Holding and operative rule:** A court first identifies whether the legislature intended a civil or criminal commitment scheme; civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that the challenged commitment's purpose or effect is punitive. Actual design and operation must be assessed cumulatively, including contrary civil evidence: a civil label is not conclusive, but confinement, connection to prior crime, indeterminate duration, criminal-style safeguards, deficient treatment or an individual treatment failure alone establishes neither punishment nor a right to release.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt both holdings, the specified limits and the scoped affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Hendricks permits proof of punitive operation in a nominally civil commitment and invalidated the demonstrated retrospective application there. Hudson preserves design-and-operation review and supplies no universal rule confining the inquiry to statutory wording. The Court must therefore distinguish an inadequately administered but genuinely civil program from custody that actually imposes punishment.

The superintendent's concern that day-to-day conditions should not transform a scheme's classification supports a demanding burden and careful remedial distinctions. It cannot make proved punitive operation legally irrelevant. Clinical testimony, treatment records and plans, staff and patient accounts, confinement practices, program design and other reliable direct or circumstantial evidence can bear on the cumulative question. No statistical study or successful cure is required, and officials cannot defeat strong combined proof merely by isolating every circumstance. Conversely, employee misconduct or poor care alone does not establish that the custody is punishment.

Young's allegations concerning security, confinement, abuse, staffing, therapy and practical release remain allegations. The separate Turay injunction and reported progress are evidence within their actual scope, not conclusive proof of punishment, compliance or cure of an earlier unlawful application. Statutory examination, review and release mechanisms are relevant civil evidence, but their existence does not prove their operation for Young. The Court neither finds punishment nor rewrites those mechanisms. The supported claim requires adjudication under the governing burden, rather than categorical foreclosure by the civil label.

#### II. The existing punishment remand stands, with distinct constitutional and remedial predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's reviewed punishment-remand portion is affirmed, preserving the opportunity to establish Young's supported claim under the former chapter 153 habeas framework applicable to his 1994 application. If the clearest proof establishes punishment and the challenged custody retroactively adds it for conduct predating enactment, custody under that unlawful application must end through appropriate habeas relief, while independent lawful custody is preserved; a conditions violation that leaves custody lawfully civil instead requires relief through its proper conditions channel.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion adopts the remand, temporal rule and remedy distinction. The separate due-process, equal-protection and commitment-procedure rulings Young did not seek to review remain undisturbed.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh's application-date rule leaves this pre-April 24, 1996 application under former chapter 153 law; amended section 2254(d) does not govern its merits. Separately directed provisions and ordinary habeas requirements retain their operation. The Ninth Circuit already allowed an opportunity to prove punitive operation by the clearest proof. That disposition is proper under Hendricks and Hudson, so correcting the account of governing precedent requires instructions rather than vacatur of the opportunity itself. The remand creates no universal entitlement to an evidentiary hearing and no present writ or release order.

Punishment and retrospectivity require separate findings. The existence of a conditions remedy cannot defeat a proved unlawful-custody claim; conversely, conditions defects do not automatically require release. The court must consider the supported record and material genuine factual disputes under the former habeas rules. No replacement confinement authority is supplied.

Double Jeopardy remains distinct. A punitive character alone proves neither the relevant prior jeopardy nor that the challenged confinement is a second punishment for the same offense. Those matters require separate determination under the governing attachment and offense-identity law if the preserved claim remains necessary after the punishment and retrospective-custody findings. Hendricks did not decide them. The Court establishes no independent double-jeopardy entitlement here and need not add a second ground if Ex Post Facto relief fully resolves the custody claim. The remaining punishment, chronology, same-offense and custody determinations are adjudicative work under the stated rules, not permission to treat a civil label or shared past misconduct as conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kansas v. Hendricks: applies its rebuttable civil-intent and clearest-proof framework, cumulative operational inquiry and retrospective-punishment consequence; its particular Kansas facts are not transferred to Young.
- Hudson v. United States: applies its preserved design-and-operation inquiry; it did not replace actual-operation review with a statutory-face-only rule or overrule Halper in its personal-monetary-penalty field.
- United States v. Ursery: preserves the demanding rebuttal of civil classification without transferring its forfeiture outcome to personal confinement.
- United States v. Ward and Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez: preserve the intent-and-effect inquiry and cumulative use of punitive indicators, without a conclusive single factor or new exhaustive checklist.
- Lindh v. Murphy: applies the pre-enactment application rule, preserving separately directed provisions rather than exempting every habeas issue from every amendment.

### Kansas v. United States — 2001-01-22

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Kansas_v_United_States_merits_2001-01-22.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Kansas_v_United_States_merits_2001-01-22.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Kansas_v_United_States_merits_2001-01-22.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The pleaded funding arrangement preserves a genuine program choice

**Holding and operative rule:** The pleaded child-support and Temporary Assistance for Needy Families funding arrangement is not unconstitutional coercion merely because declining substantial related assistance is politically and financially difficult. Coercion remains possible when the structure and practical operation of threatened funding loss leave no genuine choice; clear notice, program relationship and the absence of an entitlement to unchanged grants are relevant but do not independently settle that question, and no fixed dollar, percentage or insolvency threshold governs.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt this actual-choice rule, its application to the pleaded arrangement and the limited affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Dole, New York's monetary-incentive holding and Steward Machine distinguish substantial inducement from compulsion. Child-support collection directly serves the funded family-assistance system and the resources available for supported families. The challenged arrangement does not impose administration duties after Kansas declines the supported program.

The Court accepts well-pleaded facts on review of dismissal, but the legal conclusion that refusal is impossible does not become an additional fact. The historical fiscal-year 1996 figures—about $29.3 million in child-support funds and $101.9 million in welfare funds, with federal support covering 66 percent of operations and 80 percent of computer expenditures—show a substantial incentive. They establish neither present forfeiture, a percentage of the State's total resources nor a concrete inability to exit. The alleged burdens on Kansas and beneficiaries are real considerations, but this arrangement does not plead practical conscription.

The inquiry considers the threatened funds, their connection to the conditions, notice and transition, and consequences of refusal where relevant, without a mandatory checklist. Competent qualitative evidence, program operation, administrative experience and feasible alternatives can establish compulsion; statistics are not the exclusive proof. A material allegation of actual compulsion could not be dismissed as irrelevant simply because participation is labeled voluntary. None is established beyond the dependence and difficulty alleged here, and discovery need not be ordered to search for an unpleaded theory.

Printz's actual source-and-function rules concern compelled executive work; they neither convert this funding choice into an unconditional command nor create a universal compensation right. Kansas cannot compel continuation of superseded grant terms, but that lack of entitlement alone would not defeat a genuine coercion claim. The statute's actual qualifications below prevent treating every implementation defect as automatic loss of both funding streams.

**Statutory limits of this application:** Section 602(a)(2) requires the chief executive's certification that the State will operate a child-support program under an approved part-D plan during the fiscal year. Complete refusal to operate that program differs from deficiencies in a continuing program.

For fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 1997, section 609(a)(8) requires a finding of failure to achieve the paternity-establishment percentages or other performance measures, incomplete or unreliable data, or lack of substantial compliance with part D. Performance findings rest on section 654(15)(B) submitted data or a section 652(a)(4) review; data-reliability findings rest on section 652(a)(4)(C)(i) audits, and substantial-compliance findings on section 652(a)(4)(C) audits. The substantial-compliance branch excludes section 654(24), (27)(A) and (27)(B)(i). In the succeeding corrective fiscal year, the State must have failed to take sufficient corrective action to achieve required performance or compliance, or again submitted incomplete or unreliable data. The reduction is 1–2 percent, 2–3 percent for the second consecutive finding and 3–5 percent for the third or later consecutive finding. It reduces otherwise payable TANF amounts for quarters after that corrective year and before quarters following the first full quarter in which the required performance or substantial compliance is achieved. Technical noncompliance counts as substantial compliance only if the Secretary determines that it does not adversely affect program performance; technically incomplete or unreliable data count as adequate only on the Secretary's parallel determination that the defect does not adversely affect measurement of paternity establishment or other performance. No triggering finding or actual sanction is adjudicated here.

Under section 666(d), the Secretary may exempt a law or procedure required by or pursuant to that section if the State demonstrates, using caseload, processing-time, administrative-cost, average-collection and other requested data or estimates, that it will not improve effectiveness and efficiency. The exemption is discretionary, remains subject to continuing review and ends if circumstances change. It supplies neither a guaranteed exemption nor an exemption from every part-D obligation; the result does not depend on predicting an exemption for Kansas.

Section 654(26) continues to require safeguards against unauthorized use or disclosure; prohibits whereabouts disclosure to another party subject to a protective order and to a person where physical or emotional harm is reasonably feared; requires notice to the Secretary when those prohibitions apply and reasonable evidence of domestic violence or child abuse indicates harmful disclosure; and requires court screening before further disclosure of flagged information received from the Secretary. If the court finds further disclosure could harm the parent or child, the court and its agents must not disclose it.

**Scope and remedy:** The judgment dismissing the reviewed coercion challenge is affirmed. The granted question supplies no separate ruling on privacy, procedural due process, every collection or disclosure mechanism, or a particular sanctions application. The safeguards remain statutory commands, not findings validating every implementation. No funds, old-terms payment, penalty, severed replacement program or discovery order is awarded.

**Precedent treatment:**

- South Dakota v. Dole: applies the distinction between inducement and coercion with general-welfare, notice, relatedness and independent constitutional limits preserved; its five-percent setting becomes no numerical safe harbor.
- New York v. United States: applies its monetary-incentive holding and distinguishes its unavoidable take-title command; no constitutional entitlement to retain grants on superseded terms follows.
- Steward Machine Co. v. Davis: applies its distinction between financial pressure and compulsion without treating every expensive choice as voluntary by definition.
- Printz v. United States: distinguishes its actual compulsory-assistance and fiscal-remand questions from conditional funded cooperation; its source-and-function limits and absence of a controlling compensation entitlement remain intact.

### Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Brentwood_Academy_v_Tennessee_Secondary_School_Athletic_Association_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Brentwood_Academy_v_Tennessee_Secondary_School_Athletic_Association_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Brentwood_Academy_v_Tennessee_Secondary_School_Athletic_Association_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The association's actual public operation makes this regulation governmental

**Holding and operative rule:** An athletic association's challenged regulatory activity is state action when public-school officials, acting in their official educational capacity, substantially govern and operate it as the common instrument for administering their schools' athletic programs. The demonstrated official operation here establishes governmental responsibility despite private incorporation, voluntary membership and private-school participation; membership percentages, funding or public benefit alone would not suffice.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six Justices directly adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas oppose the attribution ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron prevents government from avoiding constitutional responsibility through a corporate form. Its special-creation and permanent-majority-appointment combination is sufficient, not an exclusive checklist. American Manufacturers requires actual governmental responsibility and protects an independent private carrier's choice; it does not make an institution private when public officials actually operate the challenged function. Tarkanian's national association differs from the collective action of schools within one State. Its discussion of that distinction supports the comparison without itself deciding this case.

About 84 percent of the members are public schools. At the time of the penalties every voting governor was a public-school administrator, selected from principals, assistant principals and superintendents. Their governance of eligibility and competition, official school roles and meetings ordinarily during school hours establish collective public administration. Their official capacity in this work is the Court's legal inference from the established structure and function, not an undisputed lower-court conclusion. No factual remand is needed merely to recount that structure.

Private membership and voluntary selection do not turn this common administration of school athletics into an independent private choice. Public control need not be permanently guaranteed by the voting rules for this actual operation to be governmental. No separate state command to impose the recruiting penalty is required. Gate receipts supply the principal revenue and dues about four percent; staff are not state-paid, retirement eligibility includes no state-paid employer contribution, and State Board representatives do not vote. Those limited contacts are not substitutes for the governance ground. The 1996 recognition and voluntary-membership language replaced the earlier express designation. The district court's finding of unchanged material operation does not erase that amendment; the actual operation nevertheless supplies the necessary connection.

#### II. Attribution returns the preserved rights and relief questions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's no-state-action judgment is reversed and the case remanded with the association's challenged athletic regulation treated as governmental. The lower courts must adjudicate preserved constitutional merits and lawful relief; attribution alone neither invalidates the recruiting rule or penalties nor reinstates the district injunction or establishes damages.

**Authority:** The six-Justice coalition identified above directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron separates actor status from violation and remedy. The Sixth Circuit reversed the district court's summary judgment and injunction on attribution rather than resolving all the constitutional grounds. Correcting that premise returns the unreviewed questions under their own rules. The challenged action consists of four years of athletic-program probation, two years of exclusion from football and boys' basketball playoffs, and a $3,000 fine, imposed after recruiting communications about spring football practice. The Court does not decide whether that conduct was protected speech, whether the rule or procedure violated a constitutional right, or what relief any proved violation warrants. Those questions are distinct from the presented attribution issue and remain for ordinary lower-court adjudication; no further evidentiary hearing, damages award or particular injunction is directed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.: extends its governmental-responsibility principle to established collective official operation without requiring its precise special-charter and permanent-appointment combination; preserves that combination as sufficient and preserves the separation of actor status, constitutional merits and relief.
- American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan: preserves the requirement of actual governmental responsibility; distinguishes an institution operated by officials in their public capacity from a genuinely private choice merely permitted or reviewed by the State.
- National Collegiate Athletic Association v. Tarkanian, 488 U.S. 179: distinguishes the national association's relationship to one state university from this common administration by institutions of one State; the earlier decision remains effective and its single-State observation is not treated as an automatic attribution holding.

### Director of Revenue of Missouri v. CoBank ACB — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Director_of_Revenue_of_Missouri_v_CoBank_ACB_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Director_of_Revenue_of_Missouri_v_CoBank_ACB_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Director_of_Revenue_of_Missouri_v_CoBank_ACB_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### The amended cooperative-bank provision preserves state income taxation

**Holding and operative rule:** Banks for cooperatives remain subject to otherwise applicable state income taxation under the Farm Credit Act's entity-specific arrangement after the 1985 removal of the federal-stock-linked exemption. The amendment deleted both the broad institutional exemption and its ownership condition; it did not create a permanent immunity for the bank's operating income, and CoBank cannot obtain the disputed 1991–1994 refunds on that federal-exemption theory.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the complete statutory ground and limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** First Agricultural National Bank requires respect for Congress's particular bank-tax allocation. Section 2134 designates banks for cooperatives and their obligations as federal instrumentalities, but its retained exemption protects issued notes, debentures and other obligations, principal and interest. It excepts surtaxes, estate, inheritance and gift taxes, and expressly subjects interest to federal income tax in the holder's hands. It does not exempt the institution's operating income.

The 1971 law separately exempted the bank, its property, franchises, capital, reserves, surplus, other funds and income only during federal stock ownership. That exemption retained taxation of real and tangible personal property on the same basis as similar property and federal income taxation of obligation interest in the holder's hands. Federal investment was repaid by 1968. In 1985 Congress removed both sentences containing the institutional exemption and ownership condition while replacing the relevant federal stock-purchase mechanism. Reading the whole enacted change, Congress removed a conditional exemption that no longer operated; it did not enlarge that exemption by deleting it. The 1988 holder-interest amendment confirms the retained obligation-specific structure.

Sections 2023 and 2098 expressly exempt the distinct Farm Credit Banks and federal land bank associations, their capital, reserves, surplus and derived income, retaining comparable-value taxation of real estate and federal holder-income taxation for their protected mortgages and obligations. Those differentiated provisions cannot be transferred to this bank category.

CoBank's strongest argument treats present silence about consent as restoring default instrumentality immunity. The amended allocation answers that contention without a universal rule about constitutional immunity, statutory silence or every deletion. Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services distinguished federal identity, substantive tax privileges and the federal forum bar; it left tax merits open. This case properly reaches those merits through the state review system. Independent state tax and refund requirements remain for remand; the Court calculates no liability or refund.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas: preserves its federal-forum holding and express reservation of substantive tax privileges; distinguishes this state-court review of the merits. Instrumentality status, substantive exemption and procedural privilege remain separate.
- First Agricultural National Bank of Berkshire County v. State Tax Commission, 392 U.S. 339: applies its respect for the particular congressional tax allocation; does not extend one category's immunity to every federal bank or decide the broader constitutional question it left open.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I. The particular preservation need and limited execution justify this restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers may temporarily restrict unaccompanied entry into a home while obtaining a warrant when particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual risk of evidence destruction, a restraint reasonably confined to preservation and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization together justify that intrusion. The approximately two-hour restriction here satisfies those conditions in combination; probable cause, refusal of consent, awareness of an investigation, disposable drugs, jailability or duration alone creates no automatic exception or safe harbor.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and the complete remand. Stone-Zsela dissents; Stevens would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted and, because the Court reaches the merits, would affirm suppression. Souter's additional concurrence does not limit his full join.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton preserves the home's special warrant protection. Whren's objective initial-stop rule does not authorize a greater residential intrusion, and Bond requires an independent basis for an added intrusion despite lawful presence. Edmond addresses suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints; its program-purpose threshold supplies no rule for this individualized preservation measure.

Probable cause was uncontested. McArthur's wife reported seeing him slide drugs under the couch; he knew officers were investigating and had ready access to the identified disposable evidence. These particular circumstances reasonably supported a destruction concern without requiring police to wait for an observed attempt. Refusal to consent supplies neither guilt nor an independent exigency. One officer and the wife pursued a warrant while another restrained unaccompanied reentry. McArthur made two or three entries for cigarettes and telephone use with observation just inside the doorway. The officers conducted no evidentiary search before the warrant. Their approximately two-hour effort was diligent and the actual restriction reasonably preserved the evidence pending a magistrate's decision.

Place gives duration and diligence independent importance; no fixed two-hour allowance follows. Welsh requires attention to offense gravity. These offenses were modest but jailable: up to thirty days for the marijuana count and one year for paraphernalia. That distinguishes Welsh's civil, nonjailable nighttime home-arrest setting without making jailability sufficient. The substantial home interest and modest offenses weigh against restraint, but the actual preservation need and limited warrant-directed execution make this measure reasonable.

Segura's independent-source result does not validate every earlier seizure, and its broader Part IV discussion had only two votes. The Court relies on the justification before restraint, not the later discoveries. Limited observational consent is not general search consent or abandonment of the home-access objection. No general power to occupy or search homes, police-created unlawful exigency, requirement to exhaust every imaginable alternative or universal warrant-exception checklist is decided.

#### II. The presented suppression ground cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Illinois appellate judgment is reversed insofar as suppression rests on this temporary restriction of residential access, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings. Any actually preserved independent warrant, admissibility or exclusionary-rule issue remains governed by its own law; none is found established or newly created here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition directly joins this holding and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court treated the temporary restraint as an unlawful seizure and suppressed the later warrant evidence on that ground. Because the actual restraint was justified, that ground fails. The Court does not give the later warrant retroactive force, independently resolve every feature of that warrant or determine guilt. Correction of this suppression ground requires further proceedings, not an automatic conviction, dismissal of charges, release, new trial or new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: preserves the home's warrant protection; no general residential-entry authority is created.
- Whren v. United States: preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis and independent limits on greater intrusion, scope and duration; traffic probable cause is not residential authority.
- Bond v. United States: applies the requirement of independent justification for an added intrusion; lawful presence and limited observation do not imply consent to search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond: distinguishes suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints from this individualized preservation measure; its program-purpose holding remains intact.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: applies attention to actual duration and diligence in temporary preservation; no fixed permissible interval is transferred from luggage to a home.
- Welsh v. Wisconsin, 466 U.S. 740: preserves the importance of offense gravity and home protection; distinguishes its civil, nonjailable nighttime arrest without making every jailable offense sufficient for restraint.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796: preserves its independent-source holding while declining to treat it as approval of every initial seizure; the broader securing-premises discussion in Part IV remains a two-Justice position, not a majority holding.

### Urofsky v. Gilmore — 2001-02-20

Authority: [controlling holdings](../records/Urofsky_v_Gilmore_merits_2001-02-20.md#holdings); [precedent treatment](../records/Urofsky_v_Gilmore_merits_2001-02-20.md#precedent-treatment); [operative procedure](../records/Urofsky_v_Gilmore_merits_2001-02-20.md#procedure-after-action). The following holding and precedent blocks are copied from the Record; the Record controls.

#### I-A. The mandatory condition can be challenged without a refused request

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff whose concrete intended or continuing activity is subject to an enforceable approval condition may challenge that condition without first seeking and being denied permission. The supported teaching and research activities here permit review of the condition itself; relief must still correspond to the plaintiffs' actual continuing injury under the amended coverage, and no hypothetical future refusal is adjudicated.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this threshold ruling and the general-entitlement holding that follows.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged burden is the necessity of advance written public permission, not only the possibility of a refused request. The record identifies removed Internet assignments, proposed research concerning Victorian poetry and other sexual subjects, scholarly databases, and Smith's website blocking and partial restoration. Published accounts identify summary-judgment submissions for material portions of those claims; this is not merely an unspecified fear of workplace regulation. None of these plaintiffs requested approval, and the record reports no denied request. Those facts limit any refusal claim but do not defeat this condition challenge.

Smith's blocking is not a denied statutory application. Nor does a document labeled a statement of undisputed facts establish that the Commonwealth conceded every assertion. The amended dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements narrow the statute; they do not establish that all the intended inquiry lies outside it. Article III power rests on the supported concrete condition burden, not an assumption of jurisdiction to decide disputed merits. The continuing covered activities and beneficiaries of final prospective relief must be determined at their proper scope.

#### I-B. Protected subject matter alone creates no unrestricted employee resource right

**Holding and operative rule:** The lawful or constitutionally protected character of material, its usefulness to an employee's job, and delegated professional discretion do not alone give every state employee a personal right to choose its use through government equipment free of approval. The reversal of the district court's wholesale declaration of invalidity remains in force, without sustaining every possible application to nonacademic employees or resolving the distinct individual scholarly claim.

**Authority:** Part I has all nine direct joins, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring requires identification of the employee's personal interest before balancing and permits lawful institutional control of an assigned instructional production. An employer ordinarily defines its prescribed work, allocates equipment and prevents misuse. The message and task matter; the computer's ownership is not a complete answer to an independently protected personal claim. The professors expressly disclaim personal recreational use, which is outside review.

The district court addressed the earlier, broader content definition and work-related activity across state employment. That reasoning cannot establish the amended law's substantial unconstitutional reach or support the same wholesale decree on these six faculty claims. NTEU preserves the distinction between a supported personal right and relief for unrepresented employees. This ruling does not adopt a rule that one lawful application defeats every First Amendment overbreadth challenge. Genuine institutional decisions remain subject to independent constitutional and other applicable limits, and the individual university question requires its own answer.

#### II. Independent scholarly expression requires its own protection and burden inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Independent university inquiry, teaching or communication on matters of public concern may carry a personal First Amendment interest according to its function and context; salary, connection to duties and use of public equipment do not alone make it the institution's prescribed message. Once that interest is established, Pickering and NTEU require the State to justify the actual prospective approval burden, including its effect on scholars and audiences, with a commensurate connection to legitimate operational needs; this rule grants no personal control over curricula, assignments, budgets, professional standards or equipment allocation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: six direct joins. The five Associates also join Part III's limited remand. Stone-Zsela joins this rule but concludes that the present record already establishes a violation requiring relief; his conclusion is not part of the Court's application or mandate. O'Connor assumes an interest without joining this affirmative rule; Scalia and Thomas reject the claimed entitlement.

**Controlling explanation:** Boring decided an assigned secondary-school production and expressly left university academic freedom and a universal duty-speech rule open. It cannot make every scholarly undertaking institutional expression. Keyishian protected individual educators and a library employee against the challenged speech and membership restrictions; its concern with official orthodoxy is not exclusively an institution's right. Sweezy's plurality and separate concurrence support attention to scholarly freedom on different grounds, not a single comprehensive academic-freedom test. Neither decision grants ownership of public research resources.

Pickering and Connick require contextual protection and employment justification. NTEU gives the actual prospective burden on speakers and audiences weight; it does not automatically classify every work activity as personal expression. Givhan confirms that location and private communication do not alone defeat protection. Waters preserves genuine lawful employer grounds but supplies no universal investigation procedure or answer to this statutory condition.

The inquiry must account for the amended content definition, required-use limitation, both approval branches, written approval, public availability and actual administration. Efficiency, resource management and preventing harassment are legitimate interests. The State may use operational evidence and supported predictive judgments; completed harassment, expert studies, automatic strict scrutiny or proof of actual disruption in every case is not required. Conversely, ownership and the availability of approval do not by themselves answer the burden of obtaining public advance permission for protected exploratory inquiry. Delegation to deans or department heads is relevant practice, not an enacted guarantee of universal peer review. Scholarly labels, underinclusiveness and a sexual subject alone establish neither protection nor invalidity. The standard preserves genuine academic governance and independently applicable constitutional prohibitions.

#### III. Apply the amended condition to the represented scholarly claims before directing relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is vacated insofar as it finally rejects the individual academic claim, and the case is remanded to identify continuing covered personal scholarly activity and apply the stated protection, burden and justification rules. Any declaration or injunction must redress an established violation and extend no further than the represented rights and effective relief require; the Court directs no present injunction, access approval, damages, special hearing or new discovery.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, with Ginsburg as author: five direct joins to the whole application and remedy. Stone-Zsela would reverse and direct confined relief now; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the academic disposition. The five-Justice holding requires no combination of opposing rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court's categorical employee-role and exclusively institutional academic-freedom rules bypassed the necessary inquiry. The district court's broader statutory definition and decree do not supply its answer. The existing record contains specific scholarly burdens and Commonwealth evidence. Five reported misuse incidents and complaints support preventive concerns without establishing five harassment judgments or criminal guilt. Two incidents preceded the Act and were reported as addressed under existing policies; that does not prove those policies prevent every future harm. Smith's asserted scholarly purpose and the complaints remain distinct from a statutory refusal.

Prompt approvals with minimal paperwork and blanket permissions support the contention that the condition is modest. They are examples, not statutory deadlines, assurances that every request will succeed or elimination of the written public-approval burden. A research connection does not replace the statutory requirement that access be required for an approved project or other undertaking. The lower courts must assess the actual protected scope and fit under the amended law, using the existing submissions and ordinary procedural rules for any properly authorized further proceedings.

This remand is not based on an evidentiary vacuum or unrecovered original exhibits. It presumes neither that plaintiffs must win nor that every approval system fails. No improper motive, unlawful refusal or unrestricted access entitlement is found. The remaining work applies the stated rule to activity, coverage, burden and remedy; it does not postpone the governing legal standard. No isolated invalidation of the public-records requirement or decision about an unpresented project follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education: applies its antecedent-personal-interest requirement and lawful institutional-output rule; resolves part of the university question it reserved without converting it into a universal duty-speech rule or overruling its assigned-production holding.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applies its requirement of justification commensurate with an actual prospective burden on protected expression and audiences after identifying the personal interest; preserves the independent limits on relief for unrepresented persons and imports no blanket class remedy.
- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563: applies the employment balance to identified protected scholarly activity and the actual condition.
- Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138: preserves contextual public-concern and operational analysis; no occupational label resolves it.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District, 439 U.S. 410: preserves protection despite private communication or workplace location, subject to the actual employment inquiry.
- Waters v. Churchill: preserves its limited majority rules concerning genuine grounds and mistaken accounts; extends neither its plurality's full investigative framework nor any automatic employer immunity to this condition.
- Keyishian v. Board of Regents, 385 U.S. 589: applies its protection of individuals against the challenged orthodoxy, speech-vagueness and membership restrictions as support for personal scholarly freedom; distinguishes its specific defects from a complete entitlement to public resources.
- Sweezy v. New Hampshire, 354 U.S. 234: preserves the plurality's authorization/due-process ground and Frankfurter and Harlan's distinct justification ground; draws support for scholarly freedom without combining them into a new majority test.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves its particular damages-immunity holding and reservation of the underlying constitutional extension; it supplies no adverse merits rule for this prospective academic claim. No precedent is overruled.

## Standing practice

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.

## Published noncontrolling positions

### Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College — 2000-11-27

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The dissent would recognize an employee's direct claim when attributed expression protected by the public-employment rules substantially motivates an adverse employment action, subject to the employer's proof that it would have acted for an independent lawful reason. The First Amendment constrains the punitive use of expression against the person officials target; mistaken identity does not remove that person's injury or justify suppressing protected criticism. Actual authorship would therefore be unnecessary. Pickering–Connick would determine protection and legitimate employment interests, and Mt. Healthy would govern causation and the same-decision defense. Erroneous attribution or an imperfect investigation alone would prove neither liability nor the other elements. Waters's limited protection for genuine reliance on a reasonable account of disciplinable speech would remain; it would not protect hostility to protected criticism. Letters, notices, chronology, testimony, and competent circumstantial evidence could establish the actual ground under ordinary Crawford-El procedure, without a statistical requirement.

The dissent would also replace Harlow's defense insofar as lack of sufficiently similar precedent defeats damages after an underlying violation is established. An official would instead have to prove actual and objectively reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly permitting the challenged conduct. Legal silence, custom, or general supervisory power would not suffice. In the dissent's view, §1983's remedy and the protections for reasonable factual mistakes within the substantive inquiry make that narrower reliance defense adequate. Elder's independent consideration of relevant law and ordinary procedural protections would survive, as would independent absolute immunity. This would change existing immunity doctrine, including positions Stone-Zsela previously accepted. He would reverse and remand to determine protected status, adverse action, motive, causation, any supported reliance defense, and compensation for proved injury. No damages judgment would follow now, and prospective relief would require an actual continuing threat rather than a presumed revival of the expired notice.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/Wasson_v_Sonoma_County_Junior_College_merits_2000-11-27.md#separate-writings).

### City of Indianapolis v. Edmond — 2000-11-28

**Thomas, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia.** The dissent would reverse because this program genuinely performs systematic license and sobriety checks that provide an independent roadway justification. Under the dissent's reading of Whren and the checkpoint precedents, the added narcotics objective and exterior dog use do not invalidate a stop whose actual operation stays within that legitimate scope. The dissent answers the concern about nominal checks by requiring real, independently supportable roadway functions and lawful execution; it does not approve a drug-only checkpoint merely because it efficiently detects crime. It would return the case for proceedings under that objective mixed-function rule rather than approve every execution detail.

**Thomas, dissenting, Part II, joined by no other Justice.** Thomas would apply the restrained scope and objective reasonableness analysis of the checkpoint cases to these brief systematic automobile seizures. He rejects a primary-purpose threshold transferred from suspicionless searches of bodies, homes, or businesses. He also questions whether indiscriminate stops accord with the Fourth Amendment's original understanding, but does not seek to overrule Sitz or Martinez-Fuerte here because that issue was not developed for decision. Scalia joins neither this additional critique nor that reservation.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/City_of_Indianapolis_v_Edmond_merits_2000-11-28.md#separate-writings).

### Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices — 2000-12-11

**Kennedy, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The dissent accepts Russell's and Shrink Missouri's contribution standard and recognizes that the influence evidence justifies some prophylaxis. It finds the State's operational answer inadequate for this amount because projected funding losses are substantial and concentrated in affected competitive races. Average expenses and a special election with different donor competition do not sufficiently establish replacement capacity. Recruiting more donors is a relevant possibility, not proof that campaigns can replace large shares of their funding. The protected association is support for the chosen candidate; public enrollment, self-financing, or expression through another speaker does not answer loss of that route. The dissent relies on the scale and concentration of the burden, not every static loss, an inflation floor, completed-bribe requirement, or unlimited-giving rule.

**Kennedy, dissenting, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas.** The dissent applies the expressive-burden rationale these Justices joined in Day to both candidate-financing and independent-expenditure triggers. The State attaches an adverse public-funding consequence to outsiders' protected activity, supplying a reason to stop below the trigger. Caps shorten the burden and the favorable-expenditure deduction improves fit, but neither removes that legal consequence. Voluntary initial grants are legitimate; making their program attractive does not justify burdening nonparticipants' expression or merely offsetting an opponent's advantage. The State has not shown why its anti-dependence interest requires grants measured by another speaker's activity. This objection does not erase the contribution/expenditure distinction or invalidate every public grant or adjustment.

The dissenters seek component-specific declaratory and prospective relief, implemented on remand under 1 MRSA §71(8): preserve provisions and applications that can operate independently, subject to the construction rule's plain-meaning qualification. They would neither rewrite a trigger or ceiling nor invalidate unrelated reporting, reclaim initial grants, reopen elections, or impose retrospective liability. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas need not reach the additional whole-system theory after their proposed targeted relief. O'Connor joins the Court's rejection of that additional ground while continuing to seek invalidation of matching.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/Daggett_v_Commission_on_Governmental_Ethics_merits_2000-12-11.md#separate-writings).

### Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph — 2000-12-11

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone-Zsela joins the entire Court opinion and would require Randolph to identify a concrete likelihood of required costs preventing effective statutory vindication. Her sworn resources, applicable schedules, contracts, forum correspondence, and other competent evidence may substantiate that objection; neither statistical research nor prior payment is necessary. Through ordinary focused procedure, the party seeking compulsion must disclose materially relevant cost and allocation information within its control. Contractual silence cannot become an impossible demand for concealed facts. He would compel arbitration if the obstacle is unproved or lawfully removed, otherwise withhold enforcement to the necessary extent, while preserving unresolved independent grounds. His initial likelihood and disclosure formulation is not attributed to the other remand Justices or treated as a complete majority rule.

**Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Stevens and Souter.** These Justices distinguish whether an arbitral forum is capable of deciding a category of federal claims from whether this claimant can afford to use it. A burden imposed on a party claiming that Congress excluded a statutory category from arbitration does not itself establish the same burden for financial access. They would resolve the concrete forum, allocation, lender practice, and affordability questions through the focused opportunity, with substantiation required, without announcing a comprehensive substitute burden rule. Contract silence proves neither prohibitive cost nor accessibility, and a nonbinding assurance is no enforceable allocation. Breyer joins the Court's inquiry and access ground but not this broader burden critique.

**O'Connor, dissenting from the cost disposition, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.** These Justices join finality and reject both silence-based invalidity and indifference to a genuine statutory-access barrier. They would require the resisting party to demonstrate a likelihood of prohibitive expense and reverse the cost ruling because no likely forum, charges, or amount chargeable to Randolph was established. Exact advance billing is unnecessary, but a conjectural risk should not mandate cost-development litigation. They would preserve ordinary properly supported procedural grounds and the unresolved class-action alternative on remand, without ordering unconditional arbitration or deciding statutory merits. Their disagreement is with the required new cost opportunity on this showing, not the possibility that a demonstrated cost barrier can defeat enforcement.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/Green_Tree_Financial_v_Randolph_merits_2000-12-11.md#separate-writings).

### Bush v. Gore — 2000-12-12

**O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join reviewability, the supported equality defect, rejection of separate Article II and due-process reversal, the section 5 distinction, and corrective vacatur. Their disagreement concerns state law: the December 11 majority's notes 17 and 22, read with its comprehensive-code account and the relation between certification and contest, establish December 12 finality. Palm Beach's recognition that §5 is not itself a federal prohibition does not erase a State's choice to meet that date. Correction must therefore stay within that state limit. They do not find that correction has already become impossible, infer current progress, authorize an extension, or award electoral votes. The supervising court must apply the retained deadline to the actual feasible process rather than receive an unsupported terminal factual conclusion from this Court.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting from the judgment.** They agree on reviewability, unequal treatment, absence of a separate established due-process violation, and §5's limited federal operation. Article II supplies an independent reason to reverse the challenged directions. First, de novo treatment of discretionary board decisions combined with automatic inclusion of late contest tallies removes the remaining legislative allocation among certification, executive discretion, and judicial contest. Palm Beach permits some late amendments; it does not abolish every residual limit. Second, the legal-vote construction conflates failure to tabulate a properly marked vote with failure to mark in the prescribed manner. The statutory instruction and machine provisions and the Secretary's delegated interpretation matter; §101.5614(5) does not furnish a general answer for these ballots.

They recognize §102.168's rejected-legal-vote ground, change-or-place-in-doubt language, and investigative authority, but find this use materially displaces the particular institutional and substantive limits. The pre-election existence of an intent provision does not decide its scope. Their disposition reverses the affected directions and remands for conformity with the pre-existing allocation and voting requirements, without invalidating the entire contest statute or deciding individual ballots. An equality protocol alone cannot cure unauthorized relief. Only if the Court rejects this Article II ground do they accept equality correction, confined to the state-law December 12 limit. They adopt neither an unsupported present impossibility finding nor a blanket termination of every lawful state action. The two rejected contest claims remain undisturbed.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting from corrective vacatur.** They join bounded review, rejection of independent Article II and due-process reversal, the federal-calendar rule, and refusal to impose a terminal state-law construction. They would affirm. A common clear-intent rule can produce different results from different physical evidence, and board disagreement, changing evidentiary judgments, or incomplete implementation detail does not itself establish that this judicial decree authorizes unconstitutional treatment. Spencer's and Burton's accounts warrant focused examination of disputed treatment through ordinary objections and judicial correction; they do not demonstrate that such correction is unavailable or that the operative statewide command is constitutionally defective.

Harper and Reynolds protect equal participation and weight, but do not demand mechanical uniformity for different evidence. Burdick permits neutral administration, and the actual Shaw rule requires an identified deprivation. The legislature's contest and corrective provisions and the state majority's substantial explanation support authoritative interpretation under Palm Beach; Yoshina prevents treating disagreement alone as fundamental unfairness. They require neither partisan bad faith nor proof that a winner changes. Their disagreement concerns the established defect and its attribution to the decree. If a constitutional defect is found, they accept a lawful corrective opportunity; that conditional position does not make them supporters of vacatur. They authorize no count beyond binding state law and make no feasibility or winner finding.

These are published noncontrolling propositions; no later treatment has been entered. Source: [published separate writing](../records/Bush_v_Gore_merits_2000-12-12.md#separate-writings).

### Mariani v. United States — 2000-12-12

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They would sustain both corporate challenges within the presented direct business-corporation treasury-to-federal-candidate class while joining the independent threshold and false-source answers. Their common ground accepts the contribution standard and legitimate direct-candidate corruption risk but finds the government's justification insufficient for complete exclusion of this source. A fund made from other voluntary contributions is a different pool, and lawful corporate administration does not establish that it answers the chosen association's lost treasury support. Candidate-connected party financing sharpens the fit problem without proving that every transaction is identical or that all underinclusive laws fail.

National Right to Work Committee's solicitation holding, protection for economic payers, and corporate-channel rationale are substantial contrary authority. The dissent preserves those holdings but would not treat their deference as conclusive under the later actual close-fit rule and this complete-source challenge. Austin's independent-expenditure holding likewise remains undisturbed; the dissent's position is not expenditure scrutiny transplanted into contributions. No completed-bribe requirement, unlimited giving rule, new dollar ceiling, or finding of an actual failed fund is adopted. The three would reverse only the corporate constitutional answers and remand for conforming declaratory relief and ordinary determination of effects on actual charges. They would affirm the independent false-source answer and order neither acquittal nor a remedy against unchallenged party practices.

### Gitlitz v. Commissioner — 2001-01-09

Breyer dissents. He reads section 108(d)(7)(A)'s corporate-level direction together with paragraph (B)'s capture of disallowed shareholder losses as a specific exclusion-and-offset mechanism. In his view, excluded corporate discharge should not first become shareholder income that releases the very losses designated for corporate reduction. ### Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — 2001-01-09

Souter dissents, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. They read the defined waters term, the aquatic-protection objective and section 404(g)(1)'s state-administration structure to authorize this supported habitat-water application. Riverside Bayview leaves isolated waters open; its adjacency facts do not establish an exhaustive limit. Sweet Home and PUD No. 1 support applying broad protective language according to its actual conditions. The agency's interpretation reinforces their textual conclusion without turning every preamble into a source of binding authority or disregarding section 101(b)'s state role.

They would also sustain this application under Home Builders' complete independent externality route. The regulated subject is discharge of dredged or fill material into the claimed waters. Filling removes aquatic habitat used by a shared mobile population; loss in one State can diminish the resource available elsewhere, while another State cannot restore that out-of-state habitat through ordinary territorial authority. The regional production and conversion evidence supports substantial class-wide importance, and the agency's aquatic-use and interstate-bird findings, together with the breeding-colony material, connect the site functionally to that mechanism. Direct permit review of habitat filling reasonably fits the resulting spillover. These are supported inferences and bounded predictive judgments, not new measurements of population loss.

Their strongest contrary concern is that the regional report does not describe every pond or quantify this fill's effect. They find the combined functional evidence sufficient without requiring a separate substantial effect for each discharge, a site percentage, a specified scientific method or proof of failed interstate agreements. The estimate of up to 50 percent of United States migratory-waterfowl production is not a percentage attributable to this site or to Illinois. Neither bird presence nor conservation's national importance alone is sufficient. They identify no independent necessary-regime predicate, and Holland's treaty holding is not a Commerce holding. Their preserved view that actual habitat-altering construction may itself be economic conduct does not replace their full join to this externality ground. They would affirm jurisdiction without reopening the abandoned permit-merits challenge or awarding a permit.

### Lopez v. Davis — 2001-01-10

Stone-Zsela dissents, joined by Stevens and Kennedy. Congress's nonviolent-conviction criterion and successful-treatment condition make a genuine opportunity for consideration part of the rehabilitative scheme. Permission to deny a reduction does not make that adjacent eligibility language idle. A firearm sentencing circumstance may strongly indicate danger, but it is not the conviction Congress specified and does not establish that treatment and individual circumstances can never matter.

The dissent distinguishes Campbell's rules resolving recurring subsidiary issues from a rule foreclosing the selection Congress made available. Yang likewise permits relevant discretionary judgment within the statute, rather than revision of statutory eligibility. The dissent accepts structured consideration, including strong or nearly decisive weight for preconviction firearm conduct and relevant postconviction circumstances. It rejects neither every category nor every consideration of offense conduct.

The dissent would set aside this categorical bar, require the Bureau to establish whether Lopez successfully completed the qualifying treatment, and, if so, consider a reduction through a reasoned decision addressing safety and rehabilitation. The record establishes treatment admission eligibility, not completion. The Bureau could still deny a reduction on lawful individual grounds. No particular reduction, immediate release, universal constitutional hearing right or liberty entitlement would follow.

### Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association — 2001-02-20

Kennedy dissents, joined by Scalia and Thomas. American Manufacturers requires actual governmental responsibility for the challenged choice. Public employment and numerical predominance do not alone establish that administrators act in a governmental capacity when they voluntarily govern an association representing public and private members. Although every voting governor was a public-school administrator when the penalties issued, the rules permit private-school administrators and guarantee no permanent governmental majority. The nonvoting state representatives, independent gate-receipt financing and retirement eligibility without a state-paid employer contribution do not supply that missing responsibility.

The dissent accepts that Lebron states a sufficient, nonexclusive structural rule and that private incorporation is not conclusive. It nevertheless finds no comparable actual governmental control, state compulsion, exclusive public function or adequately established official character for this enforcement. Tarkanian's single-State distinction is relevant but does not itself convert an association into government. The Court's strongest argument is that schools can exercise their responsibilities collectively without an external command. The dissent regards the inference of official capacity as unproved by this voluntary structure and rejects a rule that treats the officials' associational choices as governmental merely because they predominate. It gives effect to the actual 1996 textual amendment while recognizing unchanged operation. It would affirm the attribution ruling and decide neither recruiting-rule constitutionality nor the appropriate relief.

### Illinois v. McArthur — 2001-02-20

**Souter concurs and joins the Court opinion in full.** Exigency and permissible scope depend on the risk at the time of the proposed action. In his assessment, while McArthur was inside with immediate access to the hidden drugs, the actual destruction risk could have justified prompt entry and search. Once McArthur stepped outside and police could control access, the same immediate necessity for a greater warrantless evidentiary intrusion was absent. Temporary control of reentry while pursuing a warrant respects the preference for a magistrate's authorization and affords a reasonable opportunity to secure it. Police need not let an occupant return simply to recreate the emergency that would support a greater intrusion. This analysis distinguishes the risk and available response at each point; it does not find that a greater search occurred, infer an unreported occupant or turn the later discoveries into the earlier justification. The conditional greater-intrusion view belongs to his concurrence and is not an additional Court holding.

**Stone-Zsela dissents.** Control of access to a home is a seizure requiring an adequate warrant exception. The wife's contemporaneous observation of concealment strengthens probable cause and bears on risk, but hiding an existing stash does not itself show imminent destruction. McArthur could see his wife confer with police and leave with an officer; those facts support anticipation of a search, but add no particularized urgency beyond that awareness and the disposable character of drugs. Treating that combination as sufficient would authorize a similar restraint in nearly every residential drug investigation. An actual destruction attempt is not invariably required, but the inference must concern imminent loss rather than probable cause alone.

The two-hour duration and accompanied entries reduce the burden but cannot supply the missing exception. Segura does not validate every initial seizure because a later warrant produces evidence. Welsh gives the modest offenses real weight without misclassifying them as nonjailable. Contextual reasonableness evaluates a demonstrated exception; it cannot replace the warrant boundary. Stone-Zsela would affirm suppression for the search resulting from the unsupported restraint, subject to any independently preserved exclusionary-rule issue actually left unresolved. He would not invalidate the later warrant on an unpresented ground or dismiss independently supported charges.

**Stevens dissents.** He first would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because these slight possession offenses make an unsuitable occasion to define the limits of restraints on residential possession. Because the Court decides the merits, he would affirm. Welsh requires serious attention to offense gravity, and his understanding of Segura gives deprivation of home possession independent significance despite an eventual warrant. Even accepting probable cause, diligent warrant pursuit, accompanied entries and the comparatively limited restraint, he concludes that the substantial interest in private use of the home outweighs this modest preservation interest. His position concerns these facts; it neither denies that the offenses permit imprisonment nor categorically prohibits every misdemeanor preservation measure. He does not demand an observed destruction attempt in every case.

### Urofsky v. Gilmore — 2001-02-20

**Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I and II and dissents from the academic disposition.** He recognizes an individual interest in bona fide independent university teaching and research, distinguished by its scholarly function from the State's prescribed message. Boring left that question open; Sweezy and Keyishian support protection against ideological control. Pickering and NTEU then require an adequately justified fit, and he concludes that this record already establishes the violation.

The removed indecency-law assignment, planned Victorian-poetry study, website interference and curtailed inquiry establish concrete activity and burden. The amended definition still reaches protected material studied for scholarly purposes. No denied request or statistical showing of abstract harm is needed to challenge the compulsory condition. The State's efficiency and harassment interests are legitimate, but content-based discretionary permission and public identification of research are insufficiently connected to misuse or unwanted exposure of colleagues. The narrower definition, academic delegation and successful permissions do not remove that burden or justify public approval records. Ordinary professional, curriculum, budget, resource-use and anti-harassment rules remain available.

He would reverse the rejection of the individual academic claim and direct a declaration and injunction against enforcing the prior-approval and associated public-disclosure condition on the plaintiffs' bona fide independent teaching and research. The lower court should identify covered scholarly activities and frame workable terms, not reopen whether the established defect warrants relief. He would not restore the agency-wide employee decree, award damages against the official-capacity defendant, grant a personal-use right or decide a hypothetical refused project. Independent disputes over scholarly versus prescribed institutional activity retain their own facts.

**O'Connor concurs in Part I and dissents from vacatur of the academic disposition.** She assumes a qualifying scholarly interest and applies the case-specific Pickering balance reflected in her NTEU position. She does not adopt the Court's affirmative personal-interest rule or Scalia and Thomas's antecedent-entitlement rejection. Boring reserves university inquiry while preserving institutional control, and Waters recognizes genuine employer operational concerns.

The narrowed dominant-theme and lascivious-content requirements, separate research and other-undertaking approval branches, internal administration, prompt permissions and blanket approvals support the actual condition. The record also shows genuine misuse concerns. Preventive regulation need not await actionable harassment or completed injuries. Written and publicly available approvals impose a real accountability burden, but that burden does not outweigh these interests on this record. Administrative delegation is relevant without becoming a statutory universal peer-review requirement; successful approvals are relevant without guaranteeing all future requests. She would affirm the faculty disposition without denying every personal academic interest, sustaining a future discriminatory refusal or adopting a universal rule permitting advance restrictions.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in Part I and dissents from the academic vacatur on two independently sufficient grounds.** First, scholarly value and delegated discretion do not establish the claimed personal entitlement to these publicly supplied research means. Boring's university reservation is not itself an affirmative access right; neither its limited assigned-production rule nor every duty-related statement is mechanically controlling. Keyishian's independent restrictions on orthodoxy and association survive, while Sweezy's separate rationales establish no general faculty authority over public resources. The present limited equipment condition does not itself prescribe an academic conclusion or association.

Second, even assuming a personal interest, the condition survives the employment balance. Their NTEU position gives weight to preventive and administrable restrictions; Waters preserves genuine permissible employer purposes without a free-standing investigative right. The narrower covered category, reported misuse and institutional approval of required research or other undertakings provide adequate protection and accommodation. The written public-approval burden does not demand completed misconduct or expert prediction before prevention is permissible. They would affirm on either ground, preserve independent constitutional prohibitions and decide no future refused project. O'Connor's agreement in result supplies no join to their antecedent-interest ground or to this entire writing.
