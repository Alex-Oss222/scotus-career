PETITIONER’S REPLY 


Introduction 


This case presents the question whether the Free Exercise 
and Equal Protection Clauses prohibit government 
discrimination on the basis of religion in the specific context 
of a government program providing special education 
services to disabled children. In the Petition for Certiorari, 
we showed that this question is an important and recurring 
one that has arisen throughout the nation in a variety of 
factual contexts (Pet. 11-16, 20-21). The State Respondent’s 
Brief in Opposition (“Opp.”) does not appear to disagree. ' 
Stripped of its formulaic catch phrases—Respondent asserts, 
e.g., that Petitioner’s claims are “fact-bound” (Opp. 8), and 
that the alleged split is “more apparent than real” (Opp. 11)— 
Respondent’s brief effectively concedes that the decision and 
reasoning of the Ninth Circuit conflicts with the decision of 
the Eighth Circuit in a case involving similar facts, Peter v. 
Wedl, 155 F.3d 992 (8th Cir. 1998), and also with those of the 
Sixth and Third Circuits. Hartmann v. Stone, 68 F.3d 973 
(6th Cir. 1995); Brown v. Borough of Mahaffey, Pa., 35 F.3d 
846 (3d Cir. 1994).2 As Respondent puts it, “[a]n 
examination of those decisions, admittedly, may reveal some 
analytical tension between them” (Opp. 8). “Some analytical 
tension” is a decided understatement. 


' Respondent Reedsport School District filed its own Brief in 
Opposition (Dist. Opp.). Because the District has, for the most part, 
merely incorporated by reference the State’s arguments, this Reply 
focuses on the State Respondent’s Brief in Opposition, except where 
specifically noted.

and this Court’s decisions in Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. 
City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993), and McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618 
(1978). (Opp. 10-11).

Having conceded a conflict, the State Respondent advances 
two arguments against certiorari: first, Respondent insists 
that this case “will soon be moot” (Opp. 11); and second, 
Respondent contends that this case is too “fact-bound” (Opp. 
8) to serve as a vehicle for resolving the admitted conflict 
among the Circuits on the important constitutional questions 
raised in the Petition. Neither argument has much substance. 


Respondent suggests that the case “is on the verge of 
becoming moot” (Opp. 12), in light of a contemplated change 
in the state’s regulation (Opp. 11-14). This suggestion is 
wrong. Even a repeal of the state’s regulation would not 
moot Petitioner’s claims against the Reedsport School 
District, the other Respondent in this case,’ just as a similar 
mid-litigation regulatory change did not moot the claims in 
Peter v. Wedl. See 155 F.3d at 995-996 (noting change in 
rule), on remand, Westendorp v. Indep. Sch. Dist. No. 273, 35 
F. Supp. 2d 1134 (D. Minn. 1998) (awarding “retrospective 
equitable relief’ in light of school district’s past denial of 
special educational services). Here, as in Peter, Petitioner 
ultimately seeks relief against the District. This relief may 
include, for example, compensatory provision of on-site 
special services for the number of years that such services 
were unlawfully denied in the past. See Westendorp, 35 F. 
Supp. 2d at 1138 (ordering six years of on-site special 
education services at child’s religious school as remedy for 
six years of unlawful denial of such services).*

Here, the Reedsport School District treated Kai Moore
