19. Since 1986, the Fallon Paiute-Shoshone Tribes have 
had a specific, codified, policy and process to allow agents 
of a state government to “discover property which will 
substantially aid the requesting government and which 1s 
stolen, or illegal to possess or is the instrumentality, 
evidence, or fruit of a crime,” which requires: (1) that an 
agreement between the state and Fallon Tribes have been 
executed, (2) that a felony investigation be in process, (3) 
that a tribal “judge shall issue a Tribal search warrant to be 
executed and returned as are other Tribal search warrants,” 
and (4) that a hearing be held whereby the person from 
whom property may have been seized could have due 
process of law.

BEFORE: BEYER, VAN WINKLE AND MCDADE 


This case is before this Court following an Appeal from 
an Order issued by Fallon’s Tribal Judge, Joseph J. Van 
Walraven. Judge Van Walraven issued an Order Quashing 
Service of Process as to Specially Appearing Defendant’s 
on May 15, 1993. In that Order Judge Van Walraven ruled: 


1. “That the doctrine of sovereign or qualified immunity 
does not prevent the Fallon Tribal Court from exerting 
personal jurisdiction over the State Defendant’s; and 


tN 


That the Court is without jurisdiction over the persons 
of the State Defendant’s for the reason that the 
Business Council of the Fallon Paiute Shoshone Tribes 
has enacted no procedure for service outside of its 
territorial jurisdiction by Certified Mail.” 


We believe that the basis for Judge Van Walraven’s 
ruling is undoubtedly predicated on Title 1, chapter 30, 


23 


section 40 of the Fallon Law and Order Code which reads as 
follows: 


“The Nevada Rules of Civil Procedure and the general 
Civil Laws of the State of Nevada shall be used as a 
guide in cases in the Tribal Court involving claims 
between parties but not so as to impede the 
administration of justice nor contravene the application 
of any Tribal law or custom or federal law nor to 
produce any absurd result nor impose any regulatory 
scheme or policy not contemplated by the Tribe nor to 
interfere with the Tribes’ laws or policies on any 
subject regulated by the Tribes.” 


In review of the Nevada Rules of Civil Procedure, 
Rules | thru 5, we note that there is no provision for Service 
by Certified Mail. Even to the extent that Judge Van 
Walraven felt compelled to utilize the provisions of NRS 2s 
a guide, clearly there was no authority for the type of 
service utilized by the Plaintiff/Appellee. 


We note also that Judge Van Walraven issued an Order 
on May 3rd, 1993 applying the Nevada Rules of Civil 
Procedure as of that date “for the remainder of this case”. 
Most assuredly the parties were put on notice as to the 
Court’s intent given the provisions of 1-30-40 of Fallon’s 
Tribal Law and Order Code. 


24 


As such, we affirm Judge Van Walraven’s Order 
Quashing Service
