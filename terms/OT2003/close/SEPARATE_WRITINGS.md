## 2003-10-14 — Demore v. Kim; No. 01-1491; judgment below, 276 F.3d 523 (9th Cir. 2002).

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** He joins Parts I, II-A and II-B and accepts the entire vacatur, remand and interim-release remedy so Kim can receive the individual assessment available under the statutory pathway. He withholds assent from the allocation of persuasion to Kim. In his view, when genuinely contested inclusion or constitutionally insufficient continuing authority requires individual review, the Government should prove by clear and convincing evidence that reasonably available conditions, separately or together, cannot adequately address flight risk or danger; he would also accept governmental preponderance with the same object of proof and effective relief. Mathews directs attention to liberty and error; Salerno and Addington offer regime-specific support for the preferred quantum; Cooper illustrates the consequences of a burden that wrongly allocates uncertainty. Krimstock supports meaningful review and consideration of alternatives, while his separate preponderance position there remains his own. Kim seeks liberty in a civil immigration proceeding, not release pending appeal of his criminal convictions, so section 3143(b) does not persuade him to place the risk of uncertainty on Kim. The neutral decisionmaker should receive contrary evidence, assess residence, family and community ties, convictions, release conduct, appearance evidence and feasible conditions, and give reviewable reasons; failure of the governmental showing should permit conditional release.

He also explains that lawful initial detention does not justify continuation after its nature, duration or circumstances lose a reasonable relationship to removal proceedings pursued with reasonable diligence. A resident would identify a concrete continuing-custody concern; the Government would supply the chronology, basis, delay explanations and anticipated course and prove disputed diligence facts by preponderance. Legitimate substantial defenses are not automatically misconduct; solely dilatory conduct may count against the resident, and unjustified governmental or institutional delay weighs against categorical continuation. If that authority fails, individualized justification and effective relief are required; material change permits renewed review. Gilbert, Fairley, Foucha and Jackson support the continuing-authority inquiry; Flores preserves lawful temporary custody, and Zadvydas contributes the removal-purpose relationship without its separate calendar or burden. The present statutory holding leaves this constitutional limit open. His proposals are noncontrolling, supply no finding that Kim's past detention crossed the limit, and neither condition the entered judgment nor alter its remand instructions.

**Justice Souter, concurring, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** They join the entire lead opinion because its substantial-question pathway affords genuine individual consideration and protects Kim's existing release during the inquiry. They retain the broader view that a lawful permanent resident must receive a reasonably prompt, impartial opportunity to contest the necessity of custody on flight-risk and danger grounds. A past criminal category cannot conclusively establish present need throughout the proceeding. Carlson retained individual discretion, Flores retained real release and review avenues, and Zadvydas ties detention to its purpose; National Center and the continuing-deprivation decisions support effective inquiry into the actual justification. A rebuttable initial category presumption may be permissible, but meaningful consideration and effective release authority are essential. Their statutory join accepts Part III's allocation within that pathway, without making it a universal burden or approving categorical constitutional validity outside the threshold. That broader constitutional position is noncontrolling.

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** They accept pre-order operation of section 1226(c), but would reverse the resident-wide constitutional hearing requirement and remand for implementation in the actual custody and removal posture. Congress may make a properly established criminal category sufficient during reasonably dispatched removal proceedings. Flores supports temporary categorical arrangements; the statutory classification, rather than risk alone, supplies the substantive predicate. Gilbert, Krimstock and Fairley require testing the actual legal justification, not adding a new risk exception to a valid category. Joseph and the proper review channels must address genuine membership disputes. A substantial challenge does not itself suspend Congress's custody rule, and the express witness-protection exception does not authorize the Court's implied bond pathway. The disputed charges require their own lawful treatment; the requested continuance and elapsed six months do not establish the exceptional constitutional defect asserted here. O'Connor and Kennedy join the Court's jurisdiction holding; Scalia and Thomas reach these merits on the Court's jurisdiction determination. This proposed reversal is noncontrolling.

**Justice Kennedy, additionally concurring in part and dissenting in part.** His disagreement with the statutory pathway preserves individual protection against unreasonable, unjustified or arbitrary continuing detention and denial of prescribed review. The Government must have actual support for mandatory category membership; unreasonable delay or failure of that justification can require individual flight-risk and danger consideration. His Flores and Zadvydas positions and Krimstock's inquiry into continuing authority support that distinction. The present record, including Kim's requested continuance and incomplete findings, does not establish that constitutional defect from elapsed time alone. This qualification of his reversal position is noncontrolling.

**Justice Scalia, additionally concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They read section 1226(e)'s second sentence as a bar on setting aside an executive detention or release action, including through this challenge to the mandatory framework. A legal or constitutional label cannot evade the action-based language. Their application of retained channels under other statutes does not settle this text. They decline Part I and proceed to the statutory merits on the Court's contrary jurisdiction determination, without announcing a comprehensive Suspension Clause rule. Their jurisdictional objection is noncontrolling.

## 2003-10-27 — Greenville Women's Clinic v. Commissioner; Fourth Circuit Nos. 01-2090 and 01-2235; 317 F.3d 357

Stone-Zsela concurs in part, concurs in the privacy judgment and dissents in part. He would affirm the clergy condition only as voluntary patient-requested assistance, allowing ordinary emergency and referral arrangements while forbidding an unreviewable private veto or compelled religious counseling. He also would sustain the contextual professional-care rules, including their application only to reasonably ascertainable obligations. He regards the professional setting and limiting constructions as sufficient for these facial dispositions. On identifiers, his ground is more demanding than Part IV-A: once compelled disclosure of intimate treatment records is established, the State must justify identity access by a concrete regulatory need that reasonably cannot be met through less identifying review. He would direct findings identifying the authorized recipients, demonstrated tasks, confidentiality protections and review mechanism, maintaining the existing privacy injunction to the extent needed to prevent unnecessary identifier disclosure while permitting nonidentifying inspection. They would affirm the clergy provision on the as-needed, patient-requested construction; the administering official's described preferences do not amend that bounded legal duty. They would sustain the best-practices provision because recognizable clinical standards supply a professional core, while leaving unforeseeable sanctions open to a properly grounded particular challenge. On records, they retain their Doe constitutional-source objection to extending substantive informational privacy. Independently assuming the asserted right, they find the direct clinical-regulation task, copying qualification and confidentiality controls sufficient for this facial challenge. They acknowledge the reported releases and statutory exceptions, while distinguishing an actual improper request or disclosure from invalidity of general inspection authority. Their grounds would affirm the appellate inspection disposition rather than reinstate the district court's categorical compelling-interest requirement.

## 2003-11-03 — Mitchell v. Esparza; No. 02-1369; 540 U.S. 12

Stone-Zsela concurs in rejection of automatic structural treatment and in preserving independent sentence grounds, but dissents from definitive foreclosure of the omission theory. He would vacate the categorical rationale and require the lower court to resolve the 1996 filing-date gate, identify the constitutional law available at each state adjudication and evaluate any legally available harmlessness inquiry against the actual charge and verdict. ## 2003-11-25 — Valeria v. Davis; Ninth Circuit No. 01-15219; judgment below, 307 F.3d 1036

Breyer dissents in part, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. They join the Court's governing legal rule but would vacate and remand because the lower court repeatedly treated absence of a prior discrimination remedy and racial animus as decisive. In their view its additional nonracial conclusion remains intertwined with those erroneous prerequisites, rather than independently applying Coalition's structural inquiry. Selective removal of bilingual-instruction authority, ordinary local control over comparable educational choices, the overwhelmingly minority population affected and the campaign record present a concrete question whether racial considerations defined the political allocation.

Their remand would examine the text and practical allocation, comparable decisions, context, waivers and amendment burdens on the developed record, respecting existing findings within their actual scope. It would not prescribe a new mandatory checklist or presume discrimination from demographics. If no racial mechanism is established, the State prevails. If one is established, the State must meet Adarand's common standard, and relief must address the proved unequal political barrier under ordinary remedial and state severability rules. The initiative would remain operative absent independently justified interim relief. They would neither direct a preferred curriculum nor declare the initiative unconstitutional on the present record.

## 2003-12-09 — Virginia v. Maryland; No. 129, Original; 540 U.S. 56

Kennedy, joined by Stevens, would sustain Maryland’s regulatory-authority exception and dismiss the broad exemption claim. They read the instruments as securing an enforceable riparian entitlement within Maryland’s river sovereignty, subject to compatible regulation. A later determination of title can confirm preexisting sovereignty; historical disagreement does not itself show that Maryland lacked regulatory authority. The Award’s full dominion over the shore and qualified use beyond it are distinct.

The dissent emphasizes that protected property and use ordinarily coexist with police-power regulation. An advance permit can test compliance with navigation and proper-use limits while remaining subordinate to the federal entitlement. The specific mutual-consent provisions also show that regulatory authority could be expressly allocated. Maryland therefore could not use a permit to nullify protected access, suppress Virginia’s development for its own sake or impose an unrelated policy veto, and judicial relief would remain available against such interference. But those constraints do not invalidate every compatible licensing requirement. Nor does ordinary interstate-water apportionment automatically answer the regulatory question for a river within one State’s territorial title.

They agree only conditionally that the supported permit history would not independently establish surrender if the instruments created the exemption the Court finds. Because they reject that antecedent construction, this observation supplies no join in the Court’s sovereignty or acquiescence opinion.

## 2003-12-10 — McConnell v. Federal Election Commission; 540 U.S. 93; Nos. 02-1674, 02-1675, 02-1676, 02-1702, 02-1727, 02-1733, 02-1734, 02-1740, 02-1747, 02-1753, 02-1755 and 02-1756.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the payroll portion only.** Stone-Zsela would reverse the invalidation of the whole-salary class and sustain section 323(b) within its enacted boundaries. The proved integrated fundraising and shared federal-election operation, tested under Lamb's government-borne justification and meaningful-association requirements, supports the federal-election-activity rules. Employees materially engaged in that operation are a resource channel, and the monthly more-than-quarter threshold leaves employees with only limited federal duties outside this class. The four regard the operational connection and threshold as sufficient on the facial record; the five-Justice Court instead requires a distinct determination connecting the whole salary consequence to the mechanism and local association. That disagreement concerns payroll fit and remedy. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only on the separate federal-power claims.

In the remedial portion, joined by no other Justice, Stone-Zsela would additionally direct the district court to retain an effective route through available procedures to review concrete genuine-issue, nonprofit, disabling-association and unjustified-disclosure claims before the challenged communication or political activity loses its practical value. Particular relief would require the governing record and showing. That affirmative timing instruction implements his practical-protection position; the Court's decree preserves ordinary application review and its governing standards.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Scalia joins throughout, and Thomas joins except the demand to invalidate every new section 504 application.** The writing distinguishes real candidate-controlled resources and tacit donor conduits from a party's own independently controlled or jointly expressed message. Colorado I and the separate Colorado II and Lamb positions protect the latter; accepted contribution and true-source rules remain. The three oppose the national-party and state-party sweep, the nonfederal section 323(d) residue and section 323(f)'s expression applications lacking the actual resource connection. They prefer payroll invalidation while directly joining the Court's narrower fit remand. They accept compliant-fund relief and section 323(e)'s direct official-request relationship with all its exceptions.

The three would protect the expanded primary treasury applications despite accepting ordinary primary reporting. Their preferred treasury disposition would require examining the complete enacted fallback: they would permit only explicit or functionally explicit electoral direction supplied by the message, with context explaining rather than generating the exhortation, and with any plausible nonelectoral meaning defeating coverage. They reject deletion of the limiter and preserve the statutory trigger, absence of backup time/geographic conditions and section 100.22(b) saving clause. Their own Maine and Bartlett positions support that formulation; they do not overrule Austin or demand canonical electoral words. All join the functional Day/MCFL nonprofit protection.

For coordination they accept actual requested resources and enforceable financial substitutes before airing, while seeking a present declaration protecting party-owned joint expression and independent issue/editorial work. They would protect contracts for separate unbroadcast independent messages under sections 201 and 212, preserving reports of unpaid obligations for messages already public and the dissemination-trigger regulation. Ordinary disclosure's informational justification does not justify exposing still-private plans at another advertisement's first airing.

Kennedy and Scalia would retain the whole injunction against the new section 504 duty: candidate-request administration and earlier agency practice do not sufficiently answer its new public exposure of rejected requests and nonelectoral legislative activity. They independently support narrower protection for unbroadcast noncandidate requests if broader relief is unavailable. Thomas joins that narrower protection and the issue-only objection, but not the claim that candidate-access and aired federal-election records all fail. These views neither invalidate old FCC rules nor create a general reporting exemption.

**Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part on attribution and broadcaster files.** His Day reporting and Mariani attribution positions support ordinary external financial reports and actual candidate-finance identification. His McIntyre and Stengel anonymity position gives distinct force to a command placing the speaker's identity on genuine independent issue advocacy. Section 311 has no section 201 monetary threshold; he would protect that on-message issue expression while preserving definite electoral-advocacy and otherwise valid candidate-finance attribution. Under section 504 he accepts candidate requests, including refusals, because actual candidate-access enforcement has a direct request-based relation. He also accepts external transaction files for aired noncandidate federal-candidate or federal-election messages. He would protect unaired noncandidate requests and national-legislative/issue-only records lacking that electoral relation. Where the request independently meets the valid candidate or election branch, an overlapping issue description does not remove its valid ground. These distinctions preserve real finance disclosure without treating every broadcast or organization as outside anonymity protection.

**Stevens, dissenting in part on section 305; joined by Ginsburg and Breyer.** Intended use of opponent-reference advertising, past practice and present effects on campaign strategy establish a sufficiently concrete controversy despite temporal distance. Lee and Maine Right to Life recognize civil burdens on planned conduct. Broadcasters may continue charging the same rate, but that causal uncertainty does not eliminate the statutory condition on the intended advertising course. The three would reach and sustain the condition: factual candidate identification and approval serve electoral information, substantially overlap section 311, add a limited compliance burden and condition a statutory rate benefit rather than generally prohibit speech. The rule reaches each candidate's opponent references, including responses, and is not viewpoint selective on that basis. The writing adopts no universal rule sustaining every financial penalty attached to protected speech.

## 2003-12-15 — Castro v. United States; No. 02-6683; 540 U.S. 375

**Scalia, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the jurisdiction holding and concur in vacatur and remand on an independent ground. Liberal construction of allegations does not authorize overriding a prisoner’s chosen procedural vehicle and imposing the costs of an unchosen first section 2255 motion. Castro selected and reiterated Rule 33; the judge imposed the additional label. The concurrence would avoid involuntary first-motion conversion when its adverse risks cannot be removed, rather than treat warnings as sufficient authority to change the vehicle. A court cannot reliably foresee every consequence, and an apparent immediate advantage may disappear on appeal. Their position respects the enacted successive predicate rather than creating an equitable exception once that predicate exists. They agree that law of the case does not conclusively bar inquiry, but do not join the Court’s affirmative warning framework or its warning-based application.

## 2004-01-13 — Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP; No. 02-682.

**Scalia, dissenting in part, joined by O'Connor and Thomas.** The alleged customer diversion is the consequence of deficient compelled assistance, rather than an independently sufficient antitrust wrong. CSU preserves the requirement of such a wrong and the absence of a general duty to assist rivals; NYNEX separates regulatory misconduct from injury to the competitive process. Exclusionary purpose and a sharing command cannot supply the antitrust dealing obligation the complaint assumes. The dissent would dismiss the prospective claim for that conduct defect. It accepts Cargill's distinct equity standard and does not treat the damages ruling as a substitute for the missing offense analysis. All three join the Court's damages holding.

**Ginsburg, dissenting in part, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy and Breyer.** The alleged impairment of end-user orders may itself be the instrument by which Verizon discourages rival patronage. McCready therefore supplies a potentially closer causal model than the remote expense chain in Steamfitters. The Court should remand for assessment of Trinko's actual personal loss, intervening steps, overlap with carrier recovery and workable allocation under Associated General Contractors. The dissent would allow any amendment properly permitted by ordinary rules and dismiss an insufficient theory, while allowing a sufficiently direct independent claim to proceed. It does not find the damages predicates established. All four join the conduct and prospective-relief holdings.

## 2004-01-14 — Frew v. Hawkins; No. 02-628; 540 U.S. 431.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's prospective-enforcement rules, modification framework and complete reversal and remand. He would additionally require a concrete compliance schedule for established failures, evidence of performance and lawful enforcement if officials fail to comply. These directions would follow ordinary review of the actual compliance findings and remedial issues. Because the decree establishes a class-wide obligation, he would require officials proposing a narrower corrective measure to show that it fully cures the proved breach.

The Chief Justice reasons that recognizing an enforceable decree must produce effective implementation of its lawful federal obligations. Verizon Maryland supplies the prospective-officer remedy, Firefighters recognizes lawful implementation beyond a bare statutory minimum and Rufo preserves the operative decree until lawful modification. Those principles support his proposed practical requirements for curing established failures. He would also require the court to decide any supported Rule 60(b)(5) request under Rufo, so enforcement protects the beneficiary class while supervision ends when the decree's obligations and legitimate purposes are satisfied. His position addresses the officials bound by this decree and its covered beneficiaries, with enforcement tailored to its federal purposes.

## 2004-01-21 — Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation v. EPA; No. 02-658.

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The Act specifically assigns BACT's case-specific weighing to the state permitting authority. The dissent reads the federal prevention provisions to enforce required permits, required consideration and independently specified federal conditions, but not to create a federal merits-review channel over the completed discretionary balance. ## 2004-01-26 — Fellers v. United States; No. 02-6320.

## 2004-02-05 — Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton; D.C. Circuit No. 01-5373; judgment below reported at 323 F.3d 1062.

**Stevens, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Souter.** They join Parts II and III and the judgment. The Court's necessary-inclusion ground sustains this application of the taking prohibition, as they agreed in Home Builders. They would also affirm on a further ground: the activity directly producing the alleged habitat injury is commercial housing construction and land conversion. Sweet Home identifies that physical habitat alteration as the conduct reached by the taking prohibition. Their additional ground therefore looks to the actual economic application, rather than to the developer's collateral equipment purchases or a generalized claim that species preservation benefits the national economy.

Wickard and Perez permit class-wide regulation of connected economic activity, and Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association permits regulation of economic land use for environmental ends. On that reasoning, the actual construction supplies a sufficient economic connection without a market in the toad or a newly found ecological spillover. They accept the continuing statutory requirements of actual habitat-caused injury and ordinary causation; their additional ground concerns the Commerce Clause challenge.

Home Builders's contrary enacted-class holding directly obstructs this ground, and they do not join Part I. Stevens and Souter would reconsider that holding to the extent it excludes reliance on the actual injury-producing economic activity in this application. They regard treating the prohibition's broader coverage as a reason to disregard this commercial application as a mistaken class inquiry. They neither claim that their earlier dissent already controls nor distinguish Home Builders through a new factual premise. Their ground is an additional reason for the same judgment; it would not make every noneconomic taking an economic activity.

## 2004-02-24 — Banks v. Dretke; No. 02-8286.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join the ruling permitting consideration of the developed evidence and the Cook certificate disposition. They reject Farr materiality after considering disclosure's investigative and impeachment uses: the murder, the independent pistol-assault account and Banks's admitted gun assistance leave them confident in the penalty result despite Farr's concealed role.

Because they reject the Farr route, they also address the independently presented penalty-assistance claim. ## 2004-02-24 — Doe v. Chao; No. 02-1377.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens and Breyer, dissenting.** They would restore Doe's $1,000 award. In their reading, the opening liability command identifies the persons entitled to recovery once an intentional or willful violation causes a genuine adverse effect, and the proviso supplies an independent minimum for those persons. The actual-damages language permits larger compensation for proved loss rather than imposing a further threshold on the minimum.

They distinguish a fixed amount expressly enacted by Congress from unlimited presumed general damages. The intentional-or-willful requirement and genuine adverse effect restrict recovery and answer the concern that a harmless technical violation would produce an award. Breyer's emphasis on that culpability limit is part of the shared dissent. MacArthur left this monetary issue open; its separation of injury from ultimate recovery does not, for the dissenters, decide which reading of the proviso is correct.

## 2004-02-24 — General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline; No. 02-1080.

**Thomas, joined by Kennedy, dissenting.** They read the enacted prohibition on discrimination because of age as covering either direction within the protected class. Congress specified the age-forty boundary but did not place a second, older-versus-younger restriction in the operative command. They reject narrowing that language through general statutory purpose or assumptions about the usual victim of age discrimination. They would sustain statutory coverage and leave remaining liability and defenses for ordinary proceedings.

**Scalia, dissenting.** Scalia would sustain coverage because the EEOC's bilateral interpretation is a permissible construction entitled to acceptance. His ground does not require agreement that the employees' reading is the single best interpretation. The regulation treats discrimination between protected-age workers bilaterally. Its separate additional-benefits provision says such benefits may be lawful if the employer has a reasonable basis to conclude they will counteract problems related to age discrimination, while forbidding their use to carry out otherwise prohibited practices. Scalia would preserve that qualification for application below with any statutory defenses, rather than exclude this claim categorically.

## 2004-02-24 — Groh v. Ramirez; No. 02-811.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees that the warrant violated particularity. He would nevertheless grant immunity because the detailed application, magistrate's approval and clerical character of the omission made the officer's mistaken reliance objectively reasonable in the legal circumstances. He distinguishes the existence of a defect from sufficiently clear notice imposing individual liability.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** They resist treating a defective warrant as automatically making the execution an unreasonable warrantless search. On the officer's asserted account of a confined search following the detailed application, they would examine the actual reasonableness of execution rather than make the form defect conclusive. That account remains an assertion, including the disputed oral explanation. Independently, they would grant qualified immunity even assuming a constitutional violation, because the application and judicial approval leave sufficient uncertainty about individual liability. The Court's denial of immunity therefore conflicts with both their alternative merits approach and their separate notice ground.

## 2004-02-24 — Olympic Airways v. Husain; No. 02-1348.

**Scalia, joined by O'Connor, dissenting.** They would vacate and remand. In their reading of Saks and the treaty text, failure to alter ordinary cabin conditions is not itself the event that Article 17 requires. Calling nonperformance an unexpected response risks converting the accident condition into a duty-of-care inquiry.

They distinguish the attendant's affirmative statement that the plane was full. A false statement may be an external event even if mere nonperformance is not. They would have the lower courts examine any properly available theory based on that affirmative conduct and its actual causal significance under the existing record and ordinary preservation rules. They do not assume that the misinformation independently caused the injury or that the unoccupied seats establish that separate causal link.

## 2004-02-25 — Locke v. Davey, No. 02-1315

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal insofar as the judgment compels payment for the established pastoral-formation function, and dissents from ending any preserved genuinely separated secular claim. He applies Strout's actual-function refusal rule and Columbia Union's requirement that proof concern the work the public funds purchase. The claimant must identify the denied benefit and unequal treatment; the State's funded-function justification must match the actual course and payment design. Religious governance, faith and speculative freeing of resources do not establish a devotional purchase. The scholarship's actual terms must determine whether independently eligible secular study exists; the business major alone proves neither separation nor inseparability. He would remand that preserved question for equal consideration, while rejecting unconditional payment and damages on the devotional-subsidy theory.

Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. The actual award is independently directed educational assistance, including living costs, within Witters's surviving relationship. Under Lukumi and the equality principles of K.D.M. and Columbia Union, excluding an otherwise eligible student for devotional study requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring. A historical preference against ministry support does not supply the necessary justification for withholding this student benefit. Allowing a business degree only at another institution does not answer the discrimination at the eligible college. The dissent would require equal consideration under the remaining lawful academic, income and enrollment conditions and separate adjudication of any properly presented monetary claim.

## 2004-03-02 — Baldwin v. Reese, No. 02-964

Stevens dissents. He would exercise the Court’s discretion to consider Guinn and finds its Oregon-law context sufficient for fair notice on these papers. He would place on officials the burden to show that a narrower remedy fully cures the proved mandatory policy.

Under his proposed proof rule, a claimant establishes inability by a preponderance through a sworn account of available income, reasonably available assets, necessary living expenses and dependent obligations. Material hardship means sacrificing ordinary necessities or assuming an unreasonable financial burden, rather than merely preferring not to pay. Poverty-line status would be neither necessary nor conclusive. Officials could verify a material factual dispute but could not demand unavailable records or postpone a decision beyond the relevant ballot deadline. Bullock and Lubin supply the inability-to-pay protection, while Cartwright's calibrated review governs the resulting burden and justification; the preliminary hardship procedure shows that an administrable mechanism is possible without dispensing with evidence for a disputed claim.

He would require a published hardship application and objective decision criteria before the next relevant filing deadline, identification of the deciding official, written reasons for denial and prompt review early enough for a successful candidate to reach the ballot. A petition alternative would count only if its deadlines and support requirements were realistically usable. The district court would set dates tied to the election calendar and provide provisional filing when official delay would otherwise defeat relief. Officials would accept qualifying filings and maintain reasons sufficient for review, while the court retained enforcement authority. Independent lawful qualifications would remain controlling, and candidates able to pay would receive no automatic exemption. He would preserve the claimant-specific dispositions and any independently available fee award. These are his proposed additional proof and remedial rules; the operative vacatur and instructions are those in the Court's Part III.

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, joins Parts I and II but would affirm the present injunction limiting enforceable relief to Stith and Linzey upon demonstrated hardship. The constitutional rule protects every qualifying financial-exclusion claim through precedent, but this action was litigated as applied. In their view the current candidacies define the established need for an injunction; the party's standing alone does not establish that a wider order is necessary. They would permit enlargement after a concrete determination that distinct proved party injury remains unremedied by the existing terms. They agree that Rule 65(d) requires specific conditions and official duties and that a realistic alternative must protect a candidate whose inability is shown.

## 2004-06-14 — Elk Grove Unified School District v. Newdow; No. 02-1624; 542 U.S. 1.

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** The retained parent-child instructional relationship supplies an adequately alleged personal injury, and Ankenbrandt permits the federal claim without changing custody. On the merits, Sherman directly permits the materially equivalent civic Pledge where pupils may decline words and gestures, remain in place and suffer no penalty. Agostini preserves that established permissible relationship within its unified inquiry. The practice is governmental speech, but its religious acknowledgment need not become meaningless to believers. Protected abstention matters in this civic exercise; it does not validate official prayer generally. Lee and Santa Fe remain controlling for school-organized worship and pressure. These Justices would restore dismissal on the merits under the concrete civic rule.

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** Newdow's retained instructional relationship can sustain a personal claim even though he cannot represent his daughter or override the final custody allocation. The recurring official exercise directly affects that relationship; this is not merely disagreement with the mother's beliefs or ordinary curriculum. Ankenbrandt rejects a categorical family-law barrier, and Lujan requires the retained interest, concrete interference and redressability.

The Chief would overrule Sherman only insofar as it permits teacher-led elementary-school affirmation of the religious proposition within the Pledge. Sherman supplies the contrary permission and Agostini did not erase it. Lee and Santa Fe's treatment of official school sponsorship and religious pressure supports reconsidering that permission openly: teachers organize affirmation of a religious proposition as part of the school's own civic program. Longstanding use and administrative reliance deserve weight but do not justify the profession. Protected abstention under Sherman and Barnette remains, while a claimant-only opt-out leaves the official practice in place.

He would affirm the constitutional rejection and require a declaration and prospective order removing the religious affirmation from the organized exercise while preserving a secular patriotic program, historical instruction and private speech. Administrators would communicate compliant instructions before the next exercise governed by the order. Officials would bear the burden of showing a narrower effective cure. Any demonstrated material custody ambiguity would receive prompt, limited clarification without postponing relief on an otherwise established personal interest. Other governmental ceremonies present a different question.

## 2004-06-14 — Hibbs v. Winn; No. 02-1809; 542 U.S. 88.

## 2004-06-14 — Pennsylvania State Police v. Suders; No. 03-95; 542 U.S. 129.

**Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer.** These Justices join the entire Court opinion and would specify additional terms for the loss inquiry. After the employee proves the claim and otherwise compensable injury, the employer would have to prove by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use them; and that the particular loss consequently could have been avoided. Reduction or denial would extend only to that proved loss.

A paper policy, nonreporting alone, unsafe or futile channels, correction after injury or the mere possibility of a complaint would not suffice. The employee need not complain to the harasser. Injury before a reasonable protective opportunity and injury the procedure could not prevent would remain compensable if otherwise established. Even proof that all compensable loss was avoidable would eliminate that recovery rather than erase the violation or automatically decide other relief and fees. The rule would contain neither special immunity where no official act occurs nor a categorical exception from ordinary avoidability whenever one occurs. An official coercive act may make effective protection or avoidability impossible to establish on the facts. Their proposal develops the causal recovery approach in the Ellerth concurrence; it has four votes here.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices join Part I but would vacate and remand under a newly adopted complete defense. An employer would have to prove by a preponderance both reasonable care to prevent and promptly correct the harassment and the employee's unreasonable failure to use provided preventive or corrective opportunities or otherwise avoid harm. The measures must be practically accessible and reasonably effective; unsafe or futile channels, paper policies and nonreporting alone would not establish the defense, and no particular written-policy form is invariably required. The employee would retain the actual-authority/material-aid burden.

The defense would be unavailable when an official discriminatory employment act precipitated the compelled resignation—for example, a demotion, extreme pay cut or transfer imposing unbearable working conditions through delegated employer authority. Authority to make the employment decision does not mean the employer authorized its discriminatory motive. The employee would establish that official act and its causal connection; the employer would retain persuasion on both defense elements. Resignation itself would not become an official act merely because it was objectively compelled. The alleged arrest matters to authority, coercion and safe complaint opportunities but does not conclusively determine the employment-act exception.

The dissent distinguishes delegated power materially aiding an environment from an official decision directly consummating the discrimination. Agency responsibility, in its view, permits a defense for genuine diligence and unreasonable nonuse in the former setting while withholding it in the latter. That rule deliberately does more than reduce particular avoidable losses; the dissent rejects the Court's view that attributable discrimination forecloses the complete defense. It would allow adjudication of the disputed defense rather than restore summary judgment for the employer.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Scalia in this comparative-application portion, agrees that protected adult speech is involved and that the Government bears the strict-scrutiny burden. They would reverse the determination that filtering establishes a sufficient likelihood of success to retain this injunction basis. Their disagreement concerns the supported comparison, not the governing standard.

COPA's conjunctive harmful-material requirements, commercial-coverage limits and alternative good-faith adult-access defenses materially reduce the burden compared with the broader restriction in Reno. The dissent accepts verification costs, anonymity concerns and the possibility of circumvention; the privacy provision does not erase those effects. It nevertheless finds the remaining burden sufficiently fitted to a protection filtering does not supply by itself: children whose parents do not obtain or activate household controls receive the benefit of COPA's default restriction. The comparative position stated in the Playboy dissent gives that activation gap substantial weight while recognizing that filters reach foreign and non-Web sources and preserve adult choice.

In these Justices' assessment, the narrower statutory category and access routes, considered together with that additional protection, carry the Government's burden at this preliminary stage. They would remand for lawful disposition of other preserved grounds. Breyer's conclusion further depends on the national-community construction stated below; O'Connor and Scalia join the comparative reasoning without adopting that construction.

Justice Breyer additionally requires the national adult community to supply the governing community standard. His earlier Ashcroft position reads the phrase in statutory context and in light of the serious difficulty caused when restrictive local standards govern nationally distributed speech. Different jury applications of one national standard differ from different substantive standards. That construction is necessary to his conclusion that the narrower offense and access defenses sufficiently answer the filtering comparison. O'Connor and Scalia do not join this construction.

## 2004-06-29 — Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain, No. 03-339; United States v. Alvarez-Machain, No. 03-485; 542 U.S. 692.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, joins Parts I and III and concurs in the ATS reversal on an additional-action source ground. They distinguish an international prohibition, federal jurisdiction and domestic authority to impose damages. Section 1350 supplies a forum, while the offered Declaration and Covenant do not themselves enact this private action. The received common-law setting is the strongest argument for the Court's method, but in their view it does not supply sufficient congressional authorization for the additional arbitrary-detention liability.

Their earlier writings in Abebe-Jira and Kadic/Doe preserve that source objection. Suter requires an actual source of private liability; O'Melveny and Atherton reject federal rules derived merely from a general federal interest; Meyer keeps forum, consent and substantive liability separate. Central Bank's established securities action and Musick, Peeler's treatment of incidents of recognized liability do not independently authorize this different extension. They respect the already adopted official-torture, genocide and core-war-crime holdings as precedents at their exact scopes, but decline to extend them to the present detention action without a sufficient enacted source. That independently sufficient ground explains their ATS judgment agreement and their refusal to join Part II's recognition methodology.

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justice Breyer, joins the Court's opinion and explains the conduct-based reason for the bounded FTCA result. Section 2680(k) must be read with §1346(b)(1), which locates the governing law by the relevant act or omission. For this claim, Mexico was the place of the last significant act necessary to create the alleged tort: the seizure and detention took place there, and the detention injury accrued there. Domestic planning was causally relevant but did not by itself complete the false-detention claim or convert its Mexican core into a domestic injury.

Richards supplies the place-of-conduct inquiry within the FTCA's statutory structure. Smith enforced the exception for acts wholly in Antarctica; its actual scope does not require an injury-only test for every combination of domestic and foreign conduct. These Justices therefore support the Court's common disposition of the same Mexican detention notwithstanding headquarters planning, while retaining a construction attentive to liability-creating conduct. Their direct join in Part III does not adopt a categorical rule that any injury abroad is conclusive regardless of where the tortious conduct occurred.

Justice Breyer joins the Court's opinion and adds a comity concern. International acceptance of a substantive prohibition does not necessarily establish agreement that the courts of every nation may enforce it against foreign persons for foreign conduct. Territorial or nationality connections ordinarily reduce that concern. For certain grave international crimes, acceptance of universal criminal jurisdiction supports related civil recovery because many legal systems combine criminal and civil adjudication; torture, genocide and core war crimes fit that established inquiry. He identifies no comparable procedural consensus for this brief arbitrary arrest. This further reason supports rejection here while preserving the existing actions and his join in Part II; it supplies no general controlling universal-jurisdiction or exhaustion rule.
