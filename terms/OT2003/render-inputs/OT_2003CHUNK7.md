## Event

Williams v. Morton; judgment below, 343 F.3d 212 (3d Cir. 2003). Merits decision, June 2, 2004. On review of the Third Circuit's September 9, 2003 judgment affirming summary judgment for prison officials, after full merits briefing. The questions are whether the refusal to add halal meat to the prisoners' religious diet violates Free Exercise, and whether providing meat to Jewish prisoners while withholding it from Muslim prisoners violates equal protection. The corrected record describes the kosher meals as meatless. The prisoners seek inclusion of halal meat in at least some meals and damages.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Third Circuit is affirmed on both constitutional claims.

## Holdings

### I. The governing constitutional and summary-judgment standards

**Holding and operative rule:** A prison restriction on sincere religious exercise must bear a reasonable relationship to legitimate, neutral penological objectives under Turner, considering the actual alternatives for exercise, the institutional consequences of accommodation and ready alternatives. Prisoners retain the ultimate burden to show unreasonableness, while officials seeking summary judgment must establish the absence of a genuine dispute over facts material to that inquiry.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, is the opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner v. Safley supplies substantial administrative latitude together with a real inquiry into the connection between the restriction and the identified objective. O'Lone v. Estate of Shabazz applies that inquiry to religious exercise in prison; Overton v. Bazzetta preserves the four considerations and the prisoner's ultimate burden. A proposed accommodation matters to the operational interests it actually answers. The Constitution does not require the least restrictive means, and the possibility of accommodation alone does not establish unreasonableness. Rule 56 nevertheless forbids weighing evidence on a genuinely material operational issue against the prisoners. Sincerity does not depend on proof that all adherents share the asserted religious obligation.

### II. The record supports the refusal to add halal meat

**Holding and operative rule:** The supplied meal-administration, screening, cost and program-scale evidence establishes a reasonable relationship between this diet policy and legitimate prison objectives. Evidence that outside vendors could supply halal meat does not create a material dispute defeating summary judgment when it leaves those sufficient operational grounds intact and does not establish an equally effective alternative at negligible institutional cost.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The proposed accommodation concerns approximately 225 prisoners, compared with four receiving the separate kosher meals. Simplified meal administration, screening and acknowledged added expense are supported concerns at that scale. The remaining actual opportunities for religious exercise bear on Turner's second consideration under O'Lone, without replacing the distinct inquiry into the burdened diet. Accommodation also requires consideration of resources, guards and other prisoners. The outside-vendor testimony supports possible supply; it does not eliminate those separate consequences or demonstrate an equally effective, negligible-cost alternative. The objection that deference cannot resolve material facts is correct as a statement of procedure, but it does not make every feasible supply route material enough to require a trial. The Court applies the combined operational record. Added cost alone is not a categorical answer to sincere religious exercise.

### III. The asserted meat-based comparator lacks its factual predicate

**Holding and operative rule:** An equal-protection claim premised on furnishing meat to one religious group while denying it to another requires evidence of that differential benefit. The corrected and corroborated record describes both religious diets as meatless, so the asserted meat preference supplies no triable equal-protection violation.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by all nine Justices, is the opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The earlier certification describing kosher meat was corrected, and additional testimony corroborates the correction. The claim must be assessed on that evidence, not the inaccurate premise embedded in the question presented. Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. City of Hialeah supplies the requirement to examine actual governmental religious treatment; different aggregate expenditures for differently sized programs do not establish the particular preference alleged here. The Free Exercise claim remains governed by its own inquiry irrespective of whether another faith receives meat.

The contamination claim was voluntarily dismissed and is outside review. The accepted question presents the constitutional diet claims; it does not present a preserved RLUIPA claim or the separate late-witness evidentiary ruling.

## Precedent Treatment

- Turner v. Safley is applied as the four-factor reasonable-relationship standard, preserving substantial deference and the absence of a least-restrictive-means requirement.
- O'Lone v. Estate of Shabazz is applied to sincere religious exercise and the actual available opportunities within prison.
- Overton v. Bazzetta is applied for the operational factors, supported objectives and prisoner's ultimate burden.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. City of Hialeah is applied to the need for actual religious targeting or differential treatment, which the asserted meat comparison does not establish.

## Law After Decision

Turner governs this constitutional diet claim, and Rule 56 continues to require a genuine dispute about a fact material under that standard. The supported cost, administration, screening and scale grounds sustain this policy on the record presented. The meat-based equal-protection theory fails because the alleged differential benefit is absent.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part, agrees that the corrected meatless-kosher record defeats the asserted equal-protection comparator and joins the governing Turner and Rule 56 standards. He would vacate the Free Exercise summary judgment because the outside-supply testimony requires a focused assessment of whether the proposed procurement method answers the actual security, scale and cost objections. Turner and Overton permit deference, but they do not permit the court to assume a disputed operational premise; acknowledged added cost remains relevant without categorically resolving the concrete proposal.

He would direct prompt identification of the genuinely disputed facts, targeted proof and a decision on the reasonableness of rejecting the actual proposal. If a violation were proved, a prospective remedy would require the findings under 18 U.S.C. §3626: necessity, narrow scope and least intrusive correction, with substantial weight to public-safety and system effects. He would require a workable accommodation schedule and compliance reporting; relief would reach prisoners affected by the proved unlawful policy unless officials established that a narrower cure sufficed. Damages and immunity would require individual adjudication. This remedial position supports a factual remand rather than procurement based on assumed feasibility.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit's affirmance of summary judgment remains effective on the two constitutional diet claims. Those claims are concluded by the affirmed officials' judgment.

## Event

Central Laborers' Pension Fund v. Heinz; No. 02-891. Argued April 19, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, which reversed judgment on the pleadings for the pension plan, 303 F.3d 802. The question is whether ERISA's anti-cutback rule prohibits expanding postretirement employment restrictions to suspend early-retirement benefits already accrued. The retirees accrued benefits under terms permitting supervisory construction work; the plan's 1998 amendment added that work to disqualifying employment and suspended their payments. The plan seeks approval of that restriction; the retirees seek restoration of the payments withheld.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed, and the case is remanded for proceedings restoring benefits withheld under the unlawful added restriction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Seventh Circuit's reversal of judgment on the pleadings for the plan stands. The added restriction may not be applied to the protected accruals; payments withheld solely under that restriction must be restored, with the amounts calculated below.

## Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined. BREYER, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which O'CONNOR and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

## Holdings

### I. Adding a payment restriction reduces the accrued benefit

**Holding and operative rule:** Under ERISA §204(g), a plan amendment reduces an accrued early-retirement benefit when it adds a condition withholding payment for employment the terms governing that accrual permitted. The reduction occurs when the added condition diminishes the entitlement, even though the nominal monthly amount remains unchanged; lawful changes to terms governing future accruals remain possible.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls this rule and the following statutory distinction and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The benefit is the promised entitlement under its governing conditions, not an abstract figure payable only if a later-added condition is satisfied. Lockheed Corp. v. Spink preserves sponsor authority to design plans together with independent statutory limits. Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson protects the defined accrued entitlement while rejecting ownership of all plan surplus; these retirees seek the promised payment itself. They earned the benefit under terms permitting supervisory employment, and the 1998 amendment reduced that right by making continued payment depend on abandoning previously permitted work. The plan's distinction between suspension and reduction fails because the added condition itself diminishes the accrued entitlement; actual suspension demonstrates the injury here. Treating the unchanged nominal figure as conclusive would permit the plan to withhold the protected value while retaining its name. Section 204(g)'s early-retirement protection reaches benefits attributable to service before the amendment.

The corresponding formal rule in 26 CFR §1.411(d)-4 distinguishes objective conditions specified before accrual, under A-6, and changes to benefits not yet accrued, under A-2(a)(1), from added conditions or further restrictions on an already accrued protected benefit, prohibited by A-7. That formal regulation confirms the statutory reading. An inconsistent unreasoned manual statement or informal approval practice cannot displace it.

### II. Permission for original suspension terms does not authorize this cutback

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 203(a)(3)(B)'s permission to suspend specified benefits during covered postretirement employment does not authorize a later amendment reducing an already accrued benefit contrary to §204(g). For the multiemployer provision invoked here, work in the same industry, trade or craft, and geographic area covered by the plan may be made an original suspension condition; attaching an additional restriction after accrual remains subject to the separate anti-cutback command.

**Authority:** The unanimous Court adopts both grounds in the explanation below.

**Controlling explanation:** First, §203 governs vesting and forfeiture, while §204 protects benefit accrual. The absence of prohibited forfeiture does not establish the absence of a prohibited reduction. Second, §203(a)(3)(B) describes conditions that may form part of the benefit package offered before accrual. It does not grant permission to rewrite a previously earned entitlement. An original suspension term is accounted for in the benefit when earned; an added term diminishes what was already earned. Each provision therefore performs its own work. Section 203 remains available for lawful future-accrual terms, while §204 protects accrued payment rights from this added supervisory-employment restriction.

The 1998 amendment violates that protection as applied to the retirees' accrued benefits. The restoration and calculation directed by the judgment follow from the invalid condition. The separate statutory mechanisms for reductions based on substantial business hardship under §302(c)(8), 29 U.S.C. §1082(c)(8), or terminated multiemployer plans under 29 U.S.C. §1441 are outside this claim. Plan tax qualification and the authority to adopt a prospective regulatory exception are outside the participant-benefit question decided here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lockheed Corp. v. Spink is applied to preserve sponsor design power subject to independent statutory limits; sponsor status supplies no answer to the accrued-benefit violation.
- Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson is applied for enforceable accrued payment rights, distinct from ownership of plan surplus; its rejection of the surplus claim remains intact.

## Law After Decision

Section 204(g) protects the conditions of an earned early-retirement payment entitlement as well as its nominal amount. A plan must respect those conditions when amending disqualifying employment; §203's separate suspension permission does not override that protection. The rule governs the challenged accrued benefits and pending benefit claims, while lawful terms for future accruals retain their separate operation.

## Separate Writings

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Ginsburg, joins the Court's opinion on the understanding that its construction of the existing statute and regulation does not foreclose a legally authorized, explicit regulation by the Secretary of Labor or the Secretary of the Treasury permitting an expansion of disqualifying employment for already earned benefits. The concurrence leaves the existence and exercise of that agency power open. No such operative regulation permits this amendment, so the qualification does not alter the present benefit-restoration result.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the anti-cutback holding and restoration judgment. He would require the plan to identify the protected accruals, stop applying the new restriction, calculate the sums withheld solely because of it and resume payment, with the district court setting accounting and payment deadlines and retaining enforcement authority. A remedy confined to selected participants would require the plan to show that it fully cures the same unlawful condition within the affected live claim. He regards any authorized Treasury determination under 26 U.S.C. §7805(b)(8) about retroactive tax consequences as separate from participants' established payment rights. His concurrence addresses implementation of the statutory entitlement rather than replacing it with a fiduciary or surplus-ownership theory.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for calculation and restoration of payments withheld solely because of the 1998 added employment restriction and enforcement of the protected accrued terms. The plan must administer those benefits without the invalid condition.

## Event

City of Littleton v. Z. J. Gifts D-4, L.L.C.; No. 02-1609. Argued March 24, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit. The bookstore opened without applying for the required adult-business license and challenged the ordinance on its face. The Tenth Circuit held the ordinance deficient because it failed to assure a prompt final judicial determination. The questions are whether the scheme must assure a prompt judicial decision and whether ordinary Colorado procedures can supply that assurance without a special fixed decision deadline. The city seeks reversal; the bookstore seeks facial invalidation on judicial timing.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Tenth Circuit's judicial-timing facial ruling is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's decision.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The facial timing claim must be resolved under the prompt-review rules stated below. The ordinance remains operative against this objection; the Justices reach the judgment through the distinct grounds identified in the opinions.

## Holdings

### I. Judicial review must protect against suppression through delay

**Holding and operative rule:** An adult-business licensing scheme governing protected expression must assure a judicial determination promptly enough to prevent delay from suppressing that expression. Prompt access to file a challenge alone does not satisfy this requirement if the challenged restraint can remain effective indefinitely without a decision.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those seven direct joins make it controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas protects against the risk that a licensing process will suppress expression through delay. That risk continues after the applicant reaches the courthouse: an unresolved challenge can leave an erroneous denial in force as effectively as an unreviewable administrative refusal. The city's access-only position therefore does not supply the required safeguard. Thomas v. Chicago Park District distinguishes objective administration from censorship but expressly leaves judicial timing open. The Court now resolves that issue for the adult-business scheme presented. Objective criteria affect how the required protection may be furnished; they do not make the consequence of judicial delay irrelevant. Freedman v. Maryland supplies the stronger safeguards applicable to direct censorship. Recognizing the prompt-determination protection here preserves that distinction because the mechanisms needed to secure it may differ.

### II. Effective ordinary procedures satisfy this facial challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** An objective, nondiscretionary adult-business licensing scheme need not prescribe a special fixed judicial deadline when existing court procedures provide effective expedition and interim protection against suppression through delay. Colorado's identified ordinary procedures satisfy that facial requirement on this record; courts must use their available tools when delay threatens significant First Amendment injury.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those six direct joins make its rationale and application controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The licensing criteria concern objective qualifications, not official approval of expressive content. Such questions ordinarily permit relatively prompt adjudication. Colorado courts possess procedures for expedition and interim relief, and their ordinary administration may be expected to protect the constitutional interest when needed. The government has identified those operative mechanisms; the absence of a bespoke numerical timetable does not establish their inadequacy. The bookstore brought a facial challenge without applying for a license, so this record supplies no delayed licensing appeal showing that the procedures cannot perform that function.

The bookstore's proposed fixed-deadline rule would prescribe one procedural mechanism without establishing that Colorado's existing alternatives are constitutionally ineffective. Thomas supplies the distinction between neutral administration and the complete censorship procedure, while FW/PBS requires effective protection against delay. Their combined application permits the ordinary tools identified here. The disagreement about whether licensing receives a special judicial priority does not alter the reason for the safeguard: a restraint on protected expression can continue through an unresolved erroneous denial, and expedition or interim protection can prevent that harm without a special judicial code. A demonstrated delay is addressed by applying the prompt-decision rule with available expedited and interim remedies. Content censorship remains subject to Freedman's stronger safeguards.

## Precedent Treatment

- FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas is clarified: the core protection against licensing delay extends to judicial determination, while ordinary procedures can supply effective protection in this objective scheme.
- Thomas v. Chicago Park District is extended to the judicial-timing issue it reserved. Its distinction between objective administration and the complete censorship procedure remains effective.
- Freedman v. Maryland is distinguished as direct censorship; its stronger procedural safeguards continue to govern that setting.

## Law After Decision

Objective adult-business licensing must provide effective protection against suppression through judicial delay. A special fixed judicial-decision deadline is unnecessary when ordinary procedures furnish prompt adjudication, expedition and interim protection as required. Colorado's scheme survives the facial timing challenge on that basis. Concrete delay invokes application of this governing protection rather than a new unresolved timing rule.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens joins Part II and the judgment. He distinguishes ministerial adult-business licensing from the direct content censorship addressed in Freedman. In his reading, FW/PBS requires a prompt administrative decision and the possibility of prompt judicial review, rather than imposing on the licensor the full censorship-system duty to ensure a prompt judicial determination. Ordinary Colorado procedures supply an adequate possibility of promptness on this facial record. He withholds Part I because equating the two safeguards risks either burdening ordinary licensing with censorship procedures or weakening Freedman's stronger protection.

Justice Souter, joined by Justice Kennedy, joins Part I's prompt-determination rule and agrees that the facial challenge fails. Although the denial criteria are objective, adult-business licensing is triggered by the expressive materials sold, so its administration can expose unpopular sellers to delay. The mere availability of discretionary judicial tools does not by itself settle practical adequacy. Actual judicial practice must provide prompt disposition, with immediate intervention against foot-dragging and expeditious review at every stage. A rigid censorship timetable is unnecessary, and this record does not establish that the available procedures fail to furnish that protection. This is their sufficient adequacy ground in place of Part II's reliance on ordinary administration and available tools.

Justice Scalia concurs in the judgment. Even assuming the activity is protected, a general permit requirement unrelated to censorship of expressive content does not give the business a special constitutional priority in the courts. Ordinary Colorado procedures therefore suffice against the demand for special timing rules without his joining the affirmative prompt-determination entitlement. His conditional ground resolves this facial challenge without a finding that the bookstore's materials are obscene or that its actual business satisfies a distinct unprotected category.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Tenth Circuit for disposition of the facial timing claim consistently with the Court's ruling. The ordinance remains operative against that claim. A court reviewing a concrete denial must provide expedition or interim protection when delay threatens suppression of protected expression.

No. 02-1609; First and Fourteenth Amendments; FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas, 493 U.S. 215; Freedman v. Maryland, 380 U.S. 51; Thomas v. Chicago Park District, January 15, 2002. This is a facial procedural challenge, with no actual delayed licensing appeal by the applicant in the record.

## Event

Department of Transportation v. Public Citizen, No. 03-358. Argued April 21, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which required the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration to prepare an environmental impact statement and a full Clean Air Act conformity determination addressing increased Mexican truck operations. The Department seeks reversal; respondents seek the broader review before the rules operate. The question is whether the effects of the President's independent decision to lift the cross-border moratorium must be treated as effects of the agency's registration and safety rules.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requirements for an environmental impact statement and a full conformity determination predicated on the independently authorized increase in cross-border traffic are set aside.

## Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of a unanimous Court.

## Holdings

**Authority:** The entire opinion, including each holding and its operative qualifications below, is joined by all nine participating Justices.

### 1. NEPA connects the required analysis to the agency's own action and authority

**Holding and operative rule:** NEPA requires a reasonably close causal relationship between the reviewed agency action and the environmental effects to be studied. Where an independent governmental decision produces the challenged increase and the agency lacks statutory authority to prevent that increase, the agency's necessary implementation step does not by itself make those effects attributable to its rules; effects of the agency's own available choices remain subject to the applicable review requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Metropolitan Edison requires legally relevant causation, beyond a foreseeable sequence of events. Robertson makes NEPA an informed-decision procedure operating within the agency's lawful authority. FMCSA must register carriers satisfying its safety and financial conditions; it cannot deny otherwise qualified carriers admission on environmental grounds. The President controls lifting the moratorium. The additional entry attributed to that choice therefore is not an effect for which these rules require an environmental impact statement.

Respondents stress that the rules enable traffic and that environmental information serves the public as well as the agency. Both points preserve the value of NEPA procedure but do not transfer the President's decision to FMCSA. The relevant statutory allocation must substantiate the agency's asserted lack of control. Here it does. The assessment addressed the agency's rules, and the disputed omission concerns the independent increase in entry. The Court's rule continues to require assessment of environmental consequences caused by discretionary safety choices within the agency's authority.

### 2. Cumulative-impact analysis and preservation retain their distinct requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 40 CFR §1508.7, the agency must consider its action's incremental impact together with past, present and reasonably foreseeable future actions, regardless of actor; that requirement does not turn another actor's independent action into an effect of the agency rule. A party seeking review based on an omitted regulatory alternative must meaningfully present that alternative while the agency can address it, unless an obvious defect supplies the requisite notice without a specific comment.

**Controlling explanation:** The cumulative-impact provision expressly includes actions of other actors, but its starting point remains the incremental impact of the action being reviewed. The President's entry decision supplies context without becoming an increment caused by FMCSA's rule. Vermont Yankee separately requires meaningful participation sufficient to identify an alternatives objection. Respondents identified no additional regulatory alternative in their comments, and the asserted omission presents no supported obvious alternative the agency should have examined despite that silence. This forfeiture concerns those unpresented alternatives. It does not dispose of the independently preserved causation and conformity objections. An alternative premised on an environmental entry veto also lies outside FMCSA's statutory choices.

### 3. The conformity regulations separately require direct emissions or practicable continuing control

**Holding and operative rule:** For the conformity inquiry under 40 CFR §93.152, direct emissions must be caused or initiated by the federal action and occur at the same time and place. Indirect emissions may occur later or farther away, but must be caused by the action, reasonably foreseeable, and emissions the agency can practicably control and will maintain control over because of its continuing program responsibility; the independent traffic increase satisfies neither the direct nor the indirect definition here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Clean Air Act conformity regulation uses its own definition. Its caused-by requirement permits a but-for connection, so the Court does not simply transplant NEPA's close-causation rule. Even assuming that connection, truck emissions do not occur at the same time and place as the promulgation of these rules. For indirect emissions, reasonable foreseeability alone is insufficient: practicable control and continuing program responsibility are additional conditions. FMCSA cannot prevent the President's admission decision and does not possess continuing environmental entry-control authority. Its safety responsibilities do not establish control over the independently determined increase. The demanded full conformity determination therefore lacks the required regulatory predicate.

## Precedent Treatment

- Metropolitan Edison Co. v. People Against Nuclear Energy supplies NEPA's reasonably close causal relationship and defeats attribution based only on a necessary implementation sequence.
- Robertson v. Methow Valley Citizens Council supplies the procedural duty to make an informed choice within the agency's actual authority; it supplies no additional power to prevent the President's entry decision.
- Vermont Yankee Nuclear Power Corp. v. Natural Resources Defense Council supplies meaningful presentation of an alternatives objection, applied here subject to the supported-obvious-defect qualification.

## Law After Decision

NEPA review follows the legally relevant effects of the agency's action under its governing authority, with cumulative analysis of that action's incremental impact. The conformity inquiry separately applies the regulation's same-time-and-place or foreseeable-emissions-plus-continuing-control conditions. These rules remove the challenged review requirements for the President's independent entry increase while preserving review of effects attributable to the agency's actual discretionary choices.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion accompanies the unanimous decision.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit to implement reversal and remove the environmental-impact-statement and conformity requirements based on the independent traffic increase.

Department of Transportation, 316 F.3d 1002; NEPA §102(2)(C), 42 U.S.C. §4332; Clean Air Act §176(c), 42 U.S.C. §7506(c); 40 CFR §§1508.7, 1508.8 and 93.152 in their event-effective versions; the carrier-registration and safety provisions in 49 U.S.C. §§13902 and 31136. Metropolitan Edison Co. v. People Against Nuclear Energy, 460 U.S. 766; Robertson v. Methow Valley Citizens Council, 490 U.S. 332; Vermont Yankee Nuclear Power Corp. v. ## Event

Republic of Austria v. Altmann; No. 03-13. Argued February 25, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed denial of the immunity motion by Austria and the Austrian Gallery. Review is limited to whether the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act governs a suit filed after its enactment when the alleged conduct predates both the 1976 Act and the restrictive-immunity policy announced in 1952. ## Holdings

### The current FSIA governs this immunity determination

**Holding and operative rule:** The FSIA governs foreign-state immunity in a suit filed after its enactment even when the alleged conduct predates both 1976 and the 1952 restrictive-immunity policy. Its present immunity framework leaves substantive liability to independent law, and the claimant must establish an applicable statutory exception under the ordinary immunity procedure; the sovereign retains its independent merits defenses.

**Authority:** Stevens's complete opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1330, 1602 and 1604 establish an integrated framework for the immunity claim a court now must decide. Section 1602's direction that courts henceforth use that framework concerns present adjudication. Neither that word alone nor the statute's jurisdictional label conclusively resolves temporal reach. Landgraf v. USI Film Products requires attention to the provision's actual legal consequences, and Rivers v. Roadway Express preserves that inquiry despite an asserted statutory purpose. Foreign-state immunity protects against the present inconvenience of suit in American courts in a field historically governed by changing political-branch policy; the former practice supplied no guarantee that the forum policy existing at the time of conduct would remain fixed. The FSIA's text, function and comprehensive structure therefore govern the immunity assertion now before the Court.

Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer protects against a newly enlarged private-enforcement route that removes a cause-attached defense. That is the dissent's strongest analogy, but this Act instead allocates current American adjudicative immunity while leaving the substantive property claim to its separate source. Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson supports attention to filing-time status and present forum protection without itself deciding this temporal question. Verlinden B.V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria confirms the integrated immunity-and-jurisdiction design.

The proposed exception-by-exception historical schedule lacks an enacted basis and would divide the current scheme by each exception's historical origin. The statute supplies no such general division. Applying it permits Altmann's suit to proceed on the existing posture. The holding rests on this foreign-immunity regime, not a general rule that every jurisdictional or immunity change governs earlier conduct.

The particular expropriation-exception determination remains outside the limited grant. Title, the act-of-state defense, limitations, and attachment or execution remain separate legal questions outside the temporal issue; ownership and wrongful taking await their required adjudication.

## Precedent Treatment

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products is applied to the FSIA's actual temporal function and legal consequences; jurisdictional terminology alone remains insufficient.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express is applied for the continuing need for sufficient temporal analysis rather than reliance on a general statutory purpose.
- Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer is distinguished because its amendment enlarged private enforcement by removing a complete cause-attached defense; its rule remains effective in that setting.
- Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson supplies a consistent filing-time focus for statutory forum status, without independently resolving this conduct-date question.
- Verlinden B.V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria is applied for the FSIA's integrated immunity-and-jurisdiction structure.

## Law After Decision

Post-enactment suits receive immunity determinations under the FSIA even when the alleged conduct occurred before 1976 or 1952. Each statutory exception retains its own requirements, and temporal applicability does not establish substantive property liability. Independent defenses and remedies continue to be governed by their own law.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia joins the Court's opinion and adds that the relevant activity regulated by the FSIA is the present invocation of judicial power. The Act governs access to the United States forum rather than creating or modifying the underlying substantive right. Hughes Aircraft concerned a restriction attached to a cause of action wherever it could be pursued, so its jurisdictional language did not make it merely a forum rule. A claimant's possible inability to find an effective foreign forum does not transform the FSIA's intended operation into creation of a substantive claim. This applies his relevant-activity approach while accepting the Court's common statutory result.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Souter, joins the Court's opinion and emphasizes present status, the statute's absence of conduct-date cutoffs and the historically changing practice of sovereign immunity. Section 1602's use of henceforth alone is insufficient; those additional considerations explain why the asserted reliance on a permanent earlier immunity regime is weak. An exception-by-exception historical schedule would create instability inconsistent with a comprehensive current framework. Remaining substantive defenses and distinct remedies explain why amenability to suit is not a judgment of title. Their additional analysis supports the present statutory rule without deciding those independent questions.

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justice Thomas, dissents. Landgraf and Hughes Aircraft protect against attaching new legal disabilities to completed conduct, including loss of an immunity defense that formerly prevented American litigation. In their view, the FSIA's direction to future courts does not clearly make every new exception applicable to earlier conduct, and its forum character does not eliminate the resulting legal exposure. They would vacate and remand to determine whether the claim would have been immune under the pre-FSIA regime, including the restrictive theory announced in the Tate Letter and any applicable executive position. If the earlier regime already allowed jurisdiction, present application would create no new exposure on their objection. The lower courts did not perform that inquiry, so the dissent seeks its completion rather than automatic dismissal or a conclusive classification of the alleged acquisition.

## Event

Belitskus v. Pizzingrilli; judgment below, 343 F.3d 632 (3d Cir. 2003); lower-court Nos. 01-3747 and 01-3824. Merits decision, June 10, 2004, after full merits briefing. ## Holdings

### I. Recurring candidate and party injuries remain justiciable

**Holding and operative rule:** Completion of an election does not moot a challenge to a ballot-access condition when the injury is sufficiently likely to recur against the claimant and the election cycle is too short for full review. The established recurring injuries support adjudication for Stith, Linzey and the Pennsylvania Green Party; Donovan's individual claim must be dismissed because the required likelihood of recurrence to him is absent.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, controls the distinct threshold dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Candidate-specific exclusion and the party's continuing nomination interests supply concrete injuries, rather than a generalized objection to the fee policy. The record supports recurrence for the two candidates and the continuing party. Schrader v. Blackwell applies the capable-of-repetition exception to continuing party injury in elections too short for complete litigation, and Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party recognizes the continuing nomination interest. Donovan's completed-election injury lacks the corresponding likelihood of personal recurrence. The live party claim therefore preserves adjudication of its own injury without preserving every voter's past claim.

### II. Actual inability to pay requires a usable alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not make payment of a filing fee the exclusive ballot-access route for an otherwise qualified candidate who cannot pay without financial hardship. Actual exclusion on that basis imposes a severe burden under Anderson–Burdick and Cartwright and requires narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; a realistically usable nonmonetary route or hardship mechanism must preserve access for the financially excluded candidate.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by all nine Justices and controls the constitutional rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Bullock v. Carter rejects wealth-based exclusion unsupported by the proposed screening rationale, and Lubin v. Panish requires another route for candidates unable to pay. Anderson v. Celebrezze and Burdick v. Takushi direct attention to the actual burden and the State's precise interests; Cartwright v. Barnes states the calibrated framework within which outright financial exclusion is severe. Ability to pay does not establish electoral support, and administrative expense does not justify excluding a qualified candidate where a meaningful support-based or hardship route serves the legitimate objective. The hardship affidavits and resulting preliminary access support the claims of Stith and Linzey, and the mandatory policy affects the party's established recurring interests.

Reasonable evidence of financial hardship may be required. Actual inability to pay differs from inconvenience or a preference to avoid the fee. A proposed alternative must be usable in its real deadlines and requirements. Reasonable fees for candidates able to pay and independent lawful qualification or support requirements remain available. Those qualifications answer the concern that the inability-to-pay rule prevents ordinary screening; they do not allow the screening rationale to substitute wealth for support.

### III. The injunction must cure the proved candidate and party injuries

**Holding and operative rule:** In this as-applied action, the injunction must specify concrete hardship conditions and official duties sufficient to remedy the established injuries of the candidates and the continuing party. A categorical cap at Stith and Linzey cannot stand without securing the party's proved recurring injury; the district court must fit the order's scope to that remedial need and allow a meaningful lawful alternative selected by the State.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls both the five-vote vacatur and the remedial rule by direct majority support.

**Controlling explanation:** The injunction's enforceable reach differs from the constitutional rule's precedential reach. Rule 65(d) requires an order stating the actual protected conditions and the acts officials must perform, so the district court's general command requires precise replacement. But limiting relief to two names may leave the successful party subject to the same established nomination injury. The contrary remedial position treats those two candidacies as adequate protection on the current record. The party's independently established recurring claim makes that categorical restriction insufficient without an injury-specific inquiry into the effective cure.

The district court must determine the concrete reach needed for those successful plaintiffs and state a workable hardship or alternative-access mechanism in definite terms. Relief extending to other candidates requires a demonstrated connection to curing the proved candidate and party injuries. The State retains the choice among meaningful lawful alternatives. This remand performs factual and remedial work under the settled inability-to-pay rule and Rule 65(d), with the resulting injunction defined by the proven operation of the policy and the cure that operation requires.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bullock v. Carter and Lubin v. Panish are reaffirmed at their actual inability-to-pay scope: electoral access cannot be conditioned exclusively on an unaffordable fee without a realistic alternative.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, Burdick v. Takushi and Cartwright v. Barnes are applied to the actual burden, justification and fit; the exclusion here falls within their severe-burden rule.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party is applied to the party's concrete nomination interest and the need for effective protection of it.
- Schrader v. Blackwell is applied to the continuing party's recurring election injury and distinguished from Donovan's unsupported recurrence claim.

## Law After Decision

The inability-to-pay protection governs otherwise qualified candidates actually excluded by an unaffordable mandatory fee, together with the associated party and voter interests. Reasonable proof and lawful qualifications remain enforceable, but the alternative must work in practice. A remedy in an as-applied action must be concrete and fitted to the successful plaintiffs' actual injuries, including an established recurring party injury; its enforceable reach requires that remedial connection.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the threshold and inability-to-pay holdings and agrees that the two-name cap must be set aside, but would reverse it and require a policy remedy for all otherwise qualified candidates who establish inability to pay without material financial hardship. He would place on officials the burden to show that a narrower remedy fully cures the proved mandatory policy.

Under his proposed proof rule, a claimant establishes inability by a preponderance through a sworn account of available income, reasonably available assets, necessary living expenses and dependent obligations. Material hardship means sacrificing ordinary necessities or assuming an unreasonable financial burden, rather than merely preferring not to pay. Poverty-line status would be neither necessary nor conclusive. Officials could verify a material factual dispute but could not demand unavailable records or postpone a decision beyond the relevant ballot deadline. Bullock and Lubin supply the inability-to-pay protection, while Cartwright's calibrated review governs the resulting burden and justification; the preliminary hardship procedure shows that an administrable mechanism is possible without dispensing with evidence for a disputed claim.

He would require a published hardship application and objective decision criteria before the next relevant filing deadline, identification of the deciding official, written reasons for denial and prompt review early enough for a successful candidate to reach the ballot. A petition alternative would count only if its deadlines and support requirements were realistically usable. The district court would set dates tied to the election calendar and provide provisional filing when official delay would otherwise defeat relief. Officials would accept qualifying filings and maintain reasons sufficient for review, while the court retained enforcement authority. Independent lawful qualifications would remain controlling, and candidates able to pay would receive no automatic exemption. He would preserve the claimant-specific dispositions and any independently available fee award. These are his proposed additional proof and remedial rules; the operative vacatur and instructions are those in the Court's Part III.

## Holdings

##### A parental claim intertwined with the opposed authority assigned by the custody order

**Holding and operative rule:** A parent who has abandoned representation of a child may not obtain adjudication of a claimed personal entitlement to control the child's disputed school religious experience when that entitlement is intertwined with parental authority the operative custody order assigns finally to the other, opposing parent. In this setting prudential standing requires dismissal while preserving the parent's distinct right to teach his own views; a joint-custody label or family disagreement alone does not establish that bar.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Newdow's asserted entitlement must be evaluated against the actual parental interests involved. Warth requires a litigant to assert his own legal interests. Raines preserves the distinction between personal injury and unauthorized assertion of another's interests. The order requires consultation but gives Banning final authority over substantial health, education and welfare decisions if the parents disagree. Newdow remains free to instruct his daughter in his beliefs; he has abandoned the separate claim on her behalf. The requested judicial control of this school experience implicates the authority allocated to Banning and opposed by her.

The contrary position correctly observes that government interference with a parent's own religious instruction can be personal and that federal relief need not alter custody. That possibility does not establish an unrestricted personal entitlement to control this disputed experience. The actual allocation and the conflicting exercise of parental authority make adjudication of this claim inappropriate. Ankenbrandt confines the domestic-relations jurisdictional exception; the Court's prudential conclusion neither enlarges that exception to federal constitutional claims nor holds that every joint-custody parent lacks Article III injury.

The personal claim must be dismissed on this antecedent ground. The Establishment Clause question remains outside the merits reached in this action because the threshold ground prevents its adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:** Warth's own-interest limitation is applied; Raines's personal-interest and representation distinction is preserved; Ankenbrandt's limited domestic-relations rule remains intact rather than becoming a categorical federal-question bar.

## Precedent Treatment

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490: applied to require the claimant's own legal interest in the particular relief sought.
- Raines v. Byrd: applied for the distinction between a personal legal stake and unauthorized assertion of another's interest; its legislative setting is not treated as a parental-standing rule.
- Ankenbrandt v. Richards, 504 U.S. 689: preserved; its limited exception for specified domestic decrees supplies no general exclusion of federal constitutional questions.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555: its ordinary personal-injury requirements remain; the Court's disposition rests on the confined prudential ground.

## Law After Decision

The operative allocation and opposed exercise of parental authority require prudential dismissal of the retained claim in this setting. Courts must examine that allocation and the personal interest asserted, rather than treat parenthood, joint custody or family disagreement as dispositive labels. Sherman's established civic-Pledge permission and the governing establishment precedents retain their force; this threshold judgment changes no Establishment Clause rule.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** The retained parent-child instructional relationship supplies an adequately alleged personal injury, and Ankenbrandt permits the federal claim without changing custody. On the merits, Sherman directly permits the materially equivalent civic Pledge where pupils may decline words and gestures, remain in place and suffer no penalty. Agostini preserves that established permissible relationship within its unified inquiry. The practice is governmental speech, but its religious acknowledgment need not become meaningless to believers. Protected abstention matters in this civic exercise; it does not validate official prayer generally. Lee and Santa Fe remain controlling for school-organized worship and pressure. These Justices would restore dismissal on the merits under the concrete civic rule.

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** Newdow's retained instructional relationship can sustain a personal claim even though he cannot represent his daughter or override the final custody allocation. The recurring official exercise directly affects that relationship; this is not merely disagreement with the mother's beliefs or ordinary curriculum. Ankenbrandt rejects a categorical family-law barrier, and Lujan requires the retained interest, concrete interference and redressability.

The Chief would overrule Sherman only insofar as it permits teacher-led elementary-school affirmation of the religious proposition within the Pledge. Sherman supplies the contrary permission and Agostini did not erase it. Lee and Santa Fe's treatment of official school sponsorship and religious pressure supports reconsidering that permission openly: teachers organize affirmation of a religious proposition as part of the school's own civic program. Longstanding use and administrative reliance deserve weight but do not justify the profession. Protected abstention under Sherman and Barnette remains, while a claimant-only opt-out leaves the official practice in place.

He would affirm the constitutional rejection and require a declaration and prospective order removing the religious affirmation from the organized exercise while preserving a secular patriotic program, historical instruction and private speech. Administrators would communicate compliant instructions before the next exercise governed by the order. Officials would bear the burden of showing a narrower effective cure. Any demonstrated material custody ambiguity would receive prompt, limited clarification without postponing relief on an otherwise established personal interest. Other governmental ceremonies present a different question.

## Holdings

##### I. The qualifying effect must give rise to the claimant's own claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 6a excludes nonimport foreign commerce, including wholly foreign transactions, from the Sherman Act unless the conduct has a direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable effect on domestic or import commerce, or on the foreign export commerce of a person engaged in that commerce in the United States, and that effect gives rise to the plaintiff's asserted antitrust claim. A foreign injury independent of the domestic effect does not qualify merely because that effect gives another person a claim; import trade remains outside the general exclusion, and coverage restored only through the export branch permits recovery only for injury to export business in the United States.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Stone-Zsela: six direct joins. Part II's additional explanation has the five-Justice coalition of Breyer, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas support the operative construction in their separate judgment concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exception requires a connection to the claim being litigated. The purchasers' reading of the indefinite reference to a claim is grammatically possible, but the text also permits the claim-specific construction. The statute first removes nonimport foreign commerce and then restores coverage for the specified effects giving rise to a claim. A domestic injury suffered by someone else does not supply the connection between the qualifying effect and these purchasers' independent foreign losses. The exception protects the claims arising from that effect.

The territorial interpretive principle preserved by Charming Betsy favors avoiding unreasonable interference with foreign sovereigns' regulation of their own commercial affairs. Applying American damages law to foreign injury independent of domestic harm would create that interference without the required claim connection. This is a construction of the statute, not an empirical demand for incompatible foreign commands or a country-by-country balancing defense. The enactment's clarification of geographic reach, including its change from export-only wording to nonimport foreign commerce, supports this reading and confirms that wholly foreign transactions fall within the exclusion. The legislative context reinforces rather than contradicts the enacted language.

Hartford Fire involved American-market effects giving rise to the claims before the Court and expressly preserved §6a's operation. Its rejection of the asserted foreign-permission defense did not erase the interpretive canon. The purchasers' deterrence argument and government enforcement cases involving broad remedial decrees do not establish a private damages claim for independent foreign injury. Claims of foreign injury dependent on domestic harm require their own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:** Hartford Fire's domestic-effects holding is distinguished and retained; Charming Betsy's territorial interpretive principle is applied.

##### II. The assumed independent injury is excluded; the alternative requires its own procedural and statutory inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** On the assumed independence of these purchasers' foreign injuries, §6a's effects exception is unavailable. The judgment is vacated, and the court of appeals must determine preservation of the alternative claim that domestic price effects caused the foreign loss and, if preserved, assess its legal sufficiency under the statute and direct appropriate further proceedings.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Stone-Zsela, has six direct joins; Scalia and Thomas independently support the same mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The question decided rests on independence, so the asserted global cartel cannot itself establish the missing causal connection. The purchasers' alternative theory says domestic price maintenance was necessary to sustain foreign prices. That contention was not decided below; its preservation and sufficiency are therefore left for the appellate court, which must apply the claim-specific requirement rather than assume dependence from worldwide pricing. Its legal sufficiency is the genuinely unaddressed question; applying a settled rule to supported facts remains ordinary remand work.

**Precedent treatment:** Hartford Fire's retained claim-connection requirement supplies the applicable boundary; its different domestic-injury result does not decide this unaddressed alternative.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hartford Fire Insurance v. California, 509 U.S. 764: distinguished because its substantial American-market effects gave rise to the claims it sustained; its reserved §6a question is now resolved for independent foreign injury, and its retained territorial interpretive canon remains effective.
- Murray v. Schooner Charming Betsy, 2 Cranch 64: applied as an interpretive principle favoring a permissible construction consistent with customary territorial restraint. The principle operates in construing §6a, not as an automatic defense based on foreign permission.

## Law After Decision

The effects exception restores coverage only when the qualifying statutory effect gives rise to the actual claim for relief. Independent foreign injury falls outside that exception even when the same conspiracy harms American commerce. Import commerce retains its distinct statutory treatment, and an export-only basis retains the limitation to injury to export business in the United States. If preserved, the dependent-injury theory must be evaluated under those conditions; a worldwide cartel allegation alone does not establish them.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The text and statutory structure require the domestic effect to give rise to the plaintiff's own asserted claim. Hartford Fire preserves its domestic-injury holding but does not decide independent foreign injury. International enforcement interests cannot erase the statutory connection. The Chief joins Parts I and III and would require identification of the challenged transactions and causal theories, determination of preservation, and application of the domestic-effect and causation requirements under ordinary pleading and proof rules. Unsupported independent-injury theories must be dismissed; a properly preserved dependent theory receives its own analysis, without assuming dependence from global pricing.

**Scalia, concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** The text readily permits the claim-specific construction, and customary deference to other nations' regulation within their territories makes that construction sufficient to decide this case. These Justices accept the operative coverage rule, the independent-injury result and the defined remand. They withhold assent from the Court's legislative-history and broader explanatory route. Their territorial canon is a rule of statutory interpretation, not discretionary comity dependent on proof of incompatible foreign commands.

## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must give effect to vacatur, determine whether the alternative domestic-effects theory was preserved, and, if it was, consider statutory sufficiency and the further proceedings needed. The claim-specific rule governs that work. Claims based solely on the assumed independent foreign harm cannot proceed under the exception.

542 U.S. 155; No. 03-724; Empagran S.A. v. F. Hoffmann-La Roche, Ltd., 315 F.3d 338; 15 U.S.C. §6a(1)(A)–(B), (2), and final sentence; Sherman Act; Clayton Act §4. Independence of the foreign injury is the assumption underlying the reviewed question; causal dependence is an alternative contention whose preservation and sufficiency remain for consideration.

## Holdings

##### I. Genuine court-initiated reconsideration suspends finality

**Holding and operative rule:** When an appellate court begins genuine reconsideration capable of changing the parties' adjudicated rights before the original §2101(c) period expires, the ninety-day period for Supreme Court review runs from disposition of that reconsideration. A mandate recall alone or a nominal order merely extending this Court's filing time is insufficient; this petition is timely because substantive rehearing briefing began within the original period and the petition followed within ninety days after rehearing ended.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Ginsburg's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judgment's real finality determines the start of the enacted period. Young v. Harper treated genuinely accepted reconsideration as material to finality, although that case involved a late party request rather than the court's own action. The same principle applies where the appellate court itself opens a substantive rehearing question before the existing review period ends. The Ninth Circuit recalled the mandate and ordered briefing on possible en banc rehearing, leaving a real possibility of a changed judgment. Procedural initiation by the court rather than a party does not make that possibility final. The rule enforces the statutory period from an actual final judgment; it does not grant an equitable enlargement or treat an unentertained suggestion as reconsideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Young's accepted-rehearing finality principle is extended to this timely court-initiated process.

##### II. The requested relief does not restrain protected assessment, levy or collection

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1341 bars federal restraint of a State's assessment, levy or collection of taxes when a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists; it does not bar this third-party challenge seeking disallowance of credits, the related declaration and transfer of remaining organizational funds, because those remedies do not restrain the protected imposition or collection of tax liabilities. The rule turns on the actual operation of relief, not a categorical exemption for third parties, constitutional claims, credit challenges or suits increasing revenue.

**Authority:** Part II has six direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Assessment must be read with levy and collection. Together they protect the State's imposition and obtaining of taxes from federal restraint; assessment does not encompass every operation of state tax policy. These plaintiffs seek no reduction in their own liabilities or avoidance of taxes they owe. Disallowing the challenged credits would permit collection of the full tax. The declaration supports that same relief, and transferring covered organizations' remaining funds to the general fund neither refunds the plaintiffs' taxes nor obstructs their collection.

The dissent gives assessment an independent reach encompassing a decree altering computation of liability. That construction separates the word from the collection-related setting Congress specified and would turn every tax-administration effect into the prohibited restraint. The present construction still bars relief by any plaintiff that actually restrains protected assessment or collection when the adequate-state-remedy condition holds. Grace Brethren prevents declaratory evasion where the underlying tax restraint is barred; its rule is distinguished by the operation of the relief, not by a First Amendment exception. Farm Credit involved a genuine restraint of taxation and refused to create a forum exception from substantive tax status. It does not expand the Act to this different operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Grace Brethren's declaration rule is preserved and distinguished; Farm Credit's actual tax-restraint holding remains, with substantive tax entitlement and forum availability distinct.

##### III. General interference with tax administration does not independently require dismissal here

**Holding and operative rule:** The same challenge is not barred on comity grounds merely because it affects state tax administration. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes concern efforts to arrest or countermand tax collection and do not require dismissal of this claim simply because the challenged subsidy is located in the tax code.

**Authority:** Part III has the five direct joins of Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Comity respects the State's collection process, but the argument presented would convert every objection to a tax-code subsidy into interference requiring dismissal. The requested relief here has the different operation explained above. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes do not establish that generalized bar. The ordinary Article III inquiry and ultimate constitutional adjudication remain for the lower courts; this conclusion decides the asserted generalized tax-administration ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Fair Assessment and Great Lakes are distinguished at their tax-collection scope.

## Precedent Treatment

- Young v. Harper: extended from accepted party rehearing to genuine court-initiated reconsideration begun within the original review period.
- California v. Grace Brethren Church, 457 U.S. 393: preserved; declaratory relief cannot evade an applicable restriction on tax collection, and a constitutional claim creates no automatic exemption.
- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas: distinguished because the requested relief there restrained taxation; a substantive tax privilege does not itself create access to a federal forum.
- Fair Assessment in Real Estate Ass'n v. McNary, 454 U.S. 100, and Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. v. Huffman, 319 U.S. 293: distinguished as collection-interference cases, rather than a freestanding bar to this subsidy challenge.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: its personal-standing requirements remain applicable in the restored proceedings.

## Law After Decision

Genuine timely reconsideration initiated by an appellate court makes its eventual disposition the starting point for §2101(c)'s ninety days. The tax-forum restriction requires examination of the actual restraint sought; this credit challenge may proceed because its relief does not obstruct the protected tax process. The generalized comity objection likewise fails. The lower courts must adjudicate personal standing, genuinely distinct preserved barriers, the actual financing and alleged religious use, and ultimate remedial entitlement in their proper order.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the entire opinion and adds that settled judicial treatment of this category of challenge and Congress's failure to displace it support stability in statutory interpretation. His approach in Runyon and Fink counsels restraint before upsetting established statutory expectations. That history reinforces the contextual reading; it neither proves that every earlier case decided the present question nor permits disregard of unmistakable enacted language.

## Holdings

##### I. Section 706(1) requires a discrete act that governing law makes obligatory

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 706(1) authorizes a court to compel a discrete agency act that governing law requires and the agency has unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed. The claimant must identify the act and establish that legal obligation and withholding or delay; a court may compel a required decision while leaving its permissible substance to the agency, but may not undertake continuing supervision of an administrative program.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Parts I–IV and the live-claim mandate of Thomas's opinion. The same coalition supplies authority for the live-claim holdings below.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 551(13) identifies agency action through circumscribed forms—rules, orders, licenses, sanctions, relief and their equivalents, denials or failures. Section 706(1) reaches an unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed act of that kind, not every asserted shortcoming in administration. Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation rejects wholesale improvement of a program through judicial review. Lincoln distinguishes legal constraints from discretionary management, and Bennett requires the identified legal duty and appropriate review channel. Together those sources support the rule stated here, rather than a commission to improve administration.

Discretion over a required decision's content does not erase the duty to decide. Conversely, leaving nominal choice of means to the agency does not make a decree lawful if a court must continually decide whether an entire program is satisfactory. A specific statutory deadline is not indispensable to every unreasonable-delay claim. The decisive questions remain what act governing law requires and whether that act has unlawfully been withheld or delayed.

**Precedent treatment:** National Wildlife Federation's action-specific review boundary is applied; Lincoln and Bennett's source-and-duty distinctions are developed into the stated §706(1) rule.

##### II. The wilderness standard does not specify the act demanded by this program-wide claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1782(c)'s binding obligation to preserve wilderness suitability does not identify a discrete omitted act that can sustain the organizations' demand for judicial supervision of BLM's wilderness-management program. That §706(1) demand must be dismissed, while the statutory management standard retains its force.

The standard governs during review and until Congress determines otherwise. It permits continued mining, grazing and mineral leasing in the manner and degree existing on October 21, 1976, while separately requiring action against unnecessary or undue degradation or to afford environmental protection. Unless previously withdrawn, the lands remain open to appropriation under mining law during review, subject to withdrawal under §1714 for reasons other than preservation of wilderness character. Following congressional wilderness designation, the Wilderness Act's national-forest wilderness rules govern, including mineral surveys, development, access, land exchange and claimant ingress and egress.

**Controlling explanation:** A legal standard can constrain agency conduct without identifying the particular act a court may compel. The nonimpairment provision sets the required management objective with its stated qualifications; it leaves BLM choices about the measures needed. The organizations' proposed decree would require continuing judicial evaluation of management across the program. Describing the result as mandatory while leaving the means nominally open does not cure that defect. Final agency action violating a binding boundary remains subject to the appropriate review channel. Section 1732(a)'s requirement of plan-conforming multiple-use and sustained-yield management, with separately dedicated tracts governed by their dedicating law, and §1732(b)'s unnecessary-or-undue-degradation command retain their distinct operation.

**Precedent treatment:** National Wildlife Federation's prohibition on wholesale program review controls the demanded form of relief; Lincoln preserves the distinction between an enforceable standard and judicial selection of management measures.

##### III. These planning projections do not create the compulsory continuing program alleged

**Holding and operative rule:** Plan conformity prevents agency action inconsistent with an operative land-use plan until amendment, but a plan's prediction of future activity does not by itself create an affirmative duty enforceable under §706(1). A plan can reiterate an independently binding duty or clearly commit the agency to a particular obligatory act; the source, language, function and context must establish that commitment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Factory Butte provision contemplates monitoring and closure if warranted. Related Henry Mountains provisions contemplate documenting damage, recommending correction, identifying damage that may warrant designation changes and giving priority to supervision in limited and closed areas. In context these are planning directions, not a clear legal commitment to the intensive continuing operational program demanded. The general conformity command does not convert every projected activity into an enforceable schedule. This live claim therefore fails for lack of the asserted binding affirmative command.

Reported informal monitoring and commencement of a program do not make the continuing claim moot; they distinguish it from the completed requests to create files and finish route designation. Plan conformity remains a constraint on contrary action, reviewable through the proper §706(2) channel.

**Precedent treatment:** Bennett's identified-duty requirement and Lincoln's separation of law from administration are applied to the actual plan terms.

##### IV. Supplementation requires relevant major action remaining to occur

**Holding and operative rule:** When relevant major federal action remains, 40 C.F.R. §1502.9(c)(1) requires an EIS supplement if either substantial changes to the proposed action are relevant to environmental concerns or significant new circumstances or information relevant to those concerns bear on the action or its impacts. Section 1502.9(c)(2) separately permits supplementation when the agency determines it would further NEPA's purposes; that permission alone imposes no mandatory duty.

**Controlling explanation:** The approval of these management plans is complete. Continued management does not indefinitely keep the approval itself proposed, and the asserted increase in off-road-vehicle use identifies no distinct remaining major federal action to which this demanded supplement attaches. The supplementation claim therefore fails. Marsh concerns significant new information while relevant major action remains; finishing an EIS for an unfinished project does not finish that project. Later plan amendments and site-specific authorizations remain subject to their own NEPA requirements. Public Citizen reinforces identification of the actual action and legally available choices. Here the missing predicate is remaining relevant major action, not an absence of BLM's environmental authority.

**Precedent treatment:** Marsh's remaining-action rule is applied; Public Citizen's action-specific approach is preserved at its actual scope.

##### V. Completion ends the two requests directed solely to initial acts

**Holding and operative rule:** Requests solely to compel completion of the San Rafael route-designation plan and initial creation of the Henry Mountains use-supervision files are moot because those acts have been completed. They must be dismissed on that ground.

**Authority:** Part V's mootness rationale has the eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela concurs in those two component dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** The particular acts sought through these initial-completion requests now exist. A judicial command to perform them would supply no remaining relief under those requests. The continuing monitoring-program demand is a different live claim and receives the legal-force disposition stated above. Completion of an initial act does not establish fulfillment of every distinct continuing duty.

- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871: applied against wholesale program supervision, while permitting review directed to an identifiable agency act.

- Bennett v. Spear: applied for an identified binding duty and the proper review channel.

Courts may compel discrete legally obligatory agency acts unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed, leaving lawful substantive choices to the agency. A broad management standard does not itself identify every act that may be compelled. Plans constrain inconsistent action, while affirmative enforcement requires an independently binding duty or clear obligatory commitment. The Henry Mountains projections fail that requirement for the program claimed. ## Event

Pennsylvania State Police v. Suders, No. 03-95; 542 U.S. 129. Merits decision, June 14, 2004. Argued March 31, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, which reversed employer summary judgment. Suders alleges sustained sex-based supervisory harassment, inadequate assistance in obtaining a complaint route, an examination-paper episode, a supervisory arrest and resignation during that episode. ## Holdings

##### I. Constructive discharge and employer attribution require distinct proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title VII hostile-environment constructive-discharge claimant must prove unlawful discrimination because of sex, conditions so objectively intolerable that a reasonable employee would feel compelled to resign, and a causal connection between those conditions and the resignation. On the actual-authority route, the plaintiff must separately prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of the actionable environment; title, rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, including these requirements and the plaintiff-favorable application.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires an objectively and subjectively abusive environment that is severe or pervasive, and Oncale requires discrimination because of sex. Duncan establishes that a supported hostile environment does not itself establish the greater objective compulsion to resign. Realistic corrective opportunities matter to that inquiry without imposing exhaustion: an employee need not endure danger, futile procedures or indefinite delay, and announcement of an investigation alone supplies no safe harbor.

Faragher and Ellerth require a real connection between delegated power and the violation. A completed personnel action, economic injury, higher-management notice or an express discharge threat is not invariably necessary. Apparent authority depends on employer manifestations and reasonable reliance; negligence has its separate requirements. Neither route substitutes for proof of actual delegated aid by assertion.

Suders' account describes a sustained supervisory course, difficulty obtaining a practical complaint route, the examination-paper episode and resignation during a supervisory arrest. Viewed favorably to her, that material presents genuine questions about hostility, objective compulsion, practical protection, delegated aid and causation. Resignation alone proves neither intolerability nor attribution; police rank alone likewise does not establish every necessary authority connection. The categorical grounds urged for employer summary judgment cannot foreclose those inquiries.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris and Oncale supply the underlying discrimination standards; Duncan's objective-compulsion and practical-opportunity rules are applied; Faragher and Ellerth's actual-authority rule remains controlling.

##### II. Preventive care and nonuse do not erase proved authority-aided discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the employee establishes an actionable violation materially aided by delegated supervisory authority, preventive care and employee nonuse do not furnish a complete defense extinguishing that liability. After otherwise compensable injury is established, the employer may obtain an otherwise available reduction under ordinary remedial law only by proving an actual, legally cognizable loss that was causally avoidable; recovery is reduced only to that proved extent, while the statutory wrong and unavoidable injury remain.

**Authority:** Part II has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This common rule controls without aggregation of separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** Faragher rejects reliance on ineffective protection to erase proved authority-aided harassment, and Ellerth requires actual delegated aid even without a completed adverse action. Those decisions left the general complete-defense question open. The Court now resolves it: a plaintiff-proved use of employer-delegated authority remains attributable discrimination despite a preventive policy or unreasonable nonuse. General diligence does not change that historical connection.

The competing defense would encourage employers to prevent misconduct and employees to seek prompt correction. Those considerations already have concrete work to do. Practical opportunities inform whether resignation was objectively compelled under Duncan, and the employer may prove actual avoidability when the court determines recoverable loss. They do not justify eliminating an established wrong or injury the procedure could not have prevented. A policy's existence or nonreporting alone establishes neither liability erasure nor a reduction in relief.

This distinction preserves both burdens: the employee establishes the underlying claim and compensable injury, while the employer establishes the actual loss for which ordinary remedial law permits an avoidability reduction. It also preserves causation. A supported harassment injury remains distinct from loss caused by resignation; failure to prove constructive discharge does not eliminate an otherwise established harassment claim, and discharge loss cannot be recovered by relabeling it.

**Precedent treatment:** Faragher and Ellerth are applied on attribution and their reserved complete-defense question is resolved against categorical erasure; Duncan's practical-opportunity inquiry remains distinct from compensation.

##### III. The supported claims return for ordinary factfinding and lawful relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment reversing employer summary judgment is affirmed, with further proceedings under Parts I and II. The lower courts must resolve supported factual disputes through ordinary trial procedures and apply genuine independent legal grounds in their proper posture; a claim may not be disposed of solely because a policy existed or no formal discharge occurred.

**Authority:** Part III has the same five direct joins as Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiff-favorable account supports the required legal inquiries rather than conclusive employer nonliability. The Third Circuit's premise that resignation automatically establishes an official act or employer responsibility is incorrect, but its reversal of the categorical summary judgment stands under the actual-authority and loss-specific rules. Disputed discrimination, compulsion, delegated aid, causation and practical protection must receive their proper evidentiary resolution. Harassment injury, resignation injury, backpay and other authorized relief require their own causal and statutory predicates; ordinary avoidability may reduce a proved loss without deleting the underlying violation.

**Precedent treatment:** Ellerth's separation of attribution from final liability and remedy is applied; Duncan preserves claim-specific causal relief.

## Law After Decision

An employee must establish actionable discrimination, objective compulsion, resignation causation and an independent attribution basis. ## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer.** These Justices join the entire Court opinion and would specify additional terms for the loss inquiry. After the employee proves the claim and otherwise compensable injury, the employer would have to prove by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use them; and that the particular loss consequently could have been avoided. Reduction or denial would extend only to that proved loss.

A paper policy, nonreporting alone, unsafe or futile channels, correction after injury or the mere possibility of a complaint would not suffice. The employee need not complain to the harasser. Injury before a reasonable protective opportunity and injury the procedure could not prevent would remain compensable if otherwise established. Even proof that all compensable loss was avoidable would eliminate that recovery rather than erase the violation or automatically decide other relief and fees. The rule would contain neither special immunity where no official act occurs nor a categorical exception from ordinary avoidability whenever one occurs. An official coercive act may make effective protection or avoidability impossible to establish on the facts. Their proposal develops the causal recovery approach in the Ellerth concurrence; it has four votes here.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices join Part I but would vacate and remand under a newly adopted complete defense. An employer would have to prove by a preponderance both reasonable care to prevent and promptly correct the harassment and the employee's unreasonable failure to use provided preventive or corrective opportunities or otherwise avoid harm. The measures must be practically accessible and reasonably effective; unsafe or futile channels, paper policies and nonreporting alone would not establish the defense, and no particular written-policy form is invariably required. The employee would retain the actual-authority/material-aid burden.

The defense would be unavailable when an official discriminatory employment act precipitated the compelled resignation—for example, a demotion, extreme pay cut or transfer imposing unbearable working conditions through delegated employer authority. Authority to make the employment decision does not mean the employer authorized its discriminatory motive. The employee would establish that official act and its causal connection; the employer would retain persuasion on both defense elements. Resignation itself would not become an official act merely because it was objectively compelled. The alleged arrest matters to authority, coercion and safe complaint opportunities but does not conclusively determine the employment-act exception.

The dissent distinguishes delegated power materially aiding an environment from an official decision directly consummating the discrimination. Agency responsibility, in its view, permits a defense for genuine diligence and unreasonable nonuse in the former setting while withholding it in the latter. That rule deliberately does more than reduce particular avoidable losses; the dissent rejects the Court's view that attributable discrimination forecloses the complete defense. It would allow adjudication of the disputed defense rather than restore summary judgment for the employer.

## Holdings

##### I. Prejudice concerns the decision to plead guilty

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant seeking plain-error relief for the unpreserved omission of the required warning that rejection of a nonbinding sentencing recommendation does not permit withdrawal must show a reasonable probability that, with the warning, he would not have pleaded guilty. **Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of the Court's opinion, including this rule and the whole-record inquiry below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Vonn's defendant-borne substantial-rights inquiry must address the decision affected by this warning. Strickland and Bagley supply the familiar reasonable-probability measure of outcome uncertainty. Harris applies that measure to an actual counterfactual outcome within Rule 52(b); this case extends that method to the plea decision. A merely possible different choice is insufficient, but the defendant need not prove that a trial would have ended in acquittal.

The judge's personal warning remains mandatory. The contrary argument emphasizes that duty and the loss of the expected sentencing benefit. Representation and evidence bearing on the consequences of trial may matter without becoming presumptions or an innocence requirement.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** All nine participating Justices directly adopt the Opinion of the Court, the common test, both applications and the mandate.

### I. A claim duplicating the federal benefits cause is completely preempted

**Controlling proposition:** A state-law claim is completely preempted by ERISA when the claimant could, at some point, have pursued the disputed plan benefit under §502(a)(1)(B) and no legal duty independent of the plan entitlement supports the challenged conduct. The removing defendant must establish federal jurisdiction and both predicates; a later bodily injury or different damages demand does not change the source of the duty, while an independently operating treatment duty remains distinct.

**Controlling explanation:** Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Taylor supplies the established removal rule for claims governed by ERISA's exclusive benefits cause. Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson distinguishes replacement by a federal cause from an ordinary federal defense, which does not alone permit removal. The inquiry therefore turns on the actual entitlement and source of duty, not the plaintiff's chosen label.

Pegram v. Herdrich requires examination of the function challenged. Inseparable medical treatment by a treating physician differs from a discretionary determination applying plan-coverage terms, even when the latter uses medical information. Rush Prudential HMO, Inc. v. Moran permits a saved substantive coverage condition while preserving enforcement through §502. Miller retains that enforcement boundary alongside its insurance-saving method. A tort claim cannot evade the benefits channel merely by requesting consequential damages unavailable through the benefits action. Nor does insurance involvement convert independently negligent treatment into plan administration. These distinctions answer the patients' reliance on a state ordinary-care duty without treating every claim against an HMO as federal.

### II. Both pleaded coverage-denial claims satisfy that rule

**Controlling proposition:** Davila's challenge to the administrator's drug-coverage denial and Calad's challenge to the administrator's further-hospitalization coverage denial each could have pursued the asserted plan entitlement under §502(a)(1)(B), and neither identified claim rests on an independently operating treatment duty. Both claims are therefore completely preempted, removal was proper in each case, and the contrary Fifth Circuit judgments must be reversed.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged wrong in each complaint is the administrator's denial of coverage, not treatment delivered by that administrator as the patient's physician. Liability for that wrong depends on the asserted entitlement under the plan. Davila's ensuing use of a covered alternative and Calad's ensuing discharge explain the alleged injuries but do not supply a different source of obligation. The Texas ordinary-care characterization and additional injury element likewise do not detach these claims from coverage entitlement.

The seriousness of the alleged harm and absence of equivalent federal compensation cannot enact a competing enforcement cause. Rush preserves federal enforcement even when insurance regulation supplies a coverage condition, and Eichorn requires relief to fit the invoked statutory channel rather than relabeling personal monetary liability as equity. The plaintiffs chose not to amend to assert an ERISA cause after removal. The cases return for implementation consistent with that actual dismissal posture and ordinary treatment of any preserved procedural matter.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** The Opinion of the Court is adopted in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Those five direct joins control both holdings.

### I. A relevant name duty can operate within a lawful investigative stop

**Controlling proposition:** During an independently lawful investigative stop, a State may require a person to disclose a name when the demand is reasonably related to the circumstances justifying the stop and does not enlarge its lawful scope or duration. A prosecution must establish the lawful stop, the properly bounded demand and the statutory violation under the applicable criminal burden; refusal cannot supply retroactive suspicion or authority for unrelated questioning.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry v. Ohio permits a brief investigation supported by reasonable suspicion and limits its scope to its justification. Nevada's precise name requirement serves the particular investigation within those limits. It differs from Brown v. Texas, where independent suspicion was absent, and Kolender v. Lawson, where an undefined demand for reliable identification left the obligation to police discretion. Statutory clarity alone is not enough; the demand must actually relate to the lawful investigation. That requirement is satisfied on this accepted record.

The contrary argument correctly distinguishes permission to ask from power to punish silence. Berkemer v. McCarty's discussion of voluntary answers addressed whether an investigative stop amounted to Miranda custody; it did not decide an enacted, definite name duty. Terry's reasonableness inquiry permits this limited obligation without authorizing compelled explanations about the suspected conduct. Atwater v. City of Lago Vista separately requires an actual enforcement function for greater fine-only station transport and confinement. The name duty supplies no automatic power to enlarge the detention.

### II. A real evidentiary-link danger preserves the privilege

**Controlling proposition:** A compelled name demand remains subject to the Fifth Amendment when disclosure presents a real and appreciable danger of furnishing criminal evidence, including an evidentiary link. The claimant must supply a sufficient contextual basis to identify that danger without being forced to disclose the privileged substance itself; a claim of privacy alone does not establish the privilege, and this record supplies no such incriminating link.

**Controlling explanation:** Hoffman v. United States protects answers that could furnish a link in criminal proof; the claimant need not first reveal the protected information. Ohio v. Reiner confirms that professed innocence does not forfeit a supported privilege. United States v. Hubbell protects compelled testimonial knowledge and its derivative use at its subpoena-and-immunity scope. These rules require attention both to what information is compelled and to the real danger presented in context.

Identification can communicate information, and a name can be incriminating in a supported case. The Court's conclusion rests on the absence of a real danger here, not a categorical identity exception. The investigative usefulness of a name does not by itself establish that disclosing this name would furnish criminal evidence. Although Stevens regards the targeted criminal investigation as sufficient contextual exposure, this record identifies no concrete connection of that kind. The privilege is not waived by silence; its substantive predicate has not been established. Dickerson retains its separate constitutional safeguards for custodial interrogation.

## Precedent Treatment

- Terry v. Ohio: applied to independent suspicion and the continuing scope and duration limits; its framework now permits the defined relevant name duty.
- Brown v. Texas: distinguished because this review accepts an independently justified stop; its suspicion requirement remains binding.
- Kolender v. Lawson: distinguished because Nevada requires a name rather than undefined reliable identification.
- Berkemer v. McCarty: confined to its Miranda-custody question; its general discussion of voluntary answers does not settle the enacted name duty.
- Atwater v. City of Lago Vista: preserved at its separate requirement of an actual function for added fine-only station transport and confinement.
- Hoffman v. United States and Ohio v. Reiner: applied to real contextual exposure, including an evidentiary link and despite asserted innocence.
- United States v. Hubbell: preserved at its compelled-testimony and derivative-use scope without eliminating the actual incrimination requirement.
- Dickerson v. United States: preserved at its distinct custodial-interrogation scope.

## Law After Decision

A State may enforce a precise, relevant name duty within an independently lawful and properly limited investigative stop. Courts must verify the stop's justification, the demand's investigative relationship and any properly raised real danger of incrimination before imposing liability. Names have no categorical exemption from the privilege. 

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting.** A spoken name conveys information, and punishment for refusal compels that information. Hoffman protects an evidentiary link rather than only a completed confession. The State seeks Hiibel's identity to investigate his possible responsibility for a reported crime; Stevens regards that concrete purpose as the contextual basis for apprehending incrimination. Requiring an additional explanation of how the name connects to proof risks demanding the very disclosure the privilege protects. Hubbell reinforces protection for knowledge used as an investigative aid, and Muniz's routine-booking context differs from questioning targeted at a suspect's offense. He would reverse under the Fifth Amendment and supplies no separate Fourth Amendment ground.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Souter and Ginsburg.** Terry's limited seizure authority permits police to investigate on suspicion while leaving answers voluntary. A useful identity question remains distinct from a criminal obligation to answer. Berkemer's refusal principle expresses that boundary even though its immediate issue was Miranda custody. Nevada's name-only construction avoids Kolender's uncertainty, and the accepted stop avoids Brown's suspicion defect, but neither supplies the additional power to punish silence. A stable voluntary-answer rule protects the limited character of suspicion-based detention without preventing officers from asking questions. The dissent would set aside this conviction under the Fourth Amendment; its disposition does not depend on a Fifth Amendment application ruling.

## Event

Pliler v. Ford, No. 03-221; 542 U.S. 225. Argued April 26, 2004; decided June 21, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 330 F.3d 1086. The questions concern warnings about deleting unexhausted claims, stays and the limitation consequences of dismissing mixed habeas petitions, and the relief available for an inadequately explained court-presented election.

Ford delivered both original petitions to prison officials April 19, 1997, against the April 24 transitional deadline, and requested stays to complete exhaustion. In Loguercio, he elected dismissal July 28; dismissal followed September 11 and state filing September 29. In Weed, he did not notify the court that he would delete unexhausted claims; dismissal followed October 14 and state filing October 24. State review ended March 25, 1998, and both federal returns were filed April 7. The Ninth Circuit required advice about deletion as a prerequisite to considering a stay and the expired period's effect on refiling, and treated repeated claims as timely by relation back. Pliler seeks reversal; Ford seeks preservation of the repeated claims or an equitable remedy.

## Holdings

### I. A court-presented dismissal election requires minimum neutral notice

**Controlling proposition:** When a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to choose between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing to exhaust other claims, and the materials before it disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat timely return, it must give neutral notice that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the original filing date and that a later petition may be time barred. The court must provide a meaningful opportunity to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements.

**Authority:** Part I, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** A court controls the election it places before a litigant. Accurate explanation protects the existing filing opportunity without selecting the prisoner's strategy. Castro v. United States supervises judicial recharacterization that can consume a first-motion opportunity; its reasoning supports this new supervisory protection for a different judicial act. Duncan v. Walker supplies the concrete risk because federal pendency does not stop the limitation period.

The duty requires neither investigation of missing records, precise deadline computation, evaluation of claim strength, strategic advice nor a promised stay. Mere statutory complexity or an unsuccessful filing is not its trigger. It is a minimum duty, not an exhaustive statement of judicial responsibility when a court presents available options. The objection that Ford selected habeas does not answer responsibility for an election the court itself directs; the duty arises from that intervention and the apparent filing risk, rather than pro se status alone.

Ford's timely filings had only five days remaining, and the period expired while they were pending. The actual communications and responsive conduct require separate examination for each petition. The protection applies to these pending dismissal consequences and later qualifying elections; it supplies no new full limitation period.

### II. A proved court-created loss can receive equitable protection

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling for omission of the required notice or another materially misleading judicial direction that causes loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevents timely return, when the petitioner establishes the required causal connection and reasonable diligence. A notice violation alone does not establish reliance, causation or diligence, and causal loss alone does not establish that tolling is the legally available remedy for the actual timing defect.

**Authority:** Part II, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch, without joining the omitted-notice branch.

**Controlling explanation:** Duncan excludes ordinary federal pendency from statutory tolling while leaving independent equity open. The Court resolves that reservation for the defined court-created impediment. The rule responds to a supported loss caused by the court's own procedure; it does not automatically convert processing delay, pro se status or an unfavorable calculation into an obstacle. The court must identify a lawful remedy that can cure the actual defect, distinguishing loss of the still-pending timely original from prevention of timely later filing.

The objection that this rewrites Duncan mistakes the source of protection. Federal pendency itself remains outside §2244(d)(2). Relief depends instead on judicial interference, causation, diligence and an effective lawful remedial basis. That distinction also prevents an incomplete election from becoming automatic reinstatement. Carey preserves the actual requirements for qualifying state pendency, and Slack preserves independent timing despite a renewed application's first-application status. Those requirements govern the resulting calculation.

### III. The petitioner must prove an effective causal remedy

**Controlling proposition:** The petitioner must prove the factual predicates for this equitable protection by a preponderance of the evidence, including reasonable diligence during the relevant period and after the obstacle ended; diligence is assessed in the actual circumstances, not as perfect diligence or a duty to anticipate and correct the very misleading direction at issue. The court must identify the precise interval requiring protection, justify its causal and lawful remedial basis, exclude independently avoidable delay, and explain how the remedy cures the whole timing defect, including any expiration before the election.

**Authority:** Part III, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins these requirements for the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch.

**Controlling explanation:** Grogan v. Garner and Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston identify preponderance as the ordinary civil allocation of factual uncertainty, subject to the statute, interests involved and any applicable higher standard. These procedural facts determine whether ordinary adjudication may proceed. The prisoner's liberty interest supplies no reason to increase the prisoner's burden, while finality is protected by the causal and remedial requirements. The Court applies those proof principles to this inquiry; distinct statutory or remedial predicates retain their own standards.

Both original petitions were timely when filed, but their period expired before dismissal. Protecting only days after dismissal cannot simply be assumed to make the returns timely. The court must distinguish a lost timely original opportunity from prevented later filing. A later omission need not physically cause earlier elapsed days, but the supported causal chain and chosen lawful remedy must actually reach that expiration. A tolling theory must justify the interval protected; a reopening theory must meet its independent lawful prerequisites. Automatically subtracting all federal pendency would contradict Duncan. Without a lawful basis curing the defect, the later application remains untimely.

### IV. Omitted warnings do not automatically join separate actions

**Controlling proposition:** A separate later petition does not automatically become an amendment to a closed original action because required warnings were omitted. Relief depending on reopening requires an independently lawful reopening predicate; whether relation back follows an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remains unresolved because that procedural predicate has not been established here.

**Authority:** Part IV, directly adopted by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit's remedy treated the repeated claims as though Ford had deleted unexhausted claims, obtained a stay of retained exhausted claims and timely amended the still-pending petitions after exhaustion. Those procedural acts cannot be supplied merely by finding defective advice. A genuinely retained and stayed original differs from a terminated action, and Slack's first-application classification does not turn one into the other. The omitted-warning theory therefore cannot sustain automatic relation back. A separately supported lawful correction must be assessed on its actual conditions.

General authority and standards for mixed-petition stays remain unresolved because no position on that distinct question commands a majority. The minimum notice rule does not require deciding that broader procedure.

### V. The repeated claims return for individualized adjudication

**Judgment and authority:** Seven Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—vacate the repeated-claim disposition and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Souter adopt Part V's affirmative-rule rationale; the other four support vacatur on their no-advisement ground. Ginsburg and Breyer would preserve the lower vacatur of the repeated-claim dismissals by partial affirmance. Parts I–IV supply the controlling rules for further proceedings despite that remedial disagreement.

**Application and remand:** Loguercio's express election and Weed's nonresponse must receive separate treatment. The lower courts must determine preservation, actual communications, responsive conduct, causal loss and reasonable diligence, then identify an authorized remedy curing each petition's timing defect or apply the time bar. The two new Loguercio assistance claims remain dismissed, and factual development concerning legal-paper access on the five new Weed claims continues under the existing direction.

## Law After Decision

Federal courts must give the specified minimum notice and deletion opportunity when their own election concerning a timely mixed petition presents an apparent substantial risk of losing timely return. A proved loss caused by deficient notice or materially misleading judicial directions may receive equitable protection under the stated proof, reasonable-diligence and causal-remedy requirements. The court must calculate the whole period and identify a lawful remedy effective against the actual defect. Federal pendency alone remains outside statutory tolling, and separate closed and later actions do not automatically merge. The general stay and contingent relation-back questions identified above remain unresolved; the factual and remedial inquiries on remand apply settled rules.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy.** The four Justices reject the compulsory advice requirements below and the new minimum warning duty. Rose supplies mixed-petition exhaustion procedure; it does not make a judge counsel responsible for explaining alternatives and limitation consequences. Castro concerns unsolicited conversion into a vehicle the prisoner did not select. Ford chose habeas, and accurate description of a nonmerits dismissal does not itself become misleading because advice is omitted. The narrower warning reduces computational demands but does not answer the objection to creating an affirmative duty. They support vacatur and adjudication of genuinely available independent grounds. O'Connor additionally joins the Court's equity and proof rules for actual materially misleading judicial directions, preserving that distinction without adopting a new omission duty; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join the new equitable formulation.

**Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Breyer throughout, and by Stevens and Souter on the fuller protective procedure.** The four Justices would recognize deletion of unexhausted claims, retention and a stay of exhausted claims, and lawful amendment after exhaustion as an available procedure. Rose's comity requirement does not categorically prohibit retaining a timely federal opportunity while the State addresses other claims. Conditions can control abusive delay; amendment and timing retain their ordinary requirements. Because Ford requested preservation of that opportunity, accurate presentation of his options should disclose the available protective procedure. The minimum warning adopted by the Court is a floor. Duncan's federal-pendency exclusion makes this protection consequential, and Castro supports responsibility for the court's own intervention.

Ginsburg and Breyer would also preserve the Ninth Circuit's vacatur of the repeated-claim dismissals by partial affirmance, correcting the relation-back mechanism while requiring lawful further proceedings. They oppose withdrawing that existing protection merely because the precise remedy needs adjudication. Stevens and Souter do not join this remedial portion; they regard vacatur and individualized equitable adjudication under the Court's rules as an adequate disposition.
