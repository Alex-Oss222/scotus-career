## Decisions and Dispositions

### In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.

Merits decision, December 15, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided December 15, 2003, on merits submission. Review of the Third Circuit's judgment, 322 F.3d 283, affirming the bankruptcy sale order through the district court. The question is whether 11 U.S.C. §363(f) permits the sale free of the identified employment-discrimination and travel-voucher successor liabilities although they are not liens or strictly in-rem rights.

American purchased substantially all TWA's assets for $742 million. The EEOC and the represented maternity-discrimination settlement class objected to an order barring successor enforcement against American. The Court assumes otherwise viable nonbankruptcy successor claims and decides the sale's effect upon them.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Represented EEOC and voucher-class successor claims | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | Free-and-clear transfer and injunction remain effective within the statutory and process limits; estate claims remain subject to the Code. |
| Ancillary appeal from refusal of a limited stay | Dismissed as moot, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | Final merits disposition ends the limited-stay appeal. |

The free-and-clear transfer and injunction remain effective against the identified EEOC and voucher-class successor claims within the process and statutory limits stated below. Claims against the TWA estate remain subject to allowance, valuation, priority and distribution under the Bankruptcy Code. The final merits disposition makes the ancillary appeal from refusal of a limited stay moot.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** The unanimous opinion of the Court. All nine Justices join both holdings, their qualifications and the complete judgment and remedy.

##### 1. The specific successor interests satisfy the sale statute.

Section 363(f)(5) permits a sale free of a successor obligation arising through acquisition and continuation of the debtor's transferred operating enterprise when an applicable legal or equitable proceeding can compel satisfaction of that interest in money. The sale proponent bears the ordinary civil burden of establishing both the property connection and this independent monetary-satisfaction condition; the represented pre-sale discrimination and voucher obligations satisfy them here.

The Court holds that the statutory interest reaches these transfer-dependent successor obligations. Section 363(f)(3) separately identifies a lien as one kind of interest, so a liens-only construction would give the broader term too little work. Yet a debtor's ownership of assets does not turn every personal debt into an interest in each asset. Here the asserted obligation of American arises through its acquisition and continuation of the enterprise.

Patterson v. Shumate requires application of the Code's operative words, and Hartford Underwriters requires compliance with its particular grants of authority. In re Dow Corning rejects independent nondebtor releases based on general plan powers; this sale therefore depends on §363(f)'s distinct conditions. Those conditions are met because these obligations could be compelled into monetary claims in liquidation. The vouchers' promised noncash benefit can receive that legally available monetary substitute. An estimate of value alone would not establish the required legal capacity for satisfaction. The favorable purchase price and the Code's priorities likewise cannot replace a statutory predicate.

##### 2. The sale's protection follows its statutory and process boundaries.

The sale order binds the represented claimant groups at the adjudicated scope and preserves their estate claims under the Code. Its protection extends to the transfer-dependent pre-sale successor obligations; independent purchaser wrongdoing, future compliance duties, and claims of persons without legally sufficient notice and opportunity to be heard fall outside that adjudicated protection.

Both claimant groups participated in the sale hearing. That process supports the treatment of the claims before the Court, rather than a finding about unidentified persons. Section 363(e) also retains its separate command: on an affected interest holder's request, the bankruptcy court must prohibit or condition a sale as necessary to provide adequate protection. The sale conditions, claimant process and requested adequate protection each perform their own statutory work. Preserving estate claims respects the Code's distribution system but supplies no independent power to release American.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Patterson v. Shumate:** Applied for the Code's operative-text method; the particular property-interest and monetary-satisfaction conditions govern this sale.
- **Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank:** Applied for fidelity to the Code's specific allocation of authority; general equitable usefulness does not supply a missing statutory entitlement.
- **In re Dow Corning Corp.:** Distinguished because §363(f)(5) supplies a specific transferred-property mechanism here. Its prohibition of independent nondebtor releases under general plan powers remains effective.

#### Law After Decision

Section 363(f)(5) reaches the identified transfer-dependent pre-sale successor interests when the proponent proves the property connection, a legally available compulsory monetary satisfaction, and the required claimant process. The rule applies across comparable enterprise sales at those predicates. Adequate protection on request, independent purchaser liability, claimant notice and the Code's estate-distribution rules retain their distinct force.

#### Source Notes

11 U.S.C. §§101(5), 105(a), 363(e), 363(f), 1123(b)(6); 322 F.3d 283. The represented claimant groups participated in the sale hearing. Otherwise viable nonbankruptcy successor claims are assumed for purposes of the statutory question.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The ordinary mandate leaves the reviewed sale treatment and injunction in force and concludes the limited-stay appeal as moot. The bankruptcy court continues estate claim allowance, valuation and distributions and enforces the sale order within the statutory and process boundaries stated above.

**End of entry: In re Trans World Airlines, Inc., merits decision, December 15, 2003.**

#### Holdings

**Authority for all three holdings:** The opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices on these holdings.

##### 1. Denver establishes the required racial justification.

A public contracting classification satisfies Adarand when the government proves an important lawful remedial objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, accounting for its actual coverage and burdens and answering supported, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Linked statistical and anecdotal evidence can establish local exclusion to which public spending contributes without a prior discrimination judgment against each contractor or individual proof of injury to every beneficiary; Denver's developed evidence and preserved fit determination meet that burden here.

Adarand supplies the common substantial-justification rule. Engineering Contractors requires a supported response to concrete alternatives, and City of Dallas requires a demonstrated local remedial connection. The accepted findings connect city contractors and city spending to actual exclusion. The studies are considered with their limitations, contracting experience and testimony. The developed response to the size-and-capacity criticism addresses how firms hire, subcontract and obtain equipment rather than simply assuming that every disparity proves discrimination.

The ultimate burden remains Denver's. The Court does not rest validity on CWC's failure to commission a regression or disprove every finding. Gratz requires attention to the mechanism's actual coverage and burdens; Grutter requires consideration of genuinely practicable alternatives rather than exhaustion of every imagined method. The record and the binding fit determination satisfy those inquiries. A prior adjudication against every beneficiary or contractor would replace the governing evidentiary inquiry with a prerequisite that these precedents do not impose.

##### 2. The women-owned-business provisions receive a separate constitutional assessment.

Government must establish an actual important objective and a substantial relationship between the sex classification and that objective under the demanding governmental proof requirement. Denver satisfies that standard through the women-specific utilization, earnings and exclusion evidence and the program's preserved fit; proof supporting the racial provisions does not by itself establish this separate justification.

City of Dallas preserves the distinct sex inquiry. The Court evaluates the evidence concerning women-owned firms in its own terms, including their opportunities and the relationship of the remedy to demonstrated exclusion. A shared remedial setting cannot replace that proof with stereotypes or evidence about different groups. The separate showing supports the same judgment for Denver.

##### 3. The unchallenged fit determination retains its effect.

An earlier determination in the same litigation ordinarily governs a later stage when the available appeal left it unchallenged and the challenged program has not materially changed. Substantially different evidence, contrary intervening controlling law, or clear error producing manifest injustice may justify departure; none is established for this preserved fit issue.

Law of the case is a practice governing reconsideration, not a restriction on subject-matter jurisdiction. CWC had the opportunity to challenge the earlier determination and has identified no material amendment reopening it. The actual Adarand standard requires substantive justification, but replacing the lower court's scrutiny vocabulary does not itself reopen an already established, more demanding fit determination. The settled issue supplies its proper part of the constitutional analysis while Denver retains the burden on the live questions. The developed record permits judgment without a new appellate factual finding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Adarand Constructors v. Peña:** Applied as the controlling common substantial-justification rule for racial classifications; its governmental burden and actual fit requirements retain force.
- **Engineering Contractors Association v. Metropolitan Dade County:** Applied to the evidence, actual burdens and supported practicable alternatives; supported local remediation remains subject to those requirements.
- **City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters:** Applied for a demonstrated local remedial connection and independent sex-classification proof. The evidence here supplies the connection and distinct showings the governing inquiries require.
- **Gratz v. Bollinger:** Applied for attention to a remedial mechanism's actual operation, coverage and burdens rather than general historical exclusion alone.
- **Grutter v. Bollinger:** Applied for meaningful consideration of concrete, practicable alternatives without a requirement to exhaust every conceivable method.

#### Law After Decision

The existing racial and sex contracting standards remain distinct and government-borne. Denver's developed evidence and preserved fit adjudication satisfy them for the live 1990 and 1998 claims. Combined evidence may demonstrate connected local exclusion; each classification still requires its own supported justification. Law of the case preserves an unchallenged fit determination subject to materially changed issues, substantially different evidence, contrary intervening controlling law, or clear error producing manifest injustice.

#### Source Notes

321 F.3d 950; Equal Protection Clause; Denver's 1990, 1996 and 1998 contracting ordinances. The prior fit determination was not challenged in the available appeal. The 1990 damages controversy and 1998 prospective controversy have the distinct procedural status stated above.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must implement the direction to enter judgment for Denver and remove the inconsistent injunction and declarations, preserving the affirmed mootness dispositions. Denver may administer the sustained program within its enacted terms and continuing equal-protection obligations.

**End of entry: Concrete Works of Colorado v. City and County of Denver, merits decision, December 18, 2003.**

The requests include identities, dates, locations and reasons for detention. The questions are whether FOIA supports the categorical withholding and whether the First Amendment or common law independently requires disclosure of the executive compilation.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Holdings

**Authority:** Holdings 1–2 rest on the seven-Justice coalition identified for those portions of the Court's opinion above. Holdings 3–4 have all nine Justices' direct joins.

##### 1. Exemption 7(A) requires a supported connection between disclosure and enforcement harm.

An agency invoking 7(A) must establish, through sufficiently specific facts or supported functional categories, how disclosure could reasonably be expected to interfere with pending or reasonably anticipated enforcement proceedings. Courts give supported security predictions substantial weight and may protect sensitive explanations, but must independently assess the connection and reasonable segregation; the undifferentiated showing here does not establish the entire withholding category.

Robbins Tire permits predictions about functional classes without proof of completed intimidation or a public explanation about each person. Landano preserves supported categorical inferences while placing the burden on the agency under 7(D); the same proof discipline applies here to 7(A)'s different statutory harm. Klamath requires the actual enacted exemption rather than confidentiality based on governmental purpose alone.

DOJ identifies serious mechanisms of harm: revealing investigative direction, identifying sources, enabling coordination or intimidation, and facilitating misleading evidence. Those mechanisms may justify properly supported categories. They do not alone explain why every withheld immigration-detention, material-witness and attorney item retains the necessary connection. Release or deportation does not automatically end investigative value, and a protected source need not be culpable. The contrary view treats official confirmation of the comprehensive investigative map as sufficient for the whole class. The Court requires support for that class's actual breadth, allowing protected common evidence rather than requiring disclosure of the intelligence being protected. A national-security label by itself cannot establish the statutory inference.

##### 2. Each remaining protection and reasonably segregable portion requires its own determination.

On remand, DOJ retains the burden on each independently invoked exemption, and the court must order disclosure of reasonably segregable nonexempt information. The following distinct predicates govern:

- Under **7(C)**, cognizable personal-privacy interests are balanced against disclosure's illumination of government conduct; association with a terrorism inquiry can create substantial privacy consequences.
- Under **7(F)**, the agency must support a reasonable expectation of danger to a person's life or physical safety.
- Under **Exemption 3 and Rule 6(e)**, the inquiry is whether disclosure would reveal protected grand-jury matters.
- A **sealing order** protects information within the actual order's coverage; the court must identify that order and its reach.

Landano requires real segregation, and Reporters Committee identifies the relevant public interest as information about governmental activity. The requested compilation can illuminate detention administration, while its private identifying details retain cognizable protection. Each asserted ground must be applied to its own facts. Attorney information requires its own supported connection: counsel neither automatically inherits every client exemption nor loses safety protection because of a public professional role. A material-witness label alone does not establish grand-jury secrecy or a sealing order's reach. The requested wholesale release must await these determinations, with appropriate protection during review.

##### 3. The First Amendment does not independently require this executive compilation.

A general request for the agency's investigative list does not establish a constitutional right merely because the list identifies detained persons or concerns proceedings to which access may attach. The asserted compilation-access claim therefore fails.

North Jersey Media Group recognizes qualified access to adversarial removal hearings with supported, appropriately limited closure; the executive list is not that adjudication. United Reporting preserves the absence of a universal right to executive information while separately governing speech-selected access conditions. The latter selection is absent here. The distinction preserves the hearing-access holding and FOIA's independent statutory duties.

##### 4. The asserted common-law route cannot displace FOIA for these agency records.

Congress's disclosure-and-exemption scheme governs access to the executive compilation at issue. Common law cannot override a valid FOIA exemption or create a second balancing route to the same withheld agency records.

Milwaukee v. Illinois supplies the principle that enacted federal law displaces federal common law when Congress has addressed the subject. Nixon v. Warner Communications likewise requires attention to the applicable statutory access route. This claim concerns the executive compilation; access to a particular judicial record presents a different institution and legal channel outside this question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **NLRB v. Robbins Tire & Rubber Co.:** Applied to 7(A)'s use of functional categories and predictive enforcement harm; it does not eliminate the need to support the class actually withheld.
- **United States Department of Justice v. Landano:** Its agency-burden and supported-category reasoning is extended to 7(A), while its distinct 7(D) confidentiality element remains specific to that exemption. Its segregability requirement is applied.
- **Department of the Interior v. Klamath Water Users Protective Association:** Applied to require the particular exemption's enacted conditions despite a substantial governmental confidentiality purpose.
- **Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press:** Applied for the government-conduct public interest and cognizable compilation-related privacy interests under 7(C).
- **North Jersey Media Group v. Ashcroft:** Distinguished as a hearing-access decision; its qualified access and closure safeguards remain effective.
- **Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp.:** Applied to distinguish a general information claim from speech-selected access restrictions.
- **Milwaukee v. Illinois:** Applied to the displacement of common law by the governing enacted disclosure scheme.
- **Nixon v. Warner Communications, Inc.:** Applied for attention to the statutory access route governing the requested records.

#### Law After Decision

FOIA permits supported functional categories and protected predictive evidence while retaining agency persuasion, exemption-specific judicial review and reasonably segregable disclosure. The statutory inquiry turns on the actual records and asserted harms. The First Amendment and common law provide no independent route to this executive compilation on the claims presented; the qualified hearing-access rule and ordinary judicial-record channels retain their separate operation.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's statutory rules, independent-access holdings and remand. He would additionally require a prompt production-and-review schedule, a usable public explanation to the extent possible, and enforceable disclosure dates for material found nonexempt. Temporary protection should extend only as necessary to complete that review. His additional direction ensures that vacating the blanket withholding produces a timely, effective adjudication while preserving independently established protections.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The dissent accepts the agency's statutory burden and rejects secrecy based on a security label alone. It finds that the declarations support this compilation as a functional investigative-map category: official confirmation can expose selections, contacts, gaps and sources even when individual roles differ or a person has been released. Robbins Tire permits that prediction. On this record the dissent would sustain 7(A) withholding, making alternative exemptions unnecessary to its result. It joins the rejection of both independent compilation-access claims.

#### Source Notes

5 U.S.C. §552(a)(4)(B), (b)(3), (b)(7)(A), (C), (F), and the segregability requirement; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e); 331 F.3d 918. The status and coverage of actual sealing orders require determination in the appropriate protected proceedings.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for the actual category, residual-exemption, sealing-coverage and segregation determinations under the stated rules, followed by enforceable disclosure of nonexempt reasonably segregable material. The court may use justified protective procedures and temporary protection while resolving the claims. The independent First Amendment and common-law compilation-access claims are concluded.

### Nebraska v. Environmental Protection Agency, D.C. Circuit No. 01-1101

Merits decision, January 8, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

D.C. Circuit No. 01-1101; judgment below, 331 F.3d 995. Decided January 8, 2004, on merits submission. The Court reviews denial of Nebraska and the City of Alliance's petition challenging the Safe Drinking Water Act's regulation of noncommunicable contaminants and EPA's arsenic rule. The questions are whether the statute is facially invalid under the Commerce Clause and whether compliance duties on States and municipalities as water-system owners impermissibly commandeer them.

The Act directs EPA to set maximum contaminant levels for public water systems. The January 2001 rule sets arsenic at .01 mg/L, with compliance in 2006. Covered providers include public and private utilities and systems supplying water across state boundaries. Petitioners presented none of their current objections during rulemaking and conceded waiver of rule-specific objections.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

The requested injunction against the statute or arsenic rule is denied. The existing rule governs covered providers according to its terms and its 2006 compliance schedule.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined Parts I and II and concurred in the judgment on Part III without adding a ground.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and II | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirms rejection of both constitutional challenges | Interstate commercial-water regulation and public-operator compliance. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Scalia | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Preserves disposition of rule-specific objections | Agency presentation and preservation; Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgment without adding a ground. |

#### Holdings

**Authority for Holdings 1 and 2:** Parts I and II of the Court's opinion, directly joined by all nine Justices. **Authority for Holding 3:** Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in that component's judgment.

##### 1. Congress may regulate the safety of drinking water sold in interstate utility commerce.

The sale and interstate supply of drinking water provide a direct commercial connection for federal contaminant standards, and noncommunicability of the contaminant does not remove that connection. Because the challenged statute validly reaches these interstate utility transactions, petitioners' facial claim that Congress lacks authority to regulate noncommunicable contaminants fails.

Lopez requires identification of the regulated class and its constitutional connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal power. The water transactions here supply that connection themselves: Congress regulates the safety of the commodity sold across state boundaries. The objection that distribution often occurs locally and arsenic is not contagious does not answer those valid applications. Home Builders requires a supported mechanism fitted to the actual regulated class; it does not turn its distinct intrastate mechanisms into exclusive routes for regulating interstate commerce. The facial claim is therefore fully resolved by the established commercial applications.

##### 2. Valid operational standards may govern public water suppliers.

A federal law may regulate States and municipalities as operators of covered water systems without compelling them to enact federal policy or enforce federal rules against unrelated persons. Work and expense required to bring the government's own operation into compliance do not alone constitute commandeering; these operational duties are valid.

Condon distinguishes federal regulation of a State's own covered activity from conscription of its sovereign machinery. That distinction controls because the Act imposes contaminant duties on public and private suppliers as suppliers. New York prohibits a compulsory legislative program or the substitute assumption of private obligations, and Printz prohibits compelled state execution of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons. Neither prohibition is triggered merely because public employees must operate their own utility in conformity with federal safety standards. Public ownership does not transform those standards into a command to govern others.

##### 3. The facial statutory claim does not excuse the actual failure to present regulatory objections.

EPA's inability to invalidate its governing statute permits judicial consideration of the facial constitutional claim, but does not excuse nonpresentation of objections to the agency's own implementation that it could meaningfully address. On petitioners' actual nonpresentation and waiver concession, the rule-specific objections remain unpreserved.

Petitioners' futility argument treats two different agency functions as one. EPA could not strike down Congress's enactment, but could consider the proposed regulatory implementation, interpret its mandate and develop the evidentiary record. The preserved facial challenge accordingly supplies no route to reopen the scientific selection of the arsenic level. This ruling applies the actual preservation record, rather than imposing a universal presentation requirement on all constitutional claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied for an identified constitutional connection before magnitude; direct interstate commodity regulation supplies that connection here.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied for a supported mechanism fitted to the enacted class; its distinct intrastate routes do not restrict direct regulation of interstate sales.
- **Reno v. Condon:** Applied to public operators' own compliance duties; the operational and sovereign-enforcement distinction controls.
- **New York v. United States:** Distinguished because the statute regulates operation of a utility rather than commanding state legislation or assumption of private obligations; its structural prohibition retains force.
- **Printz v. United States:** Distinguished because the Act assigns no compelled state enforcement program against unrelated persons; its prohibition retains force.

#### Law After Decision

Federal drinking-water safety standards may govern interstate utility sales and public suppliers' own operations on the grounds stated. The facial challenge cannot invalidate those applications. The denial of rule-specific review leaves EPA's arsenic standard and scheduled compliance requirements operative, while the distinction between operator compliance and compelled government of others remains controlling.

331 F.3d 995; Safe Drinking Water Act, 42 U.S.C. §§300f, 300g-1; U.S. Const., Art. I, §8, and Amdt. 10. The interstate utility applications and the actual agency-presentation record supply the bases for this facial and procedural decision.

The petition for review remains denied. EPA may administer the arsenic rule, and covered providers remain subject to its existing 2006 compliance schedule.

### Illinois v. Lidster, No. 02-1060

Merits decision, January 13, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

No. 02-1060; 540 U.S. 419. Decided January 13, 2004, on merits submission. The Illinois Supreme Court sustained suppression by treating this checkpoint as categorically barred by City of Indianapolis v. Edmond. The question is whether a brief, systematic checkpoint seeking witnesses to a specific fatal hit-and-run has an adequate independent justification and is reasonable in its actual operation.

Police returned one week after the fatal incident to the same place and approximate time, asked motorists a brief question and handed them a flyer. Flashing lights and partial lane obstruction produced queues of up to fifteen cars per eastbound lane. Total waiting was a very few minutes at most; contact with an officer lasted ten to fifteen seconds. Lidster's near collision with an officer and observed signs of intoxication led to a later individualized investigation.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

STEVENS, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined the purpose distinction and governing reasonableness standard. #### Holdings

##### 1. A specific witness inquiry requires its own operational justification.

Edmond does not categorically bar a genuine checkpoint seeking possible witnesses' information about another person's specific serious incident. The government must establish the actual purpose and a concrete connection between the incident, the selected place and time, and the likely information sought; Brown then requires consideration of the gravity of the concern, how the seizure advances it, and the full interference with liberty, including duration and limits on officer discretion.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion on this rule and framework, joined by all eight other Justices.

Edmond invalidated an ordinary drug-detection program directed at the stopped motorists' own offenses. Its antecedent-authority requirement remains effective. A defined inquiry addressed to possible witnesses has a different relation to those stopped, but an information-gathering label cannot itself authorize a seizure. Chandler requires actual justification in the program's operation. Brown supplies the contextual balance once that concrete need is identified. The rule accordingly permits a genuinely incident-linked, proportionate interruption; an investigation of a motorist's own wrongdoing requires its separate justification.

##### 2. This complete operation is reasonable.

The same-place, same-time inquiry one week after a fatal hit-and-run, its systematic and narrowly limited question and flyer, and its brief total delay establish a proportionate witness checkpoint. The whole detention—including the queue rather than just officer contact—satisfies Brown, so the checkpoint supplies no basis for the ordered suppression.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The fatal event supplies a grave public concern. The place and approximate weekly timing reasonably connect the inquiry to travelers who might have useful information; the justification does not depend on every driver having witnessed the collision. Uniform, short contacts limit discretion. The compulsory queue and nighttime encounter impose real interruption and apprehension, but the total wait remains small in relation to the confined inquiry.

The competing remand position gives that intrusion and uncertain information yield greater weight and would have Illinois perform the contextual assessment first. The accepted record permits the Court to resolve this particular balance now through Brown's practical assessment of the established operation. The later intoxication investigation supplies no retrospective justification for initiating the checkpoint; the incident-linked operation stands on its own grounds.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **City of Indianapolis v. Edmond:** Distinguished by the genuine witness purpose; its prohibition of ordinary suspect-screening roadblocks and antecedent-justification requirement remain effective.
- **Chandler v. Miller:** Applied to require a supported operational need rather than a label or symbolic purpose; the actual incident connection and constrained operation supply that justification here.
- **Brown v. Texas:** Applied to the public concern, advancement of that concern, full intrusion and constraints on discretion. That framework governs both the defined inquiry and this operation's reasonableness.

#### Law After Decision

Courts must distinguish a genuinely incident-linked witness inquiry from a program investigating the stopped motorists for ordinary crimes. The former still requires a concrete place-and-time connection and proportionate, constrained operation under Brown. This fatal-hit-and-run checkpoint satisfies that standard on its complete queue-inclusive record.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three Justices agree that Edmond's categorical rule does not dispose of this witness inquiry and join the governing Brown framework. They give substantial weight to the involuntary nighttime delay and the uncertain likelihood of useful information from the motorists. Because Illinois relied on a categorical rule, they would return the case for its initial contextual application. Their proposed remand does not find this checkpoint unreasonable, require a new hearing or rest on a disputed longer wait; it preserves their view that this close balance should be applied below first.

540 U.S. 419; No. 02-1060; Fourth Amendment. The total waiting interval and the shorter officer-contact interval describe distinct parts of the same seizure.

The ordinary mandate returns the case to Illinois with the categorical suppression ruling reversed and the checkpoint's reasonableness settled. The state court continues the prosecution consistently with that determination.

### SEC v. Edwards, No. 02-1196

Merits decision, January 13, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

No. 02-1196. Decided January 13, 2004, on merits submission. The Court reviews the Eleventh Circuit's reversal of preliminary relief in the SEC's enforcement action. The question is whether a promoted investment arrangement promising a fixed contractual return can be an investment contract under the federal securities laws.

Investors purchased payphones and could lease them back to an affiliated enterprise that selected sites, operated the equipment and promised a fixed annual return. The SEC alleges that operating income was insufficient and later investor funds supplied payments. Those allegations are accepted for the reviewed dismissal question rather than treated as final findings.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Eleventh Circuit's categorical investment-contract exclusions are rejected. The lower courts must reconsider the securities classification and requested enforcement relief under the complete investment-contract test and the applicable proof and remedial requirements.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the definition, application and remand.

**A fixed contractual return can be investment profit under Howey.** An investment contract may promise a fixed return: expected income from committed capital qualifies as profit, and a contractual right to payment does not itself eliminate reliance on the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts supplying that return. The SEC must establish an investment of money in a common enterprise with the required expectation of profits from others' efforts, assessed through the complete offered arrangement; these payphone arrangements cannot be excluded merely because the yield is fixed or contractually enforceable.

Howey examines the economic arrangement as a whole, including services offered with the property. Forman distinguishes investment income from a benefit bought for personal use. Its references to appreciation and participation in earnings illustrate that distinction; they do not exhaust the forms investment income may take. A fixed return is still income sought by committing capital to another's undertaking.

The contractual-entitlement argument confuses the duty to pay with the economic activity making payment possible. A promise establishes what the promoter owes, but does not generate the money needed to honor it. The equipment purchase, leaseback and operating services must therefore be considered together when evaluating dependence on management. Ownership of a tangible payphone alone does not answer that inquiry.

Reves's isolated suggestion concerning Forman's examples supplies no fixed-return exception to Howey. Its treatment of the separately listed note category retains its distinct function. Zandford requires the actual securities-law elements and operative connection, keeping correction of a categorical legal error separate from proof of a violation. On remand the lower courts must apply all investment-contract elements to the arrangement, determine any remaining violation allegations and assess requested preliminary or final relief under the governing standards.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **SEC v. W. J. Howey Co.:** Applied to the whole promoted property-and-services arrangement; expected profits include fixed investment income.
- **United Housing Foundation v. Forman:** Applied for the investment-versus-consumption distinction; its examples do not establish a fixed-income exclusion.
- **Reves v. Ernst & Young:** Its suggestion that Forman's examples exhaust investment profits is rejected as noncontrolling dictum; the distinct test for the note category remains in force.
- **SEC v. Zandford:** Applied to preserve proof of the enacted securities elements and ordinary remedial requirements after correction of a categorical legal error.

#### Law After Decision

Fixed and variable investment returns receive the same Howey inquiry. Courts evaluate the complete arrangement and its dependence on others' managerial efforts, while requiring proof of the remaining investment-contract elements and any asserted statutory violation. A contractual guarantee supplies no categorical exemption from that inquiry.

#### Source Notes

Securities Act §2(a)(1), 15 U.S.C. §77b(a)(1); Securities Exchange Act §3(a)(10), 15 U.S.C. §78c(a)(10); Howey, 328 U.S. 293; Forman, 421 U.S. 837; Reves, 494 U.S. 56. The allegation that later investments supplied payments remains an allegation at this posture.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for application of the corrected investment-contract definition and adjudication of the remaining enforcement and preliminary-relief questions. The SEC bears the applicable burdens for securities classification, a violation and each remedy sought.

**End of entry: SEC v. Edwards, merits decision, January 13, 2004.**

### Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP, No. 02-682

Merits decision, January 13, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided January 13, 2004, on merits submission. The Court reviews the Second Circuit's reinstatement of an antitrust complaint alleging discriminatory performance of an incumbent telephone carrier's network-access obligations. The questions concern independent monopoly-maintenance conduct, the customer's entitlement to damages, and its request for prospective relief.

Trinko is an AT&T customer. AT&T obtains local-network access from Verizon. The complaint alleges that Verizon degraded rival-customer order processing to discourage rival patronage. Federal and state regulators investigated access failures and imposed remedial measures and financial consequences. The complaint seeks damages and an injunction providing rivals access on terms at least as favorable as Verizon's own. The case comes at the pleading stage.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clayton Act section 4 damages | Reinstatement reversed; existing damages theory dismissed for insufficient directness, 5–4 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer | Dismiss the existing damages theory. |
| Independent section 2 conduct and section 16 prospective relief | Existing reinstatement basis vacated and claim remanded, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas | Bounded adjudication under the ordinary offense, personal-threat and equitable-remedy requirements. |

The requested access-parity decree is not authorized by the regulatory duty alone. The lower courts must evaluate the existing prospective claim under the rules below and deny relief if its offense, threatened-injury or remedial requirements fail. Communications-statute and state-law claims retain their existing status outside this antitrust review.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which SOUTER, J., joined; STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and KENNEDY, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined Parts I and III; and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, and THOMAS, JJ., joined Part II. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion dissenting in part, in which O'CONNOR and THOMAS, JJ., joined. GINSBURG, J., filed an opinion dissenting in part, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and KENNEDY and BREYER, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports the conduct remand | Independent monopoly maintenance; six direct joins. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Stevens | O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas | Dismisses the existing damages theory | Damages directness; five direct joins. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports the prospective-relief remand | Distinct prospective injury and equitable relief; six direct joins. |
| Partial dissent | Scalia | O'Connor and Thomas | Would dismiss the prospective claim | Inadequate section 2 predicate; all three join Part II. |
| Partial dissent | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Kennedy and Breyer | Would remand the damages inquiry | All four join Parts I and III. |

#### Holdings

##### 1. Regulatory access duties and independent monopolization remain separate.

A regulated firm's breach of an access duty violates Sherman Act section 2 only when the plaintiff independently establishes relevant-market monopoly power, unlawful acquisition or maintenance through an exclusionary competitive mechanism, and causally connected antitrust injury. Deliberate discriminatory degradation of rival-customer access may qualify when it maintains monopoly power and lacks an adequate business justification; regulatory breach, rival injury or exclusionary purpose alone supplies no substitute for those elements.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The saving clause preserves existing antitrust law without enlarging its duties. CSU v. Xerox requires an actual input-exclusion inquiry while giving statutory entitlements and legitimate business explanations substantial force. That separation extends to this regulated setting. The complaint identifies selective impairment of rival-customer orders as the alleged means of making competing service ineffective. Courts must examine that mechanism under ordinary pleading and proof requirements, rather than infer liability from a regulatory finding or end the inquiry because access originated in a regulatory command.

Aspen Skiing illustrates unjustified exclusion; a prior voluntary course of dealing is probative rather than an exclusive element. Kodak requires attention to actual market constraints and conduct. NYNEX distinguishes a regulatory wrong from harm to competition. Technical requirements, investment incentives, efficiency and operational explanations therefore remain material to whether the alleged discrimination constitutes unlawful maintenance. Those requirements answer the contention that the Court has simply converted compulsory assistance into an antitrust supply duty.

A freestanding leveraging label cannot avoid the offense's elements. If an attempt theory is pursued, Spectrum Sports requires anticompetitive conduct, specific intent and a dangerous probability of monopolization in the relevant market. The plaintiff bears the ordinary civil burden. The lower courts must assess the existing allegations and any amendment properly permitted under ordinary rules; an insufficient theory is subject to dismissal through the ordinary procedures.

##### 2. The existing damages theory is insufficiently direct.

A customer claiming damages from interference with its carrier must satisfy Associated General Contractors by establishing a sufficiently direct personal business-or-property injury, assessed through the causal relationship, intervening injury, more direct claimants, duplicate recovery and workable allocation. Trinko's existing theory places its service loss downstream of the carriers' impaired access and service provision, so the damages claim must be dismissed.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas.

Steamfitters applies the directness inquiry to the actual chain of injury rather than a plaintiff's status. The carriers suffer the access impairment and are positioned to seek damages for it. This complaint's consequential customer loss presents the overlapping recovery and allocation concerns that the directness requirement addresses. Alleging that the strategy targeted rival patronage does not itself eliminate the intervening carrier injury.

McCready permits recovery when a plaintiff's own deprivation is the instrument of the exclusionary scheme. The present theory instead derives the customer's service loss from impairment of its carrier's access. The distinction answers the proposed targeted-customer analogy without making noncompetitor or consumer status conclusive. This statutory damages conclusion remains distinct from Article III and from the requirements governing an injunction.

##### 3. Prospective relief requires its own injury and remedy inquiry.

Section 16 permits relief upon a significant personal threat of injury of the kind the antitrust laws protect against, arising from what makes an impending, continuing or likely recurring violation unlawful. A damages-directness defect based on duplicate recovery, allocation or a more direct damages claimant does not by itself defeat that prospective claim; the plaintiff must establish the independent offense, its personal threat and the ordinary equitable grounds for a lawful, effective decree.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Cargill distinguishes threatened antitrust injury from completed business-or-property loss and the additional allocation concerns attending damages. The alleged systematic impairment of rival service warrants an independent examination of the threat to this existing rival-carrier customer. The actual regulatory measures bear on whether a threat persists and whether additional relief is necessary. Their existence establishes neither complete redress nor entitlement to another decree.

The requested access parity cannot follow merely from telecommunications law's sharing duty. Any injunction must target established antitrust harm, respect lawful access terms consistently with the cure, and have a necessary, workable scope. A narrower effective remedy may be considered when supported by the existing claim and ordinary equity. If the independent offense, personal prospective threat or lawful effective remedy is missing, relief must be denied. These requirements resolve the governing legal questions and define the lower courts' remaining application work.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **CSU v. Xerox:** Its separation of statutory entitlements, independent exclusion and substantial justifications is extended to regulated network-input conduct; it supplies no general right to assistance.
- **Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services:** Applied for actual market and conduct analysis rather than categorical economic assumptions.
- **Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp.:** Preserved as an example of unjustified exclusion; its prior-dealing evidence is not made an exclusive gateway.
- **NYNEX Corp. v. Discon:** Applied to distinguish regulatory misconduct and rival injury from the competitive harm the offense requires.
- **Spectrum Sports v. McQuillan:** Applied to preserve the independent conduct, intent and dangerous-probability elements of any attempt theory.
- **Associated General Contractors v. Carpenters and the Steamfitters decision:** Applied to dismiss this consequential damages theory on actual causal directness, claimant priority, duplication and allocation.
- **Blue Shield of Virginia v. McCready:** Distinguished because the present damages theory rests on service loss consequent on the carrier's access impairment, rather than a direct deprivation serving as the exclusionary instrument.
- **Cargill, Inc. v. Monfort of Colorado, Inc.:** Applied independently to threatened antitrust injury and the different operation of prospective relief; damages allocation does not decide section 16.

#### Law After Decision

Regulated input conduct receives the ordinary monopoly-maintenance inquiry, with substantial attention to legitimate legal and operational explanations. Regulatory noncompliance neither proves that offense nor immunizes it. The present damages theory fails the separate directness requirement, while the prospective claim returns for the independent offense, personal-threat and equitable-remedy inquiry. Access parity requires support as a lawful antitrust remedy for established harm, rather than simply as a regulatory obligation.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting in part, joined by O'Connor and Thomas.** The alleged customer diversion is the consequence of deficient compelled assistance, rather than an independently sufficient antitrust wrong. CSU preserves the requirement of such a wrong and the absence of a general duty to assist rivals; NYNEX separates regulatory misconduct from injury to the competitive process. Exclusionary purpose and a sharing command cannot supply the antitrust dealing obligation the complaint assumes. The dissent would dismiss the prospective claim for that conduct defect. It accepts Cargill's distinct equity standard and does not treat the damages ruling as a substitute for the missing offense analysis. All three join the Court's damages holding.

**Ginsburg, dissenting in part, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy and Breyer.** The alleged impairment of end-user orders may itself be the instrument by which Verizon discourages rival patronage. McCready therefore supplies a potentially closer causal model than the remote expense chain in Steamfitters. The Court should remand for assessment of Trinko's actual personal loss, intervening steps, overlap with carrier recovery and workable allocation under Associated General Contractors. The dissent would allow any amendment properly permitted by ordinary rules and dismiss an insufficient theory, while allowing a sufficiently direct independent claim to proceed. It does not find the damages predicates established. All four join the conduct and prospective-relief holdings.

Sherman Act §2, 15 U.S.C. §2; Clayton Act §§4 and 16, 15 U.S.C. §§15, 26; Telecommunications Act §§251, 252 and §601(b)(1); Cargill, 479 U.S. 104, Part II and notes 5–6; Associated General Contractors, 459 U.S. 519; McCready, 457 U.S. 465. The complaint's access-parity prayer appears at App. 49–50. Alleged discriminatory order handling and continuing competitive harm remain allegations at the reviewed pleading stage.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower courts must dismiss the existing damages theory and assess the prospective claim under Parts I and III. That work includes the alleged market and exclusion mechanism, substantial justifications, a significant personal prospective threat, the actual protection supplied by regulatory measures, and the necessity and scope of any effective antitrust remedy. Ordinary pleading and amendment rules govern; a failure of a required predicate permits dismissal or denial at the appropriate stage. Other statutory and state-law claims retain their separate procedural status.

### Frew v. Hawkins, No. 02-628

Merits decision, January 14, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

No. 02-628. Decided January 14, 2004, on merits submission. The Court reviews the Fifth Circuit's reversal of enforcement relief for a federal Medicaid consent decree. The question is whether the Eleventh Amendment bars prospective enforcement against state officials when lawful implementation terms are more detailed than the statute itself.

Texas Medicaid beneficiaries challenged the administration of early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services. After the state agencies were dismissed, the officials entered a detailed consent decree resolving the federal dispute. The district court later found failures to comply and directed corrective measures. The Fifth Circuit required each alleged decree violation to establish a new, independent statutory violation.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The categorical immunity bar is rejected. The decree remains an enforceable federal judicial obligation. The case returns for ordinary review of the actual compliance findings and remedial issues, prospective enforcement consistent with the decree's federal purposes and adjudication of any properly supported modification request under Rule 60(b)(5).

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join both holdings and the complete ordinary remand.

##### 1. Lawful federal implementation terms are prospectively enforceable against the bound officials.

A federal court may enforce a consent decree against state officials when it resolves a dispute within federal subject-matter jurisdiction, stays within the general scope of the pleadings and furthers the governing federal law's objectives. Lawful implementation terms may be more specific than the statute and need not each independently repeat an enforceable statutory command; the party seeking enforcement must establish the operative decree and noncompliance under the applicable standards.

Ex parte Young and Verizon Maryland permit prospective relief against responsible officials enforcing federal obligations. Firefighters recognizes that a consent decree may prescribe lawful relief beyond what a contested liability judgment alone would require. The Medicaid decree meets the federal-origin and federal-purpose requirements here. Once entered, its lawful implementation terms become judicial obligations rather than optional methods officials may discard.

Texas's proposed term-by-term statutory-minimum condition would make those implementation terms unenforceable precisely where they perform their agreed remedial work. Rufo treats an operative decree as binding until lawfully altered. State administrative discretion is protected through the decree's federal scope and lawful modification, rather than by treating accepted details as immune from enforcement. Pennhurst continues to exclude independent state-law commands, and Edelman preserves the boundary against retrospective compensation from the State. This decree and the class's prospective compliance request remain within those boundaries.

##### 2. Modification and termination follow the decree's purposes and Rule 60(b)(5).

Officials seeking modification must prove a significant change in facts or law making continued enforcement inequitable; any modification must be suitably tailored to that change while preserving lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes. Actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden, and dissatisfaction, a new administration or a cheaper lawful alternative alone is insufficient; judicial supervision should end when the decree's obligations and legitimate remedial purposes have been satisfied.

Rufo supplies both the required showing and the tailoring rule. It permits practical adjustment without reducing every negotiated obligation to the bare statutory or constitutional minimum. The decree is a means to secure an effective, durable remedy, so continued federal supervision must remain tied to its obligations and purposes. Completion restores ordinary administrative responsibility; an assertion that completion occurred must be assessed on the actual record. The availability of modification does not permit officials to disregard operative duties while seeking different terms. Enforcement and modification therefore remain distinct judicial tasks under the same continuing decree.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Young:** Applied as the prospective-officer route for enforcing the lawful federal obligation.
- **Verizon Maryland v. Public Service Commission:** Applied for prospective relief against responsible officials and the distinction from retrospective State compensation.
- **Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland:** Applied to consent relief within federal jurisdiction, the pleadings' general scope and the federal law's objectives, including lawful implementation beyond minimum contested relief.
- **Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail:** Applied to the decree's continuing obligation, significant-change and accepted-risk requirements, and tailored modification preserving remaining lawful purposes.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its prohibition on independent state-law enforcement against officials is preserved; these obligations implement the federal dispute.
- **Edelman v. Jordan:** Its retrospective State-compensation limit remains effective; the relief sought here is prospective decree compliance.

#### Law After Decision

Federal implementation decrees meeting the stated jurisdictional, pleading-scope and federal-purpose conditions bind the responsible officials beyond a word-for-word repetition of the underlying statute. Courts review actual compliance findings, enforce the lawful prospective terms and determine corrective relief under the governing standards and the decree's federal purposes. Officials may obtain modification on Rufo's required showing, and unnecessary supervision ends when the obligations and legitimate purposes have been satisfied.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's prospective-enforcement rules, modification framework and complete reversal and remand. He would additionally require a concrete compliance schedule for established failures, evidence of performance and lawful enforcement if officials fail to comply. These directions would follow ordinary review of the actual compliance findings and remedial issues. Because the decree establishes a class-wide obligation, he would require officials proposing a narrower corrective measure to show that it fully cures the proved breach.

The Chief Justice reasons that recognizing an enforceable decree must produce effective implementation of its lawful federal obligations. Verizon Maryland supplies the prospective-officer remedy, Firefighters recognizes lawful implementation beyond a bare statutory minimum and Rufo preserves the operative decree until lawful modification. Those principles support his proposed practical requirements for curing established failures. He would also require the court to decide any supported Rule 60(b)(5) request under Rufo, so enforcement protects the beneficiary class while supervision ends when the decree's obligations and legitimate purposes are satisfied. His position addresses the officials bound by this decree and its covered beneficiaries, with enforcement tailored to its federal purposes.

42 U.S.C. §§1396a(a)(43), 1396d(r); Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 60(b)(5) and 65; Eleventh Amendment; Firefighters, 478 U.S. 501; Young, 209 U.S. 123; Pennhurst, 465 U.S. 89; Edelman, 415 U.S. 651. The decree resolves a federal dispute and furthers the Medicaid program's objectives. Existing compliance findings and the corresponding corrective measures remain subject to ordinary lower-court review.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower courts must review the actual compliance findings and remedial issues under their ordinary standards, determine appropriate corrective measures for established noncompliance and enforce the decree's lawful prospective obligations. They must also decide any properly supported Rule 60(b)(5) request under the significant-change and tailoring requirements. The decree remains operative until lawfully altered or its satisfied obligations and legitimate purposes warrant termination.

### Kontrick v. Ryan, No. 02-819

Merits decision, January 14, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

No. 02-819. Decided January 14, 2004, on merits submission. The Court reviews the Seventh Circuit's affirmance of denial of discharge and rejection of Kontrick's postmerits timing objection. The question is whether Bankruptcy Rules 4004 and 9006 impose a subject-matter jurisdictional limit that survives an adverse merits adjudication, or a procedural defense forfeited by failing to invoke it before that adjudication.

Ryan amended his objection to discharge to allege fraudulent family-account transfers after extensions of the filing deadline and without a further extension. Kontrick answered the theory and contested summary judgment on the merits. Only after the court decided the claim against him did he assert untimeliness. The amendment's untimeliness is conceded for the question before the Court.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The postmerits timing defense is forfeited. The adjudicated denial of discharge remains in force.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the rule, application and affirmance.

**A debtor forfeits this filing defense by first raising it after the claim is adjudicated on the merits.** Bankruptcy Rules 4004 and 9006 regulate claim processing and enlargement rather than subject-matter jurisdiction; a debtor who litigates an objection to discharge through an adverse merits determination before raising untimeliness forfeits that defense. The creditor retains the burden to prove the discharge objection, and the rules' restrictions continue to govern when the debtor timely invokes them; forfeiture supplies no power to grant an extension the rules forbid over a timely objection.

Congress conferred jurisdiction over bankruptcy proceedings through 28 U.S.C. §§1334 and 157, including objections to discharge under §157(b)(2)(J). Section 2075 and Rule 9030 prevent procedural rules from enlarging or restricting that jurisdiction. Rule 4004 generally requires an objection within sixty days after the first date set for the creditors' meeting and permits an extension for cause on a motion filed before the period expires. Rule 9006(b)(3) confines enlargement to the conditions Rule 4004 permits. Those provisions regulate the orderly assertion of claims and defenses; they do not make an omitted procedural defense a perpetual challenge to statutory competence.

Carlisle enforced a timely invoked national-rule deadline while distinguishing remedial authority from jurisdiction and leaving forfeiture open. Henderson likewise examines the source, placement and function of a timing command. Here the separate jurisdictional statutes and procedural rules make the distinction clear. Kontrick's strict-enlargement argument concerns the court's authority to extend a deadline over a timely objection, which differs from a debtor's ability to insist on an unraised defense after losing on the merits. He answered the family-account theory and litigated it through judgment. That conduct forfeited the timing defense without a finding that he intentionally surrendered it or an equitable extension of the deadline.

Equitable tolling, relation back and the precise final point for asserting the defense before merits adjudication lie outside the accepted postmerits question. The rule decided here resolves Kontrick's attempt to undo the completed merits determination.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Carlisle v. United States:** Applied for the distinction between mandatory procedural limits and subject-matter jurisdiction. The present decision resolves postmerits forfeiture of this bankruptcy defense while preserving Carlisle's timely-objection holding.
- **Henderson v. United States:** Applied for classification of a timing command through its source, placement and function rather than a jurisdictional label; the different statutory timetable at issue there retains its own treatment.

#### Law After Decision

Bankruptcy Rules 4004 and 9006 remain mandatory procedural prescriptions, but their protection is lost when a debtor first invokes untimeliness after adjudication of the discharge objection's merits. Statutory subject-matter jurisdiction survives that filing defect, and a timely invoked objection continues to receive the rules' prescribed treatment.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§1334, 157(b)(2)(J), 2075; Bankruptcy Rules 4004(a), 4004(b), 9006(b)(3), 9030. Untimeliness is conceded for review. The debtor litigated the family-account objection through an adverse merits determination before asserting the defense.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The denial of discharge stands, and the bankruptcy court may continue administering the estate with that judgment in force.

**End of entry: Kontrick v. Ryan, merits decision, January 14, 2004.**

### Rubin v. City of Santa Monica

Merits decision, January 15, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below, 308 F.3d 1008. Decided January 15, 2004, on merits submission. The Court reviews dismissal of the municipal speech and equal-protection challenge to a neutral rule restricting an optional ballot designation to specified office or principal professional, vocational or occupational identification. Rubin seeks declaratory and prospective relief permitting the designation peace activist.

Rubin qualified for the 2000 City Council ballot. The City declined his proposed generic status designation under its adopted rule, which includes activist among the excluded status labels. The field permits up to three words identifying the current principal profession, vocation or occupation or the principal one during the preceding calendar year. Candidates may submit a separately distributed statement of up to 200 words at city expense. The Court accepts the pleaded allegation that activism supplies Rubin's livelihood. The short election cycle and intended recurrence preserve the prospective controversy after the completed election.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed on the municipal speech and equal-protection claims, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested declaratory and prospective relief requiring the disputed designation is denied. The Secretary of State's separate dismissal remains undisturbed outside the granted municipal question. The completed election remains closed.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the granted-question rule, application and prospective disposition.

**A neutral generic-status exclusion may govern this optional identification field.** A government may reserve an optional ballot designation for defined official or occupational identification and exclude generic political status descriptions when the consistently applied rule is viewpoint neutral, imposes an ordinary burden and reasonably serves concise, intelligible and nonmisleading identification. The government must identify that purpose and fit; an otherwise qualifying profession may not be rejected merely because of the cause it advances. The challenged rule satisfies those conditions and defeats the presented speech and municipal equal-protection claims.

Burdick requires assessment of the actual burden against the precise regulatory interests and their fit. Cartwright identifies the complete scale: severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; substantial burdens require an important objective and a demonstrated substantial relationship to the restriction's actual reach; reasonable, nondiscriminatory ordinary burdens may be justified by important regulatory interests. The burden here is ordinary. Rubin remains a candidate, the field supplies optional identifying information, and the restriction withholds no genuine nomination or truthful party designation.

The classification is sustained on its actual function. Activist is a broad status describing many different activities, and the exclusion applies across causes. Accepting Rubin's allegation that activism is his livelihood does not require every general description of that livelihood to appear in a field reserved for more specific identification. Officials must apply that distinction without disfavoring peace advocacy or another viewpoint. The separate candidate statement reduces the practical burden but could not cure viewpoint discrimination.

Timmons and Schrader protect meaningful recognition of an actual party nomination or truthful party identity; other campaign opportunities alone cannot replace that recognition. This rule addresses a different field and supplies a reasonable identifying distinction. Van Susteren confirms that actual ordinary burdens receive meaningful, proportionate review. The same neutral functional distinction and burden assessment defeat the claim of unequal treatment between a generic status and qualifying office or occupational descriptions. Cook's prohibition on congressional-ballot policy penalties rests on a distinct constitutional source; this municipal rule imposes no comparable ideological penalty under the First and Fourteenth Amendment inquiry.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Burdick v. Takushi:** Applied for actual burden, precise interests and fit within the full ballot system.
- **Cartwright v. Barnes:** Applied at all three levels of its calibrated scale; this restriction falls at the ordinary level on its actual operation.
- **Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party:** Distinguished because the rule withholds no official recognition of a genuine party nomination; the meaningful-recognition holding remains in force.
- **Schrader v. Blackwell:** Distinguished because an optional generic status does not establish the truthful party identity protected there; its recognition rule and its rejection of alternative speech as an automatic substitute remain effective.
- **Van Susteren v. Jones:** Applied for meaningful review of an ordinary burden supported by an actual regulatory connection, while its primary-entry condition remains distinct from this designation rule.
- **Cook v. Gralike:** Distinguished at its congressional Elections Clause source and official policy-penalty scope; it supplies no authority for viewpoint-selective municipal administration.

#### Law After Decision

Election officials may apply the reviewed neutral status rule to the optional designation field while permitting qualifying office and occupational identification. The rule's identifying function, actual ordinary burden and consistent treatment across viewpoints supply its constitutional justification. An official's rejection of a qualifying profession because of its cause would fail the neutrality condition of this rule.

#### Source Notes

Rubin v. City of Santa Monica, 308 F.3d 1008; California Elections Code §13107 and implementing designation regulations; U.S. Const., Amdts. 1 and 14. California's regulation expressly excludes municipal elections; Santa Monica adopted the rule independently. The Secretary's separate dismissal lies outside the granted municipal constitutional question. Rubin's pleaded livelihood facts are accepted at the dismissal stage.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The municipal defense judgment stands, and officials may administer the sustained designation rule in future elections. Rubin retains the available candidate statement and ordinary campaign channels. The separate Secretary-of-State dismissal is undisturbed.

### Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation v. EPA, No. 02-658

Merits decision, January 21, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 8, 2003; decided January 21, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 298 F.3d 814. The questions are whether the Clean Air Act authorizes EPA to prevent construction when a state BACT determination lacks a reasoned statutory basis and whether EPA lawfully exercised that authority for the Red Dog Mine's MG-17 generator. Alaska initially identified SCR as feasible, then approved a different choice on an offset rationale and later economic reasoning.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The operative EPA construction restrictions remain effective according to their actual modified terms, including the March allowance for summer-dependent construction; the withdrawn December direction is not revived. The July 16, 2003 permit already selects SCR as BACT, subject to a decision in Alaska's favor in this case. Affirmance leaves that condition unsatisfied, so the permit's SCR term remains operative. A reasoned reconsideration through ordinary lawful agency procedures remains available; the judgment requires no fresh permit proceeding.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SOUTER, and BREYER, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** The six-Justice opinion of the Court identified above; the same coalition directly joins both rules, their explanations, application and remedy.

##### 1. EPA may enforce the substantive BACT requirement within the State's reasoned decisional range.

Sections 113(a)(5) and 167 authorize EPA to prevent construction that fails the Clean Air Act's prevention-of-significant-deterioration requirements, including section 165(a)(4)'s best-available-control-technology requirement. EPA may intervene when a state BACT determination lacks a reasoned basis under section 169(3)'s maximum-achievable-reduction standard and its energy, environmental, economic and other-cost factors, but may not replace a supported state choice merely because it prefers another permissible technology.

The Court reads the state assignment and federal enforcement provisions together. The permitting authority makes the case-specific determination, while the separately enacted federal construction provisions enforce substantive compliance. A permit bearing the BACT label cannot conclusively establish compliance with the requirement it must satisfy. EPA must identify and demonstrate the actual defect; the State retains the permissible range of reasoned choice. That limit answers the objection that federal enforcement turns EPA into an unrestricted appellate permitting authority. State review does not displace the federal enforcement channels Congress supplied.

Mead and Christensen distinguish statutory authority from an agency's persuasive advice. Walton and Heimmermann require an actual grant and its authorized exercise. The Court independently identifies the enforcement power in this Act; EPA's experience may inform persuasion but creates no power of its own. Bragg likewise preserves a retained federal command after state primacy without converting every discretionary state judgment into a nondiscretionary federal duty.

##### 2. EPA reasonably identified the defect in Alaska's explanation.

Review under 5 U.S.C. §706(2)(A) sustains EPA's orders when the administrative record supports its conclusion that the State's BACT explanation falls outside the reasoned statutory range. Alaska's abandoned offset rationale and unsupported economic reversal meet that standard here; the operative federal orders are sustained as modified.

Alaska initially found selective catalytic reduction technically and economically feasible. It then abandoned the offset theory supporting a different choice and invoked costs and competitiveness while acknowledging that it lacked the information necessary to assess those consequences. A rural-utility analogy and the mine's regional importance did not establish the asserted effect on this mine. The defect is the missing connection in the State's actual reasoning. Costs remain a statutory consideration, and the top-down method was Alaska's chosen method rather than a universal statutory command.

EPA's path is reasonably discernible from its orders and explanatory correspondence. The court reviews that path rather than supplying reasons or selecting technology. The State's acknowledged information gap does not automatically require one control technology whenever a financial statement is absent. The required reasoned explanation preserves the State's role while giving the substantive federal requirement effect.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Mead and Christensen v. Harris County:** Applied to distinguish the independent identification of statutory power from persuasive agency reasoning.
- **Barnhart v. Walton and Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.:** Applied for actual delegation and authorized exercise; expertise and uncertainty do not confer power.
- **Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association:** Applied for the survival of an identified federal permitting command after state primacy; its nondiscretionary-duty holding does not establish federal review of every state judgment.
- **INS v. Ventura:** Its allocation of initial administrative judgment remains effective; judicial review identifies the statutory defect while the responsible agency makes any subsequent BACT determination.

#### Law After Decision

EPA may enforce the requirement that a state BACT determination have a reasoned statutory basis. The State retains its case-specific weighing within the statutory range, and federal intervention requires an identified, record-supported deficiency. This application sustains the actual modified orders and leaves the existing conditional permit operative under its own terms.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The Act specifically assigns BACT's case-specific weighing to the state permitting authority. The dissent reads the federal prevention provisions to enforce required permits, required consideration and independently specified federal conditions, but not to create a federal merits-review channel over the completed discretionary balance. A reasonableness limit describes how review would operate without establishing that Congress authorized EPA to conduct it. Weak state reasons therefore cannot themselves create that power. The dissent would reverse the federal interference on the allocation ground, giving effect to the July permit's Alaska-success condition according to its terms; it does not need to choose a superior technology.

#### Source Notes

Clean Air Act §§113(a)(5), 165(a)(4), 167, 169(3), 42 U.S.C. §§7413(a)(5), 7475(a)(4), 7477, 7479(3); 5 U.S.C. §706(2)(A); 298 F.3d 814. ADEC Air Quality Construction Permit No. 9932-AC005, Revision 2 (July 16, 2003), p. 7; Solicitor General's letter of August 21, 2003. The operative permit makes the SCR condition depend on a decision in Alaska's favor.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The mandate leaves the reviewed federal enforcement result in force as actually modified. The mine operator must comply with operative construction limits and the existing July permit. Any later state reconsideration must supply a reasoned BACT determination under all statutory factors, and EPA must assess that determination within the bounded enforcement authority stated above.

### Freier v. Westinghouse Electric Corp., Second Circuit Nos. 00-7724(L) and 00-7728(XAP)

Merits decision, January 22, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided January 22, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 303 F.3d 176, Nos. 00-7724(L) and 00-7728(XAP). The questions are whether the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses authorize CERCLA section 309, 42 U.S.C. §9658, to displace earlier state limitations commencement dates for covered toxic-exposure claims, and whether requiring state courts to apply that rule violates the Tenth Amendment.

The consolidated suits allege injury and death from industrial waste deposited at the Pfohl landfill near Buffalo. The Second Circuit rejected the constitutional objections and vacated limitations summary judgment because discovery-of-cause facts remained disputed.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed in the reviewed constitutional respects, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The defendants' request to invalidate section 9658 and restore a limitations dismissal on that ground is denied. The Second Circuit's separate vacatur of limitations summary judgment and discovery-of-cause remand remain in force.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly adopt both constitutional grounds, the statutory qualifications stated here and the limited disposition.

##### 1. The federal commencement rule is a valid means of regulating commercial hazardous-waste activity.

Congress may displace an earlier state limitations commencement date for covered hazardous-substance exposure claims when that displacement is reasonably adapted to preserving remedies for latent harm within its valid regulation of commercial hazardous-waste generation, handling, disposal and response. Section 9658 satisfies that requirement because premature commencement can defeat exposure-based liability before causal discovery and thereby impair the cost allocation and preventive and remedial incentives of the regulated waste activity.

Lopez requires a real constitutional connection before the magnitude of downstream consequences can support federal power. Home Builders requires identification of an independently valid interstate regime, the actual covered class and a genuine functional relationship. Those requirements are met by commercial hazardous-waste regulation and the specific latent-exposure mechanism. The timing rule keeps remedies meaningful when injury and its cause may emerge only after a state's ordinary commencement date. Its source is that adapted relationship, not environmental importance or a statutory label.

Guillen shows that a confined litigation rule can implement an identified interstate program. Jinks likewise confirms that a claim-linked federal timing rule can be necessary and proper, though its source is authorized federal adjudication rather than the commerce power employed here. A separate federal cleanup remedy does not constitutionally foreclose additional adapted means. The defendants' procedural characterization therefore does not require restoring the earlier limitations dismissal. Courts independently decide the legal connection while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about adapted means; the Constitution requires neither indispensability nor the least restrictive method.

**Statutory operation.** Section 9658 applies to state-law personal-injury or property-damage actions caused or contributed to by exposure to a hazardous substance, pollutant or contaminant released into the environment from a facility. It substitutes the federal commencement date only when state law supplies an earlier date; otherwise state limitations law governs. Actions under section 9607 are excluded. The ordinary federal date is when the plaintiff knew or reasonably should have known that the injury or damage was caused or contributed to by the substance concerned. The party invoking the section must establish its applicable predicates under ordinary civil rules. Paragraph (b)(4)(B) states the following distinct qualifications:

> (B) Special rules
> In the case of a minor or incompetent plaintiff, the term “federally required commencement date” means the later of the date referred to in subparagraph (A) or the following:
> (i) In the case of a minor, the date on which the minor reaches the age of majority, as determined by State law, or has a legal representative appointed.
> (ii) In the case of an incompetent individual, the date on which such individual becomes competent or has had a legal representative appointed.

The underlying tort elements and the otherwise applicable period remain operative. These express statutory conditions define the upheld rule; the separate application questions remain governed by the Second Circuit's existing remand.

##### 2. State judges must apply the valid federal commencement rule.

Article VI binds state courts adjudicating covered private claims to section 9658's valid federal rule of decision even though the cause of action arises under state law. That judicial obligation does not commandeer state legislatures or executive officers to establish or administer a federal regulatory program.

Testa supplies the duty of competent state courts to apply controlling federal law. New York and Printz distinguish that duty from compelled political-branch administration. Section 9658 changes governing law in an existing judicial action; it does not demand a new state code or field-enforcement program. Calling commencement procedural cannot create an exception to the Supremacy Clause. Jinks directly confirms this distinction for a valid federal timing rule, while requiring the distinct enumerated-power source established in the first holding here. Guillen's reserved Tenth Amendment question supplies no contrary holding. Article VI follows the valid exercise of enumerated power; it is not itself a free-standing source of regulatory power.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied to require an identifiable constitutional connection before aggregate consequences can matter.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied to the independent commercial-waste regime, covered exposure class and functional latency-remedy relationship.
- **Pierce County v. Guillen:** Applied for confined litigation means fitted to a valid interstate mechanism; its separate Tenth Amendment reservation remains a reservation.
- **Jinks v. Richland County:** Applied for adapted, claim-linked federal timing rules and state judicial application of valid federal law; its federal-adjudication source remains distinct.
- **Testa v. Katt:** Applied for the duty of competent state courts to apply controlling federal law.
- **New York v. United States and Printz v. United States:** Distinguished at their compulsory legislative and executive administration grounds, which preserve the separate judicial duty.

#### Law After Decision

Section 9658 validly supplies its enacted causal-discovery commencement floor for covered industrial toxic-exposure claims. Its functional relationship to commercial hazardous-waste regulation supplies federal power, and state courts must apply it under Article VI. Each claimant must establish the statutory predicates; tort liability and the remaining limitations rules retain their own requirements.

#### Source Notes

42 U.S.C. §9658(a), (b)(4); Pub. L. 99–499, §203, 100 Stat. 1695; 303 F.3d 176; U.S. Const., Art. I, §8, Art. VI, and Amdt. 10. The quoted minor and incompetence qualifications are the text of section 9658(b)(4)(B). The suits present allegations of toxic injury and disputed causal-discovery facts.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must carry out the existing remand by applying the valid federal commencement rule to the disputed discovery-of-cause evidence and adjudicating surviving claims and preserved defenses. A covered claim cannot be dismissed using an earlier state commencement date displaced by section 9658.

### Fellers v. United States, No. 02-6320

Merits decision, January 26, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 10, 2003; decided January 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. The questions are whether officers deliberately elicited statements at Fellers's home about an indicted charge without counsel or waiver, and whether the later warned jail statements require a Sixth Amendment derivative-evidence inquiry.

The same officers discussed the charged methamphetamine conspiracy at the home, then obtained warnings, a signed waiver and further admissions at the jail, including repetition of the earlier account. The district court excluded the home statements but admitted the jail statements, and the Court of Appeals affirmed.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The admission-based affirmance is set aside. The original exclusion of the home statements remains in force. The lower courts must apply the causal-independence rule and the conditional exclusion, harmlessness and new-trial directions stated in Holding 2. Scalia and Thomas agree with reversal and remand on the erroneous Sixth Amendment framework while withholding the new causal rule.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined the initial-violation holding and concurred in the judgment. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court: initial violation | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Corrects the lower court's Sixth Amendment framework | Initial deliberate-elicitation violation and framework error. |
| Opinion of the Court: derivative statements | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg | Defines causal inquiry and conditional remand | Causal-independence rule, preponderance standard and conditional remedy. |
| Opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Agrees with reversal and remand | Joins the initial-violation ruling without adopting the new causal rule. |

#### Holdings

##### 1. The home discussion violated the attached right to counsel.

Once prosecution has begun, officers may not deliberately elicit incriminating statements about the charged crime without counsel or a valid waiver of the Sixth Amendment right. The officers' directed discussion of Fellers's indicted methamphetamine conspiracy deliberately elicited his admissions; the absence of custodial interrogation under Miranda does not answer that distinct constitutional question.

**Authority:** This portion of Breyer's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; the rule, application and correction of the lower court's Fifth-Amendment-only analysis have nine direct votes.

Massiah and Brewer protect the accused against deliberate elicitation after the right attaches. The officers came to discuss the charged distribution and named alleged associates, inviting the incriminating response they obtained. Permission to enter the home did not waive the attached right. Patterson permits a genuinely valid waiver, but no valid home waiver is established. Cobb preserves the independent Sixth Amendment inquiry; this conversation concerned the indicted conspiracy itself, so its broader inseparable-occurrence boundary does no additional work. The lower court's interrogation label therefore cannot sustain its treatment of the home encounter.

##### 2. Later warnings and waiver do not alone establish causal independence.

After the defendant establishes deliberate elicitation violating the attached right and a causal connection to a later statement, the Government must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the later statement resulted from a sufficiently independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of the violation. The court considers timing and continuity, genuine intervening circumstances, the purpose and character of the unlawful elicitation, actual use of the first account, and later warnings and a knowing, voluntary waiver; the Government must show independence is more likely than not.

**Authority:** Breyer's causal-independence rule, proof standard and defined remand have seven direct supporters. That coalition consists of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas join the judgment without this rationale.

Massiah protects the represented criminal process independently of compelled speech, and Wade recognizes derivative consequences of a Sixth Amendment violation. Brown separates voluntary speech from exploitation of an actual constitutional wrong; Kaupp applies that distinction to unlawful arrest. The Court extends the causal principle to deliberate elicitation. Patterson gives later waiver real force, but it does not make that waiver an answer to derivation. Elstad addresses a voluntary statement following an earlier warning omission, and Dickerson preserves that setting. A waiver-only extension would erase the distinct consequences of the completed violation here. The rule instead permits genuinely independent later speech while requiring proof that it was not obtained by exploitation.

Nix, Matlock and Lego support the ordinary preponderance standard for admissibility facts. Wade's clear-and-convincing standard addresses the special reconstruction difficulty when an uncounseled lineup fixes an identification in memory. The present inquiry examines the actual interview sequence, conduct, warnings and intervening events, so that distinctive identification concern does not require a heightened quantum here. An evenly balanced showing fails the Government's burden.

The short sequence, same officers and repetition of the home account require the separate causal inquiry; the lower courts must determine independence on the relevant record. The home statements remain excluded. If the Government fails to prove independence, the jail statements must also be excluded. A properly preserved harmless-error contention requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt that their admission did not contribute to the actual verdict; otherwise the conviction must be vacated and a new trial may proceed with independently admissible evidence. These are factual and remedial determinations under the announced rule, which governs this case and similarly situated pending direct-review cases.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Massiah v. United States and Brewer v. Williams:** Applied to directed elicitation concerning the indicted conspiracy without counsel or waiver.
- **Texas v. Cobb:** Applied for the distinct attached-right inquiry; its inseparable-occurrence extension is unnecessary for questioning about the indicted charge itself.
- **Patterson v. Illinois:** Preserved for genuinely valid later waiver, which remains distinct from exploitation of an earlier violation.
- **Oregon v. Elstad and Dickerson v. United States:** Distinguished at the voluntary unwarned-statement setting; later warnings alone do not answer fruits of this completed Sixth Amendment violation.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Its distinction between voluntariness and causal attenuation is extended to deliberate elicitation after attachment.
- **Kaupp v. Texas:** Its unlawful-arrest application supports the causal distinction without itself deciding this Sixth Amendment application.
- **United States v. Wade:** Applied for derivative consequences of a Sixth Amendment violation; its heightened identification-proof rule is distinguished by the reconstruction problem of an uncounseled lineup.
- **Nix v. Williams, United States v. Matlock and Lego v. Twomey:** Applied for preponderance as the ordinary suppression-fact standard; Nix's inevitable-discovery setting is distinct from the actual-statement inquiry here.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applied to any properly preserved claim that erroneous admission was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt on the actual trial record.

#### Law After Decision

Causally connected later statements following deliberate elicitation in violation of the attached right require proof of genuine independence, even after valid warnings and waiver. The Government bears preponderance on that admissibility question; any properly preserved constitutional harmlessness claim carries the separate beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. The legal rule governs pending direct-review cases, while lower courts determine the actual causal sequence and verdict consequence under those standards.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** The officers violated the attached right, and the lower court's reliance on the absence of Miranda interrogation and a Fifth-Amendment-only fruits analysis requires reversal and remand. Patterson nevertheless gives a later knowing and voluntary waiver substantial independent significance. Brown and Kaupp concern exploitation of unlawful custody, while Fellers was arrested on a warrant. The concurrence does not accept that every later voluntary, fully warned statement following this kind of Sixth Amendment violation must satisfy the Court's additional causal formula and proof component. It agrees that the lower framework cannot stand without adopting either an immediate affirmance under an extended Elstad rule or the Court's new attenuation rule.

The lower courts have not made the causal-independence and conditional harmlessness determinations under the governing Sixth Amendment rule.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the lower courts to decide causal independence and, if necessary and properly preserved, harmlessness under Holding 2. The mandate requires exclusion when independence is not proved and relief from the conviction when the resulting constitutional error is not shown harmless. Any new trial proceeds with independently admissible evidence.

**End of entry: Fellers v. United States, merits decision, January 26, 2004.**

### Lamie v. United States Trustee, No. 02-693

Merits decision, January 26, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 10, 2003; decided January 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. The question is whether amended 11 U.S.C. §330(a)(1) authorizes estate compensation for a Chapter 7 debtor's attorney who was not employed by the trustee with court approval under section 327.

Lamie represented the debtor during Chapter 11 and continued legal services after conversion to Chapter 7, without the required section 327 employment. The Fourth Circuit affirmed denial of compensation for those postconversion services.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The denial of the requested postconversion compensation from the Chapter 7 estate under section 330(a)(1) remains in force.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment, in which SOUTER and BREYER, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirms denial of compensation | Full opinion: recipient rule and sufficient textual-and-structural explanation. |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | Souter and Breyer | Expressly adopts the recipient rule and affirmance | Additional drafting-error inquiry; Stevens does not formally join the Court's opinion. Souter and Breyer join both writings. |

#### Holdings

**A Chapter 7 debtor's lawyer must qualify under the enacted compensation authorization.** Section 330(a)(1) does not authorize compensation from a Chapter 7 estate for the debtor's attorney unless the attorney qualifies as a professional employed under section 327 with the required approval. Eligibility precedes valuation of services, and Lamie's acknowledged lack of section 327 employment defeats this application; the distinct provision permitting reasonable compensation to an individual Chapter 12 or Chapter 13 debtor's attorney under section 330(a)(4)(B) retains its own operation.

**Authority:** The eight Justices identified above join the Court's opinion in full. Stevens's concurrence in the judgment expressly adopts the recipient-list operative rule, giving that rule nine direct supporters without his formal join to the Court's opinion. Souter and Breyer join both writings.

The recipient list and the description of compensable services perform different tasks. The amended list identifies the persons entitled to an award; the retained reference to an attorney in the services clause describes work performed by an eligible professional. Treating that reference as an independent entitlement would reinsert the category removed from the list. The missing conjunction and imperfect parallelism leave a workable authorization because an employed attorney may be a professional person.

Hartford Underwriters requires the statutory claimant entitlement before recovery, and Patterson v. Shumate enforces the Code's operative language. Dewsnup's confined contextual reading of lien language does not require adding a recipient to this provision. Lamie's drafting-error contention identifies real textual awkwardness, but the proposed restoration would change the functioning recipient rule rather than resolve language that cannot operate as enacted. Trustee employment provides a Chapter 7 compensation route, and section 330(a)(4)(B) separately addresses individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 debtors. The usefulness of services cannot supply an omitted estate-payment entitlement. Lamie's continued service after conversion did not itself supply the section 327 employment that this route requires.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank:** Applied to require an enacted claimant entitlement before a bankruptcy recovery; service value cannot substitute for eligibility.
- **Patterson v. Shumate:** Applied for enforcement of the operative Bankruptcy Code provision.
- **Dewsnup v. Timm:** Distinguished as a confined contextual construction of lien language; it does not require restoring this omitted recipient category.

#### Law After Decision

Under the amended section 330(a)(1), Chapter 7 debtor's counsel seeking payment from the estate must establish the required section 327 employment before a court evaluates compensation. Continued representation after conversion supplies no independent eligibility. The distinct individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 authorization remains effective under its own terms.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment, joined by Souter and Breyer.** A plausible claim that a significant deletion resulted from a drafting error calls for examination of legislative history rather than treating the existing words alone as conclusive. The recipient-list reading excluding this unretained Chapter 7 attorney is the more natural construction, and the competing drafting-error arguments presented do not justify restoring the deleted category. The concurrence expressly adopts that operative rule and agrees with affirmance, while declining the view that text and structure alone complete the inquiry in this circumstance. Souter and Breyer accept this additional caution while joining the Court's opinion in full.

#### Source Notes

11 U.S.C. §§327, 330(a)(1), 330(a)(4)(B); the 1994 amendment governing the claimed services; No. 02-693. The absence of employment under section 327 is acknowledged. The question concerns compensation from the Chapter 7 estate through section 330(a)(1).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The bankruptcy court must leave the requested estate compensation denied under this application. The ordinary mandate implements the affirmed eligibility determination.

**End of entry: Lamie v. United States Trustee, merits decision, January 26, 2004.**

### In re Application of the United States for an Order Authorizing the Roving Interception of Oral Communications, Ninth Circuit No. 02-15635

Merits decision, January 27, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided January 27, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment, No. 02-15635, 349 F.3d 1132. The question is whether 18 U.S.C. §2518(4) authorizes compelled activation of a vehicle communications system for interception when that use disables its contracted assistance services.

The thirty-day assistance orders expired during review. The Government anticipates similar requests involving the same provider. The provider's identity and protected investigative details remain sealed.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The invalidation of the expired assistance orders remains effective. Those orders supply no continuing authority. The statutory rule governs the recurring assistance dispute involving this provider.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join reviewability, provider coverage, the service-interference rule and its application.

##### 1. The expired orders remain reviewable.

A challenged action remains reviewable when its duration is too short for full litigation before it ends and there is a reasonable expectation that the same challenger will again face it. The thirty-day assistance orders satisfy the duration requirement, and the Government's anticipated further requests involving this provider establish the required recurrence.

The short authorization period prevents ordinary appellate review before expiration, and the Government's stated expectation concerns this provider rather than possible orders against strangers. Both conditions are therefore established on the actual controversy. Expiration does not remove the Court's authority to resolve the recurring statutory dispute.

##### 2. Necessary technical assistance must preserve the statutory minimum-interference limit.

Section 2518(4) permits a court to require a covered provider's necessary technical assistance only when performed unobtrusively and with a minimum of interference with the services furnished to the intercepted person. The applicant must support the proposed method and its actual service effects; the least disruptive feasible technique still exceeds that authority when it materially disables the primary contracted assistance service instead of producing incidental interruption.

The provider supplies an electronic communications link and falls within the statutory assistance class under section 2510(15). Coverage establishes who may be directed, while section 2518(4) separately limits how assistance is performed. Hartford Underwriters requires both the enacted grant and its conditions to receive effect. Reading minimum interference as whatever disruption the preferred technique requires would consume the restriction whenever technology makes it consequential.

While interception operated, the ordinary information and roadside-assistance functions ceased working. An emergency button or airbag event sent a tone over the investigators' open line instead of contacting the provider's normal emergency response. An unassured signal to investigators is not continued availability of the contracted service. The loss of that availability establishes the relevant interference without requiring an attempted emergency call or a failed rescue.

The district court's finding of no unreasonable burden on the provider concerns a different object from the customer's loss of service. Reimbursement of provider costs and section 2518(5)'s minimization of intercepted conversations likewise cannot substitute for compliance with section 2518(4). New York Telephone's ancillary assistance authority does not override the specific service constraint. Bach permits technically necessary execution within a valid warrant's authorization; it does not enlarge this statutory assistance power. Ordinary incidental interruption remains governed by the enacted minimum-interference standard. The challenged method crosses that limit, so the order cannot stand.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank:** Applied for reading an enacted authorization with its independent limits.
- **United States v. New York Telephone Co.:** Distinguished at its ancillary-assistance setting; it does not override section 2518(4)'s express customer-service constraint.
- **United States v. Bach:** Distinguished as technically necessary execution within valid warrant authority; the authorization and execution conditions remain distinct here.

#### Law After Decision

Courts may compel covered providers to give necessary technical assistance under section 2518(4), subject to its substantive protection of customer services. Technical necessity and the lack of a more convenient technique cannot authorize material disabling of the primary assistance service. A later application must establish compliance on the actual operational record, including effects on service availability.

#### Source Notes

18 U.S.C. §§2510(15), 2518(4), 2518(5); 349 F.3d 1132 (Nov. 18, 2003), Ninth Circuit No. 02-15635. The interference determination concerns the availability of the contracted service, not a finding that an emergency call was attempted. Protected provider and target details remain sealed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The mandate preserves the appellate invalidation. A later Government request must independently satisfy section 2518(4)'s assistance and service-interference conditions on the technology and facts then presented.

**End of entry: In re Application of the United States for an Order Authorizing the Roving Interception of Oral Communications, merits decision, January 27, 2004.**

### United States v. Carnes, Sixth Circuit No. 00-2103

Merits decision, February 9, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided February 9, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the Sixth Circuit's judgment, No. 00-2103, 309 F.3d 950. The granted question is whether federal power to protect interstate instrumentalities permits application of 18 U.S.C. §2511 to unauthorized interception of telephone calls whose endpoints are within one State.

Recordings were obtained through a wiretap installed on telephone lines at Carnes's girlfriend's residence. The Court of Appeals rejected the commerce objection but independently reversed the wiretap conviction on suppression grounds and directed a new trial.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed in the reviewed constitutional respect, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Dismissal of the wiretap count on the Commerce Clause ground is denied. The independent suppression-based reversal of that conviction, new-trial direction and associated sentencing mandate remain in force; this judgment does not reinstate the reversed conviction. Other components retain their existing appellate mandate.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Congress may protect covered calls carried by an interstate telephone network.** Congress may prohibit unauthorized interception of covered telephone communications carried through an interstate communications system even when the particular call begins and ends in one State. Direct interference with that instrumentality supplies the constitutional connection; the prosecution must prove the statutory interception and network-related predicates beyond a reasonable doubt, but need not prove an additional interstate endpoint or a substantial individual economic effect.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly support the instrumentality rule, its application and the limited disposition.

Lopez preserves Congress's power to protect actual channels and instrumentalities of interstate commerce. Harris applies that power to a direct intrusion upon an identified transportation instrumentality, and Guillen confirms that a confined protective mechanism need not be recast as a substantial-effects case. Here the conduct is interception of communications actually carried by the telephone system. Local endpoints do not sever that direct relationship, and physical damage to the network is unnecessary to its protection.

Holston distinguishes a statutory reference to equipment's past interstate movement from an adequate constitutional connection. Carnes's case rests on the communication's actual use of the network, not the recorder's remote origin. Home Builders supplies additional means for different regulated classes; those routes do not displace the direct-instrumentality ground. The statutory definition, intentional-interception requirement, exceptions and criminal proof remain independently operative. The local-call objection therefore supplies no constitutional basis to dismiss the reviewed wiretap count.

The independent suppression reversal, new-trial direction on the wiretap count and associated sentencing mandate remain effective. The Court's rejection of the commerce objection does not reinstate the reversed conviction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied for protection of actual interstate instrumentalities and identification of a real constitutional connection.
- **United States v. Harris:** Applied for direct protection of an identified instrumentality even against intrastate conduct.
- **Pierce County v. Guillen:** Applied for a fitted protective mechanism without requiring a substantial-effects showing for each instance.
- **United States v. Holston:** Applied to distinguish remote equipment provenance from an actual constitutional mechanism.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Distinguished as identifying additional routes for different regulated classes; those routes are not exclusive of instrumentality protection.

#### Law After Decision

Federal protection of covered telephone communications extends to local calls actually carried through an interstate telephone system. The enacted interception and network predicates remain subject to the criminal burden. The direct-instrumentality ground defeats the local-endpoints objection while the independent suppression and retrial mandate governs further proceedings in Carnes's case.

#### Source Notes

18 U.S.C. §§2510 and 2511 as applicable to the charged live interception; U.S. Const., Art. I, §8; 309 F.3d 950 (Oct. 29, 2002), Sixth Circuit No. 00-2103. The constitutional review concerns the telephone-network interception component, and the separate suppression-based reversal retains its force.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower courts must retain the wiretap count against this commerce objection and implement the existing suppression-based reversal, new-trial direction and related mandate. Further proceedings remain governed by the independent rulings already effective below.

**End of entry: United States v. Carnes, merits decision, February 9, 2004.**
