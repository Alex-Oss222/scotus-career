## Decisions and Dispositions

### Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., No. 02-572

Merits decision, June 21, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 20, 2004; decided June 21, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 292 F.3d 664. Reported at 542 U.S. 241.

AMD complained to the European Commission about Intel and sought documents and testimony from American antitrust litigation. The district court denied authority to assist; the Ninth Circuit reversed that categorical ruling. The questions are whether AMD is an interested person, whether the contemplated Commission adjudication qualifies, whether foreign discoverability is required, and how statutory discretion operates. Justice O’Connor took no part in the consideration or decision; the other eight Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Breyer.

**Not participating:** O’Connor.

The application returns to the district court for a reasoned decision whether, and on what conditions, assistance should be provided.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. BREYER, J., filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Section 1782 permits this application for a contemplated adjudicative proceeding

**Holding and operative rule.** A district court may provide assistance under 28 U.S.C. §1782(a) upon an interested person’s application for evidence from a person residing or found in the district, for use in a reasonably contemplated proceeding before a foreign governmental body exercising adjudicative authority. The applicant need not await a pending or imminent adjudication; practical rights to submit evidence and obtain review may establish the required interest without formal litigant status.

**Authority.** The seven Justices joining the Court’s opinion support every holding and explanation in Parts I–III.

**Controlling explanation.** The enacted language reaches a foreign tribunal, and Congress removed the former restriction to pending judicial proceedings. That change permits assistance before an adjudication has begun, provided the expected proceeding rests on more than speculation. AMD can submit evidence and seek review of Commission action or inaction. Those rights give it a practical legal interest in the proceeding. The Commission’s ability to make a first-instance competition determination, subject to judicial review, supplies the adjudicative function relevant here. Its investigative directorate’s refusal to request the materials does not eliminate AMD’s separate statutory right to apply. The Commission’s combined functions warrant attention to the actual use of the evidence; they do not support categorical exclusion of its adjudicative work.

##### II. Foreign discoverability and domestic analogues are not additional statutory prerequisites

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 1782 does not require the applicant to show that the evidence would be discoverable under foreign law or that an analogous domestic proceeding would permit the same discovery. Differences in national procedures do not alone establish an effort to evade foreign restrictions.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute states who may apply, where the evidence provider must be found and the proceeding for which assistance may be given. It adds an express protection for legally applicable privileges, but no foreign-discoverability or domestic-analogue condition. Requiring either would substitute an extra eligibility rule for Congress’s discretionary authorization. A foreign body’s practices, policies and views remain important when the district court decides whether assistance is appropriate. The dissent’s concern that discovery litigation itself can cause expense and regulatory interference is substantial, but supports a genuine discretionary assessment rather than categorical conditions absent from the statute. Neither a foreign body’s refusal to request evidence nor the absence of comparable foreign procedures is a statutory veto.

##### III. Eligibility leaves a genuine, application-specific discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** An eligible application does not compel production. The district court must exercise reasoned discretion in light of the contemplated proceeding and requested assistance, considering the foreign body’s receptivity, the nature and character of its proceeding, access to evidence through a participant within that body’s reach, actual circumvention of foreign proof-gathering restrictions or policies, and undue intrusion or burden; no order may violate a legally applicable privilege.

**Controlling explanation.** The word permitting assistance leaves the district court responsible for the actual production decision. Evidence from a participant subject to the foreign tribunal’s authority may present a different need from evidence outside that authority. The court must consider the Commission’s position and distinguish rejection of a particular assistance mechanism from a legally operative restriction. It may narrow, condition or deny a request with reasons, and use appropriate confidentiality protection. These considerations guide discretion; they are not new cumulative eligibility elements. The application therefore returns for identification of the materials, their relevance to the reasonably contemplated proceeding, and the protections and burdens that the actual request presents.

#### Precedent Treatment

- F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A. supplies respect for foreign regulatory interests through the statute actually at issue. Its construction of antitrust coverage does not eliminate Congress’s affirmative authorization of foreign evidentiary assistance.

#### Law After Decision

Interested complainants with practical participation and review rights may seek assistance for reasonably contemplated governmental adjudication. Neither foreign discoverability nor a domestic discovery analogue is a categorical condition. The district court retains responsibility for a proportionate order consistent with privileges, foreign receptivity, lawful restrictions and actual burdens.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Breyer dissents. Where a foreign institution’s tribunal character is seriously doubtful, he would give substantial weight to its own description of its function. The Commission’s mixed prosecutorial and adjudicative role and its opposition to this use counsel against assistance. He would also deny a private request for discovery unavailable both abroad and in analogous domestic circumstances, absent a special need supported by the foreign or intergovernmental authorities whose cooperation the statute serves. He derives these limits from statutory purpose or, alternatively, supervisory authority. Because the expense and disruption begin with the discovery litigation itself, later trimming of production does not sufficiently answer his objection. He would reverse and direct dismissal of this application.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §1782(a)–(b); Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. v. Intel Corp., 292 F.3d 664 (9th Cir. 2002); F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A., No. 03-724, decided June 14, 2004. The Commission’s adjudicative function and AMD’s participation and review rights are material to this application.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit’s return to the district court remains effective. That court must decide the scope, conditions or denial of the particular requested assistance under the statute and the Court’s stated discretionary considerations.

**End of entry: Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., merits decision, June 21, 2004.**

### Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws

Merits decision, June 23, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided June 23, 2004. On review of the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 332 F.3d 769, affirming a single district judge’s dismissal of a challenge to Maryland’s 2002 congressional map.

Duckworth alleges that dividing Anne Arundel County among four unusually shaped districts intentionally impaired Republican participation and influence. The questions are whether a single judge could dismiss the preserved constitutional claim on ordinary pleading grounds without convening three judges, and which tribunal must assess the allegations under the governing partisan-districting rule. Only the Fourteenth Amendment claim was pursued on appeal. All nine Justices participated in the consideration and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The district judge must request a three-judge court. That court must address the preserved claim and any permissible conforming amendment under ordinary procedure and the governing rule in Vieth v. Jubelirer.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O’CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Ordinary pleading weakness does not dispense with the statutory tribunal

**Holding and operative rule.** When a plaintiff presents a constitutional challenge to congressional apportionment within federal jurisdiction, §2284 requires a three-judge district court unless the constitutional claim is wholly insubstantial, obviously frivolous or clearly foreclosed. A single judge may not equate ordinary failure to state a claim with that exceptional category and enter a merits dismissal.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join the Court’s statutory holding and the directions applying existing law in Part II. That application does not reconsider or enlarge the constitutional holding of Vieth.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2284 allocates covered apportionment litigation to three judges and expressly restricts a single judge’s authority to enter judgment on the merits. Goosby v. Osser distinguishes a wholly insubstantial constitutional claim from one that is merely doubtful or may fail under ordinary merits rules. Treating Rule 12(b)(6) insufficiency as the substantiality test erases that distinction and permits the single judge to exercise the very authority Congress assigned elsewhere. The earlier map’s unsuccessful merits litigation supplies no categorical pleading bar for the changed map. Duckworth’s allegation of purposeful impairment of Republican participation presents the kind of equal-protection claim recognized by Vieth. Whether his allegations and evidence satisfy that rule belongs to the designated tribunal.

##### II. The panel applies Vieth’s actual rule and ordinary pleading procedure

**Holding and operative rule.** The required panel must apply Vieth’s partisan equal-treatment rule: identified political capacity, material impairment, prohibited purpose as a substantial motivating factor, and boundary-specific causation, supported by an independently lawful comparison and subject to the State’s same-decision defense. Irregular shape or electoral disadvantage alone does not establish those elements; ordinary notice pleading does not require merits proof, an expert model or a replacement map.

**Controlling explanation.** Vieth supplies the law for the preserved Fourteenth Amendment claim, and Martinez v. Bush illustrates its claim-specific application. Division among four districts and alleged purposeful degradation of political participation make this a substantial constitutional claim for tribunal allocation. The panel must permit the ordinary process for seeking conforming amendment and evaluate pleading, proof and any remedy under the actual rule. The Court’s reliance on that rule does not revive the Article I or independent First Amendment theories abandoned on appeal. The State continues lawful election administration subject to any separately justified order entered by the proper tribunal.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Goosby v. Osser is applied to distinguish constitutional insubstantiality from ordinary merits insufficiency.
- Vieth v. Jubelirer supplies the controlling partisan equal-treatment rule; its constitutional coalition and scope remain unchanged.
- Martinez v. Bush supplies the requirement to apply that rule to the particular claim and proof.
- Shaw v. Reno does not convert unusual district shape into a substitute for the injury required by the preserved partisan claim.

#### Law After Decision

A single judge cannot bypass §2284 by using ordinary Rule 12(b)(6) weakness as constitutional insubstantiality. A substantial congressional-apportionment claim receives merits adjudication from the statutory panel, which applies the existing substantive elements, lawful comparison, ordinary proof rules and State defense.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §2284; Goosby v. Osser, 409 U.S. 512, 518 (1973); Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws, 332 F.3d 769 (4th Cir. 2003); Vieth v. Jubelirer, No. 02-1580, decided April 28, 2004; Martinez v. Bush, decided April 29, 2004. The district divisions and purpose described here are allegations, not findings of constitutional injury.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

On remand, the district judge must notify the chief circuit judge for designation of the additional judges required by §2284. The panel then addresses the preserved Fourteenth Amendment claim, permissible amendment and any properly supported request for relief.

**End of entry: Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws, merits decision, June 23, 2004.**

### Beard v. Banks, No. 02-1603

Merits decision, June 24, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 24, 2004; decided June 24, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, 316 F.3d 228. Reported at 542 U.S. 406.

After Horn v. Banks required consideration of Pennsylvania’s preserved nonretroactivity defense, the Third Circuit held the mitigation rule available and reinstated its grant of capital sentencing relief. The questions are whether the individual-juror mitigation rule was dictated when direct review became final on October 5, 1987, and whether the actual charge and form satisfy the constitutional and statutory grounds for relief. Pennsylvania adjudicated the merits in 1995; the operative federal petition is subject to amended §2254(d). All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing position | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Availability under Teague | Affirmed, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse under the new-rule bar | The claim remains available as old law |
| Instructional violation and §2254(d)(1) gateway | Determinations affirmed, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would terminate this claim under Teague and do not independently decide these merits | Both conclusions bind the return |
| Automatic sentence relief | Vacated, 5–4; confined remand | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the relief under Teague | Conduct the whole-penalty-record effect inquiry |

The State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the restriction did not contribute to the death sentences. If it fails, a conditional writ must allow a reasonable fixed period for lawful capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. The convictions and unrelated claims remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed a concurring opinion. O’CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, KENNEDY and THOMAS, JJ., joined.

The five-Justice opinion applies the existing effect standard; Justice Stevens’s concurrence preserves his different preferred standard without withholding the remand directed by controlling law.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. An individual-juror mitigation veto is forbidden by law dictated before finality

**Holding and operative rule.** A capital instruction may not condition an individual juror’s use of supported mitigating evidence on unanimous agreement that the circumstance exists. Applying that prohibition to a judgment final after the effective-mitigation requirement was established does not announce a new rule merely because the barrier takes the form of a unanimity requirement.

**Authority.** The five Justices joining the Court’s opinion support Parts I–IV, including application of the existing effect rule in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation.** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma forbid legal exclusion of relevant mitigation from the sentencing decision. A juror who accepts mitigating evidence but is legally forbidden to use it because another juror disagrees encounters the same functional exclusion. Graham v. Collins explains that an effective route for independent culpability mitigation was dictated before its 1984 finality. Its later decision confirms that earlier rule; it is not backdated as a new command. The dissent distinguishes usable information from the agreement required within a collective sentencer. That distinction cannot sustain a legal veto over evidence an individual sentencing juror accepts. The rule leaves the jury’s ultimate verdict requirements and each juror’s assessment of weight intact. Horn required the preserved Teague defense to be answered, and this comparison supplies the answer. Because the claim applies old law, the Court need not extend the watershed exception.

##### II. The actual charge and form create the forbidden barrier

**Holding and operative rule.** A capital charge violates the effective-mitigation requirement when, read with the verdict form and trial context, it creates a reasonable likelihood that a juror cannot use supported mitigation without unanimous agreement. Banks’s combined charge and form create that likelihood.

**Controlling explanation.** The charge required death upon unanimous findings of an aggravating circumstance without mitigation, or aggravating circumstances outweighing mitigation, and directed life in other cases. It stated different proof burdens—beyond a reasonable doubt for aggravation and preponderance for mitigation—while repeatedly requiring agreement on findings and the ultimate verdict. The form then placed the circumstance findings under a common unanimity introduction. Nothing in that mechanism distinguished a juror’s individual ability to use mitigation. Different proof burdens do not explain a different voting rule. The jury’s acceptance of extreme mental or emotional disturbance does not establish that every juror could use the separate impaired-capacity and catchall categories. The reported polling supplied no cure. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood, not a merely possible restrictive reading; Middleton v. McNeil requires the complete charge and context, not isolated wording. The combined mechanism meets that standard. The defect does not arise solely from absence of a particular sentence or from the lawful unanimity required for an ultimate death verdict.

##### III. The state application was objectively unreasonable

**Holding and operative rule.** Under §2254(d)(1), the 1995 state adjudication unreasonably applied the clearly established mitigation rule to this combined charge and form. The petitioner satisfies the statutory merits gateway in addition to establishing the constitutional violation.

**Controlling explanation.** Williams v. Taylor makes unreasonable application an objective legal inquiry measured against this Court’s holdings at the state merits decision. Mills v. Maryland and McKoy v. North Carolina were available by 1995, as was Graham’s explanation of the effective-use requirement. The state analysis relied on previously accepted wording without confronting how the common unanimity requirement operated across the distinct mitigation findings. This is more than ordinary disagreement about an isolated ambiguity. The form and repeated instruction create the operative veto the governing rule forbids. Yarborough v. Alvarado preserves room for reasonable application of general rules but does not demand an earlier factual twin or turn judicial disagreement into the statutory test. The state resolution falls outside that permissible range. This statutory conclusion remains separate from the effect of the error on the death sentences.

##### IV. Sentence relief requires the separate State-borne effect inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** For this properly available original-charge mitigation restriction, Brecht’s governing rule, as applied in Penry v. Johnson, requires the State to prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. Failure requires conditional sentence relief permitting lawful capital resentencing within a reasonable court-fixed period or a lawful noncapital sentence.

**Controlling explanation.** Constitutional error and satisfaction of §2254(d)(1) permit the inquiry; neither resolves actual contribution. Penry expressly separates these steps for an original-charge mitigation restriction, and Mitchell v. Esparza rejects treating a defect as structural merely because the sentence is capital. The full penalty evidence must be assessed under the State’s burden. The instructional account establishes the legal mechanism but does not supply the complete evidentiary basis for deciding effect here. The court on return must conduct that confined inquiry while treating the old-rule, constitutional-error and statutory conclusions as settled. Brecht and Penry govern present federal review; their effect procedure is not used as a later substantive command at the 1995 state-law cutoff.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lockett v. Ohio is applied to a legal barrier preventing individual use of supported mitigation.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma is likewise applied to that barrier.
- Graham v. Collins confirms the prefinality force of the effective-mitigation protection.
- Horn v. Banks is followed by resolving the preserved Teague defense; its reserved question is answered without changing its sequencing rule.
- O’Dell v. Netherland retains its distinct old-rule and confined watershed grounds.
- Mills v. Maryland is applied to the unanimity mechanism.
- McKoy v. North Carolina is likewise applied to that mechanism.
- Boyde v. California supplies contextual reasonable-likelihood review.
- Middleton v. McNeil likewise requires the complete charge and context.
- Williams v. Taylor supplies objective statutory review rather than factual identity or a count of disagreeing judges.
- Yarborough v. Alvarado likewise preserves objective review within the permissible range of reasonable application.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson governs the separate State-borne effect inquiry.
- Penry v. Johnson applies that inquiry to an original-charge mitigation restriction.
- Mitchell v. Esparza preserves the distinction between a capital instructional error and a structural defect.

#### Law After Decision

The effective-mitigation rule already dictated before Banks’s 1987 finality forbids a unanimity veto on an individual juror’s use of supported mitigation. This actual instruction/form mechanism violates that rule, and the 1995 state rejection was objectively unreasonable. Those conclusions bind the remand; the complete penalty-record effect inquiry determines whether conditional sentence relief must issue.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens concurs. He applies the governing State-borne beyond-reasonable-doubt rule and joins the confined remand, while retaining his preferred inquiry into whether the error had substantial and injurious influence on the sentence. The Court’s existing rule, rather than a new endorsement of its burden, controls his present vote.

Justice O’Connor dissents, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Lockett and Eddings required an effective opportunity for the sentencer to use mitigation, but did not, in their view, dictate the later restriction on agreement among members of a collective sentencer. Graham’s effective-use holding must be respected without treating every subsequent jury-arrangement rule as already dictated in 1987. The individual-juror rule changes procedure rather than the conduct or persons subject to punishment, and does not produce the fundamental systemic accuracy change necessary for the watershed exception. They distinguish O’Dell’s misleading-parole-premise safeguard and Tyler’s invalid verdict-wide burden. They would reverse the relief under Teague and leave remaining independent claims to their own disposition. They do not independently decide the actual charge or statutory gateway.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Banks v. Horn, 271 F.3d 527 (3d Cir. 2001), and 316 F.3d 228 (3d Cir. 2003); Horn v. Banks, 536 U.S. 266; Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461; Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367; McKoy v. North Carolina, 494 U.S. 433; Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370; Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362; Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782. Direct-review finality is October 5, 1987; the relevant state merits adjudication occurred in 1995.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for the State-borne whole-penalty-record inquiry alone on this claim. If the State fails to establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, the lower court must enter a conditional sentence order fixing a reasonable period for lawful resentencing, with a lawful noncapital sentence if the State does not provide the replacement proceeding.

**End of entry: Beard v. Banks, merits decision, June 24, 2004.**

### Blakely v. Washington, No. 02-1632

Merits decision, June 24, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 23, 2004; decided June 24, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Washington Court of Appeals, 111 Wash. App. 851, 47 P.3d 149. Reported at 542 U.S. 296.

Blakely pleaded guilty to kidnapping. His admissions supported a binding standard-range maximum of 53 months. The judge imposed 90 months after finding deliberate cruelty, a fact neither admitted as an element nor found by a jury. The question is whether the higher outer maximum for the felony classification permits that judicial increase above the binding range. All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed; sentence vacated and case remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O’Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.

The guilty plea and conviction remain intact. On the existing admissions, the court must sentence within the lawful standard range. Any further aggravated proceeding requires independent Washington authority and compliance with applicable charging, plea, waiver and double-jeopardy limits.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, THOMAS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. O’CONNOR, KENNEDY and BREYER, JJ., each filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The binding range identifies the punishment authorized by the plea or verdict

**Holding and operative rule.** A present-offense fact legally necessary to increase a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; a lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder while retaining that burden. A higher outer statutory maximum does not permit a judge to exceed the enforceable inner range supported by the verdict or valid admissions.

**Authority.** The six Justices joining the Court’s opinion support the rule, application and remedy in Parts I and II.

**Controlling explanation.** Apprendi v. New Jersey supplies the requirement of element protection for a fact necessary to authorize greater punishment. United States v. Angle already applies that protection to binding inner ranges, including mandatory constraints below a broader statutory maximum. Harris v. United States confirms that sentencing labels and the distinction between increasing a floor and increasing a ceiling do not remove the protection. The relevant question is what punishment the actual adjudication authorizes, not the largest term the statute could allow after additional facts are established. The dissenters’ structured-sentencing concerns address whether Angle should stand; they supply no distinction under its controlling rule. Judicial evaluation remains permissible within independently authorized punishment. Here the plea supports a maximum of 53 months, so the additional cruelty finding was necessary to authorize the 90-month term.

##### II. The judicial cruelty finding cannot sustain this sentence

**Holding and operative rule.** An unadmitted and untried deliberate-cruelty finding cannot authorize the exceptional sentence imposed here. The sentence must be vacated, and lawful resentencing must begin from the range authorized by the actual plea; any further aggravation adjudication depends on independent state-law authority and valid procedures.

**Controlling explanation.** Notice that the general felony classification allowed greater exposure neither admits the aggravating element nor waives its proper adjudication. The judge’s conclusion about cruelty supplies the very finding the constitutional rule assigns elsewhere; repeating it cannot cure the omission. Nor does the asserted strength of the evidence establish an equivalent actual adjudication or a valid admission. Ring v. Arizona preserves the distinction between identifying a constitutional factfinding requirement and deciding an error’s consequences on the actual record. Nothing here permits retention of the 90-month sentence on a hypothetical replacement verdict. Resentencing must respect the plea and conviction that remain in force. If additional aggravated proceedings are sought, their state-law basis and the applicable charging, waiver and double-jeopardy limits must be established independently; the constitutional remand supplies no such authority.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Apprendi v. New Jersey is applied to the fact legally necessary for greater punishment and to the distinction between notice and valid admission.
- United States v. Angle directly governs the binding-inner-range question.
- Harris v. United States supplies the common treatment of present-offense facts raising binding floors and ceilings.
- United States v. Buckland preserves analysis of the punishment actually authorized, rather than assumed exposure.
- Ring v. Arizona retains its separate capital-eligibility and qualified remedy rules.

The Court applies these decisions without changing their operative rules.

#### Law After Decision

The existing element-protection rule governs materially equivalent binding sentencing schemes, including Washington’s exceptional-range mechanism. Judges retain discretion within punishment independently authorized by the actual verdict or valid admissions. The 53-month range supported by this plea cannot be enlarged to 90 months through the judge’s contested cruelty finding.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O’Connor dissents. She would reconsider Angle’s treatment of binding sentencing ranges. In her view, rules structuring the judge’s choice within the outer punishment authorized for the offense differ from elements defining the offense itself. She would sustain the exceptional sentence on that understanding, while recognizing that Angle as it stands requires correction.

Justice Kennedy dissents. He retains his objection to making every binding inner-range constraint an element, emphasizing the role of legislatures and sentencing institutions in developing structured sentencing. His acceptance of the jury requirement at Ring’s capital-eligibility boundary does not resolve this different objection. He would reconsider the inner-range rule and affirm; his position does not depend on characterizing this application as an unexpected first extension of the rule.

Justice Breyer dissents. He would reconsider the broader Apprendi and Angle allocation because structured judicial sentencing can serve consistency and individualized judgment within the offense’s outer authorization. His objection reaches binding floors and ceilings alike. His capital-specific view about jury participation in choosing death does not establish support for the noncapital rule applied here. He would affirm on his proposed reconsideration of that rule.

#### Source Notes

Washington Court of Appeals, 111 Wash. App. 851, 47 P.3d 149; Apprendi v. New Jersey, No. 99-478, decided June 26, 2000; United States v. Angle, decided June 20, 2002; Harris v. United States, decided June 24, 2002; Ring v. Arizona, No. 01-488, decided June 24, 2002; United States v. Buckland, decided March 10, 2003.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for lawful sentencing with the plea and conviction preserved. The sentencing court must identify the range authorized by the actual admissions and resolve any request for further aggravated adjudication under independent Washington authority and the applicable constitutional and plea limits.

**End of entry: Blakely v. Washington, merits decision, June 24, 2004.**

### Cheney v. United States District Court for the District of Columbia, No. 03-475

Merits decision, June 24, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 27, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 334 F.3d 1096.

The President established the National Energy Policy Development Group, chaired by the Vice President and formally composed of federal officials. Plaintiffs allege that private participants functioned as members and brought the group within the Federal Advisory Committee Act. The district court deferred the clear-duty question and permitted broad discovery. The Court of Appeals denied extraordinary relief. The questions concern concrete burdens on presidential advice, the role of executive privilege, functional membership and the proper appellate response.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded for reconsideration, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas would reverse with instructions to issue the writ; Souter and Ginsburg would affirm the denial.

The Court of Appeals must evaluate the actual scope and burden of the challenged demands, adequate protective alternatives, the clear-entitlement requirement and the appropriateness of intervention.

THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which SCALIA, J., joined. GINSBURG, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which SOUTER, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Extraordinary appellate relief and statutory enforcement have separate requirements

**Holding and operative rule.** Appellate mandamus under §1651 requires absence of an adequate alternative, a clear and indisputable entitlement, and a determination that issuance is appropriate in the circumstances. The plaintiffs’ separate §1361 theory requires a legally enforceable, sufficiently clear nondiscretionary duty and its other prerequisites; any APA claim requires an eligible agency defendant and satisfaction of that statute’s independent conditions.

**Authority.** Part I is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** Kerr v. United States District Court supplies the exceptional-remedy conditions and the importance of adequate protection in the district court. An appeal deadline does not mechanically govern a mandamus petition, although unreasonable delay and resulting prejudice may bear on equitable relief. Appellate mandamus is not a substitute for establishing the plaintiffs’ underlying enforcement route. The official’s title also supplies neither a categorical immunity nor an automatic interlocutory appeal. The inquiry addresses the function involved, the actual demands and their supported burdens. These distinctions preserve both independent judicial enforcement of federal law and the protections necessary for executive responsibility.

##### II. Concrete institutional burdens need not await a document-by-document privilege confrontation

**Holding and operative rule.** A court considering extraordinary protection against compulsory civil discovery directed into presidential advisory work must assess demonstrated separation-of-powers burdens from the scope and operation of the demands. A formal assertion of executive privilege is not an invariant prerequisite to addressing those distinct burdens; particular claims of privilege retain their own requirements.

**Authority.** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** Clinton v. Jones requires accommodation of concrete interference with official responsibilities while rejecting immunity based simply on presidential status. Its unofficial-conduct setting does not itself decide this official advisory dispute, but the functional accommodation principle applies. United States v. Nixon rejects absolute insulation of presidential communications in the face of a demonstrated need for identified criminal evidence; it does not make broad civil discovery and a later privilege contest the sole permissible course. A demand may burden official deliberation through its breadth and the search it requires before individual documents can be contested. Confidential advice, once disclosed, cannot be restored to confidentiality. The appellate court therefore erred in treating the absence of a formal privilege confrontation as sufficient to avoid examining these burdens. The availability of narrower district-court remedies remains important, as the dissent urges, but must be evaluated as an actual alternative to the asserted intrusion rather than presumed adequate from the possibility of future privilege objections.

##### III. Actual integration into collective advisory work can establish membership

**Holding and operative rule.** Under FACA’s definition and its exclusion for a body composed wholly of federal officers or employees, a private person may be a functional member when sustained participation integrates that person into an established body’s internal collective deliberative and advisory work. Formal appointment, voting, veto or final governmental decisionmaking authority is not indispensable; access, attendance, lobbying, consultation, influence or outside recommendations without that integration is insufficient.

**Authority.** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These are direct joins to the statutory distinction, independent of the disagreement over appellate relief.

**Controlling explanation.** Public Citizen v. Department of Justice construes FACA with attention to ordinary executive consultation and serious constitutional concerns; it does not exempt every presidential advisory arrangement. Association of American Physicians & Surgeons v. Clinton persuasively distinguishes regular work functionally indistinguishable from membership from episodic advice. Relevant facts include regular internal meetings, continuing assigned responsibilities, participation in developing collective recommendations or drafting the group’s report, and work materially indistinguishable from that of recognized members. No single fact automatically establishes membership. An executive label cannot conclusively exclude an integrated private member, and allegations of influence cannot conclusively establish one. The distinction concerns an actual organized advisory body, not every person whose views officials hear. Here private integration remains disputed. The governing inquiry concerns participants’ functions; coverage and any final enforcement require factual and claim-specific adjudication through an available legal route.

##### IV. The proper mandate is appellate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court of Appeals must reconsider the petition by applying all mandamus requirements to the actual structural burdens and available protective alternatives. Its former privilege-only approach cannot sustain the judgment; whether a writ should issue requires that complete inquiry.

**Authority.** Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** The identified appellate error does not establish that every extraordinary-relief condition is satisfied. The court must consider the breadth and necessity of the demands, whether narrower inquiry and district-court supervision adequately protect the asserted interests, the clear-duty and enforcement limitations, and the appropriateness of intervention. Thomas and Scalia would order the writ now because the uncertain duty cannot support discovery that may deliver the ultimate disclosure sought. Those concerns require consideration, but the present record leaves the complete alternatives and entitlement inquiry for appellate adjudication. Any legally authorized further process must respect the Court’s functional-membership rule and the distinct protections for particular privileged materials.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Kerr v. United States District Court* is applied to the full extraordinary-writ inquiry and adequate protective alternatives.
- *Clinton v. Jones* is extended through its concrete-accommodation principle to official advisory discovery while retaining its actual unofficial-conduct holding.
- *United States v. Nixon* retains qualified protection and the distinction between identified criminal evidence and broad civil demands.
- *Nixon v. Fitzgerald* retains its function-based official-act damages immunity, a different question from statutory-compliance inquiry.
- *Public Citizen v. Department of Justice* supplies contextual statutory caution without a blanket presidential-advice exemption.
- *Association of American Physicians & Surgeons v. Clinton*: Its functional reasoning is adopted to distinguish integrated members from outside consultants.

The statutory inquiry must preserve the independent requirements of the asserted enforcement channel.

#### Law After Decision

Concrete institutional discovery burdens must receive consideration under the full mandamus standard without an automatic privilege-first barrier. FACA membership turns on sustained integration into an actual advisory body’s collective work, rather than formal voting authority or private influence alone. The Court’s mandate requires appellate reconsideration under the complete extraordinary-relief standard.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs and would add binding stages to the remand. First, the district court must identify the FACA provision, defendants, alleged private participants and allegations of integration, and separately determine personal injury and an available enforcement route under ordinary pleading rules. A generalized allegation of private influence does not authorize a search through presidential deliberations. Akins v. FEC supports concrete injury from denial of particular statutorily protected information; Chamber of Commerce v. Reich supports qualified review of responsible implementing officers, not an unrestricted private action.

Second, initial discovery must concern membership, attendance, assigned responsibilities and actual collective participation. The court must prefer official rosters, calendars, declarations or other adequate nondeliberative evidence before seeking the substance of advice. A title or conclusory declaration cannot prevent appropriate testing of a genuinely disputed material fact.

Third, before enlarging the inquiry, the court must identify the remaining material dispute, explain necessity and assess an adequate less intrusive alternative. Operational facts and advice are analytically distinct, but no category is automatically unprivileged. Narrow requests, segregation, redaction and protective orders must address the actual sensitivity and burden. Justified in-camera inspection—review by the judge outside public disclosure—does not replace decisions about relevance, necessity or privilege.

Fourth, particular privileges and concrete objections must be resolved before protected disclosure. The party seeking protection must provide the particularity reasonably possible without defeating it. Plaintiffs must establish coverage and final-relief prerequisites; access, influence and resistance to disclosure establish neither. A formal privilege claim may be necessary to decide a privilege, but is not invariably necessary to correct an unnecessarily broad order. The Chief would require this sequence while leaving appellate mandamus subject to its own conditions. These mandatory steps do not receive another Justice’s join.

Justice Stevens concurs. Broad discovery can deliver the very disclosure sought as final relief before plaintiffs establish the sufficiently clear duty required by §1361. Discovery must relate to establishing the asserted statutory coverage. Substantive participation in the group’s recommendations or report can support membership, but repeated access alone cannot. These concerns support reconsideration rather than a direction to issue the writ immediately.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, concurs in Parts I–II and dissents from the remedial disposition. An uncertain claim of FACA coverage and an unestablished clear nondiscretionary duty cannot justify the broad compulsory inquiry into official advice allowed here. The possible later establishment of functional membership does not supply the missing present basis for §1361 discovery. They would direct issuance of the writ against the challenged district-court action.

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justice Souter, concurs in Parts I and III and dissents from vacatur. Specific requests for narrower discovery and properly supported privilege protection remain available in the district court. The petitioners have not adequately used those alternatives; the court’s failure to narrow the demands on its own does not establish a present entitlement to extraordinary appellate intervention. They accept protection of executive functions and the functional-membership distinction but would affirm the mandamus denial.

The case returns to the Court of Appeals for a complete mandamus determination. The appellate court must address the actual discovery demands, structural burdens, underlying enforcement requirements and adequate protective alternatives before deciding whether extraordinary relief is warranted.

### Schriro v. Summerlin, No. 03-526

Merits decision, June 24, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 19, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, sitting en banc, 341 F.3d 1082.

Summerlin’s murder judgment became final in 1984. The trial judge found a prior violent felony and exceptional heinousness, cruelty or depravity and imposed death under Arizona’s former scheme. His federal petition preceded AEDPA and remained pending when Ring v. Arizona was decided. The Ninth Circuit granted Ring-based sentencing relief. The question is whether the jury determination of an indispensable death-eligibility fact applies retroactively. Review excludes whether the prior-conviction aggravator independently established eligibility.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded with the Ring claim available, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the Ring-based grant and require rejection of that claim under Teague.

The rejection of the retroactivity bar is sustained on the watershed ground. The lower court must determine the actual eligibility omission and apply the governing collateral effect standard before entering any conditional sentence relief. The murder conviction and independently reviewable claims remain distinct. The prior-conviction sufficiency question remains outside this Court’s grant.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Ring changes procedure rather than the substantive reach of punishment

**Holding and operative rule.** Ring’s allocation to the jury of an otherwise required statutory aggravating-fact determination is a new procedural rule for a judgment final in 1984. It does not change the substantive aggravators, conduct or classes of persons legally subject to death, so the substantive-rule exception does not apply.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join Part I.

**Controlling explanation.** The aggravating circumstance was already necessary under Arizona law, and the State already required proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Ring changes who must determine that fact. Calling the fact an element explains its constitutional protection but does not itself remove conduct or a class of offenders from the State’s power to punish. Caspari and Horn require consideration of a preserved retroactivity defense before granting relief; classification is one step in that inquiry. Because the rule was not dictated at Summerlin’s 1984 finality, its availability depends on the separately preserved exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

##### II. The indispensable capital-eligibility determination satisfies the watershed exception

**Holding and operative rule.** In a jury-tried prosecution, Ring’s protection for the determination of a present-offense fact indispensable to authorizing death, subject to valid admission and lawful waiver requirements, satisfies both the exceptional accuracy and bedrock-procedure requirements for watershed application on collateral review. Qualifying claims may proceed under the legal regime otherwise governing the petition.

**Authority.** Part II is joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Controlling explanation.** The missing safeguard operates at the factual boundary between imprisonment and death. Independent collective scrutiny of the prosecution’s case is absent at the point where the irreversible punishment first becomes lawful. That deficiency creates an impermissibly large risk in this confined category, especially when an aggravator calls for evaluation of exceptional cruelty rather than a merely mechanical fact. A correct burden of proof does not itself supply that collective assessment. Jury determination of a punishment-authorizing fact also belongs to the basic structure of a fair criminal adjudication; making it effective at this boundary changes understanding of an indispensable safeguard.

O’Dell recognizes a confined watershed safeguard against a materially misleading capital-information premise, and Tyler recognizes the exceptional significance of an adjudication lacking the required verdict-wide reasonable-doubt safeguard. Neither already decides Ring, but both require examination of how a missing procedure operates. DeStefano’s refusal to reopen ordinary noncapital bench trials under an earlier retroactivity framework supplies substantial caution: judges can find facts reliably. It does not resolve this confined capital question under Teague’s two requirements. The Court’s conclusion rests on the withheld eligibility determination and its evaluative function, not death’s severity alone, presumed judicial incompetence or a general preference for greater mercy. Ultimate discretionary selection among already authorized punishments is distinct from the eligibility rule applied here.

##### III. Availability does not establish an omission’s effect or authorize an immediate writ

**Holding and operative rule.** The lower court must determine whether a necessary death-eligibility finding was withheld from the jury and address the remaining prerequisites to relief on the actual record. For a properly reached, nonstructural eligibility omission, Brecht’s governing collateral rule places on the State the burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the death sentence.

**Authority.** Part III is joined by the same five Justices. It directs the remaining adjudication under existing law; the separate writings preserve the differing views about the detailed actual-record method.

**Controlling explanation.** Ring separates the jury entitlement from an actual error’s remedial consequence, and Brecht supplies the collateral effect standard. Retroactivity neither establishes that an indispensable fact was omitted nor resolves contribution to the sentence. Lindh and Carriger preserve the former merits and factual-presumption regime for this pre-AEDPA petition; Slack separately governs the later appeal’s certificate. If the remaining requirements are met and the State fails its effect burden, conditional sentence relief must permit lawful correction under governing law. Further sentencing requires independent state authority and compliance with applicable constitutional limits. Whether the prior violent conviction independently supplied eligibility was excluded from review and must receive its own lawful treatment within the grant’s limits.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Tyler v. Cain* is extended through its confined accuracy-and-bedrock principle; its exception holding is not narrowed.

- *Horn v. Banks* likewise requires resolution of the preserved retroactivity defense.

- *Carriger v. Stewart* preserves that former-law merits regime.

#### Law After Decision

Ring’s indispensable present-offense death-eligibility factfinding protection is available on collateral review through a confined watershed application. The rule remains procedural, and ordinary finality rules continue outside that category. Each qualifying petitioner must establish the actual violation and satisfy the otherwise applicable conditions for relief; the State bears the governing beyond-reasonable-doubt effect burden for a properly reached nonstructural omission.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs, joined by Justices Stevens and Souter. Ring’s qualified actual-adjudication method requires attention to the actual instructions, the evidence they directed to the jury, necessary findings and material conflicts. Genuine equivalent jury findings can establish noncontribution but are not the exclusive route: reliable whole-record inferences must remain connected to the actual adjudication. New credibility findings, sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict and appellate reweighing that supplies a missing eligibility adjudication cannot substitute for that inquiry. The State’s burden under Brecht remains separate from the finding of retroactive availability.

Justice Ginsburg concurs. She joins the procedural classification, the confined watershed holding and the bounded remand, but retains her view that harmlessness for the omitted eligibility determination requires genuinely equivalent actual findings. A hypothetical jury verdict or the court’s assessment that the evidence was strong cannot supply them. Her narrower method does not turn retroactivity alone into an entitlement to sentence relief.

Justice Breyer concurs. His support rests on the jury’s independent collective role in reliable capital adjudication, including the community evaluation required by an aggravator such as exceptional cruelty. He joins the confined eligibility holding without adopting the general Apprendi theory for noncapital sentencing, and preserves his broader Eighth Amendment view of the jury’s role in choosing death without making that additional issue part of this decision.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in procedural classification and dissents from retroactivity. Constitutional allocation of authority on direct review does not establish the extraordinary accuracy showing needed to reopen final judgments. Arizona already required aggravating facts to be proved beyond reasonable doubt, and a competent judge’s findings are not shown to create the necessary impermissible accuracy risk merely because a jury is constitutionally required. The evaluative nature and capital importance of the finding do not, in their view, bridge that gap. O’Dell’s misleading-parole-premise protection and Tyler’s invalid verdict-wide burden remain distinct. They would reverse the Ring-based grant under Teague and preserve genuinely remaining independent claims.

### Tennard v. Dretke, No. 02-10038

Merits decision, June 24, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 22, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, 317 F.3d 476.

Tennard introduced an IQ score of 67 at the capital penalty phase. The special issues addressed deliberateness and future dangerousness. The lower federal courts rejected his claim and denied a certificate of appealability under a heightened relevance screen requiring a uniquely severe permanent handicap and a causal connection to the crime. The questions are whether those requirements are lawful and whether the mitigation claim warrants an issue-specific appeal.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The court of appeals must issue the appropriate certificate and adjudicate the preserved mitigation claim. The conviction and sentence remain subject to the ordinary merits and remedial process; certification alone supplies no sentencing writ.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O’CONNOR, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., each filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Supported culpability mitigation does not require a special diagnosis or crime nexus

**Holding and operative rule.** Supported intellectual-functioning and other culpability mitigation cannot be excluded from constitutional consideration merely because the defendant has not proved a uniquely severe permanent impairment or a causal connection between the impairment and the offense. Evidence may bear on culpability independently of deliberateness or future danger; relevance requires neither a completed categorical disability diagnosis nor favorable weight from the sentencer.

**Authority.** The seven Justices joining the Court’s opinion support the relevance rule and the certificate holding in Part II.

**Controlling explanation.** Graham v. Collins rejects the permanent-handicap restriction and protects the independent culpability significance of supported mitigation. Johnson v. Texas preserves that significance even when some evidence also bears on future danger. Penry v. Johnson requires a coherent, authorized means for the jury to give the evidence effect beyond the factual special issues. Tennard’s low IQ score supplies a supported basis for a culpability argument, even though it does not itself establish adaptive limitations, developmental onset or the categorical disability addressed in Atkins v. Virginia. Evidence that might have an aggravating use under future danger can still have an independent mitigating use. The dissenters would reconsider this underlying entitlement because of its relationship to structured capital discretion. The Court adheres to the settled distinction: directing the jury’s factual decisions does not permit legal exclusion of supported mitigation bearing independently on culpability.

##### II. This claim warrants an issue-specific certificate

**Holding and operative rule.** A certificate of appealability issues when the applicant makes a substantial showing that reasonable jurists could debate the constitutional claim under the habeas restrictions actually governing it. That threshold exceeds mere nonfrivolousness but does not require conclusive proof of constitutional error or statutory entitlement before the authorized appeal; Tennard satisfies it on the preserved effective-mitigation claim.

**Controlling explanation.** Slack v. McDaniel separates certification from the final merits, and Miller-El v. Cockrell requires meaningful debate about the claim as constrained by applicable habeas law. The IQ evidence and the submitted special issues make it reasonably debatable whether the jury had an effective route to use the evidence’s independent mitigating force. The unlawful heightened-relevance gate prevented the proper threshold assessment. On appeal the court must examine the complete charge and trial context, identify the relevant state merits adjudication and statutory limits, and resolve any properly preserved procedural bar. If it establishes a properly available original-charge mitigation defect, Brecht’s governing rule, as applied in Penry, places on the State the burden to show beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the error did not contribute to death. Failure then requires lawful conditional sentence correction. These are successive adjudicative questions; the supported threshold showing does not resolve their ultimate answers.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Graham v. Collins* supplies the rejection of the uniquely severe permanent-handicap screen and protection for independent culpability mitigation.
- *Johnson v. Texas* preserves effective consideration beyond the factual special issues.
- *Penry v. Johnson* supplies the coherent-verdict requirement and separates the constitutional and statutory inquiry from effect review.
- *Atkins v. Virginia* concerns categorical intellectual disability and is distinguished from ordinary mitigation relevance.
- *Slack v. McDaniel* is applied to issue-specific certification under the applicable habeas restrictions.
- *Miller-El v. Cockrell* is likewise applied to issue-specific certification under those restrictions.
- *Brecht v. Abrahamson*, as applied by *Penry*, governs any properly reached original-charge mitigation error’s separate State-borne effect inquiry.

#### Law After Decision

Capital mitigation relevance cannot be screened through a uniquely severe permanent-handicap requirement or an offense-causation requirement. Supported low intellectual functioning can justify debate over the jury’s effective ability to use independent culpability mitigation. Courts must issue a certificate when that substantial debate exists under the governing habeas limits, and then separately adjudicate the complete merits and any remedy.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia dissents. He would reconsider the Penry, Graham and Johnson mitigation entitlement because he regards its demand for discretionary mitigation beyond the special issues as conflicting with the constitutional requirement to structure capital discretion. His position rejects the underlying entitlement rather than presenting the Fifth Circuit’s displaced heightened-relevance gate as current law. On that ground he would affirm denial of certification.

Justice Thomas dissents. He maintains his objection to extending a constitutional requirement of individualized mitigation beyond the structured capital decision and would deny stare-decisis force to that broader requirement. His disagreement is independent of any factual presumption or unmade finding about Tennard’s impairment. He would affirm the certificate denial because he would reconsider the constitutional premise of the claim.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The court of appeals must issue an issue-specific certificate and decide the preserved mitigation claim under the correct relevance rule and applicable habeas conditions. If final adjudication establishes the requisite original-charge violation and the State fails its beyond-reasonable-doubt effect showing, conditional relief must provide a reasonable court-fixed period for lawful sentencing correction or a lawful noncapital sentence.

| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | Unjoined | Concurs in Parts I–III and in the judgment | Parts I–III; proposes government clear-and-convincing proof and rejects shifting the ultimate burden. |

| Dissent | Thomas | Unjoined | Dissents; would affirm under deferential review | Part I’s narrow authorization only; rejects the compelled hearing and full duration restrictions. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Congress authorized detention of the defined battlefield category

**Controlling proposition:** The AUMF authorizes military detention of an actual member of opposing Taliban forces captured in the authorized Afghanistan hostilities as an incident of the force Congress authorized, including when that person is a citizen. That Act supplies the congressional authority required by 18 U.S.C. §4001(a) for this category.

**Authority:** Lead Part I; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer directly adopt this proposition. Thomas joins this statutory proposition despite disagreeing with the remand and the additional limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress authorized necessary and appropriate force against the September 11-related forces identified by the AUMF. Capturing and incapacitating an opposing battlefield fighter is an established incident of that force. Citizenship does not categorically exempt an actual opposing fighter; Quirin supplies that limited law-of-war proposition. Section 4001(a) requires an Act of Congress, and this authorization supplies it for the defined category. Section 4001(b)'s military-institution exception concerns prison management and supplies no independent exception to subsection (a). The conclusion establishes authority for a category; the contested declaration still must be tested to determine whether Hamdi belongs to it.

##### II. A disputed citizen classification requires an effective factual contest

**Controlling proposition:** A citizen held in prolonged domestic military custody on this disputed battlefield classification must receive counsel, notice of the asserted factual basis, a meaningful opportunity to answer before a neutral decisionmaker, and effective judicial review and relief if custody is unlawful. Judicial acceptance of the executive declaration as conclusive does not satisfy that requirement; initial battlefield capture does not require the same procedures at the point of capture.

**Authority:** Lead Part II; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt the stated minimum and its remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews requires attention to the citizen's loss of liberty, the risk of an erroneous classification, and the Government's demonstrated military interests. The authority to detain an actual fighter cannot establish that a particular prisoner is one. Counsel, adequate notice and neutral assessment allow the factual dispute to be answered while preserving room for supported security accommodations that leave the contest meaningful. St. Cyr supplies the importance of effective legal-authority review; Demore distinguishes classification from the consequences of a classification actually established. The declaration here records allegations disputed by the petition; treating it as conclusive would eliminate the inquiry. The Court therefore rejects the existing restricted review and requires prompt proceedings giving this common minimum practical effect.

**Unresolved fractured question:** No majority adopts a complete burden of persuasion or the additional presumption and military-procedure permissions. The differing positions are not logical subsets yielding one proof rule: three permit the stated rebuttal allocation, two expressly withhold that assent, and the Chief requires heightened government proof. The controlling minimum remains enforceable without converting any of those proposals into law.

##### III. The authority remains tied to its combat purpose

**Controlling proposition:** Detention under this narrow battlefield authorization lasts only while the relevant active-hostilities justification for preventing return to combat continues. The authorization does not permit detention solely for interrogation; a changed purpose or end of the relevant justification requires renewed lawful authority.

**Authority:** Lead Part III; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer adopt the limitation directly; Souter and Ginsburg adopt it as a protection if the detention is sustained. These six support the same operative limit. Thomas does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Incapacitating an opposing fighter during the relevant conflict explains why detention falls within necessary and appropriate force. That explanation also limits its duration and purpose. Zadvydas supplies the method of relating detention to its statutory purpose, while its distinct immigration calendar and express security reservation remain intact. The present record establishes no cessation date; the lower court must examine the current hostilities-related basis as part of lawful-custody review. Interrogation alone cannot sustain detention under this construction after that basis fails. The statutory limit does not depend on treating the immigration regime as controlling wartime custody.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Quirin, 317 U.S. 1:** Supplies the limited proposition that citizenship does not itself remove an actual enemy belligerent from the law of war; it does not establish Hamdi's disputed status.
- **Ex parte Milligan, 71 U.S. 2:** Its civilian military-trial setting does not resolve detention of an actual opposing battlefield fighter under this authorization; the separate criminal-process position relies on its different account of the citizen's protection.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applied to the liberty, error and military interests supporting the common process minimum.
- **INS v. St. Cyr:** Its effective legal-authority review principle remains applicable with actual channel predicates.
- **Zadvydas v. Davis:** Its purpose-bound method informs the duration limit; its immigration-specific six-month framework and security reservation retain their original scope.
- **Demore v. Kim:** Its separation of actual classification from custody consequences informs review; its divided risk-burden rationale supplies no military-status proof rule.

#### Law After Decision

The AUMF now supplies an express judicially recognized basis for detention of the defined actual opposing battlefield fighters, subject to the controlling purpose-duration limit and the common procedural protections for disputed prolonged domestic citizen custody. Whether Hamdi meets the factual category and whether the present justification persists are adjudication on remand under those rules. The detailed proof allocation remains fractured.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the statutory category, common process minimum and purpose-duration limit but would require the Government to establish the qualifying facts and continuing lawful basis by clear and convincing evidence. The severe, potentially prolonged loss of liberty and the danger of classification error justify that heightened rule under Mathews. Reliable summaries and other supported security accommodations may alter evidentiary form only while preserving a meaningful defense; they may not make the executive label conclusive or shift the ultimate burden to the prisoner. The district court should fix an expeditious hearing schedule and a reasonable implementation period. Failure of the governmental showing should end military custody within a court-fixed period unless an independently lawful basis is established. The immigration calendar in Zadvydas does not supply that period.

**Part IV, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.** These three permit appropriately justified hearsay and a genuinely rebuttable evidentiary presumption: credible government evidence may require the detainee to rebut it with more persuasive evidence. The neutral decisionmaker must evaluate the evidence and permit a real response. A properly authorized military tribunal with adequate procedures may perform the initial status adjudication, with effective judicial review remaining. These are permissible accommodations, not compulsory steps or approval of an untested proceeding.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** They reject the Government's present statutory justification under §4001(a). An AUMF is not categorically incapable of authorizing detention; a demonstrated law-of-war detention within authorized force may present a sufficient basis, but the submitted justification is inadequate. Their statutory ground would require release from the challenged military custody unless a sufficient lawful basis were established. They nevertheless join the remand and the common protections because those give practical relief from the existing judgment. Their join does not accept a presumption casting the burden of rebuttal on Hamdi or a military procedure that obviates or truncates statutory habeas review.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Stevens, dissenting.** Where a citizen's enemy allegiance is contested, Milligan and the constitutional suspension structure require ordinary criminal process or a valid suspension. Quirin involved conceded belligerency and military prosecution and does not authorize replacing those protections with a judicially designed civil status hearing. They would require release from this military custody unless ordinary lawful criminal process is promptly supplied or a valid suspension changes the governing law. A new label or the majority's status proceeding cannot satisfy that alternative.

**Justice Thomas, dissenting.** Executive and military competence justify the deferential review sustained below. He accepts statutory authorization and also relies on broader executive war powers, but rejects the compelled new hearing. Intelligence gathering can serve a military purpose in his view, and political branches have substantially greater control over the necessity and duration of wartime detention than Part III permits. His position does not accept the full active-hostilities cutoff or interrogation-alone restriction.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§2241–2243; 18 U.S.C. §4001; Authorization for Use of Military Force, Pub. L. 107-40, 115 Stat. 224; U.S. Const., Art. I, §9, cl. 2, and Amend. V. The Mobbs declaration and the next-friend account present competing assertions of status.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the lower courts for prompt proceedings that provide the required counsel, notice, meaningful response, neutral determination and effective judicial review. The Government must identify the asserted authority, qualifying conduct and current custody justification. Effective relief must follow if that inquiry establishes unlawful custody.

**End of entry: Hamdi v. Rumsfeld, merits decision, June 28, 2004.**

### Holland v. Jackson, No. 03-1200

Merits decision, June 28, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Sixth Circuit, 80 F. App'x 392, which reversed denial of habeas relief from Jackson's murder conviction and life sentence. The question concerns reliance on the later Gooch witness proffer and whether the state court applied a prejudice standard contrary to Strickland. Hughes, the principal eyewitness, placed Gooch at the scene; seven years after conviction Jackson proffered her denial. The state court rejected the later request on a procedural basis and also addressed limited impeachment value. The court of appeals relied on that proffer and isolated preponderance or probability language despite the state court's statement of the reasonable-probability standard. The State preserved its objection to reliance on the new material.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Evidence development and statutory review have separate requirements

**Controlling proposition:** A federal habeas court may not deem a state merits decision objectively unreasonable by relying on material not properly before that court without first identifying a lawful statutory and procedural basis for considering it. Section 2254(e)(2) applies when deficient diligence or greater fault is attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the state factual basis; an incomplete record alone does not trigger it.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Michael Wayne Williams makes attributable failure the trigger. If that trigger is established, the claim must rely either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or on a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence. It must also satisfy the cumulative requirement that the facts would establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Those alternatives and the cumulative requirement cannot be replaced by a seven-year rule. Terry Williams and Lockyer preserve the separate objective statutory inquiry into the state adjudication, using governing substantive holdings established at that adjudication; §§2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) retain their separate factual roles. The Sixth Circuit did not resolve the state rejection of the proffer or the necessary diligence predicates before relying on Gooch's account. Potential impeachment value does not cure that omission. An alternative state merits discussion does not itself eliminate an independent procedural basis for rejecting material, while a record-admission restriction does not automatically establish an adequate and independent default of the underlying claim. Any such default and applicable exception need their own predicates.

##### II. The actual prejudice analysis controls

**Controlling proposition:** A state court acts contrary to Strickland when it actually requires a more-likely-than-not different outcome instead of a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the result. Isolated shorthand or a burden for proving underlying facts does not establish that substitution when the complete opinion applies the proper legal standard; a correct citation does not cure contrary actual reasoning.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Visciotti requires examination of the whole state opinion and supported record. References to preponderance, disbelief of an eyewitness or probability must be read in their function rather than treated as automatic legal error. Strickland separates proof of what occurred from the legal assessment of prejudice. Here the state court expressly stated reasonable probability, and its other language can be read consistently with that rule; detached phrases therefore cannot sustain the writ. Conversely, the opening citation alone does not establish a reasonable application. Lockyer requires the independent statutory threshold as well as an underlying constitutional violation, and Gentry's contextual performance analysis does not admit the new proffer or decide prejudice. If the claim remains available after the applicable gates, the appellate court must perform those inquiries on the evidence that may lawfully be considered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420:** Applied to applicant-attributed failure, diligence and the separate statutory exceptions for factual development.
- **Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362:** Applied to the independent contrary-to and objectively-unreasonable-application clauses.
- **Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668:** Its reasonable-probability standard governs constitutional prejudice; a factual proof burden is a different question.
- **Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19:** Applied to the complete state reasoning and supported record, preserving both its rejection of isolated-word analysis and its substantive reasonableness inquiry.
- **Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63:** Preserves the state-adjudication substantive cutoff, separate statutory threshold and underlying constitutional requirement.
- **Yarborough v. Gentry:** Its contextual performance review remains distinct from admission of new material and prejudice.

#### Law After Decision

The governing Williams, Strickland, Visciotti and Lockyer rules remain in force and require setting aside this inadequately grounded grant. Lawful consideration of the proffer, attributable diligence, any established default and its exception, and final constitutional prejudice are separate applications to be resolved on remand. Permission to develop evidence does not itself satisfy §2254(d) or establish a new claim.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§2254(d)(1), (d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2); Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668. The passage of seven years is a record circumstance; it is not itself an adjudication of applicant-attributed lack of diligence.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit judgment granting habeas relief is reversed. On remand, that court must identify the state merits record and resolve the lawful basis for considering the late proffer, attributable diligence and any properly presented procedural barrier. If the claim remains available, it must apply the statutory and Strickland inquiries on the evidence that may lawfully be considered. An independently sufficient established ground may dispose of the claim without unnecessary further adjudication.

Necessary factfinding returns to the district court. Added evidence may be developed only through a lawful route satisfying the actual statutory conditions. The conviction remains operative unless a lawful grant of relief follows.

**End of entry: Holland v. Jackson, merits decision, June 28, 2004.**

### Missouri v. Seibert, No. 02-1371

Merits decision, June 28, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 9, 2003. On certiorari to the Missouri Supreme Court, 93 S.W.3d 700, which suppressed Seibert's warned repetition and reversed her second-degree murder conviction. Police obtained an unwarned confession during thirty to forty minutes of questioning, paused about twenty minutes, then used the same officer, substantially overlapping questions and references to the first admissions after warnings and a signed form. The officer acknowledged deliberately using the question-first method. The question is whether those midstream warnings supplied an effective choice and waiver.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would vacate and remand for ordinary Elstad analysis.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. KENNEDY, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment. BREYER, J., filed a concurring opinion. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion. The joins and their distinct scopes are set out below.

| Writing or portion | Author and joins | Relationship to judgment | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective effectiveness rule | Souter; joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer | Controlling rule supporting affirmance | Applies independently of proof of deliberate evasion. |
| Deliberate, uncured application | Souter; joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer | Six Justices support affirmance | This sequence requires exclusion. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Kennedy | Joins the stated application | Deliberate-evasion trigger with effective cure; ordinary Elstad analysis for other omissions. |
| Concurrence | Breyer | Joins the lead opinion | Adds a fruits rule subject to good faith. |
| Dissent | O'Connor; joined by Scalia and Thomas | Would vacate and remand | Ordinary Elstad inquiry into the actual statements and circumstances. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Midstream warnings must communicate a genuine choice

**Controlling proposition:** When a confession follows unwarned custodial questioning, the prosecution must establish a knowing, voluntary and effective warning waiver by a preponderance of the evidence on the complete circumstances. In a coordinated question-first sequence, courts must assess the initial questioning's completeness, overlapping content, timing, setting, personnel, references to prior admissions and meaningful corrective measures; deliberate evasion is powerful evidence but is not necessary to find objectively ineffective warnings. A signed form cannot cure circumstances in which the warnings could not reasonably convey a real choice to stop.

**Authority:** The five-Justice objective-rule portion identified above directly controls at this generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda protects an informed choice in custodial interrogation, and Dickerson prevents replacing its constitutional safeguard with voluntariness alone. Elstad permits a genuinely effective later warning and waiver; it does not make every recitation effective simply because it follows an unwarned statement. The relevant circumstances determine whether the second interview offered an intelligible fresh choice or instead continued a confession already treated as irrevocable. The factors are considerations, not a numerical test. No fixed waiting period or additional sentence supplies a categorical safe harbor. Missouri's reliance on a voluntary answer and signed waiver does not answer whether the warnings communicated their required choice. A prior unwarned statement is also not an automatic permanent bar to a later confession: genuine separation and an effective waiver remain legally significant.

##### II. This continuous, deliberate sequence requires exclusion

**Controlling proposition:** The warned repetition obtained through this detailed initial confession, short break, same officer, overlapping content and use of prior admissions must be suppressed. The established deliberate method lacked effective intervening measures that restored an informed choice.

**Authority:** The six-Justice application portion identified above directly supports this sufficient ground. Kennedy's application join does not extend to Part I's intent-independent rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The officer first obtained the developed confession and then used it to elicit its repetition. The record supplies no meaningful change that conveyed a new choice to decline. Elstad's transition from a brief unwarned exchange to an effective warned interview is materially different. An intent requirement would not alter this result because the officer established deliberate coordination himself. The warning form consequently did not make the repeated statement admissible, and Missouri's requested reinstatement of the conviction fails. The state suppression and conviction-reversal judgment is affirmed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436:** Applied to effective warnings and knowing, voluntary waiver in the whole custodial sequence.
- **Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Its constitutional safeguard governs; voluntariness alone cannot substitute for effective warnings and waiver.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Distinguished on the actual continuity and lack of effective separation; its rule for a genuinely effective subsequent warning and waiver remains.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157:** The ordinary prosecution burden to establish waiver remains distinct from proving actual coercion.

#### Law After Decision

A direct five-Justice majority establishes objective effectiveness for later warnings following unwarned custodial questioning. Courts apply the complete-sequence rule without making subjective intent indispensable or treating any earlier omission as permanent taint. No Marks synthesis is needed. Suppression of this repeated confession follows under both the controlling objective rule and the six-Justice deliberate-uncured application.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He would retain ordinary Elstad analysis for initial warning omissions other than a deliberate two-stage method designed to defeat Miranda. For that deliberate method, the repeated statement is excluded unless effective curative measures restore an informed choice. A genuinely substantial break or an adequate additional explanation may suffice in an appropriate case, but neither automatically cures the defect and neither is established here. He joins the deliberate, uncured application but rejects making objective ineffectiveness alone the new general midstream trigger.

**Justice Breyer, concurring.** He joins the objective rule and would also exclude fruits of an unwarned interrogation unless the omission was in good faith. The proved deliberate method here fails that exception. His fruits formulation does not supply the other Justices' general rationale.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** Elstad calls for examining the actual statements and circumstances under the ordinary warning, waiver and voluntariness principles. They reject both the new objective midstream rule and a test turning on officer purpose. Actual coercion of an initial statement and its effect on a later statement remain relevant, as do the complete circumstances and a genuinely voluntary later answer. They would vacate and remand for that inquiry, rather than declare the repetition automatically admissible or treat the signed form as conclusive.

#### Source Notes

U.S. Const., Amends. V and XIV; Missouri Supreme Court, 93 S.W.3d 700. The officer's testimony establishes deliberate coordination. Warning effectiveness is distinct from a finding of actual coercion.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Missouri judgment suppressing the repeated confession and reversing the second-degree murder conviction is affirmed and remains effective. At any retrial, Missouri must exclude both the initial statement, already suppressed, and the challenged repetition. It may retry Seibert on independently admissible evidence under ordinary state procedure. The reversed conviction cannot itself support continued custody; custody must rest on valid charges and ordinary state authority.

**End of entry: Missouri v. Seibert, merits decision, June 28, 2004.**

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Section 2241 reaches these Guantánamo custody claims

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2241 permits a substantial claim that reachable United States custodial officials hold a person at Guantánamo in violation of federal law where the United States exercises the complete and continuing jurisdiction and control established here. Foreign citizenship and Cuba's reserved ultimate sovereignty do not alone defeat that statutory jurisdiction; the prerequisites and merits of relief remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice habeas portion identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute concerns custody exercised by officials whom the court can reach. The United States controls administration, access and confinement at this enduring base outside an active battlefield. Reserved ultimate sovereignty does not make that governmental control disappear. Eisentrager involved a different territorial and posttrial setting, including military convictions; its continuing framework does not foreclose these unadjudicated, potentially indefinite restraints under the control arrangement here. Braden rejects an invariable requirement that the prisoner be physically present in the forum; it need not be read to destroy Eisentrager's statutory foundation to reach this result. St. Cyr and Calcano-Martinez preserve effective statutory review while retaining actual channel predicates. The Government's proposed categorical alien-abroad rule would deny the writ despite this control and reachable custodian. The narrower statutory answer requires no universal decision about extraterritorial constitutional rights. Disputed innocence remains for the custody adjudication.

##### II. Alien status does not replace the civil claims' actual predicates

**Controlling proposition:** Nonresident aliens held at Guantánamo are not categorically disabled from invoking an otherwise available federal civil action and jurisdictional grant. Courts must examine each pleaded §§1331, 1350 and APA theory for its own jurisdiction, cause of action, reviewability, immunity and remedy requirements; neither alien status nor recognition of habeas jurisdiction establishes or defeats those conditions by itself.

**Authority:** All nine join rejection of universal alien incapacity and remand of genuinely independent statutory claims. The seven Justices joining the complete civil-remand portion identified above also require reversal of the categorical dismissal of the whole civil branch. Scalia and Thomas preserve their different disposition for claims necessarily dependent on the constitutional entitlement they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira and Kadic distinguish jurisdiction from a defined domestic action with actual norm, actor and remedy predicates. Those decisions preclude a universal incapacity to sue but establish neither an ATS detention action nor a waiver of United States immunity. The same source-specific method applies to Al Odah's pleaded civil theories. Eisentrager is not a general litigation disability for every independent statutory claim merely because its claimant is an alien held abroad. The court below disposed of the branch categorically instead of resolving those requirements. Remand permits the necessary individual legal inquiry, including the actual APA reviewability and waiver, the claimed ATS norm and action, and available relief. A statutory label alone cannot make an otherwise identical constitutional theory independent.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Johnson v. Eisentrager, 339 U.S. 763:** Distinguished on the enduring control, absence of military convictions and actual confinement setting; its statutory framework and unaddressed constitutional questions are not generally overruled.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Applied to reject an invariable physical-presence requirement when the proper custodian is reachable.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Their effective statutory-review principles inform the actual §2241 channel and preserve its predicates.
- **Abebe-Jira v. Negewo; Kadic v. Karadzic:** Their distinct jurisdiction and defined-action holdings require claim-specific civil analysis and do not themselves establish a detention action or governmental immunity waiver.

#### Law After Decision

Section 2241 now expressly reaches the defined Guantánamo control arrangement when the responsible custodian is reachable. Courts must adjudicate the actual custody basis and effective relief; the asserted substantive extraterritorial constitutional entitlement remains undecided because the statutory ground resolves the habeas question. Al Odah's independent civil theories return for their actual statutory, immunity and remedial inquiries. Whether a claim satisfies those rules is ordinary claim adjudication, not a reservation of the rule rejecting universal alien incapacity.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting.** Eisentrager retains the statutory limit for foreign nationals captured and confined outside sovereign United States territory. Braden concerns a domestic detainer and does not remove that foreign-custody limit. They also reject the independently presented constitutional entitlement on these foreign-capture and foreign-confinement facts. They therefore would affirm habeas dismissal and any civil claim necessarily dependent on that rejected entitlement. Their agreement with Abebe-Jira and Kadic's jurisdiction distinctions nevertheless prevents treating aliens as universally incapable of suit. They accept remand of genuinely independent statutory claims for jurisdiction, recognized action, immunity, reviewability and remedy analysis. That limited remand recognizes no new detention action or waiver and does not allow the identical constitutional claim to proceed through a changed label.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1350 and 2241–2243; Administrative Procedure Act; United States-Cuba lease arrangements. Allegations of innocence and unlawful treatment are disputed allegations. The petitioners have not received the military convictions present in Eisentrager.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The habeas dismissal and the categorical dismissal of the civil branch are reversed. The district court must set a prompt return and briefing schedule, obtain a return identifying the legal and factual custody basis, and provide access needed to present the custody challenge. Substantiated security needs may receive appropriate protective treatment. The court must adjudicate preserved defenses and separately examine each civil theory under its actual legal predicates. It must grant effective relief within its authority if unlawful detention or another actionable violation is established.

### Rumsfeld v. Padilla, No. 03-1027

Merits decision, June 28, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Padilla was arrested in Chicago on a material-witness warrant and brought to New York. That petition named the President, Secretary Rumsfeld and commander Melanie Marr. The questions are the proper custodian and forum and, only if reachable, detention authority.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York forum ruling | Reversed and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the exceptional forum | Prompt examination of lawful transfer authority; otherwise dismissal without prejudice. |
| New York detention-merits adjudication | Vacated, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the confined no-authority ruling and remand to implement relief from military custody | Detention legality remains for a proper action. |

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, KENNEDY and THOMAS, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which O'CONNOR, J., joined. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A new domestic physical-custody petition ordinarily follows the immediate custodian

**Controlling proposition:** A new petition challenging present physical confinement at a known domestic facility ordinarily must name the immediate custodian and proceed in a district with authority over that custodian. Senior supervisory control alone does not replace those requirements; a properly supported continuing-jurisdiction or exceptional-forum claim requires its own predicates.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2242 and 2243 direct the petition and return to the person exercising the custody challenged. The known brig commander exercises Padilla's present physical custody in South Carolina. Braden concerns the restraint actually challenged and does not make every supervising official a domestic custodian; Endo preserves an already properly filed proceeding despite a later transfer. Padilla's earlier material-witness challenge was a different proceeding, and this military-custody petition followed the transfer. It therefore cannot borrow an earlier habeas filing that did not occur. St. Cyr protects effective review of authority, while Calcano-Martinez preserves the predicates of the actual channel. An effective South Carolina forum answers the claimed absence of review without treating the Secretary's supervisory authority as sufficient everywhere. The record establishes no bad-faith transfer exception. Detention authority remains outside the Court's reach in this forum posture.

##### II. The forum remedy must preserve an available lawful route

**Controlling proposition:** On identifying this forum defect, the lower court must promptly consider an authorized transfer and apply the actual conditions of that authority; otherwise it must dismiss without prejudice to a properly filed challenge. Section 1631 requires a qualifying court's finding of want of jurisdiction, an eligible destination where the action could have been brought when filed, and the interest of justice; when those conditions are met, transfer is mandatory and the original filing date is preserved.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The transfer statute supplies an alternative to requiring a new filing only when its text applies. The court must identify the character of the defect and the available destination rather than assume that every custodian or forum problem falls within §1631. Consideration does not establish eligibility, but an available mandatory transfer cannot be disregarded. If the conditions are absent and no other lawful route permits transfer, dismissal without prejudice allows immediate proper refiling. The receiving court must address properly presented requests for interim protection and custody review within its own lawful authority. That arrangement enforces the actual forum rules while preserving the effective review channel.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Endo, 323 U.S. 283:** Its continuation of already attached jurisdiction is distinguished because the military-custody petition here followed the transfer.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Its focus on the challenged custody and reachable custodian does not replace the ordinary domestic immediate-custodian rule with senior supervision.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Applied to preserve effective statutory authority review with actual custody, respondent and forum predicates.
- **Zadvydas v. Davis:** Its removal-purpose detention holding supplies no domestic military-detention merits answer in this forum disposition.

#### Law After Decision

New domestic physical-custody petitions follow the ordinary immediate-custodian and territorial forum requirements, with actual continuing-jurisdiction or other exceptions considered on their established predicates. Conditional transfer consideration and dismissal without prejudice preserve the proper route. The AUMF's application to this domestic arrest and the constitutional detention question await an action in a forum able to decide them.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Kennedy, joined by Justice O'Connor, concurring.** The custodian and forum limitations concern personal jurisdiction or venue rather than the existence of federal subject-matter habeas power. They can be waived, and a supported exception may apply when officials obstruct access through concealment, evasive transfer or other interference with the appropriate forum. The actual filing sequence and known custodian establish no such exception here. They therefore join the ordinary-forum result and the conditional transfer consideration without treating it as a categorical subject-matter rule.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The official who personally directed this particular replacement of judicial custody by military restraint, controls it and can end it is a reachable custodian under a confined exceptional-control theory. That ground neither finds bad faith nor treats the later petition as filed before the transfer. On the merits, the domestic arrest and designation fail to establish the congressional authorization and supported belligerent predicate required under §4001(a) and the AUMF. Section 4001(b)'s prison-management provision does not exempt this detention from subsection (a). They would implement relief ending the challenged military custody while permitting an identified independently lawful custody basis. Their position does not decide that every domestic arrestee is beyond the law of war or confer immunity from prosecution.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§2241–2243 and 1631; 18 U.S.C. §4001; AUMF, Pub. L. 107-40, 115 Stat. 224. The material-witness challenge preceded the military-custody petition; no adjudicated bad-faith transfer finding is present.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The forum ruling is reversed and the New York detention-merits adjudication is vacated. The district court must promptly examine available statutory transfer authority, the character of the defect and the eligible destination. It must transfer when the actual predicates and the interest of justice require it; a qualifying transfer preserves the original filing date as the applicable statute directs. If no lawful transfer is available, dismissal must be without prejudice to immediate proper refiling against the actual custodian in the proper district.

Officials must accurately identify the custodian and location. The receiving court must consider properly presented requests for counsel, interim protection and detention review within its own lawful authority. This forum disposition leaves the legality of detention for a proper action.

**End of entry: Rumsfeld v. Padilla, merits decision, June 28, 2004.**

### United States v. Patane, No. 02-1183

Merits decision, June 28, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 9, 2003. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 304 F.3d 1013, which rejected the district court's probable-cause rationale but affirmed suppression of a pistol as the fruit of incomplete Miranda warnings. Officers investigating a restraining-order violation arrested Patane and began warnings; he interrupted by saying he knew his rights. A subsequent answer disclosed the pistol's location. The reviewed question assumes the answer was voluntary and asks whether the incomplete warning alone requires suppression of its physical fruit.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm suppression. Breyer would vacate and remand to determine whether the warning omission was in good faith, with exclusion absent that exception.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY and THOMAS, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined. SOUTER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. BREYER, J., filed a separate dissenting opinion.

The Court's opinion adopts the narrow physical-evidence remedy. The Thomas concurrence's broader duty and deterrence theory has only its two named supporters. The separate dissents seek different judgments.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Controlling proposition:** Failure to give complete Miranda warnings restricts case-in-chief use of the resulting statement but does not by itself require suppression of nontestimonial physical evidence derived from a voluntary answer. When voluntariness is properly contested, the prosecution must establish it under the applicable burden and satisfy other independent admission requirements; an unwarned answer is not thereby a valid waiver, and the rule does not extend to evidence produced by actual coercion.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the narrow remedial proposition and reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** Dickerson preserves Miranda's constitutional warning-and-waiver safeguard and prevents voluntariness alone from authorizing the answer's ordinary case-in-chief use. It left the physical-fruits question open. Tucker and Elstad show that constitutional status does not require identical exclusion for an unwarned answer and every derivative evidentiary product. Here the offered item is a pistol, the reviewed answer is assumed voluntary, and the challenge rests on the incomplete warning itself. The direct gain to investigators identified by the dissent does not eliminate those distinct remedial categories. Admitting a voluntary answer's physical product neither establishes a knowing waiver nor resolves an actual-coercion or independent acquisition claim. Chavez's fractured no-use damages disposition supplies no controlling theory of physical-fruit admissibility; its independent due-process inquiry remains distinct. The appellate court therefore erred in treating Dickerson's constitutional characterization alone as a sufficient fruits rule. The warning-only ground for suppressing this pistol must be reversed, with any genuinely preserved independent objection considered in its proper scope.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Miranda v. Arizona; Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Their constitutional case-in-chief warning-and-waiver core is preserved; Dickerson's open physical-fruits question is resolved at the voluntary, nontestimonial scope here.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433; Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Their differentiated consequences support this physical-remedy distinction without replacing the original statement's warning requirement.
- **Chavez v. Martinez, 538 U.S. 760:** Its no-use damages judgment had no controlling Fifth Amendment rationale and does not decide admissibility; its separate interrogation-abuse inquiry survives at its actual scope.

#### Law After Decision

Incomplete warnings alone no longer justify excluding nontestimonial physical fruits of a voluntary answer. Statements, actual coercion and independently unlawful acquisition retain their own rules and properly preserved remedies. An incomplete warning does not establish waiver; the dispute decided concerns this evidentiary category.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, concurring.** They also rely on the Self-Incrimination Clause's criminal-case and testimonial focus. In their view, mere omission of warnings does not itself supply the constitutional violation that excluding this physical evidence would deter. Their broader characterization is unnecessary to the five-Justice narrow remedy and is not joined by O'Connor, Kennedy or Stone-Zsela. It provides no permission for actual coercion.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The gun is the direct investigative product of unwarned testimonial disclosure. Allowing that benefit weakens the constitutional safeguard in practice and encourages bypassing warnings. Tucker's setting and Elstad's effective intervening warning do not resolve this direct-fruit problem. They would affirm suppression without requiring a new finding of deliberate evasion or actual coercion.

**Justice Breyer, dissenting.** He would exclude fruits of unwarned questioning unless the warning omission was in good faith. Because this record establishes neither good faith nor deliberate evasion, he would vacate and remand for that inquiry. Incomplete warnings and the absence of a complete waiver do not themselves decide whether the exception applies; he does not vote for unconditional affirmance or unconditional admission.

#### Source Notes

U.S. Const., Amends. V and XIV; Tenth Circuit, 304 F.3d 1013. Voluntariness is the reviewed premise. Patane's interruption of warnings does not establish a complete waiver, and the record supplies no finding resolving good faith or deliberate evasion.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Suppression is reversed insofar as it rests solely on the Miranda physical-fruits ground, and the prosecution returns to the lower court. The automatic warning-only bar to the pistol is removed. The pistol may be admitted if otherwise lawful; the court must apply any genuinely preserved independent admission requirement, including properly contested voluntariness under its applicable burden, while respecting the existing appellate rejection of the probable-cause rationale. Case-in-chief use of the unwarned answer remains governed by the separate statement rule.

**End of entry: United States v. Patane, merits decision, June 28, 2004.**

### Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, No. 03-218

Merits decision, June 29, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 2, 2004; decided June 29, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, which again affirmed the preliminary injunction against the Child Online Protection Act after this Court's 2002 remand. The question is whether that protection should continue because the Government has not adequately justified the restriction against plausible, less restrictive filtering alternatives. The Government seeks dissolution; the challengers seek continued protection and final adjudication on the proper record.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer.

The Attorney General remains bound by the existing injunction without an enforcement gap. It must prevent the demonstrated chilling and access burdens; a narrower order requires a supported showing that it would provide an effective cure. Modification requires a supported legal or factual basis. The opposing Justices would reverse the filtering-based preliminary determination and remand for lawful disposition of remaining preserved grounds.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER and THOMAS, JJ., joined, and in Part I of which O'CONNOR, SCALIA and BREYER, JJ., also joined. STEVENS, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which GINSBURG, J., joined as to the geographic-burden and criminal-chill discussion. BREYER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which O'CONNOR and SCALIA, JJ., joined as to the comparative application but not the national-community construction.

| Writing or part | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Ginsburg | All eight other Justices | Shared governing framework | Complete statutory operation, protected adult speech, governmental burden and preliminary-review standard |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas | Affirms and remands | Comparative application, continuing effective injunction and final-record procedure |
| Concurrence | Stevens | Ginsburg, except the independently sufficient geographic ground and rejection of a national construction | Joins the Court's opinion and judgment | Additional geographic-burden and criminal-chill grounds |
| Dissent, comparative-application portion | Breyer | O'Connor and Scalia | Would reverse this injunction basis and remand | Narrow statutory coverage, adult-access defenses and protection outside activated household filters |
| Dissent, national-standard portion | Breyer | No other Justice | Necessary qualification of Breyer's dissent | National adult-community construction; O'Connor and Scalia do not join this portion |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The Government must justify the complete restriction against concrete alternatives

**Controlling proposition:** When a content-based restriction burdens protected adult Internet expression and a concrete, plausibly effective less restrictive alternative is identified, the Government must establish why that alternative's comparative inadequacy warrants the broader restriction in serving the compelling child-protection interest. At the preliminary stage, an injunction may remain when the supported record shows a likelihood that the Government cannot meet that burden and the other equitable requirements are satisfied; appellate review respects the district court's discretion while independently correcting legal error.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, supplies the common framework.

**Controlling explanation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union protects adults' access to lawful Internet expression and requires workable safeguards against a criminal restriction's actual burdens. United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group places the comparative justification burden on the Government after a concrete lesser restraint is presented. Legislative predictions deserve serious consideration, but do not settle that inquiry. Neither a merely imagined alternative nor a demand for perfect filtering supplies the proper comparison.

The earlier Ashcroft decision requires the statute to be considered as a whole. COPA reaches knowing World Wide Web communications in interstate or foreign commerce, made for commercial purposes with knowledge of their character, available to persons under seventeen and harmful to minors. Its commercial category concerns a regular business course and profit objective, with knowing posting or solicitation; it requires neither actual profit nor a sole or principal business. That category does not turn protected literature into commercial advertising.

Harmful material is obscenity or material satisfying all three additional elements: taken as a whole, prurient appeal or pandering with respect to minors under contemporary community standards; specified patently offensive sexual depictions, descriptions or representations with respect to minors; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for minors. The content element includes actual or simulated sexual acts or contacts and lewd displays of genitals or post-pubescent female breasts. The community-standards language expressly appears in the prurience element; serious value remains objective under Pope v. Illinois.

The defenses, exclusions and privacy conditions stated below matter to practical adult access. Their availability reduces coverage and potential liability, but does not prove every reader has affordable, anonymous and effective access. Courts must evaluate their operation, rather than add missing offense limits or treat material's lack of value for minors as loss of adult protection.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 231(b) excludes telecommunications carriers, Internet-access providers and information-location services to the extent they act in those capacities. It separately protects intermediaries transmitting, storing, retrieving, hosting, formatting or translating another's communication without selecting or altering its content; deletion consistent with §231(c) or §230 does not defeat that protection. Section 231(c)(1)'s alternative good-faith access defenses are credit card or debit account use, adult access code or adult personal identification number, an age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable measures feasible with available technology. Section 231(c)(2) separately protects good-faith implementation or restriction activity against a cause of action only if the activity violates no law carrying a criminal or civil penalty. Section 231(d) requires protection against unauthorized access to collected information and prior written or electronic consent for disclosure from the adult concerned or the parent or guardian of a person under seventeen; disclosure is permitted when necessary to communicate, to conduct legitimate related business activity, or under an authorizing court order. These provisions protect information without guaranteeing absolute anonymity.

##### II. The present record supports continued protection and a prompt merits adjudication

**Controlling proposition:** The district court did not abuse its discretion by preserving the injunction because the supported comparison shows a likelihood that the Government cannot justify COPA's adult-speech burdens against user-controlled filtering. The injunction continues while the district court promptly resolves final validity through the integrated statutory inquiry; supported findings remain unless genuinely new evidence or legal error warrants reconsideration, and challengers need not disprove every conceivable alternative.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, controls by six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Filtering acts at the receiving end. It can address foreign-origin and non-Web material outside COPA's coverage while permitting adults to choose lawful reading without publisher-imposed verification or identification. COPA's defenses do not eliminate the record's expense, anonymity and access concerns, and its verification methods remain subject to circumvention.

The dissent correctly identifies the statute's narrower harmful-material category and its added protection for children whose parents do not activate filters. Those are relevant advantages. But a beneficial increment does not itself demonstrate why this broader adult burden is necessary. The Government's evidence does not adequately answer filtering's wider reach and reduced interference with adult choice. The Court neither assumes universal parental participation nor requires flawless filtering; it requires the actual comparative justification that Playboy assigns to the Government.

United States v. American Library Association concerns an assisted library service, distinct LSTA and E-rate disabling permissions, and effective adult access in that setting. Its statutory permission is not a finding that every request is honored; its constitutional protection concerns prompt whole-session disabling or an equally effective method without disclosure of protected reading or an approved reason, subject to reasonable technical, supervision and resource procedures. That decision establishes neither universal filter effectiveness nor justification for criminal restrictions on private publishers.

Preserving relief avoids the demonstrated chill while the court develops any material changes in date-appropriate evidence. The preliminary posture leaves final facial validity and supported particular applications for the merits record. Any severance must follow an actual statutory division, rather than create a new offense. The district court must set a prompt schedule, identify genuinely disputed updated facts and state the enforceable scope of its final decree.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, 535 U.S. 564, is applied: rejection of community standards as an automatic ground of invalidity requires integrated review and preserves consideration of actual adult burdens.
- Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union is applied to protected adult Internet access, workable safeguards and genuine textual severance; COPA's different elements receive their own examination.
- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group is applied to the Government's comparative justification burden and the need for concrete alternatives rather than perfect protection.
- Pope v. Illinois is applied to the objective serious-value inquiry; community approval does not supply that element.
- United States v. American Library Association is distinguished at its library-service, funding and effective-access scope. Its distinct statutory disabling provisions retain their own terms.

#### Law After Decision

Courts considering a preliminary challenge to a content-based adult-speech restriction must compare the complete law with concrete lesser restraints under the Government's continuing justification burden. COPA remains enjoined on the present record. Genuine changes in evidence may be considered at final adjudication under that same rule; the preliminary judgment does not resolve final facial validity.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Ginsburg in the geographic-burden and criminal-chill discussion, joins the Court's opinion and adds that those features reinforce the need for preliminary protection. National Web speakers cannot select every local audience, so a restrictive community's standard can burden protected reading elsewhere. The whole-material and serious-value requirements narrow coverage, but value for minors does not exhaust the speech adults may receive. Reno's protection of adult access and the statutory inquiry required by the earlier Ashcroft decision make that practical burden material. Stevens separately continues to regard the geographic objection as independently sufficient and rejects insertion of a national standard into the enacted community language. Ginsburg's join is confined to the geographic concern within the complete statutory inquiry and the criminal-chill discussion.

Both emphasize the distinctive chill of a criminal publisher restriction. An affirmative defense may ultimately defeat liability while still exposing a speaker to the risk and cost of prosecution, encouraging silence before a court ever adjudicates the defense. The information-protection provisions and access options therefore do not eliminate the practical burden merely by existing in the statute. Their additional concern supports retaining the preliminary restraint while the complete statutory operation is adjudicated; it does not expand the Court's common filtering holding into a final declaration of facial invalidity.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Scalia in this comparative-application portion, agrees that protected adult speech is involved and that the Government bears the strict-scrutiny burden. They would reverse the determination that filtering establishes a sufficient likelihood of success to retain this injunction basis. Their disagreement concerns the supported comparison, not the governing standard.

COPA's conjunctive harmful-material requirements, commercial-coverage limits and alternative good-faith adult-access defenses materially reduce the burden compared with the broader restriction in Reno. The dissent accepts verification costs, anonymity concerns and the possibility of circumvention; the privacy provision does not erase those effects. It nevertheless finds the remaining burden sufficiently fitted to a protection filtering does not supply by itself: children whose parents do not obtain or activate household controls receive the benefit of COPA's default restriction. The comparative position stated in the Playboy dissent gives that activation gap substantial weight while recognizing that filters reach foreign and non-Web sources and preserve adult choice.

In these Justices' assessment, the narrower statutory category and access routes, considered together with that additional protection, carry the Government's burden at this preliminary stage. They would remand for lawful disposition of other preserved grounds. Breyer's conclusion further depends on the national-community construction stated below; O'Connor and Scalia join the comparative reasoning without adopting that construction.

Justice Breyer additionally requires the national adult community to supply the governing community standard. His earlier Ashcroft position reads the phrase in statutory context and in light of the serious difficulty caused when restrictive local standards govern nationally distributed speech. Different jury applications of one national standard differ from different substantive standards. That construction is necessary to his conclusion that the narrower offense and access defenses sufficiently answer the filtering comparison. O'Connor and Scalia do not join this construction.

#### Source Notes

No. 03-218; judgment below, 322 F.3d 240; 47 U.S.C. §231; Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, 535 U.S. 564; Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union, 521 U.S. 844; United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, 529 U.S. 803; United States v. American Library Association, 539 U.S. 194; Pope v. Illinois, 481 U.S. 497. The case concerns preliminary protection before final merits adjudication.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The preliminary injunction remains continuously effective. The district court must proceed promptly under the complete statutory and comparative inquiry, identify material disputed updated facts and specify the scope and any genuine severance in its final order. A supported change to relief must preserve an effective cure for demonstrated speech burdens.

**End of entry: Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, merits decision, June 29, 2004.**

### Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain and United States v. Alvarez-Machain, Nos. 03-339 and 03-485

Merits decision, June 29, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

The two dockets are consolidated. Argued March 30, 2004; decided June 29, 2004. On writs of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which sustained the Alien Tort Statute damages judgment against Sosa and revived the Federal Tort Claims Act claim against the United States. DEA officials arranged a seizure by Mexican nationals, including Sosa, followed by detention in Mexico for less than a day before delivery into United States custody. This Court's 1992 judgment in United States v. Alvarez-Machain barred the prosecution founded on that custody, and he was returned to Mexico without a trial in the United States. Alvarez then sought civil recovery. The questions concern a domestic action for that asserted arbitrary detention and whether domestic planning removes the same foreign-detention claim from the FTCA's foreign-country exception.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| No. 03-339, ATS damages against Sosa | Reversed and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacate the damages award resting on the rejected brief arbitrary-detention action |
| No. 03-485, FTCA claim against the United States | Reversed and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Restore dismissal of the claim for Mexican detention under §2680(k) |

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined, and in Parts I and III of which SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., also joined. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, in which THOMAS, J., joined. GINSBURG, J., filed a concurring opinion concerning the FTCA, in which BREYER, J., joined. BREYER, J., filed a concurring opinion concerning the ATS.

| Writing or part | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Stevens | All eight other Justices | Common threshold principles | Jurisdiction, action and waiver distinctions; continuing force of the already adopted actions |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reverses the ATS judgment | Confined received-action method, insufficient specific norm and domestic remedial predicate |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A federal forum, a domestic action and sovereign consent remain separate

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1350 gives district courts jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort claim alleging a violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty, but does not itself convert every alleged violation into a domestic damages action or waive sovereign immunity. The international norm, its actor coverage, the domestic action, personal responsibility, causation and authority for relief must each be established under the governing law; the existing specifically adopted actions retain their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, applies these common distinctions and preserves the existing holdings without making their original recognition rationales unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira v. Negewo separates §1350's forum from the received domestic action for personal official torture. Kadic v. Karadzic and Doe v. Karadzic apply the same separation to defined genocide and core war crimes. The jurisdictional character of the statute cannot silently erase those holdings. Conversely, their recognition of particular actions does not establish liability for a different asserted norm or supply the United States' consent to suit.

The official-torture action reaches deliberate participation in or personal supervision of severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation. The existing genocide action runs against an individual for personally perpetrating, planning, or directing genocide, whether or not that individual acted under state authority. The plaintiff must establish intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group as such, through killing members, causing serious bodily or mental harm, deliberately imposing conditions calculated to bring about the group's physical destruction in whole or in part, imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group, or forcibly transferring the group's children to another group, together with the defendant's qualifying responsibility, causation, and a lawful basis for relief. The core-war-crime action reaches personally perpetrated, planned or directed deliberate killing, torture and grave sexual violence, with the relevant armed conflict, protected victim, prohibited act and connection to the conflict. Each retains its responsibility, causation and remedy requirements; official position alone is insufficient.

Those defined actions furnish the existing law against which this asserted addition must be examined. United States v. Alvarez-Machain sustained the treaty-based objection to criminal trial. Its defensive treaty holding barred that prosecution but supplied no civil action, and it does not resolve the independent predicates now presented.

##### II. The asserted brief-detention norm does not support this additional action

**Controlling proposition:** Recognition of an additional received action requires a specifically defined, widely accepted and obligatory international prohibition whose content and actor coverage support judicial application, together with a sound domestic remedial basis. The asserted rule that any detention unauthorized by domestic law is an internationally actionable tort does not establish those predicates for the detention of less than a day in Mexico followed by transfer to United States custody.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins supply the controlling ground for rejecting this new action.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira and Kadic/Doe recognize determinate personal torts through a confined domestic reception of international law. They support a disciplined inquiry into an additional action, rather than either automatic incorporation of every international objection or categorical withdrawal of that received route. Their existing conduct, actor and remedial requirements explain why the source objection in the separate opinion does not justify closing the inquiry altogether.

Alvarez relies on article 9 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, article 9 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, other international statements and the absence of an express minimum detention period. Those materials do not establish the particular customary rule and domestic enforcement predicate his broad theory requires. The Declaration does not itself impose binding treaty obligations. The Covenant's non-self-executing status prevents it from itself supplying this damages action, although a treaty with that status may still inform evidence of customary law. General language against arbitrary detention is not enough to make every violation of local arrest law an internationally actionable tort.

The Restatement's treatment of prolonged detention as state policy illustrates the specificity missing from this claim; it does not create a duration safe harbor. The defect concerns the proposed norm and its domestic enforcement basis. The existing official-torture, genocide and core-war-crime actions remain available on their established terms. This award rests on the asserted brief arbitrary-detention action and must be set aside.

##### III. Domestic planning does not relocate the same foreign-detention claim

**Controlling proposition:** A claim seeking compensation for the detention injury occurring in Mexico arises in a foreign country within 28 U.S.C. §2680(k), even when domestic planning or direction is alleged as a cause of that same detention. The claimant must establish a waived claim on its actual injury and statutory predicates; a domestic causal link alone does not avoid the exception, and §2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso does not displace it. A genuinely independent domestic injury falls outside this holding.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion is joined by all nine Justices at this bounded foreign-detention scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The asserted false detention took place and endured in Mexico; its liability-creating detention and injury were there. Planning in the United States may have causal importance, but it does not change the claim for which Alvarez seeks compensation. If an allegation of domestic direction alone removed that claim from the exception, nearly every overseas operation could be recast through a headquarters instruction and §2680(k) would lose its enacted function.

Smith v. United States enforced the foreign-country exception together with the FTCA's liability and place-of-law provisions for acts wholly in Antarctica. The present decision applies that statutory structure to the additional domestic-planning argument; Smith did not itself decide this mixed-location question. Richards v. United States identifies the governing law under §1346(b), but that choice-of-law inquiry operates within the waiver and its exceptions. Even a domestic causal wrong subject to domestic law does not alone establish consent for the same excluded Mexican detention.

Section 2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso removes specified intentional torts from that subsection's exclusion when its own conditions are satisfied. It does not repeal the separate foreign-country exception. The asserted detention claim therefore must be dismissed. The Court resolves a claim whose detention and injury occurred together abroad; a separately supported domestic injury requires its own waiver and exception analysis.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Abebe-Jira v. Negewo is applied and preserved: its jurisdiction/action distinction, confined official-torture action and distinct remedy rules remain effective.
- Kadic v. Karadzic and Doe v. Karadzic are applied and preserved at their defined genocide, core-war-crime, actor, responsibility and remedy predicates; they do not make every international norm privately enforceable.
- United States v. Alvarez-Machain is distinguished as a defensive treaty decision that barred a prosecution and required return. It created no general private damages action under treaties and supplies no civil remedy here.
- Smith v. United States is extended from its wholly Antarctic acts to the domestic planning of the same Mexican detention; its territorial and statutory-waiver analysis remains confined to its actual work.
- Richards v. United States is applied within the FTCA's consent structure. Selection of domestic law does not remove a claim from an independently applicable exception.

#### Law After Decision

The specifically adopted international-tort actions remain enforceable on their existing terms. A proposed additional action must satisfy the norm, actor and domestic remedial requirements stated here; §1350 jurisdiction alone is insufficient. The broad brief-detention theory presented fails that inquiry. Under the FTCA, domestic planning or direction of the same foreign detention does not avoid §2680(k), which operates independently of §2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, joins Parts I and III and concurs in the ATS reversal on an additional-action source ground. They distinguish an international prohibition, federal jurisdiction and domestic authority to impose damages. Section 1350 supplies a forum, while the offered Declaration and Covenant do not themselves enact this private action. The received common-law setting is the strongest argument for the Court's method, but in their view it does not supply sufficient congressional authorization for the additional arbitrary-detention liability.

Their earlier writings in Abebe-Jira and Kadic/Doe preserve that source objection. Suter requires an actual source of private liability; O'Melveny and Atherton reject federal rules derived merely from a general federal interest; Meyer keeps forum, consent and substantive liability separate. Central Bank's established securities action and Musick, Peeler's treatment of incidents of recognized liability do not independently authorize this different extension. They respect the already adopted official-torture, genocide and core-war-crime holdings as precedents at their exact scopes, but decline to extend them to the present detention action without a sufficient enacted source. That independently sufficient ground explains their ATS judgment agreement and their refusal to join Part II's recognition methodology.

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justice Breyer, joins the Court's opinion and explains the conduct-based reason for the bounded FTCA result. Section 2680(k) must be read with §1346(b)(1), which locates the governing law by the relevant act or omission. For this claim, Mexico was the place of the last significant act necessary to create the alleged tort: the seizure and detention took place there, and the detention injury accrued there. Domestic planning was causally relevant but did not by itself complete the false-detention claim or convert its Mexican core into a domestic injury.

Richards supplies the place-of-conduct inquiry within the FTCA's statutory structure. Smith enforced the exception for acts wholly in Antarctica; its actual scope does not require an injury-only test for every combination of domestic and foreign conduct. These Justices therefore support the Court's common disposition of the same Mexican detention notwithstanding headquarters planning, while retaining a construction attentive to liability-creating conduct. Their direct join in Part III does not adopt a categorical rule that any injury abroad is conclusive regardless of where the tortious conduct occurred.

Justice Breyer joins the Court's opinion and adds a comity concern. International acceptance of a substantive prohibition does not necessarily establish agreement that the courts of every nation may enforce it against foreign persons for foreign conduct. Territorial or nationality connections ordinarily reduce that concern. For certain grave international crimes, acceptance of universal criminal jurisdiction supports related civil recovery because many legal systems combine criminal and civil adjudication; torture, genocide and core war crimes fit that established inquiry. He identifies no comparable procedural consensus for this brief arbitrary arrest. This further reason supports rejection here while preserving the existing actions and his join in Part II; it supplies no general controlling universal-jurisdiction or exhaustion rule.

Negewo, January 14, 1997; Kadic v. Karadzic and Doe v. Karadzic, April 21, 1997; Smith v. United States, 507 U.S. 197; Richards v. United States, 369 U.S. 1; United States v. Alvarez-Machain, 504 U.S. 655.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower court must vacate the ATS award on the rejected theory and restore dismissal of the covered FTCA claim. Any genuinely live independent theory proceeds only upon satisfaction of its own preservation, action and immunity requirements.

### Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran, No. 03-1147

Merits decision, June 30, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided June 30, 2004, on review of the D.C. Circuit's affirmance of dismissal, 333 F.3d 228. Former hostages and their spouses and children seek recovery against Iran and its Ministry of Foreign Affairs for the November 1979 seizure and 444-day detention. The January 19, 1981 Algiers Accords secured release and included the United States' undertaking to bar the covered claims. An August 2001 default addressed liability, while damages remained unresolved. The United States intervened in October 2001 to protect its agreement-related interest. Congress then amended the sovereign-immunity exception for this litigation and corrected its case designation. The question is whether those enactments also displaced the Accords' independent bar and required preservation of the default, including the necessary application of independent-adjudication principles to this pending case.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The dismissal and judicial vacatur of the liability default remain effective. The plaintiffs' request to restore this action for a damages determination is denied because the covered claims remain barred by the Accords.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The immunity amendments leave the independent claim bar in place

**Controlling proposition:** A statute removing a specified sovereign-immunity obstacle extinguishes an independent bar to the plaintiff's claim only if its enacted text or necessary legal effect accomplishes that further displacement; legislative explanations cannot supply an omitted operative rule or direct entry of judgment. Section 626(c) changes the state-sponsor-designation condition for this identified litigation and §208 corrects its designation, but neither displaces the Accords' covered-claim prohibition; the claims against Iran and its Ministry therefore remain barred.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion directly controls this holding and the following two holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The amendment does real legal work. Section 626(c) of Public Law 107-77 inserts this designated litigation into former §1605(a)(7)(A), removing the state-sponsor-designation obstacle. Section 208 of Public Law 107-117 corrects the judge's initials in that case designation. Neither enactment supplies a rule inconsistent with the Accords' separate prohibition of these hostage-related claims.

The plaintiffs emphasize that Congress knew of the Government's objection, deliberately identified this suit and expressed a desire to preserve their judgment. That context supports taking the enactments seriously; it cannot change their subject from immunity to every obstacle to recovery. Removal of one legal barrier is meaningful even when another remains decisive. An explanatory statement favoring recovery does not itself enact abrogation, liability or a judgment.

Trans World Airlines v. Franklin Mint, Cook v. United States and Weinberger v. Rossi require a genuine legislative basis for displacing international commitments. No talismanic phrase is necessary: courts examine the operative text and its necessary legal effect. Congress may change domestic law concerning an executive agreement within its constitutional authority. These enactments simply do not accomplish the additional displacement asserted here. Dames & Moore v. Regan sustains the relevant claims-settlement framework within its authorization and historical-practice setting, rather than establishing an executive power to disregard later legislation.

The Ministry is Iran itself for these covered claims, so naming it does not avoid the same bar. That independent ground completely resolves the presented statutory-abrogation question. The broader Flatow cause-of-action objection is unnecessary because resolving it could not overcome the surviving bar.

##### II. Intervention, default and payment each retain their independent conditions

**Controlling proposition:** The United States may protect its own concrete agreement-related interest through authorized intervention and judicial reconsideration; the foreign state's nonappearance neither forfeits that interest nor establishes entitlement to judgment. Under §1608(e), the claimant must establish the claim or right to relief by evidence satisfactory to the court, and a payment provision conditioned on a qualifying judgment supplies payment authority only after a legally supportable judgment exists.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government undertook its own obligation to bar these claims. Its intervention after learning of the conflict protects that obligation, and Iran's nonappearance does not adequately protect the Government's interest. On this record, its timely intervention and request for judicial reconsideration were proper. The courts independently considered the legal bar under the procedural rules, including the Rule 60(b) motion as presented.

Argentine Republic v. Amerada Hess and Republic of Austria v. Altmann distinguish the FSIA's adjudicative-immunity framework from substantive entitlement. The claimant must establish the invoked exception and the separate right to relief. Section 1608(e) preserves that latter inquiry even upon default. The Accords defeat the covered claim despite the amended immunity condition. Public Law 106-386's judgment-payment mechanism presupposes the qualifying judgment and does not create the missing entitlement to obtain one.

##### III. The amendments change law for a pending case and leave adjudication to the courts

**Controlling proposition:** Congress may enact a new legal rule governing a pending controversy, including an identifiable controversy, subject to its lawful temporal reach and constitutional limits, while courts independently apply the rule to facts and claims. The present amendments fall on that pending-case side of Plaut's distinction: the unresolved-damages liability default did not finally dispose of the action, and the enacted immunity change prescribes neither findings nor liability. Unenacted explanations cannot become binding adjudicative commands.

**Controlling explanation:** Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society permits genuine governing-law changes in pending litigation while preserving independent judicial application. United States v. Klein protects the adjudicative function from legislative prescription of findings or liability. The distinction concerns what a statute does, not merely whether its beneficiaries or case can be identified.

Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm supplies the completed-judgment rule: once a private judgment becomes final through the ordinary judicial process, Congress may not, solely by changing the previously applicable law, require its vacatur and reinstate the same litigation. Later independent adjudication of eligibility and merits cannot restore the conclusiveness of the displaced judgment. That rule preserves Congress's authority to change law while a case remains pending and preserves constitutionally permissible judicial relief, including valid Rule 60(b) grounds; Klein and McCullough v. Virginia continue to forbid dictated findings and predetermined liability.

Here damages remained unresolved when Public Laws 107-77 and 107-117 were enacted. The liability default therefore was not the completed private judgment Plaut protects. The provisions remove the specified immunity obstacle and correct the designation while leaving courts to apply the remaining law. Judicial reconsideration on the Government's motion is likewise distinct from a legislative command reopening a completed action.

Miller v. French and Plaut's Wheeling Bridge qualification distinguish new law governing future enforcement of continuing relief from displacement of completed private judgments; meaningful adjudication of the governing predicates remains necessary. Landgraf v. USI Film Products separately addresses temporal operation: express retrospectivity does not itself establish constitutional validity. Morgan Stanley's affirmance by an equally divided Court supplies no contrary rule.

The asserted broader command to preserve liability appears in explanations, not the enactments. Applying the actual amendments therefore preserves independent adjudication and leaves the Accords bar decisive.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Trans World Airlines v. Franklin Mint, Cook v. United States and Weinberger v. Rossi are applied to require an enacted basis for displacement of the international commitment; explanatory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Dames & Moore v. Regan is applied within its claims-settlement authorization and historical-practice scope. Congress's power to enact contrary domestic law remains distinct from what it enacted here.
- Argentine Republic v. Amerada Hess and Republic of Austria v. Altmann are applied to preserve the separation between present immunity, a substantive claim and independent defenses.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society is applied to a genuine law change in pending litigation with independent judicial application.
- Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm is applied at its pending-case boundary. Its completed-private-judgment rule, prospective-relief qualification and preservation of constitutionally permissible judicial procedures retain their existing force.
- United States v. Klein and McCullough v. Virginia supply the continuing protection against legislative findings or prescribed liability.
- Miller v. French and Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co. retain their distinct treatment of changed prospective law and meaningful judicial adjudication.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products is applied to distinguish enacted temporal effect from constitutional permission.
- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., affirmed by an equally divided Court, establishes no precedent.

#### Law After Decision

The designated immunity amendments retain their actual effect, but the Accords continue to bar these hostage-related claims against Iran and its Ministry. Courts must examine each independent jurisdictional and substantive predicate before treating a limited amendment as entitlement to judgment. A real law change governing a pending controversy receives independent judicial application; legislative explanations cannot supply an unpassed command preserving liability. Plaut's separate completed-judgment rule remains unchanged.
