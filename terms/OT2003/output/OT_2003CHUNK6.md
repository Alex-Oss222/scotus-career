### Dretke v. Haley, No. 02-1824

Merits decision, 2004-05-03

#### Chronology and Posture

Dretke v. Haley, No. 02-1824, 541 U.S. 386. Argued March 2, 2004; decided May 3, 2004. Certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, which affirmed a conditional habeas writ requiring resentencing without the habitual-offender enhancement. The question is whether factual innocence of eligibility for a noncapital recidivist enhancement permits review of a defaulted constitutional challenge and what independent showing is required for relief. Haley received sixteen and one-half years for theft; the admitted prior-offense chronology fails the enhancement’s indispensable sequence, and the unenhanced offense carried a two-year maximum. His separately pleaded assistance claim was rejected on its merits in state court but was not adjudicated by the federal district court.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing or withholding assent | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Availability and satisfaction of the confined recidivist-eligibility gateway | Sustained, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Breyer would leave the question undecided | The gateway ruling governs constitutional review on remand. |
| Existing conditional sentence writ | Vacated and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg would affirm the writ and remand for implementation | Prompt adjudication of the preserved constitutional grounds and each claim’s independent requirements. |

The theft conviction remains operative. The federal court must promptly adjudicate the preserved constitutional grounds under the controlling gateway ruling and the independent requirements applicable to each claim.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court in Parts I and II, with different joins as set out below. O’CONNOR, J., and STEVENS, J., filed opinions concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Five-Justice controlling gateway ruling | Recidivist-eligibility gateway and Haley's satisfaction of it |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Stone-Zsela | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Five-Justice controlling remand ground and remedy | Independent constitutional adjudication and remand |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | O'Connor | Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Joins Part II and its vacatur; withholds Part I | Would defer the new gateway question and adjudicate assistance first |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Joins Part I; would affirm the conditional sentence writ | Thompson's no-evidence protection independently requires sentence relief |

The five direct joins in each part establish its stated rule. The distinct assistance-first preference in the separate opinion supplies no broader sequencing rule.

#### Holdings

##### Factual ineligibility for a statutory recidivist enhancement opens a confined gateway

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner who proves by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable factfinder could find the statutory facts authorizing a noncapital recidivist enhancement may obtain review of a procedurally defaulted federal constitutional challenge to that enhancement. The prisoner bears this demanding gateway burden; disagreement with discretionary sentencing is insufficient, and opening review preserves the separate requirements of a constitutional violation and applicable habeas restrictions.

**Authority:** Part I of the Chief Justice’s opinion controls the rule and its application to Haley through the five direct joins identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Sawyer v. Whitley separates facts making a defendant eligible for punishment from considerations governing discretionary selection within an authorized range. That distinction provides a workable boundary here. Conclusive factual ineligibility for an indispensable habitual-offender condition makes the additional years of imprisonment unauthorized in a way that a dispute over mitigation or sentencing discretion does not. The State’s capital-only objection identifies Sawyer’s original setting but does not explain why the same tightly confined miscarriage safeguard should stop at that line. Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing burden answers the concern that every sentencing complaint might evade default.

Texas acknowledges that Haley’s first prior conviction became final three days after he committed the second prior offense. The statute required the reverse sequence. This undisputed chronology meets the gateway burden. Schlup v. Delo supplies a distinct offense-innocence inquiry; its different probability standard is not imported here. The gateway concerns access to constitutional adjudication, rather than a freestanding right to a writ for violation of state sentencing law. The existence of a separately pleaded assistance claim does not justify withholding this confined gateway ruling.

##### The conditional writ requires independent constitutional adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Passage through the gateway does not establish the independent constitutional entitlement necessary to sustain a sentence writ. The existing conditional writ is vacated, and the case returns for prompt adjudication of the independently pleaded assistance claim and any properly reached constitutional sufficiency challenge, with each claim’s applicable collateral requirements preserved; once a properly adjudicated route supplies the complete sentence remedy, the court need not decide another route merely because it was pleaded.

**Authority:** Part II of the Chief Justice’s opinion controls the independent-constitutional-adjudication ground and remand through its five direct joins. The controlling gateway ruling in Part I governs the returned case. The Thompson no-evidence application remains unresolved for lack of a five-Justice merits coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** An innocence gateway excuses default; a writ requires an independent constitutional violation and satisfaction of the habeas restrictions actually applicable to the particular claim. The admitted chronology establishes the gateway, but the constitutional classification and application require their own supported adjudication. The Fifth Circuit treated the sufficiency claim as cognizable under Jackson; the Court returns the constitutional grounds for adjudication under their distinct requirements rather than treating the eligibility finding as sufficient to sustain the writ. The assistance claim received a state merits adjudication and therefore requires the appropriate section 2254(d) inquiry and an actual Strickland performance-and-prejudice determination. The sufficiency claim received a procedural rejection; a merits adjudication of the assistance claim does not by itself trigger section 2254(d) for that separate claim. Gatewood’s current recidivist protection retains its direct-review transition and independent collateral conditions. The remand must apply the constitutional law and temporal conditions governing the actual claim rather than move a later holding backward to an earlier state adjudication. A complete sentence remedy from one properly adjudicated route can make another route unnecessary. This case-specific mandate imposes no general priority between assistance and sufficiency claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Sawyer v. Whitley:** Extended to factual eligibility for statutory noncapital recidivist punishment; its clear-and-convincing burden and distinction from discretionary selection remain.
- **Schlup v. Delo:** Its offense-innocence gateway retains its different burden and function.
- **Edwards v. Carpenter:** Applied to preserve the assistance claim’s independent conditions when offered as cause.
- **Williams v. Taylor and Strickland v. Washington:** Retain their distinct statutory-review and constitutional performance/prejudice inquiries.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Retains its statutory aggravated-reentry-element holding, distinct from a universal constitutional recidivism rule.
- **United States v. Gatewood:** Its constitutional protection for necessary recidivist facts remains effective with its direct-review transition and independent collateral limits.
- **Monge v. California:** Its bounded double-jeopardy rule remains distinct from the present gateway and constitutional-merits questions.
- **Thompson v. Louisville and Jackson v. Virginia:** Their distinct no-evidence and sufficiency protections remain governing authority for the preserved constitutional adjudication.

#### Law After Decision

Procedural default may now be excused by clear-and-convincing factual ineligibility for a statutory noncapital recidivist enhancement. That rule governs Haley’s constitutional review and comparable eligibility claims, while ordinary discretionary sentencing objections remain outside it. Constitutional entitlement, applicable collateral restrictions and an operative custody remedy require their own adjudication. The Thompson application to this enhancement remains without a majority resolution; the case returns under the Court’s gateway ruling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices join Part II’s independent-constitutional-adjudication ground and vacatur. The separately pleaded assistance claim offers an ordinary constitutional route to the complete requested sentence remedy. They would require prompt adjudication of that claim under its actual section 2254(d) conditions and Strickland before addressing the extension of the innocence gateway and Thompson’s application to this noncapital enhancement. Edwards preserves assistance’s separate conditions; the permissible sequencing recognized in Lambrix and Trest permits that case-specific course. They acknowledge the force of the admitted chronology and the earlier no-evidence argument. Their position preserves those routes if assistance does not resolve the request and makes no categorical rule against a noncapital gateway. They would leave the gateway question open now and therefore do not join Part I.

**Stevens, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices join the confined gateway and conclude that Haley also establishes an independent constitutional violation. Thompson v. Louisville protects against punishment with no evidentiary foundation; Jackson v. Virginia distinguishes that earlier anti-arbitrariness protection from its broader reasonable-doubt sufficiency inquiry. The admitted chronology affirmatively disproves the indispensable status authorizing additional custody. Applying the earlier no-evidence rule to that predicate, in their view, requires neither a general federal remedy for state sentencing error nor retrospective application of Gatewood’s jury-and-proof rule. Because that independent ground already establishes sentence relief, another open-ended merits remand needlessly postpones the remedy. They would affirm the conditional sentence writ and return solely for a concrete lawful-sentence or custody-termination order, with present custody and service actually determined.

#### Source Notes

Dretke v. Haley, No. 02-1824; Texas Penal Code habitual-offender sequencing provision; 28 U.S.C. §§2243 and 2254; Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333; Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298; Edwards v. Carpenter, 529 U.S. 446; Thompson v. Louisville, 362 U.S. 199; Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307; Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668. The present custody and replacement-judgment facts require determination in implementing the mandate.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

On mandate, the district court must obtain the current custody and sentencing information and adjudicate the preserved assistance and properly reached sufficiency grounds on a priority schedule. The assistance inquiry must use the actual state merits date for section 2254(d), then determine performance and prejudice under Strickland. The separate sufficiency claim receives constitutional review with its default excused and every independently applicable collateral condition observed. One properly adjudicated route supplying the complete sentence remedy can make further adjudication of another route unnecessary. If a constitutional defect establishes entitlement, the court must condition continued custody on lawful resentencing within a short concrete period it sets, credit time served, identify any lawful judgment supporting custody, and specify release from unsupported custody upon noncompliance. The existing sentence-only writ is vacated pending that adjudication.

**End of entry: Dretke v. Haley, Merits decision, 2004-05-03.**

### Johnson v. California, No. 03-6539

Jurisdictional dismissal, 2004-05-03

#### Chronology and Posture

Johnson v. California, No. 03-6539, 541 U.S. 428. Argued March 30, 2004; decided May 3, 2004. Review of the California Supreme Court’s reversal of the Court of Appeal’s Wheeler/Batson ruling and remand. Independent evidentiary and prosecutorial-misconduct claims remain unresolved. The submitted threshold question is whether that state judgment is final under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a); the underlying merits question concerns the first-stage showing of discriminatory peremptory challenges.

#### Judgment

**Dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The existing state remand remains operative; the dismissal supplies no federal merits mandate.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine participating Justices.

#### Holdings

##### Substantive state proceedings must finish before this review

**Holding and operative rule:** A state judgment rejecting one federal ground for reversal is not final under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) when independent substantive appellate claims remain undecided and effective federal review remains available after an adverse final disposition, unless the party invoking jurisdiction establishes a recognized Cox exception. Johnson has established neither finality nor an exception, so this proceeding must be dismissed for want of jurisdiction.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion controls the rule, application and dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida v. Thomas applies statutory finality to an unresolved substantive remand and preserves Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn’s four practical-finality routes. None supplies jurisdiction here. First, the remaining evidentiary and prosecutorial-misconduct claims require actual decision, so the proceedings are not practically complete. Second, reversal on an independent ground may eliminate the federal jury-selection dispute, so that issue need not survive every outcome. Third, a final adverse state judgment permits effective review of the rejected federal claim; this is a practical means of correcting the asserted wrong, rather than a merely theoretical future appeal. Fourth, reversal of the jury-selection ruling would afford conviction relief and permit appropriate further criminal proceedings, rather than protect against the litigation itself, and the remaining appeal threatens no distinct destruction of federal policy beyond ordinary delay in deciding a constitutional claim.

Jefferson v. City of Tarrant distinguishes settlement of an issue from a final state judgment. Pennsylvania v. Ritchie requires practical attention to whether later review can protect the right; the independent reversal grounds and effective later review satisfy that concern here. The importance of nondiscriminatory jury selection does not itself create an exception for every rejected Batson claim. Party agreement cannot supply statutory jurisdiction. Because finality is absent, the Court cannot adjudicate the challenged first-stage Batson standard.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Florida v. Thomas:** Applied to the substantive remand and effective later-review posture.
- **Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn:** Its four practical-finality categories are preserved and each is tested against the actual remaining proceedings.
- **Jefferson v. City of Tarrant:** Applied to distinguish a finally settled federal issue from a final state judgment.
- **Pennsylvania v. Ritchie:** Its practical qualification remains; the later review here can effectively vindicate the asserted jury-selection right.
- **Batson v. Kentucky:** Its merits remain outside the Court’s jurisdiction in this proceeding.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1257 requires completion of substantive state proceedings in this posture. The established Cox exceptions remain available when their predicates are shown. This dismissal leaves existing Batson law in place and gives the state ruling no additional federal endorsement.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §1257(a); Florida v. Thomas, 532 U.S. 774; Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469; Jefferson v. City of Tarrant, 522 U.S. 75; Pennsylvania v. Ritchie, 480 U.S. 39; Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79. The continuing appeal includes substantive evidentiary and prosecutorial-misconduct issues.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The California Court of Appeal must complete the existing remand and adjudicate the independent grounds for reversal under state procedure. Johnson may seek review following an adverse final state judgment through the applicable filing process, subject to the jurisdictional and timing requirements then governing.

**End of entry: Johnson v. California, Jurisdictional dismissal, 2004-05-03.**

### Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co., No. 02-1205

Merits decision, 2004-05-03

#### Chronology and Posture

Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co., No. 02-1205, 541 U.S. 369. Argued February 24, 2004; decided May 3, 2004. Certiorari to the Seventh Circuit. Former employees assert section 1981 hostile-environment, termination and transfer claims made possible by the Civil Rights Act of 1991. The question is whether section 1658’s four-year limitation covers those claims although section 1981 itself predates December 1, 1990.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Claims wrongly excluded under the shorter borrowed period return for application of the federal period and ordinary claim adjudication.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O’CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### The four-year period follows the enactment that made the claim possible

**Holding and operative rule:** Except where law otherwise supplies a limitation, 28 U.S.C. §1658(a) requires a claim made possible by an Act of Congress enacted after December 1, 1990 to be commenced within four years after accrual, whether the Act creates a new statute or amends an existing one. The party invoking that period must identify the new substantive basis for the claim against the defendant; an amendment immaterial to an already available claim does not change that claim’s limitation.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls the rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** An amendment is an Act of Congress. Restricting section 1658 to newly numbered statutory provisions would make limitations turn on codification rather than on the legal liability Congress added. Rivers v. Roadway Express identifies section 101 of the 1991 Act as a substantive expansion of section 1981 into contractual performance, modification, termination and enjoyment of the relationship’s benefits. Patterson v. McLean Credit Union supplies the construction of the earlier text against which that expansion is identified. The employees’ hostile-environment, termination and transfer claims fall within section 1658 insofar as the amendment supplied their actionable basis.

The employer’s continuity objection has force for claims already available under the older law, but it does not exclude claims the later Act newly authorized. The dependency inquiry preserves that distinction: the same amended statute may support both old claims governed by their otherwise applicable limitations and newly enabled claims governed by section 1658. Goodman v. Lukens Steel continues to supply borrowing where no applicable federal period displaces it. The date of the complaint or citation of amended text alone cannot establish the new period. The Seventh Circuit’s categorical reliance on section 1981’s original enactment therefore requires reversal, with claim-specific timeliness and the surviving claims resolved below.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Applied to identify the new substantive contractual coverage supplied by the 1991 Act; its prospective temporal operation remains intact.
- **Patterson v. McLean Credit Union:** Retained as the construction of the earlier statutory coverage against which amendment-dependent claims are identified.
- **Goodman v. Lukens Steel Co.:** Its state-borrowing rule remains for claims without a displaced limitation category.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Its provision-specific temporal inquiry remains distinct from this construction of the enacted section 1658 date criterion.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1658 follows the post-1990 enactment that supplies a new claim, including an amendment to an older statute. A mixed complaint can contain claims with different applicable periods. The employees’ amendment-dependent claims receive the four-year period; identifying accrual and resolving any actually disputed tolling, timeliness and discrimination issues are ordinary application of that rule.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §1658(a); 42 U.S.C. §1981(b); Civil Rights Act of 1991 §101, effective November 21, 1991; Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298; Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244; Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164; Goodman v. Lukens Steel Co., 482 U.S. 656.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must identify the claims whose substantive basis depends on the 1991 expansion, restore those improperly barred under the shorter borrowed period, apply four years from accrual and adjudicate the surviving claims through ordinary procedure. Any properly certified class receives the same claim-specific analysis.

**End of entry: Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co., Merits decision, 2004-05-03.**

### Middleton v. McNeil, No. 03-1028

Merits decision, 2004-05-03

#### Chronology and Posture

Middleton v. McNeil, No. 03-1028, 541 U.S. 433. Decided May 3, 2004. Review of the Ninth Circuit’s reversal of the district court’s denial of habeas relief on McNeil’s second-degree murder conviction. The question is whether the state court unreasonably applied clearly established law when it assessed a mistaken phrase in the imperfect-self-defense instruction in the context of the complete charge.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Denial of habeas relief is reinstated on this instructional ground, and the conviction remains operative against that challenge.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine participating Justices.

#### Holdings

##### The complete charge permits the state court’s contextual conclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** On this state-adjudicated instructional claim, section 2254(d)(1) permits relief only if the state court’s rejection was contrary to, or objectively unreasonably applied, this Court’s clearly established holdings at the state merits adjudication. Assessed in the complete charge and trial context, the mistaken reasonable-person qualifier does not make the state court’s rejection objectively unreasonable; this instructional ground therefore cannot support a writ.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion controls the complete rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** California imperfect self-defense reduces murder to voluntary manslaughter when an actual fear of imminent peril is unreasonable. The charge repeatedly stated that distinction, placed the contrary proof burden on the prosecution beyond a reasonable doubt, and expressly said that the defense applied even when a reasonable person seeing and knowing the same facts would not share the belief. One definition of imminent peril then mistakenly added a reasonable-person qualifier. Cupp v. Naughten requires reading that phrase in its setting; Boyde v. California and Estelle v. McGuire require examination of the reasonable likelihood of an impermissible overall application.

McNeil argues that the faulty definition qualified every reference to imminent peril and thus withdrew the defense. The express reasonable-person contrast elsewhere in the judicial charge gives the state court a reasonable basis to reject that reading. The prosecutor’s correct, defendant-favorable explanation reinforces the contextual assessment; it does not give counsel authority to replace an unambiguously contrary judicial command. The decision rests on the content and relation of the instructions, rather than merely counting correct statements.

Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade distinguish ordinary disagreement from objective statutory unreasonableness. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record. Under those standards, the state assessment falls within the permitted range. Trial courts remain obliged to give accurate instructions, and correct surrounding language is not an automatic cure for a contrary operative burden.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cupp v. Naughten:** Applied to examine the challenged instruction in its full setting.
- **Boyde v. California and Estelle v. McGuire:** Applied for the reasonable-likelihood inquiry into an asserted constitutional misapplication of the charge.
- **Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade:** Applied to keep constitutional entitlement, objective statutory review and the date of clearly established substantive law distinct.
- **Woodford v. Visciotti:** Applied to require examination of the complete supported state reasoning and record, rather than isolated words or a correct formula alone.

#### Law After Decision

The existing whole-charge and objective habeas standards govern. The repeated actual-but-unreasonable distinction and express reasonable-person contrast sustain the state court’s application on this record. Other charges require their own contextual assessment under the same rule; this holding creates no automatic cure from repetition or counsel’s argument.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Cupp v. Naughten, 414 U.S. 141; Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370; Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62; Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362; Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19; Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63. The relevant charge repeatedly permitted an actual but unreasonable belief and expressly contrasted that belief with what a reasonable person would believe.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for implementation of denial on the reviewed instructional claim. The lower federal court must reject this ground under section 2254(d)(1) rather than order a new trial on its preferred reading of the isolated phrase.

**End of entry: Middleton v. McNeil, Merits decision, 2004-05-03.**

### Scarborough v. Principi, No. 02-1657

Merits decision, 2004-05-03

#### Chronology and Posture

Scarborough v. Principi, No. 02-1657, 541 U.S. 401. Argued February 23, 2004; decided May 3, 2004. Certiorari to the Federal Circuit, which affirmed the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims’ dismissal of an Equal Access to Justice Act fee application. The question is whether a timely application may be amended after thirty days to add the omitted lack-of-substantial-justification allegation. The operative timely application included prevailing status, financial eligibility, amount, hours and rates; counsel promptly supplied the sole missing allegation after the Secretary objected.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The corrected allegation relates back and the fee request returns for consideration on its merits.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O’CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

#### Holdings

##### A required allegation may complete this timely fee application

**Holding and operative rule:** An otherwise qualifying, timely application under 28 U.S.C. §2412(d)(1)(B) may be amended after the thirty-day filing period to supply the required allegation that the Government’s position lacked substantial justification, with the amendment relating back to the identified fee request. Demonstrated prejudice from the amendment defeats relation back; none was asserted here, and the corrected allegation must be accepted for ordinary fee adjudication.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court controls the rule, application and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Scarborough timely identified the prevailing applicant, financial eligibility, requested amount and itemized attorney work. His prompt amendment supplied the sole omitted allegation without adding operative facts or a new demand. The fee request is ancillary to an action already within the court’s jurisdiction; completing it does not enlarge the waiver’s substantive monetary coverage. The allegation remains mandatory and is actually supplied. The Government retains its burden to establish substantial justification.

Becker v. Montgomery distinguishes correction of a timely identified filing from commencement of a late proceeding. Edelman v. Lynchburg College likewise permits required completion to relate back to timely initiation under its statute. Those authorities inform this independent construction of the fee procedure; relation back is not confined to Civil Rule 15 pleadings. The Secretary’s argument that every required item must arrive within thirty days does not establish an absolute statutory prohibition on completion of this same timely claim. Nor does counsel’s participation distinguish Becker’s correction principle, which applies to represented litigants too.

The Court can assume that the allegation shares the filing deadline and still allow relation back; it need not decide the distinct deadline reach of the subsection’s second sentence. The separate question of curing a missing first-sentence showing lies outside the presented omission. Permission to complete the application leaves substantive coverage, eligibility, reasonable amount and the statutory defenses for adjudication.

##### The fee court must decide entitlement under the separate statutory conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Except as otherwise specifically provided by statute, section 2412(d)(1)(A) directs an award of reasonable fees and expenses to an eligible prevailing party other than the United States in a civil action other than tort, including judicial review of agency action, brought by or against the United States in a court having jurisdiction, unless the Government establishes substantial justification or special circumstances make an award unjust. Acceptance of Scarborough’s corrected allegation requires adjudication of those conditions and reasonable amount; it does not itself establish an award.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion controls this remand consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2412(d)(1)(A)’s fee command and its two distinct exceptions remain operative after the pleading defect is cured. Substantial justification concerns whether the Government’s position was justified to a degree that could satisfy a reasonable person, as Pierce v. Underwood explains. The separate special-circumstances exception concerns whether an award would be unjust. Neither is the prejudice-to-amendment limit governing relation back. The veterans court must hear the Government’s merits defense and decide any properly presented eligibility, amount or other statutory issue in a reasoned fee decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Becker v. Montgomery:** Applied by analogy to completion of a timely identified filing; its express signature-cure rule is not itself an EAJA authorization.
- **Edelman v. Lynchburg College:** Applied by analogy to the distinct requirements of timely initiation and mandatory later completion; the present fee rule rests on its own statutory construction.
- **Kontrick v. Ryan:** Preserved as a jurisdiction/processing distinction. The Secretary timely raised this objection, so its forfeiture holding does not decide the case.
- **Ardestani v. INS:** Its actual administrative-fee coverage limit remains; a procedural cure within this covered judicial action does not expand substantive authorization.
- **Pierce v. Underwood:** Retains the merits standard for the Government’s substantial-justification defense.

#### Law After Decision

An otherwise qualifying timely EAJA application can receive this omitted allegation by relation back, subject to demonstrated amendment prejudice. The allegation remains obligatory, and the Government’s fee defenses remain available. The treatment of a missing first-sentence showing remains outside this holding. The resulting fee proceeding applies the statute’s award command, substantial-justification defense and distinct special-circumstances exception.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** The application-content command and thirty-day requirement should be read together to require the allegation within the statutory period when the Government timely objects. The omission may add no factual surprise, but lack of prejudice cannot itself supply cure authority. Becker depended on an express rule allowing a missing signature to be corrected, and Edelman involved an authorized verification regulation; neither supplies a free-standing power to complete this EAJA application. Kontrick’s distinction between jurisdiction and mandatory procedure leaves an invoked mandatory condition enforceable. The dissent would affirm dismissal on that statutory ground without requiring a jurisdictional label.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §2412(d)(1)(A)–(B); Becker v. Montgomery, 532 U.S. 757; Edelman v. Lynchburg College, 535 U.S. 106; Kontrick v. Ryan, January 14, 2004; Ardestani v. INS, 502 U.S. 129; Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552. The Secretary asserted no prejudice from relation back; that litigation position does not establish absence of prejudice for other amendments.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

It must enter a reasoned fee decision under section 2412(d).

### Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States

Merits decision, 2004-05-10

#### Chronology and Posture

Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States, on review of 337 F.3d 1373 (Federal Circuit, August 8, 2003). Decided May 10, 2004. The question is whether section 1677e(b) requires intentional concealment, willfulness or bad faith before Commerce may use adverse inferences for failure to respond to the best of an exporter’s ability. The Federal Circuit restored Commerce’s partial adverse-facts determination and separately addressed starting-price currency methodology and a record-exclusion regulation.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed on the adverse-inference component, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Commerce’s supported partial adverse-facts determination is sustained. The Federal Circuit’s separate starting-price disposition and mootness treatment of the regulation challenge remain operative outside this review.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O’CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### Best ability measures available capability and effort, without an intent prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the governing version of 19 U.S.C. §1677e(b), Commerce may draw an adverse inference in selecting otherwise available facts when substantial evidence shows that a respondent failed to use the effort within its actual ability to comply with an information request. Intentional concealment, willfulness or bad faith is not an additional required element; Commerce must identify supported capability and inadequate effort, because a deficient response alone does not establish that separate predicate.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls this rule, its statutory qualifications and the application below.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted phrase measures performance against ability. It requires the maximum effort the party can make under its actual capabilities, including reasonable inquiry into available records, without demanding perfection or information genuinely unavailable despite adequate cooperation. Requiring intent would substitute good faith for the separate ability-and-effort condition Congress enacted. Nippon’s concern that an honest error should not produce automatic punishment is answered by that distinct evidentiary predicate and the mandatory information safeguards.

Commerce twice requested theoretical-to-actual-weight conversion information under the December 21, 1998 and January 25, 1999 deadlines. Nippon first called conversion unnecessary and then said the weights were unavailable. Its later explanation admitted that the answering staff had not sought factory records. Commerce found those records routinely maintained, readily available and not burdensome to produce; the later production supported that stated finding. The conclusion therefore rests on available capability and omitted inquiry, rather than lateness or eventual production alone. The accelerated investigation and separate production and sales databases do not compel rejection of that supported finding.

The Court independently construes the statutory words and reviews the agency’s actual determination on the whole record under section 1516a(b)(1)(B)(i). Allentown Mack requires testing evidence under the actual announced standard; Boston & Maine forbids substituting court-invented agency reasons. Those requirements are met by Commerce’s own explanation here.

##### The distinct information safeguards remain conditions of lawful use

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1677e(a) requires otherwise available facts when necessary information is absent or a party withholds requested information, misses the requested deadline or form, significantly impedes the proceeding, or supplies unverifiable information, subject to section 1677m(d); the deadline/form branch separately preserves section 1677m(c)(1) and (e). Section 1677e(b)’s supported noncooperation finding permits rather than compels an adverse inference, and section 1677e(c) requires practicable corroboration of secondary information from reasonably available independent sources.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts these qualifications as part of the operative rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The provisions impose distinct commands. Under section 1677m(c)(1), a party promptly explaining inability to comply in the requested form and manner, with suggested alternatives, triggers consideration of its ability; the agency may modify requirements as needed to avoid an unreasonable burden. Subsection (c)(2) requires attention to difficulties, particularly those of small companies, and practicable assistance. Under subsection (d), the agency must promptly identify a response’s deficiency and, as practicable within investigation limits, permit explanation or correction. It may disregard an unsatisfactory or late further response subject to subsection (e).

Subsection (e) requires consideration of necessary information notwithstanding unmet requirements when all five conditions hold: submission by the established deadline, verifiability, sufficient completeness to provide a reliable basis, demonstrated best ability in providing information and meeting its requirements, and usability without undue difficulty. Subsection (f) separately requires, to the extent practicable, a written explanation for refusing information.

Nippon’s invocation of a general preverification submission opportunity does not replace the specific questionnaire deadlines. Entirely missing conversion data differ from a minor correction, and subsection (e) does not require acceptance merely because data arrive before verification. The repeated requests, available-records findings and actual explanation sustain the reviewed partial adverse-facts determination under these preserved requirements. The statute’s permission to disregard deficient material remains a permission, rather than a command to reject every correction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB:** Applied to assess whole-record support under the agency’s actual governing standard.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.:** Applied to Commerce’s own discernible reasons; the reviewing court supplies no substitute factual justification.
- **INS v. Ventura:** Preserves the agency’s responsibility for genuinely unresolved findings and delegated calculations. It does not require repetition of this completed adverse-inference decision, and it continues to govern work required by the surviving currency-methodology mandate.
- **United States v. Mead Corp. and United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.:** Their actual-authority and statutory-limit distinctions remain intact; independent construction of the best-ability words supplies the rule here.

#### Law After Decision

Commerce may use an adverse inference for a supported avoidable failure to use available capability even without intentional misconduct. Missing information and failure to cooperate remain separate predicates, with permissive inference, practicable corroboration and the distinct section 1677m duties governing the resulting calculation. The presented record sustains the partial adverse-facts treatment. The separate currency methodology lies outside the submitted question and remains governed by the existing mandate.

#### Source Notes

19 U.S.C. §§1516a(b)(1)(B)(i), 1677e(a)–(c), 1677m(c)–(f), in the version governing the investigation; Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States, 337 F.3d 1373, 1376–1383; Commerce final determination, 64 Fed. Reg. 24329, Comment 29, 24357–24362. The differing March 1 and March 2 descriptions of a later backup submission both follow the questionnaire deadlines and do not affect the holding. Nippon’s assertions about verification assurances remain allegations.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Commerce must implement the lawful calculation on the affected sales consistently with the affirmed adverse-inference ruling and the Federal Circuit’s surviving directions. Its explanation must identify the deficient requests, available information and inadequate effort, with the applicable information and corroboration provisions observed. The trade court reviews compliance and the resulting calculation under section 1516a(b)(1)(B)(i); genuinely unresolved agency calculations remain for Commerce under Ventura.

**End of entry: Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States, Merits decision, 2004-05-10.**

### Lipscomb v. Federal Labor Relations Authority

Merits decision, 2004-05-13

#### Chronology and Posture

**Lipscomb v. Federal Labor Relations Authority.** Decided May 13, 2004, on merits review of the Fifth Circuit's June 24, 2003 judgment, 333 F.3d 611, affirming summary judgment against the Mississippi National Guard and its adjutant general. The question is whether federal law permits the FLRA to conduct a representation election among federally designated civilian technicians and whether that proceeding violates the Tenth Amendment or state sovereign immunity.

The technicians are federally paid civilian employees whose employment the adjutant general administers under 32 U.S.C. §709. Required military affiliation accompanies the civilian employment. The Guard and adjutant general seek to enjoin the representation election.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment rejecting the requested injunction stands. The FLRA representation proceeding may continue for the covered civilian technicians.

#### Opinion Topology

O’CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### Federal civilian employment determines the representation obligation

**Holding and operative rule:** A National Guard organization and adjutant general administering the federal civilian technician employment created by 32 U.S.C. §709 must comply with the applicable federal labor statute in that federal employer capacity. The uniformed-services exclusion governs military service; required military membership does not extinguish the separately designated civilian appointment or defeat this representation election.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls both holdings and their qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 709 makes these technicians employees of the relevant military Department and the United States and designates the adjutant general to employ and administer them. Sections 7103 and 2105 preserve the distinction between that appointment and military membership. Congress's express allocation supplies the federal employment relationship; the holding does not depend on treating every Guard activity as an Executive agency activity. The argument that the employer's state organizational identity precludes coverage overlooks the particular departmental employment function the statute assigns. NASA v. FLRA directs attention to the actual statutory representation relationship, while NFFE v. Department of the Interior preserves the Authority's enacted limits. Feres addresses military tort liability and does not displace express civilian labor coverage.

Representation eligibility is distinct from the legality of a bargaining demand. Section 7103(a)(12) requires neither agreement nor concession; §7103(a)(14)(C) excludes matters specifically provided for by federal statute from conditions of employment. Section 709's particular military-management, discipline and appeal exceptions remain operative. A later dispute over a concrete proposal must apply the applicable statutory procedure and the actual exclusion.

##### Neither structural objection defeats this federal employment proceeding

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying the federal labor statute to this expressly created federal employer function neither commands state administration of federal regulation of unrelated private persons nor subjects the State in its sovereign capacity to the asserted private suit. The official's additional state military authority supplies no immunity from this federal representation obligation.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Reno v. Condon distinguishes regulation of a government's covered operations from the compulsory administration of others' conduct prohibited by Printz and Mack. Here Congress regulates its departmental employment arrangement through the designated administrator. Federal Maritime Commission requires examination of actual statutory function and relief. That inquiry identifies federal employment administration here, rather than relying solely on an administrative label or the absence of damages.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Reno v. Condon:** Applied to the regulated function; its own-activity reasoning supports analysis of the identified federal employment arrangement.
- **Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States:** Distinguished because the representation duty governs the federal employer function rather than conscripting a state executive to administer regulation of unrelated persons.
- **NASA v. FLRA; NFFE v. Department of the Interior:** Applied to identify the statutory representation relationship and enforce actual limits on labor obligations.
- **Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority:** Applied in requiring actual function and remedy analysis rather than immunity by title or procedural label.
- **Feres v. United States:** Distinguished; military tort liability supplies no repeal of the civilian representation provisions.

#### Law After Decision

Federal civilian technician administration carries the labor obligations Congress attached to that function. The Guard's dual character must be assessed by the capacity and particular obligation at issue. Representation proceeds subject to the statute's genuine military and management exclusions, which govern later concrete bargaining disputes.

#### Source Notes

Lipscomb v. FLRA, 333 F.3d 611 (5th Cir. 2003); 32 U.S.C. §709; 5 U.S.C. §§2105, 7103; U.S. Const., amend. X. The matter concerns civilian representation, and the record presents no concrete bargaining proposal for adjudication.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Guard and adjutant general must cooperate with the lawful representation process on the FLRA's schedule. Any objection to a particular bargaining subject must follow the applicable statutory procedure and identify the military or management restriction on which it rests.

### Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P., No. 02-1689

Merits decision, 2004-05-17

#### Chronology and Posture

**Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P., No. 02-1689.** Argued March 3, 2004. Decided May 17, 2004, on review of the Fifth Circuit. The question is whether a partnership's post-filing membership change cures the absence of complete diversity when the partnership remains the same plaintiff.

Atlas had Mexican partners when it commenced this action against a Mexican corporation. Those partners left before trial, but Atlas remained the plaintiff. Grupo raised the jurisdictional objection after the jury verdict and before judgment. The Fifth Circuit permitted the membership change to preserve the litigation outcome.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The action must be dismissed without prejudice for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. The verdict cannot support a federal merits judgment; any judgment depending on it must be vacated.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O’CONNOR, KENNEDY, and THOMAS, JJ., joined. GINSBURG, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS, SOUTER, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### A continuing party's changed citizenship does not cure the initial defect

**Holding and operative rule:** Diversity jurisdiction depends on the citizenship of a continuing party when the action begins. A partnership's post-filing removal of nondiverse members does not cure diversity absent at commencement while that same partnership remains a party; the party invoking federal jurisdiction must establish the required citizenship at the controlling time.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court directly adopts the rule and jurisdictional remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Carden v. Arkoma Associates attributes every partner's citizenship to the partnership. Atlas therefore lacked complete diversity when it sued. The later departure of the Mexican partners changed the citizenship attributed to Atlas; it did not remove Atlas from the action. Mollan v. Torrance supplies the commencement rule. Section 1653 permits correction of defective jurisdictional allegations, not replacement of the underlying facts on which jurisdiction depended.

Newman-Green permits dismissal of an actual dispensable party, subject to dispensability and prejudice requirements. Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment when lawful dismissal of the actual nondiverse party creates complete diversity before trial and through judgment. That exception concerns changed parties, not changed citizenship of the same party. The individuals' appearance on a counterclaim does not turn their withdrawal from the partnership into a court-ordered dismissal curing the original plaintiff's citizenship.

Atlas's proposed extension treats elimination of the nondiverse membership interests as equivalent to dropping a litigant. Citizenship attribution and party status perform different work. The former identifies the citizenship of the entity that remains before the court; it does not create a Rule 21 dismissal of that entity's former members. Finality and the cost of repeating litigation cannot supply the missing jurisdictional authority. Grupo's late objection likewise cannot create jurisdiction by waiver. Kontrick distinguishes statutory jurisdiction from forfeitable procedural prescriptions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Carden v. Arkoma Associates, 494 U.S. 185:** Applied to attribute all partners' citizenship to Atlas at commencement; attribution does not make membership withdrawal a dismissal of a party.
- **Mollan v. Torrance, 9 Wheat. 537:** Applied to measure a continuing party's citizenship when suit begins.
- **Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis:** Distinguished and preserved at its actual scope: a lawful party dismissal that establishes diversity before trial and through judgment.
- **Newman-Green, Inc. v. Alfonzo-Larrain, 490 U.S. 826:** Preserved as authority to drop an actual dispensable party subject to prejudice review; it does not change original jurisdictional facts through §1653.
- **Kontrick v. Ryan:** Applied to distinguish statutory jurisdiction from claim-processing rules whose protection may be forfeited.

#### Law After Decision

A continuing partnership cannot cure an initial diversity defect by changing its membership after filing. The recognized dismissal of an actual dispensable party remains available on its own requirements. Litigation expense, consent and delayed objection supply no independent federal jurisdiction.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Ginsburg, dissenting, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer.** The four would affirm preservation of the litigation outcome against this jurisdictional objection. They accept Carden's attribution of the former members' citizenship at filing and expressly extend Caterpillar's cure principle to the real removal of those citizenship interests before trial, with complete diversity persisting through judgment. Because the partnership's jurisdictional citizenship is derived from its members, their genuine departure removes the precise obstacle that made the initial suit nondiverse.

The dissent accepts that the departed partners were not thereby dismissed as actual parties to the original action. It considers that formal difference insufficient to require destruction of a verdict reached after the relevant interests became diverse. Its proposed extension concerns this actual pretrial membership cure; expense or Grupo's delayed objection alone would not establish jurisdiction, and a defect persisting through trial would remain fatal. The four would preserve the outcome against this ground while leaving independent appellate questions to ordinary review.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§1332, 1653; Fed. R. Civ. P. 21; Carden v. Arkoma Associates, 494 U.S. 185; Newman-Green, Inc. v. Alfonzo-Larrain, 490 U.S. 826; Mollan v. Torrance, 9 Wheat. 537. The membership withdrawal was not a court-ordered dismissal curing the original plaintiff's citizenship.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit for entry of a jurisdictional dismissal without prejudice and removal of any merits judgment resting on the unsupported verdict. A renewed action must establish its own jurisdiction and comply with applicable limitations rules.

**End of entry: Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P., Merits decision, 2004-05-17.**

### Sabri v. United States, No. 03-44

Merits decision, 2004-05-17

#### Chronology and Posture

**Sabri v. United States, No. 03-44.** Argued March 3, 2004. Decided May 17, 2004, on review of the Eighth Circuit's reinstatement of the indictment, 326 F.3d 937. The question is whether Congress may prohibit bribery of a federally assisted entity's agents without requiring proof that each bribe affected particular federal dollars.

The indictment alleges three corrupt offers to a Minneapolis council member concerning development business, together with qualifying federal assistance to the city and development agency. The dismissal rested on a facial constitutional objection to the absence of a dollar-tracing element. The allegations remain for trial.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Eighth Circuit's judgment reinstating the indictment stands. The prosecution returns for trial under the enacted elements and independently preserved defenses.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment. The joins and grounds are set out below.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirmance | Bounded Spending and Necessary and Proper ground; no dollar-tracing requirement; statutory trial conditions |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | — | Affirmance on a distinct ground | The pleaded direct program relationship is plainly adapted to federal protection and defeats this facial objection |

#### Holdings

##### Congress may protect the actual assisted institution without tracing individual dollars

**Holding and operative rule:** The Spending and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit Congress to prohibit corrupt payments directed at the agents and substantial transactions of an entity receiving genuine federal assistance, without tracing the payment to particular federal dollars. Section 666's lack of a traced-dollar element does not make that assisted-entity bribery mechanism facially invalid; actual benefits, agency, covered business or transactions and corrupt intent remain necessary.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice opinion of the Court directly adopts this rule. Thomas agrees with the judgment on the distinct ground described in his concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** McCulloch v. Maryland permits appropriate means of carrying an enumerated power into execution. A bribe that corrupts an assisted institution's authorized agent can distort the institution's use of resources and its administration of federally supported work. That operational relationship permits preventive protection of the institution. The fungibility of its money reinforces the relationship but does not replace it. Salinas supplies the enacted offense's absence of a demonstrable federal-dollar effect requirement; Fischer supplies the separate requirement of actual programmatic assistance rather than ordinary commercial receipts.

The objection that a modest grant would authorize general federal policing overlooks the class Congress regulated: intentional corruption of actual recipients' agents in substantial recipient business. Geographic proximity to federal spending or receipt of ordinary procurement revenue is insufficient. Lopez and Morrison require an identified enumerated-power connection rather than generalized downstream consequences. Here the connection is protection of actual federal expenditure and its recipient institution, not an assertion that every local offense affects the national economy. Dole and Kansas govern conditions attached to a recipient's funding choice; they do not restrict Congress to a grant condition when it directly prohibits third-party bribery threatening an assisted institution.

The pleaded assistance, agency and development-business relationship permits rejection of this facial tracing objection. A materially different prosecution claimed to lack that institutional relationship requires its concrete facts and lies outside this pretrial record.

##### Every enacted element and the compensation exception retain their force

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt corrupt giving, offering or agreement to give a thing of value, intent to influence or reward a covered agent, covered institutional business or a transaction or series involving at least $5,000, and more than $10,000 in qualifying federal benefits during the statutory one-year period. Section 666(c) preserves bona fide salary, wages, fees and other compensation paid, and expenses paid or reimbursed, in the usual course of business.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The transaction-value threshold and the amount of the bribe are different requirements. The continuous one-year benefits period may begin up to twelve months before the offense or end up to twelve months after it and may straddle the offense. Fischer's genuine-assistance rule gives the benefits condition substantive work; a lawful reimbursement is distinct from the corrupt transaction charged. Salinas preserves the other statutory elements while rejecting traced federal loss as an addition. The constitutional ruling leaves those conditions for trial on the indictment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **McCulloch v. Maryland, 4 Wheat. 316:** Applied to protection of an identified federal spending undertaking through appropriate preventive means.
- **Salinas v. United States:** Applied on statutory meaning; the constitutional question about the enacted no-tracing mechanism is resolved on the actual institution-and-agent relationship.
- **Fischer v. United States:** Applied to require genuine programmatic benefits and preserve the bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense exception.
- **United States v. Lopez; United States v. Morrison:** Applied to require an actual enumerated-power connection; generalized economic consequences do not supply it.
- **South Dakota v. Dole; Kansas v. United States:** Distinguished as funding-condition decisions, while this prohibition directly regulates third-party bribery protecting an assisted institution.

#### Law After Decision

Section 666's assisted-entity bribery prohibition has a sufficient Spending and Necessary and Proper connection without individual federal-dollar tracing. Prosecution remains tied to genuine assistance, the recipient's agent and business, substantial transaction value and corrupt intent. Salinas's rejection of an added effects element and Fischer's limits operate together.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Thomas concludes that the pleaded bribery of an agent administering the directly assisted institution's development business is plainly adapted to protecting that federal undertaking. The facial claim that an express dollar-tracing element is indispensable therefore fails. He requires an evident protective relationship, and declines to treat any rational association between incidental federal receipts and local integrity as sufficient.

His reasoning applies McCulloch's means-to-power inquiry at the narrower scope of this indictment. The identifiable-undertaking and genuine-functional-relationship analysis applied in Lopez and Rancho Viejo supports this direct protective application; it does not add a statutory nexus element or dispense with proof at trial. Salinas remains a construction of the enacted elements. Thomas agrees that the case may proceed while withholding assent from the Court's broader institutional rule for all covered transactions.

#### Source Notes

18 U.S.C. §666(a)(2), (b), (c), (d)(5); U.S. Const., Art. I, §8, cls. 1 and 18; United States v. Sabri, 326 F.3d 937 (8th Cir. 2003). The corrupt offers and program relationship are allegations in a pretrial indictment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must permit the prosecution to proceed, instruct on the actual benefits, agency, transaction-value and corrupt-intent requirements, and apply §666(c)'s exception when implicated. The Government bears the criminal burden of proof. Independently preserved defenses receive ordinary adjudication.

**End of entry: Sabri v. United States, Merits decision, 2004-05-17.**

### Tennessee Student Assistance Corporation v. Hood, No. 02-1606

Merits decision, 2004-05-17

#### Chronology and Posture

**Tennessee Student Assistance Corporation v. Hood, No. 02-1606.** Argued March 1, 2004. Decided May 17, 2004, on review of the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of the denial of TSAC's immunity objection. The question is whether state immunity bars a bankruptcy court from determining undue hardship and dischargeability of a state-held student loan.

Hood reopened her Chapter 7 case and sought a hardship discharge. TSAC had filed no proof of claim and objected to the adversary complaint and summons. The relief determines the debt's enforceability through Hood's discharge, rather than seeking affirmative monetary recovery from the State.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The denial of TSAC's immunity motion stands. The bankruptcy court may adjudicate dischargeability under §523(a)(8), with Hood bearing the applicable undue-hardship burden and TSAC afforded notice and an opportunity to contest it.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. O’CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion. The part-specific joins are set out below.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Parts I and III | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirmance and hardship adjudication | Discharge jurisdiction; actual statutory conditions and remand |
| Court opinion, Part II | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer | Independently sufficient ground for affirmance | Express authorization under the Article I enforcement rule |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | O'Connor | Kennedy | Affirmance | Joins Parts I and III; withholds Part II's Article I ground |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Reverse and dismiss the adversary action against TSAC | Compulsory private process; express reconsideration of contrary Article I precedent |

#### Holdings

##### I. Determining the debtor's discharge does not require the State's consent

**Holding and operative rule:** A bankruptcy court may determine whether a state-held student-loan debt falls within the debtor's discharge when the requested relief fixes the debtor's obligations rather than awarding affirmative personal recovery against the State. The prescribed hardship complaint and summons do not change the jurisdictional character of that discharge determination, and the State's choice not to file a claim supplies no veto over it.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion supplies seven direct joins for this independently sufficient ground, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Bankruptcy jurisdiction determines the debtor's estate, obligations and discharge. Local Loan v. Hunt supplies the discharge-status principle and ancillary authority to give a discharge effect. A hardship inquiry determines whether an education debt remains outside that statutory discharge; it does not impose an independent duty on the State to pay. The creditor receives notice and an opportunity to oppose the debtor's showing. Calling the prescribed notice a summons does not convert this limited inquiry into personal recovery.

The strongest contrary argument identifies the real burdens of defending a debtor-specific adversary proceeding. Those burdens matter, but they do not alter the object and legal effect of this proceeding. Nordic Village distinguishes genuine bankruptcy jurisdiction from an affirmative money demand dressed in property language. Here the operative relief actually defines the debtor's discharge. The holding is confined to that function, rather than every proceeding bearing a bankruptcy label. Irving Trust supplies bankruptcy-administration context; its State-participation setting supplies no waiver by TSAC.

##### II. Express Article I authorization independently permits the proceeding

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may subject a State to private enforcement when the obligation and implementing adjudication lie within a valid Article I power and Congress unmistakably authorizes that exposure. Bankruptcy adjudication of the education-debt discharge exception meets those conditions, and §106(a)'s express inclusion of §§523 and 524 independently defeats TSAC's immunity objection.

**Authority:** Part II supplies five direct joins for this alternative holding, as identified above. O’Connor and Kennedy do not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid establishes the valid-obligation, appropriate-enforcement and unmistakable-authorization rule; Garrett and Hibbs retain it. Determining which debts survive the debtor's discharge is part of the Bankruptcy Clause's debt-adjustment function, and ordinary adjudication of the statutory exception implements that function. Section 106(a)(1) expressly names the relevant provisions, subsection (a)(2) authorizes determination of their application, and subsection (a)(3) permits necessary process. Clarity and constitutional power are both present. The objection distinguishing substantive power from compulsory private adjudication is the objection those precedents rejected; the Court adheres to their rule.

Authorization is distinct from consent. TSAC's nonfiling supplies no claim-linked waiver under §106(b); the estate-claim offset in §106(c) is a separate mechanism. Section 106(a) prohibits punitive damages, subjects costs and fees to 28 U.S.C. §2412(d)(2)(A)'s provisions and limitations, requires enforcement consistent with appropriate nonbankruptcy law, and creates no otherwise absent substantive cause of action.

##### III. The education-debt exception still requires an undue-hardship determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 523(a)(8) excepts the covered education obligations from discharge unless that exclusion would impose undue hardship on the debtor and the debtor's dependents. Hood must establish that statutory hardship through the applicable adjudicative process; immunity and the State's nonparticipation do not replace the showing.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by the same seven Justices as Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** The provision covers an educational benefit overpayment or loan made, insured or guaranteed by a governmental unit; a loan made under a program funded in whole or part by a governmental unit or nonprofit institution; and an obligation to repay funds received as an educational benefit, scholarship or stipend. Determining whether the statutory hardship condition is met is ordinary merits work on remand. The Court's two jurisdictional grounds permit that determination; the loan remains subject to the statutory discharge exception until Hood carries her burden.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Local Loan Co. v. Hunt, 292 U.S. 234:** Applied for discharge as a determination of the debtor's status and for authority ancillary to that determination; the present State-creditor application rests on the actual relief here.
- **New York v. Irving Trust Co., 288 U.S. 329:** Supplies bankruptcy-administration context; its participation and distribution holding does not establish waiver by this nonfiling creditor.
- **United States v. Nordic Village:** Distinguished from an affirmative personal recovery under original §106(c); its remedy-specific consent rule remains in force.
- **Florida Prepaid; Garrett; Hibbs:** Applied in Part II to express enforcement of a valid Article I obligation through an appropriate measure.
- **Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority:** Its function-and-remedy analysis remains distinct; this decision rests on bankruptcy discharge and express statutory authorization.

#### Law After Decision

The bankruptcy court may adjudicate the education-debt exception against a State creditor on both the seven-Justice discharge ground and the five-Justice Article I ground. The debtor must prove undue hardship to obtain the discharge determination sought. An affirmative recovery from the State, recovery of property it holds or a separate coercive enforcement remedy lies outside the requested discharge relief and requires its own source and remedy analysis.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Kennedy.** The two join the discharge-specific analysis and statutory remand. They retain their structural objection to general Article I authorization of private suits against a State, and therefore withhold Part II. Their agreement rests on the bankruptcy court's actual determination of Hood's discharge and the absence of an affirmative State payment obligation. Nordic Village's remedy distinction and the function inquiry emphasized in their Federal Maritime Commission positions support that limited basis. Their concurrence creates no exemption for every bankruptcy adversary proceeding.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** The two would reverse the immunity denial and remand for dismissal of this adversary action against TSAC. In their view, compelling a nonparticipating State creditor to defend a separate, individualized hardship claim imposes the private-suit burden immunity protects. A claim presumptively outside the discharge cannot escape that objection merely by being characterized as an adjustment of status. Local Loan involved a private creditor, and Irving Trust involved participation in distribution; neither resolves the coercive proceeding here.

They accept §106(a)'s clear authorization but would reconsider Florida Prepaid, Garrett and the related Article I enforcement rule to the extent necessary to preserve immunity against this private judicial compulsion. Their objection expressly challenges existing precedent. They would dismiss the adversary action against TSAC while leaving Hood's underlying bankruptcy case intact; they prescribe no substitute motion procedure.

#### Source Notes

11 U.S.C. §§106, 523(a)(8), 524, as in force for this proceeding; Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7001; U.S. Const., Art. I, §8, cl. 4; Local Loan Co. v. Hunt, 292 U.S. 234; New York v. Irving Trust Co., 288 U.S. 329. The hardship allegations await adjudication; TSAC filed no proof of claim.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the bankruptcy court for a scheduled hardship adjudication. TSAC must receive notice and an opportunity to present its position; Hood must prove the statutory basis for bringing the debt within her discharge. The court must then enter the dischargeability determination with the binding effect bankruptcy law authorizes.

**End of entry: Tennessee Student Assistance Corporation v. Hood, Merits decision, 2004-05-17.**

### Tennessee v. Lane, No. 02-1667

Merits decision, 2004-05-17

#### Chronology and Posture

**Tennessee v. Lane, No. 02-1667.** Argued January 13, 2004. Decided May 17, 2004, on review of the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of the denial of Tennessee's immunity motion. The question is whether Congress validly authorized private Title II enforcement against a State for disability-based denial of access to its courts.

Lane alleges that inaccessible courtrooms required him to crawl to a criminal proceeding and that he was later arrested after refusing another inaccessible appearance. Jones, a court reporter, alleges exclusion from professional opportunities because of inaccessible courthouses. They seek damages and equitable relief. These allegations present distinct individual claims at an immunity stage.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The denial of Tennessee's categorical immunity motion stands for the pleaded court-access claims. The case returns for adjudication of statutory coverage, actual exclusion, accommodation, causation, defenses and lawful relief under the court-access rule.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O’CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

#### Holdings

##### Reasonable judicial-access duties are valid preventive enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may authorize private Title II actions against States for reasonable disability accommodations securing access to judicial proceedings and the services through which those proceedings operate. Those bounded duties congruently prevent and remedy constitutional denials of meaningful judicial participation, including statutory violations that do not require proof of a separate individual constitutional tort; §12202 unmistakably authorizes enforcement.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court directly adopts the court-access rule and ordinary statutory remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Popovich already sustains reasonable hearing accommodations and related protection enabling meaningful participation in custody adjudication. Physical exclusion from a courtroom threatens that same opportunity through a different barrier. Boerne permits prevention beyond conduct independently adjudged unconstitutional when the statutory means fit the constitutional injury. A criminal defendant's opportunity to attend and defend and the protected opportunity to participate meaningfully in adjudication supply concrete constitutional anchors. The present inquiry examines the functional judicial-access class rather than every Title II service.

Qualification, essential eligibility, reasonable modification, effective alternatives and the applicable fundamental-alteration and existing-facilities burden limits confine the statutory response. Ordinary disability rationality review remains the classification baseline; the protected opportunity for meaningful judicial participation supplies the constitutional interest here. Garrett's Section 5 conclusion concerned Title I's general employment requirements at their full presented scope; it preserved differently fitted prevention and left Title II to its own analysis. The immediate relationship between inaccessible judicial services and loss of meaningful participation supplies the fit here.

The contrary argument is that a courthouse houses persons and activities without identical individual constitutional entitlements. A courthouse address alone is insufficient. The rule concerns participation in judicial proceedings and their operative services. Reasonably accommodating persons who serve those proceedings can protect the judicial function without making every beneficiary's constitutional interest identical. This defined preventive class therefore permits adjudication of both pleaded courthouse-access claims. Jones must establish that her actual claim concerns judicial access or those services; her professional opportunity is not equated with Lane's criminal-defense right. A claim showing only an unrelated employment benefit falls outside this constitutional basis.

##### Statutory conditions and the State's actual defense burdens remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish qualification under §12131, disability-based exclusion, denial or discrimination under §12132, and the conditions of the remedy supplied through §12133. A State invoking fundamental alteration must substantiate that defense under the applicable rule; the distinct existing-facilities defense and its procedural requirements govern only when their own conditions apply.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion adopts the claim requirements, defense allocation and qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 35.130(b)(7) requires necessary reasonable modifications unless the public entity demonstrates that they would fundamentally alter the service, program or activity. The claimant still establishes the applicable claim and necessary reasonable modification. The State must support its claimed alteration with evidence and reasons tied to that actual service and modification; a label, general inconvenience or cost assertion is insufficient.

Section 35.150(a) separately requires an existing-facilities service, program or activity, viewed in its entirety, to be readily accessible. It does not necessarily require accessibility of every existing building and does not require action threatening or destroying a historic property's historic significance. Under §35.150(a)(3), the entity bears the burden of proving fundamental alteration or undue financial and administrative burdens. The head or designee must consider all resources available to fund and operate the service and supply written reasons. Even when the proposed action creates those burdens or alteration, the entity must take another action avoiding them while ensuring receipt of its benefits or services.

Those provisions have distinct reach. Financial and administrative burden is not an added defense to every §35.130(b)(7) modification claim; the head-of-entity and written-reasons requirements likewise belong to §35.150(a)(3). Section 35.150(b)(1) permits effective alternative methods rather than structural change when those methods achieve compliance and prioritizes the most integrated appropriate setting. The court must apply those actual conditions to the claims and evidence. A barrier, ineffective judicial access and a compensable injury remain distinct inquiries. Other public-service applications lie outside the question presented.

##### Prospective relief must effectively cure the proved violation within lawful remedial scope

**Holding and operative rule:** If statutory liability and the requirements for prospective relief are established, the court must provide effective relief within its lawful authority; a narrower measure suffices only if it fully cures the proved violation for the persons the court may lawfully protect. Any injunction must impose enforceable obligations, and policy changes or implementation deadlines may be required when the actual remedial findings establish their necessity for effective relief.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Popovich establishes an enforceable access duty while separating authority to sue from entitlement to the actual award. Frew requires effective enforcement of lawful prospective obligations and appropriate correction of established noncompliance; its existing decree does not predetermine a new decree's beneficiaries or terms. On remand the court must examine actual access barriers, reasonable accommodations and alternatives, supported defenses and relevant continuing risk. The actual standing, merits and remedial findings determine the form and reach of relief. Necessary policy correction may incidentally benefit others, but affected-person status alone supplies no standing or remedial entitlement. Lane's criminal-proceeding allegations and Jones's court-reporting allegations require separate application of these rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas:** Applied and extended from reasonable assistance in parental-custody hearings to the defined physical judicial-access class; its prevention beyond individual constitutional torts and actual statutory conditions remain controlling.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Applied to require congruence and proportionality between the protected opportunity and the preventive duties, without a mandatory evidence quota or violation by each defendant State.
- **Board of Trustees v. Garrett:** Distinguished at its actual Title I scope; its Section 5 analysis leaves room for a differently supported and fitted Title II application.
- **Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs:** Preserves statutory-duty and constitutional-injury distinctions and statute-specific fit.
- **Olmstead v. L.C.:** Distinguished as a statutory integration decision, rather than a general immunity holding.
- **Frew v. Hawkins:** Applied for effective enforcement of lawful prospective obligations; its existing decree and defined beneficiaries remain distinct from the new remedies to be adjudicated here.

#### Law After Decision

Expressly authorized Title II claims concerning access to judicial proceedings and their operative services may proceed against States under Section 5. The claimant need not establish an independent constitutional tort as an additional gateway within that valid preventive class. Actual statutory coverage, effective accommodation, defenses and remedial proof continue to govern each claim, including Jones's asserted role in judicial services. The State bears the applicable fundamental-alteration burden, with §35.150's distinct existing-facilities requirements preserved. Lawful prospective relief must effectively cure the proved violation; the necessity and extent of policy correction or deadlines depend on actual remedial findings and the persons the court may lawfully protect.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the court-access holding, the State's defense burden and the qualified effective-relief guidance. He would additionally prescribe a default for a proved unlawful access policy or practice: correct that policy for the persons it governs unless Tennessee shows that narrower relief completely cures the violation. He would direct the district court to identify each inaccessible service, effective accommodation and continuing risk, then set enforceable access measures and deadlines for the violation proved. Standing, statutory liability and the independent prerequisites for damages and prospective relief remain necessary.

His additional position gives the policy's proved reach operative remedial weight and places the showing for narrower correction on Tennessee. Popovich supplies the protected opportunity and an enforceable access duty. The Chief would specify this implementation requirement now so that recognition of meaningful participation produces effective correction, while preserving effective alternatives and actual statutory burden limits.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** The two would vacate the categorical immunity denial and remand for a supported statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful judicial participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it. They accept §12202's express authorization and the constitutional hearing right. Their objection concerns the fit of the broader preventive damages regime, particularly when the asserted interest is a professional opportunity rather than participation in one's own adjudication.

Following their Popovich position, they would reconsider that decision's preventive-damages reach to the extent necessary. They do not treat it as absent. Effective participation may be supplied by alternatives even when a further statutory accommodation duty is alleged. Boerne and Garrett require examination of that relationship rather than reliance on the courthouse setting alone. Their proposed remand concerns the actual preserved claims; it is not a universal rule that all Section 5 prevention requires an individual constitutional tort, a prescribed incident count or a violation by every regulated State.

#### Source Notes

42 U.S.C. §§12131–12134, 12202; 28 C.F.R. §§35.130(b)(7), 35.150(a)–(b) (2003); U.S. Const., amend. XIV, §5; Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, decided November 4, 2002. Lane's and Jones's accounts are allegations at the immunity stage; individual liability and remedy require adjudication.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower court must determine each plaintiff's disability and qualification, the proceeding or service at issue, actual access, available reasonable accommodations and the State's supported defenses under the applicable regulation. It must resolve actionable exclusion, causation, compensable injury and lawful relief. If the predicates for prospective relief are established, it must provide an effective cure for those it may lawfully protect, with enforceable obligations and any policy changes or implementation deadlines shown necessary by the remedial findings. Lane's criminal-proceeding allegations and Jones's court-reporting allegations require separate application.

**End of entry: Tennessee v. Lane, Merits decision, 2004-05-17.**

### Till v. SCS Credit Corp., No. 02-1016

Merits decision, 2004-05-17

#### Chronology and Posture

**Till v. SCS Credit Corp., No. 02-1016.** Argued December 2, 2003. Decided May 17, 2004, on review of the Seventh Circuit's direction to use a presumptive contract-rate method under Chapter 13. The question is how deferred payments must preserve the effective-date value of an allowed secured claim over the creditor's objection.

The retained truck secured an allowed claim of $4,000. The contract rate was 21 percent; the proposed plan used 9.5 percent, comprising an 8 percent prime reference and a 1.5-point addition. The plan distributes future cash.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy.

The Seventh Circuit's contract-rate presumption is reversed. The case returns for a prime-plus determination, supported risk findings and remaining confirmation work. Thomas supports reversal on a distinct ground that regards 9.5 percent as sufficient for the interest component while preserving the other confirmation conditions.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion. The joins and grounds are set out below.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg | Reverse and remand | Prime plus supported risk; creditor's preponderance burden on a demanded higher adjustment; remaining confirmation duties |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | — | Reverse on a distinct ground | Time value for deferred cash; 9.5 percent sufficient for that component; other confirmation conditions preserved |
| Dissent | Scalia | O'Connor and Kennedy | Affirm the method below | Rebuttable contract/comparable-loan method and evidentiary application |

#### Holdings

##### Prime plus the supported risk adjustment preserves present value

**Holding and operative rule:** For deferred payments of the allowed secured claim under §1325(a)(5)(B)(ii), the Chapter 13 court must begin with the national prime rate and add the adjustment supported by the actual payment stream's additional repayment risk. The contract rate is relevant evidence when its components and comparability warrant consideration, but it carries no presumption and is not an automatic entitlement.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court directly adopts the formula, allocation and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1325(a)(5) separately allows acceptance, lien retention with effective-date present-value protection, or surrender. Section 506 and Rash identify the secured value of retained collateral; §1325(a)(5)(B)(ii) determines what deferred payments must provide for that value. Prime supplies an observable reference already containing baseline time value, risk and ordinary lending components. It is not risk-free. The court must determine the additional risk imposed by the actual plan, considering the debtor's circumstances, retained security, duration and terms insofar as they bear on repayment, without treating those considerations as an exclusive checklist.

The contract or a replacement subprime loan can include origination, marketing and profit components unrelated to preserving this allowed claim's value. The statute does not automatically reproduce those components. Useful market evidence remains admissible to the actual inquiry, and ordinary components embedded in prime are not mechanically removed. The answer to the competing market-rate method is therefore a statutory and evidentiary distinction, not disregard of real credit risk. The Court's formula must compensate the risk proved. Contrary to the time-value-only reading, uncertainty of the promised cash payments affects their present value; feasibility addresses ability to perform and does not guarantee payment.

##### A creditor must substantiate the higher adjustment it demands

**Holding and operative rule:** After the debtor supplies an adequate plan and financial foundation for a proposed formula rate, a creditor demanding a higher risk adjustment must show by a preponderance of the evidence that the additional adjustment is warranted. The debtor retains disclosure and responsive-evidence duties and the independently applicable confirmation responsibilities; this allocation concerns the creditor's requested increment.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion adopts the allocation, ordinary proof standard and qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The creditor's access to lending and collateral-market information supports assigning persuasion on its demanded increase to the creditor. Grogan v. Garner supplies the ordinary civil preponderance baseline in bankruptcy. Its discharge-exception holding addresses a different provision, but its general proof principle supports the ordinary standard for this monetary determination. Neither a creditor's substantial recovery interest nor the debtor's fresh-start interest supplies a heightened threshold.

The court weighs all relevant evidence, including evidence supplied by the debtor. An increase supported by that evidence may be awarded even if the creditor fails to establish every part of its original demand; an unsupported portion receives no presumption. At evidentiary equipoise on the additional increment, the creditor has not carried its burden. Supported estimates and economic judgment remain permissible: preponderance measures persuasion that the adjustment is warranted, not mathematical certainty or proof that default itself is more likely than not.

The debtor must first provide an adequate financial and plan foundation and respond to material contrary evidence. The court must reject an unsupported proposed rate, apply adequate present value and independently determine feasibility and other confirmation conditions. Creditor silence therefore supplies no automatic entitlement to confirmation. Conversely, the debtor's remaining responsibilities cannot transfer persuasion on the demanded increment back to the debtor after minimal creditor production; that would undo the allocation rather than implement it.

The Treasury comparison establishes that prime already contains risk and other ordinary lending components. It does not establish this plan's default risk or the adequacy of the offered 1.5-point increment. The bankruptcy court must make supported findings after considering both parties' evidence.

##### Present value and feasibility must both be satisfied

**Holding and operative rule:** The plan must provide the allowed secured claim's required present value and satisfy §1325(a)(6)'s separate requirement that the debtor can make all payments and comply with the plan. An adequate rate may not be reduced merely to make the plan affordable; if both conditions cannot be met, confirmation must be denied or a lawful amended plan considered.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Rash's adjusted replacement valuation preserves the relevant secured principal and fixes no interest methodology. Nobelman's protection of specified principal-residence mortgage rights does not preserve this truck lender's contractual rate. The court must identify the appropriate prime benchmark at confirmation, determine the supported risk addition and evaluate feasibility before approving the payment stream. The risk adjustment's scale must follow supported evidence concerning this plan.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash:** Applied to the allowed secured value of retained collateral and distinguished from the rate needed for deferred payment; its refusal mechanically to remove dealer profit and overhead from a supported collateral price remains intact.
- **Nobelman v. American Savings Bank:** Distinguished because its protection of specified principal-residence mortgage rights does not grant a vehicle lender an automatic contractual rate.
- **Lamie v. United States Trustee:** Applied to respect the distinct work of the enacted valuation and feasibility provisions.
- **Grogan v. Garner, 498 U.S. 279:** Its ordinary civil preponderance principle is applied to the creditor's assigned higher-increment burden.

#### Law After Decision

The prime-plus method and the creditor's burden to substantiate a demanded higher risk adjustment by a preponderance of the evidence govern the presented Chapter 13 deferred-secured-payment question by five direct votes. Courts must separately enforce adequate present value and feasibility. Evidence of comparable lending may inform risk without restoring a contract-rate presumption. Valuation of the secured principal remains governed by §506 and Rash.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Thomas reads §1325(a)(5)(B)(ii) to require compensation for delay in the cash the plan will distribute, while §1325(a)(6) separately addresses ability to make those payments. He accepts that uncertain payments have different economic value but considers the statute's identification of the distributed property decisive. A transferred note or other promise would itself be valued with its inherent risk; this plan distributes future cash.

The record reports an 8 percent prime rate two points above a 6 percent risk-free Treasury benchmark. The offered 9.5 percent exceeds that temporal measure, so Thomas finds it sufficient for the interest component and rejects a higher rate demanded solely for default risk or the old contract. The statutory floor does not require reducing the debtors' offered rate. He preserves every other actual confirmation condition and orders no automatic final confirmation. His rationale does not join mandatory default-risk compensation or the Court's formula.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by O'Connor and Kennedy.** The three would affirm the Seventh Circuit's rebuttable contract/comparable-loan method and return for its application. The uncertain payment stream must be valued economically. The original transaction supplies a starting reference that may be displaced by current rates, collateral coverage, different unsecured exposure, payment duration, bankruptcy supervision, joint vehicle-and-financing pricing or other actual differences. A party seeking departure must substantiate the relevant change; the contract is evidence, not an immutable right to 21 percent.

The dissent objects that a low prime starting estimate can underprice risk when disputes are expensive to litigate. Prime's own risk-and-cost spread does not establish the price of these debtors' payment stream. The three accept meaningful evidence of comparability and reject both a legally riskless cash assumption and confirmation obtained through an artificially low rate. If an adequate rate makes the plan infeasible, confirmation must fail.

#### Source Notes

11 U.S.C. §§506(a), 1325(a)(5), 1325(a)(6); Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash; Nobelman v. American Savings Bank; Grogan v. Garner, 498 U.S. 279, 286–291 (1991). The reported Treasury comparison is record testimony, App. 42, described at 541 U.S. 501 n.10; it establishes neither plan feasibility nor the necessary risk adjustment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for the bankruptcy court to identify the prime benchmark, hear relevant evidence on the plan's additional risk, apply the creditor's preponderance burden on a demanded higher adjustment and enter a reasoned rate finding. The court must separately decide feasibility and the remaining confirmation conditions. The 9.5 percent proposal may be confirmed only on findings satisfying those requirements; an insufficient plan must be denied or lawfully amended.

**End of entry: Till v. SCS Credit Corp., Merits decision, 2004-05-17.**

### Benjamin v. Fraser

Merits decision, 2004-05-20

#### Chronology and Posture

Benjamin v. Fraser; judgment below 343 F.3d 35 (2d Cir. 2003). Merits decision, May 20, 2004. The Court reviews the Second Circuit's September 2, 2003 partial affirmance and partial vacatur of orders governing conditions in New York City jails and the Office of Compliance Consultants. The questions are whether prospective relief for longstanding systemic pretrial conditions requires separate proof of each official's subjective awareness and whether the OCC's actual functions bring it within 18 U.S.C. §3626(f). City officials seek limits on continued duties and monitoring; the detainee classes seek correction of current conditions.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional-relief component | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Existing partial vacatur and facility-specific remedial remand preserved, as detailed below. |
| Continued restricted OCC monitoring | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Restricted monitoring continues at its supported scope, subject to the current prospective-relief requirements. |

The Second Circuit's existing remedial divisions remain operative. The unsupported six-foot bed-spacing prescription and ambiguous twenty-foot-candle requirement remain vacated. Food-service conditions at the Anna M. Kross Center, Adolescent Reception and Detention Center, and George Motchan Detention Center return for particularized reconsideration. The district court must consider the agreed monthly airflow checks of 15 percent of housing and intake areas as an alternative to annual balancing. The supported operational-window repairs, October 15 heating certification, and quarterly bleach power washing remain. The terminations of the isolated hot-water and residential-vermin obligations remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court with the part-specific joins set out below. O’CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in the constitutional judgment and dissenting in part, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Six-Justice controlling rationale for OCC continuation | Actual OCC classification and independent judicial assessment |

#### Holdings

##### I. Longstanding systemic pretrial conditions

**Controlling proposition:** For prospective protection against deliberately maintained, longstanding systemic pretrial conditions, detainees must prove by a preponderance a current substantial risk of serious harm and conditions that lack a reasonable relation to a legitimate nonpunitive objective or are excessive in relation to that objective. Sustained notice, a real opportunity for deliberative response and prolonged inadequate correction establish the institutional culpability present here; separate proof of every responsible incumbent's subjective appreciation is unnecessary, while ordinary negligence is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I supplies the seven direct joins identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Bell v. Wolfish supplies the protection against punishment before adjudication of guilt. That protection requires examining the maintained conditions and their justification, rather than making relief turn on separately proving each incumbent's private mental state. The record establishes years of notice of specified serious conditions and prolonged inadequate responses, alongside successful measures concerning other conditions. Daniels v. Williams excludes ordinary negligence. Crawford-El v. Britton supplies ordinary civil proof for the constitutional factual elements without reducing or replacing those elements. County of Sacramento v. Lewis makes the custodial duty and genuine opportunity for deliberation material; its emergency-pursuit standard does not govern these enduring conditions. Farmer v. Brennan supplies the knowledge and reasonable-response rule for the convicted-prisoner claim it decided, but that distinct rule does not displace Bell in this institutional pretrial action. The City's objection that danger alone cannot establish culpability is sound at that level and is answered by the deliberately maintained conditions, sustained notice and opportunity for correction here. Individual damages and convicted-prisoner claims lie outside this prospective pretrial question.

##### II. The OCC's actual limited functions

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3626(f) applies by actual delegated function, regardless of title. The OCC's restricted observation, information gathering, informal compliance assistance and reporting, with independent judicial determination of disputes and enforcement, do not make this continuous arrangement a special master. The party defending the appointment must produce a supported account of the actual delegated functions; the judge independently determines classification and evaluates reports as evidence subject to proper objections, and a materially expanded delegation requires a new functional assessment.

**Authority:** Part II supplies the six direct joins identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3626(g)(8) covers persons appointed under Rule 53 or inherent authority to exercise a master's powers. Ex parte Peterson recognizes judicial assistance while preserving the judge's responsibility. The OCC gathers operational information and informally assists compliance. It does not conduct adjudicatory hearings, subpoena or swear witnesses, rule on evidence, adjudicate discrete disputes or issue findings entitled to prescribed deference; the judge decides rights and enforces orders. The amended Rule 53, effective December 1, 2003, includes specified pretrial and posttrial appointments, investigation and enforcement duties, and ordinarily de novo review of objections to findings. Thus absence of binding findings or trial hearings alone cannot establish the classification. The complete, limited delegation here remains informational assistance rather than delegated judicial decision or enforcement. The City's functional objection receives that full assessment; neither the OCC's name nor a general exemption for monitors determines the result. The supported-functions requirement identifies the actual powers, terms and practice relied on; it does not allocate the ultimate risk of uncertainty in every classification dispute. The pending-case transition applies insofar as just and practicable.

##### III. The continuous 1982 appointment

**Independently sufficient holding:** Section 3626(f)'s appointment machinery does not recreate the OCC's completed 1982 appointment merely because later orders renew and adjust the same continuous entity. That ground independently supports continuation of this restricted arrangement, subject to the current prospective-relief requirements of sections 3626(a) and (b).

**Authority:** Part III supplies the five direct joins identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute's provisions governing appointments under subsection (f) address the appointment they prescribe. The OCC began in 1982, continued by consent through 1987 and thereafter under periodic orders; the 2001 order renewed and adjusted it. Treating that order as a fresh appointment would replace the actual continuous history with a different event. Section 802(b)(1) reaches all prospective relief, including future duties under old decrees, so this appointment conclusion preserves rather than displaces the independent restrictions on continued monitoring. Benjamin v. Jacobson requires present statutory fit. The restricted monitoring order has supported need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. The amended Rule 53 does not erase the completed appointment or make the OCC's origin an unlimited exemption from present-relief law.

##### IV.A. Each continued duty requires current and tailored findings

**Controlling proposition:** Each continued prospective duty must rest on written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing federal-right violation and relief that is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct that violation, and the least intrusive necessary means. Supported relief may exceed the bare constitutional minimum when those findings establish its remedial necessity; unsupported or completed duties must terminate, and deficient findings require the particularized work specified in the remand.

**Authority:** Part IV.A is joined by all nine on substantive relief and its statutory conditions. The six-Justice OCC coalition also applies those conditions to continued monitoring.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin v. Jacobson separates a substantive violation from the statutory justification for each prospective obligation. The age of the decrees supplies neither current liability nor a reason to abandon effective correction. Bell supplies the pretrial right; section 3626 supplies additional remedial limits. The Constitution's protection does not itself establish a six-foot spacing or numerical-lighting minimum, and the lower judgment correctly requires adequate support for each challenged prescription. The facility-specific findings support the retained measures, while food service and the airflow alternative require the identified further work. The court must give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. A departure from state or local law additionally requires that federal law require it, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation, and that no other relief correct the violation. These findings preserve effective correction at its proved scope.

##### IV.B. Effective alternatives and implementation of supported obligations

**Controlling proposition:** The City must substantiate any proposed narrower cure as adequate to correct the proved violation, while the court independently determines current necessity and statutory fit from the complete record. For supported obligations, the order must identify the responsible officials in their official capacities, the required acts and a reasonable compliance schedule, obtain verifiable condition reports within demonstrated need and lawful authority, and end duties whose current statutory justification has ceased; a valid, sufficiently definite order may be enforced through ordinary judicial procedures after the required notice and opportunity to respond.

**Authority:** Part IV.B supplies seven direct joins, as identified above. O’Connor’s reporting join requires a legally compliant arrangement and does not join OCC continuation.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin v. Jacobson requires current findings for retained duties and gives a section 3626(b)(2) movant the burden of establishing that termination provision's predicate. A City's asserted alternative must have support as a real cure; a merely cheaper or conceivable measure is insufficient. Adequate correction means correction of the proved violation, not guaranteed perfection or retention of every benefit of an old decree. Lewis v. Casey preserves a genuine opportunity for governmental design and requires consideration of effective alternatives before choosing the justified remedy. Rufo supplies distinct significant-change and tailored-modification burdens for its equitable route, not an additional prerequisite to statutory termination. Neither failure of the City's alternative nor its silence replaces the court's required findings. Identified responsibilities, dates and reliable, contestable reports make supported orders workable without extending them beyond the proved need. A completed act does not end a measure still justified to prevent recurrence; duties end when correction removes their current statutory basis, while completed lawful effects remain. Ordinary enforcement requires a valid definite command, notice, an opportunity to respond and judicial determination under the applicable procedures. The OCC supplies information and informal assistance; enforcement remains the judge's task.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Bell v. Wolfish: applied to deliberately maintained systemic pretrial conditions and their nonpunitive justification.
- Farmer v. Brennan: distinguished at its convicted-prisoner knowledge requirement; its reasonable-response and present-knowledge rules retain their force in that setting.
- Daniels v. Williams: applied to exclude ordinary negligence from the constitutional rule.
- County of Sacramento v. Lewis: applied for contextual culpability and actual opportunity for deliberation, without extending its emergency standard.

Section 802(b)(1)'s application to all prospective relief governs future operations despite the 1982 origin. She would require future conformity with section 3626(f)'s appointment, power, review and compensation requirements, or a genuinely different lawful assistance arrangement.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in the constitutional-relief judgment on actual notice and knowing unreasonable continuation of the serious conditions. Wilson v. Reasonable but unsuccessful protection would not. They join Part IV.A's need for current tailored substantive relief, but, like O'Connor, would vacate OCC continuation for future statutory conformity. Regulating those future functions does not require pretending that a new appointment occurred in 2001.

### Nelson v. Campbell, No. 03-6821

Merits decision, 2004-05-24

#### Chronology and Posture

Nelson v. Campbell; No. 03-6821; 541 U.S. 637. Argued March 29, 2004; decided May 24, 2004. The Court reviews the Eleventh Circuit's affirmance of dismissal of Nelson's section 1983 action as an unauthorized successive habeas petition. The question is whether a condemned prisoner may challenge an allegedly unnecessary surgical venous-access procedure when success would leave a lawful means of execution by lethal injection. Nelson seeks protection against the cut-down procedure, protocol information, an appropriate access protocol, and temporary time to adjudicate those requests. Alabama invokes habeas restrictions and objects to the timing.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The successive-habeas dismissal is reversed. The case returns for prompt adjudication of the procedure-specific civil claim, applicable administrative-remedy requirements, and any properly presented request for interim protection under the governing equitable and statutory conditions. The requested stay and procedure injunction remain for that adjudication; this threshold judgment does not grant either.

#### Opinion Topology

O’CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. The pleaded severable procedure challenge may proceed under section 1983

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner may proceed under section 1983 against a particular execution-related procedure when success would leave the State a lawful means of carrying out the sentence and thus would not necessarily invalidate it. At the pleading stage, Nelson's challenge to the allegedly unnecessary cut-down, supported by an alleged feasible alternative permitting lethal injection, cannot be dismissed solely as an unauthorized successive habeas petition.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls all four holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Preiser v. Rodriguez identifies habeas as the channel for its core attack on custody. Heck v. Humphrey and Edwards v. Balisok require attention to the necessary legal consequence of the actual claim. Randell v. Johnson and Muhammad v. Close likewise preserve civil claims compatible with the operative custody determination. Nelson accepts the death judgment and execution by lethal injection. His complaint attacks a particular means of obtaining venous access and alleges a less invasive alternative that permits execution. Those allegations, including the supporting anesthesiologist's account, are accepted for the pleading inquiry rather than established as medical facts. Alabama argues that delay shortly before execution is functionally a sentence attack. But a temporary restraint needed to resolve a compatible civil injury differs from a claim whose necessary relief defeats lawful sentence implementation. Merits, exhaustion and equity can address an unjustified demand without changing its channel by association. If factual development establishes that the challenged procedure is necessary to carry out lawful execution, the court must examine the channel on that changed premise. A request to prohibit execution regardless of the cut-down likewise requires its own scope and channel analysis. The classification of every execution-method challenge lies outside this pleaded severable dispute.

##### II. Civil cognizability and execution-stay equity are separate

**Controlling proposition:** Availability of section 1983 creates no automatic stay. A court considering interim protection must assess likelihood of success, relative harms, the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and the actual reasons for delay, applying a strong equitable presumption against a stay when the claim could have been brought early enough for merits consideration without requiring one; that presumption is contextual, with the reasons for timing and irreversible harm considered.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Gomez v. United States District Court permits a supported delay objection independently of habeas restrictions. Its principle protects the State's enforcement interest while retaining consideration of serious constitutional injury and irreversible consequences. It does not make proximity to execution proof of avoidable delay. Counsel requested protocol information and medical consultation in August 2003. The access plan described on September 10 changed materially by October 3, and the action followed the next Monday before the scheduled October 9 execution. That chronology defeats an assumption that this precise challenge was available unchanged for years, while leaving actual diligence and equitable entitlement for decision on evidence. The court must address the medical risk, alternative access and timing through ordinary adjudication and tailor any interim restraint to the lawful adjudicative need.

##### III. The civil remand retains the PLRA's distinct requirements

**Controlling proposition:** A cognizable procedure-specific claim remains subject to exhaustion of actually available administrative remedies where section 1997e(a) applies and to the independent statutory screening and prospective-relief conditions. Any prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular federal-right violation, and the least intrusive necessary means; preliminary relief expires after ninety days unless the required findings are made and the order is made final within that period.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The civil channel does not erase the conditions Congress places on prison-conditions actions. The exhaustion inquiry concerns remedies actually available; the record establishes neither exhaustion nor a failure to exhaust. Section 1997e(c)(2) permits dismissal without first requiring exhaustion when the claim is facially frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim, or seeks monetary relief from an immune defendant; it does not compel that procedural shortcut. Section 3626 requires the court to give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting a state or local official to exceed lawful authority or violate state or local law additionally requires that federal law require that departure, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation, and that no other relief correct it. The court must identify the harm and scope of any preliminary order and satisfy the final-relief findings before the ninety-day period expires if continued relief is warranted. The medical merits and the findings supporting any requested injunction remain ordinary work on remand under these rules.

##### IV. Meaningful prompt adjudication and the scope of any protection

**Controlling proposition:** The district court must promptly decide a properly presented interim request in time to address threatened use of the disputed procedure where the actual schedule permits, applying the medical, procedural, equitable and statutory requirements already stated. Any warranted injunction must identify the prohibited procedure or conduct and persons bound within the supported claim and injury; any stay must last only for the period justified by its purpose, record and equitable findings, with reconsideration as those premises change.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Randell and Muhammad require actual adjudication of a compatible civil injury while preserving independent defenses and relief conditions. Gomez requires attention to the State's enforcement interest, actual delay and irreversible harm. A prompt scheduling direction to the court therefore supplies no automatic restraint on the State while the court considers the request; any temporary restraint needed to complete adjudication must have its own lawful equitable basis. The court may grant or deny interim protection on the legally sufficient record. If an injunction is warranted, its precise terms must correct the supported injury and satisfy section 3626's need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness requirements. A materially broader request retains the independent channel inquiry. The period of any stay follows its actual justified purpose and may be reconsidered, including through a supported extension, as the record changes. The separate ninety-day preliminary-relief and final-finding conditions remain effective. These directions secure meaningful adjudication without turning the availability of section 1983 into entitlement to interim relief.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: applied to the core habeas boundary by the necessary effect of the requested relief.
- Heck v. Humphrey: applied to compatible injuries and the distinct favorable-invalidation requirement.
- Edwards v. Balisok: applied to the consequence of a claim rather than its procedural label.
- Randell v. Johnson: applied at its unanimous actual-custody-comparison and compatible-claim holding.
- Muhammad v. Close: applied to a civil injury whose success leaves the operative custody determination valid.
- Gomez v. United States District Court: applied and made express through the contextual strong presumption against an avoidably necessary last-minute stay.

#### Law After Decision

A severable execution-related procedure claim may proceed under section 1983 when its success leaves lawful implementation of the sentence available. Necessary legal invalidity, temporary litigation delay, medical entitlement, exhaustion and stay equity perform separate work. The strong avoidable-delay presumption governs the equitable inquiry with its actual-timing and irreversible-harm qualifications; the PLRA's distinct conditions govern any relief. Properly presented interim requests receive meaningful prompt adjudication under those conditions; any judicial restraint must have its own lawful basis, precise scope and justified duration.

#### Source Notes

Nelson v. Campbell, No. 03-6821; 541 U.S. 637; 42 U.S.C. §§1983, 1997e(a), 1997e(c)(2); 28 U.S.C. §§2254, 2244; 18 U.S.C. §3626(a). Compromised veins, cut-down risks and feasible alternative access are allegations at the pleading stage. The plan changed between the September 10 and October 3 accounts before the October 2003 filing; those dates are material to any actual delay inquiry.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit for ordinary civil proceedings. The district court must promptly decide any properly presented interim request in time to address the threatened procedure where the actual schedule permits, then resolve the supported medical and constitutional claim and applicable administrative-remedy requirements. A scheduling direction itself imposes no State restraint. Any warranted injunction must identify its conduct, persons bound and supported scope; any stay must retain its actual equitable basis for its necessary duration, subject to the statutory expiration and final-finding requirements. The death judgment remains in place.

**End of entry: Nelson v. Campbell, Merits decision, 2004-05-24.**

### Thornton v. United States, No. 03-5165

Merits decision, 2004-05-24

#### Chronology and Posture

Thornton v. United States; No. 03-5165; 541 U.S. 615. Argued March 31, 2004; decided May 24, 2004. On review of the Fourth Circuit's affirmance of the denial of suppression and the resulting convictions, the Court considers vehicle search incident to arrest after first police contact outside the car and after the recent occupant has been handcuffed and placed in a patrol car. Thornton produced marijuana and crack cocaine during the encounter before his arrest. The ensuing passenger-compartment search revealed a handgun under the driver's seat. Thornton seeks suppression and relief from affected convictions; the United States defends the search and an alternative inventory or inevitable-discovery basis.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Souter, who would vacate and remand for the independent admissibility ground and the resulting conviction consequences.

The Fourth Circuit's judgment sustaining admission of the handgun and the convictions is affirmed.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., announced the judgment of the Court and delivered the opinion of the Court as to Part I and a plurality opinion as to Part II. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment and in Part I. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion and joined Part I only as set out below.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Seven-Justice controlling first-contact proposition | First contact outside the car is not itself a constitutional bar; Stone-Zsela's join is confined to this proposition |
| Plurality opinion, Part II | Thomas | O'Connor, Kennedy, Breyer | Four support affirmance on the categorical recent-occupant ground | The categorical rule and its application after securing the arrestee |
| Concurrence in the judgment and in Part I | Scalia | Ginsburg | Supports affirmance; supplies the controlling affirmative permission under Marks | Offense-evidence rationale at the shared bounded scope; two express joins, with the four broader categorical positions retained as distinct positions |
| Dissent, with the author's limited join in Part I | Stone-Zsela | Stevens and Souter join the dissent in full | Would vacate and remand; Stone-Zsela also joins Part I's limited first-contact proposition | Access rule, application, actual alternative-ground remand, conditional exclusion and conviction-error review, ordinary factual preponderance and direct-review transition |

#### Holdings

##### First police contact outside the vehicle

**Controlling proposition:** The fact that police first approach a recent occupant after he has left the vehicle does not itself defeat search-incident authority. The validity of the ensuing search turns on its constitutional justification rather than that contact-timing distinction alone.

**Authority:** Part I supplies seven direct joins in this limited proposition, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** A moment's difference in first contact does not determine the constitutional interests underlying the vehicle-search exception. Belton addresses a search associated with an actual lawful arrest; a genuine recent occupant does not become categorically immune from that doctrine merely by exiting before an officer speaks. For the same reason, first contact inside the car cannot by itself answer whether the separate, later search is justified after the occupant has been secured. The controlling contact proposition therefore requires an actual search justification. Knowles preserves the distinction between a citation and custodial arrest; Florida v. Thomas supplies no merits answer because its disposition concerned finality.

##### A contemporaneous search for evidence of the arrest offense

**Controlling proposition:** Following the lawful custodial arrest of a genuine recent vehicle occupant who has been secured away from the vehicle, a contemporaneous search of the passenger compartment within the vehicle-search scope is permissible when there is an objective reasonable belief that the vehicle contains evidence relevant to the offense of arrest. This affirmative permission sustains Thornton's search on the supported drug arrest and immediate vehicle connection.

**Authority:** Scalia's concurrence, joined by Ginsburg, supplies the controlling narrowest ground under Marks. O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer authorize every search within the same lawful-arrest, genuine-recent-occupancy, contemporaneous passenger-compartment category without requiring the additional offense-evidence condition. The concurrence retains all those shared predicates and adds that condition, making its affirmative permission a logical subset of the four's broader permission. For example, a secured recent occupant arrested for an offense giving no reasonable basis to expect evidence in the car satisfies the broader category but falls outside the concurrence's permission; both rules retain the same temporal, spatial and passenger-compartment boundaries, so the narrower permission contains no search excluded by the broader rule within that common domain. This is two express joins in the concurrence, not six express adoptions of a replacement test.

**Controlling explanation:** The offense-evidence ground supplies a vehicle-specific justification where the secured arrestee cannot actually reach a weapon or destroy evidence in the car. Chimel supplies the immediate-control rationale whose factual premise is absent here; Robinson's categorical search of the arrested person does not by itself explain a search of an inaccessible area. The narrower ground retains Belton's vehicle setting and lawful-arrest predicate while requiring an objective connection to evidence of that arrest offense. Thornton produced marijuana and crack cocaine before arrest and had just left his car; those facts support the required connection without using the later discovery of the gun to justify the search. The dissent would require an independently satisfied existing exception, including automobile probable cause. The controlling permission instead rests on the bounded offense-linked arrest ground; its required connection prevents mere arrest from authorizing an exploratory search. Houghton's automobile rule retains its separate probable-cause basis. Marks permits a narrower position to govern despite broader judgment supporters' rejection of its limiting condition. Nichols requires compatible predicates and a common question: here both affirming groups accept the lawful arrest and address the same bounded vehicle search, rather than disagreeing about the validity of a necessary prior judgment. Their differing justifications do not create a class of searches the narrower ground allows but the broader ground forbids.

The derived ground governs the secured recent-occupant search at those shared predicates. Independently established automobile-probable-cause, inventory and Chimel grounds retain their own factual and legal requirements.

#### Precedent Treatment

- New York v. Belton: applied to a genuine recent occupant first contacted outside the vehicle; the offense-evidence permission controls for the secured recent occupant at the shared contemporaneous passenger-compartment scope under Marks, while the four-Justice lead retains its broader categorical position.
- Chimel v. California: supplies the immediate-control rationale distinguished by the controlling offense-evidence ground when the arrestee is secured away; its application to other area searches retains its own scope.
- United States v. Robinson: retains the categorical rule for searches of the person after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest.
- Knowles v. Iowa: preserves the actual-custody prerequisite and the need for an independently justified additional search; its citation holding remains distinct.
- Florida v. Thomas: retains its finality holding, which supplied no recent-occupant merits rule.
- Marks v. United States: applied to the two-Justice narrower affirmative permission logically encompassed by the four broader positions at shared predicates and scope.
- Nichols v. United States: applied to require a common legal question and compatible predicates for the narrowest-ground derivation, distinguishing agreement resting on incompatible views of an underlying judgment.
- Wyoming v. Houghton: preserves the distinct automobile-exception permission grounded in vehicle probable cause.

#### Law After Decision

First contact after a recent occupant leaves a car is not an independent constitutional bar to a search with a valid arrest-related justification. Under the controlling ground derived through Marks, lawful arrest of a genuine recent occupant secured away from the car permits a contemporaneous passenger-compartment search when an objective reasonable belief connects the vehicle to evidence of the arrest offense. Thornton's search and convictions stand under that rule. The four broader categorical positions remain separate opinions from the concurrence's two express joins. Independent automobile-probable-cause, inventory and Chimel grounds retain their own applicable conditions.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

The four-Justice lead applies Belton's contemporaneous passenger-compartment rule to a genuine recent occupant and includes containers within that compartment capable of the search's scope. Ordinary securing during the arrest does not, for those Justices, end the categorical authority. The rule avoids making the officer choose between promptly securing a suspect and undertaking the authorized arrest search. They acknowledge that Thornton could not actually reach the car and rely on the established category, not a finding of escape danger. O'Connor recognizes the force of the contrary access argument, but, like Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, retains the category here rather than adopting a replacement evidence standard. Genuine recent occupancy and contemporaneity bound their rationale.

Scalia, joined by Ginsburg, agrees that first contact outside is not decisive but rejects the categorical justification once Thornton is secured away. Chimel's immediate-control premise cannot explain an inaccessible area, and Robinson's personal search does not authorize every nearby intrusion. Their controlling narrower ground permits a contemporaneous search of the passenger compartment after lawful arrest of a recent occupant secured away from the car when it is reasonable to believe that the vehicle contains evidence relevant to that arrest offense. The drugs produced before arrest and the immediate association with Thornton's car supply that connection; finding the gun later supplies no earlier justification. Their narrowest ground retains Belton at this vehicle- and offense-specific scope rather than creating general exploratory authority. Its controlling status follows from Marks; the four categorical Justices do not expressly join its replacement rationale. Houghton's probable-cause automobile rule concerns a distinct exception. Ginsburg's protection of an identified passenger's belongings there does not decide this search of the arrested suspect's own recent vehicle for evidence connected to his offense.

Stone-Zsela, joined in full by Stevens and Souter, would require a reasonable possibility at the time of search that the arrestee can reach the searched area to obtain a weapon or destroy evidence. Chimel supplies that purpose, and Knowles rejects a categorical additional intrusion when its justification is absent. A first-contact line would leave the same defect whenever the officer spoke a moment earlier. Thornton's handcuffing and confinement in the patrol car defeat the access ground. They reject the new reduced-suspicion evidence rationale and preserve independently established probable cause and other exceptions on their own terms. The Government must prove the necessary factual predicates of its asserted warrantless incident-search exception by a preponderance under Matlock; the court determines the legal scope, and that factual quantum does not replace an exception's substantive probable-cause or suspicion requirement. They would remand for actual lawful impoundment, standardized inventory criteria under Florida v. Wells, and inevitable lawful discovery under Nix v. Williams. A possible inventory is insufficient. If no independent lawful basis makes the gun admissible, it must be excluded, followed by applicable error review of materially affected convictions and appropriate further proceedings. This conditional direction first resolves available independent grounds and does not order automatic relief from every count. They would apply their proposed access limit to Thornton and other still-open criminal direct-review cases under Griffith v. Kentucky, preserving the separate operation of finality, preservation, harmless-error and collateral-review requirements.

The Fourth Circuit's affirmance remains operative. Admission of the handgun and the resulting convictions stand under the Court's judgment.

### Yarborough v. Alvarado, No. 02-1684

Merits decision, 2004-06-01

#### Chronology and Posture

Yarborough v. Alvarado; No. 02-1684; 541 U.S. 652. Argued March 1, 2004; decided June 1, 2004. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's decision granting habeas relief on the premise that the state court unreasonably found a seventeen-year-old noncustodial during a stationhouse interview. The relevant state merits adjudication occurred in 1999. The warden seeks reinstatement of denial on that claim; Alvarado seeks relief from admission of the unwarned statements. His parents brought him to the station at the detective's request and waited in the lobby while one detective interviewed him for about two hours. He declined two offered breaks near the end and returned home.

#### Judgment

**Reversed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The denial of habeas relief on the presented Miranda-custody ground is reinstated. The mandate requires implementation of that denial.

#### Opinion Topology

O’CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court with the part-specific joins set out below. BREYER, J., filed a dissenting opinion.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous review framework | Objective custody, substantive-law date and independent statutory review |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Five-Justice ground for reversal and denial on this claim | State noncustody application remains within the statutory range |
| Dissent | Breyer | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Would preserve the Ninth Circuit's statutory and custody conclusions | Finds custody and objectively unreasonable state application on the supported circumstances; preserves independently necessary admission and prejudice work |

#### Holdings

##### I. Objective custody and the independent habeas restriction

**Controlling proposition:** Miranda custody turns on whether the full objective circumstances would make a reasonable person in the interviewee's position understand restraint equivalent to formal arrest. In habeas review of a state merits adjudication, section 2254(d)(1) additionally requires a decision contrary to this Court's then-established governing holdings or an objectively unreasonable application of them; independent disagreement, a correct formula alone, or the number of judges taking either view does not decide that inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by all nine Justices, as identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda supplies the warning consequence of custodial interrogation. Berkemer and Stansbury require objective, communicated circumstances rather than undisclosed beliefs or personal psychology; suspicion conveyed in questioning matters through its effect on that setting, not as automatic custody. Thompson classifies the ultimate custody question as mixed law and fact under the former statute. It does not displace the separate AEDPA restriction governing this case. Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade require objective statutory review and preserve the distinction between the constitutional law established at the state merits adjudication and the current interpretation of the federal review method. A general rule can clearly establish law without a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported adjudication, rather than selecting isolated language or treating a correct quotation as sufficient. Mitchell v. Esparza and Middleton v. McNeil preserve the distinction between the statutory review inquiry, constitutional error and ultimate entitlement to relief. The substantive custody law here is measured at the 1999 state adjudication; a later constitutional refinement cannot supply an earlier command.

##### II. The state noncustody determination remains within the permitted range

**Controlling proposition:** On this complete supported record, the state court's noncustody conclusion was not an objectively unreasonable application of the established arrest-equivalent-restraint rule. Alvarado therefore fails the section 2254(d)(1) condition for relief on this Miranda-custody ground, even though youth, station isolation, duration and accusatory questioning support a substantial contrary assessment.

**Authority:** Part II supplies the five direct joins identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathiason and Beheler establish that station questioning and suspicion alone do not create custody. Alvarado arrived voluntarily with his parents, was neither formally arrested nor physically restrained or threatened, received offers of breaks, and returned home afterward. These circumstances permit the state conclusion. The Court also considers the two-hour interview in a small room, separation from parents, accusations and absence of an express assurance that he could leave. Ultimate departure or a friendly tone alone does not resolve custody, but the whole supported encounter remains within the range permitted by the established rule. The contrary view treats the combination as a clear message of official control. That is substantial, yet the arrival, actual interview conditions and departure support a reasonable noncustody assessment without making handcuffs or violence necessary to custody. Alvarado was approximately seventeen years and eight months old at the interview. Obvious youth can differ from private police experience, but the established law did not require the additional age treatment demanded here, and the other circumstances independently sustain the statutory conclusion. The absence of a youth-specific precedent alone is insufficient to decide reasonableness. This habeas holding neither categorically excludes apparent age from objective circumstances nor adopts a new generally applicable direct-review age rule; that broader question is outside the necessary inquiry.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Miranda v. Arizona: applied to the custody trigger for warnings.
- Berkemer v. McCarty and Stansbury v. California: applied to communicated objective circumstances and arrest-equivalent restraint.
- Oregon v. Mathiason and California v. Beheler: applied for the insufficiency of station location and suspicion alone.
- Thompson v. Keohane: applied to custody's legal character, with its former-statute setting kept distinct from AEDPA.
- Williams v. Taylor: applied to independent statutory clauses, objective unreasonable application and the state-adjudication-date constitutional boundary.
- Lockyer v. Andrade: applied to an enforceable general rule without a factual-twin requirement and to the separation of statutory eligibility from relief.
- Woodford v. Visciotti: applied to the full supported adjudication and record.
- Mitchell v. Esparza and Middleton v. McNeil: applied only to current statutory-review method and distinct entitlement requirements, without importing later substantive constitutional law into 1999.

#### Law After Decision

The presented Miranda-custody ground is foreclosed by section 2254(d)(1) on this record. The objective arrest-equivalence inquiry remains enforceable through established general holdings, and statutory reasonableness remains a legal inquiry into the full supported state adjudication. The decision imposes no universal exclusion of visible age from custody analysis. A generally applicable direct-review age rule remains outside this adjudication.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, agrees with the review framework but finds both statutory unreasonableness and actual custody. A young person transported by parents at police request, separated from them, and questioned alone in a small station room for two hours while confronted with contradictory evidence would reasonably understand that officials controlled whether he could end the encounter. Breaks offered only near the end can reinforce that understanding; eventual release does not resolve the restraint during interrogation. Obvious youth describes the objectively apparent position of the interviewee, while undisclosed police experience invites an unrelated psychological inquiry. Stansbury and Thompson already supply the objective rule, and Williams and Andrade permit that general rule to compel a result without identical facts. The dissent relies on the supported encounter, not the disputed account that the parents requested admission and were refused, and not a later constitutional holding. It would preserve the Ninth Circuit's statutory and custody conclusions, then require whatever admission and trial-prejudice determinations remain before a final writ. Custody alone would not supply immediate release or an unconditional new trial.
