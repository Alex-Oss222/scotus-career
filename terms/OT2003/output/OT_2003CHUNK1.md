## Decisions and Dispositions

### Demore v. Kim, No. 01-1491

Merits decision on reargument, October 14, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 2003; restored for reargument April 29, 2003; reargued October 6, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 276 F.3d 523. The Ninth Circuit affirmed a conditional habeas judgment requiring reasonably prompt individual flight-risk and danger consideration for Kim, a lawful permanent resident detained under section 1226(c). The questions concern section 1226(e)'s effect on habeas jurisdiction, the pre-final-order reach of section 1226(c), and the statutory and constitutional grounds for individual custody review during removal proceedings. Kim's criminal-category membership is disputed.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument, reargument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The lower court must obtain the proper agency consideration of each asserted mandatory-detention ground and apply the statutory threshold in Part II-B. If that threshold is met, Kim receives an individualized flight-risk and danger assessment under Part III, at which he bears clear-and-convincing persuasion that he is unlikely to flee or endanger others. His existing release on $5,000 bond remains effective pending the threshold determination and any consequent lawful bond decision. The five-Justice judgment requires this complete remand, although Part III's allocation has only four opinion joins. Stone-Zsela accepts the judgment and remedy while withholding that join; his concurrence supplies no competing remand instruction.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court with respect to Parts I, II-A and II-B, and an opinion with respect to Parts III and IV. STEVENS, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined the entire opinion. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined Parts I, II-A and II-B, and filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment. O'CONNOR and KENNEDY, JJ., joined Parts I and II-A. KENNEDY, J., filed an additional opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part. SCALIA, J., filed an additional opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

The four dissenters oppose the statutory pathway and seek reversal of the constitutional hearing requirement with lawful implementation and proper consideration of preserved category objections. Their agreement on pre-order reach does not join Parts II-B, III or IV. Scalia and Thomas also oppose the jurisdiction holding. The separate writings' grounds and joins are stated below.

#### Holdings

##### I. Independent habeas reaches the legality of the mandatory framework

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1226(e) does not withdraw independently proper jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. section 2241 to determine the statutory and constitutional legality of Congress's mandatory detention framework and afford effective relief when its application lacks lawful authority. The retained channel requires the ordinary custody, respondent, forum and exhaustion predicates and does not authorize reweighing lawful discretionary bond decisions or initial judicial retrial of removability facts.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Goncalves and Kolster distinguish substantial legal objections to executive authority from review of discretionary equities. Calcano-Martinez requires the retained writ to decide the legal objection and provide effective relief; St. Cyr applies that channel to a legal eligibility rule. Zadvydas applies it to present and prospective detention authority. Section 1226(e) must independently be read in that light: its first sentence protects discretionary judgments, and its second addresses executive detention, release, bond and parole actions. Together they do not clearly foreclose adjudication of the legislative framework's legal authority.

The action-based objection is substantial: a hearing order can affect detention even when styled a challenge to the statute. But deciding what the statute lawfully authorizes differs from substituting judicial custody discretion for the executive's. Both sentences retain work under that distinction; the Court requires no particular repeal formula. Kim's existing release leaves the dispute live because the requested reversal could expose him to renewed mandatory custody. Ventura preserves the agency's initial factfinding role. Whether Congress could constitutionally eliminate every effective channel remains outside the necessary statutory resolution.

##### II-A. Mandatory coverage can precede a final removal order

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1226(c) can govern custody during pending removal proceedings before a final removal order. Its application requires the actual statutory category to be addressed through the proper classification process; acceptance of lawful initial custody does not itself concede each asserted ground of continuing mandatory coverage.

**Authority:** Part II-A, joined by all nine on the statutory merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The command to take custody upon release from criminal confinement would not fit a rule postponing all section 1226(c) authority until a final removal order. Section 1231 separately governs the post-order stage. Kim's final-order-only interpretation therefore fails. National Center preserves actual determinations of status beneath a general immigration custody rule; the charging allegation and the legal category remain distinct. Kim disputes the original theft ground and the added grounds. Proper classification review must address those issues, with initial agency determinations left to the agency under Ventura. Agreement on this pre-order construction does not decide the separate threshold-to-bond question in Part II-B.

##### II-B. A substantial category question opens individualized custody consideration

**Controlling proposition:** A lawful permanent resident challenging mandatory membership under section 1226(c) is entitled to an individualized flight-risk and danger assessment when the contention raises a question of law or fact that is not insubstantial and is not interposed solely for purposes of delay. The inquiry must address every independently sufficient asserted mandatory ground; a challenge to one ground cannot displace another established ground capable of sustaining mandatory custody.

**Authority:** Part II-B, directly joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. This five-Justice statutory entitlement is distinct from Part III's four-Justice proof allocation.

**Controlling explanation:** Zadvydas supplies a purpose-sensitive construction of detention authority in light of serious liberty concerns. National Center requires attention to the predicate of an immigration rule's application. Here the question is whether mandatory classification can conclusively determine custody while a substantial dispute over membership remains. The construction permits genuine risk consideration during that dispute rather than treating the charge as its own answer.

The Government's strongest objection is that Congress specified a witness-protection exception and otherwise commanded custody. The Court construes the threshold at which unresolved membership may support mandatory custody; it does not create a general equitable exception for every person conclusively within the class. Joseph's requirement that the Government be substantially unlikely to prevail cannot be the exclusive route to risk consideration when the lower threshold announced here is satisfied. The distinct witness-protection provision retains its terms.

A request for time to obtain evidence, the time needed to litigate, or a mixed motive does not alone make the challenge solely dilatory. A request for discretionary relief after covered removability is accepted is different from disputing mandatory membership. Corona-Sanchez requires examination of the original theft theory, while the added burglary and two-crimes theories require their own inquiry. Ventura places those initial determinations on remand. The statutory resolution leaves the broader constitutional hearing entitlement without a decision; it neither establishes categorical constitutional validity outside this threshold nor imports Zadvydas's post-order six-month mechanism.

##### III–IV. The risk allocation and the enforceable remand have different authority

**Judgment and proposition-level authority:** The five-Justice judgment vacates and remands under the complete statutory pathway, preserves Kim's present release through its determination, and directs the risk assessment described below if the threshold is met. Four Justices—Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg—join Parts III and IV as opinion reasoning. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment, accepting its remedy without joining the detainee allocation.

**Risk instruction applied on remand:** At the individualized assessment, Kim must show by clear and convincing evidence that he is unlikely to flee or endanger others. The decisionmaker must genuinely consider the individual evidence and possess effective authority to release on lawful bond conditions when that showing is made. The statutory-membership threshold and the later risk showing are separate inquiries. If Kim fails the substantial-question condition with respect to an independently sufficient mandatory ground, the categorical statute governs subject to any independently established constitutional defect; actual exclusion from the mandatory category returns custody to the ordinary section 1226(a) framework.

**Plurality explanation and limit:** Part III uses section 3143(b)'s release-pending-appeal structure by analogy to implement this confined statutory pathway. It does not directly apply that criminal statute or prescribe a universal detainee burden. The plurality answers the Government-burden objection by distinguishing the opportunity created during unresolved but substantial membership litigation from a constitutional determination that categorical detention authority has ended. Stone-Zsela accepts the practical remedy but disagrees with that allocation of uncertainty. Those opposite allocations are not logical subsets; no Marks rationale supplies a fifth opinion join. The risk instruction binds this remand through the judgment; its general precedential force remains that of a four-Justice view. The statutory ground resolves the judgment. The broader constitutional hearing and continued-detention formulations lack a majority at the necessary level of generality and therefore remain open; the application of a distinct constitutional duration defect also requires findings about the specific course and justification of Kim's confinement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Goncalves and Kolster:** Applied to preserve independent review of substantial legal-authority objections while excluding mere discretionary reweighing; their channel-specific limits remain operative.
- **Calcano-Martinez and St. Cyr:** Applied to require a capable legal-review channel and effective relief; the retained writ's ordinary predicates continue to govern.
- **Zadvydas:** Its legal-authority jurisdiction holding is applied to section 1226(c), and its purpose-sensitive construction informs the substantial-question threshold. Its distinct post-order duration and proof mechanism remains confined to its own statutory setting.
- **National Center for Immigrants' Rights:** Applied to actual category determinations beneath a general custody rule; a charge does not itself settle a genuinely contested predicate.
- **Ventura:** Applied to preserve initial agency determination of unresolved classification and factual matters while the courts supply and enforce the legal standard.
- **Matter of Joseph:** Limited insofar as its substantially-unlikely-to-succeed threshold would be the sole route to bond consideration despite the substantial-question condition now required by Part II-B. Its category-review function remains available.
- **Corona-Sanchez:** Considered as relevant lower-court authority requiring examination of the original theft classification; the added grounds receive their own analysis under the remand.

#### Law After Decision

Effective October 14, 2003, section 1226(e) preserves independently proper habeas review of the legality of the mandatory framework, with effective relief and the ordinary limits on discretionary review and initial agency factfinding. Section 1226(c) may operate before a final removal order, subject to actual consideration of disputed statutory membership. Lawful permanent residents with a category question that is not insubstantial and not interposed solely for delay have a statutory entitlement to individual flight-risk and danger consideration, with each independently sufficient mandatory ground addressed.

These jurisdiction and statutory-threshold propositions are direct majority holdings. Kim's remand applies the detainee clear-and-convincing risk showing and preserves his current release through the threshold determination and any consequent bond decision under a five-vote judgment. The burden rationale has four joins and supplies no general majority proof rule. The independent constitutional hearing and continued-detention formulations remain without a majority at the necessary level of generality; a distinct duration claim also requires findings about the course of detention. Classification and individual-risk findings on remand are applications of the stated instructions, not unresolved statutory standards.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** He joins Parts I, II-A and II-B and accepts the entire vacatur, remand and interim-release remedy so Kim can receive the individual assessment available under the statutory pathway. He withholds assent from the allocation of persuasion to Kim. In his view, when genuinely contested inclusion or constitutionally insufficient continuing authority requires individual review, the Government should prove by clear and convincing evidence that reasonably available conditions, separately or together, cannot adequately address flight risk or danger; he would also accept governmental preponderance with the same object of proof and effective relief. Mathews directs attention to liberty and error; Salerno and Addington offer regime-specific support for the preferred quantum; Cooper illustrates the consequences of a burden that wrongly allocates uncertainty. Krimstock supports meaningful review and consideration of alternatives, while his separate preponderance position there remains his own. Kim seeks liberty in a civil immigration proceeding, not release pending appeal of his criminal convictions, so section 3143(b) does not persuade him to place the risk of uncertainty on Kim. The neutral decisionmaker should receive contrary evidence, assess residence, family and community ties, convictions, release conduct, appearance evidence and feasible conditions, and give reviewable reasons; failure of the governmental showing should permit conditional release.

He also explains that lawful initial detention does not justify continuation after its nature, duration or circumstances lose a reasonable relationship to removal proceedings pursued with reasonable diligence. A resident would identify a concrete continuing-custody concern; the Government would supply the chronology, basis, delay explanations and anticipated course and prove disputed diligence facts by preponderance. Legitimate substantial defenses are not automatically misconduct; solely dilatory conduct may count against the resident, and unjustified governmental or institutional delay weighs against categorical continuation. If that authority fails, individualized justification and effective relief are required; material change permits renewed review. Gilbert, Fairley, Foucha and Jackson support the continuing-authority inquiry; Flores preserves lawful temporary custody, and Zadvydas contributes the removal-purpose relationship without its separate calendar or burden. The present statutory holding leaves this constitutional limit open. His proposals are noncontrolling, supply no finding that Kim's past detention crossed the limit, and neither condition the entered judgment nor alter its remand instructions.

**Justice Souter, concurring, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** They join the entire lead opinion because its substantial-question pathway affords genuine individual consideration and protects Kim's existing release during the inquiry. They retain the broader view that a lawful permanent resident must receive a reasonably prompt, impartial opportunity to contest the necessity of custody on flight-risk and danger grounds. A past criminal category cannot conclusively establish present need throughout the proceeding. Carlson retained individual discretion, Flores retained real release and review avenues, and Zadvydas ties detention to its purpose; National Center and the continuing-deprivation decisions support effective inquiry into the actual justification. A rebuttable initial category presumption may be permissible, but meaningful consideration and effective release authority are essential. Their statutory join accepts Part III's allocation within that pathway, without making it a universal burden or approving categorical constitutional validity outside the threshold. That broader constitutional position is noncontrolling.

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** They accept pre-order operation of section 1226(c), but would reverse the resident-wide constitutional hearing requirement and remand for implementation in the actual custody and removal posture. Congress may make a properly established criminal category sufficient during reasonably dispatched removal proceedings. Flores supports temporary categorical arrangements; the statutory classification, rather than risk alone, supplies the substantive predicate. Gilbert, Krimstock and Fairley require testing the actual legal justification, not adding a new risk exception to a valid category. Joseph and the proper review channels must address genuine membership disputes. A substantial challenge does not itself suspend Congress's custody rule, and the express witness-protection exception does not authorize the Court's implied bond pathway. The disputed charges require their own lawful treatment; the requested continuance and elapsed six months do not establish the exceptional constitutional defect asserted here. O'Connor and Kennedy join the Court's jurisdiction holding; Scalia and Thomas reach these merits on the Court's jurisdiction determination. This proposed reversal is noncontrolling.

**Justice Kennedy, additionally concurring in part and dissenting in part.** His disagreement with the statutory pathway preserves individual protection against unreasonable, unjustified or arbitrary continuing detention and denial of prescribed review. The Government must have actual support for mandatory category membership; unreasonable delay or failure of that justification can require individual flight-risk and danger consideration. His Flores and Zadvydas positions and Krimstock's inquiry into continuing authority support that distinction. The present record, including Kim's requested continuance and incomplete findings, does not establish that constitutional defect from elapsed time alone. This qualification of his reversal position is noncontrolling.

**Justice Scalia, additionally concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They read section 1226(e)'s second sentence as a bar on setting aside an executive detention or release action, including through this challenge to the mandatory framework. A legal or constitutional label cannot evade the action-based language. Their application of retained channels under other statutes does not settle this text. They decline Part I and proceed to the statutory merits on the Court's contrary jurisdiction determination, without announcing a comprehensive Suspension Clause rule. Their jurisdictional objection is noncontrolling.

### United States v. Holston, Second Circuit No. 02-1292

Merits decision, October 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission for review of the Second Circuit's judgment affirming Holston's conviction following a conditional guilty plea. The plea preserved whether prior interstate movement of recording materials alone constitutionally supports this local production prosecution under 18 U.S.C. §2251(a), and whether including that production is otherwise necessary to the valid interstate image-market prohibition. Holston seeks invalidation of the application and relief from his conviction; the United States seeks affirmance. The record concerns recordings sexually exploiting actual minors, produced locally with equipment or media that had crossed state lines.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0, against the preserved Commerce Clause challenge.** Holston's requested dismissal of §2251(a) as applied and relief from his conviction are denied on the supported necessary-inclusion ground.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The conditional plea and conviction remain effective. The case returns for ordinary implementation of the affirmed judgment, leaving any independently live sentencing proceedings to their existing course.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. The statutory materials condition and the constitutional connection perform different work

**Controlling proposition:** The prior interstate movement of ordinary recording equipment or media does not, standing alone, supply constitutional authority for prohibiting the later wholly intrastate production of child pornography. That movement can satisfy the materials-in-commerce element of 18 U.S.C. §2251(a), which remains independently required in this prosecution; constitutional authority must rest on a sufficient relationship between the regulated production class and interstate commerce.

**Authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion of the Court. The common coalition identified in Opinion Topology directly joins both holdings and their explanations at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Lopez requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can support federal authority. United States v. Griffin and United States v. Langley distinguish an enacted historical-movement condition from the constitutional basis for regulating the ensuing local class. The fact that a recording tool once crossed a state line proves its provenance. It does not itself explain how the later local production operates within interstate commerce or why its regulation is necessary to an interstate restriction. Treating that fact as sufficient would connect ordinary local conduct to federal power through the history of commonplace tools.

The express statutory connection therefore answers one inquiry but cannot complete the other. Holston's established materials movement satisfies the statutory condition used in his prosecution. The independently sufficient constitutional ground is the supported relationship between production and the prohibited image market explained below. That ground preserves the enacted element rather than deleting it or adding a defendant-specific sale, transmission or substantial-interstate-effect element.

##### II. Local production of actual-child-abuse images may be included in the supported interstate prohibition

**Controlling proposition:** The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause permits Congress to prohibit local production of images exploiting actual children when including that production is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid prohibition of the interstate market in those images. The Government must identify and materially support that mechanism on a legislative or adjudicative basis; the court independently determines its constitutional eligibility and reasonable fit to the regulated class, while Congress receives substantial latitude on supported empirical predictions and practical regulatory design.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion and common coalition. The rule and its application are directly majority-supported, without a fractured-decision inference.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Hale permits local conduct to be included when its exclusion would undermine an identified interstate restriction. United States v. Kirk applies that rule to local creation and retention of the very stock the regime prohibits, without requiring an individual sale. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt requires a materially adequate basis for the asserted mechanism and a reasonable fit between it and the enacted class.

The required relationship exists here. Local production creates the actual-abuse images whose interstate circulation the federal regime prohibits. Those images can be reproduced, concealed and substituted for other images serving the same demand. Excluding their production would leave the source of replacement and additional supply outside the restriction and provide a material avenue around the interstate prohibition. The actual recordings, actual children and identity between the produced material and the prohibited supply support that conclusion. It is an inference from the product and the regime's operation, rather than a finding that Holston distributed his recordings.

Holston's objection that his production was local and noneconomic does not defeat this necessary-inclusion ground. The class's demonstrated source role, rather than a particular producer's completed sale, explains the connection. Nor does the Court rest on marketability alone or the statute's comprehensiveness: Home Builders requires the actual mechanism to be supported. Here the characteristics of the produced material supply the relationship missing from the inadequate inquiry in that case. Material support requires neither universal formal legislative findings nor a fixed numerical benchmark or clear-and-convincing proof. The obligation remains the Government's; legislative latitude operates after the eligible mechanism is identified.

**Application and remedy:** The production of Holston's actual-child-abuse recordings falls within that supported class. The constitutional challenge to §2251(a) as applied fails, and the judgment sustaining his conviction is affirmed.

**Scope and questions outside the grant:** The holding addresses production of the actual-child-abuse material covered by the identified interstate prohibition. Distinct possession offenses and virtual imagery are outside the question presented; their treatment is not decided. Application of the supported-mechanism requirement to new production records is ordinary application of settled law, not a reservation of the rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied and reaffirmed. An eligible interstate connection must precede aggregation; equipment provenance cannot replace the required connection. Its constitutional boundary remains effective when an offense contains jurisdictional language.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Applied. Its reasonably necessary inclusion rule reaches a local class only through a supported relationship to a valid interstate regime; the actual-abuse-image source relationship supplies that relationship here.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Applied for the absence of a universal individual-sale prerequisite when local creation and retention concern the prohibited stock. Its requirement of a fitted regime relationship retains full force.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998:** Applied to the Government's material-support obligation, independent judicial determination of constitutional eligibility, reasonable fit and legislative latitude. Its remand for an inadequately examined mechanism is distinguished by the supported product-and-source relationship in this record.
- **Hiley v. Barrett, October 9, 1998:** Applied for the method of identifying the practical relationship between the regulated class and a valid supply restriction. Its recipient-possession application remains distinct from the production class decided here.
- **United States v. Franklyn and United States v. Gonzalez, October 22, 1998:** Applied for the distinction between an enacted nexus and a supported supply-regime justification. Their supply-and-recipient mechanism retains its own statutory scope.
- **United States v. Griffin and United States v. Langley, October 29, 1998:** Applied to preserve the separate work of the enacted historical-movement element and the constitutional mechanism. The existence or satisfaction of one does not supply or eliminate the other.

#### Law After Decision

Effective October 30, 2003, the supported necessary-inclusion doctrine sustains the defined local production class when actual-child-abuse images constitute the reproducible, concealable and substitutable supply of the identified interstate prohibition. Lower courts must identify the regime and require material support for the production class's anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship, independently examine constitutional eligibility and fit, and give Congress latitude on supported empirical judgments. Proof of an individual interstate sale is unnecessary to that established class-and-regime ground. The statutory materials-movement element continues to govern the prosecution independently.

This decision adds a controlling application to the existing rule. Lopez's connection requirement, Home Builders's support obligation and Griffin/Langley's separation of statutory nexus from constitutional authority retain their force. The equipment-only theory is insufficient; the supported production mechanism sustains the challenged application.

#### Source Notes

United States v. Holston, 343 F.3d 83, Second Circuit No. 02-1292; U.S. Const., Art. I, §8, cls. 3 and 18; 18 U.S.C. §2251(a).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The mandate issues in the ordinary course. The lower court implements the affirmed judgment; the conditional plea and conviction remain effective, and any independently live sentencing proceedings retain their existing scope. The Supreme Court's merits review of the reserved constitutional challenge is concluded.

**End of entry: United States v. Holston, Merits decision, October 30, 2003.**

### Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton, D.C. Circuit No. 01-5373

Merits decision, February 5, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission for review of the D.C. Circuit's April 1, 2003 judgment, rehearing denied July 22, 2003. The question is whether the Commerce Clause authorizes application of the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition to a commercial housing development threatening a species found entirely within one State. The proposed 202-acre San Diego County development threatens the arroyo toad. The circuit sustained the application by treating the commercial development as the regulated activity. Rancho Viejo seeks reversal and relief against the application; the federal officials seek affirmance.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.** The vacatur acts on the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application of the taking prohibition, not only its explanation.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Souter would affirm on the economic-application ground stated in their dissent.

The case returns through the D.C. Circuit for examination of the relevant existing legislative and adjudicative record under the two Home Builders mechanisms. A materially supported eligible mechanism with the required fit permits sustainment on that identified ground; failure to support such a connection requires relief confined to the challenged application. Rancho Viejo's request for immediate final relief against that application is not granted at this stage. The statutory prohibition remains in force; interim protection requires the ordinary equitable showing.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, J., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. A commercial project does not replace the statutory taking class

**Controlling proposition:** The commercial character of a housing project does not by itself establish Congress's authority to apply the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition. The Government must identify and materially support an eligible interstate mechanism fitted to the taking class Congress enacted, including the habitat-caused injury that class reaches.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court. The seven-Justice coalition identified above directly joins all three holdings, their explanations and the remand.

**Controlling explanation:** National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt governs this question. Section 9 prohibits taking protected wildlife without making a project's commercial character the defining condition of the prohibition. United States v. Lopez requires a constitutionally sufficient connection for that enacted class before aggregation can measure its effects.

The D.C. Circuit relied on Rancho Viejo's commercial housing development as the regulated activity. The development is commercial, and the contrary view correctly identifies habitat conversion itself, rather than an incidental purchase, as the injury-producing conduct. But Home Builders already considered and rejected that feature as an independently sufficient connection for the enacted taking class. Describing the challenge as applied to this project does not remove the need to identify the mechanism that connects the prohibition to interstate commerce. The Court applies that existing rule.

Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon supplies the statutory treatment of habitat-caused injury. It does not supply the separate constitutional connection. Wickard v. Filburn and Perez v. United States permit class-wide assessment within properly connected economic classes; under Home Builders they do not make this project's commercial character a substitute for the required connection.

##### II. The existing record must be examined under the two supported interstate mechanisms

**Controlling proposition:** A challenged intrastate-taking application may be sustained when its inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of an identified valid interstate regulatory regime, or when the regulated class generates a materially supported substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize through ordinary territorial authority and the prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. The Government must identify and support the invoked mechanism with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis; courts independently determine constitutional eligibility and reasonable fit, while Congress receives substantial latitude on supported empirical predictions, magnitude and practical regulatory design after the eligible connection is identified.

**Authority:** The common seven-Justice Court opinion directly applies both Home Builders routes and their complete governing limits. Each route remains available on remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The necessary-inclusion route requires an actual relationship between the local taking class and the operation of the identified interstate regime. United States v. Hale and United States v. Kirk supply that method. United States v. Holston applies it where local production creates the reproducible, concealable and substitutable supply of the prohibited interstate image market. That demonstrated relationship does not establish a wildlife-market relationship here. The relevant evasion or impairment must be supported for the regime actually invoked.

The externality route requires a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable effect, substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; a supported explanation why affected States cannot reasonably contain or internalize that effect through ordinary territorial authority; and reasonable fit between the federal prohibition and that spillover. An effect's occurrence in many States or the desirability of uniform national policy does not establish those conditions.

Home Builders requires material support for either route. The conservation law's comprehensiveness, biodiversity's general importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism and development pressure are insufficient by themselves. Aggregation measures magnitude only after a constitutionally eligible connection exists. Judicial review must retain an enforceable distinction between the commerce power and a general police power, without turning that inquiry into a free-form balance of federal and state interests.

The material-support obligation uses Home Builders's legislative-or-adjudicative inquiry, with its predictive latitude; it imposes no universal formal or defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, fixed numerical threshold or clear-and-convincing burden. The court must examine the actual support and fit rather than invent an absent market, spillover or causal pathway. The toad's intrastate range does not categorically defeat either supported route.

##### III. Vacatur and an existing-record remand are required

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment sustaining a challenged taking rests on the insufficient commercial-project ground and has not adequately determined whether the existing legislative and adjudicative record supports either eligible mechanism, the judgment must be vacated and the case remanded for that inquiry. If the existing record materially supports a permitted mechanism and the required fit, the lower court may sustain the application on that identified ground; if it cannot, the court must grant relief confined to the unsupported application actually challenged.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion controls the disposition and both remand conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** Home Builders requires a decision under the actual class-and-mechanism rule. The commercial ground below leaves that inquiry unperformed. An alternative mechanism sufficiently established in the record could support affirmance, but the present determination supplies no such ground. Conversely, the inadequacy of the lower court's inquiry does not establish that the complete relevant record lacks every eligible connection. Rancho Viejo therefore does not obtain immediate final relief against the application merely by showing that the lower court used the wrong ground.

The lower courts must identify the mechanism asserted, examine its existing legislative or adjudicative support, determine constitutional eligibility and fit, and enter an enforceable judgment under the two conditions stated above. They may not repeat the commercial-project rationale or substitute national importance for the required connection. This is factual and adjudicative work under settled law, rather than an unresolved legal standard or permission to manufacture missing facts.

**Application and remedy:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application is vacated, and the case is remanded through that court for the specified existing-record inquiry. The statutory prohibition remains in force. Any interim protection requires the ordinary equitable showing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998:** Reaffirmed and applied. Its enacted taking class, rejection of project commerce as independently sufficient, two supported mechanisms, Government support responsibility, independent judicial inquiry, legislative latitude and existing-record remedy govern this application.
- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied. The constitutional connection precedes magnitude, must fit the enacted class and must leave an enforceable boundary; neither project commerce nor insertion of an unexpressed commerce element supplies the required inquiry.
- **United States v. Holston, October 30, 2003:** Applied for the supported necessary-inclusion method and distinguished on its actual image-supply relationship. Its demonstrated mechanism supplies no presumed wildlife market or ecological spillover.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Preserved and applied for necessary inclusion within an identified valid interstate regime; the actual evasion or impairment relationship remains essential.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for a fitted prohibited-stock relationship without a universal individual-sale requirement. Its specific stock relationship must be supported before an analogous necessary-inclusion ground can sustain a different class.
- **Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111:** Preserved for aggregation within a properly connected economic class; it does not displace Home Builders's enacted-class rule.
- **Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146:** Preserved for class-wide assessment of connected economic conduct without a separately substantial effect from each act; it does not create a missing connection through this project's commerce.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Preserved as the separate statutory rule. Significant habitat modification must actually kill or injure protected wildlife through significant impairment of essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering, with proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability. Speculative harm and bare but-for linkage are insufficient; an omission requires an independent legal duty, and otherwise lawful development alone is no exemption. Those statutory conditions remain distinct from constitutional authority and from any section 10 permission.

#### Law After Decision

Effective February 5, 2004, the Home Builders class-and-mechanism rule continues to govern equivalent intrastate-taking applications involving commercial projects. Courts must identify and materially evaluate either supported necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime or a substantial interstate externality satisfying the cross-border, territorial-control and fit requirements. The Government's support responsibility, independent judicial inquiry and bounded legislative latitude remain unchanged.

The commercial-project rationale cannot alone sustain this application. The lower courts must resolve its constitutional validity through the existing-record inquiry and enter relief according to the supported mechanism or its absence. This decision applies the existing doctrine without creating a new Commerce category; the unresolved work concerns the record's support and the resulting application-specific judgment.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter.** They would affirm because the activity directly producing the alleged habitat injury is commercial housing construction and land conversion. Sweet Home identifies that physical habitat alteration as the conduct reached by the taking prohibition. The dissent therefore looks to the actual economic application, rather than to the developer's collateral equipment purchases or a generalized claim that species preservation benefits the national economy.

Wickard and Perez permit class-wide regulation of connected economic activity, and Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association permits regulation of economic land use for environmental ends. On that reasoning, the actual construction supplies a sufficient economic connection without a market in the toad or a newly found ecological spillover. The dissent accepts the continuing statutory requirements of actual habitat-caused injury and ordinary causation; its proposed affirmance concerns the Commerce Clause challenge.

Home Builders's contrary enacted-class holding directly obstructs this ground. Stevens and Souter would reconsider it to the extent it excludes reliance on the actual injury-producing economic activity in this application. They regard treating the prohibition's broader coverage as a reason to disregard this commercial application as a mistaken class inquiry. They neither claim that their earlier dissent already controls nor distinguish Home Builders through a new factual premise. Their ground would leave this judgment effective and make the mechanism remand unnecessary; it would not make every noneconomic taking an economic activity.

The threatened project's commercial character does not itself establish a cross-border ecological effect or a mechanism of market evasion.

The mandate returns the case through the D.C. Circuit for the lower courts to determine the invoked mechanism, its support in the existing legislative and adjudicative record, its constitutional eligibility and its fit, and to enter an enforceable judgment under the stated alternatives. The statutory taking prohibition remains operative. Any interim protection requires the ordinary equitable showing. The Supreme Court's present review concludes with its mandate; the constitutional inquiry remains pending below.

### Lomont v. O’Neill

Merits decision, March 11, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission for review of the April 2, 2002 circuit judgment recognizing the officers' own official-injury challenge and rejecting their constitutional attack on voluntary certification. Two local chief law-enforcement officers and private firearm applicants challenge the historical National Firearms Act certification arrangement under 27 C.F.R. §§179.63 and 179.85. Applicants obtain local certifications concerning identity materials and the absence of information indicating unlawful possession or purpose. Federal law leaves the officer free to refuse without penalty; the officers describe constituent pressure to participate.

The questions are whether separate state authorization is necessary for the officers' own concrete official-injury challenge and whether the optional arrangement constitutes commandeering. The challengers seek invalidation of the rules; Treasury and ATF seek affirmance.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0, on the granted official-injury and Tenth Amendment questions.** The officers may present their own concrete official-injury challenge without separate state authorization, but their challenge to the voluntary certification arrangement fails. The requested invalidation of that arrangement is denied.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The certification rules remain effective against this constitutional attack, and the officers retain the unconditional freedom to decline on which the judgment rests.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. A concrete burden on the plaintiff officer's own work does not require authorization to represent the State

**Controlling proposition:** A local officer challenging a concrete federal burden on the officer's own official work need not obtain separate state authorization merely to present that claim. The officer must establish injury, traceability and redressability; a concrete official controversy does not disappear merely because the Court ultimately concludes that the challenged arrangement imposes no unlawful command.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the common nine-Justice coalition in Opinion Topology. That coalition directly supports both holdings and their explanations.

**Controlling explanation:** Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States recognized the plaintiff officers' own redressable injury from work directed at their offices. Raines v. Byrd rejected an abstract claim of institutional dilution after fully effective legislative votes. Those decisions distinguish a concrete official injury from a claim to represent a State or institution without authority.

The officers here identify certification work and demands directed at their offices through the challenged federal arrangement. Relief removing that arrangement would remove the disputed official role. That is their own asserted burden, connected to the challenged rules and the requested relief. It is not an injury borrowed from the applicants' difficulty securing a favorable signature. Official title and a nonfrivolous legal label alone would not supply Article III injury; the concrete office-specific controversy supplies it here.

The contrary argument combines two different questions. Whether the officers may speak for the State does not decide whether they may contest an asserted burden on their own offices. Whether federal law actually compels the certification is then the merits question. Resolving that question against the officers does not retroactively erase their concrete stake. Because the officer plaintiffs support the same requested relief, independent private-applicant standing is unnecessary to the disposition and is not decided. Their standing supplies no authority to litigate independent obligations of absent applicants.

##### II. Certification remains voluntary when refusal is unconditional

**Controlling proposition:** A federal requirement that an applicant obtain local certification does not commandeer the certifying officer when the officer may unconditionally refuse without a federal penalty, compulsory investigation or substitute work obligation. The challenger must identify an actual federal command or adverse consequence; constituent pressure alone does not convert an applicant's obligation into the officer's legal duty.

**Authority:** The same opinion and common nine-Justice coalition directly support this rule, its application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** New York v. United States distinguishes a genuine choice, despite consequences for private parties, from alternatives that both require an unwilling State to assume federal responsibilities. A federal program's reliance on local cooperation therefore requires examination of the officer's actual legal choice, rather than acceptance of an optional label alone.

The historical certification arrangement preserves that choice. Federal law imposes no duty to sign and no adverse federal consequence or mandatory substitute task when an officer declines. Applicants may press their requests, and refusal may be politically unpopular. Those pressures leave the officer free to refuse; they do not transfer the applicant's federal obligation to the local office. The officers' functional-conscription argument accordingly fails on this arrangement's actual operation.

Printz and Mack's limited permission for temporary statutory records assistance is a distinct rule. It requires an affirmative statutory source, an appropriate records function implementing otherwise valid private-transaction regulation, and federal retention of substantive policy and coercive enforcement. The optional certification upheld here supplies no authority to compel a new investigation or broader program administration. Nor can voluntariness supply an agency delegation that is otherwise absent. Reno v. Condon's direct regulation of a State's own activity is likewise a separate ground; the applicants' regulated activity does not become the officers' activity because they are asked to certify.

Printz's fiscal remand adopted no majority reimbursement rationale. Courts continue to apply the existing bounded assistance rule and any applicable enacted funding provision to a properly presented compulsory program.

**Application and remedy:** The challenged certifications under 27 C.F.R. §§179.63 and 179.85 remain voluntary because the officers retain unconditional refusal without federal sanction. The judgment rejecting the commandeering claim is affirmed, and the requested invalidation of this arrangement is denied.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States, June 27, 1997:** Applied to a plaintiff officer's own concrete official injury without a categorical state-authorization barrier. The existing temporary statutory records-assistance permission retains its confined source, function and allocation-of-responsibility requirements. The fractured fiscal remand supplies no controlling reimbursement rule.
- **Raines v. Byrd, June 26, 1997:** Distinguished. Its abstract institutional-dilution claim differs from a concrete burden directed at the plaintiff's office. Its Article III injury requirement and unauthorized-representation boundary retain their force.
- **New York v. United States, June 19, 1992:** Applied to distinguish genuine choice despite consequences for private participants from compulsory state responsibility. Its prohibition on two compulsory alternatives and its separate lawful-incentive holdings remain effective.
- **Reno v. Condon, January 12, 2000:** Distinguished as direct regulation of a State's own handling of information. Its separate state-activity ground is preserved; it does not convert certification for private applicants into the officer's own regulated activity.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 11, 2004, officers presenting a concrete, traceable and redressable challenge to federal burdens on their own work may litigate without separate authorization to represent the State. The official-injury inquiry remains distinct from whether the challenged measure imposes an unconstitutional command.

Federal certification arrangements of this kind remain voluntary when officers can refuse unconditionally without federal penalty, compelled investigation or substitute work. Courts examine legal consequences and actual enforcement, with the challenger required to identify an actual command or adverse federal consequence; constituent pressure alone does not supply one. This decision adds a controlling application of official-injury and genuine-choice principles. The existing bounded compulsory-assistance rule, its unresolved fiscal rationale and Condon's direct state-activity rule retain their distinct scope.

#### Source Notes

Lomont v. O’Neill, 285 F.3d 9 (D.C. Cir., April 2, 2002); U.S. Const., Art. III and Amend. X; 27 C.F.R. §§179.63 and 179.85, governing the historical certification arrangement after the 1985 change.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The mandate issues in the ordinary course, leaving the challenged certification arrangement effective against the granted constitutional attack. Officers remain free to decline certification without federal sanction. The Supreme Court's review of the granted questions is concluded.

**End of entry: Lomont v. O’Neill, Merits decision, March 11, 2004.**

### Environmental Defense Center, Inc. v. United States Environmental Protection Agency

Merits decision, March 25, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission for review of the Ninth Circuit's September 15, 2003 judgment, which replaced the vacated January 14 opinion at 319 F.3d 398. The granted questions concern whether the Phase II stormwater permit structure commandeers municipal operators and whether its public-information duties unconstitutionally compel speech. Municipal operators may pursue listed minimum-control measures or seek a different individual program under 40 C.F.R. §122.26(d). Some measures concern third parties; the information duties address stormwater effects, pollution reduction and disposal hazards. The municipalities seek constitutional invalidation, and EPA seeks affirmance. The circuit's separate administrative remands remain distinct from the granted questions.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed on both granted constitutional components.**

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Municipal federalism challenge to the actual permit structure | Rejection affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Requested constitutional invalidation denied; access to the lawful alternative to compulsory third-party management remains. |
| Facial speech challenge to the operational information duties | Rejection affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Requested constitutional invalidation denied; the independent administrative remands remain in force. |

The Ninth Circuit's separate general-permitting, notice-of-intent and public-review, and forest-road remands remain in force at their existing scope. The lower court and EPA must continue that administrative work under the existing mandate. Municipal operators remain subject to valid discharge regulation with access to the lawful alternative to compulsory third-party management; this affirmance does not remove that alternative or enlarge the final permit authority.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. Municipal discharge regulation may offer a genuine alternative to third-party administration

**Controlling proposition:** The Federal Government may regulate a municipality's own covered pollutant discharges and offer an optional management program when a lawful, available alternative permits compliance through direct regulation of those discharges instead of compulsory administration of third-party rules. The Government must identify that operative alternative when relying on choice, and the challenger may establish that its availability is illusory; ordinary costs of valid own-operation regulation do not become federal-assistance reimbursement claims.

**Authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court. The common coalition identified above joins the entire opinion, including both holdings and their explanations at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** The municipal operators' concrete obligations concerning their systems and work supply injury, traceability and redressability. Lomont v. O'Neill rejects a categorical state-authorization barrier to that own-work claim. The merits then depend on what the rules actually require.

Reno v. Condon permits direct regulation of a State's own activity despite the employee work needed for compliance. New York v. United States requires a real alternative to compulsory sovereign administration. Some listed stormwater measures do require regulation of third parties; sewer ownership does not itself justify those commands. But sections 122.33(b)(2)(ii) and 122.34(a) provide a different-program application under section 122.26(d), excepted from the listed minimum controls. The permission to omit legal-authority information and the demonstrated alternative experience support that route's availability here.

The municipalities argue that a demanding individual application and compliance expense make the alternative illusory. Those burdens do not establish that every available route requires the same unwanted third-party administration. Lawful control of the operator's own discharge may remain compulsory and costly. Lomont's freedom to decline assistance to private applicants does not confer a right to refuse that direct regulation. The alternative's substance and actual access, rather than a permit label, supply the answer; the application provisions guarantee neither a particular final permit nor a numerical end-of-pipe limit.

Printz and Mack concern bounded statutory assistance to federal administration and contain no majority fiscal rationale requiring compensation. They do not convert ordinary municipal operating costs into reimbursable federal assistance. On this facial record the genuine direct-discharge route defeats the commandeering claim. A specific permit that eliminates that route must be judged from its actual terms and record under the same anti-command and genuine-choice rules.

**Regulatory terms material to the application:** A regulated small municipal separate storm sewer system must seek discharge authorization from its authorized State, Tribe or Territory, or from the EPA Regional Office where no authorized program operates. Federal permitting remains available when a State declines administration. Under section 122.33(b)(2)(ii), an operator seeking a different individual program uses section 122.26(d)'s application requirements. It may omit the legal-authority information described in sections 122.26(d)(1)(ii) and (d)(2), unless it wants the permit writer to consider that information in developing other permit conditions. This is permission to omit that material; the remaining applicable application requirements continue. Section 122.34(a) excepts that route from its listed minimum controls, while lawful information requirements and final discharge conditions remain separately applicable.

For the minimum-control route, section 122.34 requires a program designed to reduce pollutants to the maximum extent practicable, protect water quality and satisfy appropriate Clean Water Act requirements. The permitting authority specifies up to five years from permit issuance for development and implementation. The six measures are public education and outreach, public involvement, illicit-discharge detection and elimination, construction-site controls, post-construction controls, and pollution prevention and housekeeping for municipal operations. Public involvement must at least comply with State, Tribal and local public-notice requirements. Municipal housekeeping includes an operation and maintenance program with employee training to prevent and reduce runoff from activities such as park, fleet, building, construction and stormwater-system maintenance.

The third-party controls have distinct operative conditions:

- **Illicit discharges:** The operator must develop, implement and enforce a detection-and-elimination program, prepare the required outfall and receiving-water map if not already completed, and develop a plan to detect and address non-stormwater discharges, including illegal dumping. Its effective prohibition through ordinance or another regulatory mechanism, with enforcement procedures and actions, operates to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law.
- **Listed flows:** The operator need address the following flows only if it identifies them as significant contributors of pollutants to the small system: water-line flushing, landscape irrigation, diverted stream flows, rising groundwater, uncontaminated groundwater infiltration, uncontaminated pumped groundwater, potable-water discharges, foundation drains, air-conditioning condensation, irrigation water, springs, crawl-space pump water, footing drains, lawn watering, individual residential car washing, riparian-habitat and wetland flows, dechlorinated pool discharges, and street wash water. Firefighting discharges are separately excluded from the effective non-stormwater prohibition and need be addressed only when identified as significant pollutant sources to waters of the United States. The latter condition concerns the receiving waters, not the small-system destination used for the listed flows.
- **Construction controls:** The program covers construction disturbing at least one acre, including smaller disturbances forming part of a common development or sale plan disturbing at least one acre. It includes an ordinance or regulatory mechanism requiring erosion and sediment controls and compliance sanctions to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law; appropriate erosion and sediment practices; control of site waste capable of harming water quality; review of plans for water-quality effects; receipt and consideration of public information; and inspection and enforcement procedures. When the permitting authority grants the incorporated small-construction waiver, the municipality need not develop, implement or enforce the corresponding program for the waived sites.
- **Covered small construction and waiver:** Section 122.26(b)(15)(i) covers clearing, grading and excavation disturbing at least one but less than five acres, including smaller components of a common development or sale plan ultimately disturbing at least one but less than five acres. Routine maintenance preserving the original line and grade, hydraulic capacity or original purpose is excluded. The Director may waive otherwise applicable general-permit requirements for construction disturbing less than five acres through either of the two paths below; qualifying acreage alone does not grant a waiver. Section 122.26(b)(15)(ii) separately permits designation of other construction by the Director or, in an approved State program, the Director or EPA Regional Administrator, based on potential contribution to a water-quality-standard violation or significant pollutants to covered waters.
- **Rainfall path:** The rainfall erosivity factor must be less than five during construction, calculated under Chapter 2, pages 21–64, of the incorporated January 1997 Agriculture Handbook 703 RUSLE method. The operator must certify to the Director that construction will occur during a period meeting that criterion.
- **Water-quality-analysis path:** Controls must be unnecessary under an EPA-approved or EPA-established total maximum daily load addressing the pollutants of concern. For non-impaired waters that do not require such a load, an equivalent analysis must allocate pollutants to small construction sites or determine that allocations are unnecessary to protect water quality after considering existing in-stream concentrations, expected growth in pollutant contributions from all sources and a margin of safety. Pollutants of concern include sediment or a sediment measure, such as suspended solids, turbidity or siltation, and every other pollutant identified as causing impairment of a receiving water body. The operator must certify to the Director that construction and stormwater discharges will occur within the analysis's drainage area. The permitting authority's waiver remains permissive under either path.
- **Post-construction controls:** New development and redevelopment disturbing at least one acre, including smaller projects within a larger common development or sale plan, are covered when they discharge into the small system. The operator must ensure controls preventing or minimizing water-quality impacts, use appropriate structural or nonstructural practices, employ an ordinance or regulatory mechanism to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law, and ensure adequate long-term operation and maintenance. The construction-site waiver is not an exception to this separate provision.

Text labeled Guidance recommends methods; it does not make each suggested civic project, outreach medium, planning strategy, inspection technique or enforcement example compulsory. The minimum-control duties themselves remain duties within that route. These actual qualifications define the program considered here; the different-program route, rather than the State-law qualifications alone, provides the genuine alternative to compulsory third-party administration.

##### II. Factual information serving the operator's pollution-control function may be required within defined expression safeguards

**Controlling proposition:** Assuming municipal operators may assert the claimed speech protection, a lawful pollution-control program may require truthful, nonideological information about the system's discharges, runoff reduction and disposal hazards when the agency justifies a substantial health or environmental purpose, a direct practical connection to the regulated operation, and a confined obligation with meaningful flexibility that is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. The requirement may not compel falsehood, political allegiance, ideological or personal endorsement, or suppression of contrary views; public usefulness, a factual label or presumed agreement alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly supports this defined operational-information rule and its application, independently of the permit-choice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The physical discharge-control program exists independently of the required communication. Community inputs and improper disposal affect the pollutants the operator's system discharges. Information about those effects and practical reduction therefore serves an existing operating function, rather than making the message its own justification.

Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's private-subsidy ruling rests on an independently justified function, nonattribution of the promotional message to dissenting producers, and freedom to advertise independently and criticize the program. United States v. United Foods bars selected promotion from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. The present duty concerns factual pollution operations, with freedom to identify the federal requirement and criticize its policy. The municipality may also be identified as the information source; the decision does not assume that all communications are unattributed.

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell supplies a bounded analogy for justified product-and-hazard disclosures and undue-burden review. Municipal outreach is not automatically commercial product speech. The Court adopts this distinct operational application because the lawful physical program, substantial environmental purpose, direct practical connection, confined nonideological content and flexible means supply the required independent justification. It does not infer a general factual-speech exception from the program's breadth.

The municipalities correctly observe that direct communication is speech and that a permit cannot compel unrelated advocacy. But the actual provisions require runoff-impact and disposal-hazard information, not agreement with EPA's policy. Barnette and Wooley's protections against coerced belief remain effective. The flexible methods and ability to use existing materials bear on burden without establishing that all implementation is inexpensive. The facial challenge establishes neither compelled ideology or falsehood nor an unjustified or undue burden in these limited duties. The same justification, fit and burden requirements govern an actual different implementation. Municipal constitutional personhood generally is unnecessary to this disposition and is not decided.

**Required communications and application:** Section 122.34(b)(1)(i) requires distribution of educational materials or equivalent outreach about stormwater discharges' effects on water bodies and the steps the public can take to reduce runoff pollutants; it does not require both methods. Section 122.34(b)(3)(ii)(D) separately requires informing public employees, businesses and the general public about hazards from illegal discharges and improper waste disposal. These confined obligations satisfy the operational rule, and their facial speech challenge fails. The alternative permit route does not eliminate every information requirement and is not the independent ground for this speech holding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Reno v. Condon, January 12, 2000:** Applied to direct regulation of municipal discharge operations and the employee work required for compliance. Its own-activity distinction does not itself validate administration of rules against third parties.
- **New York v. United States, June 19, 1992:** Applied to require a lawful, genuine alternative to compulsory sovereign responsibility; two compulsory third-party programs would not supply that choice.
- **Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States, June 27, 1997:** Distinguished. Bounded temporary records assistance under affirmative statutory direction, with federal substantive policy and coercive enforcement retained, is distinct from ordinary compliance with regulation of a municipality's own operations. Its fractured fiscal remand remains without a majority compensation rationale.
- **Lomont v. O'Neill, March 11, 2004:** Applied to concrete own-work injury and examination of actual legal choices. Its unconditional refusal of private-applicant assistance is distinguished from compliance with valid own-discharge controls.
- **Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc., June 25, 1997:** Applied for an independently justified regulatory function and communication germane to it, with independent criticism preserved. Its private promotional-subsidy and attribution conditions retain their own scope.
- **United States v. United Foods, Inc., June 25, 2001:** Applied to prevent a selected message from creating its own compulsory justification. The separate physical pollution-control function distinguishes these operational duties from advertising-only promotion.
- **National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, March 17, 2003:** Its justification, practical fit and undue-burden limits inform the confined operational application here. Its commercial product-and-hazard category is preserved rather than treating municipal outreach as commercial by definition.
- **West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette, 319 U.S. 624:** Preserved against compelled ideological affirmation; operational pollution facts must remain within the adopted nonideological limits.
- **Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705:** Preserved against compelled personal ideological endorsement; the factual-information duty allows attribution to the federal requirement and independent disagreement.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 25, 2004, municipal own-discharge regulation may employ the challenged permit architecture when a lawful and actually available route permits direct compliance without compulsory administration of third-party rules. Government must identify that route when relying on choice; an operator may show it to be illusory. Ordinary costs of valid own-operation compliance are not converted into assistance reimbursement claims. Application requirements and final permit obligations remain distinct, and the retained administrative remands continue to govern permitting.

The decision also establishes a confined operational-information application: factual pollution-impact, reduction and disposal-hazard information may be required within the independent physical function, substantial purpose, direct practical fit, flexibility and justification-and-burden limits stated in Holding II. This governing permission extends beyond automatic classification as commercial product speech while retaining the compelled-belief and independent-criticism protections. The validity of a materially different final message, burden or permit condition is assessed under those existing limits on its own record.

Environmental Defense Center, Inc. v. United States Environmental Protection Agency, 344 F.3d 832 (9th Cir., September 15, 2003); Clean Water Act §§301 and 402, including 33 U.S.C. §1342(p); 40 C.F.R. §§122.26(b)(15), 122.26(d), 122.33 and 122.34; 64 Fed. Reg. 68722–68851 (December 8, 1999), effective February 7, 2000. The individual-application provisions and final permit conditions have distinct legal roles.

### ACLU of New Jersey v. County of Hudson, Appellate Division No. A-4100-01

Merits decision, April 13, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On writ of certiorari to the Superior Court of New Jersey, Appellate Division; submitted on the merits. The June 12, 2002 judgment reversed the order requiring public disclosure of federal detainee information. The question is whether authorized federal detainee-confidentiality rules displace conflicting state disclosure requirements in voluntarily accepted county custody arrangements and whether those arrangements commandeer county government.

The counties housed federal immigration detainees under voluntary paid agreements. The interim regulation stated an April 17, 2002 effective date and was published April 22, while the disclosure litigation was pending. The identical text was adopted as final on January 29, 2003. The common-law-access dismissal was not appealed and remains outside this review.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The reversal of the disclosure order remains in place for information within 8 C.F.R. §236.6. The ACLU’s request to invalidate that federal withholding ground or compel release of the covered information is denied. The unappealed common-law-access dismissal remains intact. The mandate returns the case for ordinary implementation of the affirmed judgment.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court supplies nine direct joins to every holding below. The separate notice-and-comment and effectiveness requirements within Holding II are each supported by all nine Justices.

##### I. The enacted detention-and-records authority supports this confined confidentiality rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The immigration-administration, Service-records, implementing-regulation and paid nonfederal-custody provisions of 8 U.S.C. §1103(a)(1), (2), (3), (9)(A) and (c), as governing the promulgation, authorize federal control of public disclosure of detainee information acquired through the covered federal custody function. Section 236.6 validly channels that disclosure through applicable federal laws, regulations and executive orders; a voluntary contract alone supplies neither delegated authority nor a power over unrelated county records.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court begins with Congress’s assignment. Section 1103 combines authority over immigration administration and Service records with authority to arrange and pay for nonfederal housing, care and security of persons detained under federal law. Necessary implementing regulations may preserve control of information acquired through that federal function. The Commissioner acts through the Attorney General’s delegated authority under subsection (c). Subsection (a)(1)’s exceptions for powers assigned to the President, Secretary of State, State Department officers and diplomatic or consular officers remain part of that assignment.

Haggar requires the Court to enforce statutory boundaries before accepting a reasonable delegated choice. Mead requires actual authority to act with the force of law, exercised through the relevant mechanism. The combined custody-and-records provisions supply that authority here. Neither the agency’s importance nor its use of rulemaking form supplies the missing premise by itself. Nor does the Attorney General’s legal-ruling proviso displace independent judicial examination of the delegation.

The rule reaches public and private custodians housing, maintaining, serving or otherwise holding detainees on the Service’s behalf, and persons acquiring detainee information through an official or contractual relationship with those custodians. Its public-record restriction applies insofar as records contain the covered information. Federal disclosure law continues to govern release; the rule channels requests rather than deciding every request against disclosure. Detainees retain their ability to identify themselves, communicate and seek counsel under the applicable law. This confined operation answers the objection that contracting permits unlimited local secrecy: the legal source and the federal custody connection, rather than the contract alone, define the authority.

##### II. The published interim reasons separately satisfy both APA good-cause requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Except when a statute requires notice or a hearing, §553(b)(B) permits omission of advance notice and public procedure when the agency finds and briefly explains in the issued rule that such procedure is impracticable, unnecessary or contrary to the public interest. The interim notice’s identified risk of irreversible disclosures satisfies the public-interest ground here; independently, its published explanation establishes good cause under §553(d)(3) to dispense with the ordinary thirty-day period before effectiveness for the public-release demand presented in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** The two exceptions perform different work. The first permits the agency to act before public participation; the second concerns the period before a substantive rule takes effect. Satisfaction of one does not establish the other.

The interim notice explained that disclosure through dispersed custodians could expose investigative methods, sources, witnesses and the direction and scope of investigations. It identified possible intimidation, harm, retaliation against detainees and associates, and impaired cooperation during the continuing emergency. These are the agency’s published predictions, not findings that every detainee possesses sensitive information. They support immediate federal management of release under the governing federal disclosure law.

Advance public procedure would permit the identified irreversible disclosures before that control could operate. A further thirty-day effectiveness interval would independently allow the same loss and defeat the rule’s protective function. Those distinct consequences satisfy the separate statutory requirements.

The contrary argument rightly rejects a general emergency exception, particularly months after the attacks and for a rule covering immigration detainees beyond a single investigation. An inconvenient disclosure suit does not itself supply good cause. Here the justification is the particular risk of irreversible disclosure before the responsible federal authority can apply federal law. Litigation makes the timing concrete; it does not replace the statutory showing. The demanded relief is a future public release after promulgation, so the Court applies the exceptions to that demand while preserving the distinction between the stated April 17 date and April 22 publication.

##### III. Regulation of information from voluntarily accepted federal custody does not commandeer the counties

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid federal confidentiality rule may govern information acquired through a county’s voluntarily accepted, federally paid immigration-custody operation without compelling the county to administer federal regulation against unrelated private persons. The rule’s obligation follows that custody function and its resulting information, and the personnel work needed for compliance does not convert it into prohibited conscription.

**Controlling explanation:** Condon distinguishes direct regulation of a State’s own handling of information from assigning it legislation or a regulatory program against others. Section 236.6 governs the counties’ handling of information from the federal custody work they accepted, and also governs private custodians and related information holders. Federal responsibility for disclosure remains identifiable.

New York prevents official assent from enlarging federal authority. The Court therefore resolves actual delegation and procedure independently of the counties’ agreements. Printz concerns affirmative assistance demanded of an unwilling office; its confined source-and-function rule does not make voluntary paid custody forbidden. Lomont requires genuine voluntariness and distinguishes it from a nominal option burdened by a federal command. The established voluntary entry into these paid custody arrangements supplies the relevant relationship here.

Environmental Defense Center requires examination of the actual regulated activity and legal choices. That inquiry identifies the counties’ own custody-information handling, rather than an assigned general regulatory program. Valid regulation of that accepted operation remains enforceable while it is performed. The arrangement’s label alone would be insufficient, but its actual function answers the structural objection. Ordinary compliance costs remain costs of the regulated operation.

##### IV. The valid federal restriction controls the conflicting demand for public release

**Holding and operative rule:** When state disclosure law requires public release of information covered by this valid federal custody rule and federal law restricts that release to its own disclosure channels, the federal rule controls the actual conflict. The appellate reversal of the disclosure order is affirmed for the covered information; separable unprotected material remains governed by applicable law, and any coverage dispute must identify the information’s category and legal basis rather than rely on a blanket immigration label.

**Controlling explanation:** The state release command and the federal restriction cannot both be obeyed for the same covered information. Federal supremacy resolves that conflict only after the Court establishes lawful delegated authority and procedure. The contracted federal custody connection fixes the application’s scope.

The rule expressly includes pending disclosure requests. Here the demand seeks a future public release; applying the restriction to that release does not attach a new sanction to completed conduct. The trial order remained subject to appellate review and was reversed after promulgation. The requested disclosure thus receives an answer under the governing restriction, with the existing appellate reversal left in place.

Applying the coverage rule is ordinary implementation of that judgment. The counties must continue withholding covered information while processing separable unprotected material under applicable law. Separately available federal access channels remain available on their own terms.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. (April 21, 1999):** Applied to require actual delegated authority and independent enforcement of statutory boundaries before accepting a reasonable implementing choice.
- **United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001):** Applied to the statutory delegation and exercised rulemaking mechanism; the binding custody regulation differs from Mead’s transaction-specific, revisable classification letter.
- **Reno v. Condon (January 12, 2000):** Its distinction between regulation of the State’s own information handling and compulsory administration against others is applied to information from accepted federal custody.
- **New York v. United States (June 19, 1992):** Its rule that official assent cannot enlarge federal power is preserved and applied through the independent authority inquiry.
- **Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States (June 27, 1997):** Its compulsory-assistance setting is distinguished from the voluntarily accepted paid custody operation.
- **Lomont v. O’Neill (March 11, 2004):** Its insistence on an actual voluntary relationship informs the custody inquiry; its unconditional option to refuse applicant assistance does not invalidate lawful rules for an accepted operation’s information.
- **Environmental Defense Center v. EPA (March 25, 2004):** Its actual-function inquiry is applied to custody-information handling, and its treatment of ordinary own-operation compliance costs remains controlling.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes the validity, against the presented delegation, interim APA and structural challenges, of federal control over public disclosure of information acquired through voluntarily accepted paid federal immigration custody. Contrary state disclosure requirements yield for that covered information. Each good-cause exception retains its own statutory predicate and published justification. The holding’s reach follows the custodial source and information category, while applicable federal disclosure channels and ordinary processing of separable unprotected material remain operative.

#### Source Notes

ACLU of New Jersey v. County of Hudson, 352 N.J. Super. 44, 799 A.2d 629 (App. Div. June 12, 2002), No. A-4100-01; 28 U.S.C. §1257; 8 U.S.C. §1103(a)(1), (2), (3), (9)(A) and (c), governing the 2002 promulgation; 8 C.F.R. §236.6; 5 U.S.C. §553(b)(B), (d)(3); 67 Fed. Reg. 19508–19511 (April 22, 2002); 68 Fed. Reg. 4364–4367 (January 29, 2003); N.J.S.A. 47:1A-1 to -4 and 30:8-16, as applicable to the judgment below. The later Open Public Records Act amendments were not effective when the Appellate Division issued that judgment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The mandate implements the affirmed appellate judgment. The counties continue administering covered information under §236.6 and separately available access law, with any coverage question resolved by its identified category and legal ground. Supreme Court review of the granted federal questions is concluded.

### West Virginia v. U.S. Department of Health & Human Services

Merits decision, June 16, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; submitted on the merits. The May 7, 2002 appellate judgment affirmed summary judgment for HHS in West Virginia’s facial challenge to mandatory Medicaid estate recovery and the all-or-part funding consequence for noncompliance.

The questions are whether the estate-recovery condition violates the Tenth Amendment and whether the authorized funding consequence is facially coercive before a particular sanction is selected. West Virginia resisted the condition, received an HHS warning that it could lose all or part of federal Medicaid participation, complied, and sought invalidation. Federal Medicaid receipts exceeded one billion dollars annually, while estate-recovery receipts were much smaller. No particular withholding decision is established.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested facial invalidation is denied. The estate-recovery condition remains operative with its enacted qualifications. The mandate returns the case for ordinary implementation of the affirmed judgment.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O’CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Ginsburg’s opinion of the Court has the nine complete joins identified in Opinion Topology. Both holdings, their explanations and the statutory qualifications below are controlling by direct majority agreement.

##### I. Estate recovery is a condition governing the funded program’s own expenditures

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may condition continued Medicaid participation on seeking recovery of the program’s own correctly paid assistance within §1396p(b)’s specified categories and protections, subject to the independent requirement that the funding arrangement leave a genuine practical choice. The recovery duty concerns the funded program itself; its relationship to Medicaid supports the condition but does not alone answer whether an actual threatened funding loss is coercive.

**Controlling explanation:** Kansas applies the distinction between substantial inducement and practical compulsion to related federal funding. Estate recovery replenishes the same medical-assistance program from which the expenditures were made. The duty to seek recovery therefore differs from an independently imposed command that a State administer federal policy outside the funded program.

New York distinguishes lawful monetary incentives from compulsory administration or assumption of private obligations. Printz concerns actual compelled executive assistance and does not categorically prohibit duties within genuine conditional funding. These distinctions require examination of the real arrangement rather than acceptance of a voluntary-program label.

The condition is the enacted obligation with its survivor, hardship, service and estate limits. A requirement to seek recovery is not a finding that an estate contains recoverable assets or that a particular recovery succeeds. Nor can beneficiary protections substitute for examining the pressure imposed on the State by threatened funding loss. Kansas requires those inquiries to remain separate.

**Operative statutory terms:** Section 1396p generally prohibits adjustment or recovery of correctly paid assistance except within the following categories and qualifications. These provisions define the condition being sustained.

- **Institutional category:** Under §1396p(b)(1)(A), the State shall seek adjustment or recovery from the estate or upon sale of property subject to an institutional lien for a person described in subsection (a)(1)(B). That person is an inpatient in a nursing facility, an intermediate care facility for persons with intellectual disabilities, or another medical institution, and must spend all income except a minimal personal-needs amount on medical care as a condition of receiving institutional services under the plan. After notice and a hearing opportunity under state procedures, the State must determine that discharge and return home cannot reasonably be expected.
- **Assistance received at age 55 or older:** Section (b)(1)(B) requires estate recovery for nursing-facility services, home-and-community-based services, and related hospital and prescription-drug services. Recovery for other plan items or services is optional for the State.
- **Long-term-care insurance:** Section (b)(1)(C) requires recovery of nursing-facility and other long-term-care assistance from the estate of a person receiving or entitled to benefits under the specified insurance arrangement involving disregarded assets or resources. Its exception applies to assistance under a State plan whose amendment was approved as of May 14, 1993 and disregarded assets or resources to the extent of insurance payments, or because the person received or was entitled to insurance benefits.
- **Surviving spouse and child:** Recovery under paragraph (1) may occur only after any surviving spouse dies and when there is no surviving child under 21 or in the protected blindness or disability category. For States eligible to participate in the State program under subchapter XVI, that category is a child who is blind or permanently and totally disabled; for other States, it is a child who is blind or disabled as defined in §1382c. The blindness and disability protections are not limited to children under 21.
- **Additional home-lien recovery protection:** Recovery must also wait while a qualifying sibling or caregiving son or daughter lawfully lives in the home and has continuously lawfully resided there since institutional admission. The sibling must have lived there for at least the year immediately before admission. The son or daughter must have lived there for at least the two years immediately before admission and establish to the State’s satisfaction that the care provided allowed the individual to remain home instead of entering an institution. This recovery-stage sibling protection requires no equity interest.
- **Hardship and estate scope:** Section (b)(3) requires procedures conforming to the Secretary’s standards under which the agency shall waive application for undue hardship under the Secretary’s criteria, except for paragraph (1)(C). The estate must include all real and personal property and other assets within the State-probate estate. Additional property or assets in which the person held legal title or an interest at death may be included, to that extent, including property conveyed through joint tenancy, tenancy in common, survivorship, life estate, living trust or another arrangement. That broader inclusion is mandatory for a person to whom paragraph (1)(C)(i) applies and otherwise is the State’s option.
- **Incorporated lien rules:** Section (a) generally prohibits a predeath lien for assistance paid or to be paid, with distinct exceptions for a court judgment concerning incorrectly paid benefits and the institutional real-property lien described above. The institutional lien cannot attach to the home while a spouse, a child in the protected age or blindness/disability categories, or a sibling with an equity interest who lived there for at least the year immediately before admission lawfully resides there. This pre-lien sibling protection includes the equity condition that the recovery-stage protection does not. The institutional lien dissolves upon discharge from the institution and return home.

##### II. The actual all-or-part funding mechanism does not establish facial coercion on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** A related funding condition is not facially coercive merely because its statute permits both total and partial funding cessation when lawful noncoercive enforcement remains available and the present record does not establish an unavoidable coercive demand. The State must establish practical compulsion under Kansas’s genuine-choice inquiry; a concrete, credible threatened or imposed lesser or total consequence remains reviewable on its actual operation, without a categorical requirement to endure a completed cutoff first.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers the arrangement’s structure and practical operation. Relevant considerations can include the centrality and scope of threatened funds, the condition’s program relationship, any materially new mission, notice and transition, replacement possibilities and the consequences of refusal. These are not a mandatory checklist. Competent qualitative evidence, program operation and administrative experience can establish compulsion; no exclusively statistical proof, fixed dollar or percentage, or insolvency threshold governs. Notice, relatedness and the absence of an entitlement to unchanged grant terms do not individually defeat a supported claim.

West Virginia’s dependence, the disparity between its Medicaid receipts and recovery receipts, its resistance and the all-or-part warning show substantial pressure. Compliance does not by itself establish free choice or extinguish the ongoing controversy. But the warning and statutory text do not establish that HHS selected total cessation, closed the lawful narrower route or otherwise made an unavoidable coercive demand. The statute permits continued payment to unaffected categories or parts. The facial record therefore does not justify invalidation throughout the condition’s operation.

The strongest contrary argument is that a credible threat to end an established health program can compel obedience before money is withheld. Kansas permits that showing. A theoretical lesser alternative would not answer evidence that the actual demand destroys genuine choice. Here, however, authorization of total cessation is not proof of such a demand. The Court relies on the actual all-or-part warning and legally available applications together, not on discretion as a guarantee of constitutional administration. A lesser consequence can also be coercive in its practical operation. The constitutionality of a specified total cutoff remains open because its scope and credible operational effects are not presented in this record.

**The funding mechanism in terms:** Before acting under §1396c, the Secretary must give reasonable notice and an opportunity for hearing to the agency administering or supervising the approved State plan. The Secretary must find either that the plan has changed so that it no longer complies with §1396a or that plan administration fails to comply substantially with a provision of that section. The Secretary shall then notify the State agency that further payments will not be made or, in the Secretary’s discretion, that payments will be limited to categories under or parts of the plan not affected by the failure. Cessation or that limitation continues until the Secretary is satisfied that there will no longer be the failure to comply. The alternative preserves payments to unaffected categories or parts; it does not require selection of that alternative or create an administrative-funds-only ceiling. This State-funding discretion is distinct from the mandatory beneficiary hardship safeguard in §1396p(b)(3).

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Kansas v. United States (January 22, 2001):** Its genuine-practical-choice rule governs the Medicaid condition and actual funding consequence. Its qualitative-proof rule and rejection of fixed fiscal thresholds remain controlling; the TANF-specific graduated sanctions and exemption do not become Medicaid limits.
- **South Dakota v. Dole:** Its distinction between inducement and coercion, with general-welfare, notice, relatedness and independent constitutional requirements, is applied through Kansas. Its five-percent setting supplies no numerical safe harbor.
- **Steward Machine Co. v. Davis:** Its distinction between financial pressure and compulsion is applied without treating every expensive choice as voluntary by definition.
- **New York v. United States (June 19, 1992):** Its monetary-incentive holding is applied and its compulsory-administration and ownership/liability setting distinguished.
- **Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States (June 27, 1997):** Its actual compelled-assistance setting is distinguished from conditional program participation; its source-and-function limits and fractured fiscal disposition remain intact.

#### Law After Decision

Medicaid estate recovery remains an operative funding condition within its mandatory and optional categories, survivor protections, hardship waiver and estate limits. Section 1396c’s all-or-part mechanism survives this facial challenge, with its required procedure, findings, notification and endpoint intact. Enforcement remains subject to Kansas’s genuine-practical-choice rule: an actual credible threatened or imposed funding consequence can be challenged on its scope, notice and practical effects, including competent qualitative evidence. Statutory discretion supplies lawful possible applications and remains reviewable when exercised or credibly threatened.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s complete opinion and the facial affirmance. He adds that review must provide effective relief: a court acting through an available channel must promptly restrain an established coercive sanction. A timely challenge to an actual or sufficiently imminent consequence must examine the threatened funds, notice and practical effects under Kansas. This additional remedial qualification explains how he would give effect to the preserved coercion limit; it does not alter his vote to affirm the present judgment.

#### Source Notes

West Virginia v. U.S. Department of Health & Human Services, 289 F.3d 281 (4th Cir. May 7, 2002); 42 U.S.C. §§1396a, 1396p(a) and (b), and 1396c; U.S. Const., Art. I, §8, cl. 1, and Amdt. 10. The statutory recovery conditions and funding procedure are commands and permissions, not findings that a beneficiary satisfies a recovery category or that the Secretary has selected a withholding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The mandate leaves the estate-recovery condition effective and returns the case for ordinary implementation of the affirmed judgment. HHS may administer the condition through lawful enforcement under the enacted procedures and the controlling genuine-choice rule. Supreme Court review of this facial challenge is concluded.

**End of entry: West Virginia v. U.S. Department of Health & Human Services, Merits decision, June 16, 2004.**
