## Decisions and Dispositions

### Yarborough v. Gentry, No. 02-1597

Merits decision, October 20, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

540 U.S. 1. The Ninth Circuit reversed the denial of federal habeas relief on a challenge to defense counsel's closing argument. The question is whether the state court unreasonably applied the Sixth Amendment performance standard in rejecting that claim. The matter was decided on the merits submission.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

The reviewed closing-argument habeas claim is rejected.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The District Court's denial of relief on this claim stands. The conviction remains operative against this ground. The court of appeals must implement rejection of the claim and resolve any independently pending preserved ground under its own requirements.

#### Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion joined in full by all nine participating Justices.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Controlling authority:** The complete per curiam opinion has nine direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. That coalition controls both propositions below.

**1. Review of counsel's closing argument.** A federal habeas court must assess the actual summation in the context of the supported defense and may grant relief on a state-adjudicated performance claim only when the state decision satisfies section 2254(d)(1). Omitting useful arguments or using offensive language does not by itself establish deficient performance; the court must examine what the argument actually did, and a strategy label cannot substitute for that examination.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland requires professionally reasonable representation, with room for informed choices about emphasis. Herring protects closing advocacy without prescribing a script. Williams supplies the distinct contrary-rule and objectively-unreasonable-application inquiries; independent disagreement with a state assessment is insufficient. Bell applies those requirements to particular omissions within actual defense advocacy. Visciotti requires examination of the supported reasoning and record as a whole, and Andrade preserves the substantive-law cutoff at the state merits adjudication without demanding a factual twin. These rules require attention to a summation's substance, rather than either automatic deference to a tactical description or a catalogue of preferable phrases.

**2. This state performance determination was reasonable.** The state court could reasonably conclude that counsel's closing presented a coherent accident defense, attacked the eyewitness's account and separated past misconduct from proof of intentional stabbing. Its rejection of deficient performance therefore defeats the reviewed claim under section 2254(d)(1).

**Controlling explanation:** Counsel identified intent as the dispute, challenged the witness's changing description of the lighting, explained confusion about prior convictions and argued against guilt based on character. Gentry identifies substantial omitted points, including inconsistent recollections, wound evidence and corroboration concerning another man's presence. Counsel's statement connecting disbelief of Gentry with conviction is also unsound as a statement of the prosecution's burden: disbelief alone does not prove every element. But the state court could read that compressed formulation within an argument that continued to advance accident and challenge the prosecution's proof. The question is whether its performance assessment fell outside the lawful range, not whether a stronger summation was possible. The actual defense work supports the state conclusion under Strickland and Bell. Neither the trial judge's favorable assessment nor the length of deliberations controls that inquiry.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Strickland v. Washington is applied to the actual, contextual performance inquiry; reasonable professional choice remains subject to examination of the supported record.
- Herring v. New York is applied as protection for meaningful closing advocacy, with no prescribed summation script.
- Williams v. Taylor is applied to section 2254(d)(1)'s independent inquiries and objective legal limit; its contrary-rule and unreasonable-application holdings retain their force.
- Bell v. Cone is applied to the sufficient performance ground for a confined tactical claim within substantive advocacy.
- Woodford v. Visciotti is applied to whole-record review rather than isolated wording or formal recitation of a standard.
- Lockyer v. Andrade is applied to the objective statutory gateway and the state-adjudication cutoff for clearly established substantive law.

#### Law After Decision

The established statutory and Sixth Amendment rules remain in force. They permit rejection of this closing-argument claim because the state performance assessment was substantively reasonable on the complete summation. Courts must examine actual defense advocacy and its context when applying the rule to other closing arguments.

#### Source Notes

Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668; Herring v. New York, 422 U.S. 853; Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362; Bell v. Cone, 535 U.S. 685; Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19; Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63; Gentry v. Roe, 320 F.3d 891. The reviewed claim concerns the actual closing presentation, and the presumed-prejudice theory was abandoned below.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The ordinary mandate returns the case to the Ninth Circuit to implement the stated rejection and complete any independently pending preserved habeas work. Supreme Court review of the presented closing-argument claim is concluded.

**End of entry: Yarborough v. Gentry, Merits decision, October 20, 2003.**

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Medical qualifications and secular referrals

**Controlling proposition:** A State may require objective medical qualifications and arrangements serving emergency care, but it may not place protected care under an outsider's arbitrary, legally uncontrolled licensing consent. These provisions, considered separately and in their established operation, have not been shown to impose that private veto; rejection of this delegation claim is affirmed.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine on the common qualification and arbitrary-control ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo supplies the bar against arbitrary licensing administration; Danforth and Casey identify the constitutional concern when another person effectively controls protected choice. Professional cooperation alone does not establish that control. Section 205(C)(2) requires a signed agreement with a board-certified obstetrician/gynecologist only if none is on staff; the physician must be able to admit patients to at least one local hospital providing obstetrical and gynecological services and remain available to staff and patients throughout operating hours. Section 305(A) requires staff and consulting physicians to have admission rights at one or more local hospitals offering appropriate obstetrical and gynecological services, with documented, Department-approved emergency-transfer arrangements available as its alternative when hospitalization becomes necessary. Section 309(B) independently requires physicians to have admission rights at one or more local hospitals offering appropriate obstetrical and gynecological services; neither other provision’s exception applies to it by implication. The record does not establish an actual or supported impending arbitrary refusal controlling these providers' operations under that command. Secular referrals under section 307 similarly serve needed care. Existing licenses are evidence of operation, not a requirement to await closure. A discretionary community-health-and-welfare waiver is not a guaranteed timely cure, and public-hospital constraints do not automatically bind private institutions. The clinics' strongest objection—that cooperation could be withheld for reasons unrelated to care—requires proof of effective control, including supported prospective interference, rather than treating every qualification as a private veto.

##### II. Clergy arrangements

**Controlling proposition:** Government may facilitate patient-requested religious assistance, but may not require denominational conformity, decide religious adequacy or make licensing depend on compulsory clerical authority. Because the legally operative duty under section 307 remains unsettled on this record, the clergy judgment is vacated for determination of that duty and relief conforming to this boundary.

**Authority:** Part II's religious-authority boundary has all nine joins; its application and remand have the six joins specified in the topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Commack, applying Agostini, separates private religious choice and factual verification from government religious judgment and compulsory civil religious power. Section 307 requires arrangements for listed specialties, clergy, social services and other indicated fields to be available as needed. The appellate court understood that language as patient-requested directory or contact assistance. That is a permissible mechanism, but the published account of the administering official's testimony describes advance arrangements and denominational preferences. The testimony deserves consideration without being treated as a binding concession or an amendment to the rule. The three Justices who would affirm fairly emphasize the words tying availability to need. Those words do not conclusively resolve the materially different administrative account or establish an enforceable state-law limitation. The lower court must determine the operative legal duty. An enforceably confined requested-assistance duty may stand. If compulsory affiliations, denominational evaluation or clerical licensing control are established, their operation and dependent sanctions must be restrained while secular referrals and voluntary assistance are preserved. This also resolves the clergy-delegation objection at its actual scope.

##### III-A. Contextual professional standards

**Controlling proposition:** Professional and facility standards provide constitutionally adequate notice when their actual text, medical setting and recognized functions identify the required conduct and constrain enforcement. The ordinary challenged terms and section 305(A)'s staff-and-consulting-physician provision satisfy that standard.

**Authority:** Part III-A, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Hoffman Estates supplies contextual notice analysis; Grayned distinguishes workable practical standards from standardlessness. The rules concerning facility names, waiver administration, penalty factors, policies, trained personnel, health and background checks, contagious conditions, qualifications, safe care, equipment, indicated referrals, quality review, safety, sterile storage, cleanliness, odors, grounds, pests and rooms have ascertainable functions in their professional setting. They allow judgment in applying a standard. Section 305(A) addresses physicians, whether staff or consulting, and permits documented Department-approved emergency transfer when hospitalization becomes necessary. Its complete sentence does not impose physicians' hospital-privilege duties on receptionists. These provisions meet the challengers' stricter clarity concern without depending on a determination that only civil sanctions are available.

##### III-B. The independent best-practices sanction branch

**Controlling proposition:** The State may not impose independent sanctions or adverse licensing consequences solely because conduct violates an otherwise undefined additional best practice selected by the Department. Chapter 10 and section 103(C)'s independent best-practices branch require an adequate legally operative standard before such consequences may be imposed.

**Authority:** Part III-B, joined by Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Morales supplies the requirement that meaningful criteria govern the condition authorizing compulsory compliance; Kolender and Grayned likewise require guidance against arbitrary enforcement. Chapter 10 reaches conditions not otherwise addressed, and section 103(C) separately classifies conduct contrary to Department-interpreted best practices. This is an operative enforcement branch, not simply permission to issue future rules. No identified binding criterion fixes which additional practice is compulsory. The contrary position relies on professional context, first-offense warnings and good-faith mitigation. Those protections may moderate enforcement of an intelligible duty, but do not choose the missing standard. The defect exists even with civil licensing consequences, so the disputed incorporation of section 44-7-340's criminal sanctions need not be resolved. The injunction must target this independent branch; definite duties, section 103(C)'s residual classification of their violations, and lawful prospective standard setting remain effective.

##### IV-A. Identified patient records

**Controlling proposition:** When government authorizes transfer of intimate clinic records to inspectors, the information, authorized recipients, actual official function, scope, use and safeguards must be sufficiently connected to the regulatory need. The courts must determine those permissions and connections here, restraining established improper access or dissemination while preserving lawful confidential inspection.

**Authority:** Part IV-A, joined by Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins supply the rule independently of Stone-Zsela's judgment concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** Lawall supplies the inquiry into additional permitted access; Herring makes further disclosure recipient- and function-specific. Whalen permits safeguarded health administration, and Doe's confined assumed-right application shows why every datum need not be indispensable. The direct patient-care function therefore supports identified clinical audits, complaint investigation and corroboration when sufficiently connected to their tasks. Section 102(F)(2) limits copying to documents required during inspections or investigations, but its access language still requires examination. Section 401 includes contact, social-security, birth, family and emergency-contact information, so deleting names alone may leave identification possible. Retention, training and privacy rules matter without deciding all legally permitted uses. Section 44-7-310 restricts public identification but contains facility-licensure, certificate-of-need, employee-licensing and court-order exceptions. Section 44-7-315 requires public disclosure on written request while protecting identities of individuals present in a licensed facility. Section 44-41-60 bars patient and physician names in registrar reports; section 44-41-360 requires an explicit anonymity determination under the applicable constitutional standard when the woman has not consented. These distinct protections do not establish absolute inspection secrecy. The reported flyer reproducing a minor patient's record and accounts of similar releases warrant assessment, without establishing the authority or details of every release. The direct inspection function and confidentiality protections, emphasized by the dissent, cannot alone settle the full permissions and reported practices. Conversely, the district court's universal compelling-need condition is too categorical. Redaction, consent and targeted follow-up are concrete alternatives to assess where adequate, not rigid prerequisites to all inspection.

##### IV-B. Additional interference with protected choice

**Controlling proposition:** If an actual inspection obligation causes proved additional interference with protected reproductive choice, Casey's complete framework also governs that interference, independently of the information-access claim. The lower court must identify the duty, affected population, causal burden and applicable protection, then tailor relief to the established violation.

**Authority:** Part IV-B, joined by Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins. Kennedy's privacy join does not enlarge this coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey supplies an ordered inquiry rather than a categorical assumption that all clinic oversight burdens protected choice. A prohibited baseline invasion, effective third-party veto, denial of timely necessary care, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit, including an answer to concrete practicable substantially less burdensome alternatives. Challengers must identify the actual command, affected population and attributable interference, including usable exceptions and supported prospective effects. Successful access by some cannot erase another group's supported burden; below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends while independent protections continue. Clinical judgment, truthful communication, timely necessary care and relief preserving independently lawful requirements retain Casey's protections. The inquiry arises only on proof of additional interference, not from a privacy label or the presence of identifiers. It concerns operation of the inspection duty within the preserved claims and does not reopen the earlier overall-access judgment.

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey: applied at its effective-control and complete additional-interference scopes; its particular reporting holdings remain bounded and do not establish general identified-record access.
- Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss: applied to requested private assistance versus governmental religious standards and compulsory clerical civil authority.
- Agostini v. Felton: applied through Commack's religion framework, preserving the distinction between private choice and government religious action.
- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside: applied to contextual notice and actual enforcement guidance.
- Grayned v. City of Rockford: applied to contextual notice and actual enforcement guidance.
- City of Chicago v. Morales: applied to the independent missing enforcement criterion; advance warning does not supply the legal standard.
- Kolender v. Lawson: applied to the independent missing enforcement criterion; advance warning does not supply the legal standard.
- Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall: its information-recipient-function inquiry extends to additional clinic-to-inspector access while preserving connected confidential health administration.

#### Law After Decision

Objective medical cooperation and the ordinary contextual standards survive these challenges. Patient-requested religious help remains permissible; the legally enforceable clergy duty must be identified before the challenged operation is finally sustained. The independent undefined best-practices sanction branch cannot support sanctions until a sufficient operative standard exists. Additional governmental access to intimate clinic records receives the information-recipient-function inquiry, with legitimate sufficiently connected identified inspection preserved. Proved additional interference with protected choice also receives Casey's existing ordered protections. These are separate rules and remedies; professional qualification, religious power, ascertainable duties and information permissions do not answer one another.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stone-Zsela concurs in part, concurs in the privacy judgment and dissents in part. He would affirm the clergy condition only as voluntary patient-requested assistance, allowing ordinary emergency and referral arrangements while forbidding an unreviewable private veto or compelled religious counseling. He also would sustain the contextual professional-care rules, including their application only to reasonably ascertainable obligations. He regards the professional setting and limiting constructions as sufficient for these facial dispositions. On identifiers, his ground is more demanding than Part IV-A: once compelled disclosure of intimate treatment records is established, the State must justify identity access by a concrete regulatory need that reasonably cannot be met through less identifying review. Casey's bounded reporting permissions and Whalen's scheme-specific safeguards support that requirement. He would direct findings identifying the authorized recipients, demonstrated tasks, confidentiality protections and review mechanism, maintaining the existing privacy injunction to the extent needed to prevent unnecessary identifier disclosure while permitting nonidentifying inspection. Relief would cover the challenged policy and its patients unless the State shows a narrower restriction fully cures the established defect.

Scalia concurs in part and dissents in part, joined by Thomas. They join the medical and ordinary notice holdings and the common prohibition on compulsory religious civil authority. They would affirm the clergy provision on the as-needed, patient-requested construction; the administering official's described preferences do not amend that bounded legal duty. They would sustain the best-practices provision because recognizable clinical standards supply a professional core, while leaving unforeseeable sanctions open to a properly grounded particular challenge. On records, they retain their Doe constitutional-source objection to extending substantive informational privacy. Independently assuming the asserted right, they find the direct clinical-regulation task, copying qualification and confidentiality controls sufficient for this facial challenge. They acknowledge the reported releases and statutory exceptions, while distinguishing an actual improper request or disclosure from invalidity of general inspection authority. Their grounds would affirm the appellate inspection disposition rather than reinstate the district court's categorical compelling-interest requirement.

### Mitchell v. Esparza, No. 02-1369

Merits decision, November 3, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of a conditional sentence writ on omission of principal-offender language from this Ohio capital case. The questions are whether that omission is categorically immune from harmless-error review and whether the state disposition satisfies the applicable federal habeas standard. Ohio seeks rejection of the omission ground; Esparza seeks preservation of sentence relief. State merits rulings in 1992 and 1994 and finality in 1989 have distinct legal significance.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

The judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains sentence relief on the reviewed omission ground, and the case is remanded for the independently remaining sentence grounds.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Categorical structural-error premise | Rejected, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | The sentence writ cannot rest on automatic structural treatment of this omission |
| Reviewed omission ground under section 2254(d)(1) | Reversed and rejected, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | This ground is conclusively rejected on the state record and relevant contemporaneous law; Stone-Zsela would retain its properly dated inquiry on remand |

#### Opinion Topology

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A capital omission does not establish a categorical structural defect

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of this capital-eligibility allegation or finding is not automatically immune from harmless-error review. A habeas court must apply the statutory gateway using the constitutional holdings available at the relevant state merits adjudication and keep that inquiry separate from nonretroactivity and any legally available harmlessness inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams supplies the requirement of a contrary governing rule or objectively unreasonable application; Visciotti requires review of the complete supported state adjudication, and Andrade preserves the state-decision cutoff without demanding a factual twin. Gentry applies those distinctions to a different claim. Sullivan involved an invalid reasonable-doubt instruction that vitiated the standard for every finding. This case concerns a discrete allegation and eligibility omission. Chapman and the capital-error cases, including Satterwhite, permit meaningful review of trial error without treating capital punishment itself as a structural category. Ohio's principal-offender alternative and its prior-calculation-and-design alternative remain separate statutory routes. Presnell bars substitution of a materially unsubmitted aggravating basis, while Lowenfield permits constitutionally sufficient narrowing in the actual guilt-stage findings. Neither a sole-defendant indictment nor a capital label alone decides those federal questions. Neder and Ring now provide qualified actual-verdict review for their omissions, but their later dates do not establish constitutional duties in 1992 or 1994 or make new rules automatically retroactive at 1989 finality.

##### II. This state adjudication remains within the statutory range

**Controlling proposition:** On the actual state record and governing law at the relevant state adjudications, rejection of this principal-offender omission theory was neither contrary to this Court's holdings nor an objectively unreasonable application of them. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore forecloses the reviewed ground for the sentence writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Scalia and the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The jury was required to decide whether Esparza purposefully caused death while committing aggravated robbery, under a trial account identifying one robber and shooter, and separately considered the robbery specification beyond a reasonable doubt. The capital-specification cover and the State's sole-defendant indictment construction supported notice; taken with the actual adjudication, they permitted the state court to regard principal-offender culpability as supplied rather than to invent a different aggravator after trial. Presnell's unsubmitted different basis is therefore distinguishable. The state court's sufficiency terminology is not itself a constitutional harmlessness test, but Visciotti requires consideration of its complete supported adjudication rather than that phrase alone. Neither sufficient evidence nor sole indictment independently supplies the conclusion. The later federal evidence suggesting another participant and withheld inconsistent descriptions cannot silently become part of the earlier state record; any properly available distinct claim must satisfy its own review and factual-development requirements. Stone-Zsela would retain further inquiry into the omission itself. The Court concludes that the supported charge, trial theory and earlier governing law already permit the statutory determination. This sufficient gateway holding makes an independent new harmlessness determination unnecessary for the reviewed ground.

##### III. Independent sentence grounds retain their own force

**Controlling proposition:** Rejecting one ground for a conditional sentence writ does not resolve independent grounds that remain properly presented. The lower court must adjudicate those grounds and implement the sentence remedy, if any, that their actual disposition supports.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on the reviewed omission, leaving other district-court sentence grounds and preserved claims concerning sentencing assistance, denial of a preparation continuance and cumulative sentencing error for separate disposition. Their force cannot be inferred from rejection of the omission theory or from the already rejected guilt-phase claims. Any properly available distinct later-evidence theory likewise retains its own conditions. The remand must identify the grounds continuing to bar execution and provide the lawful sentence remedy warranted by their resolution.

The statutory gateway conclusively defeats this reviewed omission theory. Capital eligibility omissions are not categorically structural merely because they concern a death sentence. He would vacate the categorical rationale and require the lower court to resolve the 1996 filing-date gate, identify the constitutional law available at each state adjudication and evaluate any legally available harmlessness inquiry against the actual charge and verdict. The prisoner bears the applicable statutory gateway; where Chapman governs, the State must prove noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt. Sullivan's verdict-wide defect is distinct, while later Neder and Ring cannot create a 1992 or 1994 rule. Sole-defendant status matters with the actual charge and evidence and cannot replace every statutory finding. His proposed remand would specify each independently operative ground barring execution and the lawful sentence remedy, preserving any independently supported conditional writ.

The lower court must adjudicate the remaining independent sentence grounds, including the preserved assistance, continuance and cumulative-error alternatives, and determine the resulting sentence restraint and remedy. ### Boca Investerings Partnership v. United States

Merits decision, November 6, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews whether a claimed partnership conducting actual investments fails federal tax recognition because using the partnership form lacked a separate nontax business purpose. The D.C. Circuit rejected recognition while sustaining the district court's trial findings. The taxpayer seeks relief from that judgment; the Government seeks the challenged tax adjustments.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The court of appeals must apply the correct legal standard to the supported trial findings, preserving clear-error review of facts and resolving the distinct preserved adjustment theories. The mandate concerns the parties' tax years and adjustments under review.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Partnership recognition follows the actual joint enterprise

**Controlling proposition:** Federal partnership recognition under sections 761 and 7701(a)(2) turns on whether the participants genuinely intended to join in conducting an enterprise and sharing its economic results, assessed from their agreement, conduct, contributions, control, risk and actual relationship. A separate nontax need to use partnership form is not an additional prerequisite; tax avoidance remains evidence of an artificial relationship but does not alone disqualify a genuine enterprise. The ordinary tax burdens apply.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Culbertson requires examination of the whole undertaking, and Tower directs attention to the genuine relationship behind formal membership. Gregory permits scrutiny of whether the statutory arrangement actually exists. Real trades and sharing of receipts therefore do not compel partnership recognition, just as tax motivation does not replace the statutory inquiry. Gitlitz requires the Code's consequences when its prerequisites are satisfied; its rule cannot establish that those prerequisites exist here.

The D.C. Circuit accepted the district court's detailed findings after trial, including actual sharing of income, expenses, gains and losses, while making a nontax necessity for the elaborate partnership decisive. That requirement could reject a genuine enterprise merely because direct investment was possible. The Government's strongest response is that necessity expressed the supported absence of any real joint business purpose. But the repeated indispensable-necessity formulation, alongside acceptance of the findings, prevents affirmance on that relabeling alone. The created foreign entities, financing, planned exits, premiums, allocation sequence, structuring costs and actual exposure remain relevant to genuine enterprise intent. An absence of shared business purpose may be decisive on supported findings; absence of a need for partnership form is a different proposition. The accepted sharing findings likewise warrant their proper factual weight without deciding ultimate federal qualification.

##### Factual review and the separate adjustment theories

**Controlling proposition:** The appellate court must review the governing recognition standard independently while applying Rule 52's clear-error standard to trial findings. Entity nonrecognition and transaction-level sham are separate grounds: the former may assign recognized gains and losses to AHP, while the latter may eliminate those transactions' tax consequences if independently established.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court's findings cannot be displaced by contrary findings from another taxpayer's litigation. At the same time, a finding phrased as partnership intent does not immunize the ultimate legal qualification from review under Culbertson. The Government preserved its nontax-purpose argument in its trial memorandum. On remand the court must determine genuine enterprise status under the correct rule and address any properly preserved independent transaction ground, directing necessary factual work to the proper trial forum. Tax computations follow the legally supported disposition of those distinct grounds; rejection of the appellate prerequisite does not itself establish recognition or a refund.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Commissioner v. Culbertson: applied as the whole-relationship genuine-enterprise inquiry, without a separate necessity condition for partnership form.
- Commissioner v. Tower: applied to the substance of the participants' actual economic relationship.
- Gregory v. Helvering: preserved as authority to determine whether the statutory arrangement actually exists; formal labels and real trades alone remain insufficient.
- Gitlitz v. Commissioner: applied to statutory qualifications and consequences, opposing an independent anti-benefit condition while leaving partnership qualification to its own law.

#### Law After Decision

Genuine joint enterprise remains the governing recognition requirement. Tax motive, costly structuring and planned allocations are relevant evidence, while necessity for partnership form is not an independent condition. Trial facts retain ordinary appellate deference. Entity recognition and transaction validity must be resolved separately before the corresponding tax consequences are fixed.

#### Source Notes

Boca Investerings Partnership v. United States, 314 F.3d 625; district-court judgment, 167 F. Supp. 2d 298; 26 U.S.C. §§761 and 7701(a)(2); Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 52. The district court's findings followed a seventeen-day trial; the appellate court sustained those findings while adopting the legal prerequisite reviewed here.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The court of appeals must reconsider recognition on the supported facts under Culbertson, apply clear-error review to factual findings, and resolve preserved independent adjustment grounds. Any necessary factual proceedings and ensuing computations must follow the proper trial and tax procedures.

**End of entry: Boca Investerings Partnership v. United States, Merits decision, November 6, 2003.**

### Barnhart v. Thomas, No. 02-763

Merits decision, November 12, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. On review of the Third Circuit's judgment, the Court considers whether the Social Security Administration may deny an adult disability claim because the claimant can perform past relevant work without determining that the occupation currently exists in significant numbers in the national economy. Thomas's elevator-operator position ended, and the agency found retained capacity to perform that work. The Commissioner seeks reversal of the additional national-existence requirement; Thomas seeks reconsideration under that requirement.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Thomas is not entitled to a separate national-existence inquiry as a prerequisite to step-four denial. The lower court must implement the valid rule on the established capacity finding and resolve any remaining preserved challenge to that finding under ordinary substantial-evidence review. If none succeeds, the step-four denial stands.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Retained capacity for previous work may end the inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Under the adult disability definitions in sections 423(d)(2)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(B), the agency may deny a claim at step four when the claimant retains capacity to perform qualifying past relevant work, without separately proving that the occupation currently exists in significant numbers in the national economy. The claimant retains the applicable step-four burden; the agency must support the actual capacity finding and apply its valid regulations. National availability governs the subsequent other-work step.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; the Court sustains a permissible construction, not an exclusively mandated statutory interpretation.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute distinguishes inability to perform previous work from inability, considering age, education and work experience, to perform other substantial gainful work existing in the national economy. The word connecting those categories does not unambiguously carry the later qualification backward. The overall impairment-caused-inability definition leaves room for this separate capacity screen. Congress's exclusions of a particular vacancy, hiring likelihood and the claimant's immediate locality fit that structure, although those exclusions alone do not resolve nationwide obsolescence.

Section 405(a), incorporated for SSI through section 1383(d)(1), supplies the actual statutory assignment, and the binding five-step regulations exercise it. Mead requires both delegation and its actual exercise; Walton identifies both in this disability scheme, while Heimmermann preserves the enacted limits. Expertise, complexity or administrative longevity alone would not supply that authority.

Past relevant work demonstrates demands the claimant actually performed and offers a reasonable measure of retained functional ability. Thomas's strongest objection is that ability to do a vanished occupation may not establish ability to earn income in any existing job. That exposes the proxy's imperfect fit, but does not make the permissible distinction between impairment-caused incapacity and job disappearance irrational. Administrative practicality supports a choice the statute permits; it cannot override the statute. The required actual capacity and qualifying-past-work findings remain essential. The agency need not conduct the additional occupational-existence inquiry that the Third Circuit required.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Mead Corp.: applied through identified congressional delegation and exercise in binding regulations, preserving its distinction between administrative responsibility and controlling interpretive authority.
- Barnhart v. Walton: applied to permissible implementation within section 405(a) and its SSI incorporation; the same actual-delegation ground governs this different eligibility condition.
- Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.: applied to an authorized interpretive mechanism consistent with statutory limits; no general power arises from ambiguity or expertise alone.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: applied within the actual statutory assignment and permissible construction identified here.

#### Law After Decision

The valid adult-disability sequence may stop at retained capacity for qualifying past relevant work. The later national-economy inquiry does not become an additional step-four condition. The decision applies existing delegated-choice doctrine to this statutory screen while preserving supported capacity findings, valid regulatory qualifications and ordinary review.

#### Source Notes

Barnhart v. Thomas, No. 02-763; 540 U.S. 20; Thomas v. Commissioner, 294 F.3d 568; 42 U.S.C. §§405(a), 423(d)(2)(A), 1382c(a)(3)(B) and 1383(d)(1); 20 C.F.R. §§404.1520 and 416.920. Thomas's individual job ended on August 25, 1995; nationwide extinction of the occupation was asserted rather than found.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for implementation of the step-four rule and ordinary disposition of any remaining preserved capacity challenge. A labor-market survey is not required at that step; the denial stands unless a remaining challenge to the actual capacity determination succeeds.

**End of entry: Barnhart v. Thomas, Merits decision, November 12, 2003.**

### Washoe County v. United States

Merits decision, November 17, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews rejection of the claim that refusal of a federal-land pipeline right-of-way took the claimants' Nevada water rights. Nevada approved a proposed change from agricultural to municipal use, but the sole feasible municipal pipeline route crossed federal land. Federal processing stopped unless fixed Army and tribal objections were overcome. The claimants seek compensation; the United States seeks affirmance of summary judgment.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Summary judgment for the United States and denial of the claimants' partial-summary-judgment motion stand. The compensation action concludes under that judgment. The request for compensation and a remand to value the proposed municipal project are rejected.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The challenged federal position is sufficiently final

**Controlling proposition:** A claimant need not repeat an application when the responsible government has fixed its position and the established remaining use sufficiently defines the alleged restriction. The fixed opposition and absence of a further useful administrative step make this compensation claim sufficiently final for merits review.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Palazzolo requires an adequately defined governmental restriction rather than repetitive applications for their own sake. Here federal processing depended on concurrence from the Army and tribe, whose objections were fixed, and the retained agricultural use was known. The Government identified no remaining administrative step that could materially alter the position; a separate action to compel agency action is not another application necessary to understand the alleged burden. This is a record-based finality determination, not a general excuse from useful application requirements.

##### II. Refusal of an unowned route did not appropriate the water right

**Controlling proposition:** Refusing a new right-of-way across federal land does not physically take a claimant's water right where the claimant owns no crossing entitlement and the refusal neither appropriates the water nor restricts the existing lawful water use. Nevada's permission to change the place and manner of use does not itself grant an interest in federal land.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Nevada right is a protected right to use water. The State Engineer authorized a change in its use and location, while agricultural use remained available and was exercised pending perfection of the proposed municipal use. The United States did not divert water, reduce the quantity available from the ranch, restrict pumping or cut off access to the wells. Dugan and International Paper concern actual interference with water entitlements; they do not make a neighboring owner's refusal to convey a needed route an appropriation. The claimants emphasize that this was the only feasible municipal route and that the federal position preserved regional water conditions. Those circumstances explain the project's dependence on federal land, but establish neither an existing crossing right nor seizure of a quantity of the claimants' water. The property cannot be redefined as a municipal-only entitlement already incorporating that route.

##### III. The regulatory and complete-deprivation theories also fail

**Controlling proposition:** This refusal to convey a new federal-land interest establishes no compensable regulatory interference with the claimants' existing water entitlement, and their retained agricultural use defeats the asserted complete-deprivation category. Retained value alone does not defeat a genuine partial-taking claim; here the independent absence of appropriation or a supported restriction on the owned entitlement resolves that claim.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The challenged action concerns access to land the United States owns. The claimants retained pumping, well access, available quantity and lawful agricultural use; the asserted loss arose from inability to obtain the new federal route needed for the more valuable project. The Fifth Amendment does not require another landowner to furnish the missing interest or compensate for refusing it. That actual property-and-action determination is narrower than immunity for everything government calls proprietary.

Palazzolo preserves contextual review when a cognizable regulatory burden exists, and Tahoe-Sierra prevents turning a selected burdened strand into the entire property. Penn Central's combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry therefore remains controlling for such claims. The proposed narrower remand correctly observes that useful agriculture alone would not answer every partial-taking theory. It fails here because valuation cannot create the absent crossing entitlement or an unestablished restriction on the water right. The separate lawfulness of permit processing under the APA is outside this compensation question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Palazzolo v. Rhode Island: applied to a definitive governmental position and preserved for the distinction between actual property, complete deprivation and contextual compensation.
- Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency: applied against defining the hoped-for municipal-use strand as the whole protected interest; its preservation of contextual review remains effective.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City: retained for the combined inquiry where a cognizable regulatory burden is established; useful remaining property alone supplies no categorical defense.
- Dugan v. Rank: distinguished because it concerns actual diversion or interference with an owned water entitlement.
- International Paper Co. v. United States: distinguished because it concerns actual diversion or interference with an owned water entitlement.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council: its complete-deprivation category is inapplicable to the retained lawful agricultural use.

#### Law After Decision

The claim is final enough for adjudication but fails on the owned entitlement and federal action actually established. State authorization of a new water use does not include a federal crossing right. A project dependent on an ungranted route does not create compensation merely from the route's refusal, while ordinary physical and contextual takings protections remain for established burdens on property.

#### Source Notes

Washoe County v. United States, 319 F.3d 1320, decided January 28, 2003; rehearing denied March 27, 2003. The parties agreed that no material fact was disputed. The State Engineer's authorization changed the proposed use and location of the existing water entitlement; the claimants retained agricultural use and held no established federal crossing interest.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The compensation action concludes under the affirmed judgment for the United States. Any independently available APA challenge to permit processing remains outside this mandate.

**End of entry: Washoe County v. United States, Merits decision, November 17, 2003.**

### United States v. Ho

Merits decision, November 20, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews the Commerce Clause validity of applying the Clean Air Act's asbestos-removal work-practice and advance-notification requirements to Ho's intrastate commercial renovation. Ho bought a former hospital after asbestos disclosure, rejected licensed abatement bids and procured substitute paid removal work. The Fifth Circuit affirmed the two convictions, separately vacated the sentence and remanded. For the constitutional question it assumed that asbestos had not escaped; the sentence involved a distinct release inquiry.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Convictions affirmed against the preserved constitutional challenge, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The work-practice and notification convictions remain effective. The Fifth Circuit's distinct sentence vacatur and remand retain their existing grounds and scope; the original sentence is not reinstated. Acquittals and dismissed counts remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The regulated removal work directly participates in an interstate service market

**Controlling proposition:** Congress may regulate the asbestos-removal work practices in this commercial renovation because the regulated operation itself is produced and procured in an established interstate abatement-service market. Once that direct economic relationship and the enacted class's fit are supported, Congress may assess the class's aggregate market effects without requiring the individual project to produce an independently substantial interstate effect.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires a connection before magnitude; Wickard and Perez permit aggregate assessment of an actual economic class, and Hodel permits regulation of economic operations for environmental ends. Ho's licensed bids, cost-based rejection and paid substitute workers identify the very service whose production and cost the work standards regulate. Repeated avoidance of required precautions changes competitive abatement costs. Direct performance substituted for purchased abatement remains part of that service-market activity.

The enacted provisions regulate asbestos-removal work during covered facility renovation and the notice supporting it. Facility coverage reaches institutional, commercial, public and industrial structures, specified residential structures including condominiums and cooperatives, ships and active or inactive waste sites. It excludes apartment buildings of four or fewer units, does not classify a structure with a loft dwelling as residential on that account, and retains coverage of previously covered structures despite later use changes. Ho's hospital coverage is uncontested. This breadth is not rewritten as a commercial-building element.

Ho's strongest objection is that Home Builders forbids selecting a commercial project to disguise a differently defined noneconomic class. That rule remains controlling. Here removal itself is the procured and performed service, rather than unrelated conduct occurring within an owner's commercial surroundings. Property value and ownership do not supply the bridge. The demonstrated operation-level connection supports this application without adding a commerce element to the offense. Generalized national health costs do no work in that conclusion.

##### II. Preventive work standards and their notice duty may precede release

**Controlling proposition:** Valid regulation of the identified economic removal operation may prescribe preventive practices and advance notice without proof that the particular project released asbestos into ambient air or across a state boundary. The notice duty is sustained as directly ancillary to enforcement of those work standards; the Government must still establish the enacted offense elements beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Preventive standards regulate how the economic service is performed before the feared release occurs. Advance notice allows administration and enforcement at that stage. Requiring a proved cross-border injury would treat prevention as constitutionally unavailable even though the regulated activity already has the necessary market relationship. The absence of an actual release, assumed for this question, therefore does not defeat the power to regulate these practices and notice obligations.

The notice holding concerns its fitted relationship to the regulated operation, rather than aggregation of failures to report in general. An actual-release condition in a different offense retains its own role, and the separate sentencing finding under a different evidentiary standard supplies no element here. Holston's supported inclusion of local image production in a national prohibition remains a distinct mechanism: this direct service-market ground does not depend on importing its concealment, reproducibility or supply predicates. The constitutional validity of the separate sentence enhancements is outside the granted question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Lopez: applied to connection before aggregation, the actual regulated class and the prohibition on adding an unexpressed commerce element.
- National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt: applied and distinguished because this regulation addresses the actual abatement operation, rather than relying on commercial surroundings to characterize unrelated conduct; its support and fit requirements remain effective.
- Wickard v. Filburn: applied to class-wide effects of supported economic activity without an independently substantial effect from each instance.
- Perez v. United States: applied to class-wide effects of supported economic activity without an independently substantial effect from each instance.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association: applied to environmental regulation of an actual economic operation.
- United States v. Holston: preserved at its supported production-and-regime scope; the present direct-market holding uses a different established mechanism.

#### Law After Decision

The Commerce Clause permits preventive asbestos-removal and fitted advance-notice duties for the commercial removal operation supported here, without an individual release or cross-border-injury requirement. Courts must identify the regulated conduct and its supported direct market relationship before aggregation. The decision adds this application to existing Commerce doctrine; the enacted-class limitation and the requirement of proof of the statutory offenses remain effective.

#### Source Notes

United States v. Ho, 311 F.3d 589; 42 U.S.C. §§7412(h), 7414(a), 7413(c)(1) and 7413(c)(2)(B); 40 C.F.R. §§61.141 and 61.145. The constitutional question proceeds on the assumed absence of actual asbestos escape; the separate sentencing discharge finding has a different procedural and evidentiary basis.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower court implements the affirmed convictions and conducts the separately ordered sentencing proceedings within their preserved scope under existing jury-finding, acquittal and procedural rules.

**End of entry: United States v. Ho, Merits decision, November 20, 2003.**

### Valeria v. Davis, Ninth Circuit No. 01-15219

Merits decision, November 25, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews the rejection after a bench trial of the facial claim that Proposition 227 racially restructures access to educational policymaking by relocating bilingual-instruction authority from local institutions to the statewide process. The trial court found no discriminatory purpose in the pedagogical choice. Plaintiffs seek relief from the political restructuring; California seeks affirmance.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Judgment for California stands on the facial political-process claim, and the requested injunction on that theory is denied. Proposition 227 remains effective. The four dissenters would vacate and remand for the distinct structural inquiry rather than immediately invalidate the initiative.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SCALIA, KENNEDY, and THOMAS, JJ., joined. STEVENS, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined Part I. BREYER, J., filed an opinion dissenting in part, in which STEVENS, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Governing rule, joined by all nine | Racial political-process rule and distinction from neutral statewide policy |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Supports affirmance | Record-specific application |
| Opinion dissenting in part | Breyer | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Would vacate and remand | Joins Part I; seeks a proper structural application |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A racial political barrier requires its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** A statewide educational-policy change does not violate equal protection merely because the displaced program predominantly benefits racial minorities. A claimant must establish a racial classification, a deliberate racial proxy or racially selective obstruction of ordinary political participation; a prior adjudication of discrimination is not a prerequisite, and lack of racial hostility does not conclusively answer an established racial structure.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter and Seattle protect political access against decisionmaking allocations defined by the racial nature of the demand. Coalition for Economic Equity applies that protection to supported selective relocation of authority over otherwise lawful racial measures; calling the measure voluntary or a preference cannot end the inquiry. Crawford preserves neutral policy withdrawal and ordinary allocation of statewide educational authority. These decisions protect equal access, not victory on a preferred policy or an entitlement to every political level.

Demographic concentration and campaign references may bear on the mechanism, but do not alone establish race as the allocation criterion. Language proficiency is not automatically race; a deliberate proxy requires support. Conversely, an actual racial decisionmaking barrier need not be motivated by hostility. Under Adarand, an established racial mechanism requires an important lawful objective, an evidence-supported substantial relationship, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Educational importance alone would not complete that showing. Romer's class-wide exclusion from ordinary protection remains distinct from a neutral curricular choice. The absence of a prior remedial decree and the absence of animus therefore cannot operate as the categorical prerequisites used in portions of the lower court's reasoning.

##### II. This record establishes a linguistic instructional policy rather than a racial allocation

**Controlling proposition:** On the supported trial findings, text and operation presented here, Proposition 227's statewide instructional rule does not establish the required racial allocation of political authority. Judgment for California on this facial political-process claim is affirmed.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The initiative selects the method of teaching children with limited English proficiency, applying the same policy to every family seeking that method. Structured English immersion is normally intended as a transition not exceeding one year, followed by mainstream instruction upon proficiency. Every waiver requires parental consent. Waivers are available when the child already knows English; when a child at least ten would, in the school's judgment, receive better English education through an alternative; or after thirty days of immersion when the school finds special physical, emotional, psychological or educational needs and better educational development through an alternative. Twenty waivers in a grade at a school require an alternative class. Amendment requires electorate approval or a purpose-furthering statute passed by two-thirds of each house and signed by the Governor. These are real restrictions, not unlimited local discretion, but their operative criteria concern instruction and need rather than ancestry.

Plaintiffs and the dissent emphasize that bilingual education overwhelmingly serves minority pupils, campaign materials identified Latino children, and other educational choices remain locally available. Those facts establish the concentrated effect and selected subject of centralization; on this tried record they do not establish that race generated the authority rule. The supported finding concerning pedagogical purpose reinforces, but does not replace, examination of the actual political mechanism. The lower opinion's independently supportable subject-versus-race determination permits final judgment despite its erroneous categorical prerequisites. Coalition concerned expressly race-selected authority; this supported proficiency-based allocation does not present that mechanism. Another inquiry is therefore unnecessary to resolve the facial claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hunter v. Erickson: applied to racially selective political burdens without requiring entitlement to the underlying policy.
- Washington v. Seattle School District: applied to the racial nature of an authority allocation; protection is not confined to programs remedying a previously adjudicated violation.
- Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson: retained and applied against categorical exclusion of voluntary lawful racial measures; distinguished in the present application because the supported allocation concerns linguistic instruction rather than race-selected authority.
- Crawford v. Board of Education: applied to neutral policy withdrawal and statewide educational authority while preserving the distinct structural inquiry.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: its common substantial-justification standard remains the governing standard for an actual racial classification; this record fails to establish the required trigger.
- Romer v. Evans: preserved at its class-wide exclusion-from-protection scope; it does not independently invalidate every subject-specific statewide policy.

#### Law After Decision

Political-process protection requires proof of a racial decisionmaking mechanism and does not depend on a prior remedial decree or hostility alone. Neutral centralization of an instructional subject remains permissible even when its effects concentrate on minority families. Proposition 227 survives this facial equality claim on the supported absence of the required racial mechanism; independent federal educational and language-access duties retain their own force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Breyer dissents in part, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. They join the Court's governing legal rule but would vacate and remand because the lower court repeatedly treated absence of a prior discrimination remedy and racial animus as decisive. In their view its additional nonracial conclusion remains intertwined with those erroneous prerequisites, rather than independently applying Coalition's structural inquiry. Selective removal of bilingual-instruction authority, ordinary local control over comparable educational choices, the overwhelmingly minority population affected and the campaign record present a concrete question whether racial considerations defined the political allocation.

Their remand would examine the text and practical allocation, comparable decisions, context, waivers and amendment burdens on the developed record, respecting existing findings within their actual scope. It would not prescribe a new mandatory checklist or presume discrimination from demographics. If no racial mechanism is established, the State prevails. If one is established, the State must meet Adarand's common standard, and relief must address the proved unequal political barrier under ordinary remedial and state severability rules. The initiative would remain operative absent independently justified interim relief. They would neither direct a preferred curriculum nor declare the initiative unconstitutional on the present record.

#### Source Notes

Valeria v. Davis, 307 F.3d 1036, Ninth Circuit No. 01-15219; California Education Code §§305, 310, 311 and 335. The second amended complaint retained the equal-protection claim. The established pedagogical-purpose finding and the asserted structural allocation remain analytically distinct.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Judgment for California remains in place and the facial political-process claim concludes. The independent statutory claims omitted from the operative complaint are outside this judgment; any independently live claim must be identified and adjudicated under its own governing law.

### Raytheon Co. v. Hernandez, No. 02-749

Merits decision, December 2, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 8, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 298 F.3d 1030. The question is whether a neutral policy against rehiring employees separated for workplace misconduct supplies a legitimate nondiscriminatory reason in a rehabilitated drug user’s ADA disparate-treatment action. The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Raytheon while sustaining rejection of Hernandez’s untimely disparate-impact theory. Raytheon seeks judgment; Hernandez seeks an opportunity to prove intentional discrimination.

Souter took no part in the decision. Breyer took no part in consideration or decision. No reason for either absence is stated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 7–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Not participating in the decision:** Souter and Breyer.

The court of appeals must assess the remaining evidence of knowledge, pretext and intentional discrimination under the proper production-stage rule. The unchallenged prima facie determination remains undisturbed, and the untimely disparate-impact claim remains outside the remand.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A neutral misconduct policy supplies a legitimate explanation

**Controlling proposition:** A neutral, consistently applicable policy against rehiring employees separated for misconduct satisfies the employer’s burden to produce a legitimate nondiscriminatory reason in an ADA disparate-treatment action. Its legal sufficiency establishes neither its truthful application nor the actual motive; the employee retains the burden to prove intentional disability discrimination by a preponderance of the evidence.

**Authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined in full by the other six participating Justices, controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** McDonnell Douglas and Burdine separate production of a lawful explanation from persuasion about actual discrimination. Hazen Paper distinguishes a protected characteristic from an associated but analytically separate reason, while permitting proof that the separate criterion serves as a proxy. The ADA’s protection for qualifying persons with a history of addiction coexists with its treatment of workplace misconduct. Hernandez’s 1991 separation for the positive cocaine test is unchallenged; the disputed action is the refusal to rehire him in 1994. Raytheon’s asserted uniform misconduct policy supplies a lawful explanation for that decision. The lower court erred in treating the policy as legally unavailable because its operation could burden recovered addicts. That concern belongs to a distinct impact theory and cannot replace the motive showing required on the preserved claim.

##### II. The remaining evidence must receive a whole-record assessment

**Controlling proposition:** After the employer produces its explanation, a court must determine whether the whole record permits a reasonable finding that protected disability status caused the decision, drawing the inferences required on summary judgment without resolving credibility. A prima facie case and meaningful evidence of falsity may support that finding without a mandatory additional category of proof, but do not compel liability.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks rejects automatic judgment for an employee merely because an explanation is disbelieved; Reeves also rejects a demand for separate additional proof when the whole record reasonably supports discrimination. Those principles require consideration of Bockmiller’s testimony about the personnel file and submitted letters, her denial of knowledge and the recovery letter. Medina’s later explanation to the EEOC, which referred to drug use and rehabilitation, must be assessed for its connection to the decision rather than attributed automatically to Bockmiller or discarded merely because he was not the identified decisionmaker. The lawful policy label does not settle those evidentiary questions. If the record reasonably supports intentional discrimination, ordinary adjudication follows; if it permits no such finding, summary judgment is appropriate. The prima facie determination, including qualification, was not challenged in this Court and remains outside review. The parties’ assumed disability premise governs this litigation without defining the status of every former drug user.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green: applied to the sequence of prima facie proof, employer production and the remaining discrimination inquiry.
- Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine: applied to the employer’s production burden and the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden.
- St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks: applied to reject automatic liability from disbelief while preserving circumstantial inference.
- Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins: applied to distinguish a lawful independent criterion from a protected trait and to preserve proof of proxy use.
- Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products: applied to whole-record sufficiency without a mandatory additional category of evidence or judicial credibility choices.

#### Law After Decision

A lawful neutral misconduct policy can satisfy production in an ADA treatment case while leaving genuine motive and pretext for ordinary proof. Rehabilitation does not make the policy automatically unlawful, and a neutral label does not make its application conclusively nondiscriminatory. The established statutory definition of disability and the distinct elements of treatment and impact claims retain their force.

#### Source Notes

Raytheon Co. v. Hernandez, No. 02-749; 540 U.S. 44; Hernandez v. Hughes Missile Systems Co., 298 F.3d 1030; 42 U.S.C. §§12112 and 12114. The claim concerns the 1994 reemployment decision, and disability status is assumed for this litigation.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit for the knowledge, pretext and sufficiency inquiry under the announced rule, followed by the ordinary proceedings that inquiry supports. The unchallenged prima facie ruling and rejection of the untimely impact theory retain their effect.

**End of entry: Raytheon Co. v. Hernandez, Merits decision, December 2, 2003.**

### United States v. Banks, No. 02-473

Merits decision, December 2, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 15, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 282 F.3d 699. The question is whether officers executing a cocaine-search warrant violated the Fourth Amendment or 18 U.S.C. §3109 by forcing entry after loud announcement and an unanswered fifteen-to-twenty-second interval. The Ninth Circuit ordered suppression after Banks’s conditional guilty plea. The United States seeks reversal; Banks seeks exclusion and relief from that conviction.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The waiting-time and property-damage objections do not support suppression under the Fourth Amendment or section 3109. The conditional conviction is not defeated on this entry ground; the independent interrogation and counsel rulings outside the grant retain their effect.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The announced entry was reasonable in its actual circumstances

**Controlling proposition:** After meaningful notice during execution of a search warrant, officers may force entry when contemporaneous circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further waiting will permit imminent destruction of the evidence sought. The Government must justify that exception; property damage does not require a higher suspicion threshold, but the means and extent of damage remain independently reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer’s opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices, controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson makes announcement part of reasonable home entry. Richards requires particular circumstances connecting delay to threatened evidence loss, and Ramirez rejects a higher exigency tier merely because entry causes damage. Here officers loudly announced their authority and search purpose at about 2 p.m. at a small two-bedroom apartment. A warrant supported the presence of readily disposable cocaine, and fifteen to twenty seconds passed without a response. Together those circumstances supported a reasonable suspicion that further delay would allow disposal. The officers were not required to hear flushing or eliminate every innocent explanation before acting.

Banks’s strongest objection is that an innocent occupant may need more time to reach the door. But the exigency concerns time to destroy the cocaine, not simply walking distance; the small dwelling supplies context rather than a universal measure. His shower use was unknown to the officers and cannot determine their contemporaneous justification. The necessary door entry was reasonable on this record. The lower court’s four-category scheme, including a heightened threshold for destructive entry, cannot replace that actual assessment. When exigency is absent, avoidable damage may require greater patience for admission. This holding supplies neither a fixed fifteen-second rule nor a categorical exception for narcotics warrants, and preserves independent restraint on unnecessary or disproportionate damage.

##### II. Section 3109 preserves the supported destruction exception

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate an officer or a person assisting execution. Its common-law announcement rule also permits entry after notice without actual or inferred refusal when particular circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further delay will permit imminent destruction of the warranted evidence; the Government must establish those circumstances.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller supplies the statute’s common-law background, and Sabbath explains its codification of that protection. Ramirez applied that method to a supported danger exception. The same method supports the destruction qualification presented here: a rule protecting peaceful entry does not require officers, after notice, to wait while the specifically sought evidence is about to disappear. Wilson and Richards supply the historical protection and particularized threshold; the statutory conclusion rests on the statute’s common-law content rather than automatic incorporation of every constitutional exception.

The circumstances supporting the constitutional holding also establish this bounded statutory excuse after actual announcement. No inference of refusal is required. That distinction preserves the ordinary refusal requirement rather than treating silence as refusal merely because drugs can be disposed of quickly. The Court now resolves the destruction application left open in Ramirez. Generic drug risk alone remains insufficient, and the adopted exception does not dispense with meaningful notice in every drug search.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas: applied to announcement protection and justified countervailing circumstances during home entry.
- Richards v. Wisconsin: applied to particularized reasonable suspicion, contemporaneous justification and rejection of a categorical narcotics exception; its existing review allocation remains unchanged.
- United States v. Ramirez: applied to reject a damage-based higher exigency threshold and preserve independently reasonable force. Its statutory common-law method is extended from supported danger to the destruction circumstance decided here.
- Miller v. United States: applied for the statutory common-law background, without attributing this destruction holding to that earlier decision.
- Sabbath v. United States: applied for codification of common-law announcement; its treatment of breaking and unsupported danger retains its force.

#### Law After Decision

The combined circumstances of this announced warrant execution justify the elapsed interval and necessary door entry. Courts continue to assess waiting time and force contextually. Section 3109 now expressly permits the supported post-notice destruction exception, independently of actual or implied refusal, while retaining its ordinary notice-and-refusal rule and separate liberation provision.

#### Source Notes

United States v. Banks, No. 02-473; 540 U.S. 31; 282 F.3d 699; U.S. Const., Amdt. 4; 18 U.S.C. §3109. The search occurred at about 2 p.m.; the officers did not know Banks was showering.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit for proceedings consistent with lawful entry under both governing sources. The reviewed entry objections must be rejected, with any independently preserved issue resolved in its proper posture.

**End of entry: United States v. Banks, Merits decision, December 2, 2003.**

### Van Susteren v. Jones, Ninth Circuit No. 01-57210

Merits decision, December 4, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit’s affirmance of summary judgment sustaining California Elections Code §8001(a)(2). The granted question is whether the one-year prefiling disaffiliation requirement for a congressional primary candidate violates association or equal protection under the First and Fourteenth Amendments. Van Susteren sought the Libertarian nomination in the March 2002 primary after Republican registration during the preceding year. The recurring ballot-access controversy remains live after that election. He seeks declaratory and prospective access relief.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0, on both granted grounds.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested declaratory and prospective ballot-access relief against this application of the twelve-month rule is denied. The lower court’s defense judgment remains effective.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This prospective separation requirement reasonably regulates primary access

**Controlling proposition:** A State may require a reasonable prospective period of disaffiliation before entry into another party’s official primary when the complete system preserves realistic access and the condition proportionately serves identified interests in party integrity and orderly selection. On this record, California’s twelve-month prefiling requirement imposes a real but less-than-severe, nondiscriminatory burden justified by its direct connection to stable nomination routes and prevention of opportunistic switching.

**Authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Burdick requires assessment of the actual injury, the State’s precise interests and their fit. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions may rest on proportionate important regulatory interests. Van Susteren loses his desired immediate nomination opportunity. Continued advocacy or a theoretical alternative route does not erase that injury. But the prospective separation rule permits ordinary entry after the neutral period and structures movement into a primary before its competitive selection. Its operation does not make the official process realistically inaccessible in the system presented.

Storer’s approval of a one-year separation condition for independent candidates supplies a close preventive analogy, rather than an automatic answer about party primaries. Timmons permits fitted conditions on official nomination access. Tashjian and Eu protect actual associational choice, while Jones protects an objecting party from compelled outsider control of its nominee selection. The present condition does not replace that choosing electorate or dictate party governance. The objection that a willing candidate and party should be able to associate immediately therefore does not eliminate every proportionate entry rule. The direct relationship between prior separation and stable selection supports this condition without a finding that Van Susteren personally planned a raid. The holding concerns this duration and nomination system; materially different periods, discriminatory waivers or restrictions imposed after candidacy has matured require their own burden-and-fit assessment. A materially different party-endorsed candidacy record and the separate Qualifications Clause claim are outside the granted question.

##### II. The calendar difference follows the distinct selection routes

**Controlling proposition:** The approximately twenty-three-month and thirteen-month intervals before the general election asserted for partisan and independent candidates do not establish an equal-protection violation here. The primary and independent routes have distinct operative selection stages, and requiring separation before the relevant prefiling stage reasonably serves the rule’s identified nomination interests.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The comparison identifies a genuine calendar difference. It cannot be rejected merely by labeling one candidate partisan and the other independent. But measuring solely backward from the general election omits the earlier primary at which the party’s official selection occurs. The requirement’s relation to that selection and its filing process explains the different general-election intervals. Burdick’s complete-system inquiry and Storer’s separation rationale support that functional comparison. The Court sustains the difference because of its role in the actual routes, rather than assuming equal electoral prospects or treating the association interest as unprotected.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Anderson v. Celebrezze: applied to actual burden, precise interests and proportionate fit within the complete nomination system.
- Burdick v. Takushi: applied to actual burden, precise interests and proportionate fit within the complete nomination system.
- Storer v. Brown: its one-year separation rationale is extended to this closely analogous primary-entry setting, with the present burden separately assessed.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party: applied to reasonable preventive nomination conditions; its particular fusion holding remains bounded by its own system.
- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut: its protection of meaningful party choice is preserved and distinguished from the neutral entry condition here.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee: its protection of meaningful party choice is preserved and distinguished from the neutral entry condition here.
- California Democratic Party v. Jones: distinguished because the disaffiliation rule does not impose outsider control of an objecting party’s nomination.

#### Law After Decision

California may apply this one-year prospective primary-entry requirement in the system reviewed. A claimant’s immediate preference does not displace a justified selection condition, while actual burden, realistic access and fit continue to govern under Burdick. Comparison between candidacy routes accounts for their operative selection stages as well as their calendar differences.

#### Source Notes

Van Susteren v. Jones, 331 F.3d 1024; California Elections Code §8001(a)(2); U.S. Const., Amdts. 1 and 14. The Qualifications Clause claim was decided below but lies outside this Court’s grant.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The defense judgment stands and the requested prospective relief is denied. Election officials may continue applying the reviewed requirement within its sustained scope.

**End of entry: Van Susteren v. Jones, Merits decision, December 4, 2003.**

### Virginia v. Maryland, No. 129, Original

Merits decision, December 9, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 7, 2003. On exceptions to the Special Master’s report. The Court determines whether Maryland may require permits for Virginia’s protected withdrawals and improvements appurtenant to the Virginia shore of the Potomac, and whether prior permit practice established acquiescence in that authority. Virginia seeks a declaration covering itself, its subdivisions and its citizens; Maryland excepts to the recommended declaration. Issuance of the individual Fairfax permit leaves the continuing sovereign permitting dispute live. The controversy includes the nontidal river; Maryland’s exceptions concern regulatory authority and acquiescence.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Exceptions overruled; declaratory decree entered, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Kennedy.

The seven supporting Justices join rejection of both exceptions and the complete decree. Stevens and Kennedy would sustain the regulatory-authority exception and dismiss the broad exemption claim; their conditional assessment of acquiescence does not change those votes. The decree’s complete operative terms appear in Mandate, Remedy, and Stage below.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The interstate instruments preserve use independent of Maryland’s unilateral permission

**Controlling proposition:** The congressionally approved settlement reserves Virginia’s riparian use beyond its low-water boundary and does not subject that use to Maryland’s unilateral licensing veto. Virginia, its subdivisions and citizens may make the protected withdrawals and improvements appurtenant to the Virginia shore without the challenged Maryland permits, subject to navigation, Maryland’s proper use and applicable federal law.

**Authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins control both holdings and the decree.

**Controlling explanation:** The Black-Jenkins Award gives Virginia full dominion to low water and reserves use beyond that line necessary to full enjoyment of its riparian ownership, subject to navigation and Maryland’s proper use. Maryland’s riverbed title and that express reservation must both have effect. Requiring Maryland’s prior permission would subordinate Virginia’s reserved use to the other party’s unilateral decision about its needs.

The preserved portion of Article Seventh of the 1785 Compact protects adjoining shore property, its benefits and wharves or improvements without obstructing or injuring navigation. Its common-fishing clause separately prohibits hindrance or disturbance of the other shore’s fisheries and fishing with nets or seines on that shore. Historical Article Eighth required mutual consent for its specified fish-preservation, quarantine, channel, navigation and ballast regulations; that allocation informs the bargain rather than creating present mutual-consent authority over every subject. The 1958 Compact replaced the older Compact, but Article VII §1 preserved existing riparian rights and the relevant judicial constructions without enlargement or diminution. Article VII §2 continues Maryland Code Article 66C’s December 1, 1958 rules for finfish, crabs, oysters and clams in the Potomac, except as changed by Commission regulations under the Compact. Section 3 continued licenses and associated rights existing at compact effectiveness to catch those species and other shellfish for six months, then abrogated them. Neither alters §1. Article IX replaced the former Compact sixty days after the final legally necessary act. Article VIII bars unilateral alteration. Congress retains federal rights, jurisdiction and commerce power and reserves authority to alter, amend or repeal its consent.

The dissent correctly observes that ordinary property rights coexist with reasonable regulation. The controlling distinction is the Award’s interstate reservation to Virginia itself, not a general immunity for private riparian property. Navigation and proper-use limits remain enforceable through lawful interstate and federal remedies; they do not confer the unilateral permit power claimed here. New Jersey requires fidelity to the actual compact allocation, and Arizona requires the actual predicates before distinct interstate rights are displaced. Neither title alone nor an assumed general water-allocation rule replaces this settlement.

##### II. The permit history does not establish surrendered authority

**Controlling proposition:** Applications for permits and administrative cooperation do not establish acquiescence in displacement of an interstate entitlement without supported acceptance of the adverse authority claimed. Considered with the official awareness and protest history, the permit practice here does not establish Virginia’s surrender of the reserved authority, and Maryland’s acquiescence exception is overruled.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion. The dissenters’ conditional observation is not an additional join.

**Controlling explanation:** New Jersey requires attention to actual adverse exercise, notice and acceptance. Maryland relies on repeated applications, permits and amendments as practical acceptance of its authority. Those acts matter, but cooperating to secure a project while contesting sovereign authority is different from accepting displacement of the protected entitlement. The record places official awareness in 1973 and protests from 1976, alongside the Fairfax applications and later contested refusal. That course does not establish the accepted surrender Maryland asserts. Arizona likewise illustrates why the actual procedural and substantive predicates matter before a defined interstate demand is barred.

#### Precedent Treatment

- New Jersey v. New York: applied to the distinction between territorial title and separately allocated rights, and to supported adverse exercise, notice and acquiescence; its instrument-specific territorial holding remains intact.
- Arizona v. California: applied at its requirement to identify the actual entitlement and predicates for procedural displacement; its existing allocations and decree limits remain intact.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming: its distinction between enforcing established interstate obligations and creating a new allocation supports relief faithful to this settlement.

#### Law After Decision

The protected Potomac withdrawals and appurtenant improvements rest on the interstate settlement rather than Maryland’s unilateral permission. The declaration governs the covered class of Virginia activities, while navigation, proper Maryland use and applicable federal law continue to limit their exercise. Prior cooperation has not transferred the reserved authority. Concrete disputes about those continuing limits remain subject to ordinary federal interstate remedies.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy, joined by Stevens, would sustain Maryland’s regulatory-authority exception and dismiss the broad exemption claim. They read the instruments as securing an enforceable riparian entitlement within Maryland’s river sovereignty, subject to compatible regulation. A later determination of title can confirm preexisting sovereignty; historical disagreement does not itself show that Maryland lacked regulatory authority. The Award’s full dominion over the shore and qualified use beyond it are distinct.

The dissent emphasizes that protected property and use ordinarily coexist with police-power regulation. An advance permit can test compliance with navigation and proper-use limits while remaining subordinate to the federal entitlement. The specific mutual-consent provisions also show that regulatory authority could be expressly allocated. Maryland therefore could not use a permit to nullify protected access, suppress Virginia’s development for its own sake or impose an unrelated policy veto, and judicial relief would remain available against such interference. But those constraints do not invalidate every compatible licensing requirement. Nor does ordinary interstate-water apportionment automatically answer the regulatory question for a river within one State’s territorial title.

They agree only conditionally that the supported permit history would not independently establish surrender if the instruments created the exemption the Court finds. Because they reject that antecedent construction, this observation supplies no join in the Court’s sovereignty or acquiescence opinion.

#### Source Notes

Virginia v. Maryland, No. 129, Original; 540 U.S. 56; 28 U.S.C. §1251(a); Compact of 1785, Articles Seventh and Eighth; Black-Jenkins Award of 1877, approved March 3, 1879, 20 Stat. 481; Potomac River Compact of 1958, approved October 10, 1962, 76 Stat. 797.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Court enters this decree, effective upon entry:

1. Virginia, its governmental subdivisions and its citizens exercising the riparian rights reserved by the governing instruments may withdraw water from the Potomac and construct and maintain improvements appurtenant to the Virginia shore without obtaining the challenged Maryland permits. Maryland must cease conditioning those covered activities on its unilateral permit requirement.
2. Exercise of those rights remains subject to the prohibition on obstructing or injuring navigation, the Award’s restriction against interfering with Maryland’s proper use, and applicable federal law and reserved federal rights and jurisdiction.
3. The Court retains jurisdiction to give effect to and enforce this decree, including adjudication of supported disputes concerning compliance with its terms. Concrete navigation or proper-use disputes must be resolved under those limits rather than through the rejected unilateral permit condition.

**End of entry: Virginia v. Maryland, Merits decision, December 9, 2003.**

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### National-party receipt and spending: section 323(a)

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may close a demonstrated party fundraising channel used to circumvent lawful federal candidate-contribution restrictions when the government establishes an objectively grounded important corruption or circumvention interest and a close fit preserving meaningful political association. Section 323(a) satisfies that standard for national committees, including congressional campaign committees, their directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained or controlled entities, and officers or agents acting on their behalf: solicitation, receipt, direction and spending must comply with federal amount, source and reporting rules, even when the funds are designated for state or local elections.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Lamb permits prevention based on actual resources, tacit donor understandings and relevant experience without a completed bribe, a new local study or proof of legislators' subjective reliance on particular evidence. Colorado II supplies the supported circumvention principle; Shrink Missouri and Day require close fit and meaningful use of the chosen association. Here federal-officeholder fundraising, donor credits, allocation arrangements and national-to-state transfers establish an integrated candidate-financing institution. The obligation can arise when the money is solicited or received; allocating its later use to a state project does not remove that connection.

The objection that the rule burdens the party's own independent and state expression is substantial. The restriction addresses the supported receipt channel and preserves meaningful federal fundraising and lawful individual and political-committee contributions. It does not classify all party expression as coordinated. A second unlimited account would reopen the same demonstrated channel. Ordinary access, lobbying, policy agreement, party identity, donor prominence and unequal influence alone would not establish the justification; government retains the burden on a materially different application. The lower court's broad invalidation is reversed and its residual sustaining result affirmed on this receipt-and-source ground.

##### State and local registration, turnout and the Levin accommodation

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 323(b) may require federal financing for voter registration from 120 days before a regularly scheduled federal election through election day, and voter identification, get-out-the-vote or generic campaign activity in an election in which a federal candidate is on the ballot. Generic activity promotes a party rather than a federal or nonfederal candidate. Covered actors include state, district and local party committees, their directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained or controlled entities, officers or agents acting for such committees or entities, and associations or similar groups of state/local candidates or officeholders. The statutory exclusions remain: public communications referring solely to a clearly identified state/local candidate that are not registration or turnout/generic activity; contributions to state/local candidates not designated for federal-election activity; state/local conventions; and grassroots materials naming or depicting only a state/local candidate.

The Levin allocation option applies only to the first two activity classes. It requires no reference to a federal candidate; no broadcast, cable or satellite cost except a communication referring solely to a clearly identified state/local candidate; compliance with state law; and no more than $10,000 from one donor to one committee in a calendar year, with controlled-person aggregation. The spending committee must raise the funds itself. Funds from another state/district/local committee, a national or national congressional committee, their representative officers or agents, or their controlled entities are excluded. The specially permitted nonfederal amounts may not be solicited, received, directed, transferred or spent by or in the name of section 323(a) or (e) actors. Joint fundraising by two or more state/district/local committees or their agents, or by a committee for one in another State, is excluded. Section 323(e)(3)'s permission to attend, speak or appear as a featured guest remains. These cumulative conditions govern that allocation option, rather than every lawful federal-fund transfer.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado II and Lamb permit safeguards against documented donor directions and transfers through shared federal-election machinery. That machinery supplies the connection here; the rule does not rest merely on a possibility that a state voter may eventually vote federally. Defined activity categories, the state-only exclusions and modified allocation preserve substantial state activity. Own fundraising and transfer restrictions prevent multiplication of the per-committee allowance into the national channel that section 323(a) closes.

The contrary argument correctly identifies burdens on local pooling and association. Day requires the government to justify those burdens rather than point to some other speaker or demand proof that the party has become silent. The demonstrated shared financing route and the confined mixed-fund option satisfy that burden on this facial record. An organizer's predominantly local purpose does not sever the actual federal-ballot mobilization connection. The district court's invalidation of these two classes and their associated Levin operation is reversed. Concrete applications that demonstrate disabling associational burdens remain governed by the same government-borne standard.

##### Federal-candidate communications by parties and state officials

**Holding and operative rule:** The class in section 301(20)(A)(iii), applied through section 323(b), covers a public communication referring to a clearly identified federal candidate and promoting, supporting, attacking or opposing that candidate for that office, whether or not it also identifies a state/local candidate or expressly advocates a vote. Mere mention, policy inquiry or incidental electoral effect alone does not satisfy that meaning. Public communications include broadcast, cable, satellite, newspaper, magazine, outdoor advertising, general-public political advertising, mass mailing and telephone banks. The last two categories require more than 500 identical or substantially similar mailed/faxed pieces or calls within 30 days. Section 323(f) applies the federal-funding rule to such communications by a state/local candidate or officeholder or their agent, subject to its own exception for a communication connected with that person's state/local election referring only to that person, other candidates for the same state/local office held or sought, or both.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The candidate-for-office predicate identifies the federal financing channel. Colorado II and Lamb permit source safeguards directed to that channel; Bartlett and Clifton preserve genuine issue discussion and ordinary policy inquiries. The same source may otherwise migrate to an unrestricted state-candidate account. The statutory communication and actor conditions, together with the own-election exception, supply the required bounds.

The principal objection is that a party or state candidate speaks independently even when praising a federal candidate. This holding regulates the supported funding route without converting all such expression into coordination. The government must establish the enacted predicates and justify a materially different burden under the governing fit rule. Neither a mandatory vocabulary of electoral slogans nor a subjective-intent prosecution is adopted. The district court's sustaining judgments on class (iii) and section 323(f) are affirmed.

##### Full-salary consequence after the monthly federal-work threshold

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment invalidating section 301(20)(A)(iv), as applied through section 323(b), is vacated and remanded. When an employee of a state, district or local party committee spends more than 25 percent of compensated time in a month on activities in connection with a federal election, the Act subjects the whole covered salary to federal financing. The government must establish that this full-payroll consequence closely fits the supported circumvention mechanism and preserves meaningful state-party association; validating the other activity classes does not establish that fit. Adequate existing evidence may suffice. If the government cannot justify the actual operation, the district court must give corresponding relief, severing applications only where statutory independence permits.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion; direct joins by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, including the author (5 Justices), controlling at the exact scope stated.

**Controlling explanation:** Lamb separates proof of a legitimate preventive mechanism from justification of the burden actually imposed. Stengel's fit inquiry likewise requires examination of the challenged aggregate operation. Employees materially engaged in shared federal work present a real financing relation. That relation does not itself explain why crossing a quarter-time threshold requires federal funding of the remaining compensation.

The competing lower conclusions do not resolve this inquiry under the governing government-borne standard. The administrative advantage of preventing artificial division of staff duties must be tested against the whole-payroll burden and meaningful local-party operation. Conversely, indirect effects on state activity alone do not establish invalidity. The remand applies that standard to the actual operation and available evidence. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas directly join this ground and remedy while preferring broader invalidation.

##### Tax-exempt recipients and federally compliant funds: section 323(d)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 323(d) survives for its nonfederal-fund applications, but solicitation, donation or direction of funds complying with federal safeguards is protected by application severance under section 401. The enacted words cover any funds; the remedy does not rewrite them as a soft-money-only definition. It covers national, state, district and local party committees, including national congressional campaign committees, entities directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained or controlled by such a committee or its agent, and officers or agents acting for the committee or entity. Covered recipients are section 501(c) organizations exempt under section 501(a), or applicants for that status, making expenditures or disbursements in connection with a federal election, including federal-election activity; and section 527 organizations other than political committees, state/district/local party committees or authorized state/local candidate committees. Independent organizational activity outside these party-channel restrictions remains protected.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope. All nine directly join the severed protection for federally compliant solicitation, donation and direction; only the six named sustaining Justices join the nonfederal residue.

**Controlling explanation:** The satellite-organization record, including use of a nominal ballot organization for partisan registration, supports a substitute conduit for prohibited party money. Lamb and Colorado II allow a source-specific response to that demonstrated route. The defined recipients, representative-capacity limit and exceptions confine it.

Federally compliant solicitation and donations present a different burden. The government has not justified excluding that lawful funding channel merely because the recipient also engages in federal-election activity. Day and Mariani require attention to the chosen association; another fund operated by the recipient is not automatically an adequate substitute. Section 401 permits the unconstitutional compliant-fund applications to be severed while the nonfederal applications remain independently operative. The three Justices seeking full invalidation directly join the compliant-fund protection, but not the approval of the nonfederal residue. The lower injunction is affirmed for compliant funds and reversed for the surviving nonfederal applications.

##### Federal officeholders and candidates: section 323(e)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 323(e) is sustained on the federal candidate's or officeholder's own solicitation, direction, receipt, transfer or spending connection, including agents and entities directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained or controlled by, or acting on behalf of, one or more such candidates or officeholders. The federal-election branch requires federal source, amount and reporting compliance; the other-election branch imports the source restrictions and section 315(a)(1)–(3) amount limits rather than all federal reporting duties.

The own-state/local-candidacy exception applies to an individual who is or was also such a candidate, soliciting, receiving or spending solely in connection with that election, as state law permits, and referring only to that candidate, other candidates for the state/local office sought by that candidate, or both. Attendance, speaking and featured-guest participation at state/district/local-party fundraising events remain permitted. General solicitations for section 501(c) organizations exempt under section 501(a), or applying for exemption, may not specify use and exclude entities principally devoted to the defined registration, voter-identification, turnout or generic activities. Specific solicitations for those activities, or for entities principally conducting them, may be made only to individuals and for no more than $20,000 from each in a calendar year.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The supported donor obligation attaches to the official's personal fundraising intervention, even when the recipient spends in another election. Lamb recognizes actual resources and tacit conduits without a formal earmark or a completed bribe; Mariani supplies the actual-source distinction. The record of large donations obtained and credited through federal officials supports this connection more directly than party identity or ordinary access alone.

The contrary argument treats a state-election or charitable destination as wholly unrelated to the official's federal position. It overlooks the actual requester-donor relationship. The distinct statutory branches and express candidacy, participation and charitable exceptions preserve ordinary political and charitable association. All nine adopt this particular source-and-amount safeguard. The district court's facial sustaining judgment is affirmed.

##### Primary electioneering definition and treasury-funded electoral advocacy

**Holding and operative rule:** The primary definition is sustained and the facial challenge to sections 203 and 204 rejected subject to enforceable independent-speech and nonprofit protections. Coverage requires broadcast, cable or satellite communication identifying a federal candidate within 60 days before a general, special or runoff election or 30 days before a primary, preference election, nominating convention or caucus for the office sought. For candidates other than President or Vice President, reception by at least 50,000 persons in the House district or Senate State supplies the relevant-electorate requirement. The news, commentary and editorial exception excludes facilities owned or controlled by a political party, political committee or candidate. Already covered expenditures and independent expenditures are separately excluded; qualifying regulated debates/forums and communications solely promoting them by or for their sponsors are excluded. A regulatory exemption cannot include the otherwise covered section 301(20)(A)(iii) class.

Covered corporations and unions may be required to finance genuine electoral advocacy through lawful voluntarily supported segregated funds rather than their treasuries. The statutory definition does not conclusively establish constitutionally suppressible advocacy. Under Bartlett the whole message, context, actual audience request and financing must be examined; a reasonable nonelectoral interpretation remains constitutionally material. Election proximity, candidate identification, potential voter influence or opposition to a legislator's policy alone cannot defeat genuine independent issue advocacy. Actual electoral advocacy requires no rigid slogan list. Government must justify the electoral and financing connection. Genuine press activity and natural persons' independent political expenditures retain their constitutional protection.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Austin remains authority for the surviving treasury-funded electoral-advocacy setting. Its concern with resources accumulated through the commercial structure, rather than voluntary political support, is distinct from equalizing influence or labeling every independent supporter corrupt. The record of commercial and union financing of candidate-centered broadcasts through nominal issue sponsors, the objective coverage conditions and meaningful voluntary funding routes support the facial result. Corporate form and a nominal alternative fund alone would not suffice.

Bartlett protects genuine independent issue discussion; Maine Right to Life rejects reliance on a mandatory set of electoral words while retaining definite coverage. A request to contact a senator about impending legislation therefore requires constitutional examination different from an appeal to replace the senator in the election. The genuine-issue examples and disputed advertising estimates do not establish that every covered message may be suppressed. The facial judgment preserves enforceable application protection rather than resolving future advertisements in advance. Beaumont's direct-contribution holding cannot supply a rule equating independent expenditure with candidate financing. The primary-definition invalidation is reversed; section 203 operates under that definition and these constitutional limits instead of the district court's enlarged fallback.

##### Functional nonprofit protection and the statutory withdrawal

**Holding and operative rule:** Withdrawal of the statutory nonprofit exception under section 204 does not withdraw MCFL and Day's constitutional protection for a functionally qualifying voluntary political association. The claimant must establish its actual characteristics: economic claimants, material commercial accumulation, material corporate or union financing or control, and whether advocacy funds reflect voluntary political support. Incidental commercial activity or an imperfect formal refusal policy alone does not defeat protection; tax status and ideological purpose alone do not establish it.

The separate statutory exception in section 203 concerns section 501(c)(4) entities, including applicants, and section 527(e)(1) organizations using exclusively funds provided directly by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents. Prohibited-entity-origin funds do not qualify. Direct or indirect payment by a prohibited entity invokes attribution; a section 501(c)(4) entity with business receipts or prohibited-source funds must use the individual-only section 304(f)(2)(E) account to avoid that attribution. Contracts count and tax-law restrictions remain. Section 204 withdraws that statutory exception for communications distributed from a television or radio station or cable/satellite television provider, with the relevant-electorate requirement for candidates other than President or Vice President. Outside the constitutional class, the withdrawal survives by the six-Justice treasury coalition.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope. All nine join functional constitutional protection. Only Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join survival of the statutory withdrawal outside that class.

**Controlling explanation:** MCFL protects the distinct voluntary political association; Day requires a functional inquiry rather than a literal paperwork checklist. Stengel preserves the distinction between independent expression and direct candidate gifts. The government's anti-conduit argument has force when material business financing or control is present. It does not answer an association whose incidental receipts or formal defects leave its advocacy resources genuinely voluntary.

The lower decree's nonprofit protection is retained and modified to state this functional class. Application to an organization depends on its own characteristics and communication. All nine join the constitutional protection. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas continue to oppose the expanded treasury trigger outside that class; their join here does not sustain that separate restriction.

##### The enacted fallback and its actual activation

**Holding and operative rule:** The district court's fallback declaration and deletion of the final narrowing condition are vacated. The statute activates the fallback only if a final judicial decision holds the primary definition constitutionally insufficient. The Court sustains the primary scheme on the grounds and with the protections stated here, so the condition is not met. The fallback's text remains complete: a broadcast, cable or satellite communication must promote, support, attack or oppose a candidate and have no plausible meaning other than an exhortation to vote for or against a specific candidate; section 100.22(b)'s interpretation and application are preserved. The fallback contains neither the primary time windows nor its geographic threshold.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five-Justice authority at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 401 permits severance of provisions or applications, but does not authorize deletion of a limiting condition to enlarge regulation. The lower remedy removed Congress's cumulative restriction. Because the Court's actual primary disposition does not activate the alternative, the lower alternative declaration has no present operative function. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also oppose deletion of the limiter, but their preferred primary treasury disposition would require a complete backup analysis. Their conditional construction is stated in their separate writing rather than treated as an operative rule. Stone-Zsela agrees with this fallback disposition without adding a ground.

##### Actual spending coordination and enforceable requested resources

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 202 and 214(a) survive facial review where actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request, suggestion or another legally sufficient spending connection supplies candidate or party campaign resources. A written agreement or formal collaboration is unnecessary. Shared objectives, endorsement, ordinary policy inquiries, party affiliation, access, vendor identity or former employment alone do not establish coordination. An enforceable coordinated purchase commitment that already supplies an actual requested campaign resource may be classified as a contribution and expenditure before airing or payment; all nine directly support that financial-resource proposition.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope. All nine join the enforceable requested-resource contract proposition and ordinary-inquiry/identity exclusions. Only the six named sustaining Justices join the complete facial sustaining ground and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado I protects actual independence; Colorado II and Lamb recognize real resources and tacit conduits under the contribution standard. Akins makes actual candidate financing decisive rather than an organizational label or slogan requirement. Clifton protects policy inquiries and independently edited publications absent the necessary spending relation.

The objection that no formal agreement exists fails because the campaign can receive an actual financial substitute through an informal arrangement. Conversely, ordinary dialogue does not supply that substitute. A binding purchase arranged to provide the campaign's advertising can transfer the benefit before performance. This differs from publishing the private plans for a separate independent message. The January coordination rule's payment, content and conduct structure and policy-inquiry safe harbor rebut the premise of a regulatory vacuum; the regulation's applications are not adjudicated through this facial statutory holding.

The facial sustaining judgment is affirmed by six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the enforceable requested-resource application and inquiry/identity exclusions but seek a broader present declaration protecting party-owned joint expression and independent editorial activity. Their more protective remedy does not become a full join in the facial approval.

##### Sections 211 and 214(d): the actual statutory definitions

**Holding and operative rule:** The section 211 facial definition claim is rejected: statutory independent expenditure requires express advocacy and absence of concert, cooperation, request or suggestion with a candidate or the specified political-party connection. Falling outside that definition does not itself eliminate independent constitutional protection. The section 214(d) amendment challenge is also rejected because incorporation of FECA's contribution and expenditure definitions did not newly enact the preexisting direct-or-indirect-payment language.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The definition and the constitutional burden imposed on particular conduct are different questions. Colorado I and Clifton preserve independence and editorial protection even when a statutory label does not itself decide the constitutional question. Lamb permits regulation of actual requested resources without formal agreement. Incorporating definitions does not turn every policy communication into a contribution or create a new enactment of old words. These statutory grounds resolve the presented theories within the coordination disposition. Stone-Zsela concurs in the disposition without adding a statutory ground.

##### Completed repeal and unspecified regulatory applications

**Holding and operative rule:** The section 214(b) regulatory-vacuum theory is dismissed as moot after the replacement rules became effective February 3, 2003. The challenge to unspecified applications of section 214(c)'s rulemaking command is unripe. The command removes an agreement or formal-collaboration prerequisite and requires treatment of republication, common vendors, former employees and substantial discussions; it does not itself classify every contact as coordinated. Advisory opinions remain permissive.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The replacement rule ended the claimed unregulated interval. Its conjunctive spending predicates and policy-inquiry safe harbor also distinguish the generalized future-application claim from a present protected communication forced into coordination. Maine Right to Life and Lee recognize that an actual civil duty can support review; neither exhaustion of an advisory procedure nor a criminal penalty is required. National Park Hospitality's nonbinding-policy setting does not erase an operative statutory duty. Barnhart requires an actual delegated assignment rather than presumed power to rewrite a statute. The threshold disposition concerns the completed vacuum and unspecified applications, leaving an appropriate concrete regulatory challenge available.

##### The reciprocal party choice: section 213

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213's postnomination election-cycle choice is invalid as a unit: exercise of the special coordinated-party allowance cannot require surrender of genuinely independent expenditure, or vice versa. The single-committee and transfer, assignment and receipt restrictions implement that reciprocal choice and fall with it. The preexisting ordinary contribution and special coordinated-allowance rules remain operative under their lawful limits.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado I requires actual independence or coordination, not a conclusive status presumption from the party's other spending. Colorado II permits supported restrictions on genuine coordination but does not authorize extinguishing the independent option. Day and Lamb require meaningful use of the chosen association. The ordinary $5,000 multicandidate contribution route remains available after independent spending, yet that alternative does not justify conditioning another lawful allowance on abandoning independent speech.

The government's strongest argument is that earlier coordination makes later independence unreliable and that the allowance is optional. Genuine coordination can be proved and regulated on its facts; the supported record supplies no fitted basis for the conclusive reciprocal choice. Section 401 cannot create a different one-sided option. The lower invalidation is affirmed for everyone governed by the statutory choice.

##### Ordinary electioneering-finance reports

**Holding and operative rule:** Ordinary section 201 reporting survives the facial challenge. Government must establish a material connection between the information demanded and a legitimate electoral-information or source-tracing interest. A claimant seeking protective relief must substantiate the asserted associational injury; government retains justification of remaining exposure. Direct production or airing costs aggregating more than $10,000 in a calendar year trigger a report within 24 hours of each disclosure date and each later additional over-$10,000 aggregate. The report identifies the disburser, activity directors/controllers, records custodian and nonindividual principal place of business; disbursements over $200 and recipients; and relevant elections and candidates known or to be identified. Donors of $1,000 or more are identified over the period from the first day of the preceding calendar year through disclosure.

When the communication is paid from the qualifying segregated account consisting solely of direct contributions for electioneering communications by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents, donor disclosure is confined to that account, which may also be used for other purposes. Otherwise the statute reaches qualifying contributors to the disburser, not merely earmarked advertising donors. Reporting supplements other duties and preserves tax-law definitions. A demonstrated reasonable probability of threats, harassment or reprisals supports protective relief; disproportionate concrete associational burdens remain reviewable.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley and Brown permit materially connected financial disclosure with supported reprisal protection. Stengel, Akins and Mariani distinguish actual financing and truthful sources from a prohibition on the message. The defined major candidate-focused broadcasts, finite reporting events, donor thresholds and qualifying-account option provide a substantial informational relation. They permit voters to identify who pays for organized election-period advocacy and to trace financing through intermediaries.

McIntyre's protection of anonymous individual pamphlets and Bartlett's rejection of an indefinite full-committee regime do not make every paid-broadcast report invalid whenever the message lacks express advocacy. The account option and substantial funding-pool relation answer this facial claim while leaving actual injury review available. A reporting duty supplies no authority to prohibit the independent message or expose unrelated association records. The informational relation supplies the sufficient common ground. The ordinary-reporting judgment is affirmed, and any primary-definition restraint inconsistent with that independent reporting operation is reversed.

##### Executed contracts under section 304(f)(5)

**Holding and operative rule:** The complete severance of section 304(f)(5) is reversed. An executed binding purchase obligation may be treated as a reportable disbursement before payment. Under the effective rule public distribution triggers the initial report after the aggregate threshold is exceeded; that report may include executed obligations for other communications that have not yet aired. The duty concerns actual financial commitments, with the same statutory thresholds, government-borne material-information connection and supported associational protections, rather than negotiations or preliminary drafts. All nine agree that unpaid obligations for an already disseminated communication are reportable.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope. All nine join reporting unpaid obligations for an already disseminated communication. Only the six named sustaining Justices approve reporting executed contracts for separate unbroadcast communications.

**Controlling explanation:** Stengel and Akins support accounting for actual financing; Mariani supplies source transparency. An executed purchase is an existing financial transaction even while payment or a separate airing remains in the future. Reporting amounts, financing and pertinent elections does not require publication of the full script or permission to speak.

The strongest objection is disclosure of a separate message's confidential preparation when another advertisement airs. That is a real burden. The six-Justice Court concludes that the binding commitment, finite aggregate and financial character of the report supply the required relation on this facial record; cancellation possibilities and strategic privacy do not themselves defeat it. Concrete reprisal and disproportionate-burden claims remain available. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would protect only the separate unbroadcast application, preserving reports for public communications and narrowing rather than affirming the district's complete-clause severance.

##### The section 212 advance-reporting challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the presented section 212 challenge is affirmed on the actual-financial-obligation ground, rather than a separate ripeness holding. Government must justify the material electoral-information or source-tracing connection, with supported associational relief and justification of remaining exposure as stated for section 201. Statutory reports are due within 24 hours for $1,000-or-more independent-expenditure aggregates after the twentieth day but more than 24 hours before election, and within 48 hours for $10,000-or-more aggregates up to and including the twentieth day, with reports for successive corresponding aggregates for the same election. Contracts are included; receipt constitutes filing for the $1,000 report. The effective rule measures the deadlines from public distribution or dissemination.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory contract language is not eliminated simply because the agency administers a dissemination trigger. Lee and Maine Right to Life recognize operative civil duties; National Park Hospitality does not convert them into nonbinding policy. The Court's section 201 conclusion that a real binding financial obligation may be reported before payment and before a separate airing answers the parallel facial advance-reporting objection. The finite financial relation and supported associational protections supply the basis for rejecting additional injunctive relief. The Court does not establish a separate general ripeness rule through that resolution.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reach and protect the statute's advance application to an independent message before dissemination, while preserving ordinary reports and the protective distribution-based regulation. Their proposed remedy concerns the broader statutory obligation, not an assertion that the agency presently demands an advance report contrary to its rule.

##### Lowest-unit-rate certification: section 305

**Holding and operative rule:** The section 305 challenge is dismissed without prejudice because these claimants have not established the required concrete present rate/certification conflict. McConnell's next identified exposure is his 2008 election; the other identified candidate circumstances do not establish the necessary current dispute. Congressional permission to sue does not itself supply Article III injury. The dismissal leaves the condition's constitutional merits unresolved for want of a justiciable claim.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's portion of the Court's opinion; direct joins by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, including the author (6 Justices), controlling at the exact scope stated.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Raines require concrete personal injury, causation and likely redress at the actual litigation stage. Lee recognizes that civil loss of a benefit or a purchase-time condition can injure a speaker; a criminal sanction is unnecessary. The claimants' future plans and candidate-specific circumstances nevertheless do not establish the present conflict required here.

The statute requires certification at purchase. An opponent reference can retain preferred-rate eligibility through the television alternative—candidate image and readable identifying, approval and authorized-committee-payment statement simultaneously for at least four seconds at the end—or the radio alternative, the candidate's own identifying, office and approval statement. Noncompliance affects that and subsequent covered broadcasts during the relevant 45-/60-day periods. The Court's threshold disposition does not depend on finding that every station will charge a higher rate. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer regard the intended conduct and present campaign planning as sufficient and would reach the merits; the Court requires the more concrete claimant-specific showing.

##### Paul: amended ceilings, unchanged limits and the available remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Paul's section 307 candidate-contribution and comparative Press Clause claims, and the challenge to unchanged nonparty-PAC ceilings and indexing differences, are dismissed without prejudice to the proper review route. Willing-donor and campaign-receipt allegations establish a distinct asserted injury. But section 403 review of BCRA and its amendments cannot furnish the requested removal of the preexisting FECA restrictions: invalidating an increase or indexing amendment leaves the older limit and media treatment in place. This action cannot remove that restraint; dismissal leaves proper section 437h review available under its own requirements.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires likely redress as well as injury. Mariani preserves the statutory constitutional-review route, including its actual eligibility, substantiality and certification requirements. Paul's donor evidence is not Adams's relative-influence theory. Recognizing that evidence does not expand the legal consequences available from striking BCRA's increase.

The contrary argument treats the changed dollar figure as reenactment of the whole ceiling and therefore seeks deletion of the underlying restraint. The text of section 403 and the continued FECA prohibition do not support that remedy in this action. Raising or indexing other allowances likewise does not authorize judicial enlargement of the unchanged PAC ceiling. The dismissal resolves the action/remedy defect while preserving a proper challenge to the real restraint; it is not a merits adjudication of Paul's distinct Press theory.

##### Adams: increased limits and particularized competitive injury

**Holding and operative rule:** The Adams increased-limit claim is dismissed without prejudice. The asserted classwide dilution of electoral influence and deterrence of future candidacies do not establish a concrete, imminent personal competitive disadvantage traceable to the particular challenged increase and likely redressable by restoring the older ceilings. This claimant-specific disposition preserves ordinary competitor standing when its actual requirements are established.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Raines demand a supported personal nexus, not simply a prediction about the political environment. The testimony and projections concerning fundraising disparity are acknowledged. Uniform increased permissions, private donor choices and contemplated future races do not on this theory establish the requisite particular statutory injury. Restoring the prior ceilings might alter fundraising conditions without redressing a particular plaintiff's Article III injury.

The strongest alternative treats documented competition as concrete economic injury. Such a theory can be legally serious even when no substantive right to equal influence exists; standing cannot be denied merely because a claim would lose on the merits. Akins also shows that widely shared injury can remain concrete. The defect here is the actual individual and causal showing, distinct from Paul's existing receipt restraint.

##### Presented self-financing challenges: sections 304, 316 and 319

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented self-financing challenges are dismissed without prejudice because these claimants have not established an actual or imminent triggering candidate race. A self-financing candidate, an opponent potentially entitled to an increased allowance, an additional opponent and a party committee have distinct statutory positions; they cannot supply one another's injury. The constitutional merits of the presented scheme remain undecided for this Article III reason.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Day and Daggett demonstrate that a legal response to protected expenditure can burden speech before a transfer is complete. They do not supply a claimant subject to this different statutory formula. The lower record's claimant posture establishes no presently triggering race, and party concerns about supporting candidates equally do not establish actual adverse treatment under that formula.

The Senate mechanism begins at $150,000 plus four cents per voting-age resident, with twice/four-times/ten-times bands, threefold/sixfold allowances and the highest band's coordinated-party relaxation. Section 316 adjusts opposition funds by half the gross-receipts advantage using specified June 30/December 31 measurements. The House scheme begins above $350,000, with tripled individual limits, its own receipts rule and a 100-percent cap; the Senate acceptance cap is 110 percent. Increased acceptance follows notice, attributable permission ends when the opponent withdraws, and unspent excess must be returned within 50 days. These qualifications underscore why the actual claimant and election matter. The existing declarations, notices and distinct personal-loan provision are statutory context, not newly presented independent claims.

##### Sponsor identification: section 311

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 311's challenged extension of sponsor identification to electioneering communications and other covered non-express advocacy is sustained. Government must justify the material electoral-information or source-tracing connection; a claimant must substantiate protective injury while government retains justification of remaining exposure. Factual designation of the responsible sponsor has that relation to the covered paid communication and is distinct from compelled ideological endorsement. This on-message duty has its own scope and does not acquire section 201's $10,000 reporting threshold.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's portion of the Court's opinion; direct joins by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, including the author (8 Justices), controlling at the exact scope stated.

**Controlling explanation:** Stengel distinguishes organizational campaign-finance identification from McIntyre's ordinary individual leaflet. Clifton preserves accurate disclosure under separately valid authority. The source statement allows the audience to identify the payer while leaving the message's substance and volume to the speaker. The claim that every identification requirement must end at express advocacy therefore fails.

Candidate or authorized-speaker identification differs from the other-speaker responsible-for-content and connected-organization statements. The enacted box, type, contrast, image, voice, approval and duration specifications are retained as statutory context; the preserved challenge concerns the extension rather than each design detail independently. Supported reprisals and disproportionate concrete burdens remain subject to the governing associational protection. Thomas would protect on-message anonymity for independent genuine-issue broadcasts because the distinct command lacks the external report's monetary threshold; he preserves definite electoral-advocacy and valid candidate-finance attribution. The other eight affirm rejection of the extension challenge.

##### The complete minor-contribution ban: section 318

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 318's prohibition on contributions to candidates and contributions or donations to party committees by persons seventeen or younger is invalid in full. The categorical exclusion, including voluntary gifts from a minor's own resources, is not closely fitted to preventing adults from using children as false donors. Existing lawful amount, source, voluntariness and true-source restrictions remain enforceable.

**Authority:** The unanimous portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker recognizes minors' expressive rights; Buckley and Shrink Missouri supply the contribution standard. Day, Russell and Lamb require meaningful use of the chosen association. Mariani independently permits enforcement against false names and knowing reimbursement. Volunteering and independent expenditure are not adequate answers to the complete exclusion of the contribution channel.

The government invokes parental influence, evasion risk and an administrable age line. Those concerns support actual attribution and conduit rules but do not fit a prohibition reaching every genuine voluntary gift and lawful amount. Difficulty enforcing a narrower rule does not itself justify that sweep. The holding does not depend on a finding that parental evasion never occurs. Section 401 cannot enact a replacement age, earnings or consent condition. The lower invalidation is affirmed for everyone governed by the ban; complete First Amendment relief makes the alternative federalism and equality grounds unnecessary to the decree.

##### Broadcaster records of political requests: section 504

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 504 survives the facial challenge in full. Government must justify the information's material electoral or broadcast-accountability connection; supported concrete protective injury and justification of remaining exposure remain material. Licensees must maintain for public inspection records of accepted and rejected requests to buy broadcast time by or for a legally qualified candidate for public office, or for a message about a political matter of national importance, including a legally qualified candidate, a federal election or a national legislative issue of public importance. Required fields include acceptance/rejection, charged rate, airing date/time, time class and pertinent candidate/office, election or issue. Candidate requests identify the candidate, authorized committee and treasurer; other requests identify purchaser, contact name/address/telephone and chief executive officers or executive-committee/board members. Information must be placed in the file as soon as possible and retained for at least two years.

**Authority:** The six-Justice portion of the Court's opinion by Stone-Zsela identified above controls at the stated scope. Thomas additionally joins the candidate-request branch, including refusals, and aired noncandidate federal-candidate/election records, making seven for those applications. The six named Justices join the other applications and the complete reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Candidate-request files permit comparison of access, rates, time classes and placement, including refusals. A purchaser's own report does not reveal every rejected request or the station's treatment. Federal-candidate and election requests have both electoral-information and licensee-accountability functions. National-political and legislative requests have the latter function even when the message is not candidate advocacy. The rule is therefore not confined to completed purchases, federal candidates or section 201's threshold.

Stengel and Akins distinguish independently justified financial records from suppression of expression; Clifton preserves editorial independence. The government's distinct licensee function, actual request predicate and enumerated fields establish the relation here. Analogous administrative experience bears on fit without conclusively establishing constitutionality. The substantial objection is public exposure of unbroadcast strategy and association. Actual requests and refusals remain relevant distributor conduct, and the records do not require the full script. Supported concrete burdens remain reviewable without assuming a confidentiality procedure that has not been established.

Scalia and Kennedy would retain the entire new-duty injunction. Thomas supports candidate requests, including refusals, and aired noncandidate federal-candidate/election records, but would protect unaired noncandidate requests and issue-only files lacking that electoral relation. Seven thus sustain the first two applications; six sustain the remaining applications. The complete facial invalidation is reversed.

##### The independent general-press and media-exemption theories

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical claim that candidate campaigns and political organizations become immune from otherwise valid financing, source-reporting or sponsorship restrictions by describing themselves as the general press is rejected. The news/commentary/editorial exemption likewise does not automatically invalidate BCRA. Press freedom extends to noninstitutional speakers, while actual candidate receipts, paid advocacy purchases and independent news activity remain distinct regulated functions.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** McIntyre protects a noninstitutional publisher. Bellotti prevents exclusion of nonmedia speakers from First Amendment protection. Neither transforms donated campaign resources into the publisher's independent expenditure. Stengel, Akins and Mariani retain rules for actual financing and true sources; Clifton protects genuine independent editorial work. The statutory ownership/control limit and actual news function must be honored.

The alternative would treat every regulation of communicative finance as editorial licensing or require candidates and independent news facilities to receive identical financing treatment. That erases the activity-based distinctions these authorities preserve. The Court rejects the requested wholesale declarations within the live BCRA claims, leaving concrete discriminatory or independent-expression applications subject to their governing standards. Paul's separate section 307 route dismissal remains a nonmerits disposition.

##### Standing of directly regulated parties to challenge federal allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** Parties directly restricted in their own fundraising and spending may raise the presented affirmative-power and associated reserved-power objections. Their own financial duties and exposure establish personal injury, enforcement supplies causation, and relief from those duties supplies redress. A State plaintiff is unnecessary to this own-command challenge. The prudential third-party bar adopted by one district judge does not defeat it.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires personal injury rather than a particular constitutional label. Lopez adjudicated a regulated person's source objection; Printz's jurisdictional holding distinguishes commands imposed on the plaintiffs from obligations imposed only on others. New York's allocation rule is not cured by state consent. Guillen reserved the distinct private Tenth Amendment question and establishes neither a categorical permission nor a categorical prohibition.

Tennessee Electric involved competition under nonexclusive franchises, rather than a federal prohibition directly binding the claimant's conduct. Its absent-State language does not foreclose this direct claim. The contrary characterization as representation of absent sovereign preferences overlooks the plaintiffs' own imposed financial restraints. This holding concerns directly injured regulated parties and leaves a claimant asserting only a State's independent institutional injury outside the question decided.

##### Affirmative federal election authority across the challenged Title I classes

**Holding and operative rule:** Article I, section 4 authorizes fitted financial safeguards for congressional elections; presidential safeguards rest separately on the national protective authority recognized in Burroughs and Voting Rights Coalition, implemented through the Necessary and Proper Clause. Supported circumvention channels and shared federal-election operations may be reached despite state-election uses. Each challenged Title I class falls within that affirmative authority, while speech fit remains independently necessary.

The actual national-party institution supplies section 323(a)'s receipt channel, including nominally local uses. The 120-day registration and federal-ballot turnout/generic classes concern shared federal machinery; Levin own-fundraising, aggregation and transfer conditions protect the bounded allocation alternative. Class (iii) and section 323(f) concern support or opposition to a candidate for federal office, with their actual exclusions. Class (iv) concerns employees materially engaged in federal-election work, although the whole-salary speech burden requires the separate remand. Section 323(d)'s defined election-spending recipients and party routes supply a federal-financing subject even for compliant funds whose prohibition fails the separate fit test. Section 323(e)'s official-requester relationship supplies the federal connection despite another election's ultimate benefit.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Voting Rights Coalition supplies the distinct congressional and presidential sources and preserves state qualifications. Smiley and Siebold support shared electoral administration; Burroughs protects the national executive election. Thornton and Cook distinguish genuine electoral administration from altering the constitutional representative relationship. The actual financing, solicitation and shared-operation mechanisms—not the possibility that local politics might eventually affect Washington—support these classes.

The strongest contrary argument invokes an off-year state expenditure, local campaign purpose, proportional staff accounting or state-authorized pooling. A local destination does not erase the actual federal solicitation or shared-operation mechanism. Proportional payroll and pooling burdens raise serious speech-fit questions, which remain independently governed by the dispositions above. Section 323(d)'s source authority likewise does not justify forbidding compliant money. Lopez, Holston and Ho reinforce identification of an actual constitutional mechanism; their Commerce Clause element is not transplanted into election law. This source holding rejects wholesale Title I invalidation without curing any independently invalid speech burden. The preserved state-only exceptions, candidate-for-office meanings and official-solicitation accommodations limit its reach.

##### Reserved state electoral authority and direct private regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** Otherwise authorized Title I restrictions do not violate the asserted reserved-state-power or Tenth Amendment limit merely because they restrict private financing that a State permits. They directly regulate the identified parties, candidates and fundraising actors. They neither require a state legislature to enact a code nor assign a state executive a general program governing others, and they leave state qualifications, offices, election calendars and administration intact. This conclusion depends on the established affirmative source and the surviving speech limits.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** New York and Condon distinguish source-supported direct regulation from compulsory state government of others. Printz forbids compelled state executive administration of federal regulation of private persons; this private-finance arrangement imposes no such administrative command. Voting Rights Coalition separately recognizes election-specific authority while preserving state qualifications.

The contrary theory treats state campaign finance as categorically immune because it concerns state self-government. That interest does not remove an actual federal-election or federal-official financing connection. State permission to give more cannot defeat an otherwise valid federal restraint; supremacy follows after, not before, constitutional validity is established. The Court therefore rejects the wholesale structural injunction while preserving each separate speech remedy.

##### Surviving party classifications and the racial-impact alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** The facial equal-protection objection to the surviving party-specific rules fails because their demonstrated institutional fundraising, donor-credit and transfer relationships justify the relevant distinctions. Genuine resource and conduit differences can support different treatment; a party label alone cannot. Separately, all eight Associate Justices reject the Thompson racial-impact theory as presented: Valeria requires an actual racial classification, deliberate proxy or racially selective political barrier, while this theory identifies demographic consequences of a uniform federal-candidate solicitation rule rather than that trigger.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five-Justice authority at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only. The five named Associates directly join rejection of the surviving party-classification theory. All eight Associates join the distinct Thompson racial-impact holding. Stone-Zsela concurs in both dispositions without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification inquiry uses the actual financing-channel differences supporting the surviving provisions, not a declaration that all parties are corrupt or a power to equalize influence. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas seek their wider relief on speech grounds and identify no additional equality remedy beyond it; they accept the narrower actual-resource distinction.

Valeria requires attention to structural racial barriers without making hostility indispensable in every case. Disparate demographic impact alone does not establish such a barrier here. The presented uniform solicitation restriction supplies no supported racial classification, deliberate proxy or race-defined relocation of access. The Court resolves the actual theory without transforming a neutral label into immunity for a proved discriminatory mechanism.

##### Definite coverage and the separately preserved breadth objections

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented facial vagueness theories fail within the adopted meanings: the primary medium, time, candidate and electorate triggers are ascertainable; candidate promotion or opposition concerns the candidate for that office; and coordination requires an actual spending relation. Ordinary issue discussion, mere reference and incidental influence do not become the missing statutory connection. Clarity and constitutional breadth remain distinct inquiries.

**Authority:** O'Connor's portion of the Court's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices, controls at the stated scope. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Bartlett requires definite coverage and protection for genuine issue discussion. Maine Right to Life supplies cumulative attention to what the message actually communicates, and Clifton preserves ordinary inquiries. Greenville distinguishes contextual notice from an independent branch lacking an enforceable standard. Here the identified predicates provide the standard within their adopted scope; an absence of formal agreement does not abolish the spending connection.

A clear category can still burden protected speech. The particular speech objections and remedies above therefore remain necessary despite rejection of the vagueness theory. The inactive fallback retains its entire cumulative limiter. This holding does not approve an unspecified regulation or an all-influence contact rule.

### United States v. Deaton

Merits decision, December 11, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission. The Court reviews the Fourth Circuit’s affirmance of Clean Water Act jurisdiction, the wetland determination and an order to refill the excavated ditch and restore previolation elevation. The questions concern statutory coverage of wetlands adjoining the demonstrated tributary system, Congress’s channel-protection power, the Corps’ hydrology method and the restoration alternative. The earlier sidecasting-discharge ruling is settled. The wetlands adjoin a roadside ditch draining through a culvert, the John Adkins Prong of Perdue Creek and Beaverdam Creek to the navigable Wicomico River and Chesapeake Bay. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The Act and valid regulations reach this connected system

**Controlling proposition:** The Clean Water Act and the Corps’ tributary and adjacent-wetland regulations cover these wetlands adjoining the demonstrated feeder system into navigable waters. The statutory program delegates authority exercised through binding regulations; the permissible inclusion of this connected system and the responsible Corps construction of tributaries as its feeder branches receive their respective controlling weight within statutory and regulatory limits.

**Authority:** Part I’s coverage conclusion has nine joins. Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply eight direct joins to the complete delegated-choice and regulatory-interpretation rationale; Stone-Zsela’s join is confined to the coverage conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1311, 1344 and 1362 establish the discharge prohibition, dredged-or-fill permit program and definition extending beyond navigability in fact. Section 1344 assigns the program to the Secretary of the Army acting through the Chief of Engineers, with structured permit, guideline, general-permit and state-administration provisions. Haggar and Mead require that actual assignment and its exercise before deference; Barnhart applies the same sequence in a different statutory program. Ambiguity or expertise alone does not confer authority.

The Corps reasonably includes this connected tributary system and its conceded adjacent wetlands under section 328.3(a)(1), (5) and (7). The latter provision covers wetlands adjacent to the identified waters other than waters themselves wetlands. Riverside Bayview preserves adjacent-wetland coverage. SWANCC’s bird-use-only isolated-pond limit does not impose the owners’ proposed direct-feeder boundary on this supported connection.

The distinct question about the regulation’s meaning receives Auer’s fair-and-considered-interpretation rule. Tributaries reasonably includes feeder branches reaching navigable water through intermediate streams; the responsible Corps construction is consistent with that text. Earlier primary, secondary and tertiary usage confirms the meaning. A former headwaters coverage cutoff did not establish that more remote branches were linguistically never tributaries. The direct-feeder alternative is plausible but not compelled. Neither form of deference proves hydrological facts, permits interpretive amendment or displaces the Government’s ordinary civil burdens. The actual route and conceded adjacency supply the application here, rather than environmental importance alone.

##### II. Protection of the receiving navigable channel supports this application

**Controlling proposition:** Congress may prevent pollutant effects in an identified navigable waterway by regulating the established discharge class in its demonstrated tributary system and adjacent wetlands. The Government must support that connection and mechanism; once established, the channel ground requires neither an independently substantial effect from this individual fill nor tracing a particular particle into the receiving river.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez preserves direct protection of interstate channels and requires a real connection before aggregate magnitude can do work. Rio Grande Dam, Appalachian Electric Power, Oklahoma ex rel. Phillips and Grand River Dam support protection of navigable waters through relevant nonnavigable reaches; Caminetti recognizes protection against harmful channel uses. The power is not confined to clearing obstructions from boat passage.

The agreed drainage route connects the roadside ditch and adjacent wetlands to the Wicomico. The Corps supplied evidence that discharges into the connected tributaries and adjacent wetlands substantially affect receiving-water quality; the owners did not contend that the reported class effect was overstated. The approximately eight-mile route to the navigable river, intermediate ponds and dams, and disputed flow consistency therefore require attention but do not erase this supported connection. A particular parcel’s small contribution does not defeat prevention directed to that connected class.

The owners’ strongest objection is that remote drainage can turn channel protection into general land-use power. The answer is the established receiving channel, actual route and supported discharge mechanism, rather than a presumption that every ditch connects or national environmental concern. Home Builders retains a separate externality route. The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Drainage alone does not establish those conditions. The supported channel ground fully sustains this application.

##### III. The root-zone indicator answers the preserved Manual objection

**Controlling proposition:** Section 328.3(b) requires inundation or saturation by surface or groundwater at a frequency and duration sufficient to support, and under normal circumstances actually supporting, a prevalence of vegetation typically adapted to saturated soils. Within that complete definition, the Manual’s express visual indicator of saturation in a major part of the root zone, usually within twelve inches, defeats the asserted requirement of saturation to the surface; the Corps’ determination is sustained on this preserved interpretation issue.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in affirmance of this component without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1987 Manual applies vegetation, hydric-soil and hydrology criteria, with recorded water data ranked above field indicators. Its specific root-zone indicator explains how saturation can have the overriding effect on vegetation relevant to wetland hydrology. The owners’ twenty-two-well monitoring argument relied on inadequate saturation to the surface. Reading the general surface description with the express indicator answers their contention that the Corps contradicted the Manual. Vegetation and hydric soil are undisputed, and the described challenge establishes no contrary root-zone measurement requiring this Court to resolve a factual conflict.

Auer permits the responsible, text-consistent regulatory explanation; it does not allow a scientific manual to amend the regulation or make one observation conclusive. Frequency, duration, normal circumstances and the actual vegetation criterion remain essential. Genuinely contrary higher-ranked data must retain their proper weight. The claim that the Manual itself is inconsistent with the regulation was not presented. Affirmance thus resolves the asserted misuse of its language while preserving the complete regulatory conditions.

##### IV. Refilling reasonably restores the established violation’s effects

**Controlling proposition:** A restoration order must bear an equitable relationship to the actual violation, be practically achievable and serve restoration of the affected environment. The district court did not abuse its discretion by requiring refilling and previolation elevation rather than removal of the sidecast soil elsewhere, because the proposed removal would preserve the unlawful project’s drainage benefit and risk further ecological harm.

**Authority:** Part IV’s complete rationale has the eight Associate joins. All nine support the operative restoration mandate; Stone-Zsela’s agreement is confined to that operative remedial scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners emphasize that digging alone was not the established discharge and that hauling the soil to nonwetland land could have avoided a violation initially. But an option that would have avoided a violation before the work does not necessarily restore the affected wetlands afterward. The actual findings explain both the retained drainage benefit and the risk of further harm from the proposed removal. Those concrete reasons connect the ordered refilling to the previolation condition.

Cumberland Farms supplies persuasive considerations of environmental benefit, feasibility and equitable relation rather than a compulsory checklist. The Act’s restoration objective supports the properly bounded choice; it does not establish a separate prohibition on excavation or authority over unrelated land use. The existing order remains tied to this discharge and its effects, and ordinary enforcement must address any supported feasibility or compliance issue on its evidence.

### Castro v. United States, No. 02-6683

Merits decision, December 15, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 15, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 290 F.3d 1270. The questions are whether the authorization-review bar prevents review of first-or-successive classification and whether an unwarned conversion of a pro se Rule 33 filing into a first section 2255 motion can trigger later successive-motion restrictions. The district court denied Castro’s 1994 motion under both labels without notice, warning or an opportunity to withdraw or amend. His appeal did not challenge the label. The later judgment treated his express 1997 section 2255 motion as successive. He seeks ordinary first-motion treatment; the Government seeks affirmance and contests jurisdiction.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The 1997 motion must receive ordinary section 2255 treatment without requiring authorization because of the unwarned 1994 conversion. The earlier merits denial retains its effect. Scalia and Thomas reach the noncounting result on an independent improper-conversion ground.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined Part I. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The antecedent classification is reviewable

**Controlling proposition:** The authorization-review bar incorporated through section 2255 applies to actual decisions granting or denying circuit authorization to file a successive motion. It does not bar review of this antecedent classification judgment, where no authorization application was made and the appealed ruling treated a prior filing as the first motion.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker enforces the statutory bar against review of the specified authorization act. Stewart separately permits review of whether a proceeding requires authorization at all. Here the court of appeals affirmed a district-court dismissal on the premise that the 1994 conversion consumed Castro’s first motion. Its additional observation that he could not satisfy the successive criteria does not transform that ordinary appellate judgment into adjudication of a nonexistent authorization request. The Government’s characterization therefore does not establish the barred act. The inquiry remains action-specific; changing a label cannot reopen an actual authorization denial.

##### II. Unwarned conversion cannot consume the first-motion opportunity

**Controlling proposition:** Before a federal court makes a pro se filing bearing another label count as the litigant’s first section 2255 motion for later second-or-successive restrictions, it must give notice of the intended conversion, warn that later section 2255 motions will face those restrictions, and provide a meaningful opportunity to withdraw or amend the filing to include the claims the litigant wishes to present. Without those protections, the converted filing cannot supply that later counting predicate.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins establish the rule independently of the judgment concurrences.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal courts sometimes construe pro se pleadings to reach their substance rather than lose a claim through an imperfect label. Haines supports that practice; it does not justify secretly consuming a separate statutory opportunity. The Court supervises its federal courts’ own recharacterization practice in light of section 2255’s first-motion and successive-motion structure. Notice identifies the changed vehicle; warning explains the future consequence; withdrawal preserves the choice to defer that vehicle; amendment permits an informed, complete initial submission.

The Government argues that Congress enacted no warning exception and that an actual prior adjudication should count. But the question is whether the court’s unsolicited label establishes the statutory first-motion predicate, not whether fairness excuses a concededly successive motion. Lonchar requires an actual lawful dismissal ground, and Stewart recognizes the distinct classification inquiry. Neither already prescribed these warnings; the Court adopts them now. The broader objection to all involuntary conversion identifies genuine risks, but does not require eliminating useful liberal construction to resolve this adverse counting consequence. The rule regulates that consequence and does not make every warned conversion lawful in every respect. Otherwise applicable amendment and limitation rules retain their force.

##### III. The later counting consequence must be corrected

**Controlling proposition:** The unwarned 1994 conversion cannot make Castro’s express 1997 motion second or successive, and failure to appeal the earlier label does not supply the missing informed election. The later motion must return for ordinary first-motion adjudication, while the earlier merits judgment remains intact.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices; Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on their independent ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Castro selected Rule 33 and reiterated that choice. The court supplied the alternative section 2255 label without explaining its consequences or permitting withdrawal or amendment. Notice serves an informed decision whether to amend, withdraw or challenge conversion, including on appeal. The absence of an appeal therefore cannot cure the very lack of information that prevented informed choice. Even assuming law of the case applies, it is an adjudicative practice rather than an absolute limit on judicial power and does not bar correcting this later counting consequence.

The supervisory rule applies at least in the case announcing it, including the later use of the 1994 conversion to bar the post-enactment motion. This is not a general exemption for filings predating AEDPA. The district court must identify any valid independent procedural or timeliness bar and decide the ineffective-assistance claims that properly survive. Those ordinary adjudications determine entitlement to collateral relief; first-motion classification itself does not.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Felker v. Turpin: applied to the specified authorization-review act, preserving the statutory bar at that scope.
- Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal: applied to the separate reviewability of antecedent classification; its preserved-premature-claim holding remains distinct from the warning rule announced here.
- Lonchar v. Thomas: applied for legally supported dismissal predicates, without attributing this new conversion procedure to its earlier holding.
- Haines v. Kerner: applied to liberal construction of pro se pleadings, with the new warning condition governing the adverse first-motion counting consequence.

#### Law After Decision

An unwarned judicial conversion of a differently labeled pro se filing does not count as the first section 2255 motion for later successive restrictions. Courts must provide all three protections before imposing that consequence. The rule applies to the classification before the Court and comparable conversion practice, while genuine successive motions, independent first-motion requirements and the earlier merits judgment retain their respective force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the jurisdiction holding and concur in vacatur and remand on an independent ground. Liberal construction of allegations does not authorize overriding a prisoner’s chosen procedural vehicle and imposing the costs of an unchosen first section 2255 motion. Castro selected and reiterated Rule 33; the judge imposed the additional label. The concurrence would avoid involuntary first-motion conversion when its adverse risks cannot be removed, rather than treat warnings as sufficient authority to change the vehicle. A court cannot reliably foresee every consequence, and an apparent immediate advantage may disappear on appeal. Their position respects the enacted successive predicate rather than creating an equitable exception once that predicate exists. They agree that law of the case does not conclusively bar inquiry, but do not join the Court’s affirmative warning framework or its warning-based application.

#### Source Notes

Castro v. United States, No. 02-6683; 540 U.S. 375; 290 F.3d 1270; 28 U.S.C. §§2255 and 2244(b)(3)(E); Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 33. The classification concerns the unwarned 1994 conversion and the express 1997 motion; the independent timeliness inquiry retains its own factual requirements.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for the district court to restore the 1997 motion to its docket without demanding authorization based on the unwarned 1994 conversion, identify any independently valid procedural or timeliness bar, and adjudicate the claims that properly survive. The prior Rule 33 merits denial remains effective.

**End of entry: Castro v. United States, Merits decision, December 15, 2003.**

### Maryland v. Pringle, No. 02-809

Merits decision, December 15, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 3, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Court of Appeals of Maryland, 370 Md. 525, 805 A.2d 1016. The question is whether the known combination of packaged cocaine, cash and shared travel supplied probable cause to arrest Pringle, the front-seat passenger. Police stopped the small private car for speeding at about 3:16 a.m.; the driver consented to a search. Officers found $763 in rolled cash in the glove compartment and five cocaine baggies concealed behind the rear-seat armrest. The three occupants supplied no ownership information. Maryland seeks reversal of the arrest-based suppression ruling; Pringle seeks exclusion of his ensuing confession and relief from the conviction.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Maryland Court of Appeals’ arrest-based suppression judgment is reversed. The lawful arrest supplies no ground to exclude the ensuing confession as the product of an unlawful seizure. The case returns for ordinary further proceedings on any independently preserved issue before the conviction’s ultimate status is settled.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

The Court’s opinion has complete joins from all nine Justices on the rule, application and bounded remand.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Personal probable cause can rest on a supported common-possession inference

**Controlling proposition:** Officers may arrest a vehicle occupant for possession when facts known at arrest, considered together, establish a fair probability that the occupant knowingly exercises sole or joint dominion or control over the contraband. The Government must identify the particular facts supporting that common-possession inference; mere presence, a general passenger presumption or silence alone is insufficient, while exclusive ownership need not be established.

**Authority:** The complete Court opinion has nine direct joins and controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Maryland possession law permits individual or joint knowing control. The Fourth Amendment requires a practical probability of that participation, rather than trial proof or a division of probability among three possible exclusive owners. Ornelas requires independent review of the legal conclusion with proper protection for historical facts and grounded inferences. Wardlow and Valentine require the actual connection to wrongdoing; they do not permit a category or association label to supply it.

Ybarra protects a customer against suspicion arising merely from presence among strangers in a public establishment. Di Re requires attention to affirmative information identifying another participant. Both preserve individualized cause, without forbidding a supported inference of joint possession. Houghton’s detached-container rule governs the scope of an already justified automobile search; it does not presume passenger guilt or a criminal enterprise and supplies no personal arrest cause here. The search’s validity and the arrest’s justification therefore remain separate inquiries.

##### II. The combined circumstances connected Pringle to the cocaine

**Controlling proposition:** The five separately packaged cocaine baggies accessible within the common passenger compartment, associated rolled cash, small private automobile and shared early-morning travel together supplied probable cause that Pringle participated in sole or joint possession. This conclusion rests on the contemporaneous combination, including the drugs’ concealed location, rather than his later confession.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The cocaine was concealed behind the upright rear armrest, not observed in Pringle’s sight or found on his person. The state court’s strongest objection—that an innocent passenger could travel without knowing of hidden drugs—is therefore substantial. Its majority and concurrence did not confuse probable cause with proof beyond a reasonable doubt. But the known packaged cocaine, its accessibility in the common passenger space, the cash directly before the front passenger and the common journey supported the practical inference of shared knowing possession. The possibility of an unwitting passenger did not make that inference unreasonable.

Cash, late travel, accessibility and companionship each lack the force of the supported combination; none independently establishes knowing participation. The occupants’ failure to identify an owner adds no independent badge of guilt and imposes no duty to confess; it means only that reliable information identifying a different exclusive possessor did not displace the otherwise supported joint inference. That differs from Di Re’s affirmative identification and Ybarra’s incidental public-place association. Houghton supplies no shortcut around this independent assessment, and the officer’s announcement that everyone would be arrested adds no probable cause. Pringle’s later confession and account of the others’ knowledge cannot supply facts the officer lacked at arrest. The supported common-possession combination resolves this arrest, while preserving the requirement of an actual personal connection in materially different circumstances.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Ornelas v. United States: applied to independent legal review of probable cause, with historical findings and grounded factual inferences retaining their proper treatment.
- Illinois v. Wardlow: applied to the requirement of a supported personal connection to wrongdoing; the actual cocaine and common-transport evidence meet that requirement here under the higher probable-cause threshold.
- United States v. Valentine: applied to the requirement of a supported personal connection to wrongdoing; the actual cocaine and common-transport evidence meet that requirement here under the higher probable-cause threshold.
- Ybarra v. Illinois: distinguished because this supported shared-transport inference differs from incidental presence among customers in a public establishment.
- United States v. Di Re: distinguished because officers lacked its affirmative information identifying a different transaction participant; joint possession remains distinct from exclusive ownership.
- Wyoming v. Houghton: its object-based detached-container search rule remains separate from personal arrest cause and supplies no presumption of a common criminal enterprise.

#### Law After Decision

A supported common-possession inference can establish probable cause for more than one occupant without proof of exclusive ownership. Courts must identify the contemporaneous personal connection in the complete circumstances. The combination here justifies Pringle’s arrest and removes the resulting arrest-based ground for excluding his confession.

#### Source Notes

Maryland v. Pringle, No. 02-809; 540 U.S. 366; Pringle v. State, 370 Md. 525, 805 A.2d 1016; U.S. Const., Amdts. 4 and 14. The cocaine was concealed behind the rear armrest. The later confession supplies no contemporaneous justification for arrest, and the disputed account of a release promise is not an established finding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Maryland courts for further proceedings consistent with the conclusion that Pringle’s arrest was lawful. The arrest-based derivative-exclusion ground must be removed, with any independently preserved issue addressed in its proper posture.

**End of entry: Maryland v. Pringle, Merits decision, December 15, 2003.**
