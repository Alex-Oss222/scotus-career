## The two paths and the changed-premise test

**Proposed affirmance path.** The new model retains P1 but finds P2 unproved for Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. Their actual Houghton dissent specifically requires a passenger or effect connection, refuses to presume transfer or common concealment from companionship, and protects against transferring the driver's suspicion to a passenger. Here the driver owned the car, the cash was in its glove compartment, the cocaine was concealed behind the rear armrest, and Pringle committed no reported incriminating act before arrest. Accessibility can indicate opportunity without knowledge. Packaging and cash establish drug activity without necessarily identifying its participants. The threat to arrest everyone unless someone admitted ownership cannot supply the absent individual basis. Their Wardlow and Valentine positions reinforce scrutiny of an unsupported personal inference. On this path the arrest was unlawful, and the preserved arrest-derived suppression judgment would be affirmed; the state's unpreserved and alternatively unsuccessful attenuation theory, absent independent-source or inevitable-discovery claims, and Miranda warnings would not rescue it.

That is a substantial legal path. It is not dismissed as a demand for trial proof or a mandatory checklist of observed acts. Arrest is more intrusive than the detached-container search, and the actual Houghton majority does not supply a general inference of passenger guilt. The question is whether these differences support changing the three Justices' historical Pringle positions on the same operative facts.

**Retained reversal path.** P2 rests on the entire known arrangement: five packaged cocaine baggies in an accessible common passenger compartment, rolled cash in the front glove compartment, and three people transporting that combination together in a small private automobile at approximately 3:16 a.m. Those facts support an inference of knowing common possession, rather than a rule imputing the driver's separate misconduct to every passenger. The drugs were concealed; no observed awareness or handling is invented. The totality nevertheless may establish a practical probability without direct observation of knowledge. An unwitting passenger is possible. That possibility does not itself defeat probable cause, and joint possession does not require dividing suspicion into three mutually exclusive sole-owner possibilities. No silence-based inference fills a gap. Absence of a reliable exclusive attribution is relevant only after the positive common-possession inference is supported.

**The historical comparison resolves this review in favor of the retained path.** Historical Houghton's Stevens dissent, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, already rejected treating a passenger's spatial association with a driver as an acceptable reason to presume criminal partnership. It required individualized probable cause and relied on Di Re, Ybarra and the same personal-privacy objection. Its four footnotes reinforce that objection rather than create an exception allowing mere association. The actual Houghton dissent elaborates concrete transfer, shared ownership or concealment indicators and preserves common compartments and independent grounds; it does not supply a newly contrary disposition, replace an earlier acceptance of passenger guilt, or categorically exclude circumstantial evidence of joint possession. Its more explicit formulation is important evidence of these Justices' position, but the substance of the objection existed when all three historically joined Pringle.

Historical Pringle expressly required probable cause particularized to the person and then found the combined cash, packaged drugs, common accessibility and travel circumstances sufficient. It separately used historical Houghton's generalized passenger/common-enterprise observation in answering Ybarra. The complete opinion does not say the three former dissenters accepted the arrest only because precedent compelled a general passenger inference. Historical Houghton itself distinguished a person's search from a container search, and did not decide that drugs in a car automatically authorize passenger arrest. Those Justices therefore had the same opportunity to object to extending vehicle-search authority to personal seizure; their full Pringle joins remain particularly strong evidence about this application.

The contrary possibility remains: without the historical majority's broad supporting rationale, the three might give their personal-connection objection greater weight. But an unstated stare-decisis compulsion cannot be presumed to explain their historical joins. Neither the new screen nor the verified writings identifies a changed Pringle fact, possession element, arrest standard, remedy or individual prior objection that would make that explanation more persuasive than their direct same-case decisions. The actual Houghton majority removes a source of corroborating reasoning; it does not adopt the dissent's proposed exception or forbid independently supported joint-possession probability. Wardlow and Valentine likewise reject unsupported shortcuts while expressly preserving grounded totality analysis without a required observed offense or prescribed extra fact. They do not decide this actual contraband-and-transport combination.

The additive provisional affirmances are therefore corrected in reconciliation. This is not a claim that history determines every vote or that the fresh model was procedurally defective. It is the required qualitative comparison: the proposed departure relies on an individualized-suspicion objection that substantially existed historically, while the verified same-case join shows that objection did not prevent accepting this narrower factual inference. No adequately supported new Justice-specific premise warrants three changed judgments.

## Genuine rationale difference and precise historical treatment

The actual Houghton majority is materially narrower in its stated rationale than the passage historical Pringle quoted: it rests on vehicle probable cause, the object capable of concealment and the detached-container rule, and expressly does not presume a passenger guilty or engaged in a common criminal enterprise. This is not treated as an immaterial citation-format change. An opinion in this Pringle cannot repeat the broad historical passage as this Court's existing rationale or let vehicle-search authority do the work of personal probable cause.

The necessary replacement is the independent fact-specific P2 explanation below. Its evidentiary ingredients and joint-possession conclusion are present in the historical Pringle opinion itself; no new adjudicated fact is supplied to repair a missing basis. Ybarra's incidental public-place presence and Di Re's affirmative identification of another participant remain distinguishable on their actual grounds, not by a universal private-car exception.

**Historical departure:** The reconstructed explanation omits historical Pringle's reliance on historical Houghton's generalized passenger/common-enterprise rationale because the actual prior Houghton majority expressly supplied only object-based detached-container authority without imputing guilt. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer preserve their actual Houghton joins; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg preserve their actual particularized-connection dissent. Each accepts P2 only as the independently supported factual inference stated here. This supported rationale limitation changes neither the historical Pringle disposition nor its operative individualized common-possession application. The proposed three judgment departures are not adopted for the separate reasons above.

## Final operative propositions and remedy

**P1 — particularized probability of knowing possession.** Police may arrest a passenger when contemporaneously known facts, considered together, establish a fair probability that he knowingly exercises sole or joint dominion or control over the contraband. A supported common-possession inference can particularize that probability to more than one occupant; exclusive ownership need not be proved. Mere presence, a generalized passenger presumption, another person's suspected wrongdoing or silence cannot substitute for that support. Probable cause remains higher than reasonable suspicion and distinct from trial proof.

**P2 — this combination is sufficient.** The five separately packaged cocaine baggies accessible in the common passenger compartment, the rolled cash in the glove compartment directly before Pringle, and the three occupants' early-morning travel together in the small private car support a practical probability of knowing common possession including Pringle. The rear armrest was upright and concealed the cocaine until lowered. Thus knowledge is inferred, not observed. Neither cash, late travel, access nor companionship independently establishes guilt, and the combination is evaluated without presuming that every private-car companion participates in a driver's offense.

The presence of actual packaged contraband and associated cash in this shared transport supports more than the unproved crime or participant connection in Wardlow and Valentine. This application requires the higher arrest threshold to be satisfied on its own facts; a prior reasonable-suspicion holding supplies no automatic answer. Ybarra does not eliminate a supported common-possession inference, and Di Re requires honoring specific information singling out another participant. No such information displaced the affirmative inference here. That last point creates no duty to identify an owner and treats neither denial nor silence as guilt. The officer's arrest-all announcement supplies no probable cause. The later confession, description of the journey, friendship and account of the others' knowledge are excluded from antecedent justification.

The Court does not find that all three actually agreed to possess or distribute cocaine, determine the other occupants' guilt, or adopt a rule governing every hidden-drug automobile, bus, taxi or private container. The state court's contrary connection assessment is answered on its substance, not misdescribed as demanding proof beyond reasonable doubt.

**P3 — confined reversal and remand.** Reverse the Maryland Court of Appeals' arrest-based suppression judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with the lawful arrest. This arrest supplies no unlawful-seizure basis for excluding the ensuing confession. Any independently preserved admissibility or other issue remains in its actual procedural posture; no new issue or automatic conviction reinstatement is supplied. The disputed release-promise account is not a coercion finding. No attenuation, independent-source or inevitable-discovery ground is invented, and Miranda warnings do not cure an unlawful arrest; P2 finds this arrest lawful on the earlier facts.
