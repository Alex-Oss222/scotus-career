## I. Governing propositions and record application

### A. Process, intentional racial injury and Title VI

The pleaded intentional-process claims require racial purpose as a substantial or motivating factor, proved by a preponderance, with actual injury and causation appropriate to the claim. The purpose need not be exclusive or predominant. Direct and circumstantial evidence are available; supported findings receive clear-error review. Arlington Heights, the unanimous purpose and same-enactment portions of Brooks, Montiel, and Crawford-El supply that sequence. No merely possible lawful explanation satisfies a same-decision defense once it is reached.

The lower court used ordinary preponderance and expressly rejected a sole-purpose requirement. It considered unfavorable hearing times, short notice, geography, limitations on debate and public input, software, demographic allocation and the actual legislative process. Its no-racial-purpose findings also rest on accessible mapping software, hearings and increased attendance, available recordings and submissions, ordinary means of contacting legislators, and the distinction between criticism of the process and proof of its racial purpose. The extensive partisan control of deliberation is not categorical immunity, but neither does it compel a racial-purpose finding. All eight affirm the intentional-process judgment because the supported finding has not been shown clearly erroneous. No speculative same-decision defense is needed or newly found.

The same distinction protects the independent treatment of racial intent in districting. The lower court found an objective of Republican advantage and legal survival, but no purpose to injure Black or Hispanic electoral opportunity. Political objectives can coexist with prohibited racial purpose; these findings are accepted as this record's supported findings, not as a rule making the two purposes mutually exclusive. No equal-protection violation is established merely by the map's racial design. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain Shaw/Miller/Cano's controlling independent-injury rule. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas need not relinquish their objections to that rule to affirm the intentional claims actually presented here: neither a proved independent intentional deprivation nor a racial-predominance finding requiring a different result is supplied.

The §601 count requires intent on its own terms. Powell, Sandoval and South Camden also recognize a distinct bounded direct prospective action under a valid applicable §602 effects regulation. No such preserved regulatory claim is identified here. Thus the lower court's judgment can stand on the failed §601 proof, but a proposition that all private Title VI relief invariably requires intent cannot receive a join. Section 1983 is a vehicle for an established federal right; Powell and South Camden reject the regulation-created effects right at issue there. The four Associates who disagreed with that §1983 holding retain their published disagreement but do not create an unpleaded regulatory claim in Martinez. The other four apply the statutory precedents notwithstanding their original objection to Powell's implied action, as they did in Sandoval and South Camden.

The earlier dismissal of Count I's §2 process theory is reported in footnote 7. The approved packet identifies no separately preserved appellate challenge to that earlier ruling, and the published merits opinion supplies no full rationale for it. These commitments do not revive that theory or announce a new rule about §2 process claims. They decide the intentional-process question actually presented.

### B. Racial §2: sufficient ground, district scope and qualifications

The six Associates applying the ordinary framework require the applicable capacity, cohesion and opposing-bloc predicates, causation, and unequal opportunity under the full circumstances. Racial polarization is accepted; an expected opportunity district does not erase polarization. No numerical floor, guarantee of election, maximization requirement, conclusive proportionality defense, or automatic offset of local injury is permissible. Actual nomination opportunity matters independently of general-election success under Brooks and Cano. Valdespino's dependable-crossover route remains law, while a genuinely independent failure of proof can sustain judgment without revisiting a disputed capacity screen.

The basis for affirmance is the tried district-specific evidence and limited requests for relief, not the mere number of districts labeled Black or Hispanic. The performance findings concern voters' preferred candidates, not the race of whoever holds office. Incumbency and uncontested elections receive their proper qualifications; the source separately considered open-seat prospects and competing projections. No finding that every future election will have identical conditions is adopted.

This affirmance is bounded. It does not adopt paragraph 161's broad suggestion that every loss through divided support necessarily represents only a failure to use an equal opportunity. ### C. Three distinct partisan branches under actual Vieth

**Controlling rule.** Vieth directly adopts a five-Justice equal-protection rule prohibiting the State from selecting district boundaries to impose a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of their political affiliation or activity. It requires an identified existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity; a concrete and practically significant impairment of that capacity by an identified boundary operation; imposition of that disadvantage because of the affected voters' political affiliation or activity as a substantial motivating purpose; and material causation. Plaintiffs must prove all four by a preponderance through an independently grounded, legally and practically feasible comparison. The evidence may take varied forms; a replacement map is not universally required, but a claim depending on a different configuration must establish its feasibility. Political awareness and incidental partisan benefit alone are insufficient, and deliberate material impairment is not independently justified as ordinary partisan advantage. Once plaintiffs carry their burden, the State must prove by a preponderance that materially the same lawful decision would have occurred without the forbidden purpose. A conceivable explanation does not suffice. No awaited election loss, statewide entrenchment, complete exclusion or numerical threshold is required. These commitments add no new threshold, formula or Court standard.

**Congress: selected remand for Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The imported Gingles compactness, fixed-party cohesion and majority-bloc requirements are not universal elements of the constitutional claim. The record contains more than a bare statewide ratio: local testimony describes approximately 69,909 residents, predominantly Black Democrats, reassigned from CD 10 to CD 11 to restore a Republican margin, and footnote 94 reports the Campbell alternative, S33C0009. Neither fact establishes liability, personal standing, an identified political capacity, lawful feasibility or causation by itself. But the court rejected the local showing partly because it did not establish a statewide case and discounted the alternative through its imported Gingles predicates. The four will not substitute new appellate findings about the evidence's failure under the now-controlling distinct rule.

The trial stipulation to partisan advantage is relevant to purpose but does not by itself establish every element or connect the challenged assignments to a material impairment of identified voters. Likewise registration, the statewide voting index and projected 18-to-7 seats cannot replace identified voters, activity, injury and causation. Their empirical shortcomings remain relevant. The remand must determine what this tried record actually proves under the correct rule, including personal exposure and a lawful comparison. It is not a liability remand to draw a fairer map.

**Purported independent totality ground.** Part III.D.2.b contains real negative findings about evidence of candidate slating access, past exclusion and representative responsiveness. Those findings remain available. Complete exclusion, unresponsiveness and inevitable defeat are not prerequisites under Vieth; a material discriminatory impairment of an existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity may exist while the affected voters retain some capacity to organize or win. The conclusions based on millions of recruitable voters, convertibility of party identity and earlier changes in political control do not themselves establish that the challenged assignments caused no present material impairment. The alternative therefore does not independently dispose of every properly presented congressional opportunity claim under the new rule. In contrast, rejection of a claim demanding only proportional seats remains independently valid.

**House: selected remand for the same four, on a distinct record basis.** Footnote 58 establishes the claim's existence and says it was rejected under the Part III test. It does not supply a House-specific lawful comparison, practical injury finding, proof concession or alternative ground that independently resolves Vieth's elements. Neither the congressional Tampa evidence nor general legislative partisan-purpose findings may be treated as proof of the House claim. The four vacate that legal-test-dependent disposition for application to the actual preserved House record. They do not find that the House plaintiffs supplied a sufficient comparison or injury, and do not infer that they supplied none merely because the published footnote is short. The claim may fail on the existing record under the correct rule.

**Senate: affirm.** Footnote 58 separately reports an express concession in the second amended proposed findings and conclusions that no evidence had been offered to prove political gerrymandering of the Senate plan. That evidentiary failure survives Vieth's change of standard; a new rule does not itself supply evidence. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm on that independently sufficient ground. Kennedy also regards the concession as sufficient assuming a cognizable claim. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas retain their threshold position and see no basis to disturb this result even under the majority's framework. The concession says nothing about racial SDs 1 and 18 or the common process count. No Senate partisan retrial or amendment is directed.

**Opposing congressional and House dispositions.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas retain their published Vieth position that this partisan-dilution category lacks a judicially enforceable entitlement to the alternative distribution, despite feasible maps and partisan purpose. They would affirm without embracing the lower court's imported statutory test or preserving every rationale it offered. They continue to recognize independently defined population, racial, exclusion and retaliation claims at their own scopes. Their objection is to Vieth's affirmative partisan equal-treatment cause and is recorded as a dissent from controlling law, not as the law entering Martinez.

Kennedy likewise would affirm these presented partisan claims while retaining his Vieth refusal to adopt the new affirmative measure. He does not join a categorical declaration that no future partisan participation or association claim can be adjudicated. The congressional evidence of preferred seat results and reassignments does not persuade him that an independently defined constitutional burden, distinguished from unfavorable common-electorate composition, has been established. For the House, his sufficient ground is that same objection to the announced constitutional measure; it is not a fabricated finding that no House evidence existed. If the controlling framework is applied over his objection, he accepts that House proof must be read from the actual record and cannot be supplied or negated by inference from the congressional discussion. He does not convert that conditional procedural agreement into an affirmative remand vote.

### D. Article I, predominance-only injury and ordinary procedure

Footnote 99's first two theories remain distinct, although both fail:

- **Automatic Article I / seat-ratio theory:** All eight affirm rejection of an independent congressional entitlement to a statewide partisan seat ratio. Vieth Part IV is unanimous and directly controls: Article I, §§2 and 4 do not convert separate district elections into proportional statewide representation. An allegation that projected partisan results are predetermined cannot substitute for the separately defined and proved injury. The conclusion rejects this offered theory without declaring every possible Article I claim outside judicial review.
- **Proposed stricter delegated-power standard:** All eight reject the proposed shortcut that congressional districting, because it exercises delegated rather than reserved power, is subject to a different effects threshold allowing the asserted disproportionality to establish the equal-protection violation. Vieth itself governs congressional districting and supplies the controlling personal-injury standard. The four remand voters require that rule's actual elements; the four opponents would not recognize a broader claim than the one they rejected in Vieth. The ruling does not decide a general theory about every distinction between delegated and reserved governmental powers.

All eight also reject partisan predominance as sufficient by itself. The four Vieth majority Associates require its distinct elements; the others reject a broader automatic rule for their own reasons. They do not adopt the historical lower opinion's description of racial predominance as automatically unconstitutional, which is inconsistent with current Shaw/Miller law. The record identifies no preserved standalone First Amendment claim. Ordinary preservation and amendment rules govern any such proposed claim; Vieth's separate association rationale is not a majority holding and is not imported as a new claim here.

For the four remand voters, vacatur is confined to the congressional and House partisan equal-protection dispositions. The District Court must apply the complete Vieth rule to the actual preserved record and decide any conforming amendment or supplementation under ordinary procedure. It may use adequate existing evidence, enforce valid preservation and disclosure rulings, and resolve genuinely sufficient independent grounds. There is no automatic evidentiary reopening, admission of excluded material, new trial, statewide redrawing, disproportionality presumption or liability finding. Remand is not confined to the existence of a cartographic exhibit, and no novel heightened pleading requirement follows.

Only a proved violation permits relief. The State receives the first opportunity to correct the actual deprivation; any further remedy must address responsible boundaries, comply with other governing districting requirements and extend no further than necessary. The court must use a prompt workable schedule suited to the election calendar. Existing operative plans remain in effect pending supported relief. Racial, process and Senate partisan affirmances are not reopened by the congressional/House remand. Separate preclearance proceedings, the reported HD 101 interim measure and state facial review neither establish federal merits validity nor receive a new disposition here.

## III. Compatibility and final limits

The four congressional/House remand voters join Vieth's complete rule at the same level of generality, including its substantive independently grounded comparison, flexible proof, ordinary preponderance burdens, State's same-decision defense and tailored State-first remedy. They do not join an injury inferred from political motive, a statewide representation entitlement, a compulsory-election-loss rule, or unconditional evidence reopening. Their racial and process affirmances do not immunize the partisan claims.

The three categorical opponents may share an affirmance and separate rationale; Kennedy's noncategorical objection remains separate. The §2 noncoverage opponents share only the statutory judgment with the six application voters. No additional joins are inferred from conditional agreement. Any later circulation must test these actual grounds and reservations, not merely the shared disposition.

No case 71 historical Supreme Court disposition, rationale or lineup was researched or exposed. These commitments are supported provisional positions for reconciliation, not a prediction certified against history. No author assignment, Chief vote, new liability finding, map, election postponement, funding cutoff or prescribed evidentiary hearing is included.
