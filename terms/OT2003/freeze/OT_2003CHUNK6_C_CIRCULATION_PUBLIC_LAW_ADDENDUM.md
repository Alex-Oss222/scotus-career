## Ordinary proof: Crawford-El, June 16, 1997

### Crawford-El v. Britton, D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203

Merits decision, June 16, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

On granted review of the D.C. Circuit's en banc judgment, 93 F.3d 813. The questions concern the burden of proving unconstitutional motive, qualified immunity's effect on proof and procedure, and discovery needed to establish motive. Leonard Crawford-El alleges that Patricia Britton misdirected legal papers and personal belongings during prison transfers in retaliation for communications with the press. The D.C. Circuit's August 27, 1996 judgment had already vacated the retaliation dismissal and remanded while imposing a clear-and-convincing motive burden. The current review addresses those legal instructions; the allegations remain contested.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated insofar as the en banc judgment requires clear-and-convincing proof of unconstitutional motive and imposes directions dependent on that burden, and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The live retaliation claim returns for ordinary civil proceedings and lawful tailored case management; the existing remand is otherwise preserved. No final merits finding or prescribed trial follows. Independently resolved claims and unresolved defenses retain the status stated below.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt its proof, discovery, causation and remand propositions, retaining existing qualified immunity.

The Chief Justice also filed a concurrence, without another Justice joining. He supports the Court's complete opinion and disposition under existing immunity and separately proposes a narrow legal-reliance defense that the Court does not adopt.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Proof of unconstitutional motive

**Holding and operative rule:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine participating Justices, supplies the controlling rationale for every holding below. Stone-Zsela separately proposes a change to immunity but independently joins this disposition and analysis under the existing defense; that proposal is not part of the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Kimberlin separates motive in the constitutional wrong from Harlow's objective immunity defense and rejects direct-only proof. It did not decide the alternative heightened quantum. The Court decides that question now: §1983, the First Amendment rule at issue and the Federal Rules provide no source for a special clear-and-convincing burden. Protecting officials against costly unfounded suits is a substantial concern, but it cannot supply an independent evidentiary code. New York Times' speech-protective rule for defamation does not establish the same quantum for official retaliation. Anderson v. Liberty Lobby tells courts to apply an independently governing substantive burden at summary judgment; it does not authorize invention of one. Leatherman's municipal pleading decision supports attention to the source of procedural authority while expressly reserving individual-officer immunity pleading. The Court does not treat that reservation as already resolved or abolish every specificity practice. This claim remains subject to particularized conduct-date immunity, legally sufficient constitutional allegations and ordinary proof. A motive allegation alone defeats none of those requirements.

##### Discovery and stage-specific screening

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow protects against unwarranted litigation burdens, while Anderson v. Creighton permits appropriately tailored factual inquiry. Those principles permit identification of the challenged acts and cognizable injury, early resolution of actual legal immunity and restriction of requests to material factual issues. They do not permit excluding circumstantial evidence or evidence held by the official simply because the plaintiff cannot possess it before discovery. Rules 7(a), 12(e), 26 and 56 provide tools for clarification and controlled development. A properly supported summary-judgment motion requires affirmative responsive evidence; a general attack on credibility is insufficient. Courts nevertheless cannot decide a genuine credibility dispute by demanding conclusive pre-discovery proof obtainable only through necessary inquiry. The D.C. Circuit's writings must be distinguished: Williams' absolute proof-first proposal lacked a majority; D. Ginsburg permitted tailored discovery on a sufficiently specific forecast tied to the elevated burden; Edwards and four colleagues applied ordinary rules. Directions dependent on the invalid quantum cannot govern this remand. The Court orders no fixed discovery plan, trial or new interlocutory-appeal entitlement.

##### Actual causation and independent defenses

**Holding and operative rule:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle applies Mt. Healthy's distinction between an actual same-decision showing and a hypothetical permissible reason. That causal distinction applies here without importing school-specific curricular rules into prisons. Britton's asserted safekeeping explanation may prevail if the necessary facts are established; its possible validity alone does not erase the motive-dependent claim. Conversely, some improper purpose or mixed motives do not alone establish recovery. Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and the independent immunity defense remain unresolved. Kimberlin and Settle govern current procedure and doctrine, not the notice Britton possessed in 1989. Pell and Turner continue to limit prison-access rights; no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized. The Court cannot select contested inferences about threats, elapsed time, other prisoners' treatment or property handling to enter a new merits dismissal on this record.

**Remedy and limits:** Vacate the en banc judgment only insofar as it imposes the heightened burden and directions dependent on it, and remand the live retaliation claim under ordinary civil standards, preserving the existing remand otherwise. Previously resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The District's municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No final immunity, liability, injury, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.
- Siegert v. Gilley: preserves early examination of a cognizable constitutional deprivation without converting its threshold holding into a special motive burden.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby: applies a valid governing substantive burden at summary judgment, without authorizing courts to invent a new one.
- New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: distinguishes the constitutional speech basis of its defamation burden from this retaliation action against an official.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: applies the claimant's protected-conduct and motivating-factor burden and the defendant's separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board: applies its actual-causation distinction, not school-specific substantive rules; a possible lawful reason is insufficient by itself.
- Pell v. Procunier and Turner v. Safley: preserve prison-access limitations and supply no unrestricted press-conference right.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: preserve the distinction between legal immunity review and disputed factual sufficiency; no new interlocutory-review right is created.

#### Law After Decision

Ordinary preponderance proof and competent direct or circumstantial evidence govern this motive-dependent claim. Specific support, early legal immunity decisions and tailored procedures remain available. The Court rejects both an unsupported higher quantum and a categorical discovery barrier derived from it, while retaining actual constitutional elements, actual causation and the existing conduct-date immunity defense.

## Institutional modification: Rufo, January 15, 1992

### Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004

Merits decision, January 15, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

*Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail*, 502 U.S. 367 (1992), Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004. October Term 1991. Argued 1991-10-09; decided 1992-01-15. Consolidated certiorari from the First Circuit, 915 F.2d 1557, affirming refusal to modify a jail consent decree, 734 F. Supp. 561. Officials seek relief under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b)(5) from a single-cell requirement to permit proposed double-celling. Both judgments and the standard governing modification are before the Court.

Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter participated at argument and at decision. Thomas took no part: argument preceded his October 23, 1991 oath and no reargument is established.

#### Judgment

**Both First Circuit judgments vacated and cases remanded, 6-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter.
**Opposing:** Blackmun, Stevens (would affirm denial of modification).
**Not participating:** Thomas.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone | White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter | Vacatur and remand | Full opinion |
| Concurrence in the judgment | O'Connor | — | Vacatur and remand | Judgment only |
| Dissent | Stevens | Blackmun | Would affirm denial of modification | Full dissent |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Threshold for modifying an institutional consent decree

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Rule 60(b)(5), the party seeking to modify an institutional consent decree must prove a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable; an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief, and dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices. O'Connor supplies a sixth remand vote on her separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that prospective institutional relief remains subject to an equitable assessment of changed circumstances. Swift addressed a particular antitrust decree and does not impose its demanding factual formulation on every continuing public institution. System Federation confirms that continuing decrees can respond to altered circumstances; United Shoe ties modification to the decree's actual operation and purposes. Rule 60(b)(5) therefore requires a supported significant change, not proof that officials could never have foreseen it. Anticipation still matters greatly. Officials who accepted a known risk must explain why enforcement has nevertheless become inequitable, rather than merely regret their bargain. Here repeated commitment to single occupancy cannot be ignored. But the district court's alternative reliance on that term's centrality does not show that it assessed the requested accommodation free of the restrictive premise. Remand permits that assessment. The Court does not find that population growth, cost, or any other asserted change actually satisfies the officials' burden.

##### Fit and limits of the proposed modification

**Holding and operative rule:** A justified modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite a consent decree to the constitutional minimum. Bell's clarification of constitutional limits does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted single-cell obligation, and financial pressure cannot authorize unconstitutional confinement.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; the same five-Justice majority controls the remedial standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a threshold change does not authorize abandonment of the settlement. A consent decree can require more than a court could have compelled after a constitutional trial, as Local No. 93 explains. Bell rejects a categorical constitutional objection to double-celling, but it does not dissolve an independently accepted single-cell promise. The district court must connect any alteration to the established change and examine whether the revised protection remains faithful to the decree's purposes. It may consider practical administration and cost in choosing lawful relief while preserving its own equitable judgment; those considerations cannot excuse unconstitutional conditions or substitute for the threshold showing. System Federation supports adaptation, not a unilateral withdrawal from binding obligations. Because the proper standard was not applied, this Court selects no replacement design. The need for double cells, the proposed arrangement's constitutionality, and the adequacy of remaining safeguards are left for the district court on the record.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

Modification under Rule 60(b)(5) concerns the continuing prospective operation of an existing institutional decree following a demonstrated significant change in fact or law. It does not establish a legislative power to compel reinstatement of a completed private damages or limitations judgment, nor does it decide whether a separate valid statutory remedy may provide collateral relief from such a judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *United States v. Swift & Co.*, 286 U.S. 106 — its disposition is preserved; its demanding factual formulation is confined to its setting rather than imposed universally.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — applied to preserve equitable power to adjust continuing obligations to changed conditions, while maintaining judicial control of lawful relief.
- *United States v. United Shoe Machinery Corp.*, 391 U.S. 244 — applied to connect modification to the continuing decree's purpose and operation.
- *Board of Education v. Dowell*, 498 U.S. 237 — reinforces contextual treatment of institutional equitable relief; no school-desegregation termination rule is imported.
- *Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland*, 478 U.S. 501 — consent obligations may exceed what constitutional liability alone would require.
- *Bell v. Wolfish*, 441 U.S. 520 — its constitutional treatment of double-celling does not erase a consensual single-cell requirement.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 60(b)(5) now supplies a two-part institutional-decree inquiry: significant change making continued enforcement inequitable, then suitably tailored relief. Actual anticipation weighs heavily against relief without an absolute unforeseeability requirement. Consent obligations may exceed constitutional minima; fiscal concerns cannot excuse violations. Application to this proposed design remains open.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

O'Connor concurs only in the judgment. She agrees that reconsideration is necessary because anticipation was treated as dispositive, relevant financial concerns were excluded, and any departure from single occupancy was treated as defeating the decree. She emphasizes the district judge's experience and discretion and does not compel administrative deference on whether modification is warranted. Stevens, joined by Blackmun, accepts flexibility but would affirm: the officials anticipated growth and reaffirmed single occupancy in 1985, and the trial court's alternative explanation adequately protects settlement reliance.


## Institutional remedy: Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996

### Lewis v. Casey, No. 94-1511

Decision, June 24, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 29, 1995; decided June 24, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's December 27, 1994 judgment, 43 F.3d 1261, reviewing the Arizona access-to-courts injunction entered October 13, 1993.

The certified class comprises adults in Arizona Department of Corrections custody. The district court found deficient assistance causing dismissal with prejudice, supported by named plaintiff Bartholic's evidence, and inability to file, supported by witness Harris's evidence. Recurring resource and assistance barriers, differences between institutions and improvements after filing are also in the record. These findings establish real obstruction at their supported scope, not an identical violation throughout every prison.

The State conceded initial standing while disputing proof warranting relief. That concession does not resolve Article III or admit statewide liability. The Ninth Circuit stated that the identified general actual-injury issue was not before it; that statement is not a blanket forfeiture ruling. The represented reach of the wrong and the statewide decree remain at issue.

The decree under review is already modified: the $46 eligibility threshold and typewriter requirement were vacated below; copying-price justification and master-fee objections were remanded. Other claims litigated in the class action are outside this access appeal. The original PLRA, enacted April 26, 1996, governs the prospective-relief questions at the scope stated below.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unsupported classwide liability and remedial approval | Set aside and remanded, 8-1 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens | Preserve demonstrated obstruction and supported findings; determine supported scope and lawful relief. Stevens would preserve liability at its demonstrated scope and remand the excessive remedy on narrower grounds. |
| Prospective relief under the original PLRA | Approval set aside for lawful reconsideration, 9-0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | | Reconsider prospective relief under the original PLRA; no termination motion or automatic stay is entered. |

The remand preserves Bartholic's and Harris's demonstrated obstruction and the existing evidence. It does not affirm an unqualified statewide core-Bounds violation, order dismissal of all claims, require relitigation of established factual obstruction, or guarantee continuation of the present decree. Any relief must fit the proved or properly established current wrong and the governing statutory requirements. No termination motion, automatic stay, new appointment, fixed resource minimum or prisoner-release order is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Opinion of the Court, Part IV | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Sets aside prospective-relief approval for statutory reconsideration | Original-PLRA rules and statutory remand; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition without adding a ground |
| Concurrence | Scalia | O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas | Supports remand with additional grounds adopted by four Justices | Additional standing, represented-injury and categorical access-scope rules; four-Justice position |
| Concurrence | Thomas |  | Joins the disposition and adopted rules; adds separate institutional doubts | Doubts about affirmative assistance and structural administration; no overruling of Bounds |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Souter | Ginsburg, Breyer | Agrees with remand on Parts I, III and IV and distinct grounds | Joins I, III, IV; distinct concrete-grievance, representation and continuing-assistance grounds |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens |  | Accepts statutory remedial remand; opposes the broader classwide disposition | Joins I and IV; narrower liability-preserving remedial route; rejects II and III |

#### Holdings

##### I. Effective access cannot be measured by books alone

**Operative rule:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. It guarantees neither a preferred library program, physical browsing in every case, professional counsel for every prisoner nor the decree's numerical requirements.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join.

**Controlling explanation:** Bounds identifies effective access as the protected object and leaves administrators a choice of adequate methods. Murray preserves that choice without making books an automatic safe harbor. The district findings concerning Bartholic's dismissed action and Harris's inability to file are concrete consequences of inadequate assistance. They cannot be rewritten as an absence of injury simply because a library existed.

An efficient paging system with an inventory and prompt delivery may work for prisoners capable of independent use. Respondents' conditional acknowledgment does not establish that Arizona's actual system worked or that it serves prisoners with different literacy or language needs. The complete arrangement and actual obstacle matter.

The evidence distinguishes findings from testimony and common practices from statewide conclusions. Bartholic is a named representative; Harris supplies witness evidence. No fixed total of injured prisoners follows. Nor must a prisoner prove eventual victory in a case that inadequate access prevented him from pursuing. The opinions differ on the additional threshold described in Part II, not on the reality of these preserved findings.

##### II. The access violation requires a real connection to a qualifying claim

**Operative rule:** A Bounds access-liability claim requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying, nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to the validity of restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing, but a deficient inventory, preferred staffing arrangement or abstract wish for better research does not alone establish the violation.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt this substantive access-liability rule. The additional preliminary Article III merits screen and categorical restrictions in Scalia's concurrence have only four votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Libraries and assistance are means of enabling protected legal claims, rather than independent constitutional ends. The required connection identifies what a deficient arrangement actually prevents. A technical dismissal caused by inability to meet a filing requirement, an inability to file a contemplated qualifying claim, or sufficiently concrete threatened obstruction can establish that connection. A procedural deficiency does not itself make the underlying claim substantively frivolous.

The rule is not a demand for ultimate success or a rigid sequence of preliminary prison tests. Evidence and reasonable inferences may establish the real claim and obstacle before an avoidable filing failure occurs. Lujan's ordinary requirements of personal injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain; today's five-Justice proposition does not additionally require preliminary proof of legal merits as an Article III screening rule.

Direct and collateral sentence challenges and prison-conditions claims fall within the governing doctrine. Genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin's actual substantive-constraint rule. The Court does not adopt a categorical exclusion of all other important retained rights, declare that every affirmative assistance duty ends on filing, or provide a general subsidy for private civil litigation. Properly presented independent communications or petition claims retain their own predicates. Those reservations do not turn every resource request into an access violation.

##### III. The scope of the wrong and the needs of repair control relief

**Operative rule:** Common relief must correspond to a demonstrated common barrier and an identified affected group; individual obstruction or a resource deficiency alone does not establish an unrestricted statewide violation. Relief must respect restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests and give the State a genuine opportunity to devise an effective cure, while permitting measures actually necessary to repair the proved wrong.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this rule and the broader remand. Stevens supports a narrower remedial remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower courts' core-Bounds shortcut allowed resource deficiencies to do the work of proving the full violation. The findings preserve actual obstruction but do not establish the same denial at every institution, or separate broad entitlements for all non-English-speaking or lockdown prisoners. Bartholic's location in a lockdown unit does not itself show injury caused by lockdown status rather than inadequate assistance. This evidentiary limit is not a finding that only two inmates were ever injured.

Common evidence may prove a common wrong; every class member need not already have lost a lawsuit. Geographic and functional reach nevertheless require support. Jenkins permits necessary implementation of a demonstrated violation without making each salary, hour or program measure an independent constitutional right. It does not justify general prison improvement or indefinite control.

Turner sustains restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests, with substantial deference especially relevant to dangerous or specially restricted prisoners. Delay caused by a valid restriction does not become unconstitutional merely because an access injury results. Conversely, asserting security does not prove that every restriction is valid; the actual relationship must be examined.

Arizona had participated and objected, but an opportunity merely to rebut a presumptive statewide Gluth template did not provide the required genuine initial design role. Earlier resistance elsewhere does not establish obstruction here or forfeit that role. The State must now receive that opportunity within enforceable constitutional limits. These conclusions warrant setting aside the unsupported approval while retaining findings, common evidence and lawful targeted relief.

##### IV. The original PLRA governs existing prospective relief and any replacement

**Operative rule:** Section 802 applies amended section 3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996; relief here requires findings that it is narrowly drawn, extends no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation of the particular plaintiffs, and is the least intrusive means necessary, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Relief exceeding official state-law authority or otherwise violating state or local law additionally requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation, and absence of any other corrective relief.

**Authority:** Part IV of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices. The Chief Justice joins the statutory remand disposition without adding a ground. The same eight-Justice authority governs the distinct statutory conditions in Holdings V and VI.

**Controlling explanation:** The enactment expressly reaches existing prospective relief, including this federal prison-conditions decree. Prospective relief includes relief other than compensatory monetary damages. The habeas exclusion does not remove a prison-conditions action merely because the access services may assist habeas litigation.

The existing findings may establish necessary predicates at their actual scope; the statute requires substance, not ritual wording. But reasonableness, modest cost or absence of demonstrated hardship does not replace need, narrowness and least intrusiveness. Effective alternatives and public safety require actual evaluation. Necessary implementation can qualify even when each measure is not itself a freestanding constitutional entitlement.

Section 3626(a)(1)(B)'s three conditions operate separately from (A). Subsection (C) confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable limits; it does not create a new prohibition on every otherwise lawful means. No state-law conflict or prisoner-release remedy is found here.

Any new preliminary relief must be otherwise authorized, narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary for the harm requiring it, and least intrusive, with substantial weight to safety and criminal-system effects and respect for the separate state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the required prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. The old permanent decree is not automatically converted into a new preliminary order.

No constitutional challenge to section 802 is decided. The decree must be reconsidered under these actual terms without erasing established access wrongs or presuming that every old measure must survive or disappear.

##### V. Termination and stays depend on their own statutory predicates

**Operative rule:** The original section 3626 termination provisions do not dissolve relief merely upon enactment: the immediate-termination entitlement for relief entered without the required findings remains subject to written, record-based findings that relief is necessary to correct a current OR ongoing federal-right violation and remains narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary, and least intrusive. The automatic stay requires an actual pending qualifying motion and runs only for the original statutory period described below.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice Part IV coalition identified above; the Chief Justice agrees with the statutory disposition without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Under (b)(1), any party or intervener may seek termination at the applicable two-year period after relief was granted or approved, one year after denial of a termination motion under that paragraph, or, for preenactment relief, two years after enactment. Earlier agreement is permitted. Under (b)(2), a defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination when the necessary findings were absent, subject to (b)(3)'s written findings on the record. The original condition is current OR ongoing, not a requirement that both words separately be satisfied. Section (b)(4) preserves earlier modification or termination otherwise legally available.

Courts must promptly decide modification or termination motions. Under original (e), relief subject to a pending (b)(1) or (b)(2) motion is automatically stayed beginning on the thirtieth day after filing; for a motion under other law, the period begins on the 180th day. It ends when the court enters the final order deciding the motion. No qualifying filing is established here, and the Court creates none.

Consent decrees remain subject to subsection (a). Private agreements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct, and available state-law remedies for breach remain. The statute does not govern relief entered by a state court solely on state-law claims. These distinctions authorize no new settlement or termination in this case.

The present remand follows review of the decree and intervening law. It is not an automatic stay, a ruling on any termination motion or a finding that current necessary relief is unavailable.

##### VI. Continued use of a special master must satisfy the original statute

**Operative rule:** A special master in this prison-conditions remedy must be disinterested and objective, give due regard to public safety, and be warranted by a finding of sufficient remedial complexity; appointments, compensation, work and continued need must comply with section 3626(f)'s separate conditions below. Those requirements apply to the master's statutory role regardless of title and do not themselves establish misconduct or invalidate every prior act or accrued fee.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice Part IV coalition identified above; the Chief Justice agrees with the statutory disposition without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** When an appointment under (f) is necessary, the institution and plaintiffs each submit up to five names. Each side may strike up to three from the opposing list, and the court selects from the remaining names. A party may bring the prescribed interlocutory appeal on partiality. Compensation may not exceed the section 3006A hourly rate for appointed counsel, plus reasonably incurred costs; Judiciary appropriations supply payment.

The court must review continuing need every six months. Authorized hearings and proposed findings must be on the record; ex parte findings and communications are forbidden. A master may assist development of remedial plans and may be removed at any time, but service must end when the relief terminates. The statute is not evaded by calling a person exercising a master's powers something else.

The already ordered opportunity to challenge fees survives. Actual questions about accrued charges and temporal application require determination on remand; the Court orders no repayment and assumes no retrospective invalidity of all work. The district court may retain necessary lawful assistance only on the statutory conditions. No automatic new appointment follows from the complexity of the former decree.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817, and Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539:** Preserve meaningful access and effective methods rather than a constitutional library specification. Bounds is not overruled; four proposed categorical limits do not become Court law.
- **Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1:** Preserves choice among effective assistance arrangements without a general right to appointed counsel for every prisoner.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, and City of Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95:** Preserve personal injury, causation, appropriate prospective exposure and proof at the litigation stage. The Court does not adopt the four-Justice preliminary merits screen or decide standing in gross from a party concession.
- **Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78:** Applies reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, with actual examination and proper deference rather than automatic acceptance of a security assertion.
- **Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995:** Applies violation-linked repair while preserving implementation measures necessary to correct a proved wrong. Each means need not independently violate the Constitution.
- **Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367:** Preserves lawful responsive modification; the new statute's distinct mandatory requirements must also be applied.
- **Sandin v. Conner, June 19, 1995:** Preserves its actual rule for meaningful state-created prison liberty under genuine substantive constraints. No categorical hardship replacement is imported to exclude access to those claims.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc., June 30, 1994:** Preserves connection between remedial terms and their predicate; its speech-specific standard is not a universal least-restrictive-means rule or a substitute for section 3626.

No precedent is overruled. Gluth's earlier remedy supplies background and a proposed template, not a finding of statewide injury in this record.

#### Law After Decision

The controlling access-liability rule requires actual or evidence-supported imminent obstruction of a qualifying nonfrivolous claim, without requiring ultimate success or an impossible completed filing. Five Justices adopt that rule; only four would additionally make preliminary arguable-merits proof a jurisdictional screen, impose their subgroup-standing rule, or categorically confine affirmative Bounds capability to sentence/conditions claims and initial presentation. Those additional propositions are not law.

Effective assistance, security, represented scope and violation-linked relief remain distinct. Supported common proof may justify corresponding common relief, but the existing findings do not sustain this unrestricted statewide decree. The demonstrated access wrong survives. The original PLRA independently controls prospective relief, termination procedures, conditional stays and masters at the exact scopes above; enactment creates no motion or automatic dissolution.

The former decree's operational numbers are not constitutional minima. Its library-placement rule had a 150-person population-or-capacity trigger and exceptions for Aspen DWI, Flamenco, Papago DWI, Maricopa and Picacho; exempt facilities still owed access opportunities. Documented safety/security inability and medical determinations permitted postponement. Requested use was ten actual hours weekly with extra deadline time; opening hours varied between fifty without advance scheduling and sixty, seventy or eighty under population-based scheduling bands. Assistance was needs-based, and librarian qualifications included library-science, law or paralegal degrees.

The phone provision concerned three twenty-minute weekly calls to attorneys, designated representatives or legal organizations, with extra emergency calls, private unrecorded communications, payment or collect calling, exclusions for uncompleted calls and a documented-abuse qualification. These terms, copying and other asserted communications protections require their own supported remedial connection or properly presented independent legal basis. None is uniformly adopted or categorically prohibited today.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Scalia concurs, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. They would require actual or sufficiently imminent obstruction of a nonfrivolous claim as an Article III prerequisite, not merely as the substantive access rule adopted by five Justices. After trial a named plaintiff must prove personal injury from the type of administrative deficiency challenged; standing is not conferred in gross. Bartholic's literacy-related obstruction and Harris's witness evidence do not, in their view, establish separate named-plaintiff injury supporting every non-English or lockdown remedy. The separate absence of proof of an illiteracy violation throughout the system is a merits and evidentiary-scope failure.

These four would confine the affirmative Bounds capability to bringing direct or collateral sentence attacks and prison-conditions claims before a court. They reject a general duty to discover unspecified grievances, provide legal sophistication for effective litigation after entry, or subsidize every private civil action. They do not authorize obstruction of an independently protected right, require cutting off every resource immediately on filing, or overrule Bounds. Their additional rules have four votes.

Justice Souter concurs in Parts I, III and IV and in the remand judgment, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer. They require a concrete personal grievance and an inadequate means of pursuing it, not an abstract desire for a better library. They reject requiring preliminary proof of arguable legal merits before assistance needed to evaluate the grievance. Ordinarily a prisoner must identify a concrete concern about confinement or conviction and obstruction of research, consultation, filing or litigation as pertinent. They reserve the exceptional possibility of substantial denial of every means of access without presuming that situation here.

Bartholic supplies a named representative's anchor; Rule 23 typicality and adequacy govern representation, without a separate Article III showing by every unnamed member. The unsupported breadth may be rejected on the merits without deciding the four's additional standing theory. Necessary assistance may continue between complaint and disposition. Important retained rights, including family-status interests, are not categorically excluded, but no general civil-litigation subsidy follows. These three preserve the demonstrated wrong and accept the common proof, security, State-choice and statutory limits requiring remand.

Justice Thomas also concurs separately. He distinguishes officials' obstruction of access from an affirmative obligation to finance legal assistance. Griffin and Douglas involved bounded equal-access protections; Ross, Finley and Murray resist turning those decisions into a general assistance entitlement. He questions the fit of Bounds with those foundations and stresses limits on structural judicial administration. He nevertheless joins the Court's disposition and adopted rules without overruling Bounds. His stricter institutional views do not displace the controlling Jenkins rule permitting necessary implementation or the actual Sandin rule; no other Justice joins these additional doubts.

Justice Stevens joins Parts I and IV and concurs in the remedial remand, but dissents from Parts II-III and the broader classwide disposition. Personal denial of effective access is the injury. His Bounds and Murray approach does not require a prisoner to establish the legal merit of a grievance before receiving the assistance needed to present it, and he would decline the new categorical narrowing urged by four Justices.

The concrete findings and the State's initial-standing concession make this a poor vehicle for wider jurisdictional and merits restrictions, although the concession cannot create jurisdiction. He would preserve the adjudicated wrong at its actual scope and correct the excessive decree under the original PLRA. He also rejects the Court's assessment of the State's remedial opportunity: Gluth could reasonably provide a starting point, officials made proposals and objections, and some objections were accepted. He recognizes that a genuine negotiation breakdown could warrant remand but does not find the denial of a fair opportunity asserted by the other Justices. Neither his account nor his criticism of participation establishes bad faith or forfeiture. He endorses no unqualified statewide liability or constitutional entitlement to every decree term.

### Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, No. 91-794

Merits disposition, 1993-06-18

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued 1992-12-02; decided 1993-06-18. On writ of certiorari to Supreme Court of Virginia, 242 Va.322, 410 S.E.2d 629: Davis withheld retroactively from pending pension-tax refund claims; state refund construction rested on that premise.

The question presented is whether Davis applies to these still-open direct-review claims and what tax remedy must follow.

Virginia taxed federal retirement benefits while exempting comparable state and local retirement benefits. The retirees sought refunds for earlier tax periods while their cases remained open. The state court relied on prospectivity and also construed its refund statute through that premise. The record requires separation of the federal rule’s temporal reach from the state’s available procedures and corrective remedies.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

#### Judgment

Reversed and remanded, 8–1.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review

**Controlling proposition:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether those events predate or postdate announcement. A State cannot use selective prospectivity to withhold the rule; Chevron Oil is displaced to the extent it permits that practice, without reopening final judgments or deciding every conceivable prospective disposition.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, supported on this holding by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, controls by a majority of 6 Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that similarly situated pending litigants receive the same governing federal law. Griffith rejects selective application on criminal direct review, and Beam supplies the civil foundation when the Court already applied the rule to its own litigants. Davis applied federal nondiscrimination law to the pension taxation before it; Michigan’s willingness to refund did not reserve the rule’s temporal operation. Virginia therefore cannot use Chevron Oil to preserve the same discrimination for other pending claimants. Reliance and fiscal disruption are substantial practical concerns, but they cannot make the substantive federal rule vary among contemporaneous open cases. The state refund interpretation here depends on the rejected prospectivity premise and is not an independent state ground insulating that error. The Court distinguishes finality and remedy from choice of governing law. It does not reopen closed judgments, calculate refunds, or decide a hypothetical case in which the Court expressly reserves application to the parties before it.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — nondiscrimination rule applied to pending claims.
- Griffith, 479 U.S.314 — equal direct-review application extended to this civil setting.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — its rejection of selective application clarified through a direct majority rule.
- Chevron Oil, 404 U.S.97 — displaced only insofar as it permits selective prospectivity after application to the original litigants.

##### Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate

**Controlling proposition:** The state courts must apply Davis and determine the correction state law and federal due process require. If the State compelled payment before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, meaningful backward-looking relief is required; lawful refunds, retroactive nondiscriminatory assessments or a combination may suffice, while a meaningful prepayment opportunity presents a different remedial setting.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, supported on this holding by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, controls by a majority of 8 Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that application of the federal rule does not mechanically select a refund amount. McKesson requires meaningful backward-looking correction when a State collects first and provides no adequate prepayment opportunity to challenge an unconstitutional discriminatory tax. The State may have more than one lawful means of correcting the discrimination, including refunds, constitutionally permissible assessment of previously favored taxpayers, or a combination. Davis similarly distinguishes the nondiscrimination command from the appropriate corrective mechanism. The present record requires the state courts to examine their procedures and actual remedial authority rather than assume either an automatic full refund or unconditional retention of the taxes. Genuine procedural bars and remedial considerations may operate consistently with federal law, but they cannot merely reintroduce selective prospectivity under another name. The Court does not impose liability on absent taxpayers without lawful process or invent a state appropriation. It reverses the federal-law premise and remands the remedy for determination under the proper sources.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McKesson, 496 U.S.18 — pay-first systems require meaningful backward-looking relief; alternatives preserved.
- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — correction of unequal taxation distinguished from a particular refund formula.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — procedural and remedial questions kept distinct from temporal governing law.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Six directly adopt the general rule; eight adopt the remedial holding and reversal. Finality and genuine remedial rules survive; selective prospectivity does not.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy, joined by White, concurs in the judgment and joins the background and remedial portion. Davis was sufficiently grounded in existing federal tax-immunity law that its application to these claims is required without eliminating every role for Chevron Oil. They decline the broader general retroactivity formulation.

Scalia joins the Court and explains that judicial decisions declare the governing law; policy-based temporal lawmaking is inconsistent with that role. His broader theoretical account does not enlarge the Court’s stated trigger or reopen final judgments.

O’Connor dissents. Her Beam position preserves Chevron Oil’s attention to a genuinely new rule, reliance and disruption in civil cases. She would sustain Virginia’s prospective treatment, regarding Michigan’s conceded refund as insufficient to compel the general retroactivity conclusion. Her position does not command the Court.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### This Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor is permissible

**Controlling proposition:** An uncounseled misdemeanor conviction valid under Scott because no imprisonment was imposed may supply the otherwise authorized criminal-history point in the Guidelines setting presented here: the later prosecution is counseled and the sentencing framework provides a genuine opportunity to contest reliability, relevance and significant overrepresentation through its lawful procedures, including a permitted §4A1.3 departure. This limited permission neither makes a departure mandatory nor adopts a general rule for a scheme that conclusively requires enhanced incarceration without that opportunity.

**Authority:** O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, permits this application under a broader valid-conviction rule; Souter's concurrence in the judgment expressly permits this Guidelines application on the narrower reliability-and-opportunity ground. Five Justices support the stated permission at this level; no complete rationale has five joins, and Souter supplies no fifth vote to overrule Baldasar generally.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court permits this use within the sentencing framework actually presented. Scott leaves the original fine-only conviction valid; the question is the consequence of that conviction in a later counseled prosecution. Section 4A1.1(c) assigns one point to a prior sentence not included under its more serious categories, subject to its cap, and the commentary expressly includes DUI rather than treating it as a minor traffic infraction. The framework also provides an opportunity to contest sentencing information and permits a downward departure for significant overrepresentation under §4A1.3. A claim that every such Guidelines use is forbidden therefore does not prevail here. Nichols did not present that overrepresentation contention, and the Court neither orders a departure nor finds independent proof of the DUI conduct. Baldasar involved a different later misdemeanor-to-felony use and yielded incompatible rationales; its judgment cannot be turned into five identical reasons governing this case. The shared permission is confined to these conditions. The broader justification, automatic-enhancement question and proposed general overruling retain their distinct voting support.

- Baldasar v. Illinois, 446 U.S. 222 (1980): its particular judgment and fractured reasoning remain; only four Justices vote for general overruling, and the Court permits the present Guidelines application without deciding a conclusive mandatory-enhancement scheme.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 (1977): does not convert Blackmun's original-invalidity rationale in Baldasar into five votes for the valid-but-unavailable rationale, or make four present overruling votes five.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Scott remains the original fine-only validity rule. Baldasar's particular judgment and fractured reasons remain; four votes do not overrule it generally. Marks does not create missing joins. Custis concerns a distinct ACCA forum, while Parke and Godinez do not establish waiver here. Stinson preserves constitutional scrutiny of commentary. The five-Justice permission extends only to the identified Guidelines setting.

The relevant November 1990 rules distinguish the score from departure. Section 4A1.1(a) assigns three points for a prior imprisonment sentence exceeding one year and one month; (b) assigns two for a prior sentence of imprisonment of at least sixty days not included under (a); (c) assigns one for each other prior sentence not included under (a) or (b), capped at four points. A prior sentence is a sentence previously imposed after adjudication of guilt by guilty plea, trial or nolo contendere for conduct not part of the instant offense. Section 4A1.2(e)(1) counts a prior imprisonment sentence exceeding one year and one month if imposed within fifteen years of commencement of the instant offense, or whenever imposed if it resulted in incarceration during any part of that fifteen-year period. Subsection (e)(2) counts other prior sentences imposed within ten years of commencement, and (e)(3) excludes prior sentences outside those periods. Commencement includes relevant conduct; the distinct age-under-eighteen provisions retain their own periods, and no such issue or disputed commencement date is presented here. Note 5 expressly counts DUI and similar offenses and excludes them from the minor-traffic category. No different enumerated exception or timing objection to this DUI is presented.

Section 4A1.2(c)(1)'s listed or similar misdemeanor offenses count only with at least one year's probation, at least thirty days' imprisonment, or similarity to an instant offense. That list comprises careless or reckless driving, contempt, disorderly conduct or disturbing the peace, driving without a license or with a revoked or suspended license, false information to police, fish and game violations, gambling, hindering or failure to obey police, insufficient-funds checks, leaving an accident, local-ordinance violations except ones also violating state criminal law, nonsupport, prostitution, resisting arrest and trespassing. Subsection (c)(2) excludes its listed or similar hitchhiking, juvenile-status or truancy, loitering, minor-traffic, public-intoxication and vagrancy offenses. The Court does not recategorize DUI into those exceptions.

Note 6 excludes sentences already reversed or vacated for legal error or newly discovered exonerating evidence, and convictions previously ruled constitutionally invalid, while allowing consideration of underlying conduct under §4A1.3 where lawful. It does not decide every collateral-review forum. The background commentary counts otherwise includable uncounseled misdemeanor sentences when no imprisonment was imposed and leaves collateral-attack availability to courts.

Section 4A1.3 permits departure on reliable information when the category significantly overrepresents criminal-history seriousness or the likelihood of further crimes. A lower-category range supplies the reference for that ground; departure is discretionary and cannot go below Category I's lower limit on this criminal-history-adequacy ground. A prior arrest record itself may not be considered under §4A1.3. Nichols did not seek departure on that ground; no departure is invented, and the Court does not decide whether a sufficient showing could be made. The original point's arithmetic is not optional merely because departure may be available.

Five Justices permit this Guidelines application; four endorse general later-use permission and general Baldasar overruling, and only those four adopt a broad future-enhancement-warning rule. The complete plurality rationale is not an Opinion of the Court. Baldasar is not generally overruled. A conclusive mandatory-enhancement scheme without the opportunity material to Souter's concurrence remains undecided. The Government's waiver and indigency alternatives remain unresolved because the affirmance rests on the respective sufficient use grounds; no valid waiver or nonindigency is found. The actual 235-month sentence and eight-year supervised-release term are left undisturbed, without declaring an alternative category-II departure, proving the underlying DUI anew, or creating a fixed 25-month reduction.
