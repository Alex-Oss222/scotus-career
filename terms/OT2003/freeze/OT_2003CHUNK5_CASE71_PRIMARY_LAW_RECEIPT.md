## Verified legal consequence and qualifications

1. Section 30(1), notwithstanding Florida Statutes section 100.061, sets the 2004 party-nomination primary for the Tuesday nine weeks before the general election. The candidate with the highest number of votes in each contest receives the nomination. If two or more candidates share the equal highest number, lots decide the nominee. The rule requires a plurality, not an absolute majority or a second primary. Its text addresses nomination to the office; it does not itself guarantee victory in the general election.
2. Section 30(2), notwithstanding section 100.091 and any contrary provision of the Florida Election Code, eliminates the second primary between the Act's effective date and January 1, 2006. Section 32 makes the Act effective January 1, 2004 except where otherwise provided. Section 30 contains no different effective date. Sections 5 and 10 expressly become effective when the Act becomes law; those distinct provisions do not accelerate or postpone section 30.
3. Section 30 also supplies 2004 deadlines for designating and qualifying the Lieutenant Governor running mate, campaign reporting schedules, and two elections for contribution-limit purposes. None conditions the operation of section 30(1) or (2) on a further filing, agency decision, or finding. Section 31 directs harmonization with amendments enacted in the 2003 Regular Session or Special Session A, giving each full effect if possible. This receipt verifies this chapter and does not infer unexamined conflicting enactments or later law.
4. Accordingly, the April 29, 2004 analysis cannot assume that a second-primary majority requirement returned on January 1, 2004. The verified legal premise for the 2004 primary is plurality nomination without a second primary. No conclusion about what later legislation might do after the event date is drawn.
5. The chapter does not establish that any particular district will or will not afford minority voters an equal opportunity to elect their candidate of choice under a plurality system. It supplies no new finding on candidate entry, vote splitting, racial cohesion, turnout, crossover voting, open or closed primary status, write-in qualification, or expected performance. The lower opinion's district-specific findings, assumptions, concessions, and limits remain distinct; their sufficiency under governing law must be assessed without substituting a statutory change for evidence.
6. In particular, this source does not establish a qualifying write-in candidacy, make the conditional open-primary scenario inevitable, or show that voters will necessarily coalesce behind one candidate before a single plurality primary. The lower court's forecasts about those matters remain forecasts. It also does not itself establish a new preserved claim or resolve whether an evidentiary remand is required. Those questions await the completed governing-law handoff and Justice-specific modeling.
