### Issue 1: Bankruptcy discharge and state immunity

* **Controlling Law:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank permits express private enforcement against States under valid Article I obligations. Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett applies that rule to Title I and holds that §5 does not independently sustain Title I's general employment requirements at the full scope presented. Historical categorical Article I immunity rules cannot replace these holdings.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify valid substantive power, unmistakable enforcement authorization and the actual remedy. An adjudication of a debtor’s estate and discharge differs from affirmative personal recovery against the sovereign.
* **Other Governing Law:** Bankruptcy Clause; 11 U.S.C. §§106(a), 523(a)(8) and the discharge provisions; Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001.
* **Material Other Authority:** The longstanding in rem character of bankruptcy provides an additional source-specific basis. A procedural summons does not by itself change the substantive relief sought.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The controlling Article I rule already rejects the asserted categorical bar; the precise discharge proceeding also presents the in rem distinction.
* **Open Point:** Whether determining undue hardship and discharge requires jurisdiction over the State as a personal defendant, notwithstanding the proceeding’s estate-centered effect.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit sustained Congress’s bankruptcy authority to authorize the proceeding despite the State’s immunity objection.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** TSAC argues that an adversary complaint and summons compel the State into a private federal suit and that nonparticipation preserves immunity.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Hood argues that federal bankruptcy jurisdiction determines discharge without requiring a creditor’s consent; she also relies on Congress’s explicit authorization.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** The discharge may proceed on the in rem ground without revisiting the Article I precedent. Affirmative recovery from the treasury is a different remedy.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Denying immunity permits an undue-hardship adjudication, not immediate discharge. Treating the complaint as an impermissible personal suit would require dismissal or consideration of a lawful alternative discharge procedure. Under the Article I holding, a categorical immunity objection cannot succeed without openly changing entering law.
