## 89. Republic of Austria v. Altmann — June 7, 2004

### Reconciled common rationale

**A1 — Reach of the current immunity scheme.** The FSIA governs this post-enactment suit's claim to sovereign immunity although the alleged conduct preceded both 1976 and the 1952 restrictive-immunity policy. Sections 1330, 1602 and 1604 form an integrated current regime; the relevant exceptions retain their own predicates. Section 1602's direction that courts henceforth decide immunity claims within that regime concerns the immunity assertion now presented, not a newly created substantive property claim.

**A2 — Text, function and temporal analysis.** The answer does not follow from calling the statute jurisdictional, nor from the word henceforth taken alone as an express command covering past conduct. Landgraf's provision-specific inquiry and Hughes Aircraft's warning about newly enlarged enforcement exposure remain effective. Foreign-state immunity, however, supplies present protection from the inconvenience of a United States suit in a field historically governed by changing political-branch decisions; it did not guarantee that the forum policy existing when the conduct occurred would remain fixed. The FSIA leaves the substantive source of the property claim intact. Its present-immunity function, text and comprehensive structure together resolve the uncertainty; no general exception for governmental defendants or all immunity statutes is announced. Dole Food supports attention to filing-time statutory status and present forum protection without itself deciding this temporal question.

**A3 — Disposition and scope.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit on the temporal issue and allow the property litigation to proceed. The separate determination below that the expropriation exception applies remains outside the granted question. Ownership and taking are allegations at this stage. The grant does not decide title, act of state, limitations or execution. Reject the proposed exception-by-exception historical cutoff as the governing temporal construction: the enacted integrated scheme contains no general schedule dividing its present immunity determinations according to each exception's historical origin.

## 90. Central Laborers' Pension Fund v. Heinz — June 7, 2004

### Reconciled operative rule and application

**H1 — Protected payment entitlement.** Under section 204(g)'s anti-cutback rule, a plan amendment reduces an accrued early-retirement benefit when it adds an employment condition withholding payments that the participant could receive under the terms governing accrual. The nominal amount does not preserve the benefit when a new condition reduces the right to receive it. The loss occurs when the added restriction diminishes the accrued entitlement; an actual payment suspension demonstrates the injury here but is not what alone makes the amendment a cutback.

**H2 — Separate statutory commands.** Section 203(a)(3)(B) permits specified original suspension terms without treating the benefit as forfeitable; it does not authorize adding such a term after accrual contrary to section 204(g). In the multiemployer setting described, the suspension permission concerns work in the same industry, trade or craft and geographic area covered by the plan. Accrual and vesting are distinct statutory inquiries: absence of prohibited forfeiture does not establish absence of prohibited cutback. Lawful terms may govern future accruals, while already earned benefits retain their protected terms.

**H3 — Application and remedy.** The 1998 addition of previously permitted supervisory construction work to disqualifying employment violates the current anti-cutback command as applied to these accrued benefits. Affirm the Seventh Circuit's reversal of the plan's judgment on the pleadings. The case returns for restoration of payments withheld solely under the added restriction and the necessary benefit calculation.

All eight Associates support H1–H3. Spink and Jacobson retain sponsor design authority together with independent accrued-benefit limits; neither grants authority for this amendment. Section 204(g)'s exceptions for substantial business hardship under section 302(c)(8), 29 U.S.C. §1082(c)(8), and terminated multiemployer plans under 29 U.S.C. §1441 are outside the supplied claim. The holding applies to the challenged added employment condition, not an amendment invoking those separate statutory mechanisms.

The source's formal-regulation discussion confirms the same distinction already identified in the packet: 26 CFR §1.411(d)-4, A-6 permits objective conditions specified before accrual; A-2(a)(1) allows changes concerning benefits not yet accrued; A-7 prohibits adding conditions or further restrictions to an already accrued protected benefit. Its force is distinct from an unreasoned manual statement or inconsistent informal approval practice. The reconciled statutory ground does not depend on finding a new administrative reliance fact or deciding plan tax qualification. No post-event administrative measure enters.

## 92. City of Littleton v. Z. J. Gifts D-4, L.L.C. — June 7, 2004

### Reconciled propositions and distinct grounds

**L1 — Prompt judicial determination.** For the protected-expression licensing scheme presented, the safeguard against undue delay includes a prompt judicial determination, not merely an opportunity to file suit promptly. A potentially erroneous licensing denial cannot suppress protected expression indefinitely while the proceeding remains unresolved. FW/PBS's core protection against delay includes the judicial stage. Six Associates adopt this proposition: O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**L2 — Facial sufficiency of ordinary procedures.** A special fixed judicial deadline is not required for this scheme's objective, nondiscretionary licensing criteria. The available ordinary Colorado procedures supply powers of expedition and interim protection; their ordinary administration can produce prompt decisions, with judicial intervention when necessary to avoid significant First Amendment injury. Judges may be expected to use those tools consistently with the constitutional protection, and this facial record supplies no actual delayed appeal establishing their inadequacy. These considerations sustain the ordinance against the judicial-timing facial objection without placing a separate numerical judicial timetable in the ordinance. O'Connor, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this reasoning together with Stevens, subject to his different understanding of the underlying safeguard. This is a five-Associate proposition coalition.

**L3 — Judgment and next stage.** Reverse the Tenth Circuit's facial invalidation on judicial timing and remand consistently with the governing decision. The ordinance remains operative against that objection. A concrete application involving undue delay may require expedition, interim protection or other appropriate relief. That is application of the governing protection, not a claim that delay occurred here. All eight Associates reach this judgment, but their sufficient grounds differ.

**L-S — Stevens's distinct procedural ground.** Objective adult-business licensing is ministerial regulation rather than the direct content censorship addressed in Freedman. FW/PBS requires a prompt administrative decision and the possibility of prompt judicial review, but does not put the full censorship-system burden of ensuring a prompt judicial determination on the licensor. Ordinary court procedures supply the adequate possibility of promptness for this facial challenge. Stevens rejects equating that protection with Freedman's stronger requirement, because doing so risks either imposing censorship burdens on ordinary licensing or weakening the censorship safeguard. He joins L2 and L3, not L1 as formulated.

**L-SK — Souter's and Kennedy's distinct adequacy ground.** These Justices join L1 and the judgment and agree that a rigid Freedman-style timetable is unnecessary. They withhold assent from an adequacy explanation resting on the mere availability of judicial tools and an undifferentiated characterization of the scheme as neutral. Although the denial criteria are objective, adult-business licensing is triggered by the expressive materials to be sold, which creates a risk that unpopular sellers will encounter delay. The procedures must be accompanied by actual judicial practice providing prompt disposition; evidence of foot-dragging requires immediate intervention, and judicial oversight or review must be expeditious at every stage. The facial challenge fails without evidence that the available procedures cannot furnish that protection. Their ground is not a vote that ordinary procedures fail here.

**L-Sc — Scalia's sufficient conditional ground.** Even assuming protected activity, a general permit requirement unrelated to censorship of expressive content gives the business no special constitutional priority in the judicial process; the supplied ordinary procedures defeat the asserted demand for special timing rules. He therefore joins the judgment, without L1 or the lead opinion's affirmative timing entitlement. His earlier FW/PBS position additionally denies constitutional protection to the defined category of sex-pandering business. The approved packet does not supply the detailed ordinance definition or an adjudicated pandering predicate needed to make that broader ground an operative factual premise here. His historical Littleton writing expressly supplies the conditional ground first, which is sufficient without adding that premise. This is preservation of an independently stated sufficient ground, not adoption of protected status by Scalia or invention of an obscenity finding.

## 93. Belitskus v. Pizzingrilli — June 10, 2004

### Frozen common propositions retained

**B1 — Actual financial exclusion.** An otherwise qualified candidate unable to pay the ballot fee without financial hardship must have a realistic nonmonetary route. Mandatory payment without such a route severely excludes that candidate and associated electoral interests; under Anderson–Burdick as stated in Cartwright, the restriction requires narrow tailoring to a compelling interest. Administrative expense and screening seriousness do not justify choosing candidates by ability to pay when a usable support-based or hardship alternative serves the legitimate interest. Reasonable fees for candidates able to pay and lawful qualification and support requirements remain available.

**B2 — Application.** Sustain the inability-to-pay challenge of Stith, Linzey and the Pennsylvania Green Party. Bullock and Lubin protect against actual inability-to-pay exclusion, not ordinary inconvenience or a preference for a cheaper route. Reasonable proof of hardship may be required; the alternative must be usable. No financial threshold or affidavit form is invented.

**B3 — Justiciability.** The recurring candidate and party injuries support adjudication for Stith, Linzey and the party. Donovan's individual claim is moot because the requisite likelihood of recurrence to him is absent; affirm the direction to dismiss his claim. Schrader's continuing-party holding does not preserve every voter's completed-election claim.

All eight Associates support B1–B3. The legal rule's precedential reach is distinct from the permissible scope of the injunction.

**B4 — Fitted candidate-and-party relief.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the substantive ruling and Donovan disposition, vacate the appellate injunction restriction insofar as it caps enforceable relief at Stith and Linzey without securing the party's established recurring injury, and remand for a specific order addressing those actual plaintiff interests. Rule 65(d) requires concrete hardship conditions and official duties. The State may choose a meaningful lawful alternative; the order's scope must fit the proven operation and remedial need. The district court's open-ended protection of every financially unable candidate is not automatically restored, and party standing is not class certification.

**B5 — Current named-candidate limitation.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the appellate limitation to Stith and Linzey when they demonstrate the relevant hardship, together with the candidate-and-party merits disposition and Donovan dismissal. They regard those terms as adequate on the established as-applied record. ## 99. F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A.

### Corrected operative commitments

**E1R — Statutory coverage and the claimant's connection.** Section 6a's general exclusion reaches nonimport foreign commerce, including wholly foreign transactions; it is not limited to American exports. The statutory structure, the prescriptive-comity principle of avoiding unreasonable interference with foreign sovereign regulation, and the enactment's clarification rather than substantial expansion of preexisting geographic coverage favor the latter construction. The 1982 change from export-only wording to nonimport foreign commerce confirms the general exclusion's coverage of wholly foreign transactions. The cited legislative history supports that statutory context; it does not erase a contrary enacted term.

The domestic-effects exception protects claims arising from the qualifying domestic injury. Extending it to independent foreign injury would regulate foreign commercial affairs without that claim connection. The construction does not call for an empirical finding of incompatible foreign commands, an open-ended country-by-country balancing trial, or an automatic foreign-permission defense. ## 95. Norton v. Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance

### Final operative commitments

All eight Associates reverse the Tenth Circuit judgment insofar as it restored the challenged section 706(1) theories, and remand to implement the following component dispositions. The generalized nonimpairment demand, the pleaded continuing monitoring-program demand and the demand for supplementation of these already approved plans fail. Requests solely to compel completion of the San Rafael route-designation plan and creation of the Henry Mountains use-supervision files are moot because those initial acts have been completed. Completion does not adjudicate a different continuing statutory or regulatory duty; none is invented from the source's tentative discussion of possible pleading scope.

**N1R — Discrete and legally required action.** Sections 551(13) and 706(1) permit compulsion of a discrete agency act the agency is legally required to take and has unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed. The listed forms of agency action and their failures concern circumscribed acts; a general demand for a better administrative program is not such an act. Where a decision is legally required but its permissible substance is discretionary, the court may compel the decision while leaving that substance to the agency. A specific statutory deadline is not an indispensable condition of every unreasonable-delay claim. Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation supplies the prohibition on wholesale program correction; Lincoln and Bennett preserve the distinction between legal duties, administrative discretion and the appropriate review channel. The complete section 706(1) formulation is adopted here rather than falsely attributed to those earlier cases.

**N2R — Nonimpairment is binding but does not specify the demanded act.** Section 1782(c) requires management without impairing wilderness suitability during review and until Congress determines otherwise. Its exception permits continued existing mining, grazing and mineral leasing in the manner and degree conducted on October 21, 1976. Its proviso separately requires action against unnecessary or undue degradation or to afford environmental protection. Unless already withdrawn, the lands remain open to appropriation under mining laws during review, subject to a withdrawal under section 1714 for reasons other than preservation of wilderness character. After congressional wilderness designation, the Wilderness Act's national-forest wilderness rules apply, including their mineral-survey, development, access, exchange and claimant ingress-and-egress provisions. These qualifications prevent turning the provision into an unqualified prohibition of every land use or alteration.

The nonimpairment obligation is a real management standard, but it does not specify the particular omitted agency act sought in this program-wide demand. Leaving nominal choice of means to BLM would still require a court continually to decide whether its management was good enough. The generalized section 706(1) claim therefore fails. Final agency action inconsistent with a binding legal boundary remains subject to its proper review channel. Section 1732(a)'s plan-conformity duty, including the governing law for tracts dedicated to particular uses, and section 1732(b)'s unnecessary-or-undue-degradation duty remain distinct provisions; one provision's exception is not transferred to another.

**N3R — The actual plan provisions supply no binding intensive-program command.** Plan-conformity law prevents BLM from taking actions inconsistent with an operative plan until the plan is amended; review of such an action under section 706(2), subject to the applicable review requirements, is distinct from compelling every projected operation under section 706(1). A plan's statement that an activity will be undertaken does not by itself establish a binding affirmative commitment. A plan may reiterate an independently binding duty or contain language clearly making a particular act obligatory. The plan's source, language, function and context must establish that commitment.

The Henry Mountains supervision and monitoring projections do not establish the binding intensive continuing program claimed here. The Factory Butte provision calls for monitoring and closure if warranted; the related provisions concern documenting damage, recommending corrective action, identifying damage that may warrant designation changes, and prioritizing supervision in limited and closed areas. These are planning directions, not a clear commitment to the demanded legally compulsory operational program. Neither future-tense wording nor the general plan-conformity command supplies that missing legal force. The plan remains guidance and a constraint on contrary action without converting every projection of future activity into an enforceable schedule of agency operations.

The reported informal monitoring and acknowledgment that a program commenced do not establish satisfactory management and do not make the continuing-program claim moot. They distinguish it from the completed requests for initial acts. The continuing claim fails because the relied-upon planning projections are not the binding affirmative command alleged. It is unnecessary to decide whether every element of such a monitoring program, if actually made mandatory by another legal source, would be sufficiently discrete. This corrects any suggestion in the fresh commitment that an intensive or continuing program is necessarily nondiscrete or that ongoing activity automatically satisfies an enforceable duty.

Closure remains conditional; neither a distinct preserved closure claim nor an established triggering determination is supplied. The Court does not order closure, find environmental damage absent, or decide enforcement of a regulation-based monitoring duty under 43 CFR 8342.3. Any asserted continuing-file demand founded only on these same nonbinding program projections would meet the same legal answer; this conditional legal consequence neither finds that such a separate claim was pleaded nor decides an independently sourced duty. No historical inference concerning the pleading or a particular budget clause becomes a new factual finding.

**N4R — Completed approval and supplementation.** Mandatory EIS supplementation under 40 CFR 1502.9(c)(1) applies when relevant major federal action remains and either the agency makes substantial changes to the proposed action relevant to environmental concerns, or significant new circumstances or information relevant to those concerns bear on the proposed action or its impacts. These are alternative triggers. Section 1502.9(c)(2) separately permits supplementation when the agency determines it would further NEPA's purposes; that permission creates no mandatory duty by itself.

Approval of the plans at issue is complete. Continued management under those approved plans does not perpetually keep plan approval proposed, and the asserted increase in ORV use identifies no distinct remaining major federal action to which the demanded supplement attaches. This supplementation theory therefore fails. Marsh concerns significant new information while relevant major action remains; completed preparation of an EIS for an unfinished project is not completion of the project. Future amendments or site-specific authorizations retain their own NEPA obligations. The Court does not find the increased-use information insignificant or hold every later implementation decision exempt.

The June 7 Public Citizen ruling reinforces the need to identify the actual agency act and available choices. It does not establish that BLM lacks environmental authority merely because FMCSA could not veto the President's border-entry decision. No alternatives forfeiture from that different case is imported. The present missing predicate is a remaining relevant major action, not an independent actor's controlling choice or a lack of BLM authority over land management.

## 98. Pennsylvania State Police v. Suders

### Common rule and procedural commitments

**SC — Eight direct Associate joins.** An employee claiming hostile-environment constructive discharge must establish actionable discrimination because of sex and working conditions so objectively intolerable that a reasonable employee would feel compelled to resign, together with causation between those actionable conditions and the resignation. Harris and Oncale retain the distinct objectively and subjectively severe-or-pervasive environment and because-of-sex requirements; an actionable environment alone does not establish the additional compulsion threshold. Duncan, decided May 27, 2003, already governs objective compulsion: realistic corrective opportunities matter without becoming an exhaustion condition. The employee need not endure danger, futile procedures or indefinite delay, and announcement of an investigation alone is no safe harbor.

Attribution remains separate. Under the actual-authority route, the plaintiff must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of the actionable environment. Title, rank and workplace proximity alone are insufficient. A completed personnel action, economic injury, higher-management notice or an express threat of discharge is not invariably required. Apparent authority requires employer manifestations and reasonable reliance, while negligence retains its own requirements; neither replaces actual-authority proof by assertion.

The plaintiff-favorable account includes sustained alleged supervisory harassment, inadequate assistance in obtaining a complaint route, the examination-paper episode, the supervisory arrest and resignation during it. Those matters support genuine inquiries concerning environment, compulsion, authority aid, practical corrective opportunity and causation. They are not conclusive findings of liability, unlawful arrest, effective protection or unreasonable nonuse. All eight reject restoration of employer summary judgment on the supplied categorical grounds. Neither resignation nor police rank automatically proves an official employment act.

Duncan did not decide a universal independent intent-or-foreseeability formulation, and its noncontrolling complete-rule proposal is not adopted here. Stevens and Ginsburg had joined only its reasonable-jury application, not that separate complete proposal. The present defense question can be answered without resolving every possible independent mental-state formulation. Any genuinely preserved, distinct causation or mental-state issue retains its ordinary procedural footing; no unpresented element is invented or found unsatisfied. This limit does not make the present defense question unresolved.

### SA — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: compensation follows proved avoidability

These four affirm the judgment reversing employer summary judgment and remand under SC and SA. They reject the Third Circuit's reasoning that constructive discharge automatically establishes an official act or employer responsibility, but retain the judgment because the claimed complete defense is not the governing consequence of prevention and nonuse under their rule.

After the employee proves an actionable, employer-attributable wrong and otherwise compensable injury, the employer must prove by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use them; and that the particular claimed loss consequently could have been avoided. Recovery is reduced or barred only to that proved extent. This is a causal limitation on loss recovery, not a complete defense extinguishing the discrimination.

A paper policy, nonreporting alone, an unsafe or futile channel, correction after injury or the mere possibility of a complaint does not satisfy the rule. The employee need not complain to the harasser. Injury incurred before a reasonable protective opportunity and injury the procedure could not prevent remain recoverable if otherwise established. Proof that all compensable loss was avoidable can eliminate that loss recovery but does not by itself eliminate the violation, decide other relief or determine fees. The plaintiff retains the actual-aid burden; the employer bears the avoidability burden.

SA creates neither special immunity when no official employment act occurs nor a categorical exception from ordinary avoidability whenever an official act occurs. An official coercive act may make effective protection or causal avoidability impossible to prove on the facts. Duncan's earlier inquiry whether realistic options defeated compulsion remains distinct from this later compensation inquiry. Failure to prove constructive discharge does not itself extinguish an otherwise established harassment claim; discharge damages cannot be relabeled to evade a failed discharge predicate.

The complete-defense argument stresses employer diligence and incentives to seek prompt correction. SA gives those considerations real work through compulsion and proved avoidability, while refusing to extinguish a plaintiff-proved abuse of actual delegated power or unavoidable injury. Its material-aid attribution requirement is narrower than automatic supervisory responsibility. Once that requirement is proved, general prevention policy and unreasonable nonuse do not change the historical fact that employer-delegated power aided the discrimination.

### SB — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: a newly adopted complete defense

These four vacate the appellate judgment and remand under SC and SB. The Third Circuit's categorical rule that every constructive discharge excludes a complete prevention/nonuse defense must be removed. The employer is not entitled to reinstatement of summary judgment, because the defense's factual predicates and the official-act issue have not been conclusively established in its favor.

For a supervisor-created hostile environment, including one culminating in an objectively compelled resignation, the employer may establish a complete defense by proving by a preponderance both that it exercised reasonable care to prevent and promptly correct the harassment and that the employee unreasonably failed to use the preventive or corrective opportunities provided or otherwise to avoid harm. The opportunities must be practically accessible and reasonably effective. Unsafe or futile channels and paper policies are insufficient. No particular written-policy form is invariably required, and nonreporting alone establishes neither element. The plaintiff separately retains the actual-authority/material-aid burden.

The defense is unavailable when an official discriminatory employment act precipitates the compelled resignation. Such an act uses delegated employer authority to effect a significant employment decision, such as a demotion, an extreme cut in pay or a transfer imposing unbearable working conditions. Authority to make the decision does not mean the employer authorized its discriminatory motive. The plaintiff must establish the asserted official employment act and its causal connection; the employer retains persuasion on the two affirmative-defense elements. A resignation does not become that official act merely because it qualifies as constructive discharge. The arrest allegations bear on coercion, actual authority and the safety of complaint routes, but this record neither conclusively classifies the arrest as the requisite employment act nor conclusively excludes that possibility.

SB is a new resolution of a question expressly reserved in the current Faragher, Harrison and Ellerth decisions. It is not an automatic importation of their historical namesakes, a statement that a complete defense already existed, or a replacement of the governing actual-authority rule with supervisory-status liability. Its agency distinction is between authority materially aiding an environment and delegated authority consummating an official discriminatory employment decision. The former threshold establishes a relationship sufficient for presumptive attribution; the latter makes the employer's official decision the immediate source of the compelled resignation and defeats the asserted diligence defense. Genuine preventive care and unreasonable failure to use practical protection perform a separate function at the former stage.

SA's strongest objection is that this approach can eliminate liability and unavoidable loss even after actual delegated aid is proved. SB answers that Title VII agency responsibility permits a bounded affirmative defense promoting prevention and reasonable use of real corrective opportunities, while withholding it where an official discriminatory act precipitates resignation. Its supporters accept that the rule does more than reduce particular proved avoidable losses. They do not claim SA is a logical subset, that its causal restriction has been satisfied whenever SB is satisfied, or that ordinary negligence alone governs all supervisory harassment. A negligence-only rule, compulsory exhaustion and automatic immunity from a policy's existence are rejected.

## Exact propositions tested

### N. Minimum notice and opportunity

The affirmative duty applies when a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to elect between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing to exhaust other claims, and the materials already before the court disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat a timely return. The notice must state that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the first filing date and that a later petition may be barred. The prisoner must have a meaningful opportunity to delete the unexhausted claims and proceed on the exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements.

No missing-record investigation, exact deadline computation, claim-strength evaluation, strategic recommendation or promised stay is required by this minimum rule. Mere complexity or an unsuccessful filing is insufficient. The rule applies to Ford's pending review and later qualifying elections; it neither reopens unrelated final cases nor creates a new full year.

For the four joining Associates, declining to require a strategic recommendation or a promised stay does not license omission of an actually available requested protective procedure from a court's presentation of the options. They may join this minimum rule and retain that fuller position in a separate explanation. They do not require the lead proposition itself to validate the entire stay procedure, but cannot join a declaration that the minimum warning exhausts all duties in Ford's circumstances.

### E. Defined equitable availability

Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling to remedy a proved court-created impediment of the specified kind: omission of the required notice or another materially misleading direction that caused the loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevented timely return. A notice violation does not by itself prove reliance, causation or diligence. The inquiry requires a lawful remedial basis capable of curing the actual timing defect; causal loss alone does not establish that tolling, as opposed to some other lawful correction, is the available remedy.

**Necessary scope:** The relevant obstacle is a court-created loss of the timely opportunity or court-caused prevention of return. Routine processing delay, pro se status or an unfavorable calculation does not automatically become such an obstacle. This resolves equitable availability for the defined problem, not a general code for every equitable-tolling claim.

### P. Proof, diligence and the causal interval

The petitioner bears the burden of establishing the factual predicates by a preponderance of the evidence. Reasonable diligence concerns the relevant period and conduct after the obstacle ended. It is assessed in the actual circumstances, including the court's communication and the petitioner's supported understanding; it is not perfect diligence or a requirement to anticipate and independently correct the very misleading direction at issue.

The remand court must identify the actual interval needing protection and a legally sufficient causal account for it, exclude independently avoidable delay, and explain how the chosen lawful remedy cures the full timing defect. It must confront time already expired before the election; it cannot silently toll only later days and call the petition timely, or automatically subtract all federal pendency.

**Necessary scope:** The pre-election inquiry does not require a later omission to have physically caused earlier elapsed days. It distinguishes the loss of a still-pending, timely original proceeding from prevention of a timely later filing. A lawful reopening theory must satisfy its own prerequisites; a tolling theory must justify the interval it protects. The court must determine whether the supported causal chain and permissible remedy reach the earlier expiration. The audit approves neither automatic retroactive tolling nor a categorical rule that pre-election expiration defeats every possible remedy. Independent delay remains excluded to the extent it is actually avoidable and causally material.

### R. Scoped relation back and surviving branches

A separate later action does not automatically become an amendment to a closed earlier action merely because the specified warnings were omitted. If relief depends on reopening, an independent lawful reopening predicate is necessary. Whether relation back follows an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remains open because that procedural predicate has not been established. A genuinely retained and stayed original petition is a different procedural situation. Agreement on R does not decide general stay authority.

Every Associate preserves the separate dismissal of two new Loguercio assistance claims and the factual-development direction concerning access to legal papers for five new Weed claims, together with the existing denial of automatic new-claim relation back. Preservation of the repeated-claim equitable argument remains for proper determination; the lower dissent's objection is not a found waiver.

## Proof-standard basis and competing route

Grogan, 498 U.S. at 286–291, expressly treats preponderance as the ordinary civil allocation and asks whether a particular interest or statutory structure warrants departure. Stevens authored the unanimous opinion; Souter and O'Connor were among its joining members. Herman & MacLean, 459 U.S. at 387–390, explains the ordinary allocation, the role of judicial selection when no governing standard is prescribed, and the reasons for heightened proof in proceedings threatening certain individual interests. Its note 27 also identifies the distinct historical concern over equity setting aside written instruments for fraud. Steadman, 450 U.S. at 95–104, concerns an APA-prescribed standard and cannot itself transplant the APA into habeas.

The strongest contrary route would decline a numerical standard because habeas implicates finality and liberty, or demand heightened proof before equitable interference with an otherwise operative deadline. The proposed inquiry concerns what happened in federal procedure, its causal effect and reasonable diligence. Success restores an opportunity for ordinary adjudication; it establishes no constitutional merits, entitlement to release, or automatic reopening. The prisoner's liberty interest supplies no reason to heighten the prisoner's burden to prove these procedural facts. The State's finality interest warrants the substantive causal and remedial limits already stated, rather than an unsupported higher evidentiary quantum. This is not an equity suit to invalidate a written instrument for fraud. Existing proof requirements attached to a different statutory or remedial predicate remain intact.

For those reasons, the ordinary burden is a supported extension for the four Associates who accept the defined equitable claim. It does real work when the actual communication, reliance, diligence or causal period is disputed. It is not inferred merely from statutory silence or from a general preference for access. The civil authorities do not establish notice liability, create equitable jurisdiction, settle preservation, or justify changing any formal judgment.
