## Judgment and full common ground

All eight Associates vacate the Sixth Circuit judgment and remand to review the district court's independent retaliation-evidence ground and remaining preserved defenses under ordinary civil rules. All eight join the rule, its application and this remedy in full.

Heck's favorable-invalidation prerequisite applies only when success on the actual civil claim necessarily establishes the invalidity of an operative conviction, sentence or custody determination. It does not cover Muhammad's claim that a retaliatory inflated charge caused six days of prehearing detention when that claim accepts the later insolence adjudication and success would affect neither the underlying criminal judgment nor the duration of his sentence. The disciplinary setting itself supplies no missing necessary-invalidity connection.

Heck preserves genuinely independent injuries, Balisok makes the actual consequence of the claim decisive rather than its procedural label, and Randell requires identification of the operative custody determination before comparing the civil theory. Muhammad's success is compatible with the accepted insolence determination; an earlier improper threatened-behavior charge and its temporary detention consequence can be remedied without setting that adjudication aside. Close's finality argument therefore does not sustain the circuit's categorical rule. His separate contention that retaliation lacks adequate proof remains for appellate review of the district court's actual basis.
