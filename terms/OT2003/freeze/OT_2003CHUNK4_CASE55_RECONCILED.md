**Rule, strongest objection and exact scope:** An uncounseled guilty plea resulting in imprisonment requires a knowing, intelligent and voluntary relinquishment of counsel, including understanding the right to counsel at the plea, the charge and the plea's consequences. It does not invariably require warnings that a lawyer may discover a viable defense or give an independent opinion about pleading guilty. Counsel's useful evaluative role supports real understanding, but does not show that every accused lacks it without those precise warnings. Faretta addresses trial representation's different tasks; Patterson and Johnson v. Zerbst support the stage-sensitive inquiry.
