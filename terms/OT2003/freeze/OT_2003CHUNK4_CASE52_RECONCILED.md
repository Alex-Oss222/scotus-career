## Comparison of the two provisional rationale departures

The historical concurrence by Scalia specifically accepted the official Department of Labor view as reasonable and binding under his approach to authoritative agency interpretation. It objected to replacing that agency construction with an independently definitive judicial interpretation. His Darden, Shumate and Lamie positions do not establish a new view that this particular ERISA scheme has an unambiguous answer. The current Clackamas rule concerns a different statute and preserves attention to the whole employment relationship; it supplies no changed agency-authority premise. The neutral packet already identifies the Government's supporting interpretation and the Department's role. Failure to recover the advisory opinion directly is a research limitation, not a change in the agency's historical interpretation or a Justice-specific reason to abandon Scalia's ground. His separate agency rationale therefore survives.

Thomas historically considered ERISA's structural indications consistent with working-owner coverage but insufficient to establish it categorically. An exemption that can apply to some owner-employees who independently meet the common-law definition does not prove that every member of the proposed working-owner class is an employee. The current Darden rule requires the common-law inquiry when the specific statute remains unhelpful; current Clackamas reinforces the need to examine the relationship rather than use ownership alone. Shumate concerns a qualifying trust and does not decide the antecedent employment classification. Lamie enforces enacted eligibility but does not settle whether these ERISA indications displace Darden. The additional verified section 1108(c) dual-role provisions and (d) transaction categories do not eliminate Thomas's objection: they presuppose an entitlement or defined category without conclusively classifying every working owner. No concrete changed premise supports his provisional categorical full join. His narrower remand position is restored.

## Supported propositions and limits

The six statutory Associates hold that a working owner may occupy employer and participant capacities in an ERISA pension plan that covers one or more genuine employees other than the owner and spouse. The owner-only exclusion defines arrangements outside Title I plan coverage; it does not exclude a working owner from participant status in an otherwise covered employee plan. Scalia directly supports the same operative coverage result through the agency interpretation. Thomas supports the narrower proposition that ownership and control alone do not establish the categorical exclusion applied below; he does not supply an eighth vote for conclusive participant classification under the broader rule.

Darden remains the common-law default where statutory guidance is insufficient. For the six, the integrated ERISA scheme supplies the needed specific guidance; for Thomas it does not fully answer this classification. Clackamas's ADA relationship inquiry remains effective in its own statutory field. Shumate gives effect to a qualifying beneficial trust interest with an enforceable transfer restriction under applicable nonbankruptcy law; it does not decide every transfer into the plan or make every owner repayment immune from avoidance.

The regulation retains its stated purpose, its plans-without-employees rule, and its distinct owner/spouse and partner/spouse definitions. This case establishes other covered employees; it does not adjudicate disputed coverage under regulatory paragraph (d). Section 1108(c)(1)'s benefits permission requires computation and payment consistent with the plan as applied to other participants and beneficiaries. Its compensation and dual-service clauses do different work. Section 1108(d)'s transaction restrictions and defined categories remain separate and require the transaction's applicable statutory version. The later amendment cannot silently govern the earlier loan or repayment.

The plan's anti-inurement provision does not itself create a categorical ban on working-owner participation. Whether the particular loan and delayed repayments comport with independent plan, statutory and bankruptcy requirements remains for the courts below. The grant does not authorize an automatic recovery for either party or erase the existing procedural status of the trustee's avoidance claim. No general owner-only plan protection, beneficiary theory, fraudulent-transfer immunity or universal equation of owner and employee is adopted.

## Formal disposition and actual join barriers

All eight accept either **reversal and remand** or **vacatur and remand** if the judgment conclusively rejects the categorical ownership exclusion and directs the same legally supported further proceedings. No Associate makes the formal verb an independent condition of assent. A merely tentative remand that leaves the categorical exclusion available is outside that agreement.

The six statutory Associates and Scalia also accept vacatur with the same definitive dual-capacity holding, leaving the actual qualification, anti-alienation, anti-inurement and transfer work for remand. Thomas's barrier is substantive: he additionally requires the unresolved common-law classification inquiry and does not treat Yates's status as conclusively established by the statutory category. His agreement to either formal verb must not be reported as agreement to eliminate that inquiry. This qualification corrects the provisional model; it is not inferred from the historical word “reversed.”
