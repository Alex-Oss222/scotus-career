## Rules and adversarial alternatives

**P1 — constitutional timing and force.** After meaningful announcement, the reasonableness of the delay and entry depends on the circumstances known when force begins. Wilson protects the opportunity for peaceful entry; Richards requires particular facts and rational inferences for threatened destruction and rejects a drug-case categorical excuse. Ramirez rejects a higher announcement threshold because the door must be damaged, while independently restricting the means and amount of force. The time needed to preserve the evidence, as well as the opportunity to hear and respond, matters. A clock interval by itself establishes neither compliance nor violation.

**P2 — this entry.** This entry was reasonable. Loud daytime notice at this small apartment afforded a practical opportunity to acknowledge the officers. Silence through the supported interval, combined with the concrete warrant-supported presence of quickly disposable cocaine, permitted the officers to conclude that waiting longer would materially increase the risk that the warranted search would be frustrated. They did not dispense with notice merely because the warrant involved drugs. The unsupported alternative that Banks might not have heard them is a possible innocent explanation, not knowledge that nullifies the objectively reasonable inference. Neither the shower nor evidence found afterward supplies that inference. The door breach used to obtain the authorized entry is not shown to have exceeded that purpose.

**P3 — independent statutory route.** Section 3109's ordinary notice-and-refused-admission route is satisfied. Refusal can be reasonably inferred from failure to respond after a meaningful opportunity under the actual circumstances; an express verbal refusal is not indispensable. Here the loud announced demand, small dwelling, daytime execution and elapsed interval permit that inference. The disposable evidence is contextual support for assessing the delay, not a substitute for notice or a declaration that every silence is refusal. The statute is satisfied through notice and implied refusal; no liberation predicate is asserted, and an independent statutory destruction exception is unnecessary to the disposition.

**P4 — remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's suppression ruling on entry and remand for proceedings consistent with the conclusion that this entry violated neither the Fourth Amendment nor section 3109. The entry ground does not defeat the conditional conviction. The mandate preserves the independent interrogation rulings outside the grant and permits only any otherwise live proceedings; it does not itself determine an unpresented sentencing question.

**Competing path: insufficient notice interval.** The strongest contrary position is that silence for fifteen to twenty seconds is common when an occupant must stop a lawful activity, dress or move to the door; treating silence plus cocaine as enough could dilute Richards into a short automatic drug timer. The absence of sounds of destruction strengthens that objection. The supported response is that the inquiry asks for reasonable, circumstance-specific inferences rather than certainty of actual refusal or destruction. Loud afternoon notice, the small premises and the entire elapsed interval materially differentiate this case from an unannounced drug entry, a barely completed announcement or execution when occupants are likely asleep. The conclusion cannot be transferred by the interval alone to a materially different dwelling, time or response. No fixed second knock or required sound of destruction follows from Wilson or Richards. This inference is sufficient without using the lower court's impermissible force tier.

**Competing statutory route: common-law destruction qualification.** Ramirez's common-law method could support extending the statute to a specifically justified destruction risk, and Wilson, Miller and Sabbath supply the relevant historical background. But Ramirez adopted a danger exception, not every constitutional exception. The ordinary refusal route supported here resolves the presented statutory question without an unsupported automatic equation of the two bodies of law. If an eventual formulation rejects implied refusal and depends instead on a new statutory destruction qualification, it requires an explicit legal account and a fresh compatibility check; these commitments are not an unqualified join to that substitute rationale.
