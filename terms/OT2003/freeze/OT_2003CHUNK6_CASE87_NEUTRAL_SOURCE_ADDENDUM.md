Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/02-1684.ZO.html, factual Part I. Alvarado was seventeen years and seven months old at the time of the offense. About a month later, after the detective contacted his home and mother, his parents brought him to the station around lunchtime. His contention that his parents asked to attend and were refused is a disputed account, not a finding established here. The parents waited in the lobby. One detective interviewed him in a small room for about two hours, recorded with his knowledge, without Miranda warnings. She challenged his initial denials, invoked contrary witnesses, urged honesty and appealed to the victim's family's interests. He progressively admitted helping steal the truck and hide the gun. Near the end she offered breaks twice; he declined. He then went home with his father. At trial he described the conversation as friendly and said he did not feel coerced or threatened. The suppression motion was denied as noncustodial. The conviction ultimately was second-degree murder with fifteen years to life. The state appellate opinion is People v. Soto, 74 Cal.
