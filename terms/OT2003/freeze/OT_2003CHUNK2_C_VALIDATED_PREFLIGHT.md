## 2. Corrections to framing and scope

1. **Do not merge contribution, independent expression, disclosure, and source restrictions.** Colorado II sustains its presented coordinated-party ceiling under an objective circumvention showing and close fit; it does not classify every joint message, party transaction, or soft-money use. Beaumont concerns NCRL's direct candidate contributions on a cumulative source-and-channel record, expressly leaving its independent-expenditure judgment outside review. Austin's surviving independent-expenditure setting and Day/MCFL's actual nonprofit characteristics require their own treatment.
2. **Do not turn an issue into an entering holding.** The packet's statement against demanding surrender of independent speech describes a substantial §213 path; the current cases do not supply an unqualified rule deciding every conditional benefit. Likewise, express words are not an established universal constitutional boundary, but Maine Right to Life is a bounded statutory construction, not a holding that every objectively defined election-advertising category is constitutional. Bartlett preserves genuine issue discussion and definite coverage.
3. **FECA §323(c) is context, not a new independent merits challenge.** Kollar-Kotelly's Title I discussion expressly reports that no plaintiff specifically challenged the fundraising-cost subsection. Its text must be retained when describing the scheme, without creating a separate constitutional judgment absent a demonstrated preserved issue.
4. **Section 211 is presented within the coordination question.** McConnell's brief, Questions Presented 4, identifies §§202, 211, and 214. Do not omit the independent-expenditure definition merely because the district overview groups most discussion under §214.
5. **Section 201 has at least three separate jobs:** defining electioneering communications; ordinary disbursement/donor reporting; and §304(f)(5)'s treatment of executed contracts as disbursements. Its contract-reporting challenge is not automatically identical to §212's independent-expenditure reporting challenge. The district treated their ripeness differently.
6. **Section 204 is not a freestanding abolition of all nonprofit constitutional protection.** It withdraws §203's statutory exception for the specified targeted communications. Whether constitutional protection independently remains under MCFL and Day is a separate question. A literal statutory checklist is not the complete constitutional test in this timeline.
7. **Preserve the Paul appellants' actual threshold and merits theories.** Their Question 6 and brief pp.45–49 challenge the BCRA-amended individual candidate-contribution limits as restraints on the candidates' press/editorial activity. They separately challenge unchanged PAC ceilings and lack of indexing. They identify declarations and reported maximum contributions supporting actual funding burdens and dispute the district's characterization of their showing as mere allegations. That is distinct from Adams's theory that increased limits marginalize less wealthy voters/candidates. A single statement that all §307 plaintiffs seek equal influence would misstate the live controversy.
8. **Do not conflate the scope of a statutory amendment with direct-review jurisdiction.** The government argues, brief p.126 n.53, that the Paul nonparty-PAC claim attacks unchanged FECA limits and belongs in the §437h route, not BCRA §403 review. That argument does not automatically dispose of the separate challenge to the amended individual candidate ceiling. Resolve each actual claim and the relief it seeks; do not assume that every old FECA provision became newly reviewable.
9. **Section 311's challenged extension and its other details differ.** The district describes the litigated objection as extension of sponsor identification to electioneering communications and other non-express advocacy. It states that the added identification details themselves were not independently challenged there. Preserve the enacted details as context and the appealed extension as the component; do not manufacture an independent challenge to every font, box, image, or approval-statement requirement.
10. **Press Clause and media-exemption claims are expressly presented.** Paul's questions and the per curiam opinion supply them. This is a statement of an existing live theory, not an added grant. The broader immunity claim, the differential-treatment argument, and the specific candidate-contribution standing claim must remain distinct.

## 5. Threshold components require separate determinations
