## 85. Thornton v. United States

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Thornton v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-5165; 541 U.S. 615.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-24
* **Question Presented:** Whether Belton authorizes a vehicle search incident to arrest when police first contact the recent occupant after he has left the vehicle, including when he is secured away from it before the search.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit, affirming denial of suppression and Thornton’s convictions under the recent-occupant rule.
* **Relief Requested:** Thornton seeks suppression of the handgun and relief from the affected convictions; the United States defends the search and alternative admissibility grounds.
* **Material Record:** Thornton parked and exited before the officer approached. After discovering drugs on his person, the officer arrested and handcuffed him and placed him in the patrol car, then searched Thornton’s vehicle and found a handgun. The district court alternatively invoked possible inventory discovery; the appellate judgment relied on Belton.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The lawful arrest does not itself establish every exception for a vehicle. Preserve the distinct inventory or inevitable-discovery ground rather than assume its elements. The timing of first contact and actual access are different proposed lines.
* **Relevant Dates:** The search preceded this May 2004 direct review. No later historical vehicle-search rule is entering governing law.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Florida v. Thomas involved the recent-occupant question but ended on statutory finality, leaving the search merits open.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Limits of vehicle search incident to arrest

* **Controlling Law:** Knowles v. Iowa rejects a full search incident to a citation because the exception’s custodial safety and evidence rationales are absent. Florida v. Thomas reserved recent-occupant merits. Surviving New York v. Belton authorizes a contemporaneous passenger-compartment search following custodial arrest of a vehicle occupant.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify an actual lawful custodial arrest and the scope of the invoked exception. Probable cause, standardized inventory and inevitable discovery require their own predicates.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; the exclusionary remedy in a federal criminal prosecution.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chimel v. California limits incident searches to the arrestee and immediate-control area. Nix v. Williams requires the Government to establish inevitable lawful discovery; Florida v. Wells requires standardized inventory criteria.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Belton supplies the existing categorical vehicle rule; its reach to recent occupants and secured arrestees presents the disputed extension and possible limitation.
* **Open Point:** Whether first police contact is decisive or whether the exception should instead be tied to actual access at the search.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Fourth Circuit treated Thornton as a recent occupant and rejected a distinction based on whether the officer approached before or after he exited.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Thornton seeks to confine Belton to contact with an occupant and stresses the absence of access once secured.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The Government argues that a uniform recent-occupant rule avoids arbitrary distinctions and protects officers; alternative discovery may independently sustain admission.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** None independently established for disposition.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States defends the search and judgment as respondent.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Extending the existing categorical rule affirms on search-incident grounds. A first-contact limit or a purpose-based access limit defeats that rationale but requires consideration of the independent inventory/discovery ground before ordering suppression. The latter limit would openly change Belton’s categorical operation and require direct-review transition treatment.
