It independently rechecks all eight Associates using the neutral law addendum and the complete Thompson and Jackson primary texts supplied at the two authorized neutral-source paths, together with the original authorized case 73 inputs and earlier public positions identified in the original handoff. ## Clean validation and effect of the additional law

The supplement supplies earlier authority and complete canonical sentencing propositions, not a new event or factual premise. Thompson v. Louisville, 362 U.S. 199, 199, 204–206, invalidates criminal punishment resting on an evidentiary record entirely devoid of support for the charged offense. Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307, 313–324, distinguishes that anti-arbitrariness protection from its broader reasonable-doubt sufficiency rule. The Thompson rule is not merely another name for Jackson review: a record may contain some relevant evidence yet fail Jackson's rational-factfinder inquiry. Neither opinion directly adjudicated this habitual-offender chronology.

Stevens's Jackson concurrence, 443 U.S. at 326–339, including its notes, opposes the expanded federal sufficiency inquiry while expressly preserving the no-evidence cases and habeas protection against extreme failures of the state process. It supplies a concrete earlier ground for considering this case without inventing a general constitutional rule about all sentencing findings. His later published Sawyer, Schlup and Monge positions remain pertinent to the separate gateway and the distinction between statutory eligibility and discretionary punishment.

The added authority materially changes the scope of the available analysis: the original handoff recognized an unresolved Winship/Jackson classification problem but omitted the independently arguable Thompson route. The supplement also sharpens the claim-specific section 2254(d) trigger. It does not establish a new fact, a state merits adjudication of the defaulted claim, a precise finality date, or a retroactive application of Gatewood. The neutral legal addition is appropriate for this independent recheck because it precedes commitment on the corrected set of legal grounds and contains no withheld case-specific material.

## Separate constitutional and collateral inquiries

**Earlier no-evidence protection.** On the supplied record, the first prior conviction became final three days after the second prior offense. Texas accepts that the statutory sequence necessary for habitual-offender eligibility was absent. The direct-relief position applies the earlier prohibition on wholly arbitrary criminal deprivation to custody that depends on this affirmatively disproved, indispensable factual predicate. Its federal ground is the absence of any evidentiary basis for the status authorizing the additional punishment, not the proposition that every departure from state sentencing law is federal error. It requires no reassessment of witness credibility or balancing of disputed proof.

**Jackson and the later recidivist rule.** Jackson's full reasonable-doubt sufficiency inquiry requires identifying the facts to which that constitutional standard applies. The canonical Almendarez-Torres decision is a statutory aggravated-reentry-element holding; it supplies no general constitutional recidivism rule. Gatewood, effective June 30, 2003, supplies a constitutional rule for necessary maximum- or minimum-authorizing recidivist facts, including chronology, and applicable federal charging protection. Its direct-review transition under Griffith and independent collateral requirements remain explicit. It is present law, but cannot silently become clearly established law at an earlier state merits adjudication or automatically govern a final conviction on collateral review. Monge's surviving bounded double-jeopardy holding does not answer independent due-process, jury or charging claims.

**Claim-specific statutory review.** The August 2000 federal application invokes amended section 2254. The assistance claim was rejected on its merits in state court and therefore requires the applicable section 2254(d) inquiry, using the actual relevant state merits date, followed by actual Strickland performance and prejudice before relief. The sufficiency claim was rejected on a procedural ground. The supplied record does not establish a state merits adjudication of that claim, and no section 2254(d) merits-deference hurdle is added to it by inference from the disposition of assistance. An innocence gateway can excuse its default but neither supplies its federal merits nor erases any independently applicable collateral condition.

The precise state merits date and criminal-finality date remain unverified. They cannot be selected to make Gatewood govern. Thompson and Jackson predate the identified 1997 prosecution, so their earlier existence requires no invented date. The dispute about applying Thompson's protection to this indispensable sentencing predicate must still be decided on its legal substance. The direct-relief Justices regard that confined application as enforcement of the earlier anti-arbitrariness rule, not retroactive use of Gatewood's later jury-and-proof rule. The assistance-first Justices do not resolve that extension or a hypothetical Teague defense now. No party waiver, new-rule exception or previously unrecorded collateral bar is invented.

## Enhanced comparison of supported paths

**A. Vacate and adjudicate assistance first.** The unresolved federal assistance claim can supply the requested sentence relief on an ordinary constitutional ground. Edwards preserves independent conditions for assistance used as cause; Williams and Bell govern statutory eligibility and actual assistance adjudication; Trest and Lambrix permit reasoned sequencing of nonjurisdictional limits. The strongest objection is now sharper than in the original handoff: the admitted chronology supports an earlier, independent no-evidence claim, making another merits remand potentially unnecessary. The five retaining this path answer that Thompson involved conviction facts, the new noncapital gateway remains a separate question, and the existing assistance route should be decided promptly before those additional doctrinal questions. They do not declare Thompson categorically irrelevant to sentencing or call the sufficiency claim subject to section 2254(d) without its own trigger.

**B. Recognize the confined gateway and affirm sentence relief under the earlier no-evidence rule.** Clear and convincing, uncontested chronology shows that no reasonable factfinder could find the indispensable habitual-offender sequence. The gateway is confined to factual eligibility for added punishment, not discretionary mitigation or ordinary dissatisfaction with sentence severity. Once that separate gateway permits review, the absence of any evidentiary basis for the indispensable statutory status establishes the distinct due-process violation under the Thompson protection. The strongest objection is that this moves a conviction-based precedent into sentencing and may conceal retroactive application of the later Gatewood rule. The three adopting this path answer that they require neither a new jury entitlement nor Jackson's full sufficiency review: the State imposed additional imprisonment on a factual premise its record affirmatively disproves. Their earlier element-function and miscarriage positions support applying the already existing prohibition on wholly arbitrary deprivation to that discrete predicate. Gatewood confirms their present concern with such predicates but does no retroactive legal work.

**C. Recognize the gateway but remand the federal merits.** This was the original position of Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. It remains a legally possible narrower disposition if the record leaves a material constitutional or collateral predicate unresolved. Its strongest justification is caution about the classification and temporal questions. Its strongest objection is that, on the supplied admitted chronology, Thompson gives those Justices a sufficient earlier constitutional ground without new factfinding or the uncertain temporal reach of Gatewood. They therefore no longer select an open-ended constitutional-merits remand. A concrete, independently established obstacle could require limited further adjudication, but the absence of an exact date does not itself create such an obstacle to their earlier-rule ground.

## Judgment components, joins and remedy

The five assistance-first commitments remain O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. They support vacating the existing appellate judgment and conditional-writ disposition, followed by prompt federal adjudication of the state-merits assistance claim under its actual dated section 2254(d) inquiry and Strickland. If that route supplies relief, the lower court must implement the sentence remedy. Otherwise it must adjudicate any genuinely remaining, properly available cause, gateway and constitutional routes under their own conditions. They preserve the Thompson argument rather than ordering final rejection of it, and impose no universal sequence for all habeas cases.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg now support affirming the existing conditional sentence relief on two separately stated propositions: the factual-eligibility gateway is available and satisfied here, and the admitted absence of any evidentiary basis for the indispensable enhancement chronology violates the earlier no-evidence protection. Their agreement is at this operative level of generality; it does not require agreement on a general noncapital sentencing-sufficiency rule, Gatewood's collateral reach or a broader gateway for discretionary sentencing. Their conditional-writ judgment is not a finding that the independent assistance claim succeeds.

Under the three direct-relief commitments, implementation must require Texas, within a reasonable concrete period set by the federal court, to impose a lawful sentence without the invalid habitual-offender enhancement or end custody attributable to that invalid punishment. The theft conviction survives. The lower court must identify the actual present custody and judgment status, credit service already completed, and give the writ practical effect against custody unsupported by a lawful sentence. The known two-year unenhanced maximum informs that inquiry; it does not establish a new state judgment, completed compliance, an independent custody basis or a present unconditional release command. Under the five remand commitments, the same remedial principles apply if the independently adjudicated constitutional route establishes entitlement.
