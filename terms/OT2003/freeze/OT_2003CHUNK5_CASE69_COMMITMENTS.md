Section 209(a)'s first sentence addresses any state or subdivision standard relating to control of emissions from new motor vehicles or engines subject to that part; its second sentence separately bars emission approval requirements made a condition of initial retail sale, titling or registration. Section 209(b)'s waiver is not automatic: the eligible State's aggregate protective determination and the Administrator's statutory findings govern, with notice and hearing. Section 246 requires specified covered-area plans, percentages, model-year phasing and clean-fuel conditions, preserves the fleet operator's choice subject to that subsection, and supplies a structured credit program. It therefore cannot be paraphrased as either a ban on all fleet purchasing requirements or an unrestricted authorization for every local emissions program. ## Proposed judgment and competing constructions

**Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Vacate the judgment and remand for rule-specific review under the corrected construction. A legally compelled emissions or emissions-linked fuel criterion for the acquisition of a new vehicle can constitute an emission standard even when the purchaser receives the command. Which side of a transaction must obey is an enforcement mechanism; it does not invariably change the substantive criterion. The first sentence of §209(a) does not confine itself to manufacturers or sellers. Certification permitting a vehicle's sale therefore does not by itself establish that a state may compel private fleets to buy only a narrower emissions-defined class.

The second sentence identifies additional forbidden approval conditions; it does not exhaust the first sentence's meaning. The substantial contrary argument is that the statutory architecture chiefly addresses uniform vehicle design and certification and permits purchaser choice among certified vehicles. The seven accept private choice and recognize that genuinely proprietary choices require their own analysis. But a local command imposed on third-party purchasers is not necessarily the purchaser's own choice, and the first sentence's language cannot be reduced categorically to certification or manufacturer-directed instructions. Section 246's carefully framed program is consistent with fleet purchase mandates being legally consequential standards; its conditions must be examined rather than generalized away.

This holding removes only the lower court's categorical exemption. It does not decide that every fuel choice is an emission standard, that every affected vehicle is new and within the part, or that the six rules all lack an independent statutory basis. Boston Harbor supplies a method for identifying genuine proprietary action in its field, not a universal exception to express preemption. The lower court must separately consider the actual operation and statutory treatment of public procurement, existing vehicles, and any properly supported waiver or authorization. No new injunction or all-rules invalidation follows automatically.

**Souter:** Affirm on the reviewed purchase-versus-production distinction, read in the statutory setting described below. His vote does not validate unrelated operation, emissions-modification or certification requirements, or resolve an independent challenge outside the preserved question.

## Coalition and handoff limits

Seven Associate votes support vacatur/remand and the stated rejection of a categorical purchase exemption. Souter supports affirmance and a narrower understanding of the statutory category. This is not an assigned majority or dissent and does not incorporate Stone's eventual position. No opinion author is selected.

The seven agree on judgment and the specified rule; no broader common rationale is inferred from agreement about labels or federal uniformity. Souter's position is not a logical subset controlling their rule, and no Marks claim is made. All eight preserve unresolved rule-specific issues from automatic adverse determination. Assembly must keep the legal construction, the later application, and the particular remedy separate. If a later proposed holding invalidates an individual Fleet Rule, it requires that rule's full operative terms, relevant qualifications, and the record supporting each statutory predicate; these commitments do not supply those missing findings.
