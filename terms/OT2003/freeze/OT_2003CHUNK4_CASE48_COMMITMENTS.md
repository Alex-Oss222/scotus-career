## Supported dispositions and operative grounds

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the Ninth Circuit and sustain Washington's confined exclusion as applied to Davey's pursuit of a devotional pastoral-ministry degree. They share a Free Exercise ground permitting this limited choice not to support preparation for religious ministry, rather than a conclusion that the Establishment Clause requires exclusion of this individual scholarship. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the Ninth Circuit's constitutional rejection of the exclusion and remand for equal administration of the scholarship under the remaining lawful requirements and determination of any properly presented monetary remedy.

**Five-Associate ground.** A State may decline to support the devotional preparation for ministry presented here while offering an educational scholarship that remains available to religious students at religious institutions, permits theology courses, and supports secular study. This confined course-of-study funding choice does not itself prohibit religious exercise or impose a civil disability on belief or religious affiliation. The permission concerns the particular devotional-degree condition and its application to Davey's pastoral training; it is not a declaration that public assistance reaching a religious school is constitutionally forbidden.

The program's actual design matters. Awards are individual educational assistance: funds pass through the institution for distribution to the student and may pay educational expenses including room and board. That design cannot be silently equated with the restricted provider-redeemed tuition purchase in Zelman or the ordinary integrated tuition in Strout. Witters preserves an individual vocational-benefit relationship; its Establishment Clause permission does not itself resolve whether a State must include devotional ministerial preparation among the courses of study it supports. Walz's distinction between constitutional prohibition and permissible separation supports the confined latitude claimed here. Strout confirms that refusal of support for a defined devotional function is analytically different from a prohibition on sincere exercise, without supplying an automatic answer for this individual benefit.

Columbia Union and K.D.M. forbid excluding independently identifiable secular work or service sites merely because of religious affiliation or location. Washington admits religious colleges and students, permits theology courses, and excludes pursuit of the devotional degree itself. Davey's undisputed pastoral-ministry course of study is within that condition. The business double major does not convert the challenged degree-based scholarship into the particular restricted secular-department grant adjudicated in Columbia Union. The program also permits scholarship support for a secular degree at a different institution while devotional theology is pursued elsewhere. These concrete limits distinguish the condition from an institutional or personal religious disqualification. The source reports the parties' agreement about the devotional meaning of theology; it does not establish a general governmental power to decide religious orthodoxy.

**Three-Associate ground.** When the State provides this independently directed individual educational benefit, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible student because the student's chosen course of study is devotional without the compelling justification and narrow tailoring required for an express religious disability. The student may use the funds for ordinary educational and living expenses, the institution distributes rather than redeems the payment for a specified state purchase, and religious status is generally permitted. These features place the benefit within the preserved Witters relationship rather than the prohibited public purchase in Zelman. A historical preference against subsidizing ministry cannot, without more, establish the necessity of excluding this student from the existing neutral benefit.

Lukumi supplies the rule against selective religious burdens; Columbia Union and K.D.M. require examination of the actual establishment justification and feasible equal treatment. Davey's concurrent business study exposes the categorical exclusion's reach beyond any demonstrated public purchase of devotional teaching. Permitting business study only at another institution does not answer why the same independently eligible benefit must be withheld at the existing college. Washington may retain neutral academic, income and enrollment conditions and genuine controls on prohibited state purchases; the present exclusion rests on the student's devotional study rather than proof that these payments purchase a forbidden religious service.

## Join and remedy boundaries

The five-Associate coalition does not adopt an Establishment Clause holding that these student payments necessarily purchase integrated devotional teaching. Their sufficient ground is the limited Free Exercise permission identified above. The three-Associate coalition does not support treating neutral routing or private choice as immunity for every public religious expenditure; the independent-benefit finding is necessary to its application. Neither side imports an automatic rule from the lower court's heightened-scrutiny label.

For the reversing five, the adverse federal judgment is reversed and the State's favorable disposition of the degree-exclusion claim is restored. For the affirming three, prospective relief removes this religious-degree condition from consideration of Davey's otherwise eligible scholarship; any properly presented monetary claim must receive the independent remedy adjudication that the record requires. The institution's requested signature is not a State-imposed attestation, and Davey's refusal supplies no separate default ground.

The material separate-writing function is the disagreement about whether the confined ministry-support choice survives the equality principles governing this individual benefit. Assignment and final opinion form remain for assembly. McCallum must receive only the actual completed public holding, which will determine whether this division produces controlling law.
