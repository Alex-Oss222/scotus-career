# Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States: independent commitments

Event and research cutoff: May 10, 2004. Authorized inputs: frozen case 77 neutral packet, neutral source/scope supplement and entering-law slice, including its complete statutory reading copies and effective public-law selections. No current controlled Chief Justice position, comparator, reconciliation or raw Decision Record was consulted. No authorship or final Court coalition is selected.

## Disposition and sufficient propositions

All eight Associates would affirm the Federal Circuit on the adverse-inference component. Section 1677e(b) permits an adverse inference when Commerce supports a finding that the interested party failed to cooperate by not acting to the best of its ability to comply with an information request. Intentional concealment, willfulness or bad faith is not an additional indispensable element. The inquiry concerns the party's capabilities and actual effort in the circumstances: it must make the inquiries and use the records reasonably available to it, rather than merely avoid intentional obstruction. A missing or late answer alone does not establish the separate failure-to-cooperate predicate.

Substantial evidence supports the predicate here. Commerce requested the conversion factor twice under identified questionnaire deadlines. Nippon's successive assertions that conversion was unnecessary and that the necessary weights were unavailable were followed by production after the preliminary adverse calculation. Its own later explanation identified the failure to ask production facilities; Commerce found the relevant records routinely maintained, readily available and not burdensome to produce. The administrative conclusion therefore rests on identified available capability and omitted effort, not lateness standing alone or an invented finding of fraud.

The later production does not erase that supported finding. Nippon's accelerated-investigation, database and inadvertence arguments bear on effort and available ability and must be considered, but they do not compel a contrary factual conclusion when the necessary factory inquiries were not made. The specific questionnaire deadlines govern the requested information; the asserted general preverification opportunity does not itself replace them. The agency treated entirely missing conversion information differently from a minor correction, and section 1677m(e) does not require acceptance merely because information arrives before verification: its conditions include the established submission deadline and demonstrated best ability as well as verifiability, reliability and usability.

## Statutory qualifications preserved

These commitments adopt no absolute rule that every failure to answer authorizes an adverse inference. Section 1677e(a) requires otherwise available facts when necessary information is unavailable or an enumerated withholding, deadline/form failure, significant impediment or unverifiability condition exists. That direction remains subject to section 1677m(d); the deadline/form branch separately preserves sections 1677m(c)(1) and (e).

Section 1677e(b)'s separate supported finding permits an adverse inference; it does not compel one. The inference selects among otherwise available facts. Section 1677e(c) requires corroboration of secondary information, to the extent practicable, from reasonably available independent sources.

Section 1677m(c)(1) requires consideration of a party's ability when it promptly explains its inability to comply in the requested form and manner and suggests alternatives; the agency may modify requirements as needed to avoid an unreasonable burden. Subsection (c)(2) separately requires attention to difficulties, particularly those of small companies, and practicable assistance. Subsection (d) requires prompt identification of a deficient response and a practicable opportunity to remedy or explain it within investigation limits; an unsatisfactory or late further response may be disregarded subject to subsection (e). Subsection (e)'s five conditions for required consideration are cumulative: submission by the established deadline, verifiability, sufficient completeness to be reliable, demonstrated best ability in providing information and meeting its requirements, and use without undue difficulties. Subsection (f)'s practicable written explanation for refusal remains separate.

The repeated requests, identified deadlines, admitted failure to inquire at the facilities and Commerce's actual explanation support this adverse-inference application under those provisions. Nippon's request for mandatory use of its later data fails on the presented grounds. The rule permits no adverse inference merely because information was genuinely unavailable despite adequate effort, and does not convert a statutory permission to disregard into a compulsory refusal of all corrections.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Intent-required path.** The strongest argument is that cooperation connotes deliberate conduct and adverse facts should secure accurate duties rather than punish honest misunderstanding. This would require a finding of willfulness or bad faith and a remand if absent. Its difficulty is the enacted definition of the relevant failure in terms of best ability: Congress separately requires more than missing information but does not make intent indispensable. A conscientious but unsuccessful effort and an avoidable failure to seek available records are different even when neither involves deceit. All eight reject an added categorical intent requirement.

**Ability-and-effort path.** The strongest objection is that it can collapse into strict liability for any inaccurate response, particularly in an accelerated investigation. The separate evidentiary finding and the preserved section 1677m protections answer that objection. The agency must identify what the party could reasonably provide and the available effort it failed to make; proof of the omission alone is inadequate. Here the agency identified the existing production records, the repeated requests and the failure to ask for those records. All eight accept both the rule and this substantial-evidence application.

**Remedy alternative.** A legal ruling rejecting an intent requirement followed by automatic agency remand would be justified if a material agency finding or delegated choice remained undone. Ventura preserves that allocation. Here Commerce already made the ability/effort finding and exercised the relevant adverse-inference choice on stated grounds. Whole-record review can sustain that actual determination without ordering an unnecessary repetition. The separately ungranted starting-price and record-regulation components retain the treatment fixed below.

## Remedy, limits and readiness

Affirmance sustains the Federal Circuit's restoration of Commerce's partial adverse-facts determination on the question reviewed. The remainder of the Federal Circuit's mandate, including its distinct currency-pricing disposition and the mootness treatment of the regulation challenge, remains operative; these are not new Supreme Court merits holdings. Any implementation or recalculation required by those surviving directions must observe the governing statutory information and corroboration rules. No new replacement margin is selected here.

The one-day discrepancy in the later backup-submission accounts remains unresolved because either date followed both questionnaire deadlines and changes none of these commitments. Nippon's reported verification assurances remain allegations, not newly found facts. The sources support the bounded statutory and application ruling without assuming concealment or misconduct beyond the actual inadequate effort found by Commerce.
