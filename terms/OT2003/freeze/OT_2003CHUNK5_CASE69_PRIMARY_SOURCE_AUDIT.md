## Main source findings

There is no single unconditional commercial-availability exemption shared by the six Rules. Two require technical-infeasibility certification; Rule 1191's provision covers only medium-duty vehicles and also requires inability to use its purchase-offset mechanism; three other provisions require a demonstration to the Executive Officer and retain their own availability/use wording. Controls, fuel requirements, timing, categorical exemptions, and purchasing scope differ.

The District brief's printed pages 10-11 broadly characterize the Rules as permitting any CARB-certified vehicle when designated vehicles are unavailable. Intervenors similarly describe the Rules as selection among existing certified products. Those are litigants' summaries and arguments. They do not erase the conditions and sunset in the enacted texts. The Irwin declaration's assertions about available certified vehicles and unintended unavailability, JA 216-221, are witness evidence and advocacy context, not textual amendments to the Rules.

## Rule 1186.1: sweepers

**Source:** JA 16-23, PDF pages 11-14. Availability certification is (e)(1)(C), JA 21, PDF page 13; the operative purchase condition and sunset are (d)(1)(B), JA 19, PDF page 12.

**Coverage and trigger.** The rule applies to sweeping by or for government agencies. Its coverage and fleet-operator definition attach the 15-vehicle threshold to the governmental agency receiving the service, including when a private firm supplies that service. It is not safe to substitute a requirement that every private contractor itself own 15 sweepers. The threshold counts the agency's passenger cars and light-, medium-, and heavy-duty vehicles, excluding Rule 1191(f)(1) emergency vehicles. Sweepers must weigh at least 14,000 pounds. The defined purchase/lease date is execution by both parties of a contract requiring delivery within one year.

**Purchase route.** Starting July 1, 2002, each replacement/additional sweeper purchase or lease must be alternative fueled, or, **before July 1, 2004**, have an approved Technical Infeasibility Certification for that particular transaction. The latter route requires every applicable approved control device, maintenance to the manufacturer's specifications, exclusively low-sulfur diesel when diesel is used, and compliance with Rule 1186 without its (i)(3) exception unless the operator demonstrates that solely technical reasons make no certified sweeper under Rule 1186(c)(2) commercially available. Approved devices must handle all diesel exhaust, including auxiliary-engine exhaust, and be CARB certified at purchase. Low-sulfur diesel is no more than 15 ppm sulfur. Diesel hybrid-electric and dual-fuel technologies do not count as alternative fuel under this Rule.

**Availability and certification.** Six months before purchase/lease, the operator must submit a signed, dated request attesting accuracy, identify name/address and fleet composition, make/model and dust-suppression systems, and demonstrate either that no alternative-fuel engine/chassis is commercially available from any manufacturer for its sweeping operations, with only technical reasons acceptable for choosing the chassis, or that a fueling station for commercially available alternative-fuel sweepers suited to its operations is unavailable within five miles of the storage/maintenance yards. The Executive Officer must approve or deny in writing within 90 calendar days after a completed request. A deficient request must be denied with written reasons. Following denial the operator must comply with (d)(1), may reapply, and must continue complying until approval. Rule 306 filing/evaluation fees apply.

**Separate service-contract route.** Government agencies signing sweeping-service contracts after July 1, 2002 must contract for alternative-fuel sweeping, or solicit such bids and, only if none are submitted, contract for nonalternative sweeping with low-sulfur diesel, installed and maintained approved controls, and the same Rule 1186 compliance/solely-technical qualification. After January 1, 2003, renewal or extension options count as new contracts. This (d)(2) route has no July 2004 cutoff in its text.

**Other exemptions.** Fleets consisting exclusively of manufacturer or manufacturer-representative test/evaluation vehicles are exempt. A sweeper purchase solely dedicated to serving government agencies outside the Rule is exempt from subdivision (d), upon demonstration to and approval by the Executive Officer. Purchase, lease, and service-contract records and exemption proof must be supplied on request.

**Unresolved textual points.** The full Rule contains no general availability escape from the purchase requirement after June 30, 2004. No separate resolution adopting this Rule is reproduced in the appendix; JA 184 quotes a board direction to return with proposed amendments for cleaner conventional engines, not an enacted general exemption. The scan also confirms that (e)(1)'s purchase/lease certification paragraph points parenthetically to (d)(2), although (d)(2) concerns service contracts. That cross-reference is preserved, not silently repaired. The sources do not establish an authoritative interpretation resolving its effect on service contracts. Rule 1186 itself, whose dust-control exemption is incorporated, is not reproduced in full in these three sources; only the express incorporated qualification can be reported here.

## Rule 1191: public light- and medium-duty fleets

**Source:** JA 24-31, PDF pages 15-18. Availability exception (f)(8), JA 30, PDF page 18; offsets and used-vehicle conversion, JA 28-29, PDF page 17. Staff explanation is JA 91-94, PDF pages 56-57.

Beginning July 1, 2001, public fleet operators with 15 or more vehicles must procure listed vehicles, or other CARB-certified equivalent low-emitting gasoline or alternative-fuel vehicles, when adding or replacing vehicles; bids must specify compliance. The compliant list begins with LEV, ULEV, SULEV, and ZEV vehicles. Light-duty and medium-duty categories separately move to ULEV or cleaner when the Executive Officer determines that at least half of the respective California new-vehicle sales are ULEV or equivalent. The list is updated every six months.

The commercial-availability exception is limited to **medium-duty** vehicles. It requires demonstration to the Executive Officer both that a LEV-or-cleaner engine/chassis configuration is not commercially available **and** that the operator cannot use (e)(3). Paragraph (e)(3) permits a non-LEV gasoline/diesel medium-duty vehicle if sufficient alternative-fuel ULEV/SULEV/ZEV purchases already in the fleet as of July 1, 2001 offset its emissions, or if sufficient alternative-fuel SULEV/ZEV vehicles are purchased concurrently. Under (e)(4), one noncompliant medium-duty vehicle/engine is offset by one alternative-fuel light/medium ZEV, two alternative-fuel light/medium SULEVs, ten alternative-fuel light-duty ULEVs, or three alternative-fuel medium-duty ULEVs. The ULEV credits in the formula remain subject to (e)(3)'s distinction between prior purchases and concurrent purchases.

Paragraph (e)(5) expressly permits purchase of used vehicles below LEV if converted to LEV-or-cleaner operation using CARB-certified conversion kits. The staff explanation recognizes new and used qualifying purchases and anticipates ULEV conversion when that tier governs. The actual rule text supplies the operative terms; staff expectations should not silently rewrite it.

Categorical exemptions cover emergency/rescue vehicles of the identified law-enforcement, fire, hospital, medical, or paramedic entities used to respond to potential threats to life/property; surveillance/undercover vehicles; heavy-duty on-road vehicles; exclusive manufacturer test fleets; privately owned/operated light- or medium-duty fleets providing public-agency contract services; purchase/lease contracts signed as of adoption; and nonpassenger military vehicles. Records must be retained for two years and supplied with exemption proof on request. There is no equivalent blanket unavailability exemption for light-duty vehicles in (f)(8).

## Rule 1192: transit fleets

**Source:** JA 46-51, PDF pages 33-36. Availability exception (e)(2), JA 50, PDF page 35.

The Rule covers public transit operators and private operators under contract to a public transit agency, with fleets of 15 or more vehicles in the covered categories. Heavy-duty transit vehicles weigh at least 14,000 pounds; urban buses at least 33,000 pounds. Alternative fuel excludes diesel hybrid-electric and dual-fuel technologies. The alternative-fuel purchase/lease rule applies from adoption to covered agencies other than municipal operators with fewer than 100 vehicles; municipal operators with 15-99 vehicles begin July 1, 2001. Private contractors follow the schedule applicable to their contracting agency.

Upon demonstration to the Executive Officer, (e)(2) exempts heavy-duty passenger-transport vehicles for which no alternative-fuel engine/chassis configuration is commercially available or could be used. The exact availability/use construction must be preserved. It should not be reduced to a categorical exemption whenever a preferred product cannot be purchased; the language's treatment of theoretical usefulness is contested in the litigation.

Other exemptions cover paratransit; out-of-District long-distance buses generally equipped with luggage compartments, restrooms, and overhead storage; up to ten vehicles per fleet used exclusively for manufacturer testing/evaluation; school buses; nonpublic transport such as employee or prisoner transportation; and purchase/lease contracts signed before adoption, excluding later-exercised options. Two-year records and proof of claimed exemption are required. The Rule's transit-service coverage includes private contractors; it is not accurately described as having no private application.

## Rule 1193: solid-waste fleets

**Source:** JA 52-57, PDF pages 36-39. Availability exception (e)(3), JA 55, PDF page 38.

The Rule covers public and private operators of covered heavy-duty solid-waste collection, transfer, and roll-off fleets. The covered fleet threshold is 15, with staged requirements: collection fleets of 50 or more begin July 1, 2001; collection or combined covered fleets of 15 or more begin July 1, 2002. Collection purchases/additions must be alternative fueled or, under (d)(1)(B), dual fueled before July 1, 2003. The separate (d)(2) route for covered transfer/roll-off purchases begins July 1, 2001 and permits alternative or dual fuel without the same cutoff in that paragraph.

Upon demonstration to the Executive Officer, (e)(3) exempts solid-waste collection vehicles required under (d) for which no alternative-fuel engine/chassis configuration is commercially available or could be used. Preserve the exact covered noun and availability/use wording. The exception is not a freestanding command permitting every otherwise noncompliant vehicle.

Other exemptions cover up to ten exclusive manufacturer test/evaluation vehicles per fleet; vehicles outside residential/commercial solid-waste collection or transfer-station/landfill service; contracts signed before adoption even with later delivery, except options exercised after adoption with delivery after the effective date. Operators may apply to the Hearing Board for a variance; merely applying is not a variance. Paragraph (e)(6) permits dual-fuel purchases until July 1, 2003 upon demonstration that all existing 1995-and-later diesel collection vehicles in the fleet have CARB-certified particulate controls achieving at least a 71 percent reduction. The saved rule also uses July 1, 2003 in (d)(1)(B); staff-report descriptions of earlier proposals are not a basis to change the enacted text. Fleets of 15-49 must submit their intended alternative-fuel-source letter by July 1, 2001. The Rule requires records and exemption proof and contains a severability clause.

## Rule 1194: airport passenger transport

**Source:** JA 58-65, PDF pages 39-43. Availability exception (e)(2), JA 63, PDF page 42.

The Rule covers public and private airport operators with 15 or more covered vehicles, including limousine, fare shuttle, courtesy shuttle, rental/hotel, and taxi services. Only vehicles substantially operating at or dedicated to commercial airports count, but covered vehicles across multiple District airports are added together. Vehicles covered by the other listed fleet rules are excluded.

From July 1, 2001, limousine and specified terminal/courtesy services must acquire CARB ULEV/SULEV/ZEV passenger cars and medium-duty vehicles. Unscheduled multiple-party shuttle vans have a 50-percent compliant-purchase/lease requirement starting July 1, 2001 and 100 percent beginning July 1, 2002. Heavy-duty airport/parking/rental/hotel shuttles must acquire alternative-fuel vehicles beginning July 1, 2001. Taxi pickups must acquire ULEV/SULEV/ZEV vehicles beginning January 1, 2002. Vehicles acquired with the designated subsidy must operate within the District for at least three years.

Upon demonstration to the Executive Officer, (e)(2) exempts vehicles required under (d) for which no alternative-fuel engine/chassis configuration is commercially available or could be used. This language expressly refers to alternative-fuel configuration even though some substantive duties allow listed emission-tier vehicles; do not replace it with a more generalized availability formula.

Other exemptions include equipped long-distance out-of-District buses and the separate taxi cofunding provision. A taxi operator may buy a non-ULEV light/medium vehicle if the District or designated agent does not fully offset the compliant alternative-fuel vehicle's purchase cost minus the operator's $10,000 within ten working days after notice and documentation that the operator contribution is immediately available; the period is two working days for an accident rendering the replaced taxi inoperable and requiring immediate replacement, with the same contribution showing. The operator still pays conversion to taxi specifications, incidental costs, sales tax on its contribution, licensing, and registration. A permitted noncompliant purchase must occur within ten working days after notice of permission or the operator must reapply for cofunding. Wheelchair-accessible taxis may be non-ULEV if they meet the airport and accessibility specifications, including the stated door-height/lift requirements. These exemptions are separate from commercial availability. Records and exemption proof are required.

## Rule 1196: public heavy-duty fleets

**Source:** JA 66-74, PDF pages 43-47. Technical certification (e)(1)(C), JA 69, PDF page 45, visually checked against the scan.

Coverage is public heavy-duty fleets of at least 15 vehicles, weighing at least 14,000 pounds, excluding vehicles governed by the other listed fleet rules. Privately owned/operated heavy-duty fleets supplying public-agency contract services are expressly exempt under (f)(2), though another Fleet Rule may cover a particular operation.

Beginning July 1, 2002, new additions to an existing fleet or formation of a new fleet must use alternative-fuel heavy-duty engines/vehicles, qualifying dual-fuel heavy-duty vehicles, dedicated gasoline heavy-duty vehicles, or a diesel vehicle/engine with an approved Technical Infeasibility Certification for that transaction and approved controls maintained to manufacturer specifications. The dual-fuel definition requires CARB certification to an applicable optional nitrogen-oxide standard plus particulate aftertreatment meeting the latest preadoption CARB-certified M-100 methanol engine's particulate level. Approved controls handle all diesel exhaust, must be fitted at purchase, and require diesel sulfur no greater than 15 ppm.

For certification, a signed, dated, accurate request is due at least 60 days before purchase/lease, with operator identity and fleet composition. Paragraph (e)(1)(C) requires demonstration that no engine and chassis/body configuration meeting (d)(1), (d)(2), or (d)(3) is commercially available from any manufacturer, with only technical reasons acceptable for choosing a chassis/body configuration, or could be used in a specific application. This is the exact source of the sixth availability qualification; it is not an unconditional exclusion from the Rule.

Paragraph (e)(1)(D) addresses lack of an alternative-fuel station within five miles of storage/maintenance or extended home-base locations supplied by mobile fueling. Paragraph (e)(1)(E) addresses compliant vehicles exceeding the incremental cost-effectiveness criterion of the most recent Carl Moyer or Mobile Source Emission Reduction Review Committee program, whichever is greater. The scan confirms that the list contains no explicit connective between C, D, and E. This audit therefore does not resolve whether each is an independently sufficient certification ground or whether some operate cumulatively. The District's brief describes cost/use as possible exemptions but does not supply a definitive administrative construction. The sunset sentence in (d)(4) refers specifically to **(e)(1)(D) after June 30, 2004**; it does not textually sunset C's availability/use criterion.

The Executive Officer must decide a completed request within 45 calendar days, must deny a request that fails (e)(1), and must give written reasons. After denial the operator must satisfy (d)(1)-(3); a new request or variance application does not itself suspend that obligation. Rule 306 fees apply.

Other exemptions include emergency/rescue vehicles; the private contract fleets described above; up to ten exclusive manufacturer test/evaluation vehicles per fleet; preadoption contracts, except later options with delivery after effectiveness; tactical military vehicles; vehicles routinely moving material into/out of the District; the specified state/special-district vehicles serving the Riverside Mojave area when equipped with approved controls; limited controlled conventional vehicles where the remainder of the fleet satisfies (d)(1) or (d)(2), with the three-vehicle total/per-yard limits and aggregate deployment plan in (f)(8); Postal Service winter-holiday leases no longer than 120 days in twelve months; and out-of-District public jurisdictions' vehicles housed/operated within the District no more than 180 days in twelve months. The complete paragraphs in the mechanical extract control each category. Compliance records older than one year need not be supplied under (g)(1); exemption proof remains required on request.

## Public/private and new/used distinctions

Public ownership is not a uniform condition: 1191 and 1196 expressly exempt the respective private contractor fleets, while 1186.1, 1192, 1193, and 1194 contain private applications. A Rule's exclusion of vehicles governed by other Fleet Rules is not a general exemption for private business.

Rule 1191(e)(5) expressly regulates purchases of used vehicles and permits conversion through CARB-certified kits. Its staff report expressly recognizes both new and used qualifying purchases. None of the six texts supplies a blanket exemption for all used vehicles. Several operative provisions refer to new purchases, replacement purchases, or additions to a fleet; those transaction descriptions should not automatically be equated with the federal Act's definition of a new motor vehicle. These sources do not adjudicate the precise used-vehicle boundary of each Rule. They also do not establish a particular past purchase or an as-applied enforcement dispute as the exclusive controversy.

## Section 246, waiver, and statutory context

Intervenors' brief printed page 36 and notes 8-9, PDF page 25, expressly states that respondents never argued adoption of the Fleet Rules pursuant to section 246. Their defense is that the Rules are outside the preempted category and need no saving provision. Note 8 acknowledges differences from the federal fleet program, especially its numerical emission standards versus the Rules' fuel-type requirements. Note 9 similarly characterizes arguments about saving the Rules under sections 209(b) and 177 as answers to positions respondents did not advance.

The District's printed pages 36-41, PDF pages 25-27, use section 246 and the related federal program as structural statutory context. The brief disputes a new section-246 preemption theory as not raised below or in the certiorari petition. That preservation objection must not be restated as proof that the Rules comply with section 246. The District's printed page 43, PDF page 28, advances section 209(d) use restrictions as an additional argument and acknowledges that point was not litigated below. It does not establish a lower-court determination of that ground.

The intervenors' printed page 43, note 11, PDF page 28, states that only California has the section 209(b) waiver option, not every local government. That is advocacy concerning the statutory mechanism, not evidence that a waiver approving these six local Rules exists. CARB certification of individual vehicles, waiver approval of California standards, and approval of the Fleet Rules are distinct propositions. The statutory and agency materials appended to the District brief were read as context; they do not establish approval of these Rules under section 246 or a Fleet-Rule-specific waiver.

## Scope of the challenge and identified gaps

The joint appendix does not reproduce the complaint or its prayer. Its docket entries identify the first amended complaint as requesting declaratory and injunctive relief, JA 5/PDF 5, and identify summary judgment on counts 1-6, JA 7/PDF 6. The judgment docket entry broadly declares all six Rules lawful and valid, grants respondents' motions, denies petitioners' motions, and dismisses on the merits, JA 12-13/PDF 9. The materials support a challenge to the Rules, but the precise complaint prayer cannot be quoted from these sources. Obtaining that pleading would resolve its exact terms.

The specific unresolved source questions are: the authoritative construction of the availability/use wording; the effect of Rule 1186.1(e)(1)'s service-contract cross-reference; whether any timely enacted amendment outside these supplied sources altered the July 2004 sweeper purchase sunset; and the connective relationship of Rule 1196(e)(1)(C)-(E). No such amendment or definitive administrative construction is established by these three sources. Proposed future amendments, broad litigant summaries, and expected market availability do not supply them by inference.
