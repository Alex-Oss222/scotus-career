Control extracted objective record facts from a published primary opinion. Historical Supreme Court outcomes, votes, reasons and orders are excluded. These facts require fresh neutral validation; allegations remain allegations. Research cutoff is the scheduled event date.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/03-6821.ZO.html, factual Part I and objective complaint references. The action was dismissed at the pleading stage, so the medical facts and alternatives are allegations. Nelson had severely compromised peripheral veins. In August 2003 counsel asked the warden for the venous-access protocol and a physician consultation. The warden declined to supply the protocol. On September 10 the warden described a half-inch arm incision 24 hours before execution; on October 3 the description changed to a two-inch incision in arm or leg one hour before execution, using local anesthesia, without assurance of a physician's presence. Counsel requested the protocol again and was refused. The action was filed the following Monday, three days before the scheduled October 9 execution. It sought an injunction against the cut-down, a temporary stay to litigate the claim, disclosure of the protocol, and an appropriate venous-access protocol. An anesthesiologist's affidavit supported the alleged availability of a less invasive percutaneous central-line procedure. The complaint offered that alternative as permitting the scheduled execution. The district court dismissed as an unauthorized successive habeas petition, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed on that ground. The record here supplies no finding on exhaustion of available administrative remedies and no independent operative Supreme Court stay order.
