## Full propositions supported

**PC1 — NEPA's causal boundary.** NEPA requires review of environmental effects having a reasonably close causal relationship to the agency action under review. The contemporaneous section 1508.8 includes effects caused by that action at the same time and place and effects caused by it later or farther away that are reasonably foreseeable. Foreseeability and a necessary place in a sequence do not alone make the agency legally responsible for the effects of an independently controlled decision. Where FMCSA cannot prevent otherwise qualified carriers' entry on environmental grounds, its registration and safety rulemaking is not the relevant cause of the additional entry that the President independently authorizes by lifting the moratorium. NEPA does not require FMCSA to prepare an environmental impact statement on that entry increase as an effect of these rules.

Metropolitan Edison supplies the reasonably close causal relationship; Robertson supplies NEPA's informed-decision procedure rather than a freestanding environmental veto. The agency's lack of an environmental veto does not by itself remove all procedure: the point is that the particular admission decision and resulting increase belong to a separate decisionmaker, while this agency's actual choices must still be examined for the effects they cause. The record supports that allocation, not an immunity for any agency with limited powers. Lincoln and Bennett reinforce respecting statutory decisionmaking boundaries while enforcing actual legal duties.

**PC2 — Cumulative effects and alternatives.** Section 1508.7 requires the reviewed action's incremental impact to be considered with other past, present and reasonably foreseeable future actions regardless of actor. It does not dispense with identifying an incremental impact of the reviewed action or make every consequence of another actor's decision a consequence of the agency rule. The President's distinct action therefore does not become attributable to FMCSA merely by relabeling its entry effects cumulative. Effects caused by FMCSA's actual discretionary safety choices retain their ordinary NEPA treatment; this record's assessment addressed the agency rules, and the challenged omission is the independent traffic increase.

The commenters' failure to identify additional regulatory alternatives when FMCSA could consider them defeats the request to require an unpresented specific alternative on the account supplied. Vermont Yankee requires meaningful presentation of such an objection rather than forcing an agency to anticipate every unarticulated proposal. That conclusion concerns these missing alternative proposals; it is not forfeiture of every environmental objection, a demand for technical precision beyond fair notice, or a finding that a supported obvious defect could never be reviewed. No supported obvious omitted alternative is supplied here. A court cannot create an environmental exclusion alternative the agency lacks statutory authority to choose.

**PC3 — Clean Air Act conformity.** The conformity issue has its own regulatory terms. Under section 93.152, direct emissions of a criteria pollutant or its precursors must be caused or initiated by the federal action and occur at the same time and place. Indirect emissions must be caused by the action, may occur later or farther away but must remain reasonably foreseeable, and must be emissions the agency can practicably control and will maintain control over because of its continuing program responsibility. The challenged increased cross-border traffic emissions are not direct emissions of issuing the rules. They do not satisfy the indirect-emissions definition because FMCSA does not control the independent decision to admit otherwise qualified carriers and lacks a continuing environmental entry-control responsibility. The order demanding a full conformity determination for that traffic increase cannot stand.

The conformity regulation's explicit practicable-control and continuing-program-responsibility conditions do separate work. They are not silently inserted as identical wording into NEPA. The possibility that safety standards may have some emission consequences does not establish continuing control over the distinct traffic increase attributed to lifting the moratorium. The rule applies the supplied allocation of authority to the actual demanded determination, rather than declaring every FMCSA activity exempt from conformity review.

**PC4 — Judgment and remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it requires the environmental impact statement and full conformity determination for the independent increase in Mexican truck operations. Remand for disposition consistent with PC1–PC3 and removal of those reviewed requirements. The mandate does not require a new environmental inquiry into a supposed discretionary entry veto. The agency remains bound by the ordinary duties governing effects of its actual choices, but the present challenge identifies no additional preserved omitted alternative or distinct controllable effect requiring a new ordered analysis. This resolves the presented review requirements rather than issuing an advisory approval of every environmental feature of the rules.

## Adversarial comparison and scope

**Competing path A: affirm the broader review order.** The strongest argument is that these rules are a necessary implementation step, increased truck emissions are foreseeable and NEPA informs the public as well as the agency; information can be useful even when the agency cannot veto the ultimate policy. The cumulative-effects regulation expressly permits consideration of actions by other actors. This is a serious procedural argument and is not answered merely by saying that NEPA supplies no environmental veto.

The commitment rejects path A because both direct and indirect effects still must be caused by the reviewed action, with Metropolitan Edison's legally relevant connection. The President controls the opening decision, the agency cannot use environmental grounds to exclude qualified carriers, and the demanded analysis concerns that independently determined increase rather than a choice among agency-controlled means. A necessary chronological step does not transfer responsibility for every effect of the independent policy. Cumulative analysis does not eliminate the action's incremental-impact premise. The informational function operates within that statutory causal scope. Separately, the conformity definition expressly requires practicable continuing agency control, which the record does not establish for the demanded traffic effects.

**Competing path B: reverse narrowly but order another agency-effects inquiry.** This would preserve the authority distinction while asking the lower court or agency to identify any emission effects of discretionary safety choices. It is lawful where the actual challenge identifies such an omitted effect or a preserved alternative. Here the approved record says the assessment addressed the agency rules and identifies the contested omission as the traffic increase caused by lifting the moratorium; commenters identified no additional alternative. A remand cannot manufacture a new defect. PC4 therefore removes the unsupported requirements and leaves genuinely independent preserved proceedings to their actual posture, without commanding an additional inquiry absent a supported predicate.

No holding here treats the President's asserted exemption as automatically exempting the agency. No opinion author, assignment or Chief vote is supplied. No private discussion is invented.
