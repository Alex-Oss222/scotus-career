# Case 77: neutral source and scope supplement

This preparation supplies objective agency and lower-court material for the May 10, 2004 event. It adds no Supreme Court action or commitment.

Its recitations distinguish the agency determination, the trade court's three remands and final judgment, the appellate holding, and the parties' arguments. ## Material record and supported positions

Commerce requested a theoretical-to-actual-weight conversion factor in the initial questionnaire and again in a supplemental questionnaire. The applicable questionnaire response deadlines were December 21, 1998 and January 25, 1999. Nippon first said conversion was unnecessary because quantity types were consistent within product types, then said the necessary weights were unavailable. Its later explanation was that staff preparing the answers had not sought the production-facility records. The subsequent ability to produce a factor, and the factory records' availability, were the agency's stated evidence of inadequate effort. That evidence remains subject to review; the omission alone is not an independent finding of inadequate cooperation.

Nippon's published administrative position was that it made an inadvertent error amid an accelerated investigation, that the production and sales databases did not overlap, and that it corrected the error before verification. It invoked the general seven-day-before-verification rule, minor-correction practice, section 1677m(e), and the accuracy and nonpunitive purposes of antidumping calculations. Its assertions about assurances at verification and unwillingness to permit an explanation are allegations reported in the agency notice, not independently established events. Domestic producers responded that specific questionnaire deadlines controlled, that entirely missing data were not a minor correction, and that no timely inquiry at the factories had been made. Commerce adopted the specific-deadline and available-data grounds and returned the information on April 12, 1999.

The published sources differ in describing the later backup submission: the Federal Circuit identifies March 2, while the final notice identifies March 1. Both place it after the two questionnaire deadlines; the first conversion-factor submission is described as February 22, three days after the preliminary determination. No present path depends on choosing between March 1 and March 2, and the preparation does not resolve that discrepancy by assumption.

Commerce did more than label the omission untimely: its final notice states that the requested data were routinely maintained, readily available, and not burdensome to produce, and its first remand determination addressed the failure to ask the factories. Nippon disputes whether that establishes the statutory degree of cooperation. A properly confined decision may address both the legal trigger and the substantial-evidence application without assuming fraudulent concealment.

## Qualifications that must remain distinct

Section 1677e(a) requires use of otherwise available facts under its triggering conditions, subject to section 1677m(d), with the explicit section 1677m(c)(1) and (e) qualifications on the deadline/form branch. Section 1677e(b) permits, rather than compels, an adverse inference after its separate best-ability finding. Section 1677e(c) requires practicable corroboration of secondary information from reasonably available independent sources. Section 1677m(c) addresses prompt notice of inability with full explanation and proposed alternatives, and assistance with difficulties; section 1677m(d) separately requires prompt deficiency notice and a practicable opportunity to remedy or explain within investigation limits. Its permission to disregard deficient or late further responses remains subject to subsection (e).

Section 1677m(e)'s five conditions are cumulative: submission by the established deadline, verifiability, sufficient completeness for reliability, demonstrated best ability in providing the information and meeting its requirements, and use without undue difficulty. Section 1677m(f)'s practicable written explanation for refusing information remains separate. The complete statutory words are copied in the slice; no qualification is replaced by a general fairness formula. A best-ability construction does not silently validate any selected margin, dispense with these provisions, or create an absolute prohibition on every late correction.

## Scope and remedy

The submitted question concerns adverse inferences. The Federal Circuit separately rejected Commerce's yen starting-price methodology and vacated as moot the trade court's invalidation of section 351.104(a). Those are components of the judgment below; they are not independent grants for Supreme Court resolution. The presented adverse-inference question permits a bounded ruling and the consequent remedy. Rejection of an intent requirement does not itself require the agency's inference or eliminate whole-record substantial-evidence review. If further agency work is lawfully necessary, Ventura governs its allocation; an unneeded administrative redo is not automatic.

No additional contested factual premise is adopted. The existing approved neutral packet and this source supplement support independent modeling. A proposal to adjudicate the separate starting-price question, the general validity of the record-exclusion regulation, a specific replacement margin, or a new dispute over accepted corrective data would require a scope and source check before it is added.
