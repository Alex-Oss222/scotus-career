The indictment alleges three corrupt transactions concerning Minneapolis development, an agent who sat on the city council and development-agency commission, approximately $28.8 million in city federal assistance and $23 million in agency assistance. The $5,000 payment, $10,000 offer and proposed $80,000 kickback are allegations, not guilt findings. The indictment was dismissed before trial and reinstated by the Eighth Circuit. No statutory element is established merely by rejecting this facial challenge.

Salinas rejects demonstrable effect on federal dollars as an added element. Fischer confines benefits to a genuine assistance relationship. The actual offense still requires corrupt giving, offering or agreement to give a thing of value, intent to influence or reward a covered agent, covered business or a transaction or series involving at least $5,000, and more than $10,000 in qualifying benefits during the statutory continuous one-year period. That period may straddle the offense within its twelve-month limits. Section 666(c) preserves bona fide salary, wages, fees, other compensation and ordinary-course expenses. Transaction value and bribe value remain distinct.

## Supported paths and answer to the principal objection

The affirmative path uses Spending plus Necessary and Proper power: Congress may protect the integrity and effective administration of genuine federally assisted institutions by prohibiting corrupt influence over their agents' substantial transactions without tracing each bribe to a particular federal dollar. Corruption of an agent controlling the assisted institution's business can impair that institution's ability to perform its federally supported work; fungibility reinforces this operational relationship but is not an independent grant of police power. McCulloch permits appropriate preventive means, and Salinas and Fischer identify the statutory class and its existing boundaries. Lopez, Home Builders and the unanimous Rancho Viejo application require a real fit to the source of enumerated power, but their Commerce mechanisms are not additional elements of a Spending-power offense.

The opposing path would treat the lack of a transaction-specific federal effect as fatal or require a permissible limiting construction. Its strongest point is that a small federal grant must not become a pretext for national control of every local wrong. The response is that this statute regulates intentional corruption of agents of actual assistance recipients in substantial recipient transactions, rather than all misconduct in a locality that receives aid. The record supplies that program-and-agent relationship. Dole and Kansas concern conditions on a recipient's choice of aid; they do not establish that criminal protection against third-party bribery is available only as a consensual grant condition. No State administration command is imposed here.

## Scope, remedy and handoff

All eight support affirmance of the Eighth Circuit's rejection of this facial challenge. The operative proposition is that the pleaded statutory program-protection mechanism is constitutionally permissible without a traced effect on particular federal dollars. All eight support trial under the actual offense, including its corrupt-intent, agency, benefits, transaction-value and compensation-exception requirements. None treats Salinas as having already decided the constitutional question, rewrites the statute to add a traced-dollar element, or adjudicates criminal liability. A concrete materially different application may be tested on its actual program relationship; the present decision does not manufacture such facts.
