## Law entering the event

The exact reusable title is **Claim preclusion after federal diversity judgments**, present operation effective **Semtek, February 27, 2001**. Its federal-common-law source analysis and distinction between judgment effect and a procedural merits label are relevant, but its rendering-state incorporation rule does not decide this federal-question judgment. The more direct current authority is **SEC v. First Jersey Securities, September 28, 1998**, Kennedy's Part I, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: a prior judgment does not release genuinely later distinct transactions merely because their method resembles the earlier conduct; optional supplementation does not require adding every later wrong. **Arizona v. California, June 19, 2000**, Stevens's unanimous Part II, distinguishes final settlement effect from an issue actually adjudicated. Its continuing-original-action forfeiture holding is not transferred here.

Surviving Lawlor supplies the distinct-later-conduct principle, Montana the actual-issue inquiry, and ordinary contempt law requires breach of a definite operative command rather than enlargement of a judgment into protection against every later governmental action. The relevant statutory settings are 19 U.S.C. §§1555(b), 1557(a) and 26 U.S.C. §4081. This event interprets the earlier judgment's effect; it does not admit a new statute or infer any displaced historical legislative predicate. No identified earlier current-term holding changes these rules.

## Competing lawful paths

**Affirmance:** Preserve the earlier judgment while recognizing that a later tax-dependent revocation, following the IRS response, presents a different operative administrative action and an issue that the first judgment did not decide. Ammex remains able to test that later action through its proper review channel. A contempt motion cannot supply the missing adjudication of excise-tax status.

**Reversal:** If the Government were merely attempting to relitigate the same claim or an actually decided issue, a different legal label would not avoid preclusion; if the later action violated a definite prior command, enforcement could be appropriate. Ammex's strongest argument is that Customs could have presented the tax rationale earlier and cannot withhold defenses to defeat finality. That argument is insufficient on the supplied record because the relevant distinction is the later administrative determination following the IRS response, not the number of reasons Customs can invent for a single unchanged action. No finding establishes that the prior judgment adjudicated the later tax-dependent determination or commanded permanent authorization despite independently applicable legal conditions.

The surviving path affirms the refusal of contempt and the rejection of the asserted preclusive bar. It neither validates the revocation's tax merits nor holds that every new agency rationale creates a new claim.
