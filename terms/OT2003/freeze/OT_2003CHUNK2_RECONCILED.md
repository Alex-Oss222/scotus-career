- Section 323(d) reaches specified §501(c) organizations that engage in the defined federal-election spending, and specified §527 organizations, with the political-committee, state/local-party and authorized state/local-candidate exceptions. Its words cover **any** funds. Federal-compliant donations cannot silently be read out of the statute.
- Section 323(e)(1)(A) addresses federal elections, whereas (B) imports source and amount restrictions for other elections without importing every federal reporting requirement. Its own-state/local-candidacy exception, attendance/speaking/featured-guest permission, general charitable solicitation exception, and individual-only $20,000 annual specific-solicitation exception retain their separate terms. Section 323(f) has the own-state/local-election, state/local-candidates-only exception.
- The §201 primary category has broadcast/cable/satellite, identified-candidate and 60-/30-day conditions, plus the relevant-electorate/50,000-reception condition for nonpresidential candidates. News/commentary/editorial, qualifying debate/forum, already-covered expenditure/independent-expenditure and regulatory exceptions retain their different predicates, including the media ownership/control limit and the restriction on the regulatory exemption. The fallback is conditional and cumulative; its no-plausible-meaning-other-than-an-exhortation-to-vote condition is enacted law, not disposable surplusage.
- Sections 203 and 204 concern treasury sources and a separate statutory nonprofit exception; §204 withdraws that exception for its defined targeted communications. Direct individual-origin requirements, prohibited-source attribution, the segregated individual-only account, contracts, and tax-law limits remain distinct. Constitutional Day/MCFL protection is not repealed by the withdrawal of a broader statutory exception.
- Section 202 classifies actually coordinated electioneering disbursements or contracts as contributions/expenditures; it is not itself a universal ban. Section 211 defines independence. Section 214(a) separately concerns coordination with parties; (b) repeals the identified rule; (c) commands replacement regulations without an agreement/formal-collaboration prerequisite; (d) incorporates contribution/expenditure definitions without newly enacting the preexisting direct/indirect-payment words.
- Section 213 concerns the postnomination cycle choice between independent expenditure and the special coordinated-party allowance. Ordinary $5,000 multicandidate contributions/coordinated spending remain available after independent spending. The single-committee and transfer/assignment/receipt rules implement that choice. Section 318 has no own-earnings, consent or independent-choice exception for persons seventeen or younger.

For clarity, the Levin own-fund rule excludes funds supplied by another state/district/local committee of any state party, a national or national congressional committee, their representative officers/agents, and their directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained or controlled entities. The specially permitted nonfederal amounts may not be solicited, received, directed, transferred or spent by or in the name of §323(a) or (e) actors, apart from the expressly retained §323(e)(3) attendance/speaking permission. They may not be solicited, received or directed through joint fundraising by two or more state/district/local committees or their agents, or by such a committee for a committee or agent in another State. The broadcast exception is a communication referring solely to a clearly identified state/local candidate. The four state-activity exclusions do not convert registration or turnout/generic activity into excluded activity merely by naming only a state candidate: the state-only public-communication exclusion itself excludes activity in classes (i) and (ii). Section 323(b)'s covered actors also include associations or similar groups of state/local candidates or officeholders. The party tax-exempt recipient rule includes a §501(c) organization exempt under §501(a), or an applicant for that determination, that makes federal-election expenditures/disbursements; the separate §527 branch carries its own listed exceptions.

The news exception applies to a news story, commentary or editorial distributed through broadcasting facilities **unless** a political party, political committee or candidate owns or controls those facilities. A qualifying candidate debate/forum must comply with Commission regulations; the separate promotional exception covers a communication solely promoting it and made by or for its sponsor. Section 203's statutory nonprofit exception concerns §501(c)(4) entities, including applicants for that status, and §527(e)(1) organizations using exclusively funds provided directly by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents. Funds originating with a prohibited §316(a) entity are not direct individual funds. Direct or indirect payment of any communication cost by a prohibited entity invokes the attribution rule. A §501(c)(4) entity with business receipts or prohibited-entity funds is treated as using those amounts unless the communication is paid from the individual-only §304(f)(2)(E) account. Section 204 withdraws the statutory exception for an electioneering communication distributed from a television/radio station or cable/satellite television provider; for a candidate other than President or Vice President it also requires the relevant-electorate reception threshold. Those enacted rules remain distinct from Day's constitutional protection and from disclosure's optional-account route.

## 3. Title I — selected grounds and rival lawful paths

### A. National-party money, §323(a)

**Selected sustaining ground (Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer):** Reverse the decree's invalidation and sustain the national-party restriction on this facial record. The regulated transaction is receipt or solicitation of a donation into the national party's federal-candidate financing institution, including the circumvention paths documented through federal-officeholder fundraising, donor understandings, fundraising credits, allocation and state-party transfers. That record supplies more than party identity, public dislike of wealth, or ordinary access. The danger can arise when funds are raised; their ultimate use for a state project does not erase the donor's demonstrated relation to that fundraising channel. Source/amount/reporting compliance at receipt closes the demonstrated route without a ceiling on how much compliant money the party may spend.

The government's fit burden is met here by that functional record, the meaningful retained individual/PAC contribution channels and increased national-party allowance, and the documented operation of substantial federal fundraising. The finding is not that losing soft money costs nothing, that all fundraising losses can necessarily be replaced, or that electoral success proves adequate association. A second unlimited account would preserve the same covered solicitation and receipt mechanism; nominal allocation of its later spending is not an adequate answer to it. The $60,000 alternative illustrates a different legislative line, not proof that this line destroys meaningful association. No particular donor is adjudged corrupt.

**Selected contrary ground (Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas):** Affirm the invalidation of the nonfederal/mixed-use receipt and spending sweep and reverse the residual approval of the national party's independently controlled candidate-advocacy spending. Protect actual candidate transfers, candidate-controlled financial substitutes and genuine conduits under existing law; reject treating every donation and every use by the national institution as such a transfer. The record supports regulation of officeholder solicitation and tacit conduits but does not justify the complete new channel/source restriction for the party's own genuinely independent and nonfederal expression. Mariani's accepted distinction between direct control and materially different party arrangements, Lamb's separate fit requirement and these Justices' Colorado opinions supply the ground. Their relief does not invalidate ordinary FECA contribution limits or immunize sham accounts.

**Adversarial test and join limits:** The best argument against the sustaining path is the real loss of the chosen party channel for state activity and independent issue speech. The sustaining Justices answer at the receipt transaction and actual institutional fundraising record, not by calling those expenditures coordinated. The best argument against the contrary path is that a use-based limitation leaves the proven solicitation channel intact. The three answer with the narrower direct-officeholder and genuine-conduit controls and insist that the additional institutional presumption needs its own operational justification. Neither path may claim that ordinary access itself is corruption. The three will not join a rule validating all party money on a label; the five will not join a rule requiring a completed bribe or a formal earmark.

### B. State/local federal-election activity, §323(b)

**Registration and turnout/generic activity; Levin conditions.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse invalidation of the first two classes and sustain the challenged Levin conditions. The documented national-to-state transfers and donor directions exploit shared federal-election machinery, not merely the statistical fact that a state voter may also vote federally. The limited activity categories, express state-only exclusions and modified allocation route preserve substantial state activity. Requiring each committee to raise its own Levin funds, and restricting transfers and joint fundraising, prevents multiplication of the per-committee allowance into the very federal channel Congress closes. The restrictions do not prohibit the separate §323(e)(3) attendance/speaking permission. The facial holding does not decide a materially different demonstrated application or treat every local expenditure as federally connected.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm invalidation of these broad categories and their operative restrictions. A federal candidate on the same ballot, a 120-day period, and generic party identification can include independently chosen local activity without actual candidate resources or a demonstrated conduit. Levin preserves some funding but burdens chosen-party pooling and joint fundraising; the government cannot cure an inadequately fitted underlying category merely by offering a constrained alternative. Their ground accepts the actual transfer/donor-understanding evidence and permits tailored regulation of those transactions; it rejects the categorical reach, not all allocation or contribution law.

**Candidate-promoting public communications, class (iii).** The five sustaining Justices would affirm the district judgment on this class. The words promoting/supporting or attacking/opposing a candidate **for that office**, in this party-financing setting, provide a determinate candidate-election connection. Mere mention, issue inquiry or a statement that has only an incidental electoral effect does not by itself satisfy that formulation. It is a source-and-receipt safeguard on the documented party financing route, not a declaration that independent party expression is coordinated or that candidate gratitude alone justifies an expenditure ceiling. They reject both a mandatory list of electoral slogans and a subjective-intent prosecution.

The three contrary Justices would reverse this affirmance to the extent it subjects the party's independently controlled or jointly expressed message to the new source restriction without an actual resource/conduit connection. Their Colorado I/II and Bartlett separate positions require speaker-supplied electoral direction for independent expression and distinguish expression from candidate-controlled resources. A message's helping a candidate and a party's sophistication do not supply the missing connection. Genuine contributions remain regulable.

**Employee services, class (iv).** Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse invalidation and sustain this facial class: workers materially engaged in the shared federal campaign infrastructure are an identifiable resource channel, and a monthly more-than-quarter threshold leaves staff with only limited federal work outside this particular rule. They accept a practical payroll boundary as part of the supported integrated-operation safeguard; they do not rely on absence of a separately briefed objection.

O'Connor and Souter would vacate the class-(iv) judgment and remand for a distinct fit determination under Lamb. The rule subjects the whole covered salary to federal funding when federal work crosses 25 percent; the existing competing legal conclusions do not connect that full-payroll consequence to the particular circumvention mechanism and meaningful state-party operation under the governing burden. Neither Leon's indirect-effect-only rationale nor automatic validation of the other classes decides this question. Adequate existing evidence may suffice; no new study or evidentiary hearing is commanded. If the government cannot justify the actual operation, the court must provide corresponding relief, with applications severed only where statutory independence permits.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas prefer affirmance of class-(iv) invalidation: charging the entire salary to the federal account reaches substantial nonfederal work without a sufficiently fitted connection. They also **join the preceding vacatur/remand ground and remedy as a narrower disposition** if broader invalidation is not adopted. This express remedial agreement does not concede constitutionality or allow the district court to put the proof burden on parties. The alternatives are adjudicative choices, not unresolved source possession. No finding is made that the whole historical record contains no payroll evidence.

**Competing considerations across the classes:** The sustaining path must answer local burdens and changed allocation, not simply cite federal-election influence. The contrary path must answer the demonstrated shared fundraising/transfers and the impossibility of closing the national account while leaving that same channel untouched. Each class receives its own answer above. Class (iv)'s distinct remedial convergence is not extended to classes (i)–(iii).

### C. Party dealings with tax-exempt groups, §323(d)

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the blanket invalidation **only for nonfederal-fund applications** and affirm relief for solicitation, donation or direction of federally compliant funds. Covered election-spending tax-exempt groups can become substitute party conduits; the documented use of a nominal ballot organization to finance partisan registration and the broader satellite-organization record supply the preventive mechanism. The specified recipient classes, representative-capacity limitation and express exceptions matter. A restriction on noncompliant funds can operate independently and is materially directed to avoidance of Title I's lawful source/amount/reporting rules.

The statute nevertheless says any funds. The government has not supplied a sufficient distinct justification for barring donations already satisfying the federal safeguards merely because the recipient also engages in federal-election activity. A different PAC operated by that recipient does not automatically answer the lost chosen channel under Day and Mariani. Section 401 permits severance of the federal-compliant applications; it does not permit pretending that Congress wrote a soft-money-only definition. The proposed mandate would expressly identify the protected applications and the surviving nonfederal applications, retaining the existing recipient and actor exceptions.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm invalidation of §323(d) in full. They agree that hard-money applications fail, but also find the nonfederal sweep insufficiently fitted: covered recipients may conduct independent issue advocacy or genuine state activity, and the provision disallows the party's assistance without requiring that particular transaction to finance a federal candidate or function as a conduit. They accept the ballot-organization example as a real circumvention case and would preserve ordinary false-source, earmark and actual-resource enforcement. They will join the narrower protection for federal-compliant funds without joining approval of the balance.

The strongest sustaining argument is fungibility and predictable substitution of tax-exempt entities for party accounts. The strongest contrary argument is the complete bar on lawful assistance to an organization conducting mixed activities. The five's application severance answers the additional hard-money burden and leaves a source-specific anti-evasion rule; the three regard purpose-independent nonfederal coverage as a remaining substantial defect. Neither side invents a statutory earmarking exception or a finding that every tax-exempt group is a party affiliate.

### D. Federal officeholders and candidates, §323(e)

All eight Associates would affirm the district's facial sustaining judgment, on the ground stated here, while preserving the enacted exceptions. The relevant connection is the federal candidate's or officeholder's own solicitation, direction, receipt, transfer or spending, including action through the specified agents and controlled entities. The record of large donations obtained with the official's personal intervention and credited to that official supplies a direct obligation/circumvention risk even where the ultimate recipient spends in a state election. This does not depend on finding an express bargain or treating ordinary political access as a sufficient interest.

The federal-election branch retains federal source, amount and reporting rules. The other-election branch retains source and amount restrictions, not all federal reporting rules. The own-state/local-candidacy exception applies to an individual who is or was also such a candidate; the solicitation, receipt or spending must be solely in connection with that election, lawful under state law, and refer only to that candidate, another candidate for the same state/local office, or both. Attendance, speaking and featured-guest status remain permissible. General solicitations for eligible §501(c) organizations may not specify use and exclude entities principally devoted to the registration, voter-identification, get-out-the-vote or generic campaign activities described in §301(20)(A)(i)–(ii). The separate specific-solicitation route concerns funds for those activities or entities principally devoted to them, reaches only individuals, and permits no more than $20,000 solicited from each in a calendar year. These accommodations preserve ordinary participation and charitable association while preventing the personal federal fundraising route from becoming an unlimited channel.

For Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, this is a **distinct supported ground**, not a withdrawal of their objections to §§323(a), (b), (d) or (f). Their Lamb joins accept actual resources and tacit conduits; the official's own fundraising intervention supplies the materially closer donor-official connection missing from a party label alone. Their Mariani/Beaumont dissents do not immunize every business-source channel. They accept this facial source-and-amount safeguard with its unusually explicit solicitation exceptions on the present record, not a universal power to restrict an official's issue speech.

The strongest contrary path would protect solicitations for projects unrelated to the official's own campaign as pure association. The adopted answer is that the supported fundraising obligation attaches to the official's request, not only to the spending destination. A law barring attendance, endorsements, or ordinary issue cooperation would raise a different burden; the enacted permissions prevent that from being this case. No unpresented solicitations are adjudged unlawful.

### E. State/local officeholders' federal-candidate communications, §323(f)

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm, applying the same qualified candidate-for-office meaning used for class (iii). This rule closes the state-candidate account route to the federal candidate-promotion financing that the party safeguards address. Its actor and communication predicates, plus the state/local-only own-election exception, limit the reach. It does not prohibit all state-candidate issue speech or all references to federal officials. The government must prove the statutory predicates in enforcement; a state title alone supplies no coordination finding.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the sustaining judgment for independently controlled and joint expression lacking an actual candidate-resource/conduit connection. Their narrower lawful alternative is control of actual transfer or candidate-directed financial substitutes, leaving the state candidate's own message protected. They agree that the statutory own-election exception must be retained but do not regard it as curing the independent-expression sweep. The government's best counterargument is substitution of a state candidate's unrestricted account for a prohibited federal channel; the three require the real resource connection rather than the fact of praise or criticism alone.

## 4. Electioneering category and treasury sources

### A. Primary definition and the distinct facial treasury challenge

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse invalidation of the primary definition and sustain the challenged §§203–204 scheme against the presented categorical/facial attack **with the constitutional limits below expressly preserved**. The enacted medium, calendar, identified-candidate, and relevant-electorate elements are objectively ascertainable. Maine does not establish a constitutional magic-words prerequisite, and Bartlett's invalid general influence formula does not itself invalidate every more definite legislative category. The record independently supports the concentration of candidate-centered broadcasts in the chosen periods and electorate. The rationale is not that every ad influencing an election is suppressible.

The treasury rule requires its own source justification under Austin's surviving independent-election-spending setting and MCFL/Day, not Beaumont's direct-contribution holding. Austin's concern with a treasury accumulated through the business structure rather than voluntary political support retains force in that distinct setting; it is not an equality-of-influence interest or a finding that every independent supporter corrupts a candidate. The evidence of commercial/union financing of candidate-centered advertisements through nominally issue-oriented sponsors, protection of economic payers whose funds were accumulated for other purposes, and the lawful voluntary political funding route support rejecting a facial exemption for all covered entities. Corporate form or a nominal alternative fund alone would not do so. Genuine voluntary nonprofits retain the actual-characteristics protection in part B below, and Bartlett continues to protect genuine independent issue advocacy against an inadequately fitted prohibition. A particular communication or materially different organization may establish that constitutional application without first showing a right to invalidate the whole statute.

These five will not join a declaration that every communication satisfying the calendar/candidate criteria may constitutionally be barred from every treasury. Their facial judgment is not an automatic merits rejection of a future concrete genuine-issue or Day application. Nor do they add a textual pending-legislation exemption, reclassify all issue advertising as contributions, require speakers to obtain advisory permission, or state that choosing a different date/name/funding pool entails no burden. The new category's facial adequacy is a distinct proposition from the validity of every source-restricted application.

**Adversarial comparison:** Leon's account of genuine legislative advertisements, including the Fast Track call-to-action example, shows a real protected-speech concern. The competing statistical denominators and coding show why neither the unqualified 1998 estimates, the rejected 2000 0.6-percent estimate, nor the disputed treatment of 17 percent can be treated as an uncontested constitutional measurement. The five accept the objective concentration evidence and the statute's substantial legitimate operation while leaving constitutional application protection available. The stronger facial-remedy alternative would find the demonstrated issue-speech category substantially overbroad; these five decline to invalidate the whole primary category because the category also performs lawful informational and election-finance work and the record does not justify collapsing the distinct restrictions into one universal prohibition. They give no dispositive weight to public willingness to silence a speaker or to a mere capacity to edit out a candidate.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would retain relief from the expanded treasury prohibition through §401 application severance of §203, and reverse the corresponding sustaining portions of §§203–204; they would not invalidate the primary category's independently valid reporting operation. Candidate mention, timing, geographic reception and political impact do not establish speaker-supplied electoral direction or actual candidate resources. Their Maine and Bartlett separate positions, Colorado protection of independent expression, and the chosen-channel objections in Mariani and Beaumont support treating forced use of another pool as a real burden. Congress's objective line solves notice more readily than constitutional breadth. The documented genuine legislative advocacy is not made suppressible by an election window.

Their remedy leaves the preexisting lawful express-advocacy and actual-contribution scheme intact, leaves Austin within its existing scope, preserves applicable disclosure on its separately justified ground, and does not grant corporations immunity for genuine candidate financing. They do not rest on a constitutional list of canonical words. The strongest objection is that electoral advocacy can intentionally avoid such words; their answer permits functional explicit electoral direction supplied by the message but refuses to have election timing or candidate gratitude generate that direction. Their position on reporting is recorded separately rather than inferred from this source-restriction result.

### B. Nonprofit protection and §204

All eight Associates adopt the following proposition: withdrawing §203's specified **statutory** nonprofit exception does not withdraw constitutional protection for an actual voluntary political association under MCFL and Day. The inquiry concerns economic claimants, material commercial accumulation, material corporate/union funding or control, and whether the advocacy resources reflect voluntary political support. Incidental revenue and the lack of a formal refusal policy alone do not disqualify an association. Conversely, nonprofit status, ideological mission or funding from some individuals does not by itself immunize a materially commercial conduit.

The five sustaining the facial scheme would modify the lower decree so that it does not suggest §204 is facially void or that constitutional protection depends exclusively on a literal historical MCFL checklist. The retained declaration protects the Day/MCFL class; application to a named organization requires its actual characteristics and the actual communication. The statutory exception can otherwise be withdrawn in its stated targeted-communication category. There is no finding that every named plaintiff qualifies or that its direct candidate contributions share that protection.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join that nonprofit proposition and its protection but do not join approval of the expanded treasury reach in part A. As an independently sufficient narrower position, they agree that §204 cannot be enforced against the protected Day/MCFL class. They do not assert a categorical ideological-nonprofit contribution exemption rejected in Stengel. The government's strongest response—protecting against use of a nonprofit to pass commercial funds into election advertising—answers material conduit financing, not a mere checklist omission. The contrary claim that §204 itself eliminates all constitutional exemptions is rejected by every Associate.

### C. Fallback and the district's deletion

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer sustain the primary definition and its facial treasury operation with the constitutional application protections above. Their disposition does not activate fallback. They would vacate the district's unnecessary fallback declaration and deletion of its final substantive limiter. If their primary disposition becomes the final judgment, no backup rule becomes operative.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas propose a different complete treasury disposition. Their primary §203 ruling finds the broad candidate/time/geography definition insufficient to justify the newly expanded treasury restriction. They therefore must examine the enacted backup for that regulation before granting relief; preserving primary ordinary reporting alone does not avoid this question. They would vacate the lower deletion of the no-plausible-meaning limiter, and sustain backup treasury coverage only under a construction requiring the communication itself to supply an explicit or functionally explicit exhortation to vote for or against the identified candidate. Ordinary context may identify the candidate or explain the message's words, but may not generate an electoral command absent from the message. Any plausible nonelectoral meaning defeats this cumulative coverage. The protected primary applications remain protected when they fail that complete backup test. Independently valid primary §201 financial reporting does not become the backup's timeless disclosure category by virtue of this source-regulation ruling.

That backup analysis is part of the three's preferred remedy, conditional on their primary treasury conclusion becoming the final judgment. It is not an irrelevant hypothetical or a device to evade the statutory activation condition through an application-severance label. If the final judgment holds the primary definition insufficient to support the relevant regulation, §401 cannot defeat the express trigger. If the final judgment sustains the primary scheme, the backup remains inactive. The exact judgment must identify the regulation and applications adjudicated; neither a valid reporting use nor an invalid isolated application supplies a mechanical answer for every statutory use.

Their construction materially differs from their historical position: Kennedy, joined by Scalia, rejected even the complete backup as uncertain and overbroad; Thomas independently demanded the historical express-advocacy boundary. Actual Maine and Bartlett now provide a concrete functional-electoral-message rule and protect genuine issue expression. Their own actual Maine separate position permits functional electoral appeals without a canonical vocabulary while forbidding external context from supplying the missing appeal. That is the narrower construction they would apply to Congress's cumulative words. It does not adopt Maine's majority rule that properly confined context can perform the broader interpretive work that these three resisted.

For completeness, the five's position if a different final judgment activates fallback is to sustain the enacted cumulative definition only where candidate promotion/opposition and the whole message, with properly limited context, admit no plausible meaning other than an exhortation to vote for or against a specific candidate. They rely on the actual Maine majority and Bartlett's definite-coverage and issue-expression protections. The three do not join where context supplies rather than explains electoral direction. This is a real disagreement; neither rationale is silently declared the logical subset controlling the other.

Every proposed backup formulation preserves the complete enacted text, including its cumulative final condition and the express saving of §100.22(b). Backup concerns broadcast, cable and satellite communications; it contains neither the primary 30-/60-day windows nor its geographic threshold. No Justice supports the lower court's enlargement by deleting the final condition. No judgment on backup reporting beyond the actual presented and necessary regulation is inferred from these treasury positions.

## 5. Coordination, independence, party choice and minors

### A. Sections 202, 211 and 214(a), (d)

All eight agree that an actual candidate/party request for a financial resource or genuine spending collaboration can be shown without a signed agreement, a formal contract between the political actors, or proof of a completed bribe. Ordinary affiliation, endorsement, policy discussion, vendor identity or a former employment relationship alone does not establish that spending connection. Clifton's policy-inquiry holding and Colorado I's actual independence rule remain effective. Nothing in §214(c) requires treating every contact as coordination.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the facial sustaining judgment on §202 and §214(a), applying the actual expenditure connection. A third party's purchase responding to a candidate's or party's actual campaign spending request may supply a contribution even without express words urging a vote; the contribution classification is functional, not merely semantic. The objection that all coordinated communications require express advocacy fails under Akins's actual candidate-financing rule, Colorado II's supported coordinated-spending holding and Lamb. This does not decide that every jointly developed message is constitutionally identical or that every regulatory content/conduct category is valid. Policy inquiries and independently edited issue publications remain outside coordination absent the necessary spending relation.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the blanket §202/§214(a) approvals and remand for declarations protecting a party's own joint expression and the independently controlled issue/editorial activity protected by Clifton and Bartlett, while leaving actual candidate/party-controlled financial substitutes and genuine conduits regulable. Kennedy's Colorado I/II position, joined by both, is the narrower-party-expression and chosen-channel ground; Bartlett and Clifton reinforce that mere issue collaboration is insufficient. Their Lamb joins permit real resources and tacit conduits and preclude a formal-agreement prerequisite. They do not invalidate the entire coordination mechanism or impose expenditure scrutiny on all cash and in-kind candidate contributions. As narrower remedial agreement, they join a declaration of the actual-resource rule and the inquiry/identity exclusions, but not a categorical approval of all joint messages.

**Section 202's separate contract-timing component:** All eight accept contribution treatment of an enforceable coordinated purchase commitment insofar as it already supplies an actual requested candidate or party campaign resource. A binding obligation arranged to provide the campaign's advertising can confer the financial benefit before the advertisement airs or the vendor is paid. It is not necessary to wait for a completed broadcast or a completed corrupt bargain. The five sustain the facial contract mechanism on that actual-spending relation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept this real-resource application but retain their remand/declaration protecting the party's own joint expression and the distinct independently edited issue activity protected by Clifton and Bartlett. They do not exempt every outside spender's collaboration simply because the spender calls the financed service its own message.

Kennedy and Scalia historically objected separately to §202's advance-contract language; Thomas's broader coordination objection also reached it. Their actual Lamb joins now affirm the contribution classification of genuine candidate resources and tacit conduits under a distinct contribution standard. An enforceable requested resource, rather than the still-private content of an independent message, answers the specific timing objection at that limited scope. Their §201/§212 advance-disclosure objection remains different: making an unrelated future private message public need not transfer any campaign resource, whereas §202 classifies an actual financially committed substitute. This is a reasoned present join to the financial-resource application, not an inference from accepting ordinary reporting or from Kennedy's historical candidate/party split. Mere discussion, an unaccepted proposal or independently controlled issue inquiry does not satisfy this ground. No general regulatory rule about cancellation, valuation or every contract is adjudicated.

All eight would reject a freestanding facial attack on §211's definition merely because it includes party cooperation/request/suggestion alongside candidate connections. The definition does not by itself make every communication outside it constitutionally unprotected. The stated express-advocacy element and absence-of-actual-coordination conditions remain; the preceding constitutional limits on particular burdens remain independent. The three's broader joint-expression view is not surrendered by accepting the statutory definition.

All eight would affirm rejection of the asserted §214(d) amendment challenge on its actual statutory ground: incorporation of FECA's contribution/expenditure definitions does not newly enact the preexisting direct/indirect-payment language or turn every policy communication into a contribution. An attack on those old words is not converted into a new BCRA amendment challenge. This ground neither validates every old application nor negates the constitutional remedies stated above.

**Alternatives and limits:** The government's strongest case concerns financed campaign work arranged in fact but without a formal agreement; the challengers' strongest case concerns ordinary lobbying or a publisher's editorial collaboration. The five sustain actual-connection applications and the facial statutory mechanism while reserving a concrete constitutional characterization of materially different joint speech. The three require protection of that joint-expression category in the present declaration. No Associate adopts a rule that common vendors, former employees or policy questions automatically establish coordination. The January rule's conjunctive payment/content/conduct structure and its inquiry safe harbor defeat the premise that no clarifying rule exists, without deciding that regulation in this statutory direct appeal.

### B. Section 214(b) repeal and (c) rulemaking command

All eight would affirm the threshold disposition of the regulatory-vacuum claim and the unripe attack on unspecified future applications of the rulemaking command. The promised replacement rules became effective February 3, 2003; the claimed unregulated interval no longer supplies prospective relief against the completed repeal. The command does not itself designate every contact as coordinated, and the actual rules preserve an express policy-inquiry safe harbor. Their validity and concrete application require the appropriate regulatory challenge, not a presumption in this statutory appeal that all future contact investigations will be unconstitutional.

This conclusion does not rest on compulsory use of an advisory opinion, on the absence of criminal sanctions, on the proposition that a losing legal theory defeats injury, or on treating an operative present duty as nonbinding. Maine and Lee forbid those shortcuts. The statutory no-formal-agreement premise is addressed at its actual-resource level in part A; no additional general merits holding about the replacement rule follows from dismissal here. The strongest contrary argument is present chilling from an unavoidable statutory instruction. The operative clarification, separate actual-connection predicates and absence of a specified protected contact forced into coordination by the instruction itself distinguish that allegation from Maine's ceased recurring communications. A later supported present application remains reviewable.

### C. Section 213 choice

All eight would affirm invalidation of §213 as a unit. The provision conditions exercise of the special coordinated-party allowance on forgoing constitutionally protected independent expenditure, and conversely removes that allowance after independent expenditure. It is incorrect to say every ordinary contribution is forbidden: the $5,000 multicandidate route remains. That residual route does not justify the prohibition on independent spending after use of the otherwise lawful allowance or the burden on meaningful chosen-party association.

The government's strongest alternative is that a party's prior coordination makes later independence unreliable and that the allowance is an optional statutory benefit. Colorado I requires actual independence/connection rather than a status presumption; Colorado II does not authorize eliminating independent speech as the price of permitted coordinated support. The record supplies no fitted basis for conclusively converting every later transaction into coordination. Genuine coordination can be regulated on its facts. For Stevens and Ginsburg, their older institutional-channel dissent does not override the current Colorado I holding; their Colorado II and Lamb joins support real preventive controls without this conclusive choice. ### D. Section 318 minors

All eight would affirm invalidation of the complete ban. Mariani's independent true-source rule, ordinary attribution/conduit controls and the requirement of actual voluntary contribution address the identified evasion directly. The ability to volunteer or make independent expenditures does not replace the chosen contribution channel under Day, Russell and Lamb.

The strongest sustaining argument is the difficulty of distinguishing parental money and influence from a child's genuine gift, together with an administrable age line. A complete ban reaching independent decisions and every lawful amount does not fit that risk merely because enforcement of narrower controls takes work. This is a legal fit conclusion, not a finding that no parental circumvention has ever occurred or that all age distinctions are unconstitutional. The common ground fails the governing contribution standard; no Associate needs to adopt Leon's separate theory that every total contribution ban automatically receives expenditure-level strict scrutiny. Tinker supports the existence of minors' rights; it does not replace the contribution inquiry.

The entire new age prohibition falls; severability does not authorize choosing a younger age, an earnings threshold or a parental-consent exception that Congress did not enact. Other valid source, amount, voluntariness and false-name restrictions remain. There is no repayment or retrospective-liability order.

### 7.2. Statutory conditions and actual lower judgment

#### Reporting, identification and broadcast records

**Section 201 ordinary reporting.** The primary category is a broadcast, cable or satellite communication identifying a federal candidate within 60 days of a general, special or runoff election, or 30 days of a primary, preference election, nominating convention or caucus. Nonpresidential coverage additionally requires the relevant-electorate condition: reception by at least 50,000 persons in the specified House district or Senate State. News/commentary/editorial communications have the party/committee/candidate ownership-control qualification; expenditures and independent expenditures, qualifying debates/forums and their solely promotional communications, and lawful regulatory exemptions are distinct exclusions. The statute forbids a regulatory exemption for an otherwise covered section 301(20)(A)(iii) communication. The conditional fallback requires both promotion/support/attack/opposition and no plausible meaning other than an exhortation to vote; the district's deletion of the latter condition is a disputed remedy, not enacted text.

For direct production/airing costs aggregating **more than $10,000** in a calendar year, a statement is due within 24 hours of each disclosure date, including each later additional over-$10,000 aggregate. It identifies the disburser, persons directing or controlling its activity, records custodian and nonindividual principal place of business; each disbursement of **more than $200** and its recipient; and pertinent elections and candidates known or to be identified. Section 304(f)(2)(E) confines donor names and addresses to donors of **$1,000 or more** to a qualifying segregated account, measured from the first day of the preceding calendar year through disclosure. That account must consist solely of direct contributions for electioneering communications by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents; it may also be used for other purposes. Without that account, (F) reaches $1,000-or-more contributors to the disburser over the same period. It is inaccurate to say every entity need disclose only earmarked advertising donors or only donors to a special account. Reporting is additional to other reporting duties; the tax-law definitions remain unaffected.

**Section 304(f)(5).** Executed contracts are deemed disbursements. Section 104.20 starts reporting upon public distribution when the aggregate exceeds the threshold, but its report of obligations can include other contracted communications that have not aired. Thus the existence of that rule does not itself dispose of this challenge. The district sustained ordinary disclosure but severed the contracts clause in full.

**Section 212.** The statutory independent-expenditure report is due within 24 hours for $1,000-or-more aggregates after the twentieth day but more than 24 hours before an election; within 48 hours for $10,000-or-more aggregates up to and including the twentieth day; and again upon each corresponding additional aggregate for the same election. Contracts are included in the statutory wording; receipt is filing for the $1,000 report. The effective section 109.10(c)–(d) rule measures those report deadlines from public distribution/dissemination. The live challenge attacks compelled advance reporting, and the district dismissed it as unripe. That is different from a merits approval of every section 212 duty.

**Section 311.** The challenged change extends source identification to electioneering communications and other covered non-express advocacy. The additional printed-box/type/contrast, candidate approval, image, voice and four-second specifications are enacted context; the district identifies no independent preserved attack on each such detail. Candidate/authorized-speaker identification differs from the other-speaker responsible-for-content statement and connected-organization identification. Neither is section 305's price condition or section 504's public file. The district rejected the extension challenge.

**Section 504.** A licensee must keep a public record of a request to buy broadcast time by or for a legally qualified candidate for public office, or communicating a message relating to a political matter of national importance, including a legally qualified candidate, a federal election, or a national legislative issue of public importance. Accepted **and rejected** requests are covered. The record states acceptance/rejection, charged rate, airing date/time, purchased class, and pertinent candidate/office, election or issue. Candidate requests identify candidate, authorized committee and treasurer; other requests identify purchaser, a contact person's name/address/telephone, and chief executive officers or executive-committee/board members. Information is placed in the file as soon as possible and retained at least two years. The section is not restricted to completed broadcasts, electioneering communications, federal-candidate purchases or a $10,000 threshold. The district invalidated it entirely.

#### Threshold subjects

**Section 305.** The lowest-unit-rate certification is made at purchase. A candidate may refer to another candidate for the same office while keeping eligibility by satisfying the television or radio alternative. Television requires the candidate image and readable identification/approval/authorized-committee-payment statement simultaneously for at least four seconds at the end; radio requires the candidate's personal identifying, office and approval statement. A noncompliant reference loses the rate for that and subsequent covered broadcasts in the applicable 45-/60-day periods. The district dismissed for lack of standing. It described Pence's actual testimony about his advertisements and future campaign practice, Barr's showing, and McConnell's future opponent-critical plans and 2008 election. These reported features support a bounded threshold decision; they do not authorize saying civil price consequences can never injure a speaker.

**Section 307.** Beginning with contributions made January 1, 2003, the individual candidate ceiling rises from $1,000 to $2,000, the national-party ceiling from $20,000 to $25,000, the aggregate becomes a two-calendar-year allowance of $37,500 for candidates plus $57,500 for other contributions with the $37,500 nonnational-party sublimit, and the senatorial committee allowance becomes $35,000. The specified ceilings receive the enacted inflation adjustments, $100 rounding and odd-year/two-year operation where applicable; the text retains different 1974 and 2001 base years. Unchanged nonparty-PAC ceilings do not become amended provisions merely because other ceilings were changed. The lower court dismissed the increased-limit/indexing challenges, without separately resolving Paul's operative candidate-ceiling injury on its actual theory.

**Sections 304/316/319.** These are distinct candidate-response mechanisms, not Daggett's public-grant program. The Senate threshold formula is $150,000 plus four cents per voting-age resident, with the enacted twice/four-times/ten-times bands, threefold or sixfold contribution allowances, aggregate-limit exceptions and the highest band's coordinated-party relaxation. Opposition personal funds compare the opposing and recipient candidates' personal expenditures, with section 316's half-gross-receipts-advantage adjustment and specified June 30/December 31 measurements. The House mechanism uses opposition personal funds over $350,000, tripled individual limits, aggregate exception and coordinated-party relaxation, its own gross-receipts rule, and a 100-percent cap; the Senate acceptance cap is 110 percent. Receipt of notification precedes increased-limit acceptance, attributable permission ceases when the relevant opponent withdraws, and unspent excess contributions must be returned within 50 days. The distinct declarations, initial/additional notices, recipient rules and excess reports remain part of the statute. Section 304 also contains the personal-loan repayment limitation above $250,000 from post-election contributions. The presented self-financing challenge and lower threshold disposition do not establish a claimant subject to that separate loan limit. No merits ruling on it is manufactured. The district dismissed the actual self-financing claims for lack of standing.

#### D. Section 212: the statute's advance obligation is not erased by the rule

The operative §109.10 regulation uses public distribution/dissemination as its reporting trigger. That fact is verified, and it distinguishes currently administered reporting from §201's disclosure of separate outstanding contracts after another advertisement airs. But the statute itself expressly addresses contracts. The historical joint opinion doubted whether an agency's implementation could remove a facial challenge to the broader statutory speech burden, then declined separate consideration because its §201 decision made the parallel issue essentially moot. It did not adopt an eight-Justice nonripeness holding. Kennedy, joined by Scalia, expressly would reach and invalidate advance reporting; Thomas's ZX1 note 10 also rejects §212, on broader historical disclosure grounds.

The freeze's unanimous ripeness disposition is therefore corrected. The actual Lee and Maine rules recognize present civil duties and recurring protected conduct; National Park Hospitality's nonbinding policy imposed no new legal obligation and does not settle whether an agency regulation lawfully eliminates this statutory command. The historical statute and regulation were already the same, so discovery of the regulation is not a changed factual premise. Barnhart's actual statutory-assignment rule likewise supplies no blanket deference permitting an agency to erase a congressional duty. The required inquiry concerns the actual statute and preserved advance-reporting claim, not speculation about a future regulatory rewrite.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would **affirm rejection of the presented challenge without adopting a separate ripeness holding**. Their §201 holding that an actual binding financial obligation can be disclosed before payment and despite a separate future airing supplies the answer to this parallel facial advance-contract objection; no additional injunction is warranted. They do not use 'essentially moot' as a new Article III rule or adjudicate every independent-expenditure reporting provision. Their ordinary source and financial-commitment grounds, with supported reprisal protection and actual statutory limits, supply their result. Implementation and materially distinct application challenges remain open.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would **reach the advance-reporting claim, reverse its threshold rejection and protect against compelled disclosure of an independent communication before dissemination**, while preserving ordinary reporting after the corresponding speech becomes public and reporting of an unpaid obligation for an already disseminated communication. Section 401 permits this declaration/application restraint rather than deletion of the entire reporting system. Kennedy and Scalia retain their specific historical timing objection; Thomas's actual Day reporting join narrows his historical demand to invalidate §212 wholesale, but does not defeat his remaining preparatory-anonymity objection.

### 7.4. Ordinary section 201 reporting and section 311 extension

### B. Directly regulated parties may raise the presented allocation objection

**Selected commitment of each Associate:** Reach the affirmative-power objection and the associated reserved-power objection insofar as they challenge the federal restrictions imposed on these parties' own fundraising and spending. Their regulated conduct and exposure supply personal injury, enforcement supplies causation, and relief from their own restriction supplies redressability. State representation is unnecessary to that claim. Do not adopt Kollar-Kotelly's prudential bar or recast it as an Article III dismissal. No decision is required on a private claimant seeking relief solely for a State's independent institutional injury without injury to that claimant.

Lujan requires actual personal injury, not a particular constitutional label; the supplied direct-regulation posture supplies it here. Lopez adjudicated the regulated person's source objection. Printz's unanimous jurisdictional part likewise distinguished the officers' own commands from absent dealers' different claims. New York's actual division-of-authority rule protects an allocation that state consent cannot cure; the present challengers invoke that allocation to resist their own legally binding restrictions. The Court's actual Guillen reserved private Tenth Amendment standing and merits; it establishes neither a categorical bar nor a categorical private cause of action. Tennessee Electric concerned competition with nonexclusive franchises, not a prohibition directly binding the challenger's conduct. No Associate joins a conclusion that a federalism label alone establishes injury, or that an adverse merits conclusion defeats standing retroactively.

### C. Affirmative authority, by statutory component

### D. Reserved state sovereignty and the Tenth Amendment

**Selected commitment of each Associate:** Reject the distinct claim that the otherwise authorized parts of Title I are invalid merely because they restrict private financial conduct that States allow in state campaigns. ### C. Title I, by provision and Justice

**Section 323(a):** The five sustaining the national-party receipt restriction retain the historical result, but their actual Day, Mariani, Lamb and Beaumont joins require a supported candidate-financing or circumvention mechanism and meaningful chosen-channel analysis. Stevens and Ginsburg's actual Colorado I institutional-channel view gives them a distinct reason to credit the party/officeholder record; O'Connor and Souter's narrower Colorado I positions require the receipt-versus-independent-message distinction; Breyer's actual Colorado II and Beaumont reasoning supplies the cumulative resource, source and functioning voluntary-channel inquiry. None relies on a historical rule that a restriction survives unless it makes advocacy virtually impossible. Scalia and Thomas historically sought to overrule the contribution/expenditure distinction; actual Stengel, Day and Lamb joins reject that universal premise. Kennedy's actual Colorado/Lamb distinction likewise governs his objection. All three still find the national-party sweep insufficiently confined to genuine resources and conduits, while accepting regulation of the latter. This is the same principal adverse result on a narrower actual-law basis, not a historical all-contributions invalidity rule.

**Section 323(b)(i), (ii), Levin and (iii):** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the historical sustaining result for the defined registration window, shared-ballot voter identification/GOTV/generic work, and communications promoting, supporting, attacking or opposing a federal candidate for office. Their actual Colorado II/Lamb joins support the shared federal operation; Maine/Bartlett prevent equating every issue reference with the candidate-for-office category. The Levin source, cap, independent-fundraising and allocation conditions remain the enacted ones in section 2; they do not become a universal ceiling on every local activity. The additional hard-money transfer restraint within the Levin mechanism receives the same distinct fit inquiry: it maintains the specified locally raised mixed-financing option rather than a general ban on otherwise lawful federal-fund transfers. The five accept that confined mechanism; the three's broader objection includes its exclusion of chosen lawful support. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain protection for own expression and actual issue/editorial independence, while their Lamb joins prevent an automatic exemption for genuine federal resources or tacit conduits. No formal agreement is demanded.

**Section 323(b)(iv):** Historical ZO sustained the entire salary rule using workload-splitting circumvention and the absence of a developed contrary reason. The actual rule taxes the whole salary once more than 25 percent of compensated monthly work is connected with federal election activity; it is not a proportional federal-time allocation. O'Connor's Stengel aggregate-limit fit remand and Souter's join there, together with both Justices' full Lamb government-burden join, supply a concrete changed premise: a plausible anti-splitting objective does not automatically justify the entire remainder of compensation. They require a separate actual-operation showing. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer accept the supported payroll channel and administrable trigger despite that objection; they do not shift the burden to proving inaudibility. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas prefer invalidation under their chosen-channel and expression/resource grounds but expressly join the narrower fit remand and its requirement of relief for unjustified burdens. This is direct agreement on the remand, not an arithmetic combination of incompatible theories.

**Section 323(d):** Historical ZO sustained the solicitation ban and construed donations narrowly to avoid the federal-money problem. The present five instead protect both solicitation and donation/direction of federally compliant funds through §401 application severance. Actual Day's meaningful chosen association and Mariani's refusal to treat a different fund pool as an automatic substitute make the compliant solicitation application a distinct constitutional burden; no source-evasion justification follows merely from the recipient's tax status when the proposed financial transaction is itself federally compliant. O'Connor's direct-source and practical-fit writings supply that limit; Souter's actual-finance/issue-association distinction and Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer's Day/Lamb joins require it as well. The nonfederal conduit restriction remains justified for each of the five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain the broader objection to the recipient-category ban, and independently agree with the narrower compliant-funds protection. The remedy changes as well as the scope: it does not pretend that enacted words already exclude every compliant solicitation, and it does not transfer a charitable-solicitation exception from §323(e).

**Section 323(e):** Kennedy historically sustained this provision, while Scalia expressly refused his join and Thomas's strict contribution framework rejected it. The current all-eight result is grounded in the actual official's or candidate's request and its donor-obligation/circumvention relation, not simply party identity. Scalia's and Thomas's full Lamb joins accept tacit conduits and financial resources without formal earmarking or proof of a completed bribe; their actual Stengel/Day contribution joins displace the historical insistence that every contribution restriction receives the same invalidating expenditure analysis. That concrete premise matters where the federal officeholder personally recruits the funds, including nominally nonfederal transactions. The actual branch limits and exceptions — the wholly state/local candidacy exception, attendance/speaking/fundraising events as enacted, unrestricted solicitation for qualifying organizations not principally engaged in the defined federal-election activities, and the separate capped individual solicitation rule for covered federal-election work — are indispensable to their fit judgment. They do not accept unlimited official control of every charitable activity. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their historical result on their actual institutional-resource and fit grounds, and Kennedy retains it on his actual resource/request distinction. No Justice adopts the historical strict-scrutiny objection merely through a historical join.

**Section 323(f), alternative equality and clarity:** The same five retain the PASO federal-candidate result; the three retain protection for the state/local speaker's own expression lacking a resource/conduit connection. A state candidate may discuss federal policy without automatically supporting a federal candidate for office. The five accept the actual party/officeholder distinctions for the reasons in section 8E; the three obtain speech relief and need no broader equality remedy. All eight preserve Valeria's actual racial classification/proxy/selective-barrier test: disparate demographic impact alone does not establish the Thompson theory, but racial hostility is not demanded where a structural racial mechanism is actually shown. Definite calendars, candidates and ascertainable conduct answer vagueness within their terms; a clear restriction can still be too broad. Section 323(c) supplies statutory context and receives no freestanding merits judgment absent a presented claim.

### D. Title II, source restrictions, disclosure and timing

**Primary §201 definition and §203:** The five retain the historical facial result, but actual Bartlett expressly protects genuine issue discussion and actual Day protects functional voluntary associations. ZO note 88 itself acknowledges that genuine-issue advertisements may lack the interests supporting regulation of campaign speech, so not every application reservation is a historical departure. The current handoff makes the actual Bartlett protection enforceable and refuses an automatic equation of candidate reference with suppressible electoral advocacy. Actual Beaumont adjudicated direct contributions and relied on cumulative NCRL source/control/voluntary-fund facts; it cannot alone establish the independent-expenditure result. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer combine the surviving Austin setting, the actual record, the precise statutory definition and these application protections. Each's actual Maine/Bartlett/Day joins fixes the limit. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas still oppose expanded nonexpress treasury restrictions, but their actual Day, Mariani and Beaumont positions preserve Austin's surviving context rather than overruling it wholesale as their historical writings proposed. The new law's expansion can be resolved at the narrower actual issue-expression/source boundary. No blanket constitutional immunity follows from incorporation or nonprofit tax status.

**Section 204:** Historical ZO protects the MCFL constitutional class despite the statutory withdrawal. Current Day demands actual nonprofit characteristics rather than a literal three-item label or organizational paperwork: incidental activity or the absence of a formal no-corporate-contribution policy is not alone disqualifying. All eight joined that actual protection. The five sustain withdrawal of the broader statutory nonprofit exception outside the protected functional class; the three additionally retain their §203 objection. This preserves constitutional protection without pretending that §204 left the statutory §203 exemption untouched. No named litigant is assigned a qualifying status without its actual evidence.

**Backup:** Historical Kennedy, joined by Scalia, addressed and rejected backup despite accepting ordinary primary reporting; Thomas independently rejected its reach. Section 4C corrects the freeze's attempted nonactivation on that basis. Actual Maine's authoritative cumulative construction and the three's own functional-electoral-message position are the specific new legal materials that make a complete, narrow backup construction available. Their refusal to let outside context supply the electoral command remains a join barrier. The five's different contextual position rests on their actual Maine joins. A lower-court deletion of the last condition cannot survive either path. This is a change in proposed reasoning and remedy for the three, fully contingent on the final primary-regulation judgment, not adoption of a later historical campaign-law test.

**Ordinary §201:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the historical reporting result on the actual finite transaction, donor thresholds, period and optional qualifying account. Scalia and Kennedy historically accepted ordinary disclosure already; Kennedy's historical ZX2 accepted the informational ground alone, and his present join likewise does not adopt a corruption rationale premised merely on his independently protected speech. Actual Stengel and their Colorado/Lamb disclosure alternatives independently support that limited result. Thomas's historical ZX1 II-C was unjoined and would reject ordinary political financial disclosure. His actual Day reporting join, actual Mariani true-source join and Stengel's reservation of different organizational-finance questions are the concrete changed premises supporting his present limited external-report join. Those premises do not imply that every political donor or member may be exposed, that the message is a contribution, or that all anonymity is immaterial. The actual statutory account alternative and Buckley/Brown supported-reprisal rule remain essential.

**Section 201(f)(5):** The five retain the historical binding-obligation result. They do not transform ZO note 84's assessment of a thin contract-performance record into a new finding that strategic harm can never occur. Scalia and Kennedy retain the specific historical prepublication objection while narrowing relief to the separate unbroadcast communication; reporting an unpaid debt for an already aired advertisement is not the harm they identified. Thomas's actual ordinary-report acceptance similarly narrows his historical entire-disclosure objection to this preparatory application. Section 401 was historically available; the change is a more precisely justified remedy consistent with their actual ordinary-disclosure commitments, not discovery of new statutory text. Current Ho's advance-notice rule does not supply a generic government interest in all anticipatory speech reports.

**Sections 202, 211 and 214:** The five retain facial validity at the real spending-connection scope. The three's present resource-versus-party-message distinction is narrower and more precise than their historical positions: Kennedy historically sustained candidate but not party coordination; Scalia declined the candidate-coordination sustaining part and joined Thomas's broader objection. Actual Colorado I/II and full Lamb joins now accept genuine candidate-controlled resources and tacit conduits under a contribution standard, while preserving a party's own joint expression; actual Clifton/Bartlett independently protects policy inquiry and edited issue expression. These premises justify the limited resource applications and the remand/declaration, including the expressly tested contract commitment. They do not immunize any outside campaign service labeled collaboration. Section 211 is independently live even though the historical writings give no separable vote on it; the current rejection rests on its actual definition and the distinction between statutory independence and constitutional protection. Section 214(d) likewise rests on the unchanged old words versus the actual amendment, not a historical vote inferred from silence. Section 214(b)'s completed regulatory-vacuum theory and §214(c)'s unripe unspecified implementation remain threshold matters; no replacement rule receives an advisory merits approval or mandatory advisory-opinion exhaustion.

**Section 212:** The five's historical discussion did not decide ripeness; Scalia/Kennedy rejected that threshold, and Thomas's historical note 10 would reach a broader invalidation. The revised five/three treatment in section 7.3D tracks those evidentiary positions under actual law. Actual Lee and Maine affirm review of civil duties; National Park imposed no new duty. These are concrete reasons to correct the original threshold unanimity, not to presume that every agency regulation is ineffective. Thomas's narrower relief rests on the same actual ordinary-report departure described for §201. The effective public-distribution rule remains in place; no present contrary enforcement is invented.

**Section 213:** All eight retain invalidation of the integrated reciprocal choice, including its aggregation and assignment/transfer implementation. For the five, the special coordinated allowance cannot be conditioned on surrender of protected independent advocacy merely because the ordinary $5,000 route remains. Their actual Colorado I and Colorado II joins distinguish the two functions. For Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, actual own-party-expression protection supplies the additional ground that both expressive choices can be protected, while genuine cash/resources remain regulable; their historical theory did not retain that same actual-resource limit. They therefore agree on the whole choice unit's invalidation without adopting a general rule against all contribution limits. Whether the aggregation is nationwide or only within the narrower state grouping need not be decided: even the narrower reading lets one committee bind other committees. Neither interpretation is turned into a new holding.

### E. Titles III–V, Press and federal power

**Section 305 and §307:** The restored distinctions in sections 7.3A–B are compelled by the full historical writings and the absence of a supported changed premise. The three §305 merits votes include the acknowledged causation difficulty, rather than pretending that their dissent found a guaranteed higher rate. Paul's evidence remains genuine injury evidence; the decisive question is the remedy available through §403. Actual Mariani provides a proper statutory channel rather than an instruction to ignore or automatically certify the claim. Adams's separate generally increased-permission theory is dismissed at its actual nexus/traceability scope; competitor standing is not universally barred. The self-financing claims under §§304, 316 and 319 remain distinct claimant-specific threshold dispositions. No merits view about responsive public grants in Day/Daggett creates a candidate presently subject to this different formula.

**Section 311:** Seven retain the historical extension result on actual Stengel and accurate source-information grounds. Thomas historically did not join ZO1 here and treated the disclaimer as analogous to McIntyre despite medium. His actual Day reporting join changes external financial reporting, while his actual Stengel handbill objection preserves a separate on-message concern. The corrected partial relief therefore has a specific current-law boundary: genuine-issue expression receives protection, but the actual accepted electoral-finance identification settings are not invalidated wholesale. The extra font/image/time details are assumed rather than independently adjudicated, consistently with the actual preserved challenge.

**Section 318:** All eight retain invalidation of the complete ban on contributions and donations by persons seventeen or younger. The five plus Scalia, Kennedy and Souter can adopt the actual contribution-fit grounds: the scant supported evasion showing, existing true-source prohibition and sweeping exclusion of minors' own resources. Thomas historically concurred in result while rejecting the contribution standard; his actual Day, Stengel and Lamb joins now accept that standard and actual resource/conduit distinction. Those concrete legal commitments permit his present join to the common fit rationale without pretending he joined historical ZO1's reasoning. The record's alternative safeguards are possible examples, not newly adjudicated replacement rules. Complete speech relief makes the alternative federalism/equality questions unnecessary.

**Section 504:** The five retain the historical result for the particular three-category licensee functions, including actual requests and rejected requests; their original current positions have independent Stengel/Akins/Clifton support. Scalia and Kennedy historically accepted ordinary reporting while objecting to this additional scheme. No material actual change answers that distinct objection, so their broader invalidation is restored, with willingness to accept less relief separately recorded. Thomas's historical blanket disclosure premise is displaced by actual Day/Mariani, permitting candidate-request and aired federal-election financial records, but his retained McIntyre/Stengel issue anonymity prevents the freeze's extension to all aired national issues. The different new result follows the actual changed electoral-finance premise only as far as it reaches. No lower-level statutory severance is inferred to rewrite the request trigger, and no prior FCC regulation is adjudicated because it was reproduced in an appendix.

**Independent Press/media theory:** All eight reject the proposed universal immunity for money used in candidate publishing or political advocacy and the automatic invalidation of the news/media exception, but only within the live BCRA components. Actual Stengel direct-gift joins, Akins's actual-finance rule, Day's functional protection and Mariani's true-source rule distinguish a campaign's donated resources from a publisher's own independent message. Scalia and Thomas's historical concern about incumbent self-protection remains a serious objection, not proof that every candidate-dollar restriction violates the Press Clause. Their actual contribution joins displace the historical all-finance-as-equivalent premise; Kennedy's actual financial-resource versus expression distinction supplies his own limit. Noninstitutional speakers retain full expressive protection and actual news functions matter; the holding supplies neither institutional press supremacy nor a blanket valid-as-applied judgment for every media exception. Paul's §307 dismissal is not a merits rejection of that separate claim.

**Affirmative federal power and direct-party standing:** The historical ZO's brief Tenth Amendment discussion cannot be treated as an eight-Justice historical source holding, and the historical dissenters' speech relief supplies no inferred vote on every alternative. The present individual table in section 8F rests instead on each Justice's actual Voting Rights Coalition, Lopez, Printz, Condon, Thornton/Cook, Guillen, Holston and Ho positions. All eight permit parties directly regulated in their own finances to raise the allocation objection; Guillen reserved a different private-standing question, while Tennessee Electric involved competitors asserting another sovereign's rights without the same direct command. There is no general adjudication of all private Tenth Amendment standing. Article I §4 supplies congressional-election authority; the established presidential-election protection recognized in Burroughs, with necessary-and-proper implementation, supplies the different presidential source. The Court does not derive election power from the movement of dollars in commerce. Each Title I channel requires the actual federal financing, official solicitation or shared-election mechanism stated in section 8C, including payroll and nonprofit intermediary applications, rather than mere eventual political effects. This is a supported source analysis separate from speech fit.

The actual Printz divergence matters specifically: its structural holding and functional dissents concern duties imposed on state executive officers, while these commands regulate private parties and officeholders' fundraising. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their actual structural Printz positions but no compelled state administration is established here; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's actual Printz dissents likewise require an affirmative source and do not rest on convenience alone. No federal qualifications rule, election calendar or state administrative machinery is imposed by these financial provisions. Federal permission to engage in an activity does not categorically immunize it from an otherwise lawful state restriction. The source disposition rejects a wholesale alternative injunction but cannot cure an independently unjustified speech burden. The claimed exclusive-power interrogatory concession was not verified and is not used. Section 318 receives no unnecessary separate federalism judgment after complete relief.

**Section 403(b):** The historical unanimous part and actual litigation posture support declining to determine independent intervenor standing because the FEC is properly before the Court advancing the same position. This narrow ground supplies the entire resolution. It neither eliminates the ordinary standing inquiry for an intervenor seeking different relief nor adjudicates a nonexistent procedural obstacle.

## Retained rules, application and remedy

All eight Associates retain P1–P3 of `freeze/OT_2003CHUNK2_PRINGLE_COMMITMENTS.md`, with the Houghton distinction above express in the explanation.

**P1 — particularized probable cause.** Officers may arrest a passenger when contemporaneously known facts, considered together, establish a fair probability that he knowingly exercises sole or joint dominion or control over the contraband. A reasonably supported common-possession inference can particularize that probability to more than one occupant; exclusive ownership need not be proved. Mere presence, a general passenger presumption or silence alone cannot establish it.

**P2 — supported inference here.** Five separately packaged cocaine baggies accessible within the common passenger compartment, rolled cash in the glove compartment directly before the front passenger, and the three occupants' shared early-morning travel in the small private automobile support a practical inference of shared knowing possession including Pringle. The drugs' location behind the upright rear armrest means they were concealed, not observed by Pringle. That matters, but it does not defeat the inference from the whole combination. Cash alone and the late hour alone would establish neither offense nor participation. No finding that every private-car companion is involved in the driver's crime is adopted.

The absence of reliable ownership information leaves the supported joint-possession inference unrebutted by a specific identification of another exclusive participant; it is not an independent badge of guilt or a duty to confess. Di Re had affirmative identification information absent here. Ybarra had incidental presence among public-place customers, rather than the present shared transportation of packaged contraband and associated cash. An innocent passenger remains possible, but probable cause does not require eliminating that possibility or obtaining direct observation of actual knowledge. Nor is probability divided into an arithmetic competition among three possible sole owners when the investigated offense permits joint control.

The lower majority and concurrence are not treated as demanding proof beyond reasonable doubt. Their substantive objection is that the concealment and lack of a direct personal act leave the inference too weak. The combination supplies the missing link here, while the preserved individuality requirement prevents using the conclusion for any automobile in which hidden drugs happen to be found. Pringle's later confession, account of the trip and account of the others' knowledge supply no antecedent justification.

**P3 — disposition.** Reverse the Maryland Court of Appeals' arrest-based suppression judgment and remand. This lawful arrest supplies no ground to exclude the ensuing confession as fruit of an unlawful seizure. Any genuinely preserved independent admissibility issue remains in its own posture; none is invented. The disputed promise account is not an established coercion finding. No attenuation, independent-source or inevitable-discovery ground is manufactured, and Miranda warnings do not perform the work of curing an illegal arrest. ## Merits: six full rule joins and two independent grounds

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain P2–P3 in full.** Before a court makes a pro se filing bearing another label count as the prisoner's first §2255 motion for later successive-motion restrictions, it must give notice of the intended conversion, warn about the restrictions on subsequent motions, and provide an opportunity to withdraw or amend to include the §2255 claims the prisoner wishes to raise. This is an exercise of federal judicial supervision of courts' own pleading administration in the statutory setting, not a new exception allowing a concededly successive motion through AEDPA's gates.

The rule operates here despite the earlier conversion's 1994 date: it governs use of that unwarned conversion as the predicate for barring the 1997 motion, and the supervisory rule applies at least to the case in which it is announced. It neither reopens the old Rule 33 denial nor declares all pre-enactment conversions exempt from statutory consequences. Haines supports liberal reading, not secret exhaustion of a statutory first opportunity. Lonchar and Stewart support attention to the actual dismissal and classification predicates without already having established these exact warnings.

Failure to appeal the label does not cure the omitted warning. Notice serves informed amendment or withdrawal and informed challenge to conversion, including appeal. The absence of an appeal therefore cannot establish the informed choice that the missing warning was designed to permit. Even assuming law of the case applies, it describes a judicial practice rather than an absolute restriction on power; it does not bar correction of this later counting consequence.
