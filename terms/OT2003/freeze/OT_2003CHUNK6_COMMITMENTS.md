## Question, disposition and sufficient rule

Each Associate would reverse the Seventh Circuit's categorical rejection of the federal period and remand. Except where law otherwise provides, section 1658's four-year period governs a claim made possible by a congressional enactment after December 1, 1990, including an amendment of an older statute. The date of the code section's original enactment does not exclude a right newly created by a later Act. The postformation hostile-environment, termination and transfer claims described in the packet fall within the amendment-enabled category to the extent they depend on section 1981(b)'s expanded contractual coverage. The lower courts must apply that period to the identified claims and determine accrual, timeliness and the remaining merits under the governing law.

Rivers establishes that section 101 created new substantive coverage prospectively; it does not itself decide section 1658. Patterson's earlier-text boundary identifies whether the asserted claim depended on the expansion. Goodman supplies borrowing where no applicable federal period displaces it. A claim already available under the earlier text does not become a new-enactment claim merely because the complaint cites amended section 1981. Different legally independent claims in one action can therefore require different limitation inquiries.

## Enhanced comparison

The live alternatives are the claim-enabling-enactment reading and the original-statute reading adopted below. The latter's strongest argument is continuity: the suit still enforces section 1981, and replacing established borrowing whenever Congress alters a statute risks uncertain boundaries. The former's strongest objection is that an amendment does not automatically create a new cause of action; the claim-specific dependency requirement answers that objection. An amendment is itself enacted by Congress, and treating its new liability as an Act for temporal purposes but not as an Act under section 1658 would make limitation coverage turn on codification technique. The claim-enabling reading gives effect both to the new enactment and to the continued availability of old claims. No Justice's commitment rests on treating every amendment as sufficient or on a general exception to statutory temporal limits.

## Clean validation and effect of the additional law

The supplement supplies earlier authority and complete canonical sentencing propositions, not a new event or factual premise. Thompson v. Louisville, 362 U.S. 199, 199, 204–206, invalidates criminal punishment resting on an evidentiary record entirely devoid of support for the charged offense. Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307, 313–324, distinguishes that anti-arbitrariness protection from its broader reasonable-doubt sufficiency rule. The Thompson rule is not merely another name for Jackson review: a record may contain some relevant evidence yet fail Jackson's rational-factfinder inquiry. Neither opinion directly adjudicated this habitual-offender chronology.

Stevens's Jackson concurrence, 443 U.S. at 326–339, including its notes, opposes the expanded federal sufficiency inquiry while expressly preserving the no-evidence cases and habeas protection against extreme failures of the state process. It supplies a concrete earlier ground for considering this case without inventing a general constitutional rule about all sentencing findings. His later published Sawyer, Schlup and Monge positions remain pertinent to the separate gateway and the distinction between statutory eligibility and discretionary punishment.

The added authority materially changes the scope of the available analysis: the original handoff recognized an unresolved Winship/Jackson classification problem but omitted the independently arguable Thompson route. ## Separate constitutional and collateral inquiries

**Earlier no-evidence protection.** On the supplied record, the first prior conviction became final three days after the second prior offense. Texas accepts that the statutory sequence necessary for habitual-offender eligibility was absent. The direct-relief position applies the earlier prohibition on wholly arbitrary criminal deprivation to custody that depends on this affirmatively disproved, indispensable factual predicate. Its federal ground is the absence of any evidentiary basis for the status authorizing the additional punishment, not the proposition that every departure from state sentencing law is federal error. It requires no reassessment of witness credibility or balancing of disputed proof.

**Jackson and the later recidivist rule.** Jackson's full reasonable-doubt sufficiency inquiry requires identifying the facts to which that constitutional standard applies. The canonical Almendarez-Torres decision is a statutory aggravated-reentry-element holding; it supplies no general constitutional recidivism rule. Gatewood, effective June 30, 2003, supplies a constitutional rule for necessary maximum- or minimum-authorizing recidivist facts, including chronology, and applicable federal charging protection. Its direct-review transition under Griffith and independent collateral requirements remain explicit. It is present law, but cannot silently become clearly established law at an earlier state merits adjudication or automatically govern a final conviction on collateral review. Monge's surviving bounded double-jeopardy holding does not answer independent due-process, jury or charging claims.

**Claim-specific statutory review.** The August 2000 federal application invokes amended section 2254. The assistance claim was rejected on its merits in state court and therefore requires the applicable section 2254(d) inquiry, using the actual relevant state merits date, followed by actual Strickland performance and prejudice before relief. The sufficiency claim was rejected on a procedural ground. The supplied record does not establish a state merits adjudication of that claim, and no section 2254(d) merits-deference hurdle is added to it by inference from the disposition of assistance. An innocence gateway can excuse its default but neither supplies its federal merits nor erases any independently applicable collateral condition.

The precise state merits date and criminal-finality date remain unverified. They cannot be selected to make Gatewood govern. Thompson and Jackson predate the identified 1997 prosecution, so their earlier existence requires no invented date. The dispute about applying Thompson's protection to this indispensable sentencing predicate must still be decided on its legal substance. The direct-relief Justices regard that confined application as enforcement of the earlier anti-arbitrariness rule, not retroactive use of Gatewood's later jury-and-proof rule. The assistance-first Justices do not resolve that extension or a hypothetical Teague defense now. No party waiver, new-rule exception or previously unrecorded collateral bar is invented.

Recognize the confined gateway and affirm sentence relief under the earlier no-evidence rule.** Clear and convincing, uncontested chronology shows that no reasonable factfinder could find the indispensable habitual-offender sequence. ## Enhanced comparison

Ordinary relation-back principles inform this construction without directly transforming the application into a civil complaint governed by Rule 15.

The strongest objection is that Congress placed the application contents beside an express thirty-day deadline, and cure can appear to make a mandatory condition optional. The six allowing cure answer that the allegation remains obligatory and is actually supplied; the timeliness and claim identity already exist, and the enacted text does not expressly prohibit completion by relation back. This record does not ask for an equitable extension of the filing period.

**Complete-application construction.** The linked directions require the statutory contents in the thirty-day application. The strongest objection is the disproportionate forfeiture of an otherwise complete timely request for an allegation adding no new facts or fee demand. Scalia and Thomas answer that Becker had an express rule authorizing cure and Edelman had an authorized regulation operating within its statute; neither supplies a freestanding power to disregard a mandatory fee-application condition. ## Common disposition and operative ground

All eight Associates would dismiss this writ for want of jurisdiction under section 1257(a). The state judgment is not final: the Court of Appeal must still decide whether independent evidentiary and prosecutorial-misconduct claims warrant reversal. A settled federal issue within that continuing appeal does not itself establish a final judgment. The state remand continues in its existing posture, and an appropriate later adverse final judgment can permit renewed federal review under the ordinary jurisdictional rules then applicable.

This is the expressly submitted finality question, not a missing-grant or docket-validation objection. The parties' original agreement cannot create statutory jurisdiction. The later-supplied full intermediate opinion reveals the substantive remand work; the missing appendix material is not treated as litigation default or as a reason to punish the petitioner. No Associate reaches or endorses the California Batson standard through this disposition.

## Practical-finality comparison

Florida v. Thomas supplies the closest controlling application and preserves all four Cox routes. They are separately tested here:

1. Proceedings are not practically concluded. The independent reversal claims require actual appellate adjudication rather than a ministerial order.
2. The federal issue need not survive every outcome. A reversal on another ground can eliminate the need to review this conviction's jury-selection ruling.
3. Effective later review is not unavailable whatever the outcome. If the conviction survives the remaining state proceedings, the rejected federal claim may be presented from the completed adverse judgment. This is a practically effective route, not merely a theoretical appeal identified without regard to its consequences.
4. Reversal of the Batson ruling would produce the appropriate conviction relief and further lawful criminal proceedings rather than protect against the litigation itself; delay is not shown to destroy a distinct federal interest beyond the ordinary importance of the constitutional claim. The predicates for this exceptional route are not established.

The strongest alternative is Ritchie's practical concern, emphasized in Stevens's Jefferson dissent: the state supreme court has settled the federal issue and additional state presentation can seem pointless. Here the remaining state claims can themselves undo the conviction, and later review can effectively vindicate the jury-selection right if they fail. Those substantive purposes distinguish pointless repetition of an already completed controversy. Florida v. Thomas, including Stevens's full join, confirms that a settled constitutional ruling plus continuing substantive state work and effective later review does not suffice. No supported exceptional route survives on the supplied record.

## Disposition and sufficient propositions

All eight Associates would affirm the Federal Circuit on the adverse-inference component. Section 1677e(b) permits an adverse inference when Commerce supports a finding that the interested party failed to cooperate by not acting to the best of its ability to comply with an information request. ## Statutory qualifications preserved

These commitments adopt no absolute rule that every failure to answer authorizes an adverse inference. Section 1677e(a) requires otherwise available facts when necessary information is unavailable or an enumerated withholding, deadline/form failure, significant impediment or unverifiability condition exists. That direction remains subject to section 1677m(d); the deadline/form branch separately preserves sections 1677m(c)(1) and (e).

Section 1677e(b)'s separate supported finding permits an adverse inference; it does not compel one. The inference selects among otherwise available facts. Section 1677e(c) requires corroboration of secondary information, to the extent practicable, from reasonably available independent sources.

Section 1677m(c)(1) requires consideration of a party's ability when it promptly explains its inability to comply in the requested form and manner and suggests alternatives; the agency may modify requirements as needed to avoid an unreasonable burden. Subsection (c)(2) separately requires attention to difficulties, particularly those of small companies, and practicable assistance. Subsection (d) requires prompt identification of a deficient response and a practicable opportunity to remedy or explain it within investigation limits; an unsatisfactory or late further response may be disregarded subject to subsection (e). Subsection (e)'s five conditions for required consideration are cumulative: submission by the established deadline, verifiability, sufficient completeness to be reliable, demonstrated best ability in providing information and meeting its requirements, and use without undue difficulties. Subsection (f)'s practicable written explanation for refusal remains separate.

The repeated requests, identified deadlines, admitted failure to inquire at the facilities and Commerce's actual explanation support this adverse-inference application under those provisions. ## Competing paths: constitutional standard

Bell's protection against punishment governs pretrial detainees. A deliberately maintained institutional condition that lacks a reasonable nonpunitive justification, or is excessive in relation to that justification, cannot be sustained merely because each current official denies personal appreciation of the hazard. For the continuing systemic failures here, serious present or imminent injury, sustained notice, actual opportunity to respond and prolonged inadequate correction support prospective protection. Daniels excludes ordinary negligence. Lewis connects culpability to custodial duties and an actual opportunity for deliberation. Farmer's convicted-prisoner actual-awareness rule, including its prospective notice and reasonable-response qualifications, cannot simply be transplanted as an individual-officer prerequisite for every institutional pretrial claim.

The contrary path applies Farmer's actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard to environmental omissions even in pretrial custody. Its strongest objection is that harmful conditions alone cannot establish unconstitutional punishment, and the lower opinion's statement that imminent substantial harm alone is enough risks abolishing culpability. The bounded affirmative formulation above rejects that negligence-only or strict-liability reading. The present record also establishes notice and persistence sufficient for prospective relief on the narrower knowledge ground; effectiveness alone is not the universal measure of reasonable response, but years of known serious deficiencies and continued ineffective measures are the supported application here.

## Competing paths: OCC and transition

Section 3626(g)(8) covers an appointment under Rule 53 or inherent power to exercise a master's powers, regardless of title. Rule 53 now permits specified pretrial and posttrial work and expressly includes investigation or enforcement duties; its default review of objections to findings is de novo. The amended rule begins with the qualification that another statute may provide otherwise and applies to pending proceedings only insofar as just and practicable.

The affirming classification nevertheless distinguishes the actual informal compliance assistant from an appointed surrogate exercising judicial decisional or delegated enforcement authority. Its reports inform the judge and the parties without deciding disputes or undertaking judicial enforcement. The possibility that a master could be assigned some information-gathering tasks does not make every court assistant a master. Ex parte Peterson recognizes assistance of more than one kind; the statute's functional qualification still has work to do. This ground concerns the actual powers, not the office's name, absence of binding finality alone, or a categorical exemption for all monitors.
