# Lane — scoped circulation reconciliation

Event and cutoff: May 17, 2004. This supplementary receipt tests the sanitized public proposals against `OT_2003CHUNK6_CASE81_RECONCILED.md`, the frozen neutral packet and entering law. It preserves the original commitments and reconciliation. No current private position, internal Decision Record or assignment material was consulted. No author is selected.

## Proposals and component responses

The first proposal requires a State asserting fundamental alteration to establish a supported defense. The second proposes curing a proved unlawful court-access policy for affected persons unless a narrower measure fully cures the violation, identifying actual barriers, accommodations and risk, and setting enforceable measures and deadlines, while preserving standing, merits and actual-relief predicates.

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer each adopt the first proposal.** It specifies the actual force of an existing qualification; the qualification is neither presumed satisfied nor merely an assertion the claimant must disprove.

All six also accept the second proposal's core remedial principle: when prospective relief is legally warranted, the relief must effectively cure the proved violation for those whom the court may lawfully protect; an effective narrower measure is permissible, while an inadequate narrower measure is not a complete remedy. Actual barriers, reasonable alternatives, relevant burdens and any supported risk must inform that determination. This agreement establishes neither current liability nor an automatic right to a particular accommodation.

**None of the six adopts, as an additional operative holding in this immunity appeal, a categorical requirement to enter a policy-wide decree or fixed implementation deadlines whenever a policy violation is later proved.** The precise objection and the narrower language they accept appear below. Their reservation concerns the scope of the judgment under review and the content of a future remedy, not the absence of an earlier express commitment. They do not find such relief forbidden, and they do not reject effective policy relief or deadlines when the actual remedial record warrants them.

## Defense allocation and its operative qualifications

The contemporaneous regulation expressly provides that a public entity must make necessary reasonable modifications unless it can demonstrate that the modification would fundamentally alter the service, program or activity. Under 28 C.F.R. §35.130(b)(7) (2003), the entity asserting that defense must establish it through evidence and reasons tied to the actual service and proposed modification. General cost, inconvenience or a label of alteration does not establish the defense. The claimant still must establish the applicable statutory claim, including qualification, disability-based denial and the necessary reasonable modification; the State's defense burden does not shift every merits element.

The distinct existing-facilities rule in §35.150(a) concerns accessibility of the service, program or activity viewed in its entirety. It does not necessarily require every existing building to be accessible. Section 35.150(a)(3) separately places on the public entity the burden of proving fundamental alteration or undue financial and administrative burdens. In that provision, the head or designee must consider all resources available for funding and operating the service and provide written reasons. Even where the proposed action would produce the alteration or burdens, the entity must take another action that avoids them while ensuring receipt of the benefits or services. These express requirements are not replaced by an unreviewable assertion of resource limits.

The two provisions remain distinct. Financial and administrative burden is not silently added as an independent textual defense to every modification claim under §35.130(b)(7); nor are the specific head-of-entity and written-reasons requirements of §35.150(a)(3) transplanted into every Title II theory. Section 35.150(b)(1) permits effective alternative methods and does not require structural changes when other methods achieve compliance, with priority for the most integrated appropriate setting. Its historic-property qualification also remains intact if actually applicable. No such defense or alternative is found established on the present allegations.

This receipt clarifies a supported defense burden without choosing an unpresented numerical proof standard for Lane. It adopts no new architectural violation, no particular construction obligation and no findings concerning either claimant's offered alternatives. It does not expand the frozen Section 5 ground to every Title II application.

## Remedy scope: strongest affirmative path and actual objection

The affirmative case for the second proposal is substantial. Popovich sustains an enforceable hearing-access duty rather than an abstract authorization to sue. Its accommodation rationale would be frustrated if a court found an ongoing violation, found lawful entitlement to an injunction and then knowingly accepted a corrective measure that left the protected claimant subject to that violation. Frew's supplied public rule likewise recognizes effective enforcement of lawful prospective obligations and appropriate corrective measures for established noncompliance. Those propositions support an effective cure, actual evidence and an enforceable order. Each of the six accepts that much.

The proposal expressly preserves standing and actual-relief predicates. It is therefore not treated as an immediate command to award damages, certify a class or rebuild a courthouse. Even with those conditions preserved, however, a mandatory default of policy-wide relief plus fixed deadlines would decide the form and reach of a later injunction. This appeal reviews denial of categorical immunity; it reviews no injunction, adjudicated violation, identified continuing policy, beneficiary class or remedial findings. The judgment can permit adjudication of lawful prospective relief without prescribing a decree in advance. That is a procedural and remedial scope objection to an additional holding, not an inference that the proposed relief would be unlawful in a later proved case.

Popovich supplies a particularly concrete comparison. The six supported its valid access duty but returned even an already tried claim for examination of actual instructions and preserved objections rather than restoring the award automatically. Kennedy's own opinion drew that distinction. Frew concerns an existing lawful federal implementation decree with defined obligations; its enforceability does not itself establish either the terms or beneficiaries of a new Lane decree. Those differences matter to all six independently of what the earlier Lane freeze happened to say.

The ordinary complete-cure principle also does not predetermine whether a change applicable beyond the named claimant is necessary to provide that claimant full relief, whether a properly constituted beneficiary class is entitled to relief, whether an effective accommodation already removes a continuing injury, or whether a fixed deadline is needed to make the order effective. These issues require the actual standing, merits and remedial determinations the proposal itself preserves. A policy change may properly benefit others as an incident of complete relief, but the phrase affected persons does not independently confer standing or authorize relief for every person touched by a policy.

The existing regulation illustrates why the details should remain connected to their actual triggers: §35.150(c)–(d) imposes particular structural-compliance and transition-plan requirements, including schedules, when their stated conditions apply. It does not convert every form of equitable Title II relief into the same structural transition plan. The regulation's original dates are not restarted by Lane. No present finding establishes the applicability or violation of those specific provisions.

## Accepted revision and integration instructions

All six accept this narrower formulation as remand guidance:

> A State invoking fundamental alteration must establish the defense under the applicable rule; an unsupported assertion does not suffice. If statutory liability and the requirements for prospective relief are established, the court must provide effective relief within its lawful remedial authority. It must consider the actual access barriers, reasonable accommodations and effective alternatives, and any supported statutory defense. A narrower measure is adequate only if it fully cures the proved violation for the persons the court may lawfully protect. Any injunction must state enforceable obligations; policy changes and implementation deadlines may be required when the actual remedial findings show they are necessary for effective relief. This judgment orders no particular accommodation, construction project, beneficiary-wide decree or schedule.

This revision answers the scope objection by making the necessity and extent of policy relief and deadlines depend on the actual remedial findings. It does not replace an obligation to cure a proved violation with permission to leave it uncured. It does preserve the difference between requiring an effective remedy when legally warranted and invariably requiring one prescribed form of implementation. No new general burden of persuasion on every remedial issue is assigned to the State; its expressly allocated alteration defense remains distinct from the court's determination of the permissible scope and necessity of relief.

Integration therefore has six express additional joins for the supported State defense burden and the qualified remedial guidance above. It has no six-Associate adoption of a categorical policy-wide or fixed-deadline holding in the present judgment. If an opinion retains that stronger prescription as a distinct proposition, these six withhold that proposition on the stated scope ground; their agreement with complete cure must still be shown at its actual narrower level. Scalia's and Thomas's frozen positions were not reopened or inferred. The six affirmances and two vacatur positions, the Section 5 limits and the original immunity-stage remedy remain unchanged.
