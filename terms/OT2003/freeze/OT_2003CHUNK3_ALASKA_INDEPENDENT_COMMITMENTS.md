## Reach, current status and alternative paths

The final EPA enforcement orders are reviewable. The July 16, 2003 permit already specifies SCR for MG-17 and provides that SCR ceases to be BACT if and when this litigation is decided in Alaska's favor. The litigation thus retains a concrete consequence. Issuance of the permit is not inferred to establish completed construction, actual SCR installation, EPA acknowledgment of compliance, termination of a particular order or a new administrative act. The withdrawn December direction to withhold a permit is not an operative command; the March allowance for summer-dependent construction remains part of the actual order status.

Sections 113(a)(5) and 167 confer enforcement authority against construction inconsistent with statutory requirements. Section 165(a)(4) requires BACT; §169(3) assigns the permitting authority a case-specific maximum-achievable-reduction judgment after energy, environmental and economic impacts and other costs are considered. The dispute is whether a substantively unreasonable BACT determination fails that federal requirement or whether the federal enforcement assignment stops at the existence of a genuine state determination and compliance with the formal PSD requirements.

Path A recognizes bounded EPA oversight: an unreasoned state selection does not satisfy the substantive BACT requirement. EPA must explain why the choice lies outside the permissible reasoned range and cannot replace a reasonable state choice with its preferred technology. Its strongest objection is that Congress expressly placed the determination with the State and did not expressly create an appellate permit board. A answers that the separate federal prevention provisions must have effect when the required determination lacks a reasoned basis, while preserving the State's permissible range.

Path B reverses federal intervention into this discretionary choice: the specific allocation of the BACT determination governs the general enforcement language, subject to independently enforceable express requirements and actual failures to make the statutory determination. Its strongest objection is that a paper permit could defeat the substantive federal maximum-achievable condition. B answers that state judicial review and enforcement of express federal minima remain available; the allocation does not imply federal review of every disputed cost judgment. B does not claim absolute state immunity or eliminate the federal duties recognized in Bragg.

If authority exists, Path A sustains its exercise here. Alaska abandoned the multi-generator offset rationale and invoked economics while acknowledging it lacked the data necessary to assess this operator's profitability and competitiveness. Regional importance and a rural-utility hypothetical do not establish the particular asserted impact. The State's initially identified technical and economic feasibility makes its later unsupported departure material. Neither missing financial information nor adoption of the top-down method establishes an invariably applicable SCR rule. The contrary exercise path would vacate if EPA substituted its own reasonable preference for another reasonably explained state judgment; it does not survive the identified gaps in this State's actual explanation.

Mead, Walton, Heimmermann and Barnhart v. Thomas require actual delegation and its exercise; expertise or ambiguity supplies neither. Bragg preserves a federal command after state primacy but carefully separates an identified nondiscretionary duty from all discretionary state judgments. Nebraska's regulation-of-public-operators holding does not settle the allocation of state and federal permitting authority here. Ventura counsels leaving the entrusted technology choice and unresolved discretionary application to the assigned agencies rather than selecting equipment judicially.

## Component counts and assembly limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support bounded enforcement authority, its reasonable exercise on this record, and affirmance. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on the allocation question; they do not reach a dispositive merits determination selecting the proper technology. All eight accept reviewability and a live controversy, with the actual conditional July permit controlling the present posture. These are eight-Justice provisional commitments only.

The five affirming Justices can join one statutory rule and application at the stated level of generality. Their judicial interpretation does not depend on conclusive deference to EPA's account of its own jurisdiction. The three reversing Justices can join the confined allocation rule but not a categorical rule eliminating all EPA action against state-permitted sources. Any final remedy must preserve the distinction between the withdrawn permit-withholding direction, operative modified construction restraints and the existing July permit. No further factfinding is needed to select these legal paths; no missing research document is treated as a litigant's failure.
