## Enhanced comparison of statutory and structural paths

The coverage path reads the statutes together: civilian employment by the Army or Air Force through the designated adjutant general is a covered federal employer function. The uniformed-service exclusion governs the military capacity; it does not eliminate the separately designated civilian job whenever its holder must also belong to the Guard. This interpretation preserves both the explicit federal employment arrangement and genuinely military exclusions. The alternative emphasizes the state organization's identity and the literal membership exclusion. Its strongest objection is that federal employee status alone does not expressly make every state Guard activity an Executive agency. The answer is function-specific: the election concerns the Secretary's federally designated departmental employees administered through the statutory delegate, not the Guard's every activity. NASA supports identifying the actual federal representation relationship despite internal independence; NFFE preserves actual statutory limits on the Authority.

For the same reason, permitting this election does not conscript an unwilling state executive to administer federal regulation of unrelated private persons. Printz/Mack remains intact. Condon confirms the importance of the regulated function, but does not itself decide a States-only statute. This case rests on Congress's federal technician arrangement and its employment administration, rather than a general power to regulate state personnel. Nixon's protection of a State's allocation of its subdivisions' powers does not determine Congress's own designation of federal departmental employment.

The immunity objection fails at capacity: this proceeding concerns administration of that federal employment function. The official's state title does not convert the federal employment task into a private suit against the State. This ground does not depend on an administrative label alone, absence of damages alone, constructive consent, or a finding that Congress unmistakably exposed all state Guard activity to suit. The FMC dissenters' demand to identify actual actor, function and relief is respected; their objection to compulsory state liability does not require attributing this federal employment proceeding to the State's sovereign capacity.
