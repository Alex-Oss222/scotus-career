# CNSS — frozen independent non-Stone commitments

**Event:** Center for National Security Studies v. United States Department of Justice, review of 331 F.3d 918; merits, January 6, 2004.
**Scope and provenance:** Fresh clean context; original frozen neutral packet, replacement preflight, A and access entering-law selections, public prior opinions and the identified lower-opinion recitations. No Stone material, target Supreme Court disposition or lineup, comparator, reconciliation or other commitment was received. Research cutoff: January 6, 2004. All eight Associates participate. This is a provisional legal handoff, not an account of private deliberations or an entered judgment.

## Competing statutory paths

**Supported-category affirmance.** Exemption 7(A) permits withholding of categories whose disclosure could reasonably be expected to interfere with pending or reasonably anticipated enforcement proceedings. Robbins Tire permits a category-specific prediction without evidence of actual intimidation. The declarations identify concrete mechanisms—official confirmation of investigative coverage, disclosure of direction, identification and intimidation of cooperating sources, and manufacture of misleading evidence. A comprehensive list can reveal more than isolated previously disclosed names. The strongest objection is that the category contains persons with no remaining investigative value and their lawyers, while the explanations do not adequately distinguish these portions. The affirmance path must rest on the evidentiary inference that official confirmation of the full investigative map creates the identified harm; national-security designation alone cannot do the work.

**Vacatur for adequate statutory review.** The same statutory rule permits categories but requires an explanation sufficient to connect each functional category to the predicted enforcement harm, coupled with reasonable segregation. An original-investigation label and general possible uses of a comprehensive list cannot by themselves establish that all immigration detainees, all material witnesses, every lawyer and every withheld item fall within that prediction. The strongest objection is that narrower identification or explanation may itself reveal the investigation's shape. Protected submissions, appropriately defined shared categories and confidential judicial examination can accommodate that concern; a public person-by-person explanation is not required. Release or deportation is relevant but never an automatic end to 7(A), and a person need not be guilty or charged to be a protected source.

On the second path, remand must resolve all remaining claimed protections under their actual predicates rather than restore unconditional disclosure. Under 7(C), balance the cognizable privacy interest against illumination of government conduct; association with a terrorism investigation can carry substantial privacy consequences even if arrest alone creates no constitutional privacy right. Under 7(F), establish a reasonable expectation of danger to an individual's life or physical safety; neither proof of completed harm nor a conclusory invocation of possible retaliation suffices. Under exemption 3 and Rule 6(e), determine whether disclosure would reveal protected grand-jury matters, not simply whether the person was once designated a material witness. Determine the existence and actual coverage of sealing orders through protective review where needed. Release reasonably segregable nonexempt material after those determinations. Counsel identities require their own supported connection; they cannot automatically inherit every client's exemption, nor be categorically denied safety protection because counsel accept public professional roles.

## Independent access components: all eight

The First Amendment does not require production of this executive investigative compilation merely because some underlying information concerns arrests or protected adjudicative hearings. North Jersey concerns access to removal adjudication, while United Reporting preserves the absence of a universal information right and separately governs a speech-based selection absent here. The requested compilation is not established as a historically open adjudicative record under the history-and-function inquiry. All eight affirm rejection on that ground; the North Jersey majority's five Associates do not renounce its hearing rule, and its three dissenters need not prevail on their broader disagreement to join this distinct disposition.

FOIA supplies the access regime for the very agency records claimed here. Federal common law cannot override Congress's enacted exemptions or furnish an alternative balancing route to obtain the same records. Milwaukee v. Illinois, 451 U.S. 304, supplies displacement when Congress addresses the issue; Nixon v. Warner Communications, 435 U.S. 589, supports attention to the applicable statutory access route. This ground resolves the asserted executive-compilation claim without a general ruling extinguishing common-law access to judicial records. No reopening or unsealing application is before the Court on this component.

## Remedy and continuity

For the six vacatur supporters, affirm the two independent-access rulings, vacate the blanket FOIA disposition and remand for meaningful adjudication of the withheld categories under 7(A), each unresolved independently invoked exemption and actual sealing coverage, and reasonable segregation. No automatic restoration of the district court's release order follows: its disclosure command preceded final determination of all surviving protections. The lower courts implement disclosure of nonexempt reasonably segregable material under the stated standards and may protect claimed sensitive material while deciding its status. No invented deadline or court-ordered production exercise is added.

For Scalia and Thomas, affirm the reviewed withholding and access judgment; 7(A) is sufficient, leaving the alternative exemptions unnecessary to the disposition. Their disagreement with the six-Justice group has a substantive dissent function concerning the sufficiency of the functional compilation category and the evidentiary weight of the declarations, not a different vote on the independent access claims.
