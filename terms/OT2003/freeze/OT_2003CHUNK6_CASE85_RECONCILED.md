## Reconciliation result

The judgment positions remain six Associates for affirmance and two for vacatur and remand. The grounds change materially from the provisional six-way categorical join. The full historical writings establish that Scalia and Ginsburg expressly rejected applying Belton's immediate-control account to a handcuffed person secured in a patrol car and affirmed solely because there was reason to believe the recently occupied vehicle contained evidence of the drug offense of arrest. No changed legal or factual premise supports their provisional categorical joins. Those joins are withdrawn in this reconciliation rather than preserved by disregarding that evidence.

The supplied grant expressly reaches the secured-arrestee issue. The historical majority's footnotes treating reaching distance and the alternative evidence rationale as outside its narrower question cannot establish a present scope bar. The broader grant does not establish that the parties briefed a new reasonable-belief formulation; no filing or concession is invented. The alternative was already identified as an analytical possibility in the neutral preparation. Its present consideration is a legal rationale for the granted search-validity question on the established arrest sequence, not a new factual claim.

## Grounds and direct join accounting

O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support the categorical contemporaneous recent-occupant rationale. Scalia and Ginsburg support only the offense-evidence rationale. Stevens and Souter reject both sufficient grounds and support the secured-arrestee limitation and remand. Six agree that the mere timing of the officer's first contact outside the car does not invalidate this search, but that result must not be used to combine their distinct complete rationales.

The categorical four answer the access objection through Belton's administrable category and continuity after an actual lawful arrest. Their ground is confined to genuine recent occupancy and a contemporaneous passenger-compartment search, including capable containers within that compartment. It supplies no unlimited time or distance rule, trunk search, remote search or general investigatory entitlement after every contact.

Scalia and Ginsburg find that answer insufficient once immediate access is ended. Robinson establishes a categorical search of the arrested person, not automatic authority over every nearby area. The evidence rationale preserves Belton at a vehicle-specific scope justified by lawful arrest and an objective reasonable belief that evidence of that arrest offense will be found. It requires more than the fact of any arrest and does not authorize exploratory searches after offenses for which the vehicle supplies no reasonable evidence connection. The drugs actually produced before arrest and the immediate vehicle association supply the connection here; the later discovery of the gun cannot justify the earlier search. This is a proposed constitutional development, not a holding already contained in Knowles, Houghton or Florida v. Thomas.

The evidence proposal's historical sources discuss both broader evidence gathering and Chimel's narrower access rule. Scalia and Ginsburg use them to explain their limited treatment of Belton, not to revive unrestricted residential incident searches or pretend that Chimel never limited Rabinowitz. Their vehicle and offense limitations answer the strongest objection that the rule licenses general rummaging, although Stevens and Souter continue to reject the reduced-suspicion basis. The other four affirmers' categorical commitments do not supply extra direct joins in this rationale. Any controlling status must be determined from actual final opinion joins, not counted by outcome or assumed through a narrowest-ground formula.

## Historical comparison and remedy

No Associate's judgment is changed from the comparator. Scalia and Ginsburg's rationale is corrected to accord with strong specific historical evidence on unchanged record premises and with their date-eligible positions; no historical departure supports their former categorical joins. O'Connor retains her substantive concern and her refusal to adopt an inadequately developed replacement standard. Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer retain categorical authority without adopting the historical narrower-grant scope ruling as current law.

Stevens and Souter's present emphasis on actual secured access addresses the expanded supplied question. Their historical dissent also rejected extending Belton to an already outside pedestrian and found no valid Chimel search. Their remedy here expressly preserves the unresolved alternative ground rather than converting judgment opposition into automatic suppression of every item or relief from every conviction.

Affirmance on either accepted incident-search ground makes deciding the unsupported inventory alternative unnecessary for the six affirmers. For the two opponents, the new secured-arrestee limit applies on direct review, but the case returns to determine the independent inventory/inevitable-discovery basis under Nix and Wells and resulting ordinary remedial consequences. Possible inventory is not inevitable lawful discovery; lawful impoundment, standardized criteria and inevitability require support. No acquittal, automatic all-count vacatur or later good-faith doctrine is supplied.
