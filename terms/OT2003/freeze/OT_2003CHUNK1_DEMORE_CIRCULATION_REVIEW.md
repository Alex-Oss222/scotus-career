# Demore v. Kim ? circulation compatibility review

Event and cutoff: October 14, 2003. This review receives only two proposed public legal formulations and the previously authorized neutral, entering-law, provisional and reconciled materials. It receives no current Stone material and does not infer its author. The existing commitment and reconciliation freezes are unchanged. This receipt records failed compatibility tests, not a judicial disposition or a new vote.

## Proposed individual-review formulation

The proposed disposition affirms as modified and remands, preserving Kim's bond and requiring enforceable review before renewed custody: meaningful inquiry into each actual inclusion objection; interim individual necessity review while substantial good-faith category disputes remain; independent review when detention becomes unreasonable or unjustified; and Government persuasion that effective conditions cannot adequately address danger or flight, either by clear and convincing evidence or alternatively by a preponderance.

The eight-Justice commitments do not supply a majority for this entire formulation. The operative differences are substantive, not wording objections:

- O'Connor and Kennedy require meaningful actual classification review and recognize unreasonable or arbitrary detention limits. They do not accept that a substantial classification challenge by itself creates interim eligibility for an individual risk hearing. They therefore do not join the proposed category-triggered pathway, irrespective of which Government proof quantum is selected. Their National Center, Flores, Zadvydas and Kennedy's Krimstock positions support their recorded distinction rather than eliminating it.
- Scalia and Thomas likewise reject the implied substantial-question-to-bail path. Their actual Krimstock and Fairley joins protect adjudication of an operative custody predicate, not a replacement of the statutory category with a new necessity condition. Neither a Government preponderance standard nor a heightened one answers that objection.
- Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support meaningful individual risk review, consideration of effective conditions and release authority. Their provisional and reconciled commitments do not specify a constitutional persuasion allocation or quantum for this case, and expressly do not adopt a universal governmental clear-and-convincing rule. Their actual Foucha joins preserve ordinary civil-commitment proof in its own setting; Salerno supplies the safeguards of a distinct criminal pretrial regime; Krimstock supplies preliminary Government justification for vehicle retention. Those grounds support the hearing they already favor, but do not turn either proposed general proof allocation into a frozen join. No actual proof-allocation dispute in Kim's bond proceedings is supplied. A holding adding such an allocation would require a supported new determination, not an inferred join. A remand leaving its appropriate implementation to the lower tribunal stays within their present commitments.
- Breyer's statutory position requires both a claim raising a question that is not insubstantial and a claim not interposed solely for delay, followed by a detainee showing, by clear and convincing evidence, that he is unlikely to flee or pose a danger. Those are not replaceable labels for general good faith and Government persuasion. His heightened detainee allocation is part of the section 3143(b) analogy that gives weight to Congress's categorical custody decision while permitting bond where coverage is substantially disputed. Reversing the persuasion burden removes that recorded counterweight. His Zadvydas and Gilbert opinions and Krimstock/Fairley joins support effective continuing-deprivation review in their actual regimes, but do not establish his adoption of the proposed Government-burden constitutional rule for section 1226(c).

The strongest basis for a broader Breyer join would be the serious physical liberty interest, the availability of effective conditions and his support for Government preliminary justification in Krimstock. The obstacle remains the law actually controlling this detention class, his expressly selected statutory avoidance rationale and integral detainee burden, and the absence of a supplied proof-allocation defect requiring a different rule. Neither historical evidence nor the recent public positions supplies a concrete changed premise that resolves that obstacle. His historical separate opinion explicitly selected the same detainee allocation; merely restating liberty interests does not support departure. No such join is added.

This conclusion leaves the positive hearing support of Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg intact. It does not determine that a Government burden could never be lawful or that those Justices would reject it in a properly supported case. It determines that the present freezes and circulation proposal do not establish the needed complete joins, and no desired numerical result can supply them.

## Proposed common vacatur/remand among the four reversal supporters and Breyer

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remove the Ninth Circuit's resident-wide constitutional hearing requirement and implement the statutory category regime, preserving actual inclusion challenges and justified individual constitutional objections. They specifically refuse the rule that a not-insubstantial, not-solely-for-delay coverage claim creates bond eligibility while the claim remains unresolved.

Breyer would vacate only with an enforceable remand requiring that substantial-question inquiry, bond consideration if both conditions are met, detainee clear-and-convincing risk persuasion and preserved release pending those determinations. A remand applying only the ordinary category regime would withhold the statutory protection his vote requires. A remand applying his protection would impose the legal duty the four reversal supporters reject.

Interim release through lawful implementation and returning unresolved actual classification work to the agency are shared remedial features, not a sufficient common judgment. The implementation that ends interim release depends on the incompatible substantive standards. A bare vacatur omitting the decisive instruction would conceal rather than resolve the disagreement. The record therefore supports no new five-Justice vacatur/remand coalition on these grounds. The four reversal votes and Breyer's vacatur are not added; no equal division, dismissal, reargument, interim order or other procedural outcome is invented from the absence of a majority.

## Result

No frozen commitment is changed. No additional Justice joins either proposed complete formulation. The reconciliation's six-to-two jurisdiction positions and four-reversal, three-affirmance, one-statutory-vacatur merits positions remain, subject to lawful assembly with the Chief Justice's separately controlled position. Any unresolved disposition must remain expressly unresolved rather than being entered as a judgment unsupported by the required votes.
