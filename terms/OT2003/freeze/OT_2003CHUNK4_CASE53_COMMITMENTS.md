## Disposition and operative coalitions

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the Ninth Circuit's fair-presentation determination and remand for further proceedings consistent with the failure to fairly present this federal appellate-assistance claim. Stevens affirms the appellate judgment allowing federal consideration, on a contextual fair-notice reading of Reese's actual short petition rather than a universal duty to search lower opinions.

**Seven-Associate rule.** A prisoner must fairly alert the relevant state court to the federal nature of each claim in the petition or other papers properly presenting that claim. The state court need not inspect a lower-court opinion outside those ordinary submissions to discover an unstated federal basis. A federal constitutional provision, a federal case identifying the right, or a sufficiently clear description of the federal claim may supply notice; a particular formula is not indispensable. Federal citations for one claim do not necessarily identify another claim as federal.

Picard requires a fair opportunity to decide the federal claim itself; Boerckel carries that requirement through the ordinary available review round. Castille preserves proper presentation rather than mere presence somewhere in the record. Here the petition identifies trial and appellate counsel as subjects, but its express Fifth, Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment references address coercion to waive a jury. The appellate-assistance allegation is separately left without federal identification. The postconviction court's Jones v. Barnes citation was not part of the highest court's decision under review; the intervening appellate order was a summary affirmance. Oregon's rule requiring attachment of that appellate decision does not establish that the underlying postconviction decision was attached or incorporated. The circuit's presumption that the highest court must have read the latter therefore cannot supply the missing fair presentation.

The contrary opportunity argument treats availability of a document as enough because the reviewing court could choose to consult it. That would shift from meaningful notice to possible discovery and impair the state court's ability to choose its discretionary cases from the claims actually submitted. Neither a finding that a particular Justice read the earlier opinion nor proper incorporation is established. The holding concerns that omission; it does not prescribe exclusive citation words or forbid a genuinely incorporated federal claim.

**Stevens's ground.** The actual petition should be read as a short, imperfect assertion of a federal challenge to the fairness of the conviction and sentence, including ineffective assistance at trial and on appeal. Its statement of reasons expressly names both forms of assistance, and its argument repeats ineffective assistance while invoking federal constitutional protections. In the context of the already adjudicated federal appellate-assistance claim, that submission gives adequate notice without requiring a clerk to discover an unrelated claim. The controlling standard is fair presentation, not a rule requiring a separate federal citation beside every mention of counsel. Stevens would sustain federal consideration on that contextual ground, while declining to adopt the circuit's general presumption that a discretionary court always reads the earlier trial decision.

## Late argument and remand scope

The equivalence of Oregon and federal ineffective-assistance standards was not addressed by the Ninth Circuit and was first argued in Reese's merits brief here. The general favorable-judgment and included-argument rules leave discretion to consider an additional legal argument; they do not compel it. The seven reversing Associates decline to decide that newly advanced state-law-equivalence argument on this review. Their rule rests on the actual presentation and the ground adopted below, not a categorical statement that every new respondent argument is jurisdictionally barred. Stevens's stated contextual ground does not require deciding the late equivalence contention.

The lower opinion reports the closing of the state route and absence of a miscarriage argument; these remain matters of the actual procedural record. The remand must apply that posture and the existing default/gateway rules without automatically authorizing a new claim, new evidence or an assistance-merits award. No new gateway or merits rule is adopted. The materially opposing separate writing addresses fair notice on these papers. Assignment and opinion form remain for assembly.
