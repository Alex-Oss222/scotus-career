## Historical comparison and result

The comparator is the Fifth Circuit's June 24, 2003 judgment, not a Supreme Court merits disposition. There is therefore no historical Supreme Court Justice vote or join to import or depart from. The lower court's federal-employer-capacity analysis is consistent with the provisional result, but does not substitute for the independent statutory and structural analysis. All eight commitments are retained.

The statutory ground is the actual federal employment relationship: section 709 makes these technicians employees of the relevant military Department and the United States and designates the adjutant general to administer their employment. Sections 7103 and 2105 preserve the difference between that civilian appointment and membership or duties in the uniformed services. The State's organizational identity does not transform administration of these departmental employees into regulation of an unrelated state sovereign activity. The holding permits this representation election, not every future proposal or remedy.

## Scope and remedy

Affirm rejection of the requested injunction; the representation election may proceed for the covered civilian technicians. No actual bargaining proposal, military operational order, discipline or damages award is adjudicated. Section 7103(a)(12) requires no agreement or concession, and section 7103(a)(14)(C) preserves matters specifically provided for by federal statute. Section 709's specific discipline, appeal and other exceptions retain their force. No constructive waiver, general abrogation holding or general collateral-review entitlement follows.

The strongest contrary argument is that employee designation alone does not make a state organization an Executive agency. The response remains limited to the Secretary's expressly designated departmental employment administered through the statutory delegate. The rule does not reach the Guard for every purpose. Feres's military tort rationale does not erase express civilian labor coverage.
