# Lidster — provisional non-Chief commitments

Event: January 13, 2004; No. 02-1060. [B preflight](OT_2003CHUNK3_B_PREFLIGHT.md) and [entering law](../entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK3_B.md) control source limits and chronology. Eight Associates participate. No target historical outcome, Chief Justice vote or assignment enters this handoff.

## Two supported paths

**Distinguish the witness inquiry and sustain this operation.** Edmond governs a program designed to find evidence that the stopped motorists themselves committed ordinary crimes. This operation asks persons who may have used a particular road at a relevant time to help identify another motorist in a specific fatal hit-and-run. That actual objective does not depend on suspecting the stopped persons of wrongdoing. Brown requires examination of the concrete public concern, the operation's connection to obtaining useful information and the liberty intrusion. The same location and time one week later furnish a rational witness connection; a grave specific fatal incident supplies a concrete concern; ten-to-fifteen-second systematic contacts to request information and give a flyer supply a small intrusion. The later intoxication investigation requires its own individualized ground, supplied here by the near collision and observed impairment after a lawful stop.

Strongest objection: virtually every criminal investigation can be called an information request, and a driver is seized regardless of whether police hope to accuse him. The rule therefore must turn on actual operation and a concrete link to potential witnesses, not an information-gathering label. This operation is not generalized interrogation looking for a crime in each stopped car. No universal permission to detain witnesses follows.

**Reject categorical invalidity but remand for initial reasonableness application.** The state courts relied on Edmond categorically, leaving contextual application undeveloped. Brief face-to-face contact does not necessarily establish total restraint, queue effects or the permissible scope of officer discretion. A remand can apply the actual-purpose, connection and intrusion rule without inventing facts. Counterargument: the supplied record already states the material context, systematic short stops and specific witness objective, so an additional proceeding would simply repeat an application this Court can complete. Remand is justified only if an actual material dispute or absence of findings matters; it cannot be a routine entitlement to a second look.

Categorical suppression of every checkpoint connected to investigation would extend Edmond beyond its adopted rule. Neither branch accepts that extension. Brevity alone is not an independent justification. The case is not resolved by Pringle or Banks; each presupposes the distinct authority at issue there.

## Stable propositions and remedial difference

All eight reject the Illinois court's categorical application of Edmond. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer also sustain this operation on the supplied facts. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg prefer a contextual remand and do not supply votes for this Court's final reasonableness application. Their difference concerns the completeness and institutional treatment of operational findings, not a vote to affirm suppression. Reconciliation must test that remedial position against the actual neutral record and may not infer an absent dispute from this table.

Every final rule must preserve the particular incident, the location/time connection, the limited nonaccusatory request and the actual short systematic operation that justify this stop. It need not require a numerical success rate, exhaustion of every alternative or the assumption that nobody stopped could happen to be the offender. Probable cause arising during a lawful encounter supports the separate intoxication investigation; it does not retroactively authorize the checkpoint.
