### Issue 1: Objective custody and habeas limits

* **Controlling Law:** Thompson v. Keohane treats Miranda custody as an objective legal question applied to historical facts. Williams v. Taylor adds the governing §2254 restriction: objective unreasonableness, not mere disagreement or a tally of judges. Lockyer v. Andrade confirms that §2254(d)(1) requires a contrary governing rule or an objectively unreasonable application, rather than independent error alone; clearly established substantive law is measured at the state merits adjudication without demanding a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record rather than isolated phrases or a correct formula alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess whether the circumstances impose restraint equivalent to formal arrest, then determine whether the state court’s application of established holdings is objectively unreasonable. A general rule may be clearly established without identical facts.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment Miranda protection; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1).
* **Material Other Authority:** Berkemer v. McCarty and Stansbury v. California emphasize objective circumstances rather than undisclosed subjective perceptions. Miranda v. Arizona supplies the warning requirement when custody exists.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Objective custody and habeas review are settled; the required role of youth or inexperience was not specifically settled by the pre-event law.
* **Open Point:** Whether the established objective rule compelled a custody finding on this full record, with any required treatment of age.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit found the state analysis unreasonable because it insufficiently accounted for Alvarado’s youth and inexperience.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The warden argues that no established holding required those individualized factors and that the overall circumstances support noncustody.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Alvarado argues that a reasonable young person isolated at the station would not feel free to leave and that objective custody principles already protect that situation.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** The voluntariness of a statement and the objective custody trigger are distinct; an independent coercion claim requires its own preservation and record.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** If the state application was within the objective range permitted by established law, reverse the writ determination. If the complete circumstances compel custody under that law, proceed to admission and prejudice consequences. A new direct-review rule concerning age cannot by itself establish a prior state court’s §2254 error.
