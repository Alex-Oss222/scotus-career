## 42. Illinois v. Fisher — February 23, 2004

**Disposition commitments:** All eight Associates reverse the federal due-process basis for dismissal and remand for further proceedings. Seven would join the Youngblood application; Stevens agrees with the judgment on the narrower ground described below.

**Common minimum:** Four tests identified cocaine; a fifth independent test might have helped the defense, but the record establishes neither apparent exculpatory value before destruction nor bad faith. A pending discovery request does not by itself convert potentially useful evidence into apparently exculpatory evidence. Nor does importance alone establish the constitutional category or guarantee federal dismissal. Independent state discovery and preservation remedies remain available to the extent their own law permits.

| Justice | Commitment and individual support | Ground and join limit |
|---|---|---|
| Stevens | Reverse and remand; concurrence in the judgment. His Youngblood concurrence, 488 U.S. 59–61, rejects a categorical rule that bad faith is indispensable in every conceivable destruction case. | Four inculpatory tests, absence for more than a decade and routine destruction do not establish the exceptional loss so critical that a trial becomes fundamentally unfair. He joins rejection of a discovery-request exception and this disposition, but not an unconditional bad-faith rule covering every future case. |
| O'Connor | Reverse and remand; full join to the ordinary potentially-useful-evidence holding. She joined Youngblood's majority, and her Kyles join concerns suppressed favorable evidence rather than an unknown unperformed test. | Retain Trombetta's apparently exculpatory, irreplaceable category. |
| Scalia | Reverse and remand; full join. His Youngblood majority join supplies the bad-faith requirement for merely potentially useful evidence. | A request is not proof of bad faith, and the Court cannot presume the fifth test's result. |
| Kennedy | Reverse and remand; full join. His Youngblood majority join governs this classification. | No categorical approval of all destruction policies; actual bad faith remains independently provable in a proper case. |
| Souter | Reverse and remand; full join on the preserved Youngblood framework. His Kyles opinion requires established favorable suppression and cumulative materiality, neither a presumed exculpatory test nor an exception based solely on a request. | Preserve the distinction between destruction of unknown-value evidence and withholding favorable evidence held by the case team. |

**Alternative-path comparison:** The request-based path gives the State notice of the defense's interest, which can be relevant to a genuine bad-faith inquiry. Its decisive weakness is treating notice as a categorical substitute for bad faith and apparent exculpatory character. The established-rule path recognizes the practical and constitutional distinction between potentially useful testing and known favorable material; its strongest objection is the risk that irreplaceable evidence could be destroyed in good faith despite overwhelming defense importance. Stevens preserves that exceptional fundamental-fairness possibility as his separate view. The seven-Justice proposed rationale applies the received rule to this record without purporting to displace Trombetta or decide every preservation policy.

**Remedy:** Reverse the federal constitutional dismissal and remand. The Supreme Court does not itself resolve every state-law remedy or order a new scientific test of nonexistent material. The remand follows rejection of the erroneous categorical federal exception.


## 46. Olympic Airways v. Husain — February 24, 2004

**Disposition commitments:** All eight Associates provisionally affirm Article 17 liability on the supported, unusual, repeated refusal of requested accommodation found to contribute to death. Their common ground treats the concrete interaction as a causal external event; it establishes neither a negligence-only rule nor liability for every omitted service.

**Governing application:** Saks requires an unexpected or unusual event external to the passenger, permits flexible assessment of the circumstances, and requires only a qualifying link in the causal chain. It excludes an internal reaction to usual aircraft operation and distinguishes an accident from injury alone. Its examples include deliberate acts, so deliberate refusal is not disqualified simply because the actor intended it. The neutral packet supplies an actual refusal after repeated requests, available accommodation, departure from policy and practice, and causal contribution. The Court need not reclassify ordinary smoke exposure itself as the accident. Tseng preserves the treaty's independent conditions and exclusivity in its field; Zicherman leaves compensable loss to applicable domestic law subject to those conditions.

|---|---|---|
| Stevens | Affirm. He joined O'Connor's Saks opinion, including its flexible causal-chain rule, and the unanimous Zicherman opinion. | His disagreement with Tseng exclusivity does not prevent relief under Article 17 here. No separate local-law holding is needed. |
| O'Connor | Affirm. Her Saks opinion supplies the precise rule, and her Zicherman and Tseng opinions preserve the treaty's liability conditions. The unusual refusal is a distinct causal occurrence from the passenger's internal response to ordinary smoke. | Policy noncompliance alone cannot substitute for an unusual external causal event. **Alternative-path comparison:** The carrier's strongest path identifies ordinary smoke and the passenger's asthma as the physical mechanism and argues that a failure to alleviate that mechanism is not an accident. That path correctly rejects injury-only and negligence-only liability, but it treats the final physical cause as the sole legally relevant cause. Saks expressly permits another unusual external link, and its deliberate-act examples reject a categorical intentional-conduct exclusion. The survivors' path succeeds because the record contains the distinct repeated interaction and an unusual refusal, not merely a general wish for better service. Their path would fail if reduced to ordinary operations plus an internal medical response; all eight preserve that limitation.

**Uncertainty and reconciliation need:** No target historical opinion or supposed oral-argument concession supplies these commitments. The packet describes unusualness as a supported trial finding. The exact preserved challenge to that finding may matter if later neutral verification changes that premise; it must then return through neutral correction and affected modeling rather than being patched in assembly. The current commitment is not a general rule that every omission is an event.

**Remedy:** Affirm the reviewed treaty liability without altering independent damages categories or imposing a new tort trial. A contrary accident ruling would not itself authorize replacement local damages within Tseng's covered field. No later treaty regime is used for this 1998 flight.
