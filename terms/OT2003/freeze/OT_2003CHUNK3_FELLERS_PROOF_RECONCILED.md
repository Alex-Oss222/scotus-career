## Supported exact formulation

Once the defendant establishes the deliberate-elicitation violation and its causal connection to the later statement, the Government must establish by a preponderance of the evidence that the later statement resulted from a genuinely independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of the violation. The Government must show that causal independence is more likely than not; evidentiary equipoise does not satisfy its burden. Adequate later warnings and a knowing, voluntary waiver remain important but do not alone establish that distinct proposition.

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer expressly accept this proof standard within their already-supported causal-independence rule. Scalia and Thomas do not join that substantive rule or its proof-standard component. Their final judgment support remains the narrower vacatur/remand recorded in `OT_2003CHUNK3_FELLERS_RECONCILED.md`. The clean addendum's references to their original affirmance proposals report the original neutral-stage baseline and do not supersede the completed historical reconciliation of those judgment positions.

## Source verification and limits of inference

Brown v. Illinois, 422 U.S. 590, 603?604, supplies the prosecution's admissibility burden and separates warnings and voluntariness from causal attenuation. It does not itself announce a numerical proof quantum for the present Sixth Amendment application.

Nix v. Williams, 467 U.S. 431, 444?445 and note 5, applies preponderance to inevitable discovery after an actual Sixth Amendment violation. Its proof discussion relies on the ordinary suppression standard in United States v. Matlock, 415 U.S. 164, 177?178 and note 14, and Lego v. Twomey, 404 U.S. 477, 488?489. Nix does not decide later-statement attenuation. Its specific authority here is that the Sixth Amendment label does not make clear-and-convincing proof universal and that ordinary admissibility facts generally use preponderance. Matlock concerns consent authority, and Lego concerns voluntariness; neither makes voluntariness sufficient to establish attenuation. Lego also preserves a State's authority under its own law to require a higher standard; this federal prosecution supplies no basis to displace such an independent state protection.

Nix's note 5 expressly distinguishes Wade's requirement of clear-and-convincing proof of an independent basis for an in-court identification after an uncounseled lineup. Wade, 388 U.S. 218, 240?241, addresses the special danger that the lineup fixes the identification in memory and makes reconstruction of an untainted recollection unusually difficult. The present inquiry concerns the actual course of the later interview, warnings, waiver, timing, officers' conduct and intervening events. It can involve credibility judgments, but so does voluntariness under Lego. Nothing in this record establishes Wade's distinctive identification-reconstruction problem as a necessary feature of the later statement.

Stevens's Nix concurrence at 457, note 8, expressly agrees with preponderance for objectively testable inevitable-discovery evidence despite his refusal to join the entire Court opinion. O'Connor joined that Court opinion. The other four supporters rely on the controlling pre-divergence suppression cases together with their already-frozen requirement that independence itself be established. Their acceptance is a supported extension of those proof principles, not a claim that any one earlier case already decided this precise question.

The primary Cornell texts of Brown, Nix including the Court's note 5 and Stevens's note 8, Lego's majority explanation and Matlock's note 14 were checked directly. The strongest higher-proof analogy and its reason were assessed; Wade's quantum was not imported merely because both cases concern the Sixth Amendment.

## Exact reach and mandate clarification

The defendant's established original violation and causal connection trigger the Government's burden. This addendum sets no new numerical threshold for the defendant that the original packet did not supply. It preserves the causal considerations already frozen and does not permit speculation that lawful officers inevitably would have obtained the same confession at some future interview. The relevant independence is that of the statement actually obtained.

The separate harmless-error burden remains beyond a reasonable doubt under Chapman if a properly preserved harmlessness contention is reached. Preponderance for admissibility does not reduce the prosecution's trial burden or the harmlessness burden. If causal independence fails and harmlessness is not established where properly available, the affected conviction is set aside for lawful proceedings under the original remedy.

The original model uses vacate and remand; historical Fellers uses reverse and remand. In this record both formulations remove the Eighth Circuit's admission-based affirmance and return the matter for the stated causal and, if necessary, harmlessness determinations. The substantive commitments do not condition support on that conventional verb. Assembly may use reverse and remand to express correction of the lower court's constitutional legal error, or vacate and remand as originally frozen, provided the mandate preserves the actual scope just stated and does not become an immediate final suppression, acquittal or new-trial command independent of the required findings. No change in disposition coalition follows from the verb alone.
