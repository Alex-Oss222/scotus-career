## Operative grounds and barriers

Section 2 requires relevant-market monopoly power and unlawful acquisition or maintenance, not incumbent status, regulatory breach, exclusionary purpose or rival injury alone. A supported competitive mechanism and causation remain necessary. An attempt theory actually pursued additionally requires specific intent and independent dangerous probability under Spectrum Sports. Aspen provides an example of unjustified exclusion, not a universal checklist of indispensable facts. None of the eight adopts a general duty to assist rivals.

The five remand Associates treat the alleged discrimination in rival-customer order processing, used to impair competing service and deter its customers, as a specific potential monopoly-maintenance mechanism requiring ordinary adjudication. The alleged impairment of the competing route to end users identifies the theory; the regulatory access standard does not establish liability. Technical demands, actual investment incentives and substantial legitimate operational explanations remain material. Insufficient allegations or supported failure of proof permit dismissal or summary judgment. No discovery, hearing or trial is guaranteed.

The three dismissing Associates consider the same alleged customer diversion the asserted consequence of deficient compelled assistance, rather than an independently sufficient antitrust wrong. A sharing obligation plus exclusionary purpose does not establish the antitrust dealing duty this claim assumes. That conclusion addresses the actual supplied allegation without granting regulated-industry immunity or requiring voluntary prior dealing in every section 2 case.

The five damages-directness Associates distinguish injury to AT&T's ability to obtain and furnish service from Trinko's consequent service loss. The direct carrier claim, duplication and allocation problems defeat this existing damages theory. Targeting rival patronage does not by itself eliminate that intermediary relationship. This is statutory directness, not Article III and not a categorical consumer exclusion. No particular invoice, pass-on charge or quantified loss is invented. Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer instead regard the alleged end-user order impairment as a potentially direct instrument of exclusion, closer to McCready than Steamfitters' illness-mediated expenses. They preserve adjudication rather than find standing established: personal business/property loss, causal relation, overlap and workable allocation remain requirements.

## Independent section 16 disposition and actual prayer

Cargill requires a personal threat of injury of the kind antitrust law protects against, arising from what makes the conduct unlawful. Section 16 does not require completed business-or-property loss. Cargill notes 5 and 6 prevent transferring duplicate damages, difficult allocation and the existence of a more direct damages claimant into conclusive injunction barriers. Section 4 directness alone therefore does not dispose of this prayer.

The five remand Associates preserve examination of the alleged systematic strategy as a prospective threat to this existing rival-carrier customer under their surviving section 2 theory. They do not find that threat proved. The lower court must identify a significant personal threat from an impending violation or one continuing or likely to recur, assess the actual protection supplied by regulatory measures, and decide whether lawful effective relief can address that threat. Regulation is material but is neither presumed complete redress nor disregarded.

The complaint seeks preliminary and permanent access terms for rivals at least as favorable as Verizon enjoys. No Associate accepts that parity demand merely because telecommunications law requires sharing. Any decree must rest on an independently established antitrust wrong, causation, threatened injury, necessity and workable scope. A narrower remedy may be considered only if the existing claim and ordinary equitable principles support it; no new allegation or hypothetical violation is supplied. If no sufficient offense, personal threat or legally supportable effective remedy exists, relief must be denied. Judicial administration of general interconnection parity cannot replace those requirements. The exact verified prayer is appended by script below.

O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dismiss the injunction because the section 2 predicate fails. They accept Cargill's different injury standard and do not deny equity merely because damages are unavailable. Their ground requires no finding that agency action eliminated every future threat.
