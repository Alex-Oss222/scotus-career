### Issue 1: Federal civilian employment and official capacity

* **Controlling Law:** Reno v. Condon distinguishes regulation of a government’s own activity from compelled regulation of others. Printz v. United States protects sovereign administration against impermissible conscription. The state-immunity cases require source and capacity analysis rather than immunity by title alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An official’s state office does not determine the legal character of every task. Apply the obligations of a valid federal employment arrangement to the accepted federal employer function while preserving genuinely military exclusions.
* **Other Governing Law:** 32 U.S.C. §709; Federal Service Labor-Management Relations Statute, including 5 U.S.C. §7103(a)(3) and the exclusion for uniformed services.
* **Material Other Authority:** Feres v. United States concerns military tort liability; it does not silently repeal express federal civilian labor coverage.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Own-activity and capacity distinctions are established; their application to the Guard’s dual civilian and military structure is the presented issue.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Guard’s administration of federal technicians is covered federal agency activity despite its state organizational identity.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Fifth Circuit held that the Guard and adjutant general act in a federal capacity when employing these technicians and therefore are subject to federal labor obligations.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The Guard argues that its state status and military mission preclude FLRA authority and that federal orders invade state sovereignty.
* **Respondent or appellee:** FLRA relies on Congress’s express designation of the technicians and the federal employment function, distinguishing civilian bargaining coverage from command of military operations.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Particular proposals or remedies may still be excluded by statutory military-management limitations; representation eligibility does not resolve every bargaining dispute.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The federal authority defends Congress’s civilian-employment framework.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Sustaining coverage affirms and permits the representation election, with military exclusions applied in later concrete disputes. Finding no covered federal agency would halt this proceeding without resolving all technician employment law. A state-immunity disposition would require identifying an actual suit or remedy against the State rather than assuming one from the official’s title.
