## Neutral posture and lawful scope

Act 34 is the operative plan. The voters renewed their challenge to it; the appeal therefore concerns a live replacement-plan claim. The earlier population defect and use of Act 1 for the 2002 election under a stay do not adjudicate a partisan defect in Act 34. Neither an injury finding nor a finding of continuity between the plans is supplied. No Justice treats the earlier unlawful map as a lawful benchmark merely because an election occurred under it.

The Court can decide the legal sufficiency and justiciability questions without deciding a map's factual liability. It cannot find a constitutional violation, order a particular replacement map, identify an unproved injured district, deem an unpreserved association theory pleaded, or decide how a nonexistent record establishes durable entrenchment. A remand may direct the lower court to apply an announced rule to actually pleaded claims and any amendment ordinary procedure permits. It is not a finding that every claim survives or a compulsory opportunity to manufacture evidence.

## Enhanced issue comparison

**Path A — categorical political-question disposition of the asserted partisan-dilution category.** The objection is that neither equal protection nor Article I identifies the acceptable partisan allocation from which a court can measure excess. Purpose, map shape and projected seats do not independently create the missing legal entitlement. The strongest answer is Bandemer's majority justiciability holding and the ability to adjudicate ordinary discrimination and direct political exclusion. The three supporters distinguish such independent individual wrongs from a judicially supplied entitlement to a partisan share of representation and would overrule Bandemer's contrary category-level justiciability conclusion.

**Path B — affirm this dismissal without foreclosing every future claim.** The present standards fail to identify a concrete, administrable personal injury in the operative map; that failure does not demonstrate that a workable constitutional claim is impossible. The strongest objection is that leaving the category open without a presently adequate standard may indefinitely deny relief. The response is that the Court cannot turn that concern into a new merits rule unsupported by the existing claim, while specific unequal treatment or expression burdens remain potentially adjudicable through their own elements.

**Path C — vacate the equal-protection dismissal for a confined district-specific inquiry.** Ordinary discrimination doctrine permits inquiry into intentional, materially unequal political opportunity caused by the challenged district assignment. The ground is an individual entitlement to equal participation, not a party's entitlement to seats. Purpose, departure from neutral criteria, actual electoral consequences, feasible lawful comparisons and exclusion from effective political processes must perform their distinct evidentiary functions. The strongest objection is that any preferred map can be called more neutral and any losing candidate can be described as excluded. The four remand supporters require a materially adverse personal effect and a causal comparison justified independently of the preferred seat total; they refuse liability from purpose, irregular shape, lost political advantage or statewide disproportionality alone. Their differences about the necessary adverse effect and the controlling formulation remain below.

**Article I component.** All eight reject the proposed automatic rule that a projected supermajority of seats without a statewide popular-vote majority itself violates Article I. District elections do not constitute a statewide election for a proportional delegation. Cook's official-ballot penalty rule supplies no such conversion. Statistical disparity may be evidence under a separately valid injury rule; it does not independently establish this Article I theory.

## Justice-specific commitments and join limits

### Stevens

Vacate the equal-protection dismissal and remand for the confined personal-district inquiry in Path C; affirm rejection of the independent Article I tally theory. His Bandemer join with Powell supports judicial examination of deliberate arbitrary district manipulation; his later joins in Shaw, Miller, Holder and Cano require actual legally cognizable harm and prevent carrying forward a purpose-or-design-alone theory. His Cook opinion distinguishes electoral administration from official disadvantaging of political positions.

He supports a rule allowing a voter to challenge deliberate district assignment that materially and unjustifiably impairs that voter's equal electoral opportunity because of political affiliation, when actual causation is established against a feasible lawful comparison rooted in neutral districting considerations. No fixed count of compactness, boundary or election factors is necessary. A legislature's partisan objective can be evidence, but must be connected to the actual unequal effect. Ordinary procedure determines the sufficiency of the live Act 34 allegations and any requested amendment.

His strongest objection to affirmance is that a demand for comprehensive statewide political exclusion would foreclose cognizable individual harm. His join barrier is a rule making partisan motive, predominant design or shape alone sufficient, or a rule requiring every injured voter to wait through a fixed number of elections. He would join a more confined actual-injury and causation rule if it provides a usable adjudicative route and does not erase direct political exclusion. No prior-map continuity or liability is presumed.

### O'Connor

Affirm the partisan dismissal on Path A and reject the Article I tally theory. Her own Bandemer concurrence is direct date-eligible authority: choosing how much partisan advantage is permissible requires a policy baseline the Constitution does not supply, and major-party seat entitlements are distinct from personal equality. Her Voinovich opinion and Holder position preserve statutory voting-rights adjudication without making proportionality an individual constitutional entitlement. Her Cano Part II.A join accepts genuine participation injury while distinguishing it from expected electoral prospects.

She would join overruling Bandemer's justiciability holding for this partisan-dilution category, while preserving direct exclusion, independently proved political retaliation, population and racial protections under their own law. Her strongest counterargument is that the ordinary injury-purpose-causation structure might discipline the inquiry. She rejects it as a standard for the proposed representational claim because its injury baseline still depends on a judicial choice of the politically fair map. She does not require a ruling on a preserved independent expression claim where none is supplied.

### Scalia

Affirm on Path A; reject the Article I tally theory. His Holder noncoverage position, Growe opinion preserving institutional responsibility and causal proof, and joins in Cano's separate injury/cause rule and Cook's limited ballot-penalty rule distinguish an actual legal violation from free-ranging review of electoral advantage. He would reject Bandemer's category-level justiciability holding because its acknowledged judicial standard has not been identified in a legally manageable form here.

The strongest opposing path offers neutral criteria and a lawful comparator. His objection is that the selection and weighting of those criteria would decide how much partisan effect to permit without an enacted or constitutional scale. He refuses a purpose-based presumption, proportional tally benchmark or general compactness test. His categorical position does not immunize an actual ballot exclusion, unconstitutional population deviation or separately established suppression of protected expression. He could join a disposition resting on the present claim's insufficiency, but would state that the partisan-dilution category itself should be closed.

### Kennedy

Affirm this partisan dismissal on Path B; reject the Article I tally theory; do not overrule Bandemer's justiciability conclusion. His Holder position demands a principled comparison rather than a choice among desirable governmental designs. His Cook join and the published McConnell separate position protect actual political expression against official disadvantage. His Cano Part II.A join requires an actual interest and causal impairment, while preserving participation injury before an election is lost.

He finds no sufficiently defined present standard connecting Act 34 to a personal unconstitutional burden; the projected seat rule and predominant-purpose presumption do not provide one. The strongest counterargument is a confined association or retaliation route. He regards such a route as a possible future or properly presented claim, not an established legal basis for remanding the present asserted theories. He refuses a categorical declaration that no manageable standard can ever emerge, but also refuses treating openness to a possible standard as agreement to any proposed new test. His current commitment does not supply an affirmative remand vote. A genuine new neutral predicate or preserved actual expression claim would require the affected stage to be revisited, not an unexplained assembly switch.

### Souter

Vacate the equal-protection dismissal under Path C and remand; reject the Article I tally theory. His own De Grandy opinion separates real local opportunity from proportional numbers, his Brooks opinion separates purpose and causation, and his Cano Part II.B applies the need for a genuine participation injury rather than favorable constituency composition. His Holder join supports a workable comparison with independent roots rather than requiring a uniquely ideal system.

He supports a district-specific comparison in which a claimant identifies personal adverse treatment, a feasible lawful district alternative grounded in ordinary criteria, an actual material loss of equal electoral opportunity attributable to the challenged assignment, and purposeful partisan discrimination. Once prohibited purpose substantially motivates the established injury, the ordinary same-decision burden rests with the defenders. He does not require a particular number of evidentiary factors or import the racial §2 capacity predicates into the constitutional claim.

His strongest objection to Path A is that manageable comparisons can be concrete and local without constitutionalizing proportionality. His barrier to a broader remand rule is using an alternative map's preferred partisan output as the comparison's sole justification, or treating purpose as proof of adverse effect. He accepts the narrowest administrable actual-injury rule that preserves that inquiry, with no finding that these voters have already satisfied it and no election-count waiting rule.

### Thomas

Affirm on Path A and reject the Article I tally theory. His Holder position rejects dilution adjudication that requires courts to select an electoral arrangement without a legal benchmark; his Cano statutory noncoverage position preserves that objection, while his unanimous constitutional-rule join there accepts independent injury and causation. His Cook application join addresses an official ballot penalty, not a right to a politically preferred district composition.

His strongest counterargument is that ordinary discriminatory-intent adjudication can discipline the claim. He answers that an intention to create partisan advantage is not itself a constitutionally defined disadvantage, and that the proposed alternative map still requires the court to choose the political good to be allocated. He joins categorical nonjusticiability of the asserted partisan-dilution category and preserves separate constitutional rights with established content. He will not join a presumptive violation from partisan design, a seat ratio or a transposed Gingles test. An opinion confined to affirmance for insufficiency is acceptable as a narrower judgment basis but does not exhaust his ground.

### Ginsburg

Vacate the equal-protection dismissal under Path C and remand; reject the Article I tally theory. Her Hunt and Easley opinions enforce the distinction between design and an actually presented independent deprivation while allowing ordinary adjudication of the latter. Her Holder and Cano joins support practical comparisons and actual political participation, without a requirement that a claimant first suffer an electoral defeat.

She joins Souter's confined district-specific formulation above: actual personal harm, an independently grounded lawful comparison, purpose and causal effect, with the defenders' same-decision burden following the requisite purpose showing. The remand determines what the Act 34 pleadings actually assert and what amendment ordinary procedure permits. It does not convert an aesthetic objection or expected loss of favorable constituents into a constitutional claim.

Her strongest counterargument to remand is that Cano rejected similar prospect-and-composition allegations. She distinguishes legal clarification at a dismissal stage from a finding that the present allegations already prove a violation; any insufficient design-only claim must still fail. She refuses automatic map relief, compulsory new discovery without ordinary entitlement, a fixed electoral-loss waiting period or a statewide offset that extinguishes a proved personal injury.

### Breyer

Vacate the equal-protection dismissal under Path C and remand; reject the Article I tally theory. His Miller and Cano joins reject design-only liability and accept actual participation rights. His Cook join distinguishes official partisan penalties from neutral administration; his published McConnell positions recognize the practical relationship between political rules and effective participation without creating a right to a preferred electoral outcome.

He supports adjudication when purposeful manipulation materially blocks an effective opportunity for voters to affect representation through the political process, rather than merely making a party's victory harder. Durable insulation from meaningful electoral challenge is a sufficient proposed way to show that injury if causally demonstrated against a lawful comparison, but anticipated seat disproportionality alone is not. He accepts direct materially unequal participation without requiring that every case establish a fixed history of electoral losses. On this dismissal record the lower court should examine the actual pleaded injury under the clarified rule; no entrenchment finding is made here.

His strongest objection to a broad district comparator is that almost any lawful alternative can shift political prospects. He joins only an actual material-opportunity rule that excludes ordinary electoral competition and does not constitutionalize any preferred number of seats. He could join Souter's formulation at that level, with the concrete understanding that ordinary loss of a hoped-for victory is insufficient. He does not join a predominance presumption without the independent injury showing.

The four remand supporters share the negative rules and the actual personal injury, prohibited purpose and causation requirements stated above. Their affirmative descriptions do not authorize assembly to announce a more elaborate test, a specific historical benchmark, a fixed election count, a universal district-shape threshold or a strict-scrutiny rule without separately testing each join. No fractured-rationale aggregation is assumed.

A remand on their ground would leave the population disposition intact, direct examination of the operative Act 34 claim and any ordinarily permissible amendment, and require any later relief to cure a proved personal injury with appropriate opportunity for state redistricting. A stay is not independently ordered. No prior-map continuity is presumed, no Act 1 election result alone establishes Act 34 liability, and no standalone First Amendment claim is deemed preserved.
