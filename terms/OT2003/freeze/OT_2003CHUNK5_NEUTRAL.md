## 56. Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara
* **Citation or Docket:** 335 F.3d 357 (Fifth Circuit, June 18, 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-09
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal court enforcing a Swiss arbitral award under the New York Convention may enjoin the losing party from pursuing Indonesian proceedings to annul the award and restrain enforcement.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit vacated the preliminary foreign anti-suit injunction and the surviving civil-contempt consequence. The district court’s separate judgment recognizing the award was the subject of a different appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** The award creditor seeks restoration of the injunction; Pertamina seeks preservation of vacatur and freedom to pursue the foreign proceedings.
* **Material Record:** Contracts concerning an Indonesian geothermal project required Swiss arbitration. After a substantial award and a United States enforcement judgment, Pertamina sought annulment and anti-enforcement relief in Indonesia. The district court restrained substantive participation and imposed civil contempt for violation of its temporary order.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged relief is an in-personam foreign anti-suit injunction, not an appeal deciding recognition of the award itself. The continuing civil-contempt issue follows the injunction’s validity; criminal contempt and enforcement merits cannot be assumed included.
* **Relevant Dates:** Arbitral award December 2000; United States recognition judgment December 2001; foreign restraint proceedings 2002; circuit judgment June 18, 2003; assigned simulation date March 9, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1) supplies review of an injunction. Equitable discretion must operate within the treaty and statutory framework, respect foreign proceedings, and identify necessity rather than protect a litigant from ordinary parallel costs.

### Issue 1: Foreign proceedings during Convention enforcement

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted decision establishes a controlling New York Convention anti-suit test for this setting. The Fifth Circuit's competing approach is subject to review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate adjudicative authority to recognize the award, the effect of a foreign annulment or restraint, and the necessity of an injunction against a litigant. Relief must address an established threat within the court’s authority and remain proportionate.
* **Other Governing Law:** New York Convention Articles III, V and VI; Federal Arbitration Act Chapter 2. The Convention allocates recognition and annulment questions among courts and supplies grounds for withholding or postponing enforcement.
* **Material Other Authority:** The district and appellate reasoning identify competing anti-suit approaches. Their tests are arguments within this review, not independently adopted rules. The treaty text controls over a free-floating preference for either enforcement or international noninterference.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Convention permits proceedings in more than one national court and gives the enforcing court its own recognition task. A foreign proceeding’s existence does not itself remove that authority.
* **Open Point:** Whether the shown Indonesian proceedings threaten the United States court’s ability to decide or effectuate its own lawful judgment sufficiently to justify this injunction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The creditor argues that the Indonesian action attacks a final recognition judgment and frustrates the Convention’s enforcement policy. Pertamina argues that the Convention anticipates multinational proceedings and that the United States court can decide the effect of foreign orders without suppressing their pursuit. Indonesia’s amicus participation makes the sovereign interests concrete. The district court relied on protecting its judgment; the circuit found that justification insufficient in this treaty structure. Neither characterization settles the separate recognition appeal.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm vacatur if the domestic court can preserve its independent jurisdiction and judgment without this restraint. Reverse if a demonstrated foreign interference makes a tailored injunction necessary and the equities justify it. A narrower remand would require identification of a specific, otherwise unremediable threat. The separate enforcement judgment remains outside the judgment under review.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule can reject ordinary parallel proceedings as sufficient necessity without denying every possible foreign anti-suit power. It need not decide conclusively which country has primary annulment jurisdiction or whether a later annulment defeats recognition.

## 57. Batzel v. Smith

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Batzel v. Smith
* **Citation or Docket:** 333 F.3d 1018 (Ninth Circuit, June 24, 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-18
* **Question Presented:** Whether denial of a California anti-SLAPP motion is immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine; if review is authorized, whether §230 protection for republication of a third-party email depends on the sender’s actual intent or the moderator’s reasonable perception of intended publication.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit accepted interlocutory jurisdiction, construed §230 to require a reasonable perception that the email was supplied for Internet publication, and remanded the immunity issue. A separate appeal addressed a sponsor’s final summary judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** The moderator seeks protection from the defamation suit under §230 and California procedure; Batzel disputes immediate jurisdiction and the claimed immunity.
* **Material Record:** A handyman emailed allegations connecting Batzel’s artwork to Nazi theft to a museum-security website operator, who circulated the message. Batzel disputes the allegations. The sender later said he did not intend publication. Whether the recipient reasonably perceived permission to publish is disputed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal from denial of the special motion was interlocutory. A state statute’s permission to appeal does not itself establish federal appellate jurisdiction. The separate final judgment concerning Mosler cannot bootstrap independent claims into review. Falsity, intent, and reasonable belief must not be treated as found merely because the case describes the allegations.
* **Relevant Dates:** Email and publication in 1999; circuit judgment June 24, 2003; assigned event March 18, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1291 and the narrow collateral-order doctrine govern this federal appeal; California Code of Civil Procedure §425.16 supplies the special motion. Section 1292(b) certification is a distinct route, not presumed here.

### Issue 1: Immediate appellate jurisdiction

* **Controlling Law:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. applies cumulative conclusiveness, separateness, importance, and effective-unreviewability requirements; litigation expense or a claimed right to avoid trial is not enough. Swint v. Chambers County Commission rejects immediate review of an ordinary liability defense and independent pendent review merely for convenience.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess the category of order under federal finality law. Every collateral-order condition must be met; the existence of a substantive defense and state appeal permission are separate from federal jurisdiction.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1291 and 1292; the California special motion and its probability-of-success inquiry.
* **Material Other Authority:** Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. supplies the narrow exception. Recognized immunity appeals do not automatically extend to every anti-litigation protection.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law disallows appeals justified only by irretrievable trial burdens. It has not categorically adjudicated this particular state special motion.
* **Open Point:** Whether this anti-SLAPP denial satisfies every federal collateral-order requirement.

### Issue 2: Material supplied by another information-content provider

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted holding conclusively resolves the meaning of information provided by another under §230 for an email not actually intended for publication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** If jurisdiction exists, separate service-provider or user status, another person’s creation or development of the challenged content, treatment as publisher or speaker, and whether that content was provided within the statute’s meaning.
* **Other Governing Law:** 47 U.S.C. §230(c)(1), (e) and (f), as in force at the publication; applicable defamation law remains subject to the statute’s actual reach.
* **Material Other Authority:** The circuit’s competing constructions are persuasive arguments within the matter, not adopted governing law. Editorial selection and independent creation of defamatory content are materially different activities.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The enacted protection does not make every Internet speaker immune from the speaker’s own content. The intent or reasonable-perception question remains unresolved in the adopted law.
* **Open Point:** Whether actual intended publication, reasonable perceived publication, or a broader supplied-content construction governs this email, if the Court may reach it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The moderator invokes the anti-SLAPP protection from litigation and argues that a reasonable belief in intended publication suffices under §230. Batzel argues that the appeal is premature and that an unintended private communication was not provided for public dissemination. The circuit treated the special motion as an immediately reviewable immunity and adopted the reasonable-perception construction. A broader reading would eliminate any publication-intent condition; an actual-intent reading would allocate the sender’s misunderstanding differently. Those merits alternatives cannot supply missing appellate jurisdiction.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacate the interlocutory merits disposition and require dismissal if federal finality is absent. If jurisdiction exists, affirm or revise the §230 construction and remand for any disputed predicate facts; none of those paths permits fact-finding about the alleged theft in this Court. Preserve any genuinely independent final sponsor judgment outside the granted issues.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The jurisdiction rule can govern this category of anti-SLAPP denial without resolving all specialized appeal statutes. Any merits rule must distinguish ordinary editing from creating content and state who bears the actual protection predicates.

## E. SOURCES

* Batzel v. Smith, 333 F.3d 1018 (Ninth Circuit, June 24, 2003).

## 58. South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe of Indians

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe of Indians
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-626; 541 U.S. 95.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-23
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Clean Water Act requires a discharge permit for a pump conveying pollutant-containing water when the pollutants did not originate in the pump, including the material distinction between transfer within one water body and addition to another.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment requiring a permit for the S-9 pumping station, treating the C-11 canal and WCA-3 impoundment as distinct waters.
* **Relief Requested:** The water district seeks reversal of the permit requirement; the Tribe and environmental plaintiff seek its preservation.
* **Material Record:** S-9 pumps canal water containing phosphorus into the Everglades conservation area. The parties dispute whether the canal and conservation area are meaningfully separate despite hydrological connections. The pump conveys, rather than generates, the pollutants.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant centers on whether pollutants must originate in the point source. The hydrological premise was litigated below, but the permissible scope of further review is disputed. The Government’s broader all-waters-as-one theory was not developed below in its present form and must not be adopted as an uncontested agency position.
* **Relevant Dates:** The district and circuit permit rulings precede the March 23, 2004 event. Later water-transfer rules or cases are not governing law here.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Deaton addresses the geographic reach of covered waters; Environmental Defense Center, Inc. v. United States Environmental Protection Agency addresses municipal stormwater permit conditions. This case concerns an addition through a pump and whether its source and receiving waters are distinct.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** no earlier Deaton holding is relied on. Any effective jurisdictional holding must be checked if provided; its existence is not inferred from the earlier calendar position.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment requires absence of a genuine material factual dispute. A remand must identify that dispute rather than assume facts to avoid deciding the statutory point-source issue.

### Issue 1: Conveyance, addition and distinct waters

* **Controlling Law:** Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers holds that equipment actively conveying and depositing pollutants can make a statutory discharge even if the material was not imported from offsite; mere movement or hydrological harm is insufficient. Arkansas v. Oklahoma recognizes interconnected water-quality obligations. Neither determines whether this pump transfers pollutants between distinct receiving and source waters.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a pollutant, an addition to covered waters, and a point-source conveyance. The components are distinct; proving a conveyance does not automatically prove an addition between distinct receiving and source waters.
* **Other Governing Law:** 33 U.S.C. §§1311(a), 1342, and 1362(6), (7), (12), (14); relevant state water-allocation preservation in §1251(g), and the statutory distinction between point and nonpoint controls.
* **Material Other Authority:** The definition includes pipes, ditches and conduits, supporting transport as a point-source function. Existing intake-credit rules and waterbody-specific standards bear on the Government’s unitary construction but do not substitute for its proper presentation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory definitions govern the source question. Whether two hydrologically connected areas are meaningfully distinct requires facts; the Act does not make map labels decisive.
* **Open Point:** Whether conveyance alone can be the point-source function, whether the undisputed record establishes distinct waters, and whether the broader unitary theory is properly ripe for decision.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district argues that a point source must add pollutants of its own and that the water areas are one connected system. The plaintiffs argue that pumping introduces canal pollution into a distinct receiving area. The United States proposes that all navigable waters be treated as one for the addition inquiry, a broader construction that could eliminate the permit requirement even for transfers between otherwise distinct waters. It also supports the district’s factual connection argument. The circuit’s judgment rests on a contrary distinct-water premise.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the origin requirement and affirm if distinct waters and addition are conclusively established within the proper review scope. Reject that requirement but vacate summary judgment if a material hydrological dispute remains. Accepting the Government’s broader theory could dispose of the permit claim, but would require resolving its preservation, textual and regulatory difficulties. A source-only disposition would leave other issues to ordinary proceedings without deciding them.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A conveyance rule reaches pumps moving existing pollutants without extending permitting to movement entirely within the same water body. A distinct-waters remand need not decide whether all transfers between covered waters are exempt as a matter of law.

* South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe of Indians, No. 02-626; 541 U.S. 95.

## 59. United States v. Galletti

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Galletti
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1389; 541 U.S. 114.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-23
* **Question Presented:** Whether a timely assessment of a partnership’s unpaid employment tax preserves the federal collection period against general partners liable for that same debt under state law, without separate assessments against each partner.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit sustained rejection of the Government’s bankruptcy claims because the general partners were not individually assessed within the assessment period.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal and consideration of its claims against the partners; the debtors seek their disallowance as untimely.
* **Material Record:** A partnership failed to pay employment taxes for periods from 1992 to 1995. The IRS timely assessed the partnership. General partners later entered Chapter 13, and the IRS asserted the unpaid partnership debt in their proceedings based on California partnership liability.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The assessment of the tax, the existence of state-law partner liability, and the lawful collection mechanism are different questions. This appeal does not establish that every partner owes every partnership obligation or that every administrative collection prerequisite is satisfied.
* **Relevant Dates:** The underlying employment-tax periods and assessment dates control limitations; the scheduled event is March 23, 2004. The later bankruptcy filing does not require a second assessment merely by changing the enforcement forum.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Government’s proofs of claim must satisfy applicable bankruptcy procedure and substantive liability requirements. The granted limitations issue is a legal question, distinct from unresolved claim defenses.

### Issue 1: Assessment of a tax and collection of the same debt

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted holding resolves this partnership-assessment issue. The enacted assessment provisions control.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the taxpayer whose obligation was assessed, the amount and timeliness of the assessment, and the independent law making the collection target liable for that debt. Do not confuse a new tax liability with another person’s responsibility for an existing debt.
* **Other Governing Law:** 26 U.S.C. §§6201, 6501(a) and 6502(a); applicable California general-partnership liability; Bankruptcy Code claim procedures. Section 6502 supplies the collection period for a timely assessed tax.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Updike illustrates the distinction between the assessed obligation and persons answerable for it, subject to the actual statutory provisions. Separate assessment provisions for different liabilities cannot be generalized beyond their terms.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Code distinguishes assessment from collection. State law may establish derivative responsibility without creating a separate federal tax for every liable person.
* **Open Point:** Whether the assessment-period language nevertheless requires a second partner-specific assessment before collecting the same timely assessed partnership tax.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The partners argue that each is a taxpayer who must receive a timely separate assessment before the longer collection period applies. The United States distinguishes assessment of the partnership’s employment tax from collection of the resulting debt from persons already liable under state law. The lower court treated the partners as separate required assessment targets. The Government’s position requires proof of actual partner liability and does not dispense with independent collection procedures.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if one timely assessment starts the collection period for the same debt and the partners’ liability arises independently. Affirm if the Code requires individual assessments for this liability. A reversal must leave disputed state-law liability, amount, and procedural defenses for proper adjudication rather than order automatic payment.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The interpretive rule can cover collection of a partnership’s timely assessed employment-tax debt from liable general partners. It need not govern unrelated federal penalties, personal taxes, transferee assessment procedures, or every levy requirement.

## 60. Nixon v. Missouri Municipal League

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nixon v. Missouri Municipal League
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 02-1238, 02-1386 and 02-1405; 541 U.S. 125.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-24
* **Question Presented:** Whether 47 U.S.C. §253 authorizes preemption of Missouri’s restriction on its political subdivisions’ provision of telecommunications services by protecting the ability of any entity to enter that market.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit reversed the FCC’s refusal to preempt the state restriction, reading the statutory entity language to include municipalities.
* **Relief Requested:** Missouri, the FCC and the carrier petitioner seek reversal; the municipal respondents seek a federal order displacing the state restriction.
* **Material Record:** Missouri restricted political subdivisions’ provision or sale of telecommunications services. Municipal entities asked the FCC to preempt the restriction under §253. The dispute concerns the State’s allocation of authority to its own subdivisions, not merely ordinary market regulation of independent private providers.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The three petitions concern one circuit judgment and form one listed matter. Existing local authority, a later withdrawal of that authority, and federal creation of local capacity are distinct possible descriptions; none may be assumed to erase the state-allocation question.
* **Relevant Dates:** The governing provision is from the Telecommunications Act of 1996; argument January 12, 2004; scheduled event March 24, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The three consolidated dockets remain one case-list entry; no separate briefs are created.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Interpret the scope of §253’s preemption authorization before determining whether this particular state restriction is displaced. A policy favoring competition cannot alone expand the authorized object of preemption.

### Issue 1: Preemption and a State’s distribution of local authority

* **Controlling Law:** New York v. United States preserves the distinction between regulating activity under a valid federal power and directing a State’s governmental choices. The telecommunications decisions recognize federal statutory regulation within its authorized sphere, not a general power to reallocate state municipal authority through ambiguous text.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the claimed preemption in statutory context and identify whether it regulates independent activity or changes the State’s assignment of governmental power. A serious alteration of that assignment requires a sufficiently clear congressional direction.
* **Other Governing Law:** 47 U.S.C. §253(a) and (d), including the prohibition-effect language; Missouri’s restriction and underlying municipal authorization law.
* **Material Other Authority:** Gregory v. Ashcroft supplies the received clear-statement principle for important state governmental arrangements. AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board addresses the FCC’s actual delegated regulatory field; its authority does not answer the distinct municipal-capacity issue.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Congress’s ordinary power to preempt state market barriers is not disputed at large. The question is the meaning of the particular federal authorization when the putative provider is the State’s own subdivision.
* **Open Point:** Whether the entity language clearly reaches this state decision about municipal entry, including respondents’ distinction between withholding and withdrawing local authority.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The municipalities argue that any entity naturally includes them and that targeted withdrawal of existing authority is a barrier to competition. The State and federal petitioners argue that federal removal of the restriction would alter the State’s allocation of powers without a clear direction and might confer no coherent affirmative capacity. The carrier supports the FCC’s construction. The circuit accepted the broad entity reading. A narrower municipal theory would protect existing authority from targeted withdrawal while allowing general allocation rules, but must account for the statute’s effects language.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the statute does not sufficiently authorize preemption of state control over municipal service authority. Affirm if its language clearly includes municipal providers and produces a workable preemption rule, then determine the resulting state-law capacity rather than inventing it. Constitutional power need not be resolved if statutory scope disposes of the case.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A statutory holding may preserve state decisions about their subdivisions while retaining §253’s force against ordinary market barriers. It need not decide a hypothetical statute expressly directing a different federal-state allocation.

## 61. California ex rel. Lockyer v. FERC

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** California ex rel. Lockyer v. FERC
* **Citation or Docket:** 329 F.3d 700 (Ninth Circuit, May 15, 2003); review of FERC proceedings EC01-49-002 and EC01-41-002.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-29
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Power Act permits FERC to approve a utility-affiliate reorganization after abbreviated notice and without an evidentiary hearing when it subsequently considers the objectors’ arguments on rehearing.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit denied review of FERC’s reorganization approvals, sustaining the notice, hearing opportunity, rehearing consideration, and public-interest determination.
* **Relief Requested:** California and the public power agency seek to set aside the approvals or obtain proper agency proceedings; FERC and the utility affiliates seek affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Utility affiliates sought rapid approval of reincorporation and upstream ownership changes during the California energy crisis. Published comment windows were seven and four days. The transactions closed after approval. Objectors alleged shielding of assets from creditors. FERC later considered their submissions and allowed late intervention in one proceeding.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The State’s statutory hearing claim and the creditor’s due-process theory are distinct. A transaction’s completion does not automatically establish either mootness or an adequate cure. Material disputed facts requiring a hearing must be identified, rather than assumed from disagreement with the agency’s policy balance.
* **Relevant Dates:** Applications December 2000; approvals January 12, 2001; substantive reconsideration in February 2001 and January 2002; circuit judgment May 15, 2003; assigned event March 29, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal Power Act §§203 and 313 govern approval and rehearing. The APA requires lawful procedure and reasoned, record-supported agency action; review also accounts for prejudicial error. The Act’s hearing language is not automatically a command for a trial-type hearing in every application.

### Issue 1: Meaningful notice, hearing and agency reconsideration

* **Controlling Law:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. permits recognition of an interpretation actually embodied in the agency’s decision, not invention of a new ground by counsel or the reviewing court. Arkansas v. Oklahoma requires review of supported agency findings on the record as a whole rather than substitution of a court’s preferred policy.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the notice owed, the opportunity actually provided, the relevant disputed facts, and the agency’s own responsive consideration. A claim of cure requires a meaningful chance to affect a decision the agency can still reconsider, not merely receipt of objections after the result is fixed.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Power Act §§203 and 313; 5 U.S.C. §706; Fifth Amendment where an actual protected interest is implicated; contemporaneous FERC intervention and rehearing rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mathews v. Eldridge supplies the received functional procedural inquiry. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. requires notice reasonably suited to the affected interests; neither a general reference to urgency nor a formal rehearing label answers all procedural requirements.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The governing principles allow procedures fitted to actual issues and require agency reasons. They do not establish that four-day notice is always sufficient or that rehearing always cures any deprivation.
* **Open Point:** Whether this notice and complete administrative process afforded meaningful consideration without a material unresolved factual issue requiring an evidentiary hearing.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The objectors argue that the compressed holiday-period notice and immediate closing prevented effective opposition to asset shielding. FERC argues that the notices identified the transactions, that emergency financing concerns justified speed, and that the agency fully considered the later submissions. The utility affiliates invoke the need for financing and generation investment. The circuit accepted both initial adequacy and subsequent cure. The strongest limiting inquiry is whether objectors identify a material factual dispute or lost procedural opportunity the later process could not remedy.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the notices conveyed the essential proposed transactions and the record establishes a meaningful opportunity to present material objections, without a need for trial-type fact resolution. Vacate or remand if initial deficiencies caused a concrete lost opportunity that reconsideration could not restore. Mere assertion that the agency would choose the same result is not a sufficient cure finding.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific procedural holding can sustain this expedited approval without a categorical short-notice safe harbor. It must preserve actual statutory notice rights, necessary fact hearings, and the requirement of agency reconsideration capable of changing the outcome.

## 62. United States v. Flores-Montano

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Flores-Montano
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1794; 541 U.S. 149.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.

## 70. Vieth v. Jubelirer

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Vieth v. Jubelirer

* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-04-28
* **Question Presented:** Whether partisan congressional-districting claims are justiciable, whether subordinating neutral criteria to partisan advantage presumptively violates equal protection, and whether a projected supermajority of seats without a statewide vote majority violates Article I.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must identify personal district-related injury and a live claim to Act 34. * **Relevant Dates:** The challenged maps followed the 2000 census; the final lower disposition was entered January 24, 2003.

### Issue 1: Justiciability and personal partisan injury

A categorical political-question ruling would dismiss all such constitutional claims, leaving distinct racial, population and statutory protections intact. No path supports an immediate Court-drawn map on the present factual record.

## 71. Martinez v. Bush

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Martinez v. Bush
* **Citation or Docket:** 234 F. Supp. 2d 1275 (S.D. Fla. 2002); Nos. 02-20244-CIV-JORDAN and 02-10028-CIV-JORDAN.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-04-29
* **Question Presented:** Whether Florida’s redistricting process and plans unlawfully excluded racial minorities, diluted protected voters’ electoral opportunity under §2, or imposed unconstitutional partisan entrenchment.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Three-judge Southern District of Florida, entering judgment for the defendants on process, racial-dilution and partisan-gerrymandering claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Challengers seek reversal and declaratory and injunctive correction of the relevant plans and practices.
* **Material Record:** The court conducted extensive factfinding on participation, voting behavior and district performance. It found racial polarization but anticipated continued success for Black voters’ preferred candidates in the challenged districts. Partisan purpose was established; the court found the claimed effect insufficient and imported Gingles predicates into partisan analysis.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Claims and plaintiffs must retain their own injury and proof. Findings receive ordinary clear-error review; the legal tests do not. The historic lower court’s separate treatment of Title VI is reviewed under the governing Title VI law stated for Issue 1.
* **Relevant Dates:** Lower-court opinion: December 3, 2002. The April 29, 2004 date follows Vieth by one day.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Vieth concerns Pennsylvania and a dismissal record; Martinez includes Florida-specific trial findings and independent racial and statutory issues.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Intentional exclusion from the redistricting process

* **Controlling Law:** Powell v. Ridge recognizes, and Alexander v. Sandoval applies, only a bounded private action for declaratory and confined prospective relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, applicable Title VI §602 disparate-impact regulation; Sandoval assumes the regulation's validity without deciding it and preserves agency enforcement and the §601 intent action. Powell and South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection hold that §1983 supplies no regulation-created effects right; South Camden applies Powell's prospective action to valid EPA §602 regulations and remands the direct claim together with a preserved §601 intent claim. No preserved §602 regulatory claim is identified here, so the pleaded intentional-process claim is the only private Title VI route before the Court, and it requires proof of purpose. Shaw v. Reno and Miller v. Johnson do not make racial design alone a constitutional injury.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under Washington v. Davis and Village of Arlington Heights, challengers must prove racial purpose as a motivating factor by a preponderance; supported fact findings receive clear-error review. §1983 supplies a vehicle, not a new substantive effects right.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; Title VI §601; 42 U.S.C. §1983. No particular preserved §602 regulatory claim is identified in this record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Crawford-El v. Britton rejects an invented heightened burden for unconstitutional motive; the ordinary proof standard applies.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The pleaded intentional-discrimination branch requires purpose; no preserved §602 effects claim under Powell's bounded prospective action, and no §1983 effects route under Powell and South Camden, is available here as an alternative to that proof.
* **Open Point:** Whether the purpose finding is clearly erroneous; no new intent rule is required.

### Issue 2: Section 2 electoral opportunity

* **Controlling Law:** Voinovich v. Quilter, Johnson v. De Grandy and Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District require source-specific proof of unequal electoral opportunity and permit rigorously supported crossover opportunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Establish the supported alternative-district, cohesion and bloc-voting predicates and assess the complete circumstances; neither population percentage nor proportionality alone decides equal opportunity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act §2 as then effective.
* **Material Other Authority:** Thornburg v. Gingles supplies the retained threshold framework, as developed by the cases.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Actual opportunity is governed, without an entitlement to maximized minority-held seats.
* **Open Point:** Whether the findings establish unequal opportunity under that framework.

### Issue 3: Conditional application of the proposed partisan rules

* **Controlling Law:** Davis v. Bandemer supplies surviving justiciability and a fractured merits formulation. Any Vieth judgment effective before this case governs at its actual holding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep partisan constitutional injury distinct from racial-dilution statutory prerequisites. A pending decision supplies no controlling rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment and 28 U.S.C. §§1253 and 2284.
* **Material Other Authority:** Baker v. Carr governs the political-question inquiry.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law supplies a justiciable category without mechanically transferring every Gingles predicate.
* **Open Point:** The actual Vieth rule and its application to this trial record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The court rejected discriminatory-process intent, found functioning minority electoral opportunity and rejected partisan injury under Bandemer reinforced by Gingles requirements.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Challengers argue that practical exclusion, packing and fragmenting voters cannot be cured by aggregate district totals or partisan labels.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Officials rely on participation opportunities, record-supported district performance and the distinction between political advantage and constitutionally unequal opportunity.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** An otherwise sustainable §2 judgment does not necessarily sustain the partisan branch; each branch has a separate rule and remedy.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No concession beyond the judicial record’s treatment of undisputed racial-voting predicates is represented as an oral-argument concession.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Supported nondiscrimination and opportunity findings may sustain the racial branches. A legal error on partisan injury permits a branch-specific remand without reopening those findings. If an actual effective Vieth holding forecloses that category, the partisan disposition must follow it. A finding of a particular racial violation requires a fitted map or process remedy, not maximization of minority officeholding.

* **Record and Legal Uncertainty:** The partisan remand depends on the governing rule actually adopted in Vieth; the proposed position in that case is not itself a judgment.
