## 82. Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1689; 541 U.S. 567.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-17
* **Question Presented:** Whether a partnership’s post-filing change in membership cures the absence of complete diversity at filing when the same partnership remains a party.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, allowing the post-filing citizenship change to cure the defect and preserve the litigation outcome.
* **Relief Requested:** Grupo Dataflux seeks dismissal for want of jurisdiction; Atlas seeks preservation of its favorable jury verdict.
* **Material Record:** Atlas, a Texas limited partnership, had Mexican partners when it sued a Mexican corporation. Those partners left before trial. Atlas remained the plaintiff throughout; the departure changed its citizenship rather than dismissing a separate party. Grupo raised jurisdiction after the verdict and before judgment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Subject-matter jurisdiction cannot be created by delay, consent or litigation expense. Distinguish changing a continuing party’s citizenship from dropping a dispensable party.
* **Relevant Dates:** Citizenship at the action’s commencement is the disputed measuring point. The departures occurred before trial, but after filing.

### Issue 1: Time of filing and the limited cure doctrine

* **Controlling Law:** Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis permits preservation of a judgment after lawful dismissal of a nondiverse party produces complete diversity before trial and judgment. It does not authorize judgment where the jurisdictional defect remains or make efficiency a source of jurisdiction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify citizenship of each actual party at the legally relevant time and distinguish a recognized party-dismissal cure from a later change in an existing party’s citizenship.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1332 and 1653; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 21.
* **Material Other Authority:** Carden v. Arkoma Associates attributes all partners’ citizenship to a partnership. Newman-Green, Inc. v. Alfonzo-Larrain concerns dismissal of a dispensable party, not relabeling citizenship.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The general commencement rule and a bounded party-dismissal exception coexist; the present facts test an extension beyond that exception.
* **Open Point:** Whether partnership membership changes fall within the cure rule despite the same entity’s continuous party status.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Fifth Circuit extended Caterpillar’s finality rationale because complete diversity existed before the trial.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Grupo argues that the original lack of jurisdiction cannot be cured by the continuing plaintiff’s new citizenship.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Atlas argues that actual pretrial diversity removes the federalism concern and that repeating completed litigation serves no jurisdictional purpose.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** A properly authorized dismissal of a dispensable actual party is distinct; §1653 can cure defective allegations, not change the underlying jurisdictional facts at filing.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Enforcing the commencement rule reverses and requires dismissal without prejudice to a properly grounded action. Extending the cure doctrine preserves the verdict only after establishing the proposed exception and current diversity. Neither party’s tactical timing alone answers a statutory power question.
