* **Question Presented:** Whether a timely Equal Access to Justice Act fee application may be amended after the filing period to add the omitted allegation that the Government’s position lacked substantial justification.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Relevant Dates:** The amendment followed expiration of the statutory thirty-day application period; the initial application preceded it.

### Issue 1: Curable pleading omission in a timely fee request

* **Controlling Law:** Becker v. Montgomery allows correction of an omitted signature on a timely appeal notice. Edelman v. Lynchburg College permits a later verification to relate back to a timely discrimination charge. Neither eliminates substantive eligibility or every statutory deadline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate timely initiation and adequate identification from a curable formal requirement. Relation back may cure a defect without enlarging the underlying jurisdiction or waiving a required merits showing.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2412(d)(1)(A), (B); the Government bears the burden of establishing substantial justification once a proper application invokes the fee procedure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pierce v. Underwood defines substantial justification; Rule 15’s relation-back principle is informative without treating a fee application as an ordinary complaint.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The correction precedents reject automatic forfeiture for formal omissions but require interpretation of this particular fee statute.
* **Open Point:** Whether this omitted allegation is an incurable condition or a curable pleading requirement.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit read the deadline and contents together and distinguished cure rules involving different provisions or filings by laypersons.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The Government had timely notice of the identified fee claim; adding a required allegation imposes no new claim or unfair surprise.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The Secretary argues that all statutory application contents must arrive within the same thirty days and that waiver of sovereign immunity demands strict compliance.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** The Government may still defeat fees on substantial justification, statutory ineligibility, unreasonable amount or another properly established defense.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States, through the Secretary, defends the filing requirement as a bar to the amended request.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Permitting cure reverses the dismissal and returns the fee merits to the veterans court. Treating completeness as a time-limited prerequisite affirms. A cure rule can remain confined to an identified timely application without deciding equitable tolling or entirely missing applications.
