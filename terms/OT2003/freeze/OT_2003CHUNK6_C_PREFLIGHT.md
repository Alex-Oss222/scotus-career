## 83. Sabri v. United States

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling of the facial constitutional challenge and the legal consequences of the available statutory constructions. A future application outside the claimed program relationship requires its own facts; no guilt or missing trial element is adjudicated by this preflight.

**Record supported by published recitation:** The indictment alleges approximately $28.8 million in federal funds to Minneapolis and approximately $23 million to the Minneapolis Community Development Agency in 2001. The agency funds the neighborhood revitalization program. Herron was a city council member and a commissioner overseeing the development agency's budget. The alleged transactions are a $5,000 payment for regulatory assistance, a $10,000 offer to induce an eminent-domain threat against owners, and an $80,000 proposed kickback for assistance obtaining $800,000 in development grants. These are allegations in a dismissed pretrial indictment, not findings of criminal liability. The statutory transaction amount and the amount of a bribe are distinct.

**Law correction and limits:** Salinas unanimously rejects a demonstrable effect on federal dollars as an additional element, but separately preserves an unresolved statutory connection-to-expenditure question and constitutional outer limits. Fischer supplies the programmatic-benefits boundary and preserves the distinct exceptions and offense elements. Neither decision has already decided Sabri's constitutional question. Section 666(c)'s bona fide salary, wages, fees, compensation, and ordinary-course expense exception remains operative. The relevant annual-benefits period is continuous and may begin up to twelve months before, or end up to twelve months after, the offense; it may span the offense. More than $10,000 in qualifying benefits and a covered transaction involving at least $5,000 are separate conditions.

The Eighth Circuit's primary distinction is between direct criminal protection of a federal spending program and an accepted condition on a recipient's grant. Its majority rejected the condition-only theory and sustained the prohibition under Spending plus Necessary and Proper powers. Its dissent accepted the rational connection argument as substantial but objected to the absence of a proper federal limit and to protection of local governmental integrity untethered to an identified federal interest. The neutral packet should not reduce the dissent to a demand to trace stolen federal dollars.

Lopez and Morrison require a constitutionally identifiable connection and forbid downstream costs from creating Commerce authority. Home Builders and Rancho Viejo recognize necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime when the regulated class has a genuine functional relationship to it. Those holdings do not transform Commerce requirements into a universal spending test, and they do not establish that every federal payment creates unlimited power over a recipient's affairs. Kansas and Dole govern actual funding conditions and genuine recipient choice; a private briber is not simply a consenting grant recipient. The source of power, the fit of the chosen means, the statutory elements, and any claimed structural prohibition require distinct analysis.

**Supported paths:** Uphold the institutional protection without a dollar-tracing element; reject the claimed fit and determine the legal consequence for this indictment; or evaluate a fairly available limiting construction within Salinas's still-open statutory reservation. A construction may not insert a new element into unambiguous language merely to sustain an offense. No choice among these paths is made here.

**Source:** United States v. Sabri, 326 F.3d 937 (8th Cir. 2003), including majority, dissent, and notes, read in full. ## 84. Benjamin v. Fraser

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling with the admitted Rule 53 source and its transition. The existing record permits assessment of the longstanding appointment, the actual monitoring functions, and the appealed orders without assuming a new appointment after the amendment.

**Current law:** Benjamin v. Jacobson requires written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing federal-right violation and need, narrowness, and least intrusiveness for continued prospective duties. A remedy may extend beyond the minimum substantive conduct rule when the statutory findings support that reach. Neither the decrees' age nor the absence of ritual wording determines continuation. Farmer requires actual awareness and unreasonable nonresponse for the convicted-prisoner Eighth Amendment claim it decided; obviousness permits, but does not compel, the inference of awareness. Farmer also permits consideration of present knowledge for prospective relief without retroactively creating damages liability. County of Sacramento v. Lewis does not make its rapid-pursuit harm-purpose standard universal; actual opportunity for deliberation and the custodial setting matter. Helling preserves protection against established serious future harm. These are the controlling versions; the lower opinion's historical Supreme Court quotations do not replace them.

**Additional material lower-court grounds:** The lower opinion independently concluded that the pre-PLRA appointment of the continuously existing OCC was not a post-enactment appointment governed by the special-master provisions; renewal, adjustment, and continuation were distinguished from a new appointment. It also concluded that the actual need-narrowness-intrusiveness findings adequately supported the OCC even if monitoring constituted prospective relief. Neither point appears fully in the supplied neutral summary. Both must be preserved as potentially sufficient grounds rather than silently deciding every monitoring question through functional classification alone.

The OCC was created in 1982, renewed by consent through 1987, and thereafter continued through approximately biannual orders. The City has funded it since inception. It observes, gathers information, informally assists compliance and dispute resolution, and reports to the court. It was not appointed to hold evidentiary hearings, subpoena or swear witnesses, rule on evidence, or adjudicate discrete legal and factual disputes. Its reports were not formally filed as master reports or accorded legally prescribed deference. This describes the actual delegation; it does not establish that every nonbinding or investigative appointee must fall outside the event-effective rule.

**Rule 53 transition:** Rule 53 was extensively amended effective December 1, 2003. Its actual 2003 text permits specified pretrial and posttrial appointments, expressly contemplates investigation or enforcement duties, and generally requires de novo review of objections to factual findings unless an approved stipulation selects the specified different treatment. It begins with the qualification that another statute may provide otherwise. Thus the September 2003 lower opinion's account of the former rule cannot be treated as a complete description of all masters in May 2004. The March 27, 2003 adopting order applies the amendments to later-commenced civil cases and, insofar as just and practicable, proceedings then pending. Control is responsible for source admission and checking existing completed dependencies. This preparation creates neither a new OCC appointment nor a ruling on the amendment's application to the existing delegation or the PLRA definition.

**Facility and remedy precision:** The appealed judgment preserves findings and remedies in part and vacates others; it is not a unitary reinstatement of every decree term. The Second Circuit vacated the six-foot bed-spacing remedy for inadequate support of actual or imminent substantial harm; vacated the twenty-foot-candle requirement because the lower reasoning did not clearly distinguish actual deficient lighting from a supposed constitutional numerical minimum; and vacated the finding of food-service adequacy at the Anna M. Kross Center, Adolescent Reception and Detention Center, and George Motchan Detention Center for particularized reconsideration. It directed consideration of substituting the agreed monthly airflow spot-check alternative for annual balancing. The parties' alternative involved 15 percent of housing and intake areas, not a finding that every facility met that condition.

The lower judgment sustained the operational-window repair requirement, October 15 heating-system certification, and quarterly bleach power washing on their particular records. It sustained termination regarding isolated hot-water problems and the residential vermin findings, including the supported pest-management response. Lower-court findings of inadequate ventilation, lighting, and temperature were institution-specific; no system-wide presumption replaces them. These details matter if the Court's disposition reaches component remedies. The supplied grant controls the issues; the entire lower appeal need not be treated as a separately enlarged grant.

**Supported paths:** Evaluate the pretrial institutional culpability rule separately from current violation and remedial fit; examine whether current notice and prolonged nonresponse independently satisfy any knowledge requirement actually adopted; decide OCC classification or an independently sufficient temporal ground; preserve or remand particular relief under the actual statutory findings. A negligence-only constitutional rule, automatic renewal of all decree duties, and a categorical premise that nonbinding activity can never be a master's function are not established entering law.

Fraser, 343 F.3d 35 (2d Cir. 2003), read in full including notes and cross-appeal treatment, saved as `runtime/preflight-sources/benjamin_fraser_343_f3d_35_lower_opinion.txt`, with OpenJurist and the Caselaw Access Project-derived reporter text cross-checked. ## 86. Nelson v. Campbell

**Readiness:** Ready for modeling the channel question and a remedy preserving the undecided medical, exhaustion, and equitable questions. The present materials do not independently establish a basis to grant or deny a new execution stay on its merits.

The neutral packet's statement that no effective Muhammad judgment was supplied is stale. Muhammad v. Close, decided February 25, 2004, is completed public current-term law and appears in the slice. It unanimously applies the actual-consequence comparison: a prison-related civil claim does not require favorable invalidation when success leaves the pertinent custody determination valid. Randell's unanimous compatible-claim rule also enters. Randell's separate former-prisoner lost-opportunity exception does not decide this in-custody claim. Preiser, Heck, and Edwards retain their distinct release and necessary-invalidity boundaries.

The controlled record establishes dismissal at the pleading stage. The compromised veins, risks of the proposed cut-down, and feasible alternative access are allegations supported as described by an anesthesiologist's affidavit, not adjudicated medical facts. Counsel sought information and consultation in August 2003; the described plan changed between September 10 and October 3; the action was filed the following Monday before the scheduled October 9 execution. Those supported dates prevent automatic characterization of every aspect of the claim as long available. They do not establish ultimate diligence or entitlement to equitable relief.

Success against the particular access procedure and an interim delay needed to adjudicate that claim are analytically distinct from invalidating the sentence or necessarily preventing every lawful implementation. Gomez permits a supported equitable-delay objection independently of a habeas bar; it does not create an automatic rule against every late-filed claim. No administrative-exhaustion finding is supplied. Missing research evidence is neither nonexhaustion nor litigant default. No independent historical Supreme Court stay order is admitted, no execution is presumed, and no earlier interim action is silently continued.

**Supported paths:** Treat the alleged procedure-specific claim as compatible with the death judgment and return it to the ordinary civil process; or identify, from the actual relief necessarily required, a contradiction of lawful implementation that puts it in habeas. Channel eligibility does not establish an Eighth Amendment violation, exhaustion, likelihood of success, or a right to a stay. Those questions retain their own proof, statutory, and equitable conditions.

## 87. Yarborough v. Alvarado

**Readiness:** Ready for the supplied Miranda custody and section 2254(d)(1) question. The precise day of the 1999 state merits decision is not necessary to select the established holdings identified here because none of the selected substantive custody holdings turns on a date within that year. If a modeling path introduces a potentially controlling 1999 holding, the exact adjudication date must first be obtained. No independent voluntariness or prejudice determination is silently added.

Thompson classifies ultimate custody as a legal or mixed question, but its former-statutory independent-review regime does not displace AEDPA. Terry Williams, Visciotti, Lockyer, and Mitchell preserve the separate statutory threshold, substantive-law date, complete supported record, and actual constitutional entitlement. A general principle may clearly establish governing law without a factual twin; mere independent disagreement with the state court remains insufficient. Current explanation of the federal review method does not create new substantive custody law for the state adjudication.

Stansbury's controlling formulation assesses objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee's position would understand them and excludes undisclosed subjective beliefs. It does not separately resolve the obligatory role of objectively apparent age. Age and uncommunicated personal police experience therefore require distinct treatment rather than a single subjective-characteristics category.

The controlled addendum supplies seventeen years and seven months as the age at the offense, the parents' transportation to the interview about a month later, approximately two hours with one detective in a small room, recorded questioning without warnings, challenges to initial denials, and two offered breaks near the end. The claimed request by the parents to attend and its refusal remain disputed, not established facts. He returned home afterward. His trial testimony that the interview was friendly and he felt uncoerced concerns supported subjective and voluntariness evidence; it is not a dispositive objective-custody finding. The criminal judgment was second-degree murder with fifteen years to life.

**Supported paths:** Find the state application within the range permitted by the established objective standard; or find that the full supported circumstances objectively required custody under that established law and then consider the independently required admission/prejudice consequences through the proper channel. A newly announced direct-review age rule cannot itself supply prior state error. Neither a disputed parental-exclusion fact nor hypothetical police experience may be invented to determine the custody outcome.
