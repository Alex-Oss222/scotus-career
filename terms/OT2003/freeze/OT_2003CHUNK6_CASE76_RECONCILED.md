## Operative rule, remedy and preserved limits

An alleged instructional ambiguity must be evaluated in the charge and trial context under Cupp, Boyde and Estelle. Habeas relief on a state-adjudicated claim additionally requires satisfaction of section 2254(d)(1); ordinary error or a plausible competing interpretation is insufficient. The state court could reasonably conclude that these repeated actual-but-unreasonable instructions and the express reasonable-person contrast prevented a reasonable likelihood of a constitutionally impermissible application. Its use of the prosecutor's defendant-favorable clarification did not treat counsel as empowered to replace a contrary operative judicial command.

The reversal removes this instructional ground for relief and restores the District Court's denial on that ground. Independently pending preserved claims retain their own requirements; the judgment orders no acquittal, release, retrial or evidentiary hearing. No Chapman or other harmlessness determination is necessary because the statutory gateway is unsatisfied. The different controlling habeas harmless-error rule therefore creates no material historical departure in this disposition.
