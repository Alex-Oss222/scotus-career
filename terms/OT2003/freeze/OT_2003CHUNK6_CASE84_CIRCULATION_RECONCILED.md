## Result and construction of the proposals

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the stated monitoring directions at the limited OCC scope they already support. These are actual issue-specific joins. Earlier silence does not establish a refusal, and a separate proposal does not cease to be common merely because its wording is more explicit than a frozen commitment.

Two phrases require precision. Requiring the City to **substantiate an effective alternative it proposes** is different from making every retained obligation survive unless the City disproves its necessity. Requiring a party defending an appointment to **produce a supported account of its actual delegated functions** is different from adopting an across-the-board allocation of the ultimate risk of uncertainty in every classification dispute. The supported joins below adopt the first formulation in each pair. The broader burden changes do not follow from the actual law or these circulation grounds.

## The burden distinctions are substantive

Under Jacobson, a section 3626(b)(2) movant must establish its predicate, and continued relief requires the court's written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing violation, necessity, narrowness and least intrusiveness. There is no rule that silence by the City itself establishes those findings. The six withhold a formulation that makes the City bear the entire ultimate burden of disproving continued statutory necessity or guarantees retention whenever its alternative fails. That would invert the preservation inquiry they all joined.

Rufo assigns an actual Rule 60(b)(5) modification movant its significant-change and suitable-tailoring burdens. Its operation can require the City to prove an asserted basis for modifying a valid obligation. Jacobson expressly preserves Rufo as a separate route and rejects importing it as an additional prerequisite to the PLRA termination route. Thus a court may require support for the City's asserted effective alternative without adding a Rufo threshold wherever the statute permits termination. The parties' full record, rather than a new universal burden presumption, must support the chosen remedy.

Likewise, the five OCC affirmers do not adopt a universal rule that an unexplained or uncertain appointment automatically escapes or triggers section 3626(f) depending on which party has nominally defended it. The affirmative proposal is to supply the actual powers, terms, practice and retained judicial responsibility needed for classification. Neither Rule 53 nor the supplied statute makes the office's title dispositive. A materially changed delegation requires assessment of its real functions. The existing record already supports the five Justices' classification and their independent continuous-appointment ground; this circulation does not manufacture a new factual deficit.
