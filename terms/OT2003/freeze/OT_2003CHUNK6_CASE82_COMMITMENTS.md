## Enhanced comparison

The commencement-rule path treats citizenship at filing as controlling for each continuing party. Carden attributes all partners' citizenship to the partnership; changing its membership changes the citizenship attributed to the same party. Newman-Green's dismissal of an actual dispensable party supplies a distinct cure. Caterpillar preserved a completed judgment after that kind of lawful party dismissal produced diversity before trial; its unanimous rule did not make efficiency or a later change of a continuing party's citizenship a new jurisdictional source. Section 1653 permits correction of defective allegations, not substitution of missing jurisdictional facts. Rule 21 needs an actual party to drop and cannot turn unjoined partners into parties for that purpose.

The extension path accepts that initial jurisdiction was absent and openly extends Caterpillar's cure principle to the actual pretrial departure of the only citizenship interests defeating diversity. Its affirmative basis is the interaction between Carden's attribution of members' citizenship and the established power to cure by removing the relevant nondiverse interest, together with Caterpillar's preservation of a trial conducted after complete diversity actually existed. It is not a claim that sunk expense alone creates jurisdiction or that the original defect was waived. The strongest objection is that attribution of citizenship does not make the partners litigants. The proposed extension treats their actual removal from the partnership before trial as sufficiently equivalent for this cure purpose; it does not purport to apply Rule 21 literally to a nonparty or amend the facts through section 1653.

Kontrick's distinction between statutory jurisdiction and forfeitable claim-processing rules prevents treating Grupo's timing as waiver. All eight agree on that distinction. No later current-term decision is needed to recognize that original statutory jurisdiction differs from a litigation deadline.
