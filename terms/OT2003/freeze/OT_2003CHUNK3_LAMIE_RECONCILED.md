## Result and shared statutory rule

All eight Associates affirm denial of the requested postconversion Chapter 7 estate compensation and directly accept the recipient-list rule. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also accept the complete shared sufficient textual-and-structural explanation below. Stevens agrees with that statutory construction and judgment, but his concurrence is qualified by the need to examine the asserted drafting error rather than dismiss legislative history as unnecessary. His support is not counted as an unqualified eighth vote that text alone is sufficient despite that plausible error. The initial all-eight full-rationale shorthand is corrected to preserve this genuine distinction.

Under amended section 330(a)(1), debtor's counsel in Chapter 7 cannot obtain an estate compensation award merely because counsel represented the debtor; the lawyer must qualify as a professional employed under section 327 with the necessary approval for this route. Lamie's acknowledged lack of such employment defeats the requested award. The retained reference to an attorney in the services clause describes compensable services of an eligible professional; it does not reinsert the deleted recipient. The missing conjunction and imperfect parallelism do not make that construction unworkable. The independently stated individual Chapter 12/13 authorization in section 330(a)(4)(B) remains distinct. A useful service does not itself establish authority to charge the estate.

The common controlling explanation examines the present list, its employment condition, the retained services term, the amendment and the competing drafting-error argument. Hartford Underwriters supplies the requirement of authorized claimant status before recovery, Patterson v. Shumate supports enforcement of the enacted bankruptcy provision, and Dewsnup is a provision-specific contextual construction rather than authority to insert a deleted recipient into this workable list. The result does not adjudicate the worth of the services. Chapter 7 trustee employment, lawful personal payment and the express Chapter 12/13 allowance explain why the resulting system is workable without authorizing this particular estate award.
