* **Case Name:** Nelson v. Campbell
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-6821; 541 U.S. 637.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-24
* **Question Presented:** Whether a condemned prisoner may use §1983 to challenge an allegedly unnecessary surgical venous-access procedure without necessarily challenging the lawfulness of execution by lethal injection.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, affirming dismissal of Nelson’s §1983 action as an unauthorized successive habeas petition.
* **Relief Requested:** Nelson seeks an injunction against the proposed cut-down procedure and a temporary stay needed to adjudicate that claim.
* **Material Record:** Alabama proposed an incision to obtain venous access because of Nelson’s compromised veins. He identified alternative access methods and did not seek an order forbidding all execution. He previously pursued federal habeas relief and brought this claim shortly before the scheduled execution.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The requested relief’s necessary effect, rather than the death-row setting, determines the proper channel. Timeliness and the equitable requirements for a stay are separate from the availability of §1983.
* **Relevant Dates:** The disputed access plan and emergency filing arose near the scheduled execution; the reasons for timing must be examined on the actual record.

### Issue 1: Procedure-specific challenge and the habeas boundary

* **Controlling Law:** Randell v. Johnson requires comparing the precise civil theory to the operative custody judgment and permits compatible claims without favorable invalidation. Heck v. Humphrey and Edwards v. Balisok retain the necessary-invalidity boundary for claims contradicting custody. Randell’s separate former-prisoner opportunity exception is not needed for this in-custody procedural challenge.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask whether success necessarily invalidates the sentence or prevents its lawful implementation, distinguishing the claim’s legal consequence from the temporary delay required to adjudicate it.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983; 28 U.S.C. §§2254 and 2244; the Eighth Amendment; applicable PLRA and equitable requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Preiser v. Rodriguez identifies the core habeas remedy. Gomez v. United States District Court recognizes that last-minute equitable tactics may affect a stay.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing channel rules address necessary invalidity; application to a severable venous-access procedure is open.
* **Open Point:** Whether this requested injunction necessarily defeats the death sentence or instead leaves lawful implementation available.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Eleventh Circuit treated the challenge and requested stay as a functional attack on the execution requiring successive-petition authorization.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Nelson argues that the specific surgical procedure is unnecessary and that other access methods permit the sentence to be carried out.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Alabama argues that the suit interferes with execution, must proceed through habeas and was brought too late for equitable relief.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Even a properly filed §1983 claim may fail on the medical merits, exhaustion or equitable grounds; those failures do not change its channel by label.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** If success leaves a lawful execution method available, reverse the habeas reclassification and consider §1983 and equitable requirements. If the requested relief necessarily prevents any lawful implementation, habeas restrictions apply. An appropriate temporary stay does not itself resolve that classification or establish an Eighth Amendment violation.
