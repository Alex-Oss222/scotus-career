## Decision on the proposed formulation

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer can join the proposed confined equal-protection rule, including proof of the necessary lawful comparison through supported methods other than production of a particular alternative map. This is a source-grounded circulation inference, not an assertion about an actual historical draft exchange. Their agreement retains a substantive comparison requirement: the evidence must establish a legally and practically feasible protected opportunity, independently justified from the claimant's desired partisan outcome, and materially connect its impairment to the challenged district assignments. A preferred seat count, a prior political success or an old map's existence does not by itself satisfy that requirement.

The answer has two distinct parts. Dispensing with a universally required drawing, map count, expert model or fixed factor list is already within the frozen permissible formulation and actual law. Permitting an independently sufficient causal comparison instead of invariably identifying a specific alternative district is a supported material accommodation to Souter's more specific frozen formulation and Ginsburg's join in it. Their revised commitments are expressly recorded below. The audit does not silently relabel that change as something they always said. Dispensing with a feasible lawful comparison altogether would remain incompatible with all four commitments.

Kennedy continues to affirm for the present claim's insufficiency. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas continue to affirm on their categorical ground. This audit produces four affirmative Associate joins at the stated scope, not five, and makes no aggregate calculation involving the Chief Justice. The broader five-Associate agreement against categorical foreclosure remains distinct from these four affirmative merits and remand commitments.

## What the accepted comparison requires

The proposed list of possible evidence identifies methods of proof, not independent safe harbors. A feasible alternative district is one way to demonstrate that a meaningful political opportunity can exist under lawful districting conditions and that the challenged assignment, rather than geography or lawful constraints, materially obstructs it. Actual political activity, the operation of a reassignment or another supported comparison can perform the same work when the evidence establishes those propositions without a separately drawn map. The court must explain the legal and practical basis of that comparison and its causal significance.

For example, evidence of previous organization can help identify the activity actually obstructed; it does not establish that retaining the former constituency is lawful, feasible or constitutionally owed. Evidence about the reassignment must connect the boundary's operation to an actual material impediment rather than merely a reduced prospect of electing the preferred candidate. A comparison dependent on a different district configuration must establish that configuration's feasibility under applicable legal constraints, including effects necessary to make the comparison workable. Calling an alternative attractive or traditional does not do that work. The claim's actual demands may therefore require mapping evidence in a particular case even though the rule imposes no universal production requirement.

At pleading, ordinary pleading rules govern, with no expert proof or replacement-map demand imported as a universal gate. At proof, all substantive elements and the independently justified lawful comparison must be established. The distinction does not excuse the latter showing merely because the case began at dismissal. The present record establishes neither a sufficient comparison nor personal liability. Act 1, which was malapportioned and used under a stay, cannot become a lawful benchmark simply through prior use; no finding of continuity with Act 34 is supplied.

## Actual sources doing the legal work

The actual Miller Part V-A, joined by all eight Associates, expressly rejects a universal replacement-map, study or expert-report requirement while preserving whatever comparator and proof the actual claim requires. The actual Easley opinion by Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, likewise allows maps, operation, political correlation, communications and electoral experience as evidence without a universal alternative-map or statistical-model requirement. These are operative evidentiary rules, not an inference from a preferred outcome. They defeat the claim that a cartographic artifact is invariably necessary, but they do not themselves eliminate claim-specific causation.

Actual Holder Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, requires a reasonable workable comparison with legal and practical roots independent of a desired winner tally. It rejects the need for a uniquely ideal institutional design. Its concrete local authorizations and district patterns were sufficient there; neither the statutory holding nor those particular proofs impose an exclusive evidentiary method on this new constitutional injury. The proposal keeps Holder's substantive discipline and does not transpose its Section 2 capacity conditions.

Actual Cano Part II.A, joined by all eight Associates, requires an actual protected interest and causation; organization, nomination and other participation can be materially impaired without regular electoral defeat or a further governmental act. Souter's Part II.B application, joined by the other seven Associates, rejects the particular CD28 electoral-prospect and constituency-composition claims. That application is the principal warning against translating any old political activity or alternative district into a violation. The proposal answers it by demanding an identified actual or demonstrably attainable opportunity, a substantial practical obstruction and a supported causal comparison, rather than inferring injury from motive or district composition.

Actual Brooks, Souter's unanimous constitutional opinion, supplies the distinct substantial-or-motivating-purpose and same-enactment sequence. Actual Montiel's unanimous Part I and Crawford-El's ordinary-proof rule supply preponderance and reject a merely conceivable lawful explanation as the same-decision defense. Their concrete applications do not create a districting right by themselves; they govern proof once the four remand supporters recognize the particular material-opportunity injury. Growe and the existing remedial authorities retain state responsibility and relief fitted to a proved violation.

## Express revised commitments and remaining objections

**Souter — supported material accommodation, classification (2).** His original commitment identified a feasible lawful district alternative. He now accepts proof through a feasible alternative district or another independently sufficient lawful causal comparison. The necessary substance survives: personal material impairment, an attainable opportunity under lawful conditions, independent grounds for comparison, purpose and boundary-specific causation. His own Cano application requires the real impairment, his Brooks opinion supplies the causation sequence, and his actual Miller, Holder and Easley joins support an administrable comparison without an obligatory particular drawing. Those sources give a concrete reason to accept equivalent proof once the proposal expressly retains every substantive predicate. He continues to refuse a comparison chosen solely for partisan output, ordinary disadvantage recharacterized as injury, or a finding that these voters have already satisfied the rule.

**Ginsburg — supported material accommodation, classification (2).** Her original join incorporated Souter's confined formulation. She joins the same revised comparison method because her own Easley opinion expressly rejects a universal alternative-map requirement and preserves the actual claim's proof, while her Hunt opinion and Cano joins demand an actually presented independent deprivation. The proposed actual-opportunity and causation requirements answer her original objection that a predominance-only or electoral-prospect theory would be remanded as a constitutional injury. Her remand remains confined to the live pleadings and amendment permitted by ordinary procedure. She refuses automatic discovery, a new unpreserved cause, or relief from design evidence alone.

**Stevens — compatible clarification, classification (1).** His frozen commitment required a feasible lawful comparison rooted in neutral districting considerations, not a mandatory specific map or list of factors. His actual Miller and Easley joins preserve multiple evidentiary methods; his Holder join requires the independent substantive basis; his Bandemer/Powell position and Cook opinion support adjudicating purposeful arbitrary political disadvantage when it produces the cognizable injury he has frozen. He can join the proposed requirement of material impairment of an existing or demonstrably attainable participation opportunity, including organization or nomination injury before an electoral loss. He does not revive historical Shaw-based inferred representational injury, presume harm from predominant purpose, or require every voter to wait through a fixed series of elections.

**Breyer — compatible clarification, classification (1).** His frozen position allowed a lawful causal comparison and actual material opportunity rather than an indispensable alternative map. His Miller and Easley joins affirm the evidentiary distinction, and his Cano joins require real participation effects instead of expected seat outcomes. The proposal's substantial practical obstruction is the material-opportunity threshold he required. Durable insulation from meaningful challenge remains a possible sufficient showing when independently proved and caused by the boundaries; it is not mandatory where a direct organization, nomination or participation injury is established. He refuses treating ordinary competitive difficulty, expected defeat or any lawful map that improves a party's prospects as sufficient.

The four accept the prohibited-purpose formulation as an inquiry into purposeful suppression of identifiable political opposition through the proved material obstruction. It does not condemn every consideration of partisan affiliation, incumbent continuity or political fairness. Gaffney's permission for ordinary political consideration remains relevant. An independently justified fairness policy may support the State's defense if genuinely applied and shown to produce materially the same lawful assignment without the prohibited purpose. The policy's label, a favorable outcome, or a litigation-created rationale does not establish that defense. Consistent application here concerns proof that the asserted policy actually explains the challenged decision; it is not an additional freestanding obligation to adopt a comprehensive fairness code.

## Historical comparison of the accommodation

Historical Souter, joined by Ginsburg, required a hypothetical district with different political proportions and fewer departures from traditional criteria; his fourth element and footnote 4 also addressed consequences for contiguous districts. His fifth element and footnote 5 treated unified political control as important to the inference of intent. His justification discussion and footnotes 6–7 allowed legitimate objectives, including political fairness under Gaffney, while rejecting an entitlement to proportional representation. None of these details may be collapsed into a general historical endorsement of optional alternative-map proof. The present accommodation is different and must be justified by actual changed law.

**Historical departure:** Souter and Ginsburg now allow an independently sufficient lawful causal comparison without invariably requiring the historical fourth-element hypothetical district. Their actual Miller and Easley joins reject universal map-production requirements; Holder supplies independent legal and practical comparison rather than an ideal map; and Cano replaces a design-and-packing proxy with proof of real personal material impairment and causation. Because actual impairment must now be proved independently, a particular cartographic proxy need not invariably perform that evidentiary work when other supported evidence does it. Their comparison, feasibility and causation requirements remain; this is not a relaxation justified by coalition usefulness.

Stevens's and Breyer's original historical-departure analyses remain effective. Stevens's historical inferred representational harm remains displaced by his actual Shaw, Miller and Cano joins. Breyer's historical seat-based scenarios remain insufficient by themselves under his actual Miller and Cano joins and the operative Act 34 record. The circulation does not add a new historical judgment departure for either. The other four Associates' retained judgments and grounds are unchanged.

## Assembly boundary

The proposed formulation has four supported Associate joins if its comparison and burden provisions carry the operative meanings above. These are complete-framework joins, not judgment-only support or permission to choose an unrelated standard later. A final formulation that makes comparison optional, treats prior activity as sufficient without lawful feasibility, uses a preferred seat allocation as the benchmark, imposes a universal drawing requirement, or converts political motive into injury exceeds this freeze. The proposal supplies a legal rule for the confined remand, not proof that the Act 34 allegations or any later evidence satisfy it. Final compatibility with the remaining participant and opinion assignment belong to assembly.
