## 104. Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.

**Disposition and rule presented for joins.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical ineligibility and return the request for district-court discretion. Section 1782 reaches evidence for use in the Commission's adjudicative competition process, including a reasonably contemplated proceeding before a body exercising the described first-instance adjudicative authority. AMD's procedural ability to present evidence and seek review makes it an interested person. The statute requires neither a pending adjudication nor foreign discoverability of the requested material. It authorizes, rather than compels, assistance. No present production order follows.

The relevant tribunal function is adjudication, not every activity of an administrative institution. The Commission's capacity to issue an enforceable decision subject to judicial review and AMD's legally recognized participation distinguish this request from free-standing policy advice or an unanchored investigation. The text's inclusion of criminal investigations before accusation supports the rejection of a general pendency prerequisite; reasonably contemplated adjudication still must be real. Private arbitration is not decided.

**Principal fork.** The strongest restrictive argument combines the Commission's refusal to obtain these documents, its own evidence rules, and possible circumvention of the foreign process. Those considerations can justify denying or narrowing particular assistance, but they do not insert a categorical foreign-discoverability or foreign-request requirement into a provision expressly permitting an interested person's application. Conversely, broad statutory eligibility cannot make the foreign refusal irrelevant or convert American discovery into an entitlement. Receptivity, genuine circumvention, availability through the foreign participants, and burden inform the existing discretion; no finding about their ultimate balance is supplied by this packet. Statutory privileges remain protected. A party cannot establish circumvention merely by identifying a difference between the two systems' discovery rules.

## 105. Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws

**Disposition and rule presented for joins.** Vacate the single-judge dismissal and remand for a three-judge district court under §2284. A constitutional challenge to congressional apportionment cannot be disposed of on the merits by a single judge merely because the complaint is thought insufficient under Rule 12. The Goosby exception concerns claims that are wholly insubstantial in the jurisdictional sense. Current Vieth affirmatively supplies a justiciable equal-protection claim, so this pleaded partisan-impairment claim cannot satisfy that exception.

The proper court must apply Vieth's political capacity, material impairment, substantial motivating purpose, and boundary-causation requirements, with its lawful-comparison and same-decision qualifications. Ordinary notice pleading does not require proof of those elements or a completed expert alternative map in the complaint. County division, strange shapes, or party-seat imbalance do not alone establish liability. The complaint's asserted intentional impairment of participation is not properly converted into an exclusively proportional-representation claim. The abandoned Article I and independent First Amendment claims are not restored. The remand permits appropriate pleading treatment or amendment by the proper tribunal and orders neither a liability finding nor a replacement map.

## 108. Schriro v. Summerlin

**Common ground and disputed component.** Ring's transfer of the necessary aggravator finding from judge to jury is a new procedural rule for a sentence final in 1984. It regulates the means of establishing capital eligibility rather than removing conduct or a class of persons from the State's power to punish. Arizona already required aggravating facts to be proved beyond a reasonable doubt, so this claim does not also introduce that burden. The question is whether jury determination of the death-authorizing aggravator satisfies the still-operative watershed exception. Neither a later abolition of that exception nor a presumption that every capital safeguard qualifies is available.

The strongest case for nonretroactivity is that a judge can reliably determine facts under the proper burden, a jury is not invariably more accurate, and structural importance on direct review does not establish the exceptional accuracy and fairness requirement for reopening final judgments. DeStefano rejected retroactivity for the general jury-trial right under an earlier framework; it supports that caution but does not itself decide the contemporary capital watershed issue. The strongest contrary case concerns the basic allocation of the decision that legally opens death, with a jury's collective and independent assessment guarding against erroneous authorization of that penalty. It must defend that function as an essential accuracy safeguard, not merely invoke community preference or a greater tendency toward mercy.

## 109. Cheney v. United States District Court

**Issues kept distinct.** The appellate court's power under §1651 to issue mandamus is different from the plaintiffs' §1361 theory for enforcing a clear statutory duty and their APA theory against an agency. Appellate mandamus requires no adequate alternative remedy, a clear and indisputable right, and appropriateness in the circumstances. An interlocutory dispute does not become an ordinary appeal by acquiring constitutional significance. Conversely, document-specific executive privilege is not the only possible means of identifying an institutional burden on the President's discharge of official functions.

**Coverage boundary available for joins.** The formal roster does not conclusively dispose of alleged actual membership. Private persons who are genuinely integrated as regular full participants in a fixed advisory group can be members even without a formal vote, veto, supervisory post, or power to make the final governmental decision. Mere outside advice, repeated access, lobbying, or attendance as a consultant does not by itself establish that integration. Whether this group contained such private members remains unproved. The threshold also requires the statutory kind of advisory group; every collection of persons who communicate with officials is not a committee.

AAPS expressly treats full functional participation as relevant and denies that supervisory or decisionmaking authority is necessary. That circuit opinion is persuasive, not a Supreme Court holding. Public Citizen supplies a contextual limit on statutory utilization, not an exemption for all presidential advice. Kennedy's separate Public Citizen approach concerns interference with an expressly exclusive nomination power; it cannot automatically invalidate every application to this energy-policy group. These prior authorities support a genuine statutory inquiry and careful accommodation, not a mandatory vote-or-veto prerequisite or a present finding that FACA covers this group.

## 111. Rumsfeld v. Padilla — No. 03-1027

### Supported paths and component boundaries

**P1 — ordinary physical-custody respondent and forum.** The later petition challenges present military confinement at a known South Carolina brig. **P2 — confined principal-custodian exception.** In this exceptional conversion of a named citizen's existing judicial custody to personally directed military custody, the official responsible for that conversion and empowered to terminate it can count as the custodian, where that official is actually reachable. That enactment can satisfy section 4001(a) without using the word detention. Quirin supplies the belligerent/civilian distinction and the compatibility of citizenship with enemy-belligerent status. The strongest objection is that section 4001(a) requires more definite congressional authorization for citizen imprisonment and the AUMF does not name detention, citizens or its duration. The contrary statutory path accepts that objection under Endo's liberty-sensitive construction and requires release from this military custody absent an independently lawful basis. Section 4001(b)'s management exception supplies no answer to either path.

**H-C: distinct constitutional trial path.** A citizen detained domestically for alleged adherence to the enemy cannot be held indefinitely on the Executive's allegation when ordinary courts remain available and the writ has not lawfully been suspended. On this view ordinary prosecution is the lawful course; Milligan and the suspension structure cannot be replaced by a newly balanced civil hearing. The strongest counterargument is that actual battlefield belligerents may be captured and held without criminal trial, including the citizen recognized in Quirin. The response distinguishes Quirin's conceded belligerent facts and authorized military prosecution from indefinite domestic confinement of a citizen whose status is contested. This is a distinct constitutional position, not a premise that Quirin never involved a citizen or that every civilian arrest proves civilian status.

**H-P: process if H-A is accepted.** For continued domestic custody, the citizen must receive notice sufficient to understand the factual basis of the enemy-force classification and a meaningful opportunity, with counsel, to present a contrary account to a neutral decisionmaker able to determine the factual predicate and afford effective relief. Section 2243's return and fact-determination provisions and Mathews's liberty, error and governmental-interest inquiry support that minimum. Initial battlefield operations do not require a trial at capture. A sworn declaration can be evidence without being an adjudication; neither the executive label nor the father's counteraccount is conclusive. A functioning neutral military status tribunal can perform the initial determination if its actual procedures satisfy this minimum and judicial review remains effective. It is not deemed sufficient merely because it is military.

**H-E: evidence and allocation.** Three different claims must stay separate: (1) the Government must identify the actual legal category and supply credible evidence supporting membership; (2) demonstrated battlefield or security constraints can justify hearsay, protected-source handling and appropriate rebuttable evidentiary inferences, provided a real opportunity to answer remains; (3) a fixed ultimate burden of persuasion, such as a detainee burden, governmental preponderance, or governmental clear-and-convincing proof. H-P and the first two propositions do not decide the third. S Zadvydas and C Demore supply no general burden for battlefield classification. A presumption based only on the litigation label or the declaration's existence would eliminate rather than implement the required contest.

The strongest objection to accommodation is that reliance on hearsay and burden shifting may make the imprisoned citizen disprove inaccessible assertions. The answer available to H-P supporters is an actually neutral assessment of evidentiary reliability, disclosed substance adequate to answer, counsel and an effective release remedy if qualifying status is not established. The strongest objection to a fixed government clear-and-convincing rule is that Salerno and Addington address different regimes and do not settle war-status adjudication. The record supports remand under a genuine-contest rule without pretending that a precise universal quantum has already been agreed.

**H-D: purpose and duration.** The narrow H-A authority is incapacitation of an actual opposing fighter during the relevant active hostilities, not confinement solely to interrogate or permanent imprisonment by reference to an undefined worldwide threat. The purpose limit follows from the scope of the authorized force, with S Zadvydas supplying an analogy about actual statutory purpose rather than its immigration calendar. Whether these hostilities have ended, the custody has lost that relationship, or an independent authority exists requires the actual record; the packet supplies none of those findings. Remand must allow a properly presented current-authority challenge. No automatic release date is invented.

## 114. Missouri v. Seibert — No. 02-1371

### Supported alternatives

**S1 — objective effectiveness.** Later warnings and waiver must actually communicate the choice protected by Miranda in the whole interrogation setting. Where officers first obtain a detailed confession, briefly interrupt substantially continuous questioning, then use the same interrogator and the first admissions to obtain a repetition, the later recitation does not supply an effective choice. The failure here follows from the established continuity, overlap and use of prior admissions; proof of subjective intent is not an indispensable legal element. Time and place, personnel, overlap, prior completeness and treatment of the second interview as a continuation are pertinent circumstances, not a mandatory numerical checklist. Elstad remains applicable to an independently effective later warning and waiver.

**S2 — deliberate circumvention with cure.** Retain ordinary Elstad treatment of inadvertent earlier omissions, but exclude the repeated statement when police deliberately conduct a coordinated two-stage interrogation to defeat the warning safeguard unless an effective intervening cure restores the suspect's informed choice. An actually substantial break or an effective explanation distinguishing the legal significance of the earlier and later statements can matter; neither is found here. The established deliberate method satisfies the trigger without a new intent inference. The strongest objection is that two equally ineffective warning sequences should not differ solely with officer intent. S2 answers that it identifies a confined intentional evasion of a settled safeguard while leaving ordinary warning-and-waiver analysis to govern other encounters; it does not authorize ineffective warnings in inadvertent cases.

**S3 — ordinary Elstad waiver analysis without a new midstream-warning exclusion.** Prior unwarned statements are excluded, but a subsequent statement remains admissible after genuinely knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver unless the complete circumstances undermine that waiver. The strongest objection is that the proven coordinated tactic creates precisely that defect despite a signed form. S3 rejects making an officer's intention or a generalized continuation presumption dispositive; it requires a supported determination about the subsequent waiver. Dickerson forecloses voluntariness alone as a substitute, so this path cannot simply equate a noncoerced answer or signature with valid compliance.

## 115. United States v. Patane — No. 02-1183

### Supported alternatives and independent propositions

**P-G: confined physical-fruit rule.** A voluntary custodial answer obtained without complete Miranda warnings is excluded from the prosecution's case in chief, but incomplete warnings alone do not require suppression of a nontestimonial physical object discovered through that answer. Tucker and Elstad preserve differentiated consequences, and S Dickerson expressly left the general fruit question open. The statement, the gun, actual coercion and a separately unlawful search are different questions. Reversing the warning-only exclusion permits the pistol's use if no independent admissibility defect applies; it does not establish a complete waiver from Patane's interruption.

**P-F: warning-enforcement derivative remedy.** When the prosecution uses a suspect's unwarned custodial answer to locate physical evidence, exclusion of that direct evidentiary product is necessary to preserve the constitutional warning safeguard and prevent officers from obtaining the full investigative benefit while forgoing only the words. A physical object's nontestimonial character does not eliminate the causal use of compelled testimonial disclosure protected by the safeguard. The strongest objection is that Tucker and Elstad permit some derivative use and a rule of automatic physical exclusion extends beyond the core retained in Dickerson. P-F distinguishes the earlier exceptions and the later effective waiver in Elstad rather than asserting that Dickerson already overruled them.

**Duty characterization.** One rationale for P-G treats the Self-Incrimination Clause as a criminal-case prohibition and warning omission itself as insufficient to complete that constitutional violation. Another accepts Miranda's constitutional warning obligation and reaches P-G solely because this additional suppression remedy is unnecessary for voluntary nontestimonial physical fruits. These are distinct propositions. S Chavez adopted no majority rationale resolving the earlier no-use damages theory and therefore supplies neither rationale as governing law. Any opinion claiming a broader duty rule needs its own actual joins.

**Limits.** The reviewed voluntariness premise is accepted. The record supplies no deliberate evasion finding, no actual coercion and no new Fourth Amendment ground; none is invented. The circuit's rejection of the original probable-cause suppression ground is part of the posture. A decision confined to the warning-fruit issue does not reopen that ruling by implication. The separate same-date Seibert matter supplies no authority here.

### Judgment and common legal premises

## 118. Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain, Nos. 03-339 and 03-485

### Judgment and component separation

All eight associate Justices would reverse the Ninth Circuit insofar as it sustains the ATS damages judgment against Sosa and permits the asserted FTCA claim against the United States. The two components have separate sufficient grounds. Six Justices—Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—reject the asserted new received action because the proposed rule does not establish a sufficiently specific and accepted international prohibition supporting domestic damages for this less-than-one-day detention followed by lawful custody merely because the initial arrest lacked domestic legal authority. Scalia and Thomas reject creation of this additional domestic action on their preserved source-of-authority ground. They also agree that the offered declarations and treaty do not themselves supply the requested domestic action; their agreement is not a join in a general judicial license to recognize additional norms.

All eight independently conclude that section 2680(k) bars this FTCA claim for detention in Mexico notwithstanding the alleged domestic planning and direction of that same injury. The action against the United States must be dismissed at that scope. The ATS component fails on the action/norm inquiry, not on a retrospective withdrawal of district-court jurisdiction over every substantial international-tort allegation.

### Governing law and tested alternatives

**ATS route preserving the adopted actions.** Abebe-Jira affirmatively recognizes a received domestic action for deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture: severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation. Kadic/Doe additionally recognizes personally perpetrated, planned or directed genocide with its specific group-destruction intent and defined acts, and the pleaded core war crimes with armed-conflict, protected-victim, prohibited-act and conflict-connection predicates. Those actions remain law with their separate responsibility, causation and remedy requirements. Neither the statutory forum nor the seriousness of an allegation supplies all of those elements, a sovereign waiver or an action for every international wrong.

An action for the present detention could be recognized only through its own sufficiently determinate international rule and supported domestic enforcement basis. The lower court's strongest position relies on the categorical prohibitions against arbitrary arrest or detention in article 9 of the Universal Declaration and ICCPR, the illegality of the initial capture, and the absence of an express minimum duration in those instruments. On that approach, even brief detention without legal authority can be an actionable personal wrong rather than a political grievance. The strongest objection is not that a one-day detention is legally harmless. It is that the asserted transformation of any detention unauthorized by domestic law into an internationally actionable tort lacks the particular specificity and acceptance established for the adopted torture, genocide and core-war-crime actions. The Restatement's prolonged-detention formulation, the declarations' nonbinding character, and the ICCPR's non-self-executing status matter at this step. A non-self-executing instrument can inform evidence of an international norm; it does not thereby create an operative domestic damages action.

The six received-action adherents choose the confined rejection. They do not hold that only eighteenth-century labels can ever support an action, establish a universal minimum detention duration, foreclose every independently defined arbitrary-detention claim, or decide that abduction is lawful. The present sources and asserted broad norm are insufficient for this action. Scalia and Thomas would additionally refuse an extension beyond the actions already adopted unless a sufficient enacted source authorizes the new civil liability. Their position respects those precedents as existing law rather than using the word jurisdictional to erase them.

**FTCA domestic-planning route.** Sections 1346(b)(1) and 2680(k) perform different work. Identifying allegedly wrongful planning in the United States may identify conduct, an actor and a proposed local-law basis; it does not itself answer the exception for any claim arising in a foreign country. The strongest argument for the headquarters theory invokes the FTCA's act-or-omission location and Richards's place-of-law approach: genuinely domestic wrongdoing can be governed by domestic law, so the foreign-law concern may be absent. The opposing construction treats the claim for the same seizure and detention in Mexico as a foreign-country claim despite its domestic causal direction. It preserves the enacted exception rather than allowing every overseas operational injury to become domestic through a planning allegation. All eight select that latter construction for this claim. They do not treat Smith as having already resolved every mixed-location tort or manufacture a distinct injury in the United States.

Smith applies the foreign-country exception to wholly Antarctic acts and rejects residence as a substitute place-of-law source. It supports attention to the enacted waiver and exception rather than a claimant-selected law, but its factual limits remain. Mexico also is indisputably foreign sovereign territory, which distinguishes the precise premise of Stevens's Smith dissent. The law-enforcement proviso in section 2680(h) is an exception to the intentional-tort exclusion; it is not a repeal of section 2680(k). The ATS recognized actions likewise create no FTCA consent. The prior criminal Alvarez-Machain holding allows trial because the extradition treaty lacks the asserted bar; Ker–Frisbie's trial-authority rule neither authorizes capture nor decides civil liability.

## 119. Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran, No. 03-1147

### Judgment and controlling distinctions available for assembly

All eight associate Justices would affirm dismissal on the surviving Algiers Accords bar. The case-specific amendment removes the designated foreign-sovereign-immunity obstacle; it does not also repeal the independent claim-settlement bar, create a substantive cause of action, establish liability or direct a damages award. The technical case-designation correction does not add any of those consequences. The United States may protect its own agreement-related interest, and Iran's nonappearance does not forfeit that interest or establish the claim under section 1608(e).

The unresolved-damages liability default did not finally dispose of this private action through the ordinary judicial process. Consequently, Plaut's prohibition on a legislative command to reopen a completed private judgment does not supply a ground for invalidating these amendments. A targeted change of governing law in a pending case is distinct from legislation prescribing findings or liability under unchanged law. These commitments affirm on statutory grounds and do not decide every targeted-law objection or the reserved Flatow cause-of-action question. The reported Rule 60(b) treatment below is preserved rather than silently replaced with a different procedural theory.

### Enacted law and alternative paths

Public Law 107-77 section 626(c) inserts at the end of former section 1605(a)(7)(A), before its semicolon, the words: “or the act is related to Case Number 1:00CV03110(ESG) in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia.” Public Law 107-117 section 208 substitutes “1:00CV03110(EGS)” for the earlier designation. The exact official text is in `entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK8_ROEDER_TEXT.md`. These enactments amend the identified immunity condition. They contain no enacted direction that the existing liability determination must be preserved notwithstanding all other authority and no operative award of damages.

**Affirm on the independent bar.** The Accords' covered-claim preclusion and their implementing measures remain operative. Dames & Moore supports the claims-settlement framework within its actual authorization and historical-acquiescence setting; it is not a power to disregard a contrary statute. TWA, Cook and Weinberger require attention to Congress's enacted displacement of an international commitment rather than inferring that displacement from a desired litigation result. Here the text can operate as written while the independent bar survives. Amerada Hess's distinction between the FSIA framework and separate rights, and Altmann's present-regime holding, reinforce that immunity removal does not create liability. The explicit case reference answers which acts receive the immunity exception; it does not answer every other obstacle to recovery.

**Reverse for an implied broader statutory displacement.** Petitioners' strongest position is that Congress deliberately selected this suit, knew the Government's objection, and intended meaningful recovery rather than an immunity change with no ultimate benefit. The explanatory statements concerning preservation of the judgment and the corrective amendment strengthen that contextual inference. On that reading, the amendment would displace the Accords for these claims and require further independent adjudication. Its decisive weakness is the mismatch between that comprehensive effect and the words Congress enacted: removal of one barrier is a real legal change even if another defeats relief. Explanatory statements cannot enact the omitted direction or adjudicate liability. All eight therefore reject the broader reading. Their agreement does not impose a universal magic-words requirement or hold that Congress lacks power to enact clearer domestic displacement.

**Article III alternative.** Robertson permits an actual change in substantive law governing pending litigation, even in appropriations legislation, while leaving application to the courts. Plaut protects completed private judgments against the specified legislative reopening command; Miller distinguishes prospective changed law and preserved adjudication from legislative findings. The present unresolved damages and continuing judicial proceedings do not satisfy Plaut's completed-judgment predicate. If the plaintiffs' construction merely substitutes a new legal rule, targeted identification does not alone establish legislative adjudication. If unenacted statements are treated as commands to preserve liability regardless of surviving law or proof, they both lack statutory force and disregard the independent judicial function. This distinction supports the narrow statutory resolution; it is not a separate declaration that these statutes are unconstitutional.
