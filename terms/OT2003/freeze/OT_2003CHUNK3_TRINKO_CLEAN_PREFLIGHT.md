## Entering-law validation

1. **Independent section 2 offense.** The simulated Kodak holding requires monopoly power and unlawful acquisition or maintenance, with actual markets, competitive effects and substantial business explanations assessed on the supported record. Neither a market label nor injury to a rival is enough. The simulated CSU/Xerox decision rejects a conclusive patent or copyright exclusion defense while retaining substantial force for the statutory entitlement, lawful returns, supported efficiencies and operational justifications. It creates no general duty to supply or assist rivals. Its precise subject is intellectual-property exclusion; extending its rejection of a conclusive statutory defense to regulated telecommunications requires an articulated comparison, not a claim that CSU already adjudicated telecommunications.

2. **Regulation and the saving clause.** The packet identifies sections 251 and 252 and the section 601(b)(1) antitrust saving clause. They support treating a regulatory obligation and an independent antitrust offense as distinct. Regulation does not automatically displace antitrust law; preservation of that law does not enlarge its elements. The operational setting, regulator's ability to supervise access, investment incentives, and supported legitimate explanations may bear materially on the asserted antitrust theory and appropriate relief. No source supplied here makes regulation either automatic liability or categorical immunity.

3. **Aspen and refusals.** The entering law treats Aspen Skiing as an example of unjustified exclusion in which actual conduct and business justification matter. It does not make every aspect of that case an exclusive checklist. It also does not create an unrestricted right of rival access. Any proposal to demand a terminated voluntary course of dealing, or to reject such a requirement, must explain its scope consistently with the operative Kodak and CSU holdings.

4. **Leveraging and attempt.** Discon preserves the difference between harm to an individual supplier and injury to competition and rejects substituting regulatory deception for competitive injury. Its section 1 classification cannot be transplanted as an automatic section 2 exemption. Spectrum Sports requires specific intent, anticompetitive conduct and independent dangerous probability for attempted monopolization. Mere use of power in one market to obtain an advantage elsewhere supplies no freestanding offense that avoids the appropriate monopoly-maintenance or attempt elements.

5. **Section 4 damages.** Steamfitters applies Associated General Contractors to derivative healthcare expenditures. Its holding concerns that causal chain, not an across-the-board rule excluding consumers or all persons other than a defendant's immediate purchaser. It requires assessment of directness, causal intermediaries, more direct claimants, duplication and workable allocation as relevant to the claim. Trinko's consumer status is neither an automatic qualification nor a disqualification. The injury alleged to Trinko must be distinguished from the carrier's own operational injury. McCready's treatment, as identified in Steamfitters, requires attention to whether the plaintiff's injury implements the challenged scheme rather than merely follows another person's injury.

6. **Section 16 injunction.** The packet expressly distinguishes section 16's predicates from section 4. Cargill Part II confirms that the plaintiff must allege threatened harm of the kind antitrust law protects against and arising from the feature that makes the conduct unlawful. Actual completed harm and injury to business or property are section 4 conditions that section 16 does not replicate. Cargill notes 5 and 6 separately explain that antitrust injury does not exhaust the section 4 proper-plaintiff inquiry and that some additional damages considerations do not govern section 16. Potential duplicate damages, difficulty allocating them and the presence of other more directly injured damages claimants cannot simply be imported to equity; one injunction can supply the same protection as many. A conclusion barring treble damages because of those concerns consequently does not dispose of the injunction request. Prospective relief still requires a cognizable antitrust violation, threatened personal antitrust harm, a sufficient connection between that violation and threat, and a remedy fitting them under ordinary equity principles. Cargill neither eliminates that connection nor treats every consumer as entitled to relief. A broad request to supervise interconnection compliance is not itself a substitute for those requirements.

His dissent cannot convert an alleged section 2 violation into an incipient-merger claim or remove the governing requirement of antitrust injury. In CSU, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the controlling opinion preserving supported monopoly-maintenance claims while retaining substantial statutory justification and ordinary evidentiary and remedial limits.
