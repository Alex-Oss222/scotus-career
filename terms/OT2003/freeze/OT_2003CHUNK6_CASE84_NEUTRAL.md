* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-20
* **Question Presented:** Whether pretrial detainees challenging longstanding, systemic jail conditions must prove officials’ actual knowledge and disregard of health risks, and whether a nonadjudicative compliance monitor is a special master under 18 U.S.C. §3626(f).
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, affirming in part and vacating in part orders concerning ongoing conditions relief in New York City jails and the Office of Compliance Consultants.
* **Relief Requested:** City officials seek termination or limitation of continuing decree obligations and monitoring; the detainee classes seek effective correction of current conditions.
* **Material Record:** The litigation covers multiple jail facilities under longstanding decrees. The disputed conditions include ventilation, lighting, temperature and sanitation. The OCC observes and reports compliance but does not hold adjudicative hearings, issue binding findings or decide parties’ rights. The record requires facility- and provision-specific review.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Prospective relief depends on current violations and statutory findings, not the decrees’ age. A monitor’s label does not determine whether its actual powers trigger §3626(f).
* **Relevant Dates:** Original decrees date to 1978-1979; the monitoring arrangement dates to 1982. The appealed decision issued September 2, 2003, before the event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Benjamin v. Jacobson governs earlier termination litigation concerning these decrees; it does not establish that every challenged condition persists now.

### Issue 1: Pretrial conditions and continued prospective relief

* **Controlling Law:** Benjamin v. Jacobson requires current and ongoing violations and written need-narrowness-intrusiveness findings before continuing PLRA relief, with a fair opportunity for current proof. Farmer v. Brennan requires subjective awareness for its convicted-prisoner Eighth Amendment claim; it does not decide this distinct pretrial institutional claim.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate the constitutional violation from §3626’s remedial fit. Findings must connect each retained provision to a current violation; proof of historic deficiencies alone cannot preserve the whole decree.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; 42 U.S.C. §1983; 18 U.S.C. §3626(a), (b).
* **Material Other Authority:** Bell v. Wolfish prohibits punishment of pretrial detainees and examines legitimate nonpunitive purposes and excessive restrictions. Ordinary negligence alone is not a constitutional violation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The current-violation and tailored-relief requirements already govern; the role of Farmer’s personal-awareness rule in systemic pretrial relief remains unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether longstanding objectively dangerous institutional conditions require separate proof of each responsible official’s subjective awareness before prospective correction.

### Issue 2: Monitoring versus adjudicative mastery

* **Controlling Law:** Benjamin v. Jacobson preserves effective but statutorily limited supervision of current violations. The remedial rules do not classify every court-appointed observer as a special master.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Classify the appointment by actual delegated functions. A court may gather compliance information without delegating adjudication, but cannot avoid statutory limits through a different title.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §3626(f), (g)(8); Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 53 as then effective.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ex parte Peterson recognizes auxiliary assistance to courts while retaining judicial responsibility.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Section 3626(f) governs persons performing a master’s functions; whether this monitor performs them is the presented classification issue.
* **Open Point:** Whether nonbinding observation and reporting alone bring the OCC within the special-master restrictions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Second Circuit distinguished systemic pretrial relief from Farmer’s personal-awareness inquiry and classified the OCC by its nonadjudicative functions, while requiring correction of particular remedial deficiencies.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** City officials invoke Farmer, the PLRA’s restrictions and the statutory special-master safeguards to constrain continuing supervision.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The detainees argue that demonstrated continuing institutional hazards and effective monitoring do not depend on proving each official’s private mental state; the court retains adjudicative responsibility.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** An order unsupported by current findings may be vacated even if the constitutional standard and monitor classification are correct.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Sustaining the constitutional and monitoring distinctions preserves properly supported relief while remanding deficient provisions for exact findings. Importing a subjective-awareness prerequisite requires reevaluation under that test. Treating the OCC as a master requires statutory appointment safeguards. None authorizes indefinite retention of every original decree term.
