## Separable questions and paths

The legal authority question precedes review of EPA's use of that authority. Sections 113(a)(5) and 167 authorize prevention of construction that fails statutory requirements; section 165(a)(4) imposes BACT, and section 169(3) assigns the permitting authority a case-specific assessment of achievable reduction after energy, environmental and economic impacts and other costs. Neither agency expertise nor an interpretation's official character resolves that allocation by itself. Mead, Heimmermann, Walton and Barnhart v. Thomas require actual congressional authority and its authorized exercise. The conclusion below is independently grounded in the enacted allocation, with the agency's account receiving persuasive consideration rather than a claim to decide its own jurisdiction conclusively.

**Affirmance path:** BACT is a substantive federal preconstruction requirement. The State chooses within a reasoned range; EPA may prevent construction when the State's determination lacks a reasoned basis under the statutory factors. A permit does not establish compliance solely by its issuance. The strongest objection is that substantive reasonableness review effectively makes EPA the appellate permitting authority despite the express state assignment. The answer is to preserve the State's choice among reasonable statutory outcomes and require EPA to demonstrate the defect rather than simply prefer another technology. Both assignments then retain effect.

**Reversal path:** The State is the actor designated to determine achievable reduction after the statutory considerations. Federal enforcement assures a permit, required consideration and satisfaction of applicable objective statutory requirements, but the general enforcement provisions do not themselves authorize federal displacement of the State's completed substantive weighing. The strongest objection is that this would insulate an arbitrary BACT label and deprive the substantive requirement of effect. The response is that ordinary state review and federal enforcement of independently specified requirements retain force; courts should not create an EPA merits-review channel absent the congressional allocation. This path does not depend on a constitutional immunity from environmental regulation.

**Exercise-of-authority issue:** If bounded review exists, the supplied record supports EPA's conclusion that the State had not reasonably justified rejecting SCR. The abandoned offset rationale supplies no current ground. The State's own chosen method and initial feasibility assessment matter, but top-down analysis is not universally mandated by statute. A utility-price analogy without a basis for applying that price to this self-generating mine, and praise of the mine's regional economic contribution without a link between control cost and operating consequences, do not explain the claimed economic infeasibility. The missing information is significant because the State identified the resulting analytical gap itself; it is not an automatic substantive obligation to choose the most stringent technology whenever a corporate financial statement is absent. Judicial review evaluates EPA's explanation on its actual grounds and does not newly select the technology.

## Remedy, authority and compatibility

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the same complete rationale: statutory authority for bounded reasonableness oversight and a lawful exercise on this record. Their judgment preserves the operative construction orders as modified, including the summer-dependent-construction allowance, until a compliant reasoned BACT determination satisfies the program. It does not revive the withdrawn December direction to the State. Economic impacts and other costs remain permissible factors. EPA bears the task of explaining why the State's determination fails the governing requirement, and reviewing courts apply ordinary review to EPA's actual explanation.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support reversal on the allocation ground and corresponding relief from the challenged orders. They do not join the alternative exercise-of-authority holding; disposition under their interpretation makes it unnecessary to decide whether a supervisory EPA could reasonably reject the explanation. They can acknowledge the explanation's weaknesses without agreeing that Congress assigned EPA the disputed merits-review function. Their dissent function concerns that statutory allocation and the inadequacy of a reasonableness limit to cure the asserted absence of authority.

No Justice's position rests on a subsequent environmental decision, a fabricated financial effect, an assumed litigant default, or a supposed universal statutory requirement to use top-down analysis. No separate broad anti-commandeering issue is decided. Existing Bragg, SWANCC and Whitman retain their actual statutory subjects and limits.
