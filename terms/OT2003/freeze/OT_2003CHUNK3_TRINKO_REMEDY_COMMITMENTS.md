## Live claims and exact remedy posture

Trinko is an AT&T local-service customer; AT&T obtains network access from Verizon. The amended complaint alleges discriminatory handling of competing carriers' customer orders as a strategy to discourage becoming or remaining their customers and impair their entry and competition. The concrete supplied example is the operations-support failure addressed in federal and state regulatory proceedings. These allegations receive the applicable pleading treatment and are not findings of deliberate exclusion, monopoly power, personal damages or a continuing threat.

The antitrust complaint requests both Clayton Act §4 damages and §16 injunctive relief. Its actual equitable demand is an order that Verizon provide rivals local-loop access on terms and conditions at least as favorable as those Verizon enjoys. That requested equality decree is broader than a demonstrated prohibition of a particular antitrust wrong. A surviving claim does not entitle Trinko to those terms. The decree's breadth does not itself require dismissal of every narrower remedy supported by the existing claim and an established threat.

Regulators imposed remedial measures and financial consequences. The record does not establish that their measures fully compensated Trinko or eliminated every prospective threat. Conversely, a regulator's finding or remedy does not establish the separate Sherman Act elements. Communications-statute and state-law claims are outside this antitrust review and are not revived or finally adjudicated by this artifact's proposed remand.

## Three separate legal inquiries and competing routes

### 1. Section 2: unlawful monopoly maintenance, not the regulatory violation alone

The saving clause prevents automatic displacement of existing antitrust law; it does not enlarge that law. Monopoly power and unlawful acquisition or maintenance remain distinct. The disputed conduct must impair competition rather than merely injure a competing carrier. A use of power in one market to obtain an advantage elsewhere is not a free-standing offense. If attempt is pursued, Spectrum Sports requires anticompetitive conduct, specific intent and independent dangerous probability in the properly defined market. Neither intent nor a leverage label supplies that last element.

The remand route treats the alleged deliberate discriminatory degradation of rival-carrier orders, intended to deter customers and impair rival market participation, as a potentially independent monopoly-maintenance mechanism requiring ordinary section 2 adjudication. Its strongest support is Kodak's actual conduct-and-market inquiry and CSU's refusal to make a statutory legal setting a conclusive defense while preserving substantial legitimate justifications. Its strongest objection is that the allegation may describe only nonperformance of compelled sharing, dressed in the language of anticompetitive purpose. The remand must therefore identify an exclusionary mechanism and actual monopoly maintenance beyond violation of a regulatory access standard, give investment, efficiency and operational reasons substantive weight, and permit dismissal where the allegations fail the applicable elements. It awards no presumed discovery, trial or access.

The dismissal route concludes that the supplied access-performance allegations do not establish an independent antitrust duty to provide the rival inputs or equal treatment the complaint demands. Compulsory access and a purpose to keep customers do not themselves establish unlawful maintenance. Its strongest support is the no-general-duty limitation preserved by Kodak and CSU, with the distinction between regulatory breach and injury to competition in NYNEX. Its strongest objection is that it can turn the regulated setting into the categorical shield CSU rejects and disregard the pleaded customer-diversion mechanism. A lawful dismissal must therefore rest on insufficiency of this specific conduct theory, not automatic immunity for regulation or the saving clause's irrelevance.

Aspen remains an example of unjustified exclusion. A discontinued voluntary course of dealing may be powerful evidence, but its absence is not an exclusive, universally required pleading element. None of the commitments below adopts every fact of Aspen as an exhaustive checklist. Intellectual-property exclusion in CSU and compelled interconnection are different legal settings; CSU's ordinary-elements rule and substantial-justification approach apply by reasoned comparison, not by pretending CSU decided telecommunications.

### 2. Section 4: actual personal injury and the proper damages plaintiff

Steamfitters, applying Associated General Contractors, requires more than consumer status or an asserted causal connection. The actual causal relation, intermediaries, more direct claimants, potential duplicate recovery and workable allocation must be assessed as relevant. The injury to AT&T's ability to obtain and deliver network service is not automatically the same as Trinko's recoverable business or property loss.

The dismissal route characterizes the pleaded customer damage as downstream of the competitive carriers' operational injury. It gives weight to the carriers as direct claimants and the need to distinguish their loss from consequent customer effects before treble damages can follow. The strongest contrary route treats discriminatory handling of end-user orders as the very instrument of exclusion, making those customers directly injured in a manner analogous to McCready's reimbursement denial. The disagreement below is about that actual causal characterization; it is not a rule that only direct purchasers can ever sue or that all consumers automatically have standing.

This is the existing pleaded claim. No Justice infers a particular pricing decision, invoice, passed-on charge, quantifiable loss or contractual arrangement that the neutral packet does not establish. The damages-dismissal position rests on the claim's asserted relation to the carrier-access injury, not an invented finding about those matters or a missing archive treated as failure to plead.

### 3. Section 16: threatened personal antitrust harm and a fitted decree

Cargill Part II requires threatened injury of the type antitrust law protects against, flowing from what makes the challenged conduct unlawful. Section 16 does not repeat §4's actual-business-or-property-loss requirement. Cargill notes 5 and 6 also prevent importing damages duplication, allocation difficulty or the mere presence of a more direct damages claimant as conclusive barriers to an injunction. Those differences do not eliminate the need for a personal threat and an adequately connected antitrust offense.

On the remand route, the existing allegation of a systematic strategy degrading service to customers of rival carriers, coupled with Trinko's actual customer position, permits further adjudication of a personally threatened exclusion injury. The court must apply Cargill and ordinary equity to determine a significant threat from conduct continuing or likely to recur; regulatory relief is material to that inquiry but is not established complete redress. A decree may prohibit only a proved antitrust wrong and the causal threat it poses. The court may not impose the requested equal-access regime merely because §§251 and 252 prescribe access duties. If no narrower effective antitrust remedy is legally supportable on the existing claim, or no sufficient personal prospective threat exists, §16 relief must be denied.

On the dismissal route, failure of the independent §2 claim defeats both remedies: §16 does not furnish an offense that §2 lacks. That conclusion is separate from a §4 directness defect. The three Justices taking this route still accept Cargill's distinct equity standard; they do not deny equity because AT&T is a more direct damages claimant or because allocation would be difficult.

## Component arithmetic and exact remand work

The provisional non-Chief commitments divide as follows:

| Component | Position | Associates |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary §2 monopoly-maintenance theory | Vacate the overbroad refusal/leverage rationale and preserve bounded adjudication under the actual elements | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Ordinary §2 monopoly-maintenance theory | Dismiss for no independently sufficient antitrust conduct/duty on this claim | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas |
| §4 damages | Dismiss on the shared sufficient directness ground; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas also rely on their §2 conclusion | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas |
| §4 damages | Preserve further adjudication of the asserted direct targeted-consumer injury under AGC | Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| §16 injunction | Preserve independent antitrust-injury and equity adjudication, with no automatic parity decree | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| §16 injunction | Dismiss for the missing §2 predicate, expressly distinct from the damages bar | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas |

All eight reject a free-standing leverage offense that dispenses with monopoly maintenance or the independent elements of attempt. All eight preserve Cargill's distinction between threatened antitrust injury and the additional damages-plaintiff considerations. Agreement on those rules does not imply agreement on the present allegation's sufficiency or the remedy.

The five Justices preserving §16 direct the lower courts to: assess the existing allegations under ordinary §2 monopoly-maintenance requirements and the actual market alleged; address the asserted exclusionary discrimination and substantial legitimate explanations without a categorical regulation defense; apply Spectrum Sports independently to any attempt theory actually pursued; determine whether Trinko adequately alleges and ultimately establishes a significant personal threat from continued or recurrent antitrust exclusion; assess the actual protection supplied by agency measures; and decide whether an effective, appropriately limited antitrust decree can cure that threat. Failure on a required element permits dismissal or denial at the proper procedural stage. The mandate does not require new allegations, an evidentiary hearing, discovery, trial, damages, or any specified access terms.

The §4 majority's directness ruling is confined to the current pleaded damages theory. It is not an Article III holding and cannot supply the §16 outcome by itself. Any final assembly must keep the component counts and rationale coalitions separate and test the Chief's position independently. No hypothetical amended claim or unpresented regulatory/state claim is decided.
