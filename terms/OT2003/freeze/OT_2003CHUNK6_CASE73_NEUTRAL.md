* **Case Name:** Dretke v. Haley
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1824; 541 U.S. 386.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-03
* **Question Presented:** Whether actual innocence of a noncapital habitual-offender enhancement permits review of a procedurally defaulted constitutional claim challenging that enhancement.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, affirming habeas relief after applying an actual-innocence gateway to noncapital statutory sentencing eligibility.
* **Relief Requested:** Texas seeks reversal of the gateway ruling and conditional writ; Haley seeks resentencing without the enhancement.
* **Material Record:** Haley received sixteen and one-half years for theft after a habitual-offender finding. The first prior conviction became final three days after the second prior offense, contrary to the required sequence. Texas acknowledges the statutory ineligibility. Without this enhancement the charged theft carried a two-year maximum. Counsel missed the defect at trial and on appeal; a separately asserted ineffective-assistance claim was not adjudicated by the federal district court.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The sufficiency claim was defaulted in state court. A gateway excuses default, not the need for a federal constitutional violation. Whether federal proof requirements cover this recidivist finding and whether counsel supplied cause are distinct questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** The theft prosecution began in 1997; the May 2004 event makes the already-served sentence material to practical relief.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Factual innocence of statutory sentencing eligibility

* **Controlling Law:** Sawyer v. Whitley requires clear and convincing proof that no reasonable factfinder could find death eligibility. Schlup v. Delo supplies the different gateway for innocence of the offense. Edwards v. Carpenter requires a properly available ineffective-assistance claim before it supplies cause. None decides this noncapital extension.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep gateway innocence, cause and prejudice, the underlying constitutional claim, and §2254 merits restrictions separate. A state-law sentencing error alone does not authorize a federal writ.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§2254 and 2243; Texas Penal Code habitual-offender sequencing requirements; Fourteenth and Sixth Amendments.
* **Material Other Authority:** In re Winship and Strickland v. Washington identify possible underlying constitutional routes. Extension of an existing proof rule to recidivist findings cannot be silently assumed.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Current gateways address crime innocence and death eligibility; ordinary dissatisfaction with sentencing discretion is not innocence.
* **Open Point:** Whether clear factual ineligibility for a statutory noncapital enhancement deserves a gateway and what remains necessary before relief.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Fifth Circuit treated innocence of habitual-offender status as sufficient to excuse the default.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Texas invokes finality and argues that the exceptional sentencing gateway concerns death; any relief should rest on a properly adjudicated constitutional claim.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Haley distinguishes factual ineligibility from a discretionary sentencing mistake and argues that an admitted chronology defect makes continued enhancement custody a miscarriage of justice.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Adjudicating the nondefaulted ineffective-assistance claim could supply equivalent relief and cause without a new innocence gateway.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Extending the gateway to statutory eligibility permits constitutional review but does not itself compel a writ. Refusing extension leaves cause and prejudice and any nondefaulted claim. A threshold remand could require adjudication of ineffective assistance first. Relief, if independently warranted, is a lawful sentence or release from custody unsupported by such a sentence, not erasure of the theft conviction.
