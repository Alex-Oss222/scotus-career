**Statute:** 28 U.S.C. §1782(a) authorizes the district court where a person resides or is found to order testimony, a statement, a document, or another thing for use in a proceeding in a foreign or international tribunal, including criminal investigations before formal accusation. The order may follow a tribunal's letter rogatory or request or an application by an interested person. It may direct production before a court-appointed person, who may administer the necessary oath and take testimony. The court may prescribe the practice and procedure, including foreign or international practice in whole or part. Unless otherwise prescribed, the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure govern. No person may be compelled to produce evidence in violation of a legally applicable privilege. Subsection (b) preserves voluntary production for foreign or international proceedings in a manner acceptable to the provider. The statute's permission to assist is not a command to order production.

The statutory text and amendment notes are available at https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1782. The amendment history identifies the 1964 enlargement and the 1996 insertion concerning preaccusation criminal investigations; no post-event amendment is used. ## 105. Duckworth: statutory tribunal before ordinary merits

**Enacted allocation:** Section 2284(a) requires a three-judge district court when an action challenges the constitutionality of congressional apportionment or apportionment of a statewide legislative body. Under subsection (b)(1), upon a request the district judge immediately notifies the chief circuit judge unless three judges are not required; the latter designates two judges, at least one a circuit judge. Subsection (b)(3) permits a single judge to conduct proceedings other than trial and enter otherwise permitted orders, but forbids appointing a master, ordering a reference, deciding a preliminary or permanent injunction or a motion to vacate it, or entering judgment on the merits. A single-judge temporary restraining order requires an evidence-based finding of specified irreparable damage and lasts only until the three-judge court hears and determines preliminary relief unless earlier revoked. The full court may review single-judge action before final judgment. Subsection (b)(2) requires at least five days' notice by registered or certified mail to the Governor and attorney general when the action is against a State or its officer or agency.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2284. The amendment history shows the operative text last amended in 1984. The current display is used only for that unchanged text, not later decisional law.

That older statute was broader; its substantiality doctrine does not supply permission to ignore §2284's present apportionment category or merits limitation.
