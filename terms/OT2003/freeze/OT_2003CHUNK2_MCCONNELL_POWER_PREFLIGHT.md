## 1. Claims actually presented

The McConnell appellants' Question 1 expressly challenges BCRA section 101 as exceeding Article I, section 4, independently of its First Amendment, equal-protection and vagueness objections. Their Part I.B, pages 26-35, develops that power claim against four categories: national-party participation in state and local elections; state/local-party activity; federal-officeholder and federal-candidate solicitation for nonfederal elections; and state/local-candidate communications referring to and supporting or opposing federal candidates. It asks that section 101 fall in its entirety on the power ground. It expressly does **not** challenge the premise that Congress may regulate financing of federal campaigns. That limit must not be expanded into a concession that every BCRA classification actually regulates a federal campaign.

The political-party appellants' Question 1(b) separately identifies Article I, section 4, federalism and the Tenth Amendment. Their Part II, pages 78-90, argues that Title I exceeds the enumerated election power and, alternatively, intrudes on state electoral self-government even if some affirmative power exists. Their standing section, pages 84-86, claims personal injury from restrictions and potential sanctions on the parties themselves; it rejects characterization of the entire claim as assertion of absent States' rights. Their applications emphasize section 323(a)'s national-party rule and section 323(b)'s state/local-party rule, including off-year and mixed activity. The breadth and effects attributed to the statute are advocacy; the enacted text and supported findings remain necessary for any adopted application.

These are distinct from, though sometimes overlapping with, whether the restrictions are fitted to a sufficiently important anticorruption or anti-circumvention interest under the First Amendment. A conclusion about speech scrutiny cannot silently supply an Article I source or resolve the separate structural theory. Conversely, recognizing affirmative federal authority does not resolve the First Amendment objection.

**Related narrow argument:** The McConnell brief's section 318 discussion expressly adds a short federalism objection insofar as the minors rule reaches donations of state-regulated funds. Preserve that alternative with the already presented section 318 component. The source does not supply a separate developed constitutional framework, new factual record or independent challenge to all state contribution regulation. An independent power rationale for that component would require its own statutory fit and sufficient source basis; the argument is not erased merely because the First Amendment receives more attention.

**Statute:** Use the existing exact enacted selection `sources/mcconnell/BCRA-selected-exact.md`, particularly section 101's sections 323(a)-(f), section 301(20)'s four affirmative categories and four exclusions, and section 401's severability direction. Preserve every actor, trigger and exception. The title-wide power challenge does not make all subsections operate alike. The complete Levin conditions remain in the enacted selection and validated C packet. In section 323(e)(2), paragraph (1) does not apply to solicitation, receipt or spending by a covered individual who is or was also a state/local candidate, solely in connection with that election, when permitted by state law and referring only to that candidate, other candidates for the same office, or both. Section 323(e)(3) permits attendance, speaking and featured-guest participation at a state/district/local-party fundraising event notwithstanding paragraph (1) or subsection (b)(2)(C). Section 323(e)(4)(A) permits a general solicitation without specifying use for a section 501(c) organization exempt under section 501(a), or with a pending application, except an entity principally conducting the section 301(20)(A)(i)-(ii) registration or voter-identification/turnout/generic activities. Section 323(e)(4)(B) separately permits specific solicitation for those activities, or an entity principally conducting them, only from individuals and up to $20,000 solicited from each in a calendar year. Section 323(f)(2) excludes a communication connected to the covered individual's state/local election that refers only to that individual, other candidates for the same state/local office held or sought, or both. None can disappear into a description of a universal nonfederal-funds ban. This supplement creates no new statutory construction and does not reopen the validated statute-existence determination.

## 3. Actual entering law and its limits

**Personal injury and the distinct prudential issue.** The Standards and Tests entry **Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing**, effective as to its local-use clarification January 12, 2000, retains Lujan's June 12, 1992 concrete injury, traceability, redressability and stage-appropriate proof requirements. The actual Printz jurisdiction holding unanimously recognizes a concrete injury when federal work is imposed directly on the plaintiff officers, but disallows advisory adjudication of obligations imposed only on absent dealers or purchasers. That official-duty case is relevant by analogy to direct regulatory injury; it does not decide private parties' standing to assert every State interest.

**Guillen leaves the precise threshold open.** The actual January 14, 2003 decision unanimously sustains the confined highway-information protection under Commerce and Necessary and Proper authority. It expressly leaves both the separate, unaddressed-below Tenth Amendment dual-sovereignty claim and the alternative objection to the private respondents' standing unresolved. It therefore supplies neither a categorical private-party bar nor a categorical permission. Its acceptance of the enumerated-power question cannot be inflated into a decision of the reserved question.

**Tennessee Electric must be read in context.** The pre-divergence decision, 306 U.S. 118, involved utilities complaining of competition under nonexclusive franchises, not a statute imposing the challenged funding prohibitions directly on those utilities. Its paragraph 15 includes the absent-State standing language on which the lower judge relies, in the setting of the rejected claim that federal competitive rates and resale conditions supplied an invasion of the utilities' own rights. The complete Court opinion and footnotes were obtained from the primary text reproduced by Cornell LII and saved as `sources/mcconnell/tennessee-electric-306-118.txt`; the exact relevant paragraph is in the source appendix. A broader rule or a distinction for directly regulated litigants must be justified, not treated as already adopted by Guillen. The source's omitted material is counsel argument, not an asserted missing Court opinion.

**Election power has its own constitutional source.** Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, February 26, 1996, is unanimous and authored by O'Connor. It sustains the NVRA's specified congressional-election registration duties under Article I, section 4, applying Smiley's registration principle and Siebold's shared-administration rule. It distinguishes New York's ordinary-commerce compulsion rule because the Constitution expressly allocates congressional-election authority. It separately sustains safeguards for California's chosen popular presidential election through Burroughs's national protective principle implemented by necessary-and-proper legislation; Article I, section 4 alone does not cover presidential elections. It preserves substantive qualifications and state-election administration, rejects a general power to direct state administration, and requires statutory fidelity and sensitivity to federalism in implementation. Its particular registration holding supplies an important source distinction; it is not a holding that every restriction called federal-election activity is valid campaign regulation.

**State electoral authority is not interchangeable with federal-office authority.** Thornton, May 22, 1995, holds that States cannot add personal congressional qualifications through ordinary ballot exclusion; it preserves genuine electoral administration and distinct state-office rules. Cook, February 28, 2001, forbids official congressional-ballot policy penalties. Their source propositions concern federal offices; neither converts the State's own electoral process into a field of plenary congressional authority. The Standards and Tests entry **Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions**, effective February 28, 2001 for Cook's addition, carries those limits.

**The actual structural rule differs from the historical Printz shorthand.** The entry **Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations**, effective June 19, 1992, carries New York's prohibition against requiring an unwilling State to regulate private waste generators under federal instructions or accept their property and liabilities. It preserves generally applicable regulation of state activity, and government assent does not enlarge constitutional power. The Standards and Tests entry for Printz/Mack, effective June 27, 1997, carries Thomas's five-Justice holding that Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative investigative, screening or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons; the defect is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement. Its reach to purely ministerial record-processing has only four votes. Direct regulation of state activity, state-court duties, voluntary cooperation and valid conditional spending remain; duties under constitutional grants specifically involving state governmental action, including federal elections, are reserved, and there is no compensation rule. The printed historical Printz and Alden language in the party brief is advocacy quoting another timeline, not this Court's present rule.

Reno v. Condon, January 12, 2000, unanimously distinguishes direct regulation of a State's own information handling and private redistribution from assigning the State a program governing unrelated persons. Necessary compliance work alone does not transform the former into the latter. The case also requires an actual supported commerce connection; direct regulation is not an independent grant of federal power. It leaves general applicability's indispensability and a States-only scheme unresolved.

**Commerce doctrine is an analogy only unless a valid Commerce ground is actually reached.** The actual Lopez requires an identifiable interstate mechanism before aggregation; it rejects a traditional-local-subject immunity, an economic/noneconomic switch and judicial insertion of a new statutory element. The latest effective supplied application, Holston, October 30, 2003, unanimously requires material support and reasonable fit for inclusion of a local class to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime. It distinguishes an enacted historical-movement element from the constitutional connection. Those cases instruct careful identification of power and mechanism. They do not make interstate commerce an element of the distinct Elections Clause or mechanically transfer the Commerce test into campaign regulation. No replacement Commerce justification is invented from the fact that money is spent.

## 4. Competing lawful paths to preserve for independent modeling

1. **Reach a regulated plaintiff's own enumerated-power claim.** A party personally subject to the funding rule may argue that the asserted law lacks constitutional authority, rather than purporting to represent a State. The strongest objection is the lower judge's view that this particular theory depends entirely on another sovereign's reserved election power. The response must identify the plaintiff's direct injury and the legal distinction between challenging federal authority over that conduct and suing solely for a State. Guillen leaves the specific private Tenth issue open; Lujan's actual injury requirements still apply.

2. **Maintain a distinct prudential limit for a State-rights-only claim.** Tennessee Electric and the actual lower reasoning supply this competing path if the claim, properly characterized, belongs only to an absent State. Its strongest obstacle is that these plaintiffs identify direct federal prohibitions, not merely competitive disadvantage, and present a separate enumerated-power claim. A dismissal on the narrower third-party theory must explain its exact scope. It cannot silently dispose of the direct power claim, declare the statute constitutionally valid, or be relabeled an Article III absence without independent support.

3. **Sustain a provision through its supported federal-election connection.** The enacted actor, transaction, communication and circumvention mechanism may support the conclusion that Congress regulates a national election or reasonably protects it notwithstanding incidental consequences for local campaign financing. National officeholder solicitation and communications directly supporting/opposing a federal candidate are independently important settings. The strongest objection is coverage of off-year, genuinely local or generic activity without the asserted connection. The response must address the actual statutory category and record, preserve the presidential source distinction, and satisfy the independent First Amendment standard. A federal actor's identity alone is not an unexplained answer to every local application.

4. **Reject or confine a provision whose power ground exceeds the supported connection.** The Court could conclude that a particular category regulates state/local electoral activity outside the asserted federal source, or reject an overbroad facial theory while preserving lawful applications, depending on enacted text and remedy law. The strongest objection is that segregated local channels can still form a demonstrated means of evading a valid federal restriction, and a mixed election does not eliminate federal authority. Any narrowing must be a permissible reading of the enacted text; Lopez's no-added-element principle does not permit a substitute statute. The decree must identify the provision/application, the sufficient ground and section 401's severability operation. Missing support cannot be invented as a finding that a channel is categorically harmless or corrupting.

5. **Resolve the independent structural claim on its actual operation.** Direct restrictions on private parties may differ from commands to state legislatures or officers, as New York and Condon require. The alternative argues that intrusion into the State's own electoral self-government creates a distinct structural limit even without such a command. Neither the private-actor label nor the general importance of state elections decides whether Congress has an affirmative source and whether an additional limit applies. Voting Rights Coalition's election-specific source must be addressed, and actual Printz cannot be replaced with its historical formulation. A sufficient narrower resolution may leave a broader theory undecided only within the complete-resolution rule and with the actual reason stated.

These paths describe alternatives, not provisional holdings. The section 318 alternative retains its own statutory text and existing component relief. For any provision independently disposed of on a sufficient constitutional ground, distinguish what must still be resolved to support the actual decree from a question unnecessary to that decree. Do not use a general statement of nonreach to leave a surviving challenged provision's live power objection unanswered.
