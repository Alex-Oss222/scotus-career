## Complete constitutional rule and remedy

Congress may adopt section 9658's limited federal commencement rule as a necessary and proper means of implementing its valid regulation of commercial hazardous-waste generation, handling, disposal and environmental response. The identified means addresses an actual feature of that scheme: latent exposure injuries can escape the remedial cost allocation because an earlier commencement rule ends the claim before the plaintiff knew or reasonably should have known its cause. Preserving the existing state-law remedy through that causal-discovery date gives responsible actors a reason to prevent or remediate the covered release and preserves the cost consequences of the regulated activity. The connection is the rule's operation upon the covered toxic exposure, not merely its placement within CERCLA or the national importance of environmental health.

The rule operates in state-law personal-injury or property-damage actions caused or contributed to by exposure to a hazardous substance, pollutant or contaminant released into the environment from a facility. It replaces an earlier state commencement date with the federally required date; otherwise state limitations law remains operative. Actions under section 9607 remain excluded by subsection (a)(3). The ordinary federal date depends on actual or reasonable knowledge of the causal link; subsection (b)(4)(B) preserves its distinct later-date rules for minors and incompetent plaintiffs. These are statutory coverage limits, not findings that any plaintiff has established toxic causation, the discovery date or damages.

State courts must apply that valid federal rule under Article VI. It governs the parties' legal rights within an existing judicial action, without compelling a legislature to enact a program or an executive to administer federal private-party regulation. Calling commencement a procedural subject does not defeat the constitutional source or the Supremacy Clause. The duty is not justified by Article VI alone: the Commerce and Necessary and Proper foundation must first exist.

Affirm the Second Circuit judgment in the reviewed constitutional respects. Its separate vacatur of the limitations summary judgment and fact-sensitive discovery remand remain operative. The district court applies the existing remand to the actual discovery evidence and remaining preserved issues. This Court's constitutional decision does not enlarge the granted questions into new review of the lower court's survival, wrongful-death, state-period or accrual constructions.

## Alternative-path assessment

**Validity path.** Identify the independently valid hazardous-waste program, the exposure-remedy class actually covered, and the concrete causal-discovery obstacle the limited commencement rule removes. Home Builders requires the genuine functional connection and permits reasonably adapted means without a least-restrictive-means test. Guillen demonstrates that a confined rule operating in litigation can implement a demonstrated interstate mechanism; Jinks demonstrates that a federal limitations rule is not constitutionally barred merely because it changes the timing of a state cause of action.

**Invalidity path.** Congress has no general power over state tort procedure. The strongest objection is that federal cleanup liability already exists, while section 9658 prolongs state-law claims that Congress did not create, so its relationship to the program could be described as only a generalized incentive shared by all tort law. That objection would prevail if national importance, incidental litigation costs or a statutory label were the only connection. It fails for these industrial-release actions because the exact class and latency mechanism are identified: premature accrual predictably removes the cost of the very hazardous exposure the program regulates. The separate federal cleanup remedy need not be the exclusive permissible means, and Congress need not establish that every possible exception would quantitatively defeat enforcement.

**Commandeering path.** Defendants identify a real displacement of the State's chosen deadline, but displacement of inconsistent law in a private dispute is the work of supremacy, not legislative or executive conscription. New York and Printz retain their limits. Jinks directly rejects a categorical state-deadline immunity, while Testa identifies state judges' distinct constitutional duty. Guillen's reservation of its separate Tenth Amendment contention supplies no contrary holding and cannot be reported as having decided this issue.
