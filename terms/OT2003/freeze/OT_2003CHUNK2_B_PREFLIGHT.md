## 16. United States v. Banks — December 2, 2003

**Validated posture and facts.** The Ninth Circuit's March 5, 2002 judgment concerns a conditional plea following denial of suppression. Officers executed the cocaine warrant at about 2 p.m. in a small two-bedroom, one-bath apartment. A loud knock and identification brought no response or audible activity during approximately fifteen to twenty seconds. Banks's shower use was unknown to the officers. The later discovery cannot justify or defeat the earlier action. The warrant and information about cocaine supply context; neither establishes a categorical drug-case excuse.

The lower majority used a four-category framework distinguishing forcible from nondestructive entry and exigency from its absence. Judge Fisher dissented from the entry ruling while joining rejection of the independent voluntariness and counsel claims. The latter claims are outside the supplied Supreme question. The model must not reopen them or portray the judgment below as a holding that every challenged statement was independently inadmissible.

**Entering-law question.** Wilson makes announcement part of reasonableness. Richards rejects categorical narcotics exceptions, requires particularized reasonable suspicion for an excuse, and leaves timing to the facts. Ramirez rejects a higher exigency threshold merely because entry damages property, while separately requiring reasonable force. Its controlling statutory holding concerns a supported danger excuse under §3109; it did not establish every destruction-of-evidence exception. Section 3109's notice, refusal and alternative liberation language must be treated as statutory requirements, not collapsed into a general constitutional conclusion.

There are two independent analytic choices. First, do the announced warrant, apartment size, time, elapsed interval, lack of response and readily disposable evidence together justify this entry? Second, did the officers have a statutory refusal or a properly supported common-law excuse? An implied refusal rationale may avoid deciding the full reach of a destruction exception; that is a legal choice requiring explanation, not an automatic inference from silence. Conversely, an excuse holding must identify the statutory basis and its limits. Rejecting the lower court's force tier does not by itself establish reasonable entry under the proper test.

**Justice-specific forks for modeling.** The prior public Richards and Ramirez positions supply common opposition to a drug-case categorical rule and to a force-dependent heightened threshold. The principal possible disagreement is application of the particularized standard, not reinvention of the threshold. Scalia's Ornelas dissent and the distinct Richards review alignment require keeping review methodology separate from the underlying reasonableness assessment. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's individualized-inference positions in Wardlow and Valentine warrant testing whether this record has a genuinely particular connection to destruction; those cases concerned street seizures and do not answer how long officers must wait after a lawful warrant announcement. O'Connor, Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas's broader factual-inference positions in those cases are evidence to examine, not a promised vote here. Every participating Justice still needs an independently grounded application to the actual facts.

**Disposition boundaries.** A reversal sustaining entry removes this asserted suppression ground and permits ordinary proceedings on remand; it need not adjudicate unrelated statement objections anew. If entry was unlawful, exclusion and the appropriate effect on the conditional conviction require their own existing-law analysis. Do not import any later exclusionary-rule decision. Any approved rule must preserve both the absence of a universal fifteen-second rule and the independent restraint on unnecessary property destruction.

**Validated record.** Hernandez's 1991 resignation following a positive cocaine test is not challenged. **Entering-law question.** Hicks, Hazen Paper and Reeves distinguish a legally permissible explanation, its truth, and ultimate intentional discrimination. Nor does rehabilitation protection automatically negate the ADA's treatment of workplace misconduct.

**Justice-specific forks and remedy.** Scalia's unanimous Reeves opinion and his controlling Hicks position require testing both errors: treating a neutral reason as legally unavailable and treating its legal availability as conclusive truth. ## 19. Virginia v. Maryland — December 9, 2003

**Instrument addendum.** The 1785 Compact's Article Seventh protects adjoining shore property, associated benefits and wharves or improvements while preserving navigation; its fishing clause and exceptions are distinct. Article Eighth requires mutual consent for specified fish, quarantine, channel and ballast regulations. The 1877 award, approved by Congress in 1879, places Virginia's boundary at low water and preserves use beyond it necessary to full enjoyment of riparian ownership, subject to navigation and Maryland's proper use. Those limits are operative, not optional qualifications.

The Potomac River Compact of 1958, congressionally approved in 1962, must also be acknowledged. Article VII §1 expressly preserves the existing riparian rights and the judicial constructions of the pertinent 1785 provision. Article IX replaces the former compact after the prescribed effective-date condition; Article VII prevents replacement from erasing the preserved riparian right. Do not say the entire 1785 compact simply remains unchanged and independently operative. Article VII §2 separately preserves certain Maryland fishery laws subject to commission change; §3 separately governs existing fishing licenses. Neither is an exception that extinguishes §1's riparian protection. The federal consent preserves federal rights, jurisdiction and congressional commerce power; Article VIII prohibits unilateral state alteration. The earlier 1958 draft in the historical appendix differs from the enacted version in some unrelated provisions, including treatment of fishing licenses; use the official enacted text in the source extracts.

**Record and exceptions.** The supported Virginia reply recitation identifies four questions before the Master: mootness, coverage of the entire river including nontidal waters, regulatory authority and acquiescence. Maryland excepted to the latter two; do not present the first two as exceptions still requiring an adversarial decision here. The eventual permit does not remove the pleaded sovereign regulation dispute.

Virginia's source reports official awareness of Maryland's permit program in 1973 and protests beginning in 1976. Fairfax received a water-appropriation permit in 1974, a construction permit in 1977 and subsequent amendments. Its 1996 proposal sought an offshore intake approximately 725 feet from shore in a river approximately 2,000 feet wide, for water quality and efficiency. Maryland denied it in December 1997 for asserted lack of need rather than a demonstrated navigational injury. The source reports later administrative findings favoring the project and the eventual permit's flow restriction. These are relevant to adversity and the claimed unilateral control, but statements from Virginia's brief remain attributed recitations. Do not convert assertions about officials' political intent into findings or presume that every earlier permit application was an informed surrender of sovereign authority.

**Entering-law and Justice-specific forks.** The question is the federal instruments' allocation of authority, not a general rule that ownership always entails regulation or that riparian rights always defeat it. New Jersey v. New York distinguishes the compact settlement and supported prescription/acquiescence. Souter, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer's controlling treatment requires examining adverse exercise, state knowledge and acceptance. Stevens's different assessment of the historical practice there makes the permit history materially relevant, but this shorter, protested practice cannot be treated as the same record. Scalia and Thomas's practical-construction approach to ambiguity requires testing whether the text is ambiguous here and whether the conduct has comparable explanatory force. No automatic importation of any earlier coalition is justified. Arizona v. California supplies limits on disturbing interstate decrees; it is not a universal ownership-based regulation rule.

**Lawful scope and source limit.** A declaration against unilateral Maryland licensing must retain navigation, Maryland's proper use and appropriate federal interstate remedies; it cannot authorize unlimited diversion or declare all effects on Maryland irrelevant. Conversely, sustaining regulation requires a defined compatible regulatory power that does not nullify the express allocation. Acquiescence requires a supported adverse assertion and acceptance, not applications alone. The published neutral record and bounded recitation are sufficient for modeling this dispute under §14. If a proposed disposition depends on an additional specific Maryland episode establishing surrender, obtain that narrowly identified factual source rather than inventing it or treating the absence of an excerpt as absence of evidence.

## 21. United States v. Deaton — December 11, 2003

**Validated scope and connection.** This is the second appeal; the earlier sidecasting holding is settled and not reopened. The owners excavated an approximately 1,100-foot ditch on a twelve-acre property intended for a five-lot subdivision and deposited material in wetlands. Adjacency to the roadside ditch is conceded. The route is roadside ditch, culvert, John Adkins Prong of Perdue Creek, Beaverdam Creek, Wicomico River and Chesapeake Bay. The navigable river is approximately eight miles away along the route and the bay approximately thirty-two. The existence of the drainage route is agreed; the amount and consistency of water flow are disputed. Do not upgrade agreement about the route into an unqualified finding of continuous flow at every point and time.

**Statutory and administrative components.** Address §404 authority, the §1362(7) definition and the actual tributary/adjacent-wetland regulation. SWANCC rejected bird-use-only authority over the isolated ponds presented and left Riverside Bayview in force. It did not impose a universal direct-adjacency or present-navigability requirement, and it did not decide the Commerce Clause question. The lower court reports that the owners did not press a direct-adjacency-only construction here. The Corps interpreted tributaries to include the relevant feeder system, supported by its longstanding interpretation; a former headwaters permit cutoff does not itself define the outer statutory or regulatory meaning of tributary.

Separate delegated statutory interpretation under Mead/Chevron from interpretation of the Corps' own regulation under Auer. The latter requires the responsible agency's fair, considered construction and excludes an interpretation inconsistent with the regulatory text or a mere litigation invention. The source provides both historical regulatory material and application facts; ecological desirability alone supplies neither delegation nor statutory authorization.

The wetland determination is a separate component. Vegetation and hydric soil are not disputed; hydrology is. The Corps used the 1987 Manual's root-zone saturation indicator, including water within twelve inches of the surface, while the owners relied on a lengthy monitoring record and a competing surface-saturation view. The lower court found the Corps applied rather than contradicted the Manual and upheld the scientific method. This is not authority to announce that every property with water twelve inches below ground is a regulated wetland regardless of the rest of the definition. Preserve the regulatory combination, relevant duration/conditions, and the actual issue raised. The complete lower source remains available for the precise Manual passages.

**Constitutional routes and Justice-specific forks.** The channel-protection route concerns authority to protect navigable receiving waters from demonstrated upstream pollution. The historical authorities include Rio Grande Dam, Appalachian Electric Power and Grand River Dam. The alternative NAHB externality route has its own requirements: a defined class, concrete and substantial interstate spillover, limits on state internalization and fitted regulation. General environmental importance cannot substitute for these requirements. Class-wide aggregation follows an eligible interstate mechanism; it does not generate one. A sufficient channel-protection ground can make an independent externality holding unnecessary, but the model must state that choice.

Holston's October 30 holding applies. Its treatment of an interstate market and necessary regulation of a class does not itself prove the hydrological mechanism in Deaton; its rejection of an equipment-only shortcut is also relevant to avoiding generic connections. Ho's effective holding must be added before freezing Deaton. The copied Lopez/NAHB positions and current Commerce standard supersede incompatible historical reasoning in the Fourth Circuit opinion. Scalia, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas's SWANCC statutory positions require confronting this connected system and the retained Riverside Bayview rule. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's distinct positions there require the same statutory discipline; disagreement with the isolated-pond outcome is not free-standing jurisdiction over all water. Auer and Mead positions, including Scalia's Mead dissent, separately bear on the method of interpretation. None replaces each Justice's actual legal and factual analysis here.

**Remedy.** The restoration order requires refilling the ditch and restoring previolation elevation. The owners proposed removal of sidecast soil to other nonwetland property, which the lower court found would preserve the drainage benefit and risk further harm. Review the actual reasons and the applicable abuse-of-discretion standard. Upholding jurisdiction does not automatically uphold every restoration method; invalidating a finding does not authorize unrelated redesign. The supplied relief encompasses jurisdiction, delineation and restoration, so each must receive a disposition supported by its own ground.

## 22. Maryland v. Pringle — December 15, 2003

**Validated facts and limits.** The vehicle was stopped at about 3:16 a.m. The driver and owner, Partlow, consented to search; Pringle sat in front and Smith in the rear. The officer first saw rolled cash when the driver opened the glove compartment for registration; the search found $763 there and five cocaine baggies behind the rear-seat armrest, accessible after pulling it down. The drugs were concealed, not openly displayed in Pringle's view. The occupants offered no ownership information. A disputed account of a promise to release others if someone claimed the drugs is not an established finding and is unnecessary to the supplied probable-cause question.

The subsequent confession followed warnings at the station and cannot establish antecedent probable cause. Maryland possession law allows actual or constructive dominion or control by one or more persons and requires knowledge. The probable-cause inquiry is a practical probability of the investigated offense, not proof of those elements beyond reasonable doubt.

The Court of Appeals majority required a sufficient individualized connection; Judge Raker's concurrence explicitly distinguished arrest probability from trial sufficiency. The dissent relied on the combined private-car setting, late hour, packaged drugs, cash and possible shared enterprise. Neither side should be rewritten as adopting a general all-passengers rule. Ybarra's public-place strangers and Di Re's information pointing to a specific other participant are important comparisons, not automatic answers.

**Justice-specific forks.** Ornelas requires independent review of the legal probable-cause conclusion with deference to supported facts and appropriate weight to grounded local inferences; Scalia's recorded disagreement with the review method remains separately relevant. Wardlow and Valentine establish this Court's requirement of a supported connection to wrongdoing. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's application positions require confronting the difference between mere presence and a supported shared enterprise, while O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's broader factual-inference positions require explaining what connects this passenger to this contraband. A known crime involving concealed cocaine differs from lawful gun possession; the higher probable-cause threshold differs from reasonable suspicion. Neither difference alone dictates the outcome. Refusal to volunteer ownership is not an independent badge of guilt; the analysis concerns what the total known circumstances support about possible shared possession.

**Suppression and scope.** The state high court held attenuation unpreserved and alternatively rejected it under the applicable factors, including continuing custody. Independent source and inevitable discovery were not raised. If the arrest is lawful, this arrest supplies no derivative exclusion ground. If it is unlawful, the model cannot invent a preserved attenuation argument or treat Miranda warnings alone as dispositive. The search's validity is outside the grant. Independently preserved confession questions remain separate; do not resolve a new coercion issue from the disputed promise account. Refresh Banks's effective holding, but do not transfer its home-entry inquiry into passenger probable cause.

## 23. Castro v. United States — December 15, 2003

**Validated sequence and date limitation.** Castro's 1994 Rule 33 motion concerned newly discovered evidence about a prosecution witness's immunity arrangement. The government did not object to treatment under Rule 33 and §2255; Castro's reply insisted on his chosen Rule 33 vehicle. The district court denied the motion under both labels without advance warning about conversion, successive-filing consequences or an opportunity to withdraw or amend. His appeal did not contest the label. His express 1997 §2255 motion raised ineffective assistance. The later appellate proceedings involved merits development and classification; the resulting dismissal treated the 1994 conversion as the first motion.

The neutral packet gives July 5, 1994 and April 18, 1997; the complete lower opinion states July 11, 1994 and April 22, 1997. No source here establishes why those dates differ, and no mailbox explanation is inferred. The material sequence is undisputed: unwarned conversion before AEDPA, express later motion after AEDPA. Public discussion may use 1994 and 1997. Exact dates need resolution only if a proposed timeliness holding depends on them; ordinary independent timeliness questions can remain for remand. Neither set of dates establishes merits entitlement or a limitations defense by itself.

**Review-channel component.** Castro did not apply for circuit authorization. Felker and the federal successive-motion standard bar review of an actual authorization determination, while Stewart distinguishes the antecedent classification whether authorization is needed. The lower court's additional observation about inability to meet successive requirements does not automatically convert the actual action into an authorization proceeding. Determine the action being reviewed and the statute's scope before deciding conversion. Do not use a correctable classification label as a route to review an actual authorization denial in another case.

**Conversion component and Justice-specific forks.** The lower majority treated the prior merits adjudication and failure to challenge its characterization as sufficient. Judge Roney's dissent relied on the uninformed loss of a first-motion opportunity, with Raineri, Adams, Miller, Seesing, Kelly and Henderson supplying date-eligible lower-court alternatives. These are persuasive paths, not controlling holdings of this Court. Stewart's successive-classification reasoning and Lonchar's rejection of an extra-statutory dismissal substitute are relevant but do not already establish the exact proposed notice rule. The warning question concerns when judicial relabeling counts for future statutory restrictions; it is not an equitable license to disregard AEDPA once its predicates are met.

The model must test, Justice by Justice, whether substantive adjudication under §2255 suffices regardless of the prisoner's informed choice, or whether notice, explanation of successive consequences and an opportunity to withdraw or amend are required before conversion consumes the first opportunity. The copied Felker, Stewart and Lonchar positions distinguish statutory review restrictions, classification and unauthorized procedural barriers. Their several rationales must be applied to this different conversion problem rather than treated as a preexisting unanimous warning rule. Failure to appeal a label without being told its consequences is evidence to evaluate, not conclusive informed consent. Conversely, a notice rule needs a defensible source in federal judicial administration and the statutory structure; fairness alone does not repeal enacted gates.

**Remedy boundaries.** A ruling that the 1994 conversion cannot count for successive restrictions need not reopen the old Rule 33 merits denial. It can vacate the later dismissal for ordinary first-motion proceedings, with limitations, other independent gates and ineffective-assistance merits unresolved. A blanket prohibition on every beneficial pro se recharacterization is broader than required to resolve the adverse counting consequence. A disposition sustaining dismissal must explain the statutory first-motion predicate rather than relying only on the prior court's label.
