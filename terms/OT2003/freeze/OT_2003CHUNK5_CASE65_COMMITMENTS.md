## Judgment components

**Elected trustees.** Affirm the injunction requiring Hawaii to permit otherwise qualified non-Hawaiians to run for OHA trustee and serve if elected. The independently presented equal-protection claim is sufficient: this public state office may not impose the supplied ancestry qualification when Hawaii establishes no substantial, evidence-supported relationship between excluding all non-Hawaiians and the faithful administration of the beneficiary programs. The duty to serve the beneficiaries can be imposed on every trustee without making ancestry the condition of access to elected state office. Rice establishes the public state-election setting and ancestry's racial operation; it did not already decide candidacy. Adarand supplies the governing governmental burden; Concrete Works requires actual justification and fit rather than purpose alone. Nixon v. Herndon supplies the older protection against state-created racial exclusion from public political participation.

The State's preservation of the trust and effective service to beneficiaries is a serious lawful purpose. It does not establish that every person without the ancestry is incapable of faithful trusteeship or that every person with it has the required connection. The classification concerns complete exclusion from public office, not a preference within a supported remedial benefit allocation. The judgment reaches only the ancestry candidate restriction and the resulting inability to serve if elected. Existing neutral qualifications and fiduciary responsibilities remain enforceable under their own terms.

**Appointment restriction.** Affirm the circuit's vacatur of the appointments ruling and its direction to dismiss that component for lack of standing. Election-related injury does not establish actual or imminent appointment-related injury. Warth's claim-specific requirement governs; no appointment merits conclusion follows. All eight join this component.

## Enhanced alternative-path assessment

**Invalidation under equal protection.** The claimant is personally excluded from nomination on a racial ancestry criterion in an established state election. The strongest contrary argument is that a trustee's relationship to the beneficiary community is part of the office itself, so candidate eligibility should differ from the broad electorate addressed in Rice. The difference is real and requires a new holding, but it does not establish the necessary fit: OHA is a public state office; the government has identified fiduciary service, not sovereign membership, as its function. The lower record supplies no material disputed fact requiring a new trial about the candidate's ordinary qualifications. Each Justice's commitment rests on that concrete burden and function.

**Upholding a beneficiary-representation qualification.** This path would treat ancestry as an adequately related condition for managing the beneficiary trust after all voters have equal access. Its strongest objection is that a permitted beneficiary program does not itself justify depriving otherwise qualified citizens of the ability to seek public office. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply their common-standard commitments and find the complete exclusion unjustified; they do not reject beneficiary-related measures as a category. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's Rice commitments reinforce the public-office distinction, and their earlier stricter scrutiny supplies an additional reason against the State's position rather than a reason to withhold the narrower common-standard application.

**Alternative Fifteenth Amendment and §2 paths.** The candidate exclusion affects voters' choices and the nomination process, so those are serious independent grounds. The State's strongest objection is that all citizens may now vote irrespective of ancestry, making candidate and voter discrimination distinct. Because the preserved equal-protection ground completely resolves the candidate restriction, none of the eight requires deciding the broader Fifteenth Amendment scope. No general §2 candidacy construction is frozen here. If assembly proposes either as an independent controlling ground, it requires a separate proposition-level compatibility and authority test; no judgment agreement silently supplies those joins.

No remand to redesign the OHA program or reevaluate the benefits themselves is authorized. The injunction's election-related operation and appointment dismissal implement the two resolved components. No separate writing is necessary if the common opinion uses the governing equal-protection standard at this actual public-office scope and treats the alternative grounds accurately.
