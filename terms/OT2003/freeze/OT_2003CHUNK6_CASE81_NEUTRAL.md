### Issue 1: Section 5 enforcement of equal court access

* **Controlling Law:** Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas holds that §12202 authorizes private enforcement of reasonable Title II accommodations and anti-retaliation duties enabling meaningful participation in custody adjudication, including bounded prevention beyond independently provable constitutional minima. Garrett held that §5 does not independently sustain Title I's general employment and accommodation requirements against state employers at the full scope presented, while recognizing that disability discrimination can support §5 legislation and that accommodations are not always irrelevant, and it decided no other title; Boerne requires congruence and proportionality. Popovich reserves broader services and adopts no heightened disability scrutiny.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the protected constitutional right, the demonstrated pattern and the fit between preventive obligations and that injury. Valid preventive legislation need not require each claimant to prove an independently actionable constitutional violation.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment §5; ADA Title II, 42 U.S.C. §§12131-12134, and §12202; reasonable-modification and fundamental-alteration limits.
* **Material Other Authority:** The due-process right of a defendant to attend proceedings and equal access to judicial services supply concrete constitutional anchors. Olmstead v. L.C. addresses statutory accommodation, not a general immunity holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** the §5 law permits tailored prevention and remedies; the distinct public-court setting must still be evaluated.
* **Open Point:** Whether the court-access application is a valid §5 remedy and prevention scheme supporting the express enforcement authorization.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit connected Title II court-access claims to due process and sustained the suit despite Tennessee’s immunity objection.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Tennessee argues that the statutory coverage exceeds proven constitutional wrongs and that court-access allegations cannot validate every Title II application.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The claimants point to recurrent exclusion from judicial services and bounded accommodation duties, arguing that Congress may remove predictable barriers before each denial becomes a completed constitutional wrong.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** An Article I theory would require identification of its own substantive connection; the §5 court-access ground can resolve this case without a universal Title II holding.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States defends Congress’s §5 authority and the express authorization of private enforcement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Upholding the court-access application affirms denial of immunity and permits proof of statutory liability. Rejecting it would require determining what independent constitutional or prospective claims remain. A universal ruling about every public service is unnecessary on this record; neither path awards damages on allegations alone.
