# OT2003 chunk 8 — Holland disposition-form reconciliation

Matter 116, Holland v. Jackson, No. 03-1200; June 28, 2004. Narrow historical-reconciliation circulation audit. This separate file clarifies the supported judgment wording without replacing either the [original B commitments](OT_2003CHUNK8_B_COMMITMENTS.md) or [B reconciled commitments](OT_2003CHUNK8_B_RECONCILED.md).

## Proposed disposition and conclusion

> The Sixth Circuit judgment granting habeas relief is reversed, and the case is remanded for separate treatment of the state record, applicant-attributable diligence and any applicable procedural bar, followed by the proper statutory and Strickland inquiries if available; the conviction remains operative pending any lawful grant.

**All eight Associates support this precise reversal-and-remand formulation on the same frozen grounds.** For each, changing the earlier wording from vacate the grant and remand to the sentence above is a formal clarification, not a material revision of the judgment commitment. No recorded row makes vacatur indispensable or objects to finding legal error in the Sixth Circuit's grant. Every row already commits to setting that grant aside because its stated reasoning fails to satisfy the separate statutory, record and prejudice requirements, while leaving the identified lawful inquiries available on remand.

This conclusion concerns this complete sentence and the frozen J1 limits. It does not treat reversal and vacatur as universally interchangeable. Reversal here identifies the erroneous judgment being overturned; the express remand preserves the same unresolved inquiries. It does not direct final denial of Jackson's habeas claim, conclusively reinstate every ground of the district court's decision, or turn the state judgment into an unreviewable disposition. The final clause preserves the same operative conviction status expressly stated in the original handoff.

## Remedial content preserved

The phrase applicant-attributable diligence retains the Michael Wayne Williams inquiry into deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant, including legally attributable counsel conduct. It does not find nondiligence from the seven-year interval. The §2254(e)(2) restrictions apply only if their trigger is established, with their alternative first conditions and cumulative innocence condition intact.

The phrase any applicable procedural bar requires an actually established bar and consideration of any applicable exception. It does not collapse the state court's exclusion of the later proffer into a complete adequate-and-independent default of the underlying claim. The state merits record, a lawful route for considering additional material, the distinct statutory provisions and the constitutional claim remain separate.

The phrase followed by the proper statutory and Strickland inquiries if available preserves the statutory gateway and the need for both constitutionally deficient performance and prejudice before relief. It neither guarantees access to those merits inquiries despite a dispositive restriction nor obliges the lower court to decide unnecessary questions after an independently sufficient ground resolves the claim. Whole-opinion review remains required; neither detached probability wording nor a correct citation alone determines the application. No automatic hearing, permanent exclusion of the Gooch proffer, new trial, final no-prejudice finding or final denial is added.

These are the existing J1 qualifications applied to the supplied sentence, not new conditions imposed to obtain agreement. A later disposition directing final denial, assuming applicant fault, or eliminating an otherwise lawful route would require a new substantive comparison; that is not the sentence reviewed here.
