## I. Factual development, temporal law and genuine state claim default

**Common sufficient ground:** The federal application was filed March 7, 1996. Lindh and Carriger supply former merits and factual-presumption law for the identified provisions, while Slack separately governs certification of this later appeal. Carriger does not settle section 2254(e)(2)'s capital-transition question. The litigation proceeded under former hearing law, and a new statutory hearing bar cannot be assumed. Under this Court's Keeney, Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification remains the governing former-law rule for inadequately developed material facts of a properly presented claim; ordinary negligence alone is insufficient. Banks presented the legal Farr suppression claim to the state courts. The disputed omission is the development of facts concealed by the prosecution and the witness, not an established deliberate bypass. The developed evidence cannot be excluded by replacing Keeney with a different cause-and-prejudice hearing test.

**Independent limits:** Coleman concerns an actual independent and adequate state procedural ground against a claim; it does not automatically reclassify missing facts as a separate default. Vasquez preserves the difference between supplementing the facts of a presented claim and fundamentally altering it into a new one. No sufficient new-claim or independent-default predicate is supplied for Farr. Strickler's concealment and reasonable-disclosure-reliance analysis remains relevant if a genuine default requires excuse, but no freestanding cause ruling is necessary here. Michael Wayne Williams also rejects an inference of applicant-attributed failure merely from an incomplete record; its known-report and concealed-relationship applications remain distinct. Its statutory exceptions are not imposed where the statute's temporal applicability and triggering failure have not been established.

**Competing paths and principal objections:** Texas's strongest path characterizes counsel's existing suspicions about Farr, attempted impeachment and investigative opportunities as reason to require further state-court development. The sound part of that argument is that a disclosure assurance cannot excuse neglect of every known concrete lead. Its defect here is that counsel actually raised the suppression claim and questioned Farr, while the State's disclosure representation and the uncorrected denials withheld the specific paid relationship. More fundamentally, the proposed former-law bar still requires deliberate bypass rather than ordinary negligence. Banks's path permits consideration of the concealed evidence; its limit is that hearing eligibility itself proves neither suppression materiality nor every independently presented claim. No Justice proposes a universal rule exempting pre-enactment petitions from every AEDPA provision.

## II. Farr suppression and sentence relief

**Rule common to all eight:** Brady, Bagley and Kyles require favorable suppression and a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the relevant lawful result. The evidence's legitimate uses in investigation, preparation, corroboration and impeachment are considered together, rather than each item being required to carry the burden alone. Strickler requires a separate penalty inquiry; sufficient proof of capital guilt or an eligibility circumstance does not itself settle death selection. Once a material Brady violation is established, Kyles permits no second duplicative harmlessness hurdle.

**Supported record application for the six granting relief:** Farr portrayed the Dallas trip as a shared plan for robberies and said Banks would take care of trouble. His later account identifies payment by the lead investigator, concern over drug charges and his own instigation of a supposed pharmacy robbery to obtain Banks's gun for the investigation. The investigator later acknowledged the informant relationship and $200 payment. The undisclosed facts support more than another general attack on character: they permit the defense to challenge the source, purpose and apparently independent corroborative force of the asserted plan for future violence. Farr's denials that he told the investigator about the Dallas trip remained uncorrected, while the State attacked the two defense impeachment witnesses for possible bias. Disclosure would have permitted a materially different presentation about Farr's own incentives and creation of the encounter.

The remaining record is serious. Whitehead was shot three times; the conviction and crime circumstances remain part of the penalty assessment. Jefferson described Banks striking him with a gun and threatening him. Banks personally admitted striking Jefferson and traveling to obtain the gun; he denied an intention to participate in robberies but conceded willingness to supply Farr a means and possible weapon for robbery. Those concessions are not ignored. They nevertheless do not make Farr's disputed account of a continuing shared robbery plan cumulative. The six find that effective disclosure of the specific governmental relationship and instigation creates a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty result. That conclusion stands on the Farr claim's own permissible disclosure uses and the sentencing record, without adding an independently alleged deficient-assistance violation to complete the constitutional prejudice showing.

**The two denying relief:** Scalia and Thomas regard the admitted gun assistance, the crime circumstances and the separate pistol assault as sufficient to retain confidence after the complete lawful impeachment is considered. They accept that payment and instigation are qualitatively different from general character impeachment, but find that a paid instigator does not erase Banks's own willingness to furnish the weapon or the independent violence. Their conclusion is a substantive confidence assessment, not a demand for proof of innocence, legal insufficiency of the remaining evidence or a second harmlessness test. They do not equate eligibility with death selection.

## III. The independently presented penalty-assistance alternative

**Six-Justice lawful nonreach:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer grant the full requested sentence remedy on Farr alone. The independently presented assistance theory seeks the same sentence relief and cannot enlarge that remedy here. They therefore do not adjudicate its deficient-performance, separate prejudice or contested factual-expansion questions. This is a complete remedial resolution, not a decision that assistance defects are immaterial or a reservation of any condition needed to obtain the sentence writ.

**Scalia and Thomas reach and reject the alternative:** Because they deny the Farr route, both independently assess Strickland prejudice. They assume for decision that counsel performed deficiently and that the full supported Pina, Cunningham, parental and Jefferson proffer may be considered; they neither adopt the lower appellate court's exhaustion analysis nor decide that a greater factual detail automatically constitutes a new claim. This assumption gives Banks the benefit of the asserted evidence without deciding disputed credibility or producing a new hearing entitlement.

The proffer includes paternal alcohol abuse, harsh discipline and family violence; the tying-and-whipping incident; chronic skin disease, isolation and psychological consequences; asserted learning or functional limitations; and assessments suggesting low future violence. The later Jefferson account places him as the initial aggressor when Banks defended his girlfriend and supplies context absent from the short trial account. The investigation and witness-preparation failures described by the district court are also taken in Banks's favor for this alternative analysis. They do not presume a proven organic diagnosis from an expert allegation or assign the father a willingness to describe every alleged incident that his own testimony did not show. Kelly and the other unprepared witnesses have no identified additional helpful testimony beyond the material actually specified.

Scalia and Thomas find no reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty result from competent presentation of that supported mitigation, assessed as a whole against the murder, gun assistance and pistol assault. The context of Jefferson's aggression makes Banks's action more understandable but does not eliminate its violent escalation; the childhood evidence is meaningful but, for them, does not sufficiently alter the future-dangerousness assessment. Their Terry Williams position accepted ordinary reasonable-probability review while disputing prejudice on a different mitigation record. They apply that same standard here without amended statutory deference, an additional Fretwell unfairness requirement, a demand that acquittal be more likely than conviction or automatic rejection of mitigation because aggravation is substantial.

**Adversarial comparison:** The strongest assistance-relief path would treat the undisputed lack of meaningful social-history investigation and the unpresented personal history as a lost explanation of Banks's behavior, with Jefferson's later account rebutting an impression of unprovoked violence. Terry Williams supplies a reasonable-investigation duty and the full-record prejudice inquiry. The contrary path recognizes that serious investigative failure is not itself sentence prejudice and weighs the specified evidence against the actual conduct. Scalia and Thomas choose the latter on the complete assumed proffer. They do not exclude Cunningham or Jefferson simply because their details emerged in federal court. The six granting Farr relief make no alternative merits commitment that could be mistaken for an additional holding.

**No imported combined claim:** The district court's and magistrate's references to assistance and suppression together are preserved as lower reasoning. The State contested that combination, and the appellate court gave its own exhaustion and merits reasons. No Associate manufactures a newly presented cumulative-error claim. Lawful cumulative uses within Brady and the full evidence within Strickland remain required; that is different from combining independently insufficient constitutional claims into a new theory.

## IV. Cook transcript, implied consent and appellate certification

**Common disposition:** All eight reverse the certificate denial and direct issuance of a certificate identifying the Cook transcript suppression claim, including whether the federal litigation amended the pleading by implied consent under Rule 15(b). The certificate admits that issue to appeal; it does not establish final amendment, a bargain with Cook, guilt-phase materiality, a constitutional violation or entitlement to a new trial.

**Both Slack components:** The constitutional issue is reasonably debatable because the concealed transcript records pretrial rehearsal and reconciliation of Cook's prior accounts with the testimony the State sought to present. Cook's connection of Banks to the recovered murder weapon and his account of the Dallas encounters make the use of that material more than an abstract disclosure grievance. Banks could reasonably contest the effect of the withheld material on his ability to test that account. The record does not establish a prosecution bargain, and the transcript is not treated as proof of one.

The procedural issue is independently debatable. The transcript was admitted without objection, but that circumstance alone does not prove consent to adjudicate an unpleaded constitutional claim. The stronger supporting circumstances are the actual questioning about whether Cook was instructed how to testify, comparison of the transcript with his trial answers, express examination concerning the prosecution's duty to disclose it, and later submissions pressing suppression of that transcript. Those acts permit reasonable debate whether the hearing encompassed this distinct factual basis, rather than introducing evidence relevant only to the already pleaded alleged bargain. The State may argue overlap, lack of fair notice and the limits of its actual consent on appeal. Those objections require appellate consideration rather than a requirement that Banks conclusively prove amendment and Brady entitlement before certification.
