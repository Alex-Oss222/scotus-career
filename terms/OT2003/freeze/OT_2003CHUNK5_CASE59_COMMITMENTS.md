## Provisional disposition and common rule

All eight Associates would reverse and remand. A timely assessment of the partnership's employment tax starts the applicable section 6502 collection period for that tax debt; the Government need not make a second assessment against each general partner merely because independently applicable state law makes the partner liable for the same debt. Whether the partner actually bears that liability and whether the chosen collection procedure is lawful remain separate requirements.

Sections 6201 and 6501 concern assessment of the tax; section 6502 permits collection of a tax timely assessed within its prescribed collection period. Assessment records the tax obligation. It is not a requirement to recreate the same obligation for every person whom other law makes answerable for it. Updike confirms that a proceeding against someone other than the original taxpayer can remain collection of the original tax, though its transferee statute and six-year period are not the present law. Fior D'Italia separately preserves the tax-specific nature of assessment; the employer's independent liability there is different from state-law responsibility for the same partnership debt here.

The Government must establish the actual state-law liability it invokes and comply with bankruptcy claim procedures and any independently applicable collection conditions. Reversal removes the partner-specific repeat-assessment bar, rather than ordering allowance of an unexamined amount or supplying administrative levy authority.

## Alternative-path check and remedy

**Require partner-specific assessments.** The strongest argument is that a general partner can be described as a taxpayer whose property may be used to pay the tax and therefore should receive an individualized timely assessment. But the word taxpayer does not alone create a second tax obligation or an additional assessment prerequisite. The Government's claim is based on a timely assessed partnership tax and independent responsibility for that debt. Updike expressly separates the identity of the assessed taxpayer from the persons against whom tax collection proceeds. Separate substantive liability and collection safeguards answer the concern without rewriting the assessment limitation.

**One assessment of the same debt suffices.** The strongest objection is that this could be mistaken for unlimited collection from anyone connected to the business. The operative trigger prevents that result: state law must actually make the particular partner liable for this debt, and the applicable federal collection period and procedures must be satisfied. No such liability is established solely by this Court's interpretation.

The Ninth Circuit's repeated-assessment basis for rejecting the claims is reversed. Remand requires adjudication of the Government's claims with independent liability, amount and procedural defenses preserved. The legal question is resolved; ordinary claim application is not an unresolved construction of the assessment provisions. One signed lead opinion can state the unanimous Associate ground. No separate writing or author assignment is required.
