## Provisional disposition and common propositions

All eight Associates would vacate the interlocutory section 230 disposition and remand with instructions to dismiss the appeal from denial of the California special motion for want of federal appellate jurisdiction. They do not reach the statutory republication question. The independently final sponsor judgment is outside the granted issue and does not provide jurisdiction over the moderator's distinct interlocutory appeal.

Section 1291 governs federal finality. A State's immediate-appeal provision and its characterization of a procedure as protection from litigation do not expand that statute. Cohen's requirements are cumulative and apply to the category of order. Digital Equipment supplies an independently controlling adequate-final-review ground; Swint rejects conversion of an ordinary liability defense into immunity from suit and convenience-based connected review. Section 425.16's screening of the plaintiff's probability of success does not establish an independently important, effectively unreviewable federal collateral order. Section 230's publisher defense can be reviewed following final judgment; irretrievable litigation expense does not establish the contrary.

## Adversarial paths and lawful scope

**Accept collateral-order jurisdiction.** The strongest argument is that California created a protection against the litigation process to protect public participation, whose purpose is defeated if trial occurs. Unlike the private bargain in Digital Equipment, that policy is public. Nonetheless, statutory policy alone does not establish an immunity of the recognized federal collateral-order kind. A success-probability screening motion involves liability merits; review after final judgment remains effective for the underlying statutory defense. Treating litigation burden itself as decisive would permit a wide class of ordinary defenses to evade section 1291.

**Dismiss the interlocutory appeal.** Its strongest objection is that later correction cannot undo the expense and chilling effect. Digital Equipment's adequate-final-review holding expressly treats irreversible litigation burdens as insufficient; the relevant category does not receive an exception merely because those burdens are substantial. Existing interlocutory statutes and rulemaking channels retain their separate operation.

**Reach section 230.** Actual intended publication, reasonable perceived permission and a broader supplied-content reading are competing merits constructions. None supplies the missing jurisdiction. A finality disposition must not choose among them, make factual findings about the email, or endorse the lower court's construction as controlling law.

One signed threshold opinion can state the common jurisdictional rule and mandate. There is no present separate-writing function, no author assignment and no modeled position for the Chief. The preserved section 230 issue returns to ordinary proceedings under an intact factual record.
