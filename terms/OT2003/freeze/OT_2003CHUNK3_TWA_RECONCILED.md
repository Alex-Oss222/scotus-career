## Components and operative ground

Affirm the free-and-clear sale as applied to the represented EEOC claimants' pre-sale employment claims and the represented travel-voucher class. Under section 363(f), an asserted successor obligation can be an interest in transferred property when it would attach to the purchaser through acquisition and continuation of the transferred operating enterprise; the statute is not confined to liens or strictly in-rem interests. Its application still requires the actual property connection and an independent enacted condition. Here paragraph (5) is satisfied because the identified obligations could be compelled into monetary satisfaction in bankruptcy liquidation, not simply because money would be more convenient. The voucher entitlement is a travel benefit capable of monetary replacement; the reviewed pre-sale employment remedies likewise are reducible to monetary claims on this record. Valuation and distribution remain subject to ordinary claim and priority law.

The disposition adopts neither a release of all general unsecured debts against every purchaser nor the lower court's suggested priority-policy alternative. Dow Corning's prohibition of releases through general plan powers remains effective. Sections 105(a), generalized equity, the priority scheme and the prospect of preserving employment cannot independently dispense with the specific requirements of section 363(f). The validity of nonbankruptcy successor liability is assumed for this review, as below. No property connection is inferred solely because every employer necessarily owns assets: the decisive relation is the successor claim asserted against acquisition and continuation of this enterprise. No finding of adequate notice binds an absent person whose notice was not established. Purchaser misconduct after closing and future statutory compliance duties are distinct.

The ancillary appeal from refusal of a limited stay is moot upon the final merits disposition. No new injunction, independent application or immediate claim distribution is ordered. The consummated sale remains effective within the affirmed scope.

## Enhanced comparison of lawful paths

**Statutory affirmation:** The words any interest and the separate reference to an interest that is a lien in paragraph (3) reject an equation of interest with lien. The actual successor theory follows acquisition of the operating enterprise, and paragraph (5) supplies a separate legally available monetary-satisfaction route. Its strongest objection is that property interests include nonlien rights without encompassing personal employment liabilities. The answer is confined to the asserted transfer-dependent successor interest, not the general debt: the acquisition is the proposed source of American's obligation. General insolvency alone could not reach an independent nondebtor claim.

**Reversal under a property-specific construction:** This path would distinguish leaseholds, servitudes and liens from a personal employment obligation and preserve the successor claim subject to its actual nonbankruptcy elements. Dow Corning and Hartford make this a serious reading rather than a policy objection. Its strongest difficulty is the enacted breadth of interest, the transfer-dependent nature of the particular successor claims and the independent paragraph (5) mechanism. On this record the Associates choose the bounded statutory construction above. Their affirmance does not adopt the lower opinion's broader but-for-assets language as a universal test.

**Predicate remand:** A remand would be appropriate if the legal availability of money satisfaction or transfer connection were genuinely unresolved. The lower recitations establish these predicates for the actual claimant groups, including conversion in liquidation and valuation of vouchers. The proponent cannot substitute the favorable purchase price for either predicate. No remand is ordered merely to restate established facts. The order is read in the scope of the claims before the Court; a future absent person's objection remains independently available.
