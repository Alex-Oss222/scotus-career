## Changed premises and continuing limits

The central changed premise is the Court's November 4, 2002 Popovich decision. It is a controlling seven-Justice decision, authored by Kennedy, sustaining reasonable hearing accommodations and related anti-retaliation duties in parental-custody adjudication, including statutory protection beyond a separately provable constitutional tort. It expressly refuses to divide the same statutory access action into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale offered for enforcement. The historical Lane opinions instead treated Popovich as lower-court history, without this intervening Supreme Court holding and Kennedy's own authorship.

Kennedy's actual Popovich explanation connects the serious adjudicative interest, the risk of an ineffective hearing and the usefulness and burden of assistance. It treats reasonable measures securing meaningful participation as closely fitted prevention, preserves effective alternatives, fundamental-alteration and undue-burden limits, and demands ordinary proof of statutory liability and damages. His later Hibbs dissent expressly distinguished that direct hearing connection from a freestanding minimum leave benefit. These are concrete published commitments, not an inference from a general jurisprudential label. A bounded extension to physical access to judicial proceedings therefore supports his departure from the historical vote.

The extension is bounded by judicial function. Jones's court-reporting claim is not Lane's criminal-defense claim. The six affirming Associates permit her pleaded denial of access to judicial proceedings and the services through which they operate to be adjudicated, while requiring proof that her actual claim falls within that class. A courthouse address alone does not turn an unrelated employment benefit into a fundamental-access claim, and no new holding on Title II's employment coverage follows. Nor must every member of a valid preventive class possess precisely the same individual constitutional entitlement: reasonable accommodation of participants serving judicial proceedings can protect the hearing function without equating all personal interests.

Garrett's actual Section 5 holding concerns Title I's general employment scheme at its full presented scope, and its judgment separately rests on Article I. Its retained reasoning does not impose an evidence quota, presume State compliance, declare disability accommodation categorically irrelevant to constitutional protection, or dispose of Title II. Boerne likewise permits fitted prevention without prescribed findings, a numerical count, a violation in every jurisdiction or an independent constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. These governing propositions differ from portions of the historical debate and must retain their force.

## Proposition-level support, rule and relief

All eight Associates accept section 12202's unmistakable authorization. Six—Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—support the bounded preventive Section 5 ground and affirmance. Scalia and Thomas support vacatur and the narrower remand. No Chief Justice vote is inferred or counted. No distinct rationales are summed to manufacture a majority.

The six-Associate rule permits the expressly authorized Title II action against a State for reasonable disability accommodation securing access to judicial proceedings and their operative services, including preventive statutory duties exceeding a separately provable constitutional tort. Section 12131 requires a qualified individual who meets the service's essential eligibility requirements with or without the listed reasonable modifications, removal of barriers or auxiliary aids. Section 12132 requires disability-based exclusion from participation, denial of benefits or discrimination by the public entity. Section 12133 supplies its actual remedial cross-reference. Express authorization and constitutional power do not themselves establish any of those elements or entitlement to damages.

The accommodation duty preserves effective alternatives and does not require a fundamental alteration or an undue financial or administrative burden. It establishes no right to the claimant's preferred technology, means of entry or structural renovation. Ordinary cost or convenience does not by itself negate a meaningful-hearing obligation, but the legally applicable burden limits retain their work. No statutory qualification is treated as an automatic defense proved on these allegations. The rule distinguishes an architectural barrier, ineffective judicial access and each asserted constitutional or statutory injury rather than equating them.

Affirmance permits both pleaded courthouse-access claims to proceed under that defined rule. The lower court must determine disability and qualification, the actual service or proceeding denied, effective alternatives, actionable exclusion, causation, compensable injury and the lawful remedy. Lane's and Jones's allegations remain separate; neither is an adjudicated constitutional violation. No damages, particular construction project or accommodation is ordered. A claim proving only an unrelated employment benefit is outside this Section 5 court-access basis. Unrelated public services require their own analysis.

Scalia and Thomas would confine the available statutory application to a properly preserved actual constitutional denial of meaningful participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it. They would vacate the categorical immunity denial and require that examination instead of either approving the broad preventive theory or dismissing every possible Lane claim on this preliminary record. Their proposed standard is not current law unless adopted by the necessary final coalition. Any distinct prospective-officer route remains subject to its own actual pleading and remedial predicates; Frew alone supplies no State-damages authority here.
