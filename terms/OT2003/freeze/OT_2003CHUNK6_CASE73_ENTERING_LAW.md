## Standards and Tests selection: Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway

### Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** Review of successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claims without otherwise required cause and prejudice.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must show by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. Examine the offense, required aggravating facts and every other necessary eligibility condition. Additional mitigation affecting only the discretionary selection of death does not satisfy this gateway. Opening the gateway permits consideration of an otherwise barred claim; it does not itself establish the claim or grant relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Eligibility-only scope, no-reasonable-juror inquiry and clear-and-convincing burden: Sawyer v. Whitley, “Capital innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred claims”, June 22, 1992; Kennedy's opinion joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Broader judgment concurrences supply no component.

**Present operation effective:** *Sawyer*, June 22, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits standards and proper factual-development rules remain distinct. Neither ordinary sentencing prejudice nor discretionary mitigation replaces this gateway. Freestanding innocence claims remain unresolved. No categorical exclusion of impeachment evidence follows from its insufficiency on Sawyer’s record.

**Holdings navigation:** Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992) — Habeas procedural gateways.

**Related current rules:** Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims states Schlup’s distinct January 23, 1995 offense-innocence inquiry; it does not change this death-eligibility quantum.

## Standards and Tests selection: Crime innocence as a gateway

### Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** The showing needed to overcome successive or abusive habeas barriers through innocence of the offense.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the whole probative record, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The inquiry includes the new evidence together with the existing record rather than deferring conclusively to the original jury’s credibility choices. Passing this demanding procedural gateway establishes neither innocence as a freestanding claim nor the underlying constitutional violation and shifts no ultimate merits burden. The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every submitted statement as true. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner.

For a defaulted plea-intelligence claim, the same factual inquiry applies under the correctly construed offense. The Government may rebut innocence with admissible evidence beyond the plea record. If it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the prisoner must also establish innocence of that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs impose no added burden.

**Authority by component:**

- Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway: Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.
- This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission: This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

- Plea gateway, evidence beyond plea papers and actually forgone more-serious charge requirement: Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516 — Holdings 2–3, 1998-05-18.

**Present operation effective:** Bousley v. United States, 1998-05-18, for plea-specific operation; Schlup’s general gateway remains effective from 1995-01-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway is distinct. Section 2244(b) permits refusal of a subsequent habeas application by a person held under a state-court judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy on an earlier habeas application following either an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue or a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, unless the later application alleges and rests on a factual or other ground not adjudicated in the earlier hearing and the court is satisfied that the applicant did not deliberately withhold the newly asserted ground on the earlier application or otherwise abuse the writ. Both exception conditions are required. New evidence is not automatically a new ground, and absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The qualified refusal permission does not itself sustain refusal based on the rejected clear-and-convincing crime standard. Other lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain.

**Burdens and proof:** The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner’s burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate.

**Open question:** Schlup does not decide whether every successful innocence showing compels review, a general appellate standard for refusal, or an automatic hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay.

**Related current rules:** Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims supplies the separate eligibility inquiry and proof quantum. Recall reopening completed state-prisoner habeas merits applies the appropriate gateway to completed-merits recall without changing its quantum.

**Holdings navigation:** Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway; This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission. Bousley v. United States — Collateral review of a misdescribed guilty plea; Calderon v. Thompson — Recall of completed habeas merits mandates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Defaulted ineffective assistance offered as cause

### Defaulted ineffective assistance offered as cause for another default

**Governing question:** Whether a constitutional assistance claim may excuse another default when the assistance claim has its own state procedural bar.

**Current rule:** Offering an ineffective-assistance claim as cause does not exempt it from an independent, adequate and properly applied state procedural bar. If that claim is defaulted, the prisoner must excuse its default through cause and actual prejudice or an applicable recognized miscarriage-of-justice gateway before using it as cause for another claim. Exhaustion alone is insufficient because it can reflect expiration of state remedies. The State must establish the asserted independent, adequate and properly applied ground; a successful adequacy challenge removes that bar without a cause showing for it.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent procedural requirements for the assistance claim and distinct excuse routes: Edwards v. Carpenter — A constitutional assistance claim retains its own procedural requirements when offered as cause, April 25, 2000; Souter joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** Edwards, April 25, 2000.

**Burdens and proof:** Cause requires an objective defense-external impediment or qualifying unconstitutional assistance, not ordinary attorney negligence or an assumed collateral-counsel right. Actual prejudice requires actual and substantial disadvantage from constitutional error, not a possibility of harm. The offense-innocence gateway requires reliable new evidence which, with the probative record as a whole, makes it more likely than not that no reasonable properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Exceptions and limits:** The state rule’s own good-cause exception is distinct from federal cause excusing a valid default. Default remains nonjurisdictional, no universal sequence of habeas defenses is imposed, and a gateway does not establish the constitutional merits or erase independent statutory restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

**Authority by component:**

- Merits-relief restriction, factual presumption/rebuttal and cumulative hearing conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(2)–(4), 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)–(e), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court holdings and temporal limit, and objective legal inquiry: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by the seven other Associates.
- Materially truncated or objectively indefensible mitigation assessment: Terry Williams — III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- An additional Fretwell unfairness demand in an ordinary lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with the governing rule, with actual independent grounds still requiring examination: Terry Williams — VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part III with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Applicant-attributed deficient diligence and cumulative hearing exceptions: Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams) — I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure, April 18, 2000; Ginsburg’s Part I, unanimous. Reasonable available efforts and unknown concealed relationships: the same decision, III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead; all eight Associates.

- No citation requirement and substantive whole-adjudication review: Canonical Decision Record `Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The governing rule turns on substance and the entire sequence, 2002-11-04; unanimous Part I of the per curiam opinion.
- Whole-opinion reading of prejudice terminology and substantive whole-record assessment: Canonical Decision Record `Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Read the whole opinion and assess the whole supported record, 2002-11-04; unanimous Part I of the per curiam opinion.

**Present operation effective:** The two Williams v. Taylor decisions, April 18, 2000, for the stated legal-review and diligence constructions; the enacted factual presumption and other unchanged components retain their original authority.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Unresolved interaction:** Neither exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses commands a majority in Terry Williams; no Marks synthesis supplies one. No universal unreasonable-extension or refusal-to-extend rule is adopted. Section 2254(d)(2), §2254(e)(1), and the hearing conditions retain distinct work. Michael Wayne Williams does not replace the former-law Keeney rule outside the amendment’s temporal domain. Satisfaction of a statutory gateway does not itself establish the underlying constitutional violation or authorize relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

**Current wording authority:** Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti, November 4, 2002, clarify how actual state reasoning and terminology are read under the existing statutory standard. Their particular reasonable-application outcomes do not alter the independent statutory and constitutional requirements.

## Standards and Tests selection: Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default

### Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review

**Governing question:** The order of two nonjurisdictional habeas defenses.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity and state procedural default are distinct nonjurisdictional limits. No inflexible priority requires either always to precede the other; an accepted Teague question may be resolved while disputed default predicates remain reserved. This does not excuse default, permit bypass of an established jurisdictional defect or decide constitutional merits. An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense; the defense is not a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect.

**Authority by component:**

- Lambrix v. Singletary — I. The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense, 1997-05-12; Stevens with Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven

- An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense: Trest v. Cain, 1997-12-09; Stevens for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Trest, 1997-12-09, for the no-duty rule; Lambrix, 1997-05-12, retains the sequencing rule.

**Exceptions and limits:** The specific old-rule conclusion for the influential capital recommendation at November 24, 1986 finality is an application, not a universal exception to Teague. Preserved adequate, independent and unexcused state grounds remain enforceable.

**Holdings navigation:** Lambrix v. Singletary (1997-05-12) — The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense. Trest v. Cain — Habeas procedural default and appellate initiative.

**Open question:** Trest leaves judicial authority to raise an omitted default, the proper exercise of any discretion, intentional waiver or forfeiture, actual default and applicable exceptions unresolved. Any lawful later consideration must address the actual ground with an appropriate opportunity to respond; no universal extra-briefing requirement is imposed.

## Standards and Tests selection: Renewed proof of noncapital recidivist status

### Renewed proof of noncapital recidivist status

**Governing question:** Whether evidentiary insufficiency bars a second effort to prove a noncapital recidivist sentencing allegation.

**Current rule:** Double Jeopardy does not bar renewed proof of the serious character of a prior conviction in the noncapital recidivist-status determination addressed in Monge. The personal-conduct qualification classifies the prior conviction for punishment of the present offense rather than adjudicating a new offense or a capital acquittal-like determination. Trial safeguards and an increased punishment ceiling alone do not make that determination an offense acquittal.

**Authority by component:**

- Parts I–II: bounded status classification and lawful renewed proof: Monge v. California, No. 97-6146. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** The first proof is not thereby declared sufficient or excused; renewed proceedings must satisfy applicable law. No general rule for every sentencing fact, capital determination or separate offense follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Monge v. California — Double jeopardy and renewed proof of a serious-prior qualification.

#### Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6382.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### What capital-innocence showing permits review of an otherwise barred habeas claim without cause and prejudice

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking review of an otherwise successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claim without the ordinarily required cause and prejudice must establish by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. The inquiry includes the offense, required aggravating facts and every other eligibility condition; additional mitigation affecting only discretionary selection of the punishment does not satisfy this gateway.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt both the eligibility-only scope and clear-and-convincing quantum. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor concur only in the judgment on broader grounds; their votes do not enlarge the controlling rule. No Marks synthesis is necessary.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the capital-eligibility gateway to otherwise barred claims.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The crime-innocence standard is unchanged. Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits requirements and factual development of a properly available claim remain distinct. Passing the gateway does not itself grant relief. No freestanding innocence claim or general bar to consideration of impeachment evidence is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The precise capital formulation previously left open is established effective June 22, 1992. Sawyer's refusal of further review is affirmed on the application stated below; no new hearing, sentencing proceeding or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478, preserves cause and prejudice and its miscarriage exception; only the capital-eligibility branch is specified here.
- Smith v. Murray, 477 U.S. 527, has its open sentencing application specified through eligibility rather than discretionary mitigation reweighing.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467, is applied: abuse remains an independent obstacle unless cause and prejudice or the applicable exceptional gateway is established.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436, supplies relevant plurality discussion without becoming a retrospective majority holding.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401, is applied: possible sentencing inaccuracy does not itself establish exceptional innocence.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862, and Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231, supply the distinction between death eligibility and selection of punishment.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, preserve mitigation protection on the merits without independently reopening a barred claim.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668, and United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667, supply different reasonable-probability merits inquiries; they do not replace the exceptional gateway.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992, retains Townsend for development of properly available claims without removing successive or abusive-claim barriers. Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, June 8, 1992, preserve correction of available weighing errors without eliminating independent barriers; Wright v. West, June 19, 1992, changes no general habeas-review standard.

##### Whether Sawyer's added evidence removes every reasonable basis for death eligibility

**Holding:** Sawyer's psychiatric and family evidence concerns mitigation, while the impeachment affidavits and caregiver account do not clearly and convincingly establish that no reasonable juror would find the surviving bases of death eligibility. His abusive Brady claim and successive sentencing-ineffectiveness claim therefore remain barred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five adopt this gateway application. The remaining four Justices independently support affirmance under broader inquiries but do not join this rationale.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as the stated application to Sawyer's submissions; it is not a categorical exclusion of later impeachment evidence.

**Material application:** Louisiana principal liability permits aggravated-arson eligibility without personal ignition. Sawyer's participation and the lighter-fluid evidence remain material; the preceding beating and scalding independently support the surviving cruelty aggravator. The credibility affidavits do not require every reasonable juror to reject the core account, and the medical and childhood submissions do not negate necessary eligibility facts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional merits claim is resolved after the gateway fails; the evidence is not found false in every respect, and death is not held mandatory. The accepted procedural classifications and cause determination are not reopened. The prior Caldwell ruling, a new challenge to the wording of an aggravator, and the distinct lower-court competency claim outside this petition remain unadjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's refusal of further habeas review. No new factual hearing, sentencing proceeding, trial or release follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83, supplies the underlying suppressed-evidence claim but no merits disposition here. Strickland, 466 U.S. 668, does not allow ordinary sentencing prejudice to replace the gateway. Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227, retains its distinct Caldwell retroactivity disposition.

#### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated and remanded through that court to the District Court for the probability gateway and a reasoned decision on necessary factual development. No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

#### Edwards v. Carpenter, 529 U.S. 446 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-2060.
**Decided:** April 25, 2000.

##### A constitutional assistance claim retains its own procedural requirements when offered as cause

**Holding:** An ineffective-assistance claim offered as cause for another default is not exempt from an otherwise applicable independent, adequate and properly applied state procedural bar merely because state remedies have been exhausted. If that cause claim is itself defaulted, the prisoner must establish cause and actual prejudice excusing that default, or satisfy an applicable recognized miscarriage-of-justice gateway, before the claim may do that work.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's opinion has seven votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stevens and Breyer support the remand without joining this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State must establish the independent, adequate and properly applied bar. Federal cause requires an external impediment or qualifying constitutional ineffectiveness, not ordinary negligence or a presumed collateral-counsel right; actual prejudice means actual and substantial constitutional disadvantage. The offense-innocence gateway requires reliable new evidence considered with the whole probative record showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. That gateway establishes neither the constitutional claim nor entitlement to a writ.

**Material application:** The District Court found Ohio Rule 26(B)'s good-cause practice inadequate, and the court of appeals did not decide that preserved ground. Exhaustion conceded by the State establishes neither adequacy nor merits. A successful inadequacy challenge removes this bar without federal cause for it; state good cause is a different inquiry.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The exhaustion-only rationale and dependent conditional culpability-hearing relief are reversed for the remaining adequacy, applicable default and excuse, merits and statutory inquiries. No final denial or automatic reinstatement of the District Court's different appellate remedy is ordered. Default remains nonjurisdictional, with no mandatory universal default-first sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carrier is applied to constitutionally ineffective assistance as an independent cause claim; its state-presentation requirement is not read as immunity from default after exhaustion.

- Coleman retains independent and adequate state grounds and their recognized exceptions; the decision applies them to a constitutional claim offered as cause.

- Gray preserves distinct claims and their actual procedural predicates rather than merging related allegations.

- O'Sullivan retains the distinction between completing state review and exhausting an unavailable route; its former-law source application does not classify this federal petition as pre-AEDPA.

- Trest and Lambrix retain the nonjurisdictional character of default and reject a mandatory default-first sequence for every habeas issue.

- Schlup supplies the demanding offense-innocence gateway; innocence, ordinary prejudice and legal insufficiency remain different inquiries.

- Strickland, Evitts and Jones retain constitutional performance and prejudice requirements for appellate assistance, without a duty to raise every possible issue.

- Smith v. Robbins and Flores-Ortega retain their distinct appellate-function and causally lost-appeal rules; neither automatically establishes this omitted-issue claim.

- The two Williams decisions preserve separate statutory eligibility, factual development, actual constitutional error and remedy; neither supplies a finding about Ohio adequacy or Carpenter's excuse.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

## Holdings

**1. Concealed facts may be considered under the applicable hearing rule.** For a properly presented habeas claim governed by former law, ordinary negligent failure to develop facts does not bar a necessary federal hearing; Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification governs. A distinct state claim default or statutory restriction requires its own predicate, and an incomplete state record does not itself establish either.

Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes retained that former-law rule. Banks presented the Farr suppression claim to the state courts; the later evidence supplied concealed facts supporting it. He questioned Farr and sought disclosure, while the State's assurances and uncorrected denials concealed Farr's paid relationship. This is not deliberate bypass. Texas's diligence argument identifies neither an independent adequate state claim bar nor a transformation of the existing claim into a different one. Vasquez v. Hillery distinguishes factual supplementation from such a new claim, and Coleman v. Thompson concerns an actual independent procedural default.

Carriger v. Stewart preserves former merits law for this pending capital application while reserving the temporal reach of section 2254(e)(2). That general temporal question remains unnecessary here: Michael Wayne Williams v. Taylor requires applicant-attributed lack of diligence before the statutory restriction operates, and the supported concealment and reasonable efforts defeat that trigger as well. Strickler v. Greene explains why reasonable reliance on disclosure representations is relevant; it does not require a freestanding cause ruling where no separate default is established.

**2. Farr's concealed paid role is material to the death sentence.** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its supported lawful uses, considered with the whole relevant record, create a reasonable probability of a different lawful result sufficient to undermine confidence in that adjudication. The prisoner must make that showing; once a material Brady violation is established, no additional harmless-error showing is required.

Farr's paid cooperation and role in bringing about the gun-retrieval encounter change the future-dangerousness presentation. Timely disclosure would have supported investigation, preparation and examination about why the encounter occurred, as well as impeachment of the denials of cooperation. It would have undermined the impression that Farr independently corroborated Banks's willingness to commit future armed robberies. The denials and disclosure assurances reinforced the misleading account, while the defense's attempted impeachment was weakened by the limited credibility of its witnesses.

Kyles v. Whitley requires consideration of these lawful uses together, rather than an isolated credibility deduction or a test of whether enough aggravating evidence remains. Strickler requires separate attention to confidence in the penalty decision. The murder, the pistol assault described by Jefferson and Banks's acknowledged willingness to obtain a gun remain serious evidence. They do not make the concealed origin and purpose of the Farr encounter merely cumulative. The inquiry concerns the jury's lawful death-selection decision, not simply eligibility or proof of guilt. The complete record establishes materiality and requires a conditional sentence writ.

**3. Cook's transcript claim meets both certificate requirements.** When a habeas claim is rejected on a procedural pleading ground, an issue-specific certificate requires reasonable debate both about the validity of the constitutional claim and about the procedural ruling. Banks satisfies both requirements concerning the concealed Cook rehearsal transcript and whether the federal litigation tried that claim by implied consent.

Slack v. McDaniel supplies both conditions, and Miller-El v. Cockrell distinguishes threshold debatability from ultimate entitlement. The transcript raises a substantial suppression question about the preparation of an important guilt witness. Its receipt in evidence, the prosecutor's examination about coaching and disclosure, and the ensuing suppression submissions support reasonable disagreement about the pleading ruling. ## Event

## Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Justice Stevens filed a dissent on fair notice and the Court’s discretionary refusal to decide the late equivalence contention.

## Holdings

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner ordinarily fails to fairly present a federal claim when the petition, brief or properly incorporated submission does not identify its federal basis and the state court would have to search a separate lower-court opinion to discover it. Clear federal notice may be supplied by a constitutional provision, pertinent federal authority or an understandable description of the claim; no particular citation or verbal formula is indispensable.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Stevens dissents on the application to Reese's actual petition.

**Controlling explanation:** Picard requires a fair opportunity to decide the federal claim itself. Boerckel carries that claim-specific obligation through ordinary available state review, and Castille distinguishes proper presentation from a claim's mere presence elsewhere in a record. A discretionary-review court must be able to identify the claims it is asked to decide from the papers properly submitted.

Reese's petition mentions trial and appellate assistance, but its express Fifth, Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment references concern coercion to waive a jury. It leaves the distinct appellate-assistance allegation without federal identification. The postconviction opinion's Jones v. Barnes citation does not cure that omission: the intervening appellate order was a summary affirmance, and the requirement to attach that appellate decision does not establish attachment or incorporation of the underlying postconviction opinion.

The dissent reads the petition's constitutional references collectively with its description of counsel's failings. The Court instead reads the specific references in their stated setting. Treating possible access to another opinion as enough would replace meaningful notice with possible discovery and require the court to reconstruct the federal claim. The record establishes neither proper incorporation nor actual judicial consideration supplying the missing notice.

**Preserved scope:** The argument that identical state and federal assistance standards independently established notice was first advanced in the merits brief here and was not addressed below. The Court recognizes discretion to consider an included legal argument but declines to decide this newly presented equivalence contention under Rule 15.2. Its complexity, broader implications and absence of lower-court treatment support that choice; the Court does not decide Guinn’s ultimate effect on equivalence or fair notice. The remand applies the actual presentation and state-remedy record, and ordinary default and gateway law, to any remaining properly preserved issue.
