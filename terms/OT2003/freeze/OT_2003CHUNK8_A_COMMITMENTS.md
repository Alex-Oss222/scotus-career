## 104. Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.

**Disposition and rule presented for joins.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical ineligibility and return the request for district-court discretion. Section 1782 reaches evidence for use in the Commission's adjudicative competition process, including a reasonably contemplated proceeding before a body exercising the described first-instance adjudicative authority. AMD's procedural ability to present evidence and seek review makes it an interested person. The statute requires neither a pending adjudication nor foreign discoverability of the requested material. It authorizes, rather than compels, assistance. No present production order follows.

The relevant tribunal function is adjudication, not every activity of an administrative institution. The Commission's capacity to issue an enforceable decision subject to judicial review and AMD's legally recognized participation distinguish this request from free-standing policy advice or an unanchored investigation. The text's inclusion of criminal investigations before accusation supports the rejection of a general pendency prerequisite; reasonably contemplated adjudication still must be real. Private arbitration is not decided.

**Principal fork.** The strongest restrictive argument combines the Commission's refusal to obtain these documents, its own evidence rules, and possible circumvention of the foreign process. Those considerations can justify denying or narrowing particular assistance, but they do not insert a categorical foreign-discoverability or foreign-request requirement into a provision expressly permitting an interested person's application. Conversely, broad statutory eligibility cannot make the foreign refusal irrelevant or convert American discovery into an entitlement. Receptivity, genuine circumvention, availability through the foreign participants, and burden inform the existing discretion; no finding about their ultimate balance is supplied by this packet. Statutory privileges remain protected. A party cannot establish circumvention merely by identifying a difference between the two systems' discovery rules.

## 105. Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws

**Disposition and rule presented for joins.** Vacate the single-judge dismissal and remand for a three-judge district court under §2284. A constitutional challenge to congressional apportionment cannot be disposed of on the merits by a single judge merely because the complaint is thought insufficient under Rule 12. The Goosby exception concerns claims that are wholly insubstantial in the jurisdictional sense. Current Vieth affirmatively supplies a justiciable equal-protection claim, so this pleaded partisan-impairment claim cannot satisfy that exception.

The proper court must apply Vieth's political capacity, material impairment, substantial motivating purpose, and boundary-causation requirements, with its lawful-comparison and same-decision qualifications. Ordinary notice pleading does not require proof of those elements or a completed expert alternative map in the complaint. County division, strange shapes, or party-seat imbalance do not alone establish liability. The complaint's asserted intentional impairment of participation is not properly converted into an exclusively proportional-representation claim. The abandoned Article I and independent First Amendment claims are not restored. The remand permits appropriate pleading treatment or amendment by the proper tribunal and orders neither a liability finding nor a replacement map.

## 106. Tennard v. Dretke

**Disposition and rule presented for joins.** Reverse the denial of a certificate and remand for merits consideration under the proper habeas framework. A certificate requires reasonable debate over the constitutional claim under the actual restrictions applicable to habeas relief; it does not require the applicant first to win that claim. The Fifth Circuit's extraordinary-handicap and offense-causal-nexus screen is inconsistent with the adopted Graham rule. IQ evidence can bear on culpability independently of whether it predicts future danger or explains the physical commission of the offense. No Atkins diagnosis is required to make the evidence relevant mitigation.

On the authorized record, an IQ of 67 and the deliberateness/future-danger special issues make the claim that the jury lacked an effective lawful route to give that evidence mitigating force reasonably debatable. This is a certificate conclusion, not a determination that every low-IQ special-issue sentence is unconstitutional, that the state adjudication was objectively unreasonable, or that the alleged error affected this sentence. Complete charge, actual state merits date, applicable statutory constraints, and whole-record effect remain merits work.

## 108. Schriro v. Summerlin

**Common ground and disputed component.** Ring's transfer of the necessary aggravator finding from judge to jury is a new procedural rule for a sentence final in 1984. It regulates the means of establishing capital eligibility rather than removing conduct or a class of persons from the State's power to punish. Arizona already required aggravating facts to be proved beyond a reasonable doubt, so this claim does not also introduce that burden. The question is whether jury determination of the death-authorizing aggravator satisfies the still-operative watershed exception. Neither a later abolition of that exception nor a presumption that every capital safeguard qualifies is available.

The strongest case for nonretroactivity is that a judge can reliably determine facts under the proper burden, a jury is not invariably more accurate, and structural importance on direct review does not establish the exceptional accuracy and fairness requirement for reopening final judgments. DeStefano rejected retroactivity for the general jury-trial right under an earlier framework; it supports that caution but does not itself decide the contemporary capital watershed issue. The strongest contrary case concerns the basic allocation of the decision that legally opens death, with a jury's collective and independent assessment guarding against erroneous authorization of that penalty. It must defend that function as an essential accuracy safeguard, not merely invoke community preference or a greater tendency toward mercy.

## 109. Cheney v. United States District Court

**Issues kept distinct.** The appellate court's power under §1651 to issue mandamus is different from the plaintiffs' §1361 theory for enforcing a clear statutory duty and their APA theory against an agency. Appellate mandamus requires no adequate alternative remedy, a clear and indisputable right, and appropriateness in the circumstances. An interlocutory dispute does not become an ordinary appeal by acquiring constitutional significance. Conversely, document-specific executive privilege is not the only possible means of identifying an institutional burden on the President's discharge of official functions.

**Coverage boundary available for joins.** The formal roster does not conclusively dispose of alleged actual membership. Private persons who are genuinely integrated as regular full participants in a fixed advisory group can be members even without a formal vote, veto, supervisory post, or power to make the final governmental decision. Mere outside advice, repeated access, lobbying, or attendance as a consultant does not by itself establish that integration. Whether this group contained such private members remains unproved. The threshold also requires the statutory kind of advisory group; every collection of persons who communicate with officials is not a committee.

AAPS expressly treats full functional participation as relevant and denies that supervisory or decisionmaking authority is necessary. That circuit opinion is persuasive, not a Supreme Court holding. Public Citizen supplies a contextual limit on statutory utilization, not an exemption for all presidential advice. Kennedy's separate Public Citizen approach concerns interference with an expressly exclusive nomination power; it cannot automatically invalidate every application to this energy-policy group. These prior authorities support a genuine statutory inquiry and careful accommodation, not a mandatory vote-or-veto prerequisite or a present finding that FACA covers this group.

**Old-rule path.** The adopted Graham decision establishes that pre-1984 law already required an effective way for supported culpability mitigation to affect the capital sentencing decision. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer regard a legal requirement that a juror disregard mitigation unless every juror accepts it as an application of that existing prohibition on disabling relevant mitigation. They do not rely on the later dates of Mills or McKoy to establish what was dictated in 1987. Their opposing path distinguishes an obligation to allow the sentencer to use mitigation from the particular rules governing collective agreement within a jury. That distinction remains open after Graham and must be answered rather than dismissed as already adjudicated.

**New-rule path.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas regard the individual-juror unanimity restriction as a further rule about collective decisionmaking, not a result dictated by the earlier rule governing what mitigating material the sentencer must be able to use. On this path the rule is procedural, does not remove a class of persons or conduct from punishment, and is not a watershed change to the basic accuracy framework. Both contemporaneous exceptions remain in force.
