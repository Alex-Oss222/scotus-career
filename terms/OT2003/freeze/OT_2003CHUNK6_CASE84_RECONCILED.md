## Constitutional rule and competing grounds

The six-Associate ground applies Bell's prohibition of punishment to deliberately maintained, longstanding institutional conditions. Serious present or imminent harm, sustained notice, an actual opportunity for deliberative response and prolonged inadequate correction support the prospective relief here; a condition without a reasonable nonpunitive justification or excessive in relation to that justification cannot be sustained merely because every current official's separate subjective appreciation has not been proved. Daniels excludes ordinary negligence. Lewis makes the genuine opportunity for deliberation material rather than extending its emergency-purpose test to all custodial conditions. Farmer governs the convicted-prisoner claim it decided and is not silently overruled.

The lower court's isolated formulation that substantial harm alone suffices must not be reproduced as the Supreme Court's unqualified rule. Its own record discusses years of notice and persistent inadequate response. Scalia and Thomas find that supported notice and knowing unreasonable nonresponse sufficient for this prospective judgment while rejecting removal of culpability. Neither treats unsuccessful protection alone as unreasonable, imputes innocent information across individuals to create damages liability, or uses notice acquired during litigation to prove past damages. Their ground does not join the six-Associate institutional proposition.

## OCC classification, alternative and transition

The five affirmers retain a function-specific distinction between this informal information and compliance assistant and an agent exercising delegated decisional or enforcement powers of a master. The OCC neither adjudicates discrete disputes nor conducts adjudicatory hearings, swears or subpoenas witnesses, rules on evidence, or produces findings entitled to prescribed deference; its actual reports inform the court and facilitate compliance. No one feature alone is dispositive. The judge retains responsibility for deciding rights and enforcing orders.

The lower opinion described the former Rule 53. The amendment effective December 1, 2003 permits specified pretrial and posttrial work and expressly contemplates investigation or enforcement duties, with de novo review of factual objections unless the permitted stipulation changes review. Thus trial-like hearings, binding findings and clear-error review are not universal prerequisites to master status. The affirmers acknowledge that change; they do not adopt a categorical rule exempting all monitors or infer that the procedural amendment changed constitutional law.

The five also accept the independent ground that section 3626(f)'s appointment machinery does not recreate an appointment completed in 1982. The OCC is a continuous entity; renewal and adjustment did not establish dissolution and reappointment. This does not immunize future monitoring from sections 3626(a) and (b). The actual order restricts resources and monitoring to current violations and includes sufficient need-narrowness-intrusiveness findings. Settled expectations alone do not override Congress's express treatment of future relief.

The three opponents consider the court-directed recurring compliance investigation and remedial assistance functionally master's work notwithstanding informal reporting. They distinguish retroactively recreating the original appointment from regulating current operation and compensation under the all-prospective-relief transition. Their remand would require future conformity with statutory appointment, power, review and compensation provisions or adoption of a lawful genuinely different assistance arrangement. It neither orders appointment of a replacement nor ends independently supported substantive relief. Rule 53's pending-case transition applies insofar as just and practicable.

## Existing remedial structure preserved

The grant does not reinstate the old decrees wholesale. The lower judgment's vacatur of six-foot bed spacing and the ambiguous twenty-foot-candle remedy remains; neither is a constitutional numerical minimum. Food-service conditions at AMKC, ARDC and GMDC require particularized reconsideration. The court must consider the agreed monthly airflow checks of 15 percent of housing and intake areas as an alternative to annual balancing. Supported operational-window repairs, October 15 heating certification and quarterly bleach power washing retain their actual record-based support. Termination concerning isolated hot-water problems and the supported residential-vermin response remains undisturbed.

Benjamin v. Jacobson requires written record-based current-and-ongoing violation and need-narrowness-least-intrusiveness findings for every retained duty. A remedy may exceed the bare substantive minimum when those findings support it. The Rule 53 source supplies no retroactive constitutional holding, and the lower opinion supplies no system-wide presumption of present deficiencies.
