## 72. Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1205; 541 U.S. 369.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-03
* **Question Presented:** Whether §1658’s four-year limitation governs §1981 claims made possible by the Civil Rights Act of 1991’s amendment of an existing statute.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, rejecting the four-year period because the underlying §1981 provision predated December 1, 1990.
* **Relief Requested:** The employees seek reversal and consideration of their claims under the federal four-year period.
* **Material Record:** Former employees assert hostile-environment, termination and transfer claims under amended §1981. The dispute concerns which limitations period governs, not whether discrimination occurred.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the particular legal claim made possible by the amendment; a mere reference to a newly amended statute does not establish coverage.
* **Relevant Dates:** Section 1658 uses December 1, 1990 as its dividing date. The relevant amendment took effect November 21, 1991.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The issue-specific review standard appears below.

### Issue 1: Claims made possible by post-1990 enactments

* **Controlling Law:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc. treats §1981’s 1991 expansion as new substantive coverage and preserves earlier claims on their own terms. Landgraf v. USI Film Products requires attention to actual statutory temporal operation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted date criterion to the provision that supplies the claim, preserving differences between new claims and earlier available rights.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1658(a); 42 U.S.C. §1981(b). The 1991 amendment enlarged the contract-relationship coverage addressed in Patterson v. McLean Credit Union; the Rivers holding recognizes that expansion .
* **Material Other Authority:** Goodman v. Lukens Steel Co. supplies the older borrowing rule where no federal limitation displaces it.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The record recognizes the amendment’s substantive expansion; the unresolved question concerns its classification under §1658.
* **Open Point:** Whether an amendment qualifies as an Act of Congress for claims it newly permits.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Seventh Circuit treated the preexisting statutory provision as controlling the date inquiry.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Employees argue that a claim made possible by a new Act arises under that Act even when Congress amends existing code text.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The employer favors continued state borrowing because §1981 itself is older, invoking continuity and predictable limitations.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Claims already available before the amendment may retain their prior limitation; timeliness and discrimination merits remain separate.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Applying §1658 reverses the categorical state-period ruling and remands claim-specific timeliness. Retaining state borrowing sustains the shorter-period disposition if otherwise correctly applied. A mixed complaint may require different periods for independently different claims; no rule revives a final judgment.

## 73. Dretke v. Haley

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dretke v. Haley
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1824; 541 U.S. 386.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-03
* **Question Presented:** Whether actual innocence of a noncapital habitual-offender enhancement permits review of a procedurally defaulted constitutional claim challenging that enhancement.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, affirming habeas relief after applying an actual-innocence gateway to noncapital statutory sentencing eligibility.
* **Relief Requested:** Texas seeks reversal of the gateway ruling and conditional writ; Haley seeks resentencing without the enhancement.
* **Material Record:** Haley received sixteen and one-half years for theft after a habitual-offender finding. The first prior conviction became final three days after the second prior offense, contrary to the required sequence. Texas acknowledges the statutory ineligibility. Without this enhancement the charged theft carried a two-year maximum. Counsel missed the defect at trial and on appeal; a separately asserted ineffective-assistance claim was not adjudicated by the federal district court.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The sufficiency claim was defaulted in state court. A gateway excuses default, not the need for a federal constitutional violation. Whether federal proof requirements cover this recidivist finding and whether counsel supplied cause are distinct questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** The theft prosecution began in 1997; the May 2004 event makes the already-served sentence material to practical relief.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The issue-specific review standard appears below.

### Issue 1: Factual innocence of statutory sentencing eligibility

* **Controlling Law:** Sawyer v. Whitley requires clear and convincing proof that no reasonable factfinder could find death eligibility. Schlup v. Delo supplies the different gateway for innocence of the offense. Edwards v. Carpenter requires a properly available ineffective-assistance claim before it supplies cause. None decides this noncapital extension.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep gateway innocence, cause and prejudice, the underlying constitutional claim, and §2254 merits restrictions separate. A state-law sentencing error alone does not authorize a federal writ.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§2254 and 2243; Texas Penal Code habitual-offender sequencing requirements; Fourteenth and Sixth Amendments.
* **Material Other Authority:** In re Winship and Strickland v. Washington identify possible underlying constitutional routes. Extension of an existing proof rule to recidivist findings cannot be silently assumed.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Current gateways address crime innocence and death eligibility; ordinary dissatisfaction with sentencing discretion is not innocence.
* **Open Point:** Whether clear factual ineligibility for a statutory noncapital enhancement deserves a gateway and what remains necessary before relief.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Fifth Circuit treated innocence of habitual-offender status as sufficient to excuse the default.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Texas invokes finality and argues that the exceptional sentencing gateway concerns death; any relief should rest on a properly adjudicated constitutional claim.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Haley distinguishes factual ineligibility from a discretionary sentencing mistake and argues that an admitted chronology defect makes continued enhancement custody a miscarriage of justice.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Adjudicating the nondefaulted ineffective-assistance claim could supply equivalent relief and cause without a new innocence gateway.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Extending the gateway to statutory eligibility permits constitutional review but does not itself compel a writ. Refusing extension leaves cause and prejudice and any nondefaulted claim. A threshold remand could require adjudication of ineffective assistance first. Relief, if independently warranted, is a lawful sentence or release from custody unsupported by such a sentence, not erasure of the theft conviction.

## 74. Scarborough v. Principi

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Scarborough v. Principi
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1657; 541 U.S. 401.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-03
* **Question Presented:** Whether a timely Equal Access to Justice Act fee application may be amended after the filing period to add the omitted allegation that the Government’s position lacked substantial justification.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit, affirming dismissal of the fee application by the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Scarborough seeks consideration of his amended fee application on its merits.
* **Material Record:** The timely application identified the prevailing applicant, eligibility, amount, hours and rates. It omitted the required allegation about the Government’s justification. Counsel added that allegation promptly after the Secretary objected, but after the thirty-day period.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish an incomplete timely application from an entirely late one. No fee entitlement follows merely from permission to amend.
* **Relevant Dates:** The amendment followed expiration of the statutory thirty-day application period; the initial application preceded it.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Kontrick v. Ryan is scheduled earlier this term and may affect classification of procedural rules. Only an effective holding supplies controlling law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The issue-specific review standard appears below.

### Issue 1: Curable pleading omission in a timely fee request

* **Controlling Law:** Becker v. Montgomery allows correction of an omitted signature on a timely appeal notice. Edelman v. Lynchburg College permits a later verification to relate back to a timely discrimination charge. Neither eliminates substantive eligibility or every statutory deadline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate timely initiation and adequate identification from a curable formal requirement. Relation back may cure a defect without enlarging the underlying jurisdiction or waiving a required merits showing.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2412(d)(1)(A), (B); the Government bears the burden of establishing substantial justification once a proper application invokes the fee procedure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pierce v. Underwood defines substantial justification; Rule 15’s relation-back principle is informative without treating a fee application as an ordinary complaint.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The correction precedents reject automatic forfeiture for formal omissions but require interpretation of this particular fee statute.
* **Open Point:** Whether this omitted allegation is an incurable condition or a curable pleading requirement.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit read the deadline and contents together and distinguished cure rules involving different provisions or filings by laypersons.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The Government had timely notice of the identified fee claim; adding a required allegation imposes no new claim or unfair surprise.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The Secretary argues that all statutory application contents must arrive within the same thirty days and that waiver of sovereign immunity demands strict compliance.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** The Government may still defeat fees on substantial justification, statutory ineligibility, unreasonable amount or another properly established defense.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States, through the Secretary, defends the filing requirement as a bar to the amended request.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Permitting cure reverses the dismissal and returns the fee merits to the veterans court. Treating completeness as a time-limited prerequisite affirms. A cure rule can remain confined to an identified timely application without deciding equitable tolling or entirely missing applications.

## 75. Johnson v. California

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johnson v. California
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-6539; 541 U.S. 428.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-03
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Court has jurisdiction to review a state Batson ruling while other grounds for reversal of the conviction remain unresolved on remand; the underlying merits concern the first-stage showing of discriminatory peremptory challenges.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** California Supreme Court, reversing the Court of Appeal’s Wheeler/Batson ruling and remanding while separate evidentiary and prosecutorial-misconduct claims remain undecided.
* **Relief Requested:** Johnson seeks reversal of the State Supreme Court’s Batson standard and relief from his conviction.
* **Material Record:** The Court of Appeal initially reversed on jury-selection grounds. Its partially unpublished opinion discussed other objections without deciding whether they independently required reversal. The State Supreme Court addressed the jury-selection issue alone. The full intermediate opinion was absent from the petition appendix but later included in the merits materials.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Finality under §1257 is independently mandatory despite the parties’ agreement. A later adverse final state judgment would permit renewed federal review. This is the 2004 jurisdiction case, not a challenge to prison racial classifications.
* **Relevant Dates:** State Supreme Court judgment reported in 2003; substantive state appellate proceedings remain unfinished at the May 3, 2004 event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The issue-specific review standard appears below.

### Issue 1: Final state judgment and preserved federal review

* **Controlling Law:** Florida v. Thomas requires dismissal where substantive state proceedings remain, later federal review is available and no Cox exception applies. It did not decide the underlying search question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A decided federal issue alone does not make an entire state judgment final. Test the actual proceedings against the four Cox categories; exceptional urgency cannot merely repeat the ordinary importance of the asserted right.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257(a); Supreme Court Rules 14.1(g), 14.1(i), 15.2 and 24.1(e) concerning jurisdiction and relevant opinions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn preserves limited finality exceptions. Batson v. Kentucky governs the underlying constitutional issue only after lawful jurisdiction exists.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Florida v. Thomas governs this procedural structure; the remaining inquiry is application to the particular remand.
* **Open Point:** Whether an exceptional finality category applies despite available later review and unresolved grounds for reversal.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The State Supreme Court rejected the intermediate jury-selection analysis and returned the cause for further proceedings, leaving independent appellate claims open.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Johnson argues that the federal issue has been settled against him and warrants immediate review under an exception protecting federal policy.
* **Respondent or appellee:** California defends its Batson threshold; the parties’ original jurisdictional agreement cannot confer jurisdiction.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Completion of the state appeal may eliminate the need for review or supply a final adverse judgment eligible for a new petition.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Dismissal for nonfinality leaves the state remand operative without approving its constitutional reasoning. If a genuine Cox exception were established, the Court could then address the Batson issue. Nothing shown establishes unavailable later review or a distinctive erosion of federal policy beyond the ordinary rejected constitutional claim.

## 76. Middleton v. McNeil

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Middleton v. McNeil
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1028; 541 U.S. 433.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-03
* **Question Presented:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied clearly established federal law when it assessed a mistaken phrase in an imperfect-self-defense instruction in the context of the full trial charge.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing the district court’s denial of habeas relief concerning McNeil’s murder conviction.
* **Relief Requested:** The warden seeks reinstatement of denial; McNeil seeks relief for an instruction allegedly preventing the jury from considering her honest but unreasonable fear.
* **Material Record:** The charge repeatedly described imperfect self-defense correctly, but one definition of imminent peril mistakenly included a reasonable-person qualifier. The prosecution’s explanation also distinguished honest fear from reasonable fear. The state court found no reasonable likelihood of a misleading overall charge.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is §2254 review, not direct review of whether the charge was perfectly drafted. A correct legal framework does not excuse an instruction that actually changes the burden in its full context.
* **Relevant Dates:** Only federal holdings established when the state court adjudicated the claim control §2254(d)(1); subsequent statements cannot create that baseline.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Yarborough v. Gentry concerns habeas deference in a different counsel-performance setting. Only an effective holding supplies controlling law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The issue-specific review standard appears below.

### Issue 1: Contextual instructional review under §2254

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor requires objective assessment of unreasonable application, distinct from ordinary error and from polling reasonable judges. It preserves the force of established general rules. Lockyer v. Andrade confirms that §2254(d)(1) requires a contrary governing rule or an objectively unreasonable application, rather than independent error alone; clearly established substantive law is measured at the state merits adjudication without demanding a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record rather than isolated phrases or a correct formula alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the charge as a whole under the reasonable-likelihood standard, then ask whether the state adjudication crossed §2254’s objective threshold. Correct isolated sentences cannot automatically cure a contrary operative command.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Fourteenth Amendment due process.
* **Material Other Authority:** Estelle v. McGuire and Boyde v. California require contextual assessment of alleged instructional ambiguity; Cupp v. Naughten rejects review of a phrase in isolation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law already supplies the constitutional and habeas tests. The question is their application, not creation of a new instruction formula.
* **Open Point:** No necessary new legal test; the disputed point is whether this record makes the state application objectively unreasonable.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit treated the erroneous qualifier as likely to negate the unreasonable-fear defense despite the surrounding correct instructions.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The warden relies on the repeated correct explanations and the complete trial context to defend the reasonableness of the state adjudication.
* **Respondent or appellee:** McNeil argues that the erroneous definition applied to the very condition the jury had to assess and could overpower otherwise accurate descriptions.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** None independently established for disposition.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** If the state’s whole-charge assessment was objectively reasonable, reverse the federal grant and deny the writ on this claim. If the instruction effectively displaced the defense and no reasonable application of the established contextual rule supports the state judgment, proceed to the applicable prejudice and remedy requirements. Direct-review error alone does not select the latter path.

## 77. Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nippon Steel Corp. v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 337 F.3d 1373 (Fed. Cir. 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-05-10
* **Question Presented:** Whether 19 U.S.C. §1677e(b) permits the Department of Commerce to draw adverse inferences against an exporter that failed to provide requested information to the best of its ability without finding intentional concealment, willfulness, or bad faith.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit, reversing the trade court’s rejection of Commerce’s partial adverse facts; the opinion separately addresses starting-price currency methodology.
* **Relief Requested:** Nippon Steel seeks reversal of the adverse-inference ruling and use of its later-submitted data.
* **Material Record:** Commerce repeatedly requested conversion factors between theoretical and actual steel weights. Nippon first said conversion was unnecessary, then unavailable; after the preliminary adverse calculation it produced information without having made timely inquiries of its factories. The sequence supports an inquiry into ability and effort, not merely whether a response was incomplete.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The submitted question concerns adverse inferences. The separate currency-pricing ruling is not a license to dispose of an ungranted issue.
* **Relevant Dates:** Federal Circuit judgment: August 8, 2003, preceding the May 10, 2004 event. The statute in force at the relevant time governs, without later amendments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The issue-specific review standard appears below.

### Issue 1: Best ability and evidentiary support for adverse inferences

* **Controlling Law:** INS v. Ventura requires return of genuinely unresolved agency fact-finding and delegated choices to the agency unless no lawful work remains. The court independently construes §1677e and reviews Commerce's actual factual support under the governing substantial-evidence standard.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Review the statutory construction independently under the applicable delegation framework; review the agency’s factual determination for substantial evidence on the whole record. Missing information and inadequate cooperation are distinct findings.
* **Other Governing Law:** 19 U.S.C. §1677e(a), (b), with the disclosure and deficiency provisions of §1677m; §1516a(b)(1)(B)(i).
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory best-of-ability language requires more than a missing answer but does not expressly require intentional misconduct.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Commerce can fill a genuine information gap; an adverse inference additionally requires supported failure to cooperate to the respondent’s best ability.
* **Open Point:** Whether culpable intent is an indispensable additional element and what ability-based finding suffices.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit rejected a willfulness prerequisite and required both objectively reasonable recordkeeping expectations and a supported failure to make the available effort.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Nippon distinguishes misunderstanding from deliberate noncooperation and argues that available corrective data should prevent an adverse inference.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The United States and domestic producers argue that the statute demands adequate actual effort, not merely absence of bad faith; repeated unsupported unavailability assertions undermine the process.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** None independently established for disposition.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States defends Commerce’s supported best-ability finding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Rejecting an intent requirement can sustain the adverse-inference decision if substantial evidence establishes capability and inadequate effort. Requiring willfulness would compel reconsideration under that added element. Neither path allows punishment for information genuinely unavailable despite adequate cooperation; any recalculation must follow the applicable statutory data rules.
