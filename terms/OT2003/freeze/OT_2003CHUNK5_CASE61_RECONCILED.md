## Individual compatibility and alternative path

Kennedy's Boston & Maine opinion and Stevens's, O'Connor's, Scalia's and Souter's relevant joins permit recognition of an agency ground actually embodied in its decision. Stevens's Arkansas opinion and the other participating Associates' joins require whole-record support rather than judicial substitution of policy. Ginsburg's current Alaska opinion and O'Connor's, Souter's and Breyer's joins require the responsible agency's reasoned statutory explanation. Kennedy's, Scalia's and Thomas's Alaska dissent insists on identifying the statutory assignment before evaluating how authority is exercised; sections 203 and 313 supply the relevant assignment and retained reconsideration authority here, so that disagreement provides no cause for a different result. Breyer's own Boston Edison appellate reasoning supports consideration that can genuinely alter an order rather than a formal rehearing label.

The strongest alternative is that transactions closed before objections could effectively be made. Every commitment answers it through the actual admitted submissions, substantive reconsideration and retained statutory power, not by declaring closing irrelevant in every case or assuming that a repeated result makes any procedure harmless. A concrete lost opportunity, material unresolved fact or irreversible decision would matter; none is supplied as an established defect here. Affirmance therefore does not authorize a categorical four-day notice rule or a categorical post-closing cure.

The State's section 203 notice and hearing entitlement remains a statutory command. The creditor's constitutional protected-interest status is assumed, with the process sufficient on that premise; it is not newly adjudicated. Mathews does not replace the statutory requirements. Written presentation can satisfy the hearing requirement on this record without creating a general license to deny fact hearings when their predicates exist.
