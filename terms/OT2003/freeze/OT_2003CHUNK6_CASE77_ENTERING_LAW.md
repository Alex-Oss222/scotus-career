## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard

### Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard

**Governing question:** Substantial-evidence review where the agency allegedly applied a more demanding standard than the one it announced.

**Current rule:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the agency finding must have substantial whole-record support under the actual governing substantive standard. The agency cannot silently turn reasonable doubt into near-proof of the contrary fact, or convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule. Review includes evidence fairly detracting from the finding while preserving the agency’s choice between fairly conflicting views. The agency’s §10(c) preponderance burden remains distinct from appellate substantial-evidence review.

**Authority by component:**

- An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof: Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB, 1998-01-26; Scalia Parts III–IV with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lawful expressly adopted presumptions and evidentiary rules remain possible subject to statutory authority and ordinary review. This rule creates no de novo factfinding authority, mandatory treatment of every unverified report, or finding of systematic agency manipulation.

**Holdings navigation:** Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB — Incumbent-union polling and agency evidentiary standards.

## Standards and Tests selection: Remand for an initial determination assigned to an agency

### Remand for an initial determination assigned to an agency

**Governing question:** A reviewing court confronts a material, fact-sensitive issue the governing statute initially assigns to the agency, which has not decided it.

**Current rule:** The reviewing court ordinarily must return the issue to the agency for its initial determination. It may dispense with remand only when governing law and the established record leave no lawful agency work to perform; confidence in a preferred factual answer does not itself satisfy that exception.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The agency must perform the remaining determination, 2002-11-04; unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Ventura, November 4, 2002.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

#### United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-2044; judgment below, 127 F.3d 1460.
**Decided:** 1999-04-21.

##### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts I–III, unanimous.

##### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (seven Justices).

**Current-force modification:** United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001, resolves the reserved informal-letter question for the transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification mechanism at issue there. Such a letter lacks delegated controlling interpretive force, but its reasons require independent consideration for their actual persuasive value. Haggar's treatment of genuinely delegated regulations and independent judicial factfinding remains intact; Mead neither excludes every informal interpretation nor fixes the disputed tariff classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity and no foreign advancement or improvement except assembly and incidental operations including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment of specific/compound rates; it does not exempt the entire article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing/folding; §10.16(c)'s significant nonassembly processes and unrelated processes retain their introductory conditions despite chemical-treatment examples. Timing after sewing alone does not decide the issue. Informal letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for the regulation's statutory fit and actual application without categorical nondeference. No regulatory validity, exemption, refund, duty amount, new hearing or finding of missing papers is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron supplies review within actual delegated authority and statutory ambiguity.
- NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. permits reasonable administrative classification within statutory limits.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T and National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank require enforcement of enacted boundaries before deference.
- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board separates general authority, particular rule validity and unresolved applications; it creates no automatic validation or universal remand rule.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior distinguishes legally available discretion from its lawful exercise; its distinct remedy does not compel a new Customs proceeding.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001 — controlling authority and persuasive consideration of Customs classification letters under the mechanism reviewed.

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

#### INS v. Ventura, 537 U.S. 12

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-29.
**Decided:** November 4, 2002.

##### When judicial review must return an undecided fact-sensitive issue to the agency

**Holding:** When an agency has not decided a material, fact-sensitive question that the statute assigns to it initially, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand for that determination. Remand may be unnecessary when law and the established record leave no lawful agency work, but the mixed and aged country report here does not establish that exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, exception, application and limited remedy.

**Material application:** The mixed 1997 country report did not compel an individualized risk conclusion. The Board must initially apply the distinct governing pre-IIRIRA asylum and withholding standards.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent judicial review of law remains. The Board may receive updated evidence under governing procedures; that permission requires neither reopening nor a new hearing in every case. No final protection eligibility or current country finding is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the appellate changed-conditions determination and remand through the Ninth Circuit to the Board. The unrelated political-opinion-nexus ruling survives.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80: applies the allocation of the initial administrative judgment to the agency entrusted with it.
- Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion, 470 U.S. 729: applies ordinary remand where lawful administrative work remains, preserving the exceptional case in which remand could serve no lawful purpose.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478: preserves the distinct discipline for reviewing agency factual determinations; the present correction does not reopen the unrelated nexus ruling.

#### Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-795.

**Decided:** 1998-01-26.

##### The announced reasonable-doubt polling threshold is permissible

**Holding:** The Board may require an employer to possess a good-faith reasonable doubt, supported by objective considerations, of an incumbent union's majority support before conducting a safeguarded poll. Using the same prerequisite for polling, withdrawing recognition and seeking a Board election is not, by itself, irrational or inconsistent with the Act; objective doubt is uncertainty about majority support, not proof that the union has lost it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This threshold is permissible, not obligatory for every future Board; §§7 and 8(c) supply no unconditional right to poll. Existing safeguards require a majority-claim inquiry purpose, its communication, assurances against reprisal, secret ballot, no unfair practices or coercive atmosphere, and advance time-and-place notice. Certification, contract and representation protections retain their distinct predicates.

##### The presented challenge reaches the standard actually applied

**Holding:** A challenge expressly directed to the amount of evidence demanded before polling fairly includes whether the Board applied its announced reasonable-doubt standard to the order under review. That inquiry reaches the associated whole-record application here; it creates no general permission to add an independent factual question merely because merits briefs discuss it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

##### An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof

**Holding:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the Board's finding must be supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole under the substantive standard actually governing the case. When the announced predicate is objective reasonable doubt, the agency cannot sustain its order by effectively demanding proof approaching actual loss of majority support, or silently convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts III–IV have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, providing five direct votes for this framework and its application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency’s §10(c) preponderance burden differs from whole-record substantial-evidence review. Lawful expressly adopted presumptions remain possible; expertise cannot silently alter the governing standard, and no systematic-manipulation finding or automatic credit for unverified reports follows.

##### This record requires denial of enforcement

**Holding:** The finding that Allentown lacked objective reasonable doubt when it announced the poll is unsupported by substantial evidence on the whole record. The enforcement judgment must therefore be reversed with instructions to deny enforcement of the challenged Board order; the record does not call for another agency weighing under the same or a newly demanding threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the application and complete mandate.

**Material application:** The individually accepted reports together with Marsh, Bloch and steward Mohr establish objective uncertainty without a headcount of actual minority support. Their cumulative relation to the continuing workforce controls; the later 19–13 poll provides no retroactive justification.

##### The separate remedy objection remains outside review

**Holding:** Section 10(e) bars an objection not urged before the Board unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. The separate objection to the bargaining order's explanation and decertification bar was not preserved, and no such excuse is established; that objection is not reviewed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion: Part V has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in leaving the objection unreviewed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The facial challenge is rejected by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The different majority of Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverses enforcement and directs its denial. No second weighing of the unchanged record, retroactively higher threshold, new poll or permanent decertification follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Auciello's accepted-offer cutoff remains intact. Its statutory limits, distinction between circumstantial doubt and actual minority, and separate §10(e) requirement are applied; it had not resolved incumbent polling or the weight of these reports.
- Fall River supplies separate review of rational Board policy and supported factual application. Its continuity and successor principles are not reopened, and its rebuttable presumption does not become proof of employee preference.
- Universal Camera supplies whole-record substantial-evidence review, including contrary evidence and the examiner's actual findings, while retaining deference between fairly conflicting views. The Court applies that standard to this unsupported finding rather than replacing it with de novo factfinding.
- Gissel retains its protection of noncoercive expression and its attention to the employment setting. Its reservation concerning mandatory permission for reasonable inquiry is preserved; the Court derives no unconditional polling entitlement from it.
- Hubbard's distinction between supporting arguments and new questions is applied to the wording and challenged order here. No exception based merely on merits briefing is adopted.
- Struksnes's procedural safeguards and the additional advance-notice requirement remain operative. Their satisfaction does not eliminate the separately permissible incumbent-polling prerequisite.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

## Event

**Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation v. EPA, No. 02-658.** Argued October 8, 2003; decided January 21, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 298 F.3d 814. The questions are whether the Clean Air Act authorizes EPA to prevent construction when a state BACT determination lacks a reasoned statutory basis and whether EPA lawfully exercised that authority for the Red Dog Mine's MG-17 generator. Alaska initially identified SCR as feasible, then approved a different choice on an offset rationale and later economic reasoning.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The operative EPA construction restrictions remain effective according to their actual modified terms, including the March allowance for summer-dependent construction; the withdrawn December direction is not revived. The July 16, 2003 permit already selects SCR as BACT, subject to a decision in Alaska's favor in this case. Affirmance leaves that condition unsatisfied, so the permit's SCR term remains operative. A reasoned reconsideration through ordinary lawful agency procedures remains available; the judgment requires no fresh permit proceeding.

## Opinion Topology

Ginsburg delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Kennedy filed a dissenting opinion, joined by Scalia and Thomas.

The Court's two holdings and remedy are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent on statutory authority and would reverse on that ground.

## Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer supply six direct votes for both rules, their explanations and application.

**1. EPA may enforce the substantive BACT requirement within the State's reasoned decisional range.** Sections 113(a)(5) and 167 authorize EPA to prevent construction that fails the Clean Air Act's prevention-of-significant-deterioration requirements, including section 165(a)(4)'s best-available-control-technology requirement. EPA may intervene when a state BACT determination lacks a reasoned basis under section 169(3)'s maximum-achievable-reduction standard and its energy, environmental, economic and other-cost factors, but may not replace a supported state choice merely because it prefers another permissible technology.

The Court reads the state assignment and federal enforcement provisions together. The permitting authority makes the case-specific determination, while the separately enacted federal construction provisions enforce substantive compliance. A permit bearing the BACT label cannot conclusively establish compliance with the requirement it must satisfy. EPA must identify and demonstrate the actual defect; the State retains the permissible range of reasoned choice. That limit answers the objection that federal enforcement turns EPA into an unrestricted appellate permitting authority. State review does not displace the federal enforcement channels Congress supplied.

Mead and Christensen distinguish statutory authority from an agency's persuasive advice. Walton and Heimmermann require an actual grant and its authorized exercise. The Court independently identifies the enforcement power in this Act; EPA's experience may inform persuasion but creates no power of its own. Bragg likewise preserves a retained federal command after state primacy without converting every discretionary state judgment into a nondiscretionary federal duty.

**2. EPA reasonably identified the defect in Alaska's explanation.** Review under 5 U.S.C. §706(2)(A) sustains EPA's orders when the administrative record supports its conclusion that the State's BACT explanation falls outside the reasoned statutory range. Alaska's abandoned offset rationale and unsupported economic reversal meet that standard here; the operative federal orders are sustained as modified.

Alaska initially found selective catalytic reduction technically and economically feasible. It then abandoned the offset theory supporting a different choice and invoked costs and competitiveness while acknowledging that it lacked the information necessary to assess those consequences. A rural-utility analogy and the mine's regional importance did not establish the asserted effect on this mine. The defect is the missing connection in the State's actual reasoning. Costs remain a statutory consideration, and the top-down method was Alaska's chosen method rather than a universal statutory command.

EPA's path is reasonably discernible from its orders and explanatory correspondence. The court reviews that path rather than supplying reasons or selecting technology. The State's acknowledged information gap does not automatically require one control technology whenever a financial statement is absent. The required reasoned explanation preserves the State's role while giving the substantive federal requirement effect.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Mead and Christensen v. Harris County:** Applied to distinguish the independent identification of statutory power from persuasive agency reasoning.
- **Barnhart v. Walton and Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.:** Applied for actual delegation and authorized exercise; expertise and uncertainty do not confer power.
- **Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association:** Applied for the survival of an identified federal permitting command after state primacy; its nondiscretionary-duty holding does not establish federal review of every state judgment.
- **INS v. Ventura:** Its allocation of initial administrative judgment remains effective; judicial review identifies the statutory defect while the responsible agency makes any subsequent BACT determination.

## Law After Decision

EPA may enforce the requirement that a state BACT determination have a reasoned statutory basis. The State retains its case-specific weighing within the statutory range, and federal intervention requires an identified, record-supported deficiency. This application sustains the actual modified orders and leaves the existing conditional permit operative under its own terms.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The Act specifically assigns BACT's case-specific weighing to the state permitting authority. The dissent reads the federal prevention provisions to enforce required permits, required consideration and independently specified federal conditions, but not to create a federal merits-review channel over the completed discretionary balance. A reasonableness limit describes how review would operate without establishing that Congress authorized EPA to conduct it. Weak state reasons therefore cannot themselves create that power. The dissent would reverse the federal interference on the allocation ground, giving effect to the July permit's Alaska-success condition according to its terms; it does not need to choose a superior technology.

## Holdings

### Statutory notice and opportunity for hearing

**Holding and operative rule:** An expedited approval under Federal Power Act section 203 can satisfy the statutory notice and hearing requirements through informative notice and written proceedings when the agency genuinely considers the material submissions while retaining effective authority to alter its decision, and the issues require no additional fact hearing. The abbreviated periods here, followed by substantive reconsideration, furnished that opportunity; an objector seeking further process must identify a material factual issue or a lost opportunity that the actual process failed to address.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding, the constitutional holding and the record-review holding below; all nine Justices join each proposition and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 203 requires notice and an opportunity for hearing. Its command is distinct from the constitutional inquiry. The notices identified the proposed ownership transactions, and the record supports the prescribed mailing without making actual receipt an additional statutory condition. FERC admitted the objections, permitted late intervention in one proceeding and addressed the creditor and public-interest submissions on reconsideration. Section 313 left it able to change its orders. The agency therefore provided an effective opportunity to influence the decision even though the transactions had closed.

The objectors emphasize the seven- and four-day comment windows and rapid closing. Those facts require examination of the whole process, including whether later consideration could still afford effective relief. They do not establish a lost procedural opportunity by themselves. The objections here were resolved through written submissions and reasoned orders; no identified material factual dispute required testimony or cross-examination. An unchanged result alone would not establish adequate reconsideration. The holding depends on the responsive process and retained authority shown in this record, rather than a general minimum-period or postapproval-cure rule.

### The creditor's constitutional process claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming the public power agency had a protected creditor interest, the notice, written submissions and effective reconsideration afforded it constitutionally sufficient process in these proceedings. The nature of the actual dispute required meaningful participation and responsive consideration, rather than a trial-type hearing merely because the proposed transactions might affect creditors.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court; protected-interest status is assumed for resolving the process claim and remains undecided because the process was sufficient even on that premise.

**Controlling explanation:** Mullane requires notice reasonably adapted to the affected interests. Mathews considers the private interest, the risk of error and value of additional safeguards, and the government's interests and practical needs. Here the creditor could submit its asset-shielding objection, the agency considered it, and the legal and policy issues could be addressed on the written record. The financing concerns during the energy crisis explain the need for expedition, but the effective later consideration supplies the necessary opportunity to be heard. Repeating the proceedings without identifying a material issue excluded from that consideration would add no safeguard required by this record.

### Review of the agency's own public-interest determination

**Holding and operative rule:** A court reviewing these approvals must evaluate FERC's actual reasons and the supporting administrative record. FERC's consideration of the creditor objection together with the financing and generation interests supports the challenged approvals; disagreement with that assessment supplies no basis to replace the agency's supported determination.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. permits recognition of a ground embodied in the agency's decision while forbidding a court-created substitute. Arkansas v. Oklahoma requires whole-record support for agency findings rather than a court's preferred policy balance. FERC's reconsideration orders themselves addressed the asserted creditor consequences and the transactions' financing and generation purposes. Those reasons sustain the decision on the issues reviewed. Independent creditor, fraudulent-transfer and bankruptcy claims lie outside this procedural review and retain their own governing requirements.

**Precedent treatment:** Mullane and Mathews are applied to the actual constitutional procedure; Boston & Maine and Arkansas are applied to FERC's own reasons and supported determination. Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation v. EPA preserves the distinction between statutory authority and reasoned exercise of that authority; sections 203 and 313 supply the authority relevant here.

## Event

**Household Credit Services, Inc. v. Pfennig**, No. 02-857; 541 U.S. 232. Merits decision, April 21, 2004. Argued February 23, 2004. Review of the Sixth Circuit's judgment rejecting Regulation Z's exclusion of credit-card over-limit fees from the finance charge. Pfennig's agreement specified a credit limit and a monthly charge for exceeding it. Her statements disclosed the charge separately. The question concerns the Board's authority to classify that fee, rather than concealment of the fee itself.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must apply the valid over-limit exclusion and dismiss the theory that these fees necessarily had to be included in the finance-charge subtotal. Any actually pleaded independent claim must be resolved under its own statutory duty.

## Opinion Topology

Thomas delivered the unanimous opinion of the Court. No separate opinion was filed.

## Holdings

### The Board may separately classify these over-limit charges

**Controlling proposition:** Under 15 U.S.C. §1604(a)'s express classification authority, the Board may reasonably exclude fees for exceeding an agreed credit limit from TILA's finance-charge category while leaving applicable separate disclosure duties in force. Regulation Z §226.4(c)(2) validly makes that classification, and knowingly honoring an over-limit transaction does not by itself require a different classification; the statutory-conflict or unreasonableness challenge fails here.

**Authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion of the Court controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Mead requires actual delegated authority, Haggar requires independent enforcement of statutory limits, and Heimmermann keeps authorized interpretation within the underlying prohibition. Section 1604(a) expressly permits classifications, differentiations, adjustments and exceptions necessary or proper to effectuate TILA's purposes, prevent circumvention or evasion, or facilitate compliance. Its exclusion for section 1602(aa) mortgages retains its own scope; this case concerns a card account. The distinct exemption power in section 1604(f) does not supply the rule here.

Section 1605(a)'s broad definition of charges incident to credit does not unambiguously decide this contingent excess-limit category. Pfennig reasonably argues that an issuer permitting an additional advance imposes the fee because it extended credit. But permission for a transaction does not necessarily renegotiate the agreed limit, and the statutory scheme distinguishes finance charges from other open-end charges. The Board reasonably separates the agreed price of credit from a fee triggered by exceeding its terms, facilitating intelligible price comparison and compliance. Administrative convenience alone could not contradict Congress, and disclosure elsewhere alone could not validate an unlawful classification; the express delegation and permissible statutory distinction do that work.

Section 226.4(c)(2) specifically addresses exceeding a credit limit. Subsection (c)(3)'s different exclusion for paying items that overdraw an account contains a prior-written-agreement exception; that exception does not govern (c)(2). Smiley's treatment of charges as interest under another statute supplies no contrary TILA definition. The Court therefore sustains this category while requiring compliance with its actual terms and all independently applicable disclosure duties.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Mead Corp.:** Applied to actual authority exercised through a binding regulation; its different conclusion about transaction-specific Customs letters does not govern this mechanism.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.:** Applied for independent statutory-boundary review followed by reasonableness within genuine delegated ambiguity.
- **Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.:** Applied for actual delegation and preservation of substantive statutory limits; uncertainty alone cannot confer power.
- **Chevron U.S.A. v. Natural Resources Defense Council:** Applied within those actual authority and statutory limits to the reasonable regulatory choice.
- **Smiley v. Citibank:** Distinguished as a construction of national-bank interest. That statute's category does not determine the TILA finance-charge category.
