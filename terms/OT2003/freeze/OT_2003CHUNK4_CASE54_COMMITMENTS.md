## Six-Associate controlling proposal: apply the existing confrontation rule

An unavailable witness's custodial accusation does not acquire a firmly rooted exception simply because some parts of her narrative expose her to criminal responsibility. Where no applicable rooted exception supports admission, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from the circumstances of the accusation itself. The defendant's account or other trial evidence cannot supply those guarantees by corroborating, or appearing to interlock with, the accusation.

White retains the received exception-sensitive framework; Lilly separates self-inculpatory admissions from accusations of another and applies Wright's intrinsic-guarantee requirement. The Washington Supreme Court itself rejected a firmly rooted exception and instead relied on the two accounts' overlapping omissions concerning a weapon. That is external comparison, not a circumstance making Sylvia's accusation inherently trustworthy. The omitted certainty in two separate accounts cannot repair this constitutional defect.

The supplied intrinsic grounds are insufficient on this record as well. Sylvia was questioned twice after warnings in the investigation of an assault she was alleged to have facilitated. The challenged account responded to directed questions and differed from an earlier account; she acknowledged closing her eyes during the stabbing. These record features directly bear on the accuracy of the observation used to disprove self-defense. Recent occurrence and a general eyewitness position do not remove the uncertainty about what she saw, when she saw it, or the circumstances of the custodial accusation. Calling the investigator neutral is a lower-court characterization, not a guarantee arising from disinterested spontaneous speech. Her admission that she helped locate Lee does not make each assertion concerning Crawford's use of force equally disserving or intrinsically reliable.

This conclusion answers the strongest State ground without declaring every self-inculpatory or equally inculpatory statement inadmissible. The State's particular evidence and stated reasons fail the prosecution's existing burden. Unlike an unexplained lower ruling that requires an initial assessment, the screened record supplies the trial court's intrinsic reasons, the intermediate court's identified defects and the state supreme court's corroboration rationale. A further chance to apply the same trustworthiness rule is not necessary to decide this record.

The broader replacement path is legally available but not adopted by these six. They can resolve the presented police accusation and remedy through White, Lilly and Wright at their established scope. Their actual prior joins and the record's decisive intrinsic and corroboration defects support that sufficient ground. They do not convert this case into a new formula for all hearsay, and they do not rely on a premise that a replacement is categorically forbidden. The absence of a broader change follows their supported ground rather than a manufactured factual reservation.

## Scalia and Thomas: formal police accusation requires confrontation

Both vote to reverse on the rule that a formal, recorded custodial interrogation by investigating officers, eliciting an account of past criminal events for evidentiary use against the accused, cannot replace trial confrontation merely because a judge finds the account reliable. When such an accuser is unavailable, prior opportunity for cross-examination is necessary to admit the accusation for its truth. Crawford had no such opportunity, and the formal interrogations here satisfy this defined class.

Their White reservation, Lilly refusal to adopt the reliability framework, and Hale reservation about possible reconsideration of formalized testimony support taking this presented question directly. Mattox's former-testimony treatment identifies unavailability plus prior confrontation as the relevant protection for testimony. A recorded investigatory examination serves the trial-accusation function against which that protection is directed; judicial confidence in truthfulness is not the procedure the Clause secures. The strongest contrary argument is that White and the established hearsay framework already accommodate dependable out-of-court evidence. Their answer confines the proposed change to this formal police-accusation class and rejects reliability substitution there, rather than abolishing all established exceptions.

They would displace Roberts and Lilly only insofar as their reliability framework permits admission of such a formal accusation without the required prior examination. White's accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment holdings retain their own circumstances, while its rejection of a formal-only limitation on the entire Clause is not converted into an exclusion of all other protection. Hale's accepted diligent-search/nonentry certificates concern regularly maintained independent public records and a limited search function, rather than an eyewitness account elicited to reconstruct the charged encounter; their existing admission holding is not overruled by this defined proposed rule. No broader testimonial taxonomy is necessary to the concurrence's stated position.
