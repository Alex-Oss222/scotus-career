## 40. Leebaert v. Harrington

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Leebaert v. Harrington
* **Citation or Docket:** 332 F.3d 134 (Second Circuit, 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-02-17
* **Question Presented:** Whether parents’ Fourteenth Amendment right to direct their children’s upbringing and education includes a right to exempt children attending public school from compulsory health instruction; and whether a combined parental-rights and free-exercise claim requires heightened scrutiny of an otherwise neutral and generally applicable curriculum.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit affirmed summary judgment rejecting the federal parental-rights and religious-exercise challenges; state-law claims were dismissed without prejudice.
* **Relief Requested:** The parent seeks a mandatory curricular exemption and reversal of the federal judgment; school officials seek affirmance.
* **Material Record:** A parent objected to his son’s attendance at required public-school health classes. The stipulated record concerned exposure to instruction rather than a requirement to profess belief, submit to treatment, or engage in sexual conduct. The parent did not contest that the attendance rule satisfied rational-basis review.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate constitutional claims retain their own elements. The existing concession concerns rational-basis fit, not the legal choice of scrutiny. State statutory exemptions and the precise curriculum remain distinct from a federal veto over general instruction.
* **Relevant Dates:** The appellate opinion issued June 10, 2003, before the February 17, 2004 event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment on stipulated facts; review of the constitutional framework is independent.

### Issue 1: Parental direction and neutral public instruction

* **Controlling Law:** Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. rejects a parental veto over the compelled presentation on its actual allegations while preserving coercion, affirmation, disclosure, and bodily-intrusion questions. Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth rejects a veto over an optional program and expressly distinguishes compulsory instruction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual interference with parental direction and any independent religious burden. Instructional disagreement is not automatically compelled affirmation; combining claims does not eliminate either claim’s requirements.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment liberty and First Amendment Free Exercise Clause. The relevant local curricular and statutory exemption provisions must be applied according to their terms.
* **Material Other Authority:** Meyer v. Nebraska, Pierce v. Society of Sisters, Wisconsin v. Yoder, and Employment Division v. Smith supply the preserved parental-choice and religious-exercise background within the cases’ limits.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** the compulsory-presentation decision already rejects an unlimited curricular veto. It reserves materially different personal coercion and targeting.
* **Open Point:** Whether the stipulated curriculum imposes such a distinct burden and whether the asserted combination supplies an independently actionable right rather than a label.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The parent relies on educational autonomy, Wisconsin v. Yoder, and a combined parental/free-exercise theory for strict scrutiny. The officials distinguish choice of schooling from control over each lesson in a public program, emphasize neutral attendance, and invoke the conceded reasonable fit to health education. The lower court found no constitutional basis for heightened review. An allegation of compelled profession or discriminatory exemptions would require separate analysis, but cannot be invented from opposition to course content.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the federal judgment under the actual neutral-instruction record. If the record establishes compelled affirmation, treatment, religious targeting, or a legally relevant exemption system, apply the doctrine belonging to that burden and determine appropriate relief. Preserve dismissal without prejudice of unadjudicated state claims.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A lawful rule concerns ordinary curricular attendance, not the whole parent-child relationship or every school requirement. It leaves religious coercion, personal disclosure, and bodily integrity to their independent protections.

## 41. United States v. McFarland

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. McFarland
* **Citation or Docket:** 311 F.3d 376; Fifth Circuit No. 00-10569 (en banc).
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-02-19
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Hobbs Act, consistently with the Commerce Clause, permits federal prosecution of robberies of local retail stores based on depletion of the stores’ assets and their purchase of goods originating outside the State, without proof that each robbery individually had a substantial effect on interstate commerce.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** An evenly divided en banc Fifth Circuit affirmed the district court’s conviction and sentence. The equal division supplies a judgment, not a majority appellate rationale.
* **Relief Requested:** McFarland seeks reversal of his Hobbs Act convictions on the commerce ground; the United States seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** The charges concerned armed robberies of four operating Fort Worth stores. The asserted connection is the taking of business assets from retailers participating in interstate inventory markets. A store label alone is insufficient; the actual business function and statutory commerce proof matter.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The equal division does not prevent review of the judgment. The merits inquiry concerns the enacted commerce element and the constitutional class; it does not authorize a new element or a general rule for robberies of private individuals.
* **Relevant Dates:** The en banc judgment issued October 28, 2002, before the February 19, 2004 event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Government must prove the enacted criminal elements beyond a reasonable doubt. Independent constitutional review concerns whether the established connection is legally sufficient.

### Issue 1: Robbery of operating commercial assets

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Robinson already sustains the Hobbs Act for robbery directed at operating assets of active interstate businesses when the taking directly impairs commercial operations and the statutory commerce element is established.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Connection precedes magnitude. Aggregation permits a slight individual effect within the connected economic class; a business label, generic consumer-spending chain, or generalized crime costs cannot establish that connection.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §8; 18 U.S.C. §§1951 and, to the extent a derivative challenge is granted, 924(c).
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Lopez and Wickard v. Filburn retain the class-and-connection sequence reflected in United States v. Robinson.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The controlling rule already reaches depletion of actual operating assets used in interstate commercial activity. The question is its application, not whether every robbery requires a substantial individual effect.
* **Open Point:** Whether the proved store operations and taken assets satisfy the settled rule; no new general depletion doctrine is necessary.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

McFarland argues that small local robberies remain local crimes and that attenuation through replenishment cannot suffice. The Government identifies working capital taken directly from ongoing retailers with interstate inventory, then aggregates that connected class. The divided circuit produced competing views but no majority rule. United States v. Robinson supplies the Court's controlling answer to the proposed substantial-individual-effect requirement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the record establishes operating business assets and the enacted interstate-commerce element. If a necessary statutory predicate was not proved, apply the ordinary sufficiency consequences rather than remand to prove a newly invented nexus. An unrelated personal robbery requires its own statutory and constitutional analysis.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule governs direct robbery of operating commercial assets, including relatively small losses. It does not federalize robbery because the victim is employed by an interstate company or might later spend money.

## 42. Illinois v. Fisher

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Illinois v. Fisher
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-374; 540 U.S. 544.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-02-23
* **Question Presented:** Whether destruction of potentially useful evidence in good faith violates due process merely because the defendant had requested it in discovery or it was central to the defense.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Illinois Appellate Court reversed Fisher’s drug conviction and required dismissal under a discovery-request exception to the usual bad-faith rule.
* **Relief Requested:** Illinois seeks reversal and further proceedings on the conviction; Fisher seeks retention of the dismissal.
* **Material Record:** The seized substance tested positive for cocaine four times. Fisher requested physical evidence, then failed to appear and remained absent for more than a decade. Police destroyed the substance under their regular procedures before his return. No bad faith was alleged or found; an independent fifth test might have been useful, but no exculpatory result was established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The distinction between apparently exculpatory evidence and evidence only potentially useful is essential. This is direct state review, not deferential federal habeas. State-law sanctions may exceed the federal constitutional floor.
* **Relevant Dates:** The arrest and discovery request occurred in 1988; destruction occurred in September 1999, before Fisher’s November 1999 return. The scheduled event is February 23, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The constitutional classification of the destroyed evidence governs the applicable burden; the defendant cannot obtain federal dismissal solely by invoking a pending request.

### Issue 1: Destroyed potentially useful evidence

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted decision displaces the received distinction in California v. Trombetta and Arizona v. Youngblood. Kyles v. Whitley addresses favorable evidence suppressed in the prosecution team’s possession, not a presumption that an unperformed test would exculpate.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apparently exculpatory, irreplaceable evidence and merely potentially useful evidence remain distinct. Under the received rule for the latter, the claimant must establish bad faith; a discovery request is relevant context but does not itself prove it.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; independent Illinois evidence-preservation and discovery rules retain their own remedies.
* **Material Other Authority:** California v. Trombetta and Arizona v. Youngblood supply the preservation framework; Brady v. Maryland supplies the separate favorable-evidence disclosure duty.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The rule already governs evidence that could have been tested with an unknown outcome. It does not immunize deliberate destruction of known exculpatory material.
* **Open Point:** Whether a discovery request or unusual importance creates a categorical federal exception to the bad-faith requirement.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Illinois invokes the four inculpatory tests, regular destruction procedures, and absence of bad faith. Fisher argues that a formal request placed the State on notice and that the substance was uniquely necessary to contest identity. The lower court treated those considerations as removing the need to prove bad faith. The parties’ strongest disagreement concerns the constitutional classification and duty, not whether independent testing would have been desirable.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the federal due-process dismissal if the received potentially-useful-evidence rule controls. Affirm only by adopting a supported exception or finding a different evidentiary category; neither an exculpatory result nor bad faith can be invented. Preserve any properly available independent state-law claim on remand.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule declining the proposed categorical exception leaves discovery requests relevant to actual bad faith and preserves stricter state remedies. It need not announce that every routine destruction policy is constitutionally valid.

* **Controlling Law:** Wilson v. Layne limits execution to the authority the warrant actually supplies. Hope v. Pelzer preserves particularized fair warning without requiring a previously litigated factual replica; its 2002 decision cannot itself furnish 1997 notice.

Groh argues that the judge approved a particular search, the officers stayed within the application’s limits, and the drafting error should not defeat otherwise reasonable conduct or immunity. An oral explanation, even if proved, is not a judicially imposed written limit; its occurrence is disputed anyway.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the denial of the lead officer’s summary judgment if the unincorporated application cannot cure the warrant and the defect was clearly unlawful in 1997. Reverse on a supported merits or immunity ground without reviving unappealed claims. Further proceedings on causation and damages remain necessary even if immunity fails.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule can cover complete item-description omissions and the responsible preparer without deciding every clerical mistake, cross-reference, or subordinate officer’s reliance.

* **Controlling Law:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins requires age actually to motivate the decision and distinguishes service-based vesting from age. O’Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp. rejects a rigid protected-group replacement rule while preserving age causation.

The employees rely on any individual and because of age, arguing that Congress expressly protected all workers at least forty. The circuit found a claim; the district court treated the alleged reverse-age discrimination as outside the Act. A policy’s social desirability does not itself decide the text.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the Act’s prohibition is directional, leaving any independent state-law claim to its own law. Affirm coverage if the text forbids the differential, with defenses and remedies still to be adjudicated. Either path must distinguish express age motivation from an independent entitlement to benefits.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The question supports a general statutory rule about older-worker preferences; it does not settle all age-related benefits rules, disparate impact, or constitutional equality.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

### Issue 1: An external accident in the causal chain

* **Controlling Law:** Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co. separates the Convention’s liability conditions from domestic damage categories. El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng preserves the Convention’s field-specific remedial structure.

No concession at oral argument is inferred from the historical opinion’s account.

Affirm if the established unusual refusal is a causal event within Article 17. Reverse if the treaty excludes such a link, while respecting treaty exclusivity rather than automatically authorizing a replacement state claim. An unsupported causation inference would require factual reconsideration, not a categorical rule about all medical episodes.

## 54. Crawford v. Washington

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Crawford v. Washington
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-9410; 541 U.S. 36.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-08
* **Question Presented:** Whether admitting an unavailable witness’s recorded police interrogation without an opportunity for cross-examination violates the Confrontation Clause, including whether the received reliability framework should govern such accusations.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Washington Supreme Court reinstated Crawford’s assault conviction, accepting the statement’s reliability in part because it interlocked with his own account.
* **Relief Requested:** Crawford seeks reversal of the conviction; Washington seeks affirmance of the admission and judgment.
* **Material Record:** After Crawford stabbed a man, police separately questioned Crawford and his wife Sylvia. Her recorded account undermined his account of self-defense. Marital privilege prevented her trial testimony; Crawford had never cross-examined her. The trial court admitted the recording through the declaration-against-interest exception. The state appellate courts disagreed about its reliability.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statements’ custodial, investigative setting and the absence of cross-examination are established. The prosecution’s reliance on interlocking accounts raises the distinction between intrinsic reliability and external corroboration. Whether any constitutional error is harmless remains separate.
* **Relevant Dates:** The police questioning and trial precede the state judgment; the scheduled event is March 8, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Direct review permits application of the governing constitutional rule. A preserved confrontation violation requires the State to establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under Chapman v. California; it does not require an acquittal.

### Issue 1: Custodial accusations and constitutional reliability

* **Controlling Law:** White v. Illinois retains the exception-sensitive confrontation framework and rejects confinement of the Clause to formal trial substitutes. Lilly v. Virginia holds that labeling a custodial accomplice accusation against penal interest does not make it firmly rooted; outside a rooted exception the prosecution must establish particularized intrinsic guarantees, without using external corroboration to supply them.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual hearsay exception and its foundation. Where it is not firmly rooted, examine the circumstances of making the statement for particularized trustworthiness; accuracy inferred from other trial evidence does not replace that inquiry. No categorical testimonial replacement has been adopted.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; the applicable Washington marital privilege and declaration-against-interest rule establish the state-law posture without answering the federal question.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ohio v. Roberts and Idaho v. Wright remain part of the received framework as preserved and delimited by White v. Illinois and Lilly v. Virginia. Mattox v. United States addresses the admission of previously cross-examined testimony when a witness is unavailable; Coy v. Iowa and Maryland v. Craig concern trial-witness presentation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law already rejects automatic reliability from a penal-interest label and external corroboration. It leaves the Court able to consider a proposed replacement rule, but no historical later formulation is current law.
* **Open Point:** Whether this statement satisfies the existing constitutional standard, and whether that standard should be replaced for police interrogations or a broader defined class.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Crawford argues that an investigative accusation by an unavailable witness requires confrontation, and that the recording also fails existing reliability requirements. Washington invokes the statement’s self-inculpatory features and consistency with Crawford’s account. The state court treated that consistency as a substantial assurance. A broader rule requiring prior cross-examination for a defined testimonial class would change the received framework and require explanation of its effect on White v. Illinois and the public-record boundary, rather than characterization as settled law.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse under existing law if the State has not established intrinsic trustworthiness, with harmless-error proceedings if necessary. Affirm if the actual circumstances independently satisfy that law and the corroboration error does not control. Alternatively adopt a properly justified new confrontation rule, explain its displacement of prior law and direct-review application, and apply it to the recording. None of these paths automatically resolves guilt or orders acquittal.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** An existing-law disposition can govern custodial accusations supported by external interlocking evidence without deciding every hearsay exception. A replacement could govern a defined accusation class, but needs a usable boundary and express precedent treatment.

## 55. Iowa v. Tovar

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Iowa v. Tovar
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1541; 541 U.S. 77.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-08
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Sixth Amendment requires, before an uncounseled guilty plea, specific warnings that a viable defense may be overlooked and that counsel could give an independent opinion about pleading guilty.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Iowa Supreme Court excluded Tovar’s first OWI conviction from enhancement because the plea court did not give those two warnings, and directed entry of a lesser judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Iowa seeks reversal of the mandatory-warning rule; Tovar seeks preservation of relief from the enhanced conviction.
* **Material Record:** Tovar pleaded guilty without counsel in 1996 and received two days in jail. The colloquy addressed the charge, punishment, trial rights, and counsel at trial. In a later third-offense prosecution he challenged use of that conviction. The Iowa court imposed two additional categorical warnings. Proceedings.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Because the first conviction imposed imprisonment, a valid counsel waiver matters. Competency, actual knowledge of counsel’s availability at the plea, and the necessity of two prescribed warnings are separate issues. No verified oral-argument concession is used to assign a disputed collateral burden.
* **Relevant Dates:** First plea November 1996; later third-offense prosecution in 2000; scheduled review March 8, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** This is a collateral challenge to an earlier conviction within a later prosecution, not direct review of the 1996 plea. The state burden framework must be identified without converting a decision about categorical warnings into a finding of actual waiver.

### Issue 1: Knowing waiver at a plea hearing

* **Controlling Law:** Godinez v. Moran distinguishes competency from the separate knowing and voluntary waiver inquiry; competence to plead does not prove that counsel was knowingly surrendered. Parke v. Raley permits a bounded presumption and burden sequence for collateral challenges to prior counseled convictions, not automatic validation of every uncounseled plea.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess actual understanding of the right, the charge and consequences in the circumstances of the relevant stage. A constitutional waiver requires more than capacity, while the specific advice necessary depends on what counsel would do at that stage.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; Iowa’s enhancement statute and applicable collateral procedures. States remain free to prescribe additional prospective colloquy protections under their own law.
* **Material Other Authority:** Johnson v. Zerbst, Faretta v. California, and Patterson v. Illinois supply the received waiver principles. Faretta’s trial-representation setting must be distinguished from entry of a guilty plea.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The right and actual-waiver requirements are established; neither Godinez v. Moran nor Parke v. Raley makes the particular two warnings indispensable.
* **Open Point:** Whether those warnings are constitutional prerequisites in every plea and what remains of any preserved actual-waiver objection once that categorical premise is removed.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Iowa argues that constitutional understanding need not follow a fixed two-sentence script, especially for an elementary charge. Tovar argues that waiver is uninformed without an explanation of counsel’s assistance in deciding whether to plead. The strongest narrower distinction is between mandatory wording and actual awareness that counsel is available at the plea itself. The lower judgment rested on the warning omissions, not a universal conclusion that every uncounseled plea is invalid.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the categorical rule and remand for any preserved actual-waiver issue. Affirm if those specific warnings are necessary constitutional safeguards. A finding that the particular plea lacked actual informed waiver would require its own record ground; rejecting a script does not itself establish that ground’s opposite.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A stage-sensitive waiver rule can reject indispensable wording while requiring actual understanding of the counsel right and the plea. It need not prescribe all collateral-review burdens or limit more protective state procedures.
