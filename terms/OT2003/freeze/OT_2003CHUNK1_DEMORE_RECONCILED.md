## Jurisdiction reconciliation: individual changed-premise review

### The common jurisdiction ground

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this proposition: section 1226(e) does not clearly withdraw independently proper section 2241 jurisdiction to decide the legality of Congress's mandatory detention framework. The first sentence concerns the Attorney General's discretionary judgment; the second concerns an action or decision under the section regarding detention, release, bond or parole. In their combined statutory context, those objects do not clearly make the executive's legal authority under the mandatory framework unreviewable. Effective relief remains possible if the framework supplies no lawful authority. The rule neither reviews lawful discretionary bond decisions nor retries removal facts; ordinary habeas predicates remain. It does not require a particular repeal formula or section-number reference.

The contrary reading is substantial. The second sentence is phrased by the executive action and the relief a court may grant, not only by the first sentence's discretionary object. An order requiring bond consideration would set aside mandatory detention regardless of how the underlying objection is named. A clear action-based bar cannot be evaded simply by calling the challenge constitutional or statutory. The six supporters answer with the distinction between review of an executive custody judgment and adjudication of the governing legislative authority, plus the operative legal-authority channel. This is an interpretation of both sentences, not a purported concession by the Government or a rule that the word habeas must appear.

### O'Connor: supported departure retained

Her historical concurrence expressly continued her disagreement with historical St. Cyr and treated the second sentence as a clearer, broader object than ordinary judicial-review restrictions. That textual distinction remains the strongest objection. The change here is not merely that St. Cyr is binding. In this timeline O'Connor authored Calcano-Martinez's Parts II?III, affirmatively holding that the retained writ must actually decide a substantial legal-eligibility objection and provide effective relief; she also joined the confined constitutional-access construction in AADC and the unanimous actual-channel holdings of Goncalves, St. Cyr and Zadvydas. Her operative rule expressly rejects a mandatory verbal formula while preserving independent review of executive authority. This is a materially different personal statutory and access position from the historical concurrence's retained St. Cyr dissent.

The new provision still requires independent interpretation, and those earlier cases do not mechanically decide it. Her changed position supports reading the protected executive objects against the first sentence's discretionary focus, preserving adjudication of the mandatory framework's legal authority. She does not adopt review merely because a pleading uses a constitutional label. Her merits position remains categorical validity on the stated conditions; jurisdiction is separate from success.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor joins the confined framework-review holding because her own Calcano-Martinez authorship and joined AADC, Goncalves, St. Cyr and Zadvydas positions in this timeline affirmatively preserve effective independent review of legal authority, rather than retaining the historical St. Cyr jurisdiction dissent; she reads section 1226(e)'s executive-action language within that changed statutory position while continuing to reject mandatory risk hearings on the merits.

### Scalia and Thomas: provisional jurisdiction joins revised

Each historically joined O'Connor's particular second-sentence objection. Here each joined Goncalves and later applied the retained channel in St. Cyr and Zadvydas, but each preserved disagreement with Calcano-Martinez's original permanent-scheme construction. Those precedent-following joins are genuine and must be honored within their scope. They do not establish a changed personal construction of section 1226(e), which neither case decided. Unlike O'Connor, neither adopted Calcano's permanent-scheme rationale or AADC's confined constitutional-access construction.

For each Justice, the historically decisive premise therefore remains: the second sentence is a prohibition on setting aside a custody action, not merely a restriction on ordinary judicial review or discretionary balancing. The provisional framework distinction does not identify a new record fact, statutory change or directly controlling rule that answers that objection for either Justice. The historical position remains strong evidence after source review, and the unsupported provisional departure is removed. This is not an automatic replacement of all provisional positions by history: their conditional merits analysis, actual classification safeguards, recent Krimstock and Fairley joins and bounded remedy remain independently supported and intact.

Each declines the jurisdiction holding and explains the statutory bar. Because the Court has enough other jurisdiction supporters, each may proceed on that determination to join the merits. Neither must announce a conclusive Suspension Clause result to do so. Neither supplies an entered dismissal or a new rule eliminating every constitutional forum. A separate writing should preserve the action-based objection if the final lead sustains jurisdiction.

## Merits and statutory-path reconciliation

### Categorical position: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas

Their primary commitments are confirmed at the qualified level stated in the independent handoff: proper category membership may support custody during reasonably dispatched removal proceedings without a risk hearing for every permanent resident. No final removal order is a statutory prerequisite. A substantial category dispute receives the lawful actual inquiry; missing findings are not supplied by the Court. Six months including Kim's requested continuance does not by itself establish unreasonable governmental delay. The later procedural holdings in Gilbert, Krimstock and Fairley do not turn a fact irrelevant to a valid substantive custody rule into a required risk exception.

The historical majority's description of conceded deportability and its aggregate detention figures cannot be reproduced as established present facts. The neutral packet expressly distinguishes initial-arrest acceptance from classification and supplies no such concession. That changed record premise matters to all four: their reversal removes the resident-wide constitutional hearing rule but does not adjudicate either actual charge, direct immediate rearrest or authorize custody if the mandatory category is not properly established. National Center's individual-status safeguards and Ventura's agency-fact allocation support the remand's bounded classification component.

Kennedy additionally preserves the individual threshold and continuing-liberty point confirmed by his historical concurrence: the Government must have sufficient actual support for the operative detention category; unreasonably delayed, unjustified or arbitrary continued detention can call for individualized flight-risk and danger review. He does not find that violation established on this record and does not adopt Breyer's distinct threshold-to-bail construction. No new burden or hearing timetable is imported from the historical writing into the already supported frozen commitment.

The four reversals require lawful implementation with the actual removal and custody stage established. Existing judicial release protection lasts until the lower court implements the mandate, rather than ending through an invented custody event or becoming permanent immunity. Proper statutory classification review remains available. That is the limited consequence of the neutral record's unresolved predicates, not a finding that Kim necessarily wins them.

### Constitutional position: Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg

The source review confirms their individual-hearing position. They require a reasonably prompt, impartial opportunity for a permanent resident to show that flight risk and danger do not justify continued physical custody. Congress's criminal category does not for them conclusively establish present need throughout the proceeding. Carlson's individual discretion, Flores's operative release and review avenues, Zadvydas's purpose limit and their joined continuing-deprivation decisions support the distinction between taking custody and keeping it.

Their current ground must preserve this timeline's actual limits. Foucha does not establish a universal governmental clear-and-convincing burden, Hendricks supplies no controlling general due-process framework, and Kennedy's simulated Zadvydas constitutional discussion was not joined by Scalia or Thomas. Their conclusion remains supported without importing those historical premises. They affirm the actual hearing judgment; they do not decide an unpresented proof-allocation dispute, impose a fixed calendar, demand an institutionally separate tribunal or make a finding about the agency's release reasoning. A rebuttable initial criminal-category presumption is permissible, but meaningful individual risk consideration and effective release authority must exist.

### Breyer and the precisely conditioned statutory alternative

The verified historical writing confirms the operative statutory path. Read the independent handoff's shorthand substantial/nondilatory condition in these exact terms: the resident's contention that he does not fall in the mandatory statutory category must raise a question of law or fact that is not insubstantial and must not be interposed solely for purposes of delay. A request for time to obtain evidence, the fact that litigation takes time or a mixed motive does not automatically defeat this condition. A mere plea for discretionary relief after covered removability is accepted is distinct from a question about mandatory category membership.

When both conditions are met, the resident may obtain an individualized flight-risk and danger assessment; release depends on his showing by clear and convincing evidence that he is unlikely to flee or endanger others. The statutory threshold and risk showing are distinct. The section 3143(b) analogy supplies this confined implementation, not direct application of the criminal bail statute. The construction treats unresolved substantial inclusion as the statutory issue; it does not add discretionary release for every person conclusively within the mandatory class. It rejects Joseph's substantially-unlikely-to-succeed threshold insofar as that higher threshold denies bond consideration despite the stated substantial-question condition.

Breyer does not simply move Zadvydas's six-month mechanism into section 1226(c). The present record has disputed inclusion and no final order, so his conditional historical suggestion about conceded deportability does not control his actual proposed disposition. Both charges must be evaluated; Corona-Sanchez does not decide the added burglary and two-crimes grounds. His remedy is vacatur and remand, with current release maintained pending the threshold determination and any consequent bond decision.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg retain their independently frozen conditional willingness to adopt this statutory rule and disposition if it supplies genuine bond consideration when the condition is satisfied and protects current release while the issue is decided. No historical writing affirmatively rejects this particular statutory construction; their absence from Breyer's historical writing is not affirmative agreement with it either. Their positive basis here is their own National Center and Zadvydas joins, the effective-remedy requirements of Calcano and their later Krimstock/Fairley commitments to meaningful interim process, with proof rules kept regime-specific. Their conditional join does not affirm categorical constitutional validity, foreclose the constitutional protection they recognize for residents outside the statutory threshold or supply a universal heightened detainee burden. Assembly must test the actual proposed rationale against those limits, and record any realized conditional adoption explicitly; numerical need alone cannot activate it.

O'Connor and Kennedy still do not join that construction: meaningful adjudication of category inclusion does not for them suspend mandatory custody merely because the challenge is substantial. Scalia and Thomas reject the implied pathway and imported heightened allocation. No conditional agreement on classification is counted as agreement on release eligibility.
