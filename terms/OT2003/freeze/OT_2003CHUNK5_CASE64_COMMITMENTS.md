## Provisional division and governing propositions

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer would reverse the federal-title judgment and remand. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm the reservation's inclusion of these commercially useful deposits. These are eight Associate commitments, not a final Court vote or an authorship assignment.

The five reversal commitments support this confined interpretation: the Pittman Act's reservation of valuable minerals, read in its 1919 legal setting, does not encompass the commonplace sand and gravel presented here merely because contemporary extraction makes those materials commercially useful. Valuable limits the reserved category; a reading that treats every presently saleable mineral substance as reserved does not give that qualification adequate force in this enactment. The owners' prevailing statutory rule concerns ordinary sand and gravel under Pittman patents; it does not automatically decide all sand and gravel of special quality or all federal reservations.

Western Nuclear construed a differently worded reservation of coal and other minerals under the Stock-Raising Homestead Act. It remains binding within that enactment. Amoco requires attention to the particular resource and statutory estate, and permits new valuable uses of a resource already reserved. That principle does not answer which resources this distinct qualified reservation included initially. The reversal ground rests on that category distinction, not a judicial finding of the actual 1919 price, demand or profitability of these Nevada deposits.

The three affirmance commitments reject the claimed categorical exclusion. In their view, ordinary sand and gravel can be valuable mineral resources within this reservation; Western Nuclear's commercially useful mineral category and the protective reservation context are not defeated by adding an adjective that may make explicit an economic quality already implicit in that category. Amoco expressly preserves title to a reserved substance when its economic uses improve. Unlike distinct methane and coal, the same sand and gravel remain at issue; later demand need not represent an expansion into a separately understood resource.

## Adversarial path tests

**Exclude the deposits.** Its strongest basis is the enacted limiting adjective, the 1919 mining-law setting and the description of the disputed materials as commonplace sand and gravel. Its strongest objection is that value may already be inherent in Western Nuclear's mineral definition, so the adjective may not materially narrow the class. The five resolve that interpretive disagreement by giving the Pittman qualification independent work in its own statutory transaction. They do not rest on present price as a retrospective test or treat a particular old market price as found.

**Include the deposits.** Its strongest basis is continuity with Western Nuclear's useful-mineral category, coupled with Amoco's rule that new uses of a reserved substance do not change title. Its strongest objection is that this treats the expressly qualified reservation as equivalent to a broader reservation and makes contemporary saleability do the work of identifying the 1919 estate. The three answer that economic usefulness identifies the mineral class within a protective reservation, and that title to the same material is not enlarged simply because demand later increases. They regard the asserted historical exclusion as unestablished or unpersuasive rather than as a binding premise.

**Resolve by actual 1919 valuation.** The packet supplies no deposit-specific finding supporting this path. No Associate is assigned a factual conclusion about historical Nevada demand, prices, transport costs or profitable extraction. A proposed later opinion depending on that finding requires a corrected sanitized packet and fresh affected modeling; a category-based statutory rule does not require the Court to decide such a fact.

## Remedy, limits and opinion functions

The five would reverse the federal-title judgment because the disputed ordinary materials were conveyed under the Pittman patent and therefore cannot support the asserted reserved-mineral trespass theory. Further proceedings implement that title construction and address any independently surviving matters under their actual predicates. The three would affirm the Government's reserved-title premise for these deposits. Neither disposition establishes a taking, grants a new mining claim or fixes an unadjudicated damages amount.

A signed lead opinion can state the confined Pittman construction if a final majority supports it. The three affirmance commitments support a substantive statutory dissent; Ginsburg's ambiguity-and-grant-canon ground may be explained within it or in a separate writing if the final dissent does not adopt that ground. No author or final opinion topology is assigned. No commitment reaches the validity of Western Nuclear beyond the arguments needed to distinguish or apply it.
