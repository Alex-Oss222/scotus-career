## Components and proposed disposition

Affirm the judgment reversing the invalidation of the 1990 and 1998 ordinances and directing judgment for Denver on the presented constitutional theories. The 1998 prospective challenge remains live, and the 1990 damages theory is live but fails with the supported constitutional defense. Preserve the mootness disposition for prospective relief under the superseded 1990 and 1996 ordinances and declaratory relief under the 1996 ordinance, which has no identified damages claim. Damages are not denied because repeal erased an accrued claim; the live 1990 theory fails on its merits.

The racial component is governed by Adarand's common substantial-justification standard, not historical strict scrutiny. Denver bears the burden to establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Denver may remedy supported local exclusion to which its spending contributes; neither a prior adjudication against every contractor nor individual proof of each beneficiary's injury is universally required. General societal disparity alone is insufficient.

On this record the combined market studies, actual contracting experience, testimony identifying discriminatory firms that also receive city contracts, and developed response to the availability and capacity criticisms establish the relevant local remedial problem. The district court's own accepted findings tie city spending to the exclusion. Denver's response concerning the variable size of contracting operations, hiring, subcontracting and equipment rental addresses the stated size-and-capacity alternative rather than ignoring it. Statistical evidence is evaluated with its limitations and the other evidence; no fixed disparity threshold, mandatory regression or obligation for challengers to commission a counterstudy is imposed. The government retains ultimate persuasion.

The women's component is decided separately under the actual-important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry with its demanding governmental burden. The women-specific utilization, earnings and exclusion evidence establishes the actual remedial justification; proof supporting one race category does not automatically justify the sex classification. The distinct constitutional standards remain operative even though they yield the same disposition here.

The prior fit determination remains law of the case within its preserved scope. Failure to challenge it in the available prior appeal, the absence of a demonstrated material amendment affecting the objection and the absence of a supported exception permit reliance on it. Law of the case limits ordinary reconsideration as a practice, not jurisdiction. Substantially different evidence, contrary intervening controlling law, or clear error producing manifest injustice may justify departure; none is established here. Adarand's adopted standard does not require reopening simply to rename the prior, more demanding fit determination, nor does it turn a genuinely preserved new question into a waived one.

## Enhanced paths and strongest objections

**Affirm under the actual governing standards:** The developed evidence and accepted findings establish a local remedial connection; the preserved fit ruling supplies the issue actually settled. The strongest challenge is that the appellate court employed a challenger-rebuttal vocabulary and the studies did not eliminate every possible nonracial explanation. Neither appellate vocabulary nor absence of a counterstudy carries the constitutional burden. Independent assessment of Denver's affirmative evidence and its actual responses supports the result. Mathematical elimination of all other causes is not the rule, but credible specific criticisms must be answered.

**Vacate for fresh constitutional evaluation:** A remand would be required if the existing record left a material category, alternative or burden unresolved, or if the fit objection had changed materially. The strongest support for this path is the danger of appellate fact substitution and the district court's criticisms of firm availability. The actual record contains its underlying findings and developed capacity evidence, and the proposed judgment makes no new finding. No independently unresolved issue is identified by the preserved objection. A remand solely because the lower court used historical scrutiny terminology would not resolve a live evidentiary gap.

**Restore invalidation:** This path could prevail if actual proof failed under either separate standard, as in Dallas, or if concrete comparably effective alternatives went unanswered, as in Engineering Contractors. Those precedents supply demands, not categorical bans on supported local repair. This record's identified local discrimination, connected spending and settled fit distinguish those failures. The absence of a discrimination judgment against every beneficiary or contractor is not a lawful substitute for the actual inquiry.
