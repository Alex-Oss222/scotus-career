### Issue 1: Best ability and evidentiary support for adverse inferences

* **Controlling Law:** INS v. Ventura requires return of genuinely unresolved agency fact-finding and delegated choices to the agency unless no lawful work remains. The court independently construes §1677e and reviews Commerce's actual factual support under the governing substantial-evidence standard.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Review the statutory construction independently under the applicable delegation framework; review the agency’s factual determination for substantial evidence on the whole record. Missing information and inadequate cooperation are distinct findings.
* **Other Governing Law:** 19 U.S.C. §1677e(a), (b), with the disclosure and deficiency provisions of §1677m; §1516a(b)(1)(B)(i).
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory best-of-ability language requires more than a missing answer but does not expressly require intentional misconduct.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Commerce can fill a genuine information gap; an adverse inference additionally requires supported failure to cooperate to the respondent’s best ability.
* **Open Point:** Whether culpable intent is an indispensable additional element and what ability-based finding suffices.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit rejected a willfulness prerequisite and required both objectively reasonable recordkeeping expectations and a supported failure to make the available effort.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Nippon distinguishes misunderstanding from deliberate noncooperation and argues that available corrective data should prevent an adverse inference.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The United States and domestic producers argue that the statute demands adequate actual effort, not merely absence of bad faith; repeated unsupported unavailability assertions undermine the process.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** None independently established for disposition.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States defends Commerce’s supported best-ability finding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Rejecting an intent requirement can sustain the adverse-inference decision if substantial evidence establishes capability and inadequate effort. Requiring willfulness would compel reconsideration under that added element. Neither path allows punishment for information genuinely unavailable despite adequate cooperation; any recalculation must follow the applicable statutory data rules.
