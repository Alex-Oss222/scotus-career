## Individual-position reconciliation and strongest competing paths

O'Connor and Kennedy apply the Home Builders opinion they joined in full. Their concurrence's territorial boundary is retained: effects occurring in many States, national uniformity and a desirable policy cannot establish that ordinary State authority cannot contain the actual cross-border effect. Holston reinforces material support and demonstrates a valid necessary-inclusion application; its prohibited-image facts do not establish facts about this toad or development.

Ginsburg and Breyer take the same concrete position as their Home Builders partial concurrence: the class premise has independently controlling force, and the record must actually be examined under the two lawful routes. They preserve disagreement with the class ruling without turning that disagreement into a second judgment. Their support for predictive latitude does not permit judicial creation of absent facts; conversely, inadequate lower-court analysis is not proof that no sufficient basis exists. Their recorded remand joins therefore remain full and compatible.

Scalia and Thomas require the most explicit reconciliation with prior public positions. In Home Builders they accepted the enacted class and regime route, opposed the new independent-externality doctrine and preferred confined relief. That original doctrinal objection is not erased by Holston, which decided only a demonstrated supply mechanism. The current difference is legal and procedural: Home Builders now supplies an existing operative two-route inquiry and remedy for the later application, and no adequate complete-record finding establishes failure of every current route. Each chooses to apply that settled inquiry in this later case rather than reopen its original constitutional derivation or infer record exhaustion. Their direct joins authorize actual consideration of both existing routes, not an illusory remand from which the externality route has silently been removed. Their original disagreement does not become a current refusal of the confined rule they affirmatively join. This is a qualitative application of their recorded present commitments, not a mechanical requirement that prior dissenters always follow or always repeat a dissent.

The contrary confined-invalidation path would require a materially different premise: the complete relevant record cannot support an eligible mechanism, or the Court affirmatively changes the governing doctrine. Neither premise is established by the supplied short record or a majority proposal. The lack of a demonstrated species mechanism sufficient for this Court to affirm is thus different from an exhaustive finding that no mechanism can be supported. The remand orders the unperformed inquiry, not indefinite development of imagined new facts or a special discovery entitlement.

Stevens and Souter retain their specific economic-application view. Their argument identifies habitat-changing construction itself as the conduct reached, informed by Sweet Home, Wickard, Perez and Hodel; it does not rely merely on the developer's unrelated purchases or a generalized environmental policy. The strongest objection is that Home Builders directly rejected the same sufficient-ground theory. Their present response openly asks that the contrary class ruling be reconsidered at this economic-application scope. They do not pretend that the old dissent is law, manufacture a factual distinction or claim Holston overruled Home Builders. The substantive dissent should state why actual commercial habitat conversion should qualify and why that ground would leave this judgment effective, while acknowledging the controlling rule it would change. No new ecological or market finding is attributed to them.

These differing choices about applying or renewing disagreement with precedent rest on the actual individual commitments and earlier writings. They are not a reason to create a new full-court Commerce category or force all four earlier dissenters into one position.

## Holston and statutory boundaries

Holston's actual-product reasoning concerns production of the reproducible supply targeted by the identified interstate image prohibition. Rancho's habitat injury does not create that same prohibited stock. The method transfers; the factual relationship does not. An independently supported wildlife regime or substantial interstate externality remains necessary here.

Sweet Home continues to require actual death or injury proximately caused, with ordinary foreseeability, by significant habitat modification impairing essential behavior; speculative harm or bare but-for linkage is insufficient. An omission requires an independent legal duty. The Commerce remand establishes no taking, no future occurrence of injury and no permission for the development. Otherwise lawful land use is no exemption. Any incidental-take permission remains governed by its own statutory findings and conditions; this case adjudicates no entitlement to it. These are preserved legal boundaries, not newly decided statutory applications or a new habitat-maintenance duty.
