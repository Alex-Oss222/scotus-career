## Actual earlier-date law within A

Its operative law concerns statutory assistance under section 1782 for a supported foreign governmental adjudication: interested-person eligibility, contemplated proceedings, rejection of extra discoverability conditions, and application-specific discretion. Duckworth concerns the domestic tribunal Congress requires for an apportionment claim. None of Intel's statutory predicates governs that allocation. Intel does not change Vieth's constitutional elements or the jurisdictional-insubstantiality distinction.

For the June 24 matters, Intel changes neither habeas finality or statutory review nor capital mitigation, sentencing factfinder allocation, collateral error burdens or certification. In Cheney, section 1782's affirmative assistance authorization supplies no FACA enforcement route, appellate mandamus entitlement or requirement for a particular discovery sequence. Its distinction between eligibility and actual production does not answer the separate clear-duty and executive-burden questions. No later A frozen premise materially changes because Intel is now effective.

Duckworth became effective June 23, before the five June 24 A matters. It requires the section 2284 panel for the preserved substantial congressional-apportionment claim and directs application of existing Vieth law. It adds no new partisan-districting rule. Its prohibition against equating ordinary pleading weakness with constitutional insubstantiality governs tribunal allocation; it does not replace the substantial certificate showing in Tennard, the Teague and statutory inquiries in Beard or Schriro, the binding-range authorization in Blakely, or the clear-entitlement and adequate-alternative requirements in Cheney. No later A frozen judgment, rationale, burden, remedy or join requires revision.

The five June 24 A decisions remain uncoordinated same-day events using their common entering baseline. Assignment order does not make one of them substantive entering law for another.
