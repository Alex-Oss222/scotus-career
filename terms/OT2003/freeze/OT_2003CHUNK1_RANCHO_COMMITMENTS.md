## Record and separable decisional questions

The proposed 202-acre housing development in San Diego County threatens the arroyo toad; the species' range is intrastate and the development's commercial character is established. The court below sustained the application by treating the commercial housing project as the regulated activity. Those facts do not themselves establish an interstate wildlife market, cross-border ecological consequences, a mechanism of market evasion or a need for uniform federal species protection.

The questions for the Court are: (1) whether the commercial-project ground can sustain this application consistently with Home Builders's enacted-class holding; (2) whether Home Builders's necessary-inclusion rule, or another eligible alternative mechanism, permits affirmance; and (3) whether the proper consequence of the defective inquiry is a bounded remand or final relief against the challenged application. The record description does not establish exhaustion of the entire relevant legislative and adjudicative record. Its brevity is neither evidence against the Government nor permission to invent the required connection.

The case presents an as-applied commerce challenge. It does not present facial invalidation of all species protection or an adjudicated entitlement to a section 10 permit. Sweet Home's actual injury, proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability requirements remain operative statutory law. A commerce determination cannot itself establish an actual taking, its imminent occurrence or entitlement to incidental-take permission.

## Proposed common affirmance propositions

**1. The lower court used an insufficient ground.** Under Home Builders, section 9's enacted class is the taking of protected wildlife, including the habitat injury the statute reaches. The commercial attributes of the particular housing development do not by themselves establish that class's constitutionally eligible connection to interstate commerce. The lower judgment therefore cannot stand on its commercial-project ground. Neither an as-applied label nor the project's actual commercial purpose removes the governing class inquiry.

**2. Necessary inclusion sustains this application.** Under Home Builders, Congress may include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of listed wildlife may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system, without separate proof that each species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed arroyo toad within that system, qualifies. Rancho does not show that the application is outside the statutory class, that the Act's interstate regulation is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual. Home Builders's independent externality route, with its concrete cross-border effect, substantiality, territorial-control and fit requirements, remains available but need not be satisfied.

The Government identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered and a genuine functional relationship between them; for necessary inclusion that relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis, and the externality route requires a materially adequate basis for each part of its mechanism. Courts independently determine those legal boundaries while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. Neither universal formal findings, a fixed numerical threshold, defendant-specific congressional findings, clear-and-convincing proof nor a least-restrictive-means test is required. Aggregation measures magnitude after the connection is established; it does not create that connection.

**3. This record posture requires affirmance on the necessary-inclusion ground.** The lower court's incorrect commercial-project ground does not require vacatur, because the necessary-inclusion relationship rests on the Act's text and structure and the materials already before the Court, not on a fact about this species that a remand could develop. Affirm the sustaining judgment on that identified ground. It cannot rest on the project's commercial attributes or on national importance alone.

**4. Holston supports the method, not a transferred factual premise.** Holston sustained local production because it created the reproducible supply of the identified prohibited interstate image market. The identity between the produced material and that prohibited supply supported the anti-evasion inference. Nothing in this packet establishes that Rancho's taking creates or retains comparable prohibited interstate stock. The necessary-inclusion ground here rests on the relationship specific to the Act's wildlife regime that Home Builders identified; the statute's breadth and a general conservation objective do not themselves supply it.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

### Economic application versus the enacted class

The strongest economic-application ground is not that Rancho purchases tools or materials in commerce. It is that housing construction and habitat conversion are the conduct directly regulated in this application, making the application an economic class under Wickard, Perez and Hodel. Stevens and Souter retain precisely that position from Home Builders, informed by Sweet Home's identification of habitat modification as the injury-causing conduct. They do not need to invent a market in toads or an ecological effect across a State line.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer answer that the same distinction was presented in Home Builders and rejected by its controlling Part I: the enacted prohibition covers taking without making commercial development its defining condition. A court cannot substitute the economically convenient subset for the class Congress enacted. That answer does not deny the project's commercial character or treat it as an owner's collateral expenditure; it holds that the fact cannot independently supply this statute's constitutional connection. An as-applied challenge remains available, but the asserted mechanism must fit the enacted prohibition and the actual application.

For Stevens and Souter, this is an express renewal of their legal disagreement, not a claim that their old dissent became law or that Home Builders is distinguishable on an invented fact. They would reconsider that class holding insofar as it bars reliance on the injury-producing economic activity and affirm at this limited scope. The principal objection is the force of settled directly applicable precedent. Their response is their source-grounded view that the class rule mistakenly prevents ordinary regulation of actual economic habitat conversion. They do not offer a new empirical premise or claim Holston overruled Home Builders. Their choice remains a minority position unless the necessary Court coalition expressly adopts it; it cannot silently enter the controlling opinion.

### Sustainment on necessary inclusion versus bounded remand

Home Builders's necessary-inclusion ground permits affirmance even though the lower court relied on the wrong rationale. Holston confirms that a concrete source-and-regime relationship may be established without a defendant's individual interstate transaction. This packet supplies no toad-market relationship, ecological spillover or territorial-control fact, and none is needed: the relationship lies in the taking prohibition's function within the Act's valid interstate regulation of listed wildlife, which appears from the statute's operation. Neither a declared national objective nor biodiversity's general value would itself establish it. Accordingly, every Associate commits to affirming on that ground, and none relies on a newly found market or externality mechanism.

The strongest argument for remand is that the lower courts never examined the relationship under Home Builders and that this short packet does not establish the content of the complete relevant record. But the necessary-inclusion relationship does not depend on facts about this species that a remand could develop; it rests on the Act's text and structure, which are before the Court. Remand would leave the constitutional question unsettled without bearing on the relationship that sustains the application. No special discovery entitlement or permission to fabricate evidence is involved.

### Confined invalidation versus affirmance

The developer's intrastate-range argument identifies a real factual feature but cannot establish categorical immunity: Home Builders sustained the same taking prohibition as applied to a listed species found in one State and absent from trade. Conversely, the absence of a commerce element in section 9 is not cured by judicial insertion or by the project's commercial attributes; the connection comes from the Act's integrated scheme. Final relief against this application would require a showing that the application is outside the statutory class, that the Act's interstate regulation is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual. The developer makes none of those showings.

Scalia and Thomas's earlier Home Builders preference for reversal and confined relief does not mechanically dictate the same disposition here. At that decision they joined the enacted-class holding but dissented from the necessary-inclusion holding and opposed the newly announced independent-externality route. In this later case that rule is established law governing the same taking prohibition. Their supported choices are to apply the existing rule or affirmatively reopen it. They choose the former, retaining their underlying objection as a reservation. Their recent unanimous Holston join reinforces that a necessary-regime relationship, once established, must receive effect; their acceptance here rests on Home Builders's governing rule, not on an analogy from Holston's image market.

## Precedent treatment and operative affirmance

- Home Builders is applied directly: the enacted taking class, rejection of commercial-project features as a sufficient substitute, the necessary-inclusion rule and its application to the taking prohibition, the independent externality route, the identification rule, independent constitutional inquiry and respect for reasonable congressional judgments retain their existing force. The proposed controlling application overrules no portion. Stevens and Souter's contrary proposal remains separate.
- Lopez supplies connection before magnitude, fit to the enacted class and the limit on judicially added elements. The Court neither supplies a commerce element for section 9 nor treats the absence of one as automatic invalidity.
- Holston applies the same necessity method to an actually supported image-supply relationship. It is distinguished factually; it establishes no toad supply, wildlife market or ecological spillover.
- Hale and Kirk retain supported necessary inclusion of local conduct within an identified valid regime. Their specific prohibited-stock relationship cannot be presumed for habitat injury.
- Wickard and Perez retain aggregation within properly connected economic classes. Under the governing Home Builders rule, they do not create the missing connection through the housing project's commercial character.
- Sweet Home retains statutory habitat-caused injury, proximate causation, ordinary foreseeability and distinct conditional permissions. It identifies what the taking prohibition reaches but supplies no independent commerce power or permit entitlement in this proceeding.

Affirm the D.C. Circuit judgment sustaining this challenged application on Home Builders's necessary-inclusion ground rather than its insufficient commercial-project rationale. No Commerce Clause evidentiary remand follows. Rancho's request for relief against the application is denied; it receives no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on the Commerce Clause theory.

The statutory take prohibition remains enforceable subject to its actual elements and exceptions. The affirmance does not determine that the proposed development will cause a prohibited taking or grant or deny an incidental-take permit; any independently pending statutory or permitting issue retains its ordinary procedural posture. This Court's disposition concludes its merits review of the Commerce Clause challenge.
