## Supported disposition and scope

Affirm the judgment denying review. The published notices identified the proposed transactions sufficiently; the statutory mailed-notice determination is supported without requiring actual receipt. On this particular record, the expedited opportunity followed by actual consideration of all submitted evidence and objections by an agency retaining authority to alter its orders provided the statutory opportunity for hearing and satisfies NCPA's assumed constitutional entitlement. Trial-type proceedings were unnecessary because no identified material factual dispute required them. FERC's own orders considered creditor effects and the financing/generation reasons; judicial review may accept that supported balancing without inventing an agency rationale. The approvals remain operative.

Section 203's notice and opportunity requirements are independent statutory commands. Mathews does not replace them with a general balancing license. The statutory hearing language does not require oral evidence or cross-examination when the issue is adequately presented and decided on papers. The creditor's constitutional status may be assumed as below because the process is adequate even on that premise. Rehearing is curative here because the agency actually considered the objections and could still alter its decision; reaffirmation alone would not suffice. No universal four-day safe harbor, categorical post-closing cure, or unreviewable discretion follows.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Affirmance path.** The two notices disclosed the transaction types; written notice was mailed to the prescribed state recipients; all later submissions entered the agency's consideration; and §313 left the agency power to change the order. The strongest objection is that closing within hours made public participation too late. For every Justice, that objection requires examining actual retained remedial authority and any lost evidentiary or protective opportunity. The record supports retained modification authority and identifies no irreversible procedural loss; it does not permit the Court to infer one from closing alone. This path sustains the approvals without immunizing short notice in another record.

**Vacatur/remand path.** A genuine statutory failure causing lost meaningful participation, an unaddressed material factual issue, or an agency incapable of effective reconsideration would require correction. The strongest objection to remand here is that it would order the agency to reconsider material it already fully received and addressed without identifying any defect the repeated process would cure. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject such empty repetition under the actual functional and statutory record. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas additionally reject adding a procedural condition beyond §203 without showing the enacted opportunity was denied. No Justice joins the circuit's suggestion that the correctness of the eventual result alone proves procedural adequacy.

The remedy is affirmance, with no new agency proceeding required. The agency's existing statutory reconsideration authority and ordinary review remain governed by their terms. No Associate requires a separate writing if the common explanation includes the stated statutory/constitutional distinction and real-reconsideration limitation.
