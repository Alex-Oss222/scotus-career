## Component D — medical qualification and secular referral delegation

**D1: Common sufficient ground.** The State may require objective medical qualifications and arrangements serving emergency care without thereby transferring its licensing judgment to private persons. It may not make access to protected care depend on an outsider's arbitrary, legally uncontrolled consent. Whether an arrangement is such a veto depends on its operative conditions and effect, not the label private or medical. Yick Wo supplies the prohibition against arbitrary licensing administration; Danforth and the actual Casey decision supply the effective-third-party-control limitation. Professional qualification is not categorically private governmental power.

**D2: Application separately to each medical provision.** All eight would affirm rejection of the medical and secular referral delegation theory on this record. Section 205's availability and on-staff terms identify a clinical service and avoid requiring an outside agreement where the specified physician is on staff. Section 305 additionally permits Department-approved emergency-transfer arrangements. Those provisions have concrete medical functions and alternatives relevant to whether a private actor controls licensing. Section 309 must stand on its own: a privilege qualification relates to the physician's ability to secure appropriate hospital care, but it has no supplied transfer exception. The present record does not establish that this separate command places these providers' continued operation at an identified hospital's arbitrary discretion, or that an impending refusal creates such effective control. The secular section 307 specialties likewise identify needed care and referrals rather than civil authority for consultants to decide whether a clinic may exist.

Existing licensed operations and the absence of a demonstrated refusal support the confined application; they are not a rule that plaintiffs must suffer closure before suing. Reasonably supported impending interference is cognizable. Public-hospital legal constraints do not automatically bind private hospitals. The waiver is not treated as an assured cure. Medical relevance alone also would not justify a proved private veto. The sufficient conclusion is that the challenged requirements, considered distinctly and in their established operation, have not been shown to confer that veto here.

**Strongest contrary path and answer.** A private hospital or consultant can withhold cooperation for reasons unrelated to care, and an unassured waiver may leave the provider powerless. Section 309 is the strongest example because section 305's alternative cannot answer it. This objection would warrant fitted relief upon proof of effective arbitrary control or supported impending denial; it does not establish that every professional cooperation requirement presently has that operation. The disposition rejects neither prospective proof nor a later challenge to such a refusal. It does not adopt the lower court's categorical no-valid-application formula in place of Casey.

**D3: Scope and remedy.** Affirm on this delegation ground only; preserve the separate clergy issue and independent notice and privacy claims. Do not validate every possible enforcement action or decide the previously adjudicated general access claim. If a future or otherwise preserved application proves an effective veto, relief must address that condition and available effective alternatives under the actual Casey rules and severability law, rather than automatically abolishing medical qualifications.

All eight accept D1's ordinary arbitrary-licensing limitation and D2–D3's confined result. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept the complete governing Casey framework; Kennedy accepts its core-choice and effective-control reasoning without affirmatively joining the residual lesser-burden tier. Scalia and Thomas join the ordinary qualification/arbitrary-control ground and result while preserving their prior objection to Casey's broader constitutional framework. Their agreement does not supply eight affirmative joins to that framework.

## Component R — clergy arrangements and religious authority

**R1: Common governing boundary.** All eight accept Commack's actual rule: government may facilitate private religious choice and verify objective facts, but may not decide theological correctness, require denominational conformity or grant clergy compulsory civil control. Agostini supplies the applicable religion framework as used in Commack; no independent historical Lemon-prong test is revived. Helping a patient contact clergy she requests can satisfy this boundary. Requiring institutional affiliations that make licensing turn on religious approval or official denominational adequacy cannot.

**R2: Six commitments to vacatur and bounded remand.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate rejection of the clergy claims and remand to determine the legally operative obligation. The majority's patient-requested list/contact construction is a plausible permissible mechanism, but it is not an authoritative state-high-court construction established by this record. Moore's published deposition recitation describes advance arrangements with several denominations and expresses denominational preferences. That is concrete contrary enforcement evidence. It is neither a transcript-verified concession binding all parties nor proof that every inspector necessarily follows it. The conflict cannot be removed by silently adding patient-request language to the regulation or calling the concern wholly hypothetical.

The remand must resolve whether state law and actual administration confine the duty to requested neutral assistance, or require the provider to secure clergy affiliations and satisfy religious criteria as a licensing condition. Existing material may suffice; no new hearing is mandated. A lawful, enforceably bounded requested-assistance construction permits the provision to stand at that scope. If compulsory religious arrangements, denominational evaluation or clergy control are established, enjoin that operation and dependent licensing sanctions, applying state severability law while preserving secular referral duties and voluntary religious assistance. The same inquiry answers the distinct clergy-delegation theory insofar as licensing actually depends on clerical consent. Ordinary equitable rules govern surviving interim requests.

**Strongest sustaining objection.** As needed naturally confines referrals to patient demand, a directory can implement it, and federal courts ordinarily may adopt a fair saving construction. The six recognize that path but withhold an unconditional affirmance because the operative command is arrangements, the published administering official describes more, and an enforceable state-law limitation has not been established. Their judgment is not immediate facial invalidation or a ruling that mentioning clergy is itself establishment.

**R3: Scalia and Thomas would affirm on a bounded construction.** They treat the lower majority's practical reading of section 307 as the best lawful reading of availability as needed: reasonable assistance to reach requested clergy, without obligatory religious counseling, ideological assent, denominational screening or a clergy veto. Their Commack joins forbid those latter mechanisms. The drafter's testimony deserves consideration but does not amend the enacted rule; on this reading it does not require invalidating the provision or reopening the case. They would expressly limit the affirmance to the requested-assistance construction and leave a proved contrary enforcement practice challenge available. Their barrier to R2 is its premise that the published interpretive dispute presently requires further proceedings; their barrier to any broader affirmance is compulsory religious civil power. Neither would sustain the denominational arrangement described in the testimony as a governmental religious standard.

## Component V — fair notice and enforcement discretion

**V1: Ordinary regulatory terms.** All eight would affirm rejection of the ordinary vagueness challenges. Read in their facility and professional setting, the challenged rules concerning similar names, waiver administration, penalty factors, written policies, trained personnel, health/background verification, contagious conditions, qualifications, safe professional care, appropriate equipment, indicated referrals, quality review, safety, sterile storage, cleanliness and odors, grass/pest control and room requirements have intelligible functions and contextual standards. They do not become void merely because professional judgment and factual evaluation are necessary. Hoffman Estates supplies contextual notice and enforcement analysis; Grayned distinguishes practical standards from standardlessness. This agreement does not rest on a presumption that the regulations carry only civil consequences.

**V2: Section 305(A).** All eight would reject the separate staff/consulting-language vagueness claim. Physicians is the relevant professional class, whether staff, consulting or both. Reading the full sentence does not plausibly require receptionists or every nonphysician employee to obtain hospital privileges. The enacted alternative is documented Department-approved emergency transfer when hospitalization is necessary. That resolves the claimed uncertainty in this sentence without erasing a duty or importing its alternative into section 309. Neither the ordinary terms nor section 305 fails under the stricter clarity concern asserted by the challengers; no criminal-linkage finding is needed for these conclusions.

**V3: Six commitments against the independent best-practices sanction mechanism.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the rejection of this discrete challenge and remand for a fitted decree. Chapter 10 and the independent best-practices branch of section 103(C) permit the Department to identify an otherwise unannounced obligation and classify its violation. No objective source or substantive criterion identifying which additional practice is compulsory has been established. The professional context gives many specific provisions meaning but does not itself define this residual Department-selected obligation. Section 103(C)'s operative classification prevents treating Chapter 10 simply as authority to adopt future regulations.

The actual Morales holding supplies the decisive distinction: meaningful enforcement guidance must govern the condition authorizing a command; an individual warning does not substitute for that legal criterion. Kolender and Grayned support that independent rule. This branch's common defect is the absence of a definite additional obligation, not uncertainty at the margins of an otherwise intelligible duty. The holding reaches sanctions based solely on that unbounded best-practices mechanism, not the other residual classification of breaches of definite regulatory duties. The State remains able to adopt prospectively adequate standards through lawful means.

**Strongest contrary path and answer.** Medical best practice has professional content; the first Class III response is a warning and section 103(E) considers good faith, compliance history and circumstances. Those protections can moderate application of an intelligible rule. They do not select the compulsory standard or constrain whose practices the Department may reject. The majority's first-offense account also does not prove immunity from later fines or criminal exposure. The six find the mechanism defective even assuming only civil licensing consequences, given the undefined operative duty and protected-care setting. They therefore neither resolve nor rely on the disputed incorporation of section 44-7-340.

**V3 remedy.** Restrain independent sanctions and adverse licensing consequences based solely on the undefined Department-interpreted best-practices branch until an adequate legally operative standard exists. Preserve Chapter 10 to the extent it supports lawful prospective standard setting, definite duties elsewhere, and section 103(C)'s classification of definite violations not classified I or II. The lower court must conform the decree to state severability law. This is no universal injunction against professional standards, no rewriting of section 305 or 309, and no reopening of all access regulation.

**V4: Scalia and Thomas would affirm the best-practices disposition.** Their Morales separate position distinguishes missing legal content from a rule with an intelligible contextual core. Here professional clinical practices and the regulation's health-care functions supply such a core; a later contested choice between defensible practices may require an as-applied notice or arbitrary-enforcement challenge. They would read Department interpretation as application of professionally recognizable practices, not unlimited authority to invent new crimes or retroactive obligations. Hoffman Estates supports that bounded approach. Practical clarification, warnings and mitigating factors supplement the core; they do not cure every undefined law.

Their strongest objection to V3 is that it converts disagreement about how professional standards apply into absence of any standard. Their limiting barrier is clear: affirmance does not authorize an unforeseeable sanction simply because an inspector preferred a different practice. This conclusion holds even if the challengers' asserted criminal-enforcement account is assumed for this analysis; no finding that criminal sanctions are absent is necessary. The precise section 44-7-340 linkage remains undecided, not silently resolved.

## Component P — identified records, authorized access and safeguards

**P1: Six accept a sufficient-connection rule, not universal indispensability.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply Lawall's requirement to determine the actual information, recipients, official function and safeguards when government authorizes an additional transfer of intimate clinical information. Private-clinic records becoming available to inspectors require that inquiry even though the recipient works in health administration. The rule protects against unjustified access and redistribution; it does not forbid routine lawful inspection or presume that all identified review is improper.

Whalen permits genuine safeguarded health administration. Doe's actual assumed-right holding permits the confined benefits use there without requiring every datum to be indispensable; it does not establish unrestricted inspection authority or make public disclosure essential to every claim. Lawall expressly distinguishes necessary handling from additional legally permitted access. Herring adds recipient-specific limits on outside dissemination, not a categorical bar on confidential health inspectors. These authorities leave legitimate identified clinical audits, investigation of complaints, corroboration, continuity and other sufficiently connected functions available.

**P2: Application and strongest sustaining alternative.** South Carolina has a substantial, concrete interest in verifying patient-care compliance; complete records and confidential inspector access can serve it. Section 102(F)(2)'s copying qualifier and the retention, training and privacy rules supply real limits. Those facts defeat a rule that every identifier requires a compelling-interest finding or that name redaction must always substitute for inspection. They do not establish that the full authorized audience, inspection uses, copying and later disclosures are adequately confined in every material respect.

The district court's general compelling-need formulation is too categorical; the Fourth Circuit's reliance on confidentiality and administrative purpose is insufficiently attentive to the actual permissions. Sections 44-7-310 and 315 must be read with their distinct exceptions and competing constructions. Section 44-41-60 governs registrar reports, and section 44-41-360 supplies a constitutional anonymity decision, not absolute confidentiality. A court cannot combine these different rules into an absolute bar that none enacts. Name-only redaction is likewise not necessarily effective deidentification given section 401's other fields.

The reported flyer reproducing a record of a fifteen-year-old patient's termination, reportedly obtained from DHEC, and physicians' accounts of similar releases are material evidence. They defeat a characterization of the entire concern as a purely speculative leak. The recitation does not establish every identifying detail, every release's legal authorization, the State's actual safeguards or liability for all copies. The six would require its actual significance to be assessed rather than treating it as an agreed constitutional violation or erasing it.

**P3: Judgment and remedy.** The six would vacate the reversal of the district court on this component and remand for determination of the actual access, copying, use and redistribution authority, its connection to patient-care functions, and effective safeguards. Neither blanket approval of identified access nor the district court's categorical compelling-need restriction survives as the final merits resolution. Existing material may answer the questions without a new hearing. The lower court must prevent established improper access or dissemination while preserving lawful confidential inspection and necessary clinical records, and decide surviving interim requests under ordinary equity. Any restriction must identify the defective permission or practice and follow applicable severability law.

Redacted review, consent and targeted identified follow-up are concrete alternatives whose adequacy must be assessed where they can serve the actual inspection objective. They are not rigid prerequisites to every inspection, a warrant system, universal judicial preclearance or a least-intrusive-imaginable-method rule. Sufficiently connected identified review remains permissible even where one datum is not independently indispensable. Confidentiality need not promise perfect containment, but penalties for unauthorized leaks do not justify unnecessary lawful access.

**P4: Separate access inquiry and join limit.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also accept the complete actual Casey framework if the proved inspection obligation causes additional interference with protected choice: identify the actual duty, protected baseline, affected population, causation and interference; apply the corresponding ordered protection and fit inquiry; preserve necessary timely care, clinical judgment, communication and fitted relief. Effective veto, prohibition, obstruction and other severe or meaningful interference retain their actual distinct consequences. No burden is inferred merely from the word abortion, asserted fear or the presence of identifiers, and no finding that every physician or patient is deterred is made. Kennedy joins P1–P3 on independent privacy and actual-permission grounds, not P4's residual lesser-burden rationale. None of these commitments reopens the earlier overall access or equal-protection adjudication.

**P5: Scalia and Thomas would affirm rejection of the identified-access claim.** They preserve their Doe concurrence's source objection and Lawall separate position against the broader substantive informational-privacy extension. They accept legitimate confidential inspection and reject general government immunity for unrelated releases. Independently assuming the asserted right, the direct clinical-regulation function, copying limitation and stated confidentiality controls provide a sufficient facial connection on this record under Doe's bounded method. Unnecessary dissemination, the reported releases and an actual overbroad request can support an appropriately grounded particular challenge; they do not establish the invalidity of general inspection authority here.

Their strongest opposing consideration is Lawall's controlling treatment of an authorized outside transfer, reinforced here by concrete release evidence. Their distinction is that this inspection clause itself identifies a direct health-care regulatory task, whereas the independent outside authorizations in Lawall had not been identified; they do not claim that confidentiality alone answers every legal permission. They refuse P1's extension as their affirmative rationale and P3's further proceedings because the facial challenge does not establish improper operation of the whole inspection permission. They would not restore the district court's universal compelling-interest requirement. No general waiver, absolute statutory secrecy, absence of leak evidence or insistence on completed public disclosure is part of their position.

## Join barriers, complete resolution and remedial coordination

There are common Associate judgments on medical/secular delegation and the ordinary notice issues. The remaining components divide six to two among the Associates as recorded; these are not final Court counts. No author or assignment is selected. No hypothetical later revision or persuasion is asserted.
