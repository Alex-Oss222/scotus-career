## Components and provisional disposition

All eight conclude that the officers deliberately elicited statements about the indicted conspiracy at the home without counsel or valid waiver. The Sixth Amendment inquiry is not limited to Miranda's custodial-interrogation standard. The directed announcement of a desire to discuss the drug distribution and named associates invokes the protection of Massiah and Brewer; indictment has attached the right. Invitation to enter is not an informed relinquishment of that right. Cobb's broader protected-episode rule is controlling but unnecessary to resolve questioning about the very charged offense. No Jackson assertion is newly found.

Six Associates—Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—would vacate the judgment affirming admission of the jail statements and remand for the defined independent-causation inquiry. Scalia and Thomas would affirm the conviction judgment on the later knowing and voluntary waiver, while rejecting the lower court's initial no-violation rationale.

## Competing rules and supported objections

**Causal-independence rule.** Once deliberate elicitation in violation of the attached right and its causal relationship to a later statement are shown, the Government must establish that the later statement was obtained by a genuinely independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of the violation. A valid later warning and waiver are important but do not alone resolve that question. The court examines the actual sequence, temporal separation, genuine intervening circumstances and the purpose and character of the unlawful elicitation, giving the warning and waiver their full significance. This is Brown's separation of voluntariness and causal exclusion applied to the completed Sixth Amendment violation, supported by Wade's distinct treatment of derived evidence. It does not impose a categorical bar to later questioning or a new requirement that counsel personally approve every later interview.

The strongest counterargument is that Patterson makes a knowing warning and waiver adequate for post-indictment questioning and Elstad rejects a permanent psychological disability caused by a voluntary earlier admission. The response is that Patterson governs authority to conduct the later interview, while the challenged use may still exploit an already completed constitutional violation. Elstad resolved an initially unwarned voluntary statement; Dickerson preserved its actual scope rather than extending it to all violations. Kaupp unanimously preserves causal exclusion as distinct from later warnings. Same officers and repeated admissions support inquiry here, but do not replace an evidentiary determination of the entire sequence.

**Waiver-centered extension.** A later statement made after adequate warnings and a knowing, voluntary waiver may be admitted notwithstanding an earlier voluntary statement deliberately elicited in violation of the attached right, unless the earlier conduct undermined the later statement's voluntary and informed character. This extends Elstad's treatment of successive voluntary statements to the attached-right setting and gives Patterson's valid-waiver rule decisive effect. It does not excuse the original elicitation or authorize use of the home admissions themselves.

The strongest objection is that Massiah protects a right independent of compulsion and Wade recognizes derivative consequences. The extension's proponents distinguish derived identification or physical evidence from a newly authorized personal decision to speak, and distinguish Brown and Kaupp's unlawful custody from this arrest pursuant to an indictment and warrant. They accept a genuine coercion or invalid-waiver objection but reject an additional attenuation requirement where the later interview is authorized and the statements are voluntary. This is a new legal conclusion, not an invented finding of prior counsel consultation.

## Remedy, limits and reconciliation instructions

For the six-Justice path, remand determines the later statements' admissibility under the causal-independence rule. If the Government fails that showing, exclude the derivative statements and decide any properly preserved harmlessness argument under Chapman: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the actual trial record that admission did not contribute to the verdict. Unless harmless, set aside the affected conviction for lawful further proceedings. The Court makes no acquittal, release, compulsory new evidentiary hearing, prior-assertion, actual-coercion or final harmlessness finding.

Scalia and Thomas join the initial deliberate-elicitation proposition but refuse the causal-independence rule and its vacatur. Their proposed affirmance does not make the Eighth Circuit's erroneous Fifth-Amendment-only analysis a correct statement of the Sixth Amendment.
