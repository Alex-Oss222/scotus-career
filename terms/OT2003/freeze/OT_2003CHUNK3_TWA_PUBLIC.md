## Event

**In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.** Decided December 15, 2003, on merits submission. Review of the Third Circuit's judgment, 322 F.3d 283, affirming the bankruptcy sale order through the district court. The question is whether 11 U.S.C. §363(f) permits the sale free of the identified employment-discrimination and travel-voucher successor liabilities although they are not liens or strictly in-rem rights.

American purchased substantially all TWA's assets for $742 million. The EEOC and the represented maternity-discrimination settlement class objected to an order barring successor enforcement against American. The Court assumes otherwise viable nonbankruptcy successor claims and decides the sale's effect upon them.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed as to the represented successor claims. The ancillary limited-stay appeal is dismissed as moot.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: 9–0 on both components. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The free-and-clear transfer and injunction remain effective against the identified EEOC and voucher-class successor claims within the process and statutory limits stated below. Claims against the TWA estate remain subject to allowance, valuation, priority and distribution under the Bankruptcy Code. The final merits disposition makes the ancillary appeal from refusal of a limited stay moot.

## Holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** Thomas's opinion for a unanimous Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**1. The specific successor interests satisfy the sale statute.** Section 363(f)(5) permits a sale free of a successor obligation arising through acquisition and continuation of the debtor's transferred operating enterprise when an applicable legal or equitable proceeding can compel satisfaction of that interest in money. The sale proponent bears the ordinary civil burden of establishing both the property connection and this independent monetary-satisfaction condition; the represented pre-sale discrimination and voucher obligations satisfy them here.

The Court holds that the statutory interest reaches these transfer-dependent successor obligations. Section 363(f)(3) separately identifies a lien as one kind of interest, so a liens-only construction would give the broader term too little work. Yet a debtor's ownership of assets does not turn every personal debt into an interest in each asset. Here the asserted obligation of American arises through its acquisition and continuation of the enterprise.

Patterson v. Shumate requires application of the Code's operative words, and Hartford Underwriters requires compliance with its particular grants of authority. In re Dow Corning rejects independent nondebtor releases based on general plan powers; this sale therefore depends on §363(f)'s distinct conditions. Those conditions are met because these obligations could be compelled into monetary claims in liquidation. The vouchers' promised noncash benefit can receive that legally available monetary substitute. An estimate of value alone would not establish the required legal capacity for satisfaction. The favorable purchase price and the Code's priorities likewise cannot replace a statutory predicate.

**2. The sale's protection follows its statutory and process boundaries.** The sale order binds the represented claimant groups at the adjudicated scope and preserves their estate claims under the Code. Its protection extends to the transfer-dependent pre-sale successor obligations; independent purchaser wrongdoing, future compliance duties, and claims of persons without legally sufficient notice and opportunity to be heard fall outside that adjudicated protection.

Both claimant groups participated in the sale hearing. That process supports the treatment of the claims before the Court, rather than a finding about unidentified persons. Section 363(e) also retains its separate command: on an affected interest holder's request, the bankruptcy court must prohibit or condition a sale as necessary to provide adequate protection. The sale conditions, claimant process and requested adequate protection each perform their own statutory work. Preserving estate claims respects the Code's distribution system but supplies no independent power to release American.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Patterson v. Shumate:** Applied for the Code's operative-text method; the particular property-interest and monetary-satisfaction conditions govern this sale.
- **Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank:** Applied for fidelity to the Code's specific allocation of authority; general equitable usefulness does not supply a missing statutory entitlement.
- **In re Dow Corning Corp.:** Distinguished because §363(f)(5) supplies a specific transferred-property mechanism here. Its prohibition of independent nondebtor releases under general plan powers remains effective.

## Law After Decision

Section 363(f)(5) reaches the identified transfer-dependent pre-sale successor interests when the proponent proves the property connection, a legally available compulsory monetary satisfaction, and the required claimant process. The rule applies across comparable enterprise sales at those predicates. Adequate protection on request, independent purchaser liability, claimant notice and the Code's estate-distribution rules retain their distinct force.

## Procedure After Action
