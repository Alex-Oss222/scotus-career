[B preflight](OT_2003CHUNK3_B_PREFLIGHT.md) preserves the neutral packet and primary-opinion supplement; [entering law](../entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK3_B.md) includes the actual election holdings and December 4 Van Susteren decision. ## Components and alternative paths

**Speech and municipal equal protection.** Affirm the dismissal of the challenge to the neutral generic-status designation rule. The candidate remains on the ballot, may use a qualifying office or principal-occupation description, and receives a separate 200-word statement distributed at city expense. The actual burden is ordinary, rather than substantial or severe: the rule regulates the form of optional official identification and does not deny a genuine nomination, candidacy or voting choice. The complete Burdick–Cartwright scale remains controlling; merely saying the burden is less than severe would not identify the applicable level.

The rule's relation to concise, intelligible and administrable identification supplies the important regulatory justification. Activist is a broad status applicable to many different activities, and the bar covers opposing causes alike. Accepting that activism is how Rubin earns his livelihood or spends his time does not require the State to print his preferred generic category in a field reserved for more specific office or occupational identification. The alternative statement reduces the actual burden but could not cure viewpoint discrimination. Permission for an office or occupational identification has a functional relation to the field; no suspect classification or additional unequal denial of the franchise is supplied. The same actual-burden analysis and reasonable category distinction defeat the municipal equal-protection theory.

**Strong contrary path.** Vacate and remand because the City accepts other descriptive labels while refusing a truthful account of this candidate's vocation. Schrader protects meaningful truthful identification even where other campaign channels remain. The strongest argument for Rubin is that a paid activist can have an actual occupation, and the State may not make constitutional review tautological by defining his description out of its rule. This objection requires genuine scrutiny of the classification. It does not establish that every truthful self-description must appear in every optional field. The generic-status exclusion is applied independent of cause, does not contradict his party nomination, and leaves a more specific description of his actual work available under the ordinary category rule. The official party-nomination feature protected in Timmons and Schrader is absent. No finding that Rubin's pleaded livelihood facts are false is used.

**Secretary-of-State component, conditional on accepted scope.** The published lower judgment separately dismisses the Secretary because California's rule is expressly inapplicable to municipal elections, Santa Monica adopted it independently, and the Secretary neither compelled nor encouraged that action. If within review, affirm for want of traceability and redressability against that defendant. Enjoining his conduct in state elections would not control the City's independent municipal choice. A general voter interest in reading any three words a candidate selects supplies no separate concrete injury against him. This conditional scope treatment does not add a grant or expand the supplied constitutional question; assembly must verify whether the component is presented. The City remains the proper target of the concrete designation dispute.

The short election interval and intended recurrent candidacy support the live prospective controversy. Relief concerns future administration; the completed election remains closed. No independent facial challenge to every office designation or occupational distinction is decided.

## Precedent and remedy

Burdick supplies burden, precise interests and fit; Cartwright requires identification of the ordinary level rather than a two-category severe/not-severe shortcut. Timmons and Schrader preserve the special weight of a genuine nomination and truthful party affiliation, which this optional generic description does not establish. Van Susteren confirms that important regulatory interests can justify an actual ordinary burden without deciding this different field. Cook's congressional Elections Clause rule does not itself govern a municipal election; its distinction between neutral form and ideological ballot penalties reinforces the need to examine actual neutrality.

Affirmance sustains denial of the requested declaratory and prospective relief concerning this status designation. The city may apply the neutral rule within that scope. If the Secretary component is reviewed, its dismissal stands on Article III grounds distinct from the City merits. No lower historical characterization of simulated Timmons, Burdick or Schrader is adopted.
