## Disposition and common sufficient ground

Reverse the Fifth Circuit's categorical immunity ruling and remand for enforcement proceedings concerning the federal consent decree, including any properly supported request to modify it. A valid decree resolving a federal dispute against officials may prescribe implementation obligations more detailed than the federal statute and remain enforceable prospectively. Each alleged decree violation need not independently state a new statutory violation. The federal controversy, the lawful decree and its continuing federal objectives supply the enforcement authority; consent is not a universal waiver of the State's immunity.

Young and the unanimous prospective-officer portion of Verizon Maryland permit the proper officer proceeding. Firefighters permits consent relief within the federal dispute beyond the minimum relief otherwise awarded after trial. Rufo confirms that an operative decree remains a judicial obligation and provides the mechanism for adjusting prospective duties. Pennhurst continues to bar an independent state-law command; Edelman continues to distinguish retrospective state compensation. Neither bars the supplied federal implementation decree and prospective correction.

Officials may seek Rule 60(b)(5) relief by showing a significant change in facts or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable, and a modification must be suitably tailored to that change. An effective durable remedy and satisfaction of the decree's legitimate purposes support ending unnecessary judicial supervision; a new administration, dissatisfaction or a cheaper lawful alternative alone does not dissolve accepted terms. The Medicaid decree is not governed by the PLRA provisions for prison-conditions litigation.

## Alternative-path test

The strongest contrary path treats Young's ongoing-federal-violation requirement as demanding that every enforced detail itself repeat an enforceable statutory command. That would preserve state officials' discretion wherever the statute does not prescribe the precise method. It fails because the decree lawfully settles the federal dispute and converts the accepted implementation terms into a judicial obligation; otherwise the implementation terms Firefighters and Rufo recognize could not be enforced. State flexibility is protected by the decree's federal limits and lawful modification, not unilateral disregard.

The opposite overbroad path would enforce anything a state official signed. It fails because consent cannot supply unrelated federal jurisdiction, enforce independent state law through Pennhurst, or convert prospective authority into retrospective state damages. The supplied decree's federal provenance and purposes avoid those defects. The Court need not decide general waiver or congressional abrogation to resolve this enforcement question; both are alternative theories unnecessary after a sufficient officer-relief ground, not remaining parts of the question answered.
