## Enhanced paths and material objections

The categorical path applies the existing passenger-compartment incident-search rule to a genuine recent occupant without making the officer's first contact inside the car essential. Such a contact line bears no reliable relation to the arrest risks and permits a fortuitous exit to control authority. The rule's standardized scope also survives the ordinary securing of the arrestee during the contemporaneous arrest process. Its strongest objection is that an inaccessible car supplies neither an actual weapon threat nor an evidence-destruction threat. The categorical answer is that the existing exception determines the reasonable scope of the arrest search without relitigating danger in each qualifying arrest; securing the person should not require officers to choose between safe arrest practices and the authorized contemporaneous search. Scope remains the passenger compartment and containers within it, not the trunk or a remote later search.

The limiting path rejects a first-contact distinction as a complete constitutional solution and instead ties the area search to Chimel's actual immediate-control justification. Once Thornton was handcuffed and confined in the patrol car, he could not reach the vehicle's weapons or destroy its evidence. Convenience cannot sustain that added intrusion. This would change Belton's categorical operation at the secured-arrestee boundary while preserving Robinson's personal-arrest search and the distinct automobile, protective, consent and inventory exceptions. Its strongest contrary point is practical administrability and reliance on Belton. The proposed boundary uses the actual secured position rather than speculative hypothetical escape; ordinary independent exceptions remain available when their predicates exist.

The packet permits considering a separately supported evidence-of-the-arrest-offense theory as an analytical possibility, but does not establish that a particular new version of that theory was presented and developed. This freeze does not invent a new reduced-suspicion vehicle-search exception, or treat drugs found on the person as automatically establishing automobile probable cause. No commitment relies on such a new ground. The existing categorical ground and the supported immediate-control objection suffice to state the present positions.
