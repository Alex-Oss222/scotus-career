* **Question Presented:** How a Chapter 13 plan must set the interest rate on deferred payments that preserve the present value of a secured creditor’s allowed claim over its objection.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, directing use of a presumptive contract-rate approach after rejecting the bankruptcy court’s prime-plus formula.
* **Relief Requested:** The Tills seek restoration of the formula approach; SCS seeks a rate reflecting comparable coerced lending.
* **Material Record:** The truck secured a claim valued at $4,000. The contract charged 21 percent; the plan proposed 9.5 percent using an 8 percent prime rate plus 1.5 points. Plan confirmation, valuation and credit-risk compensation perform different statutory functions.

### Issue 1: Present-value interest under Chapter 13

* **Controlling Law:** Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash supplies replacement valuation when the debtor retains collateral, with appropriate adjustments. Nobelman v. American Savings Bank concerns the distinct protection for home-mortgage rights. Neither fixes this interest methodology.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First identify the allowed secured claim; then determine a rate sufficient to preserve that value over the payment period without adding unrelated lending costs or profit.
* **Other Governing Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§1325(a)(5)(B)(ii), 1325(a)(6) and 506 as then in force.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory value-as-of-effective-date requirement and feasibility condition must both be satisfied; a rate cannot make an infeasible plan feasible by label.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law distinguishes claim valuation from the payment stream’s discounted value.
* **Open Point:** Whether the governing rate should start from prime and adjust for proved risk, a replacement-loan market, or the original contract.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Seventh Circuit treated the contract as a presumptive measure of the creditor’s coerced loan, subject to rebuttal.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The debtors argue that prime plus demonstrated risk provides an objective starting point without charging them for marketing and profit unrelated to statutory present value.
* **Respondent or appellee:** SCS argues that a forced loan must compensate actual default risk and that the original or market rate better measures its lost economic value.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** An identified efficient market may provide useful evidence, but the inquiry must distinguish market transaction costs from risk and value.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** A formula rule reverses the contract presumption and remands any unresolved risk findings. A replacement-loan or presumptive-contract rule requires reliable evidence of comparable lending and statutory relevance. No method permits automatic confirmation without a supported rate and feasible plan.
