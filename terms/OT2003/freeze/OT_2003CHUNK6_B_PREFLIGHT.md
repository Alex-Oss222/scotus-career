## 79. Tennessee Student Assistance Corporation v. Hood

Event: May 17, 2004. Slice: `entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK6_CASE79.md`.

The supplied posture is an immunity objection to adjudication of student-loan dischargeability, not a finding of undue hardship or a demand for affirmative treasury recovery. This rule cannot be replaced with a categorical historical Article I incapacity. The slice includes Federal Maritime Commission's public dissent as relevant prior continuity: its four dissenters there retained their structural objection while applying the existing rule and requiring a particular authorization inquiry. Their positions in this case still require independent modeling; neither adherence nor reconsideration is mechanically prescribed.

The official 2003 section 106 text expressly includes sections 523 and 524 in subsection (a)(1); subsection (a)(2) allows determination of application questions, and subsection (a)(3) addresses process and judgments. Its separate remedy limitations remain: no punitive damages, statutory fee limitations, appropriate nonbankruptcy enforcement law, and no newly created substantive cause of action. Subsection (b)'s proof-of-claim waiver and subsection (c)'s estate-claim offset are distinct mechanisms. The case does not establish their predicates by treating the State's objection as consent.

The 1994 amendment's historical predicate remains intact. The canonical Nordic Village holding denies unequivocal federal consent under original section 106(c) to an affirmative personal money judgment and rejects a property label unsupported by an identified res. It leaves genuine property proceedings and later enacted waiver language unresolved. The retained ruling therefore preserves the relevant predicate for Congress's more explicit 1994 text; no displaced-predicate enactment assumption or additional user choice is needed. Control independently confirmed this point.

The strongest objection to the first separates valid federal regulation from coercive private process; under current law it must be addressed as an application distinction or a candid request to reconsider precedent. The strongest objection to the second is that affirmative adversary process seeks a debtor-specific exception to nondischargeability and may exceed ordinary estate administration. The absence of treasury recovery matters but does not substitute for the full legal analysis.

Any rejection of immunity permits an undue-hardship adjudication under the actual education-debt exception. It awards no discharge on this record. The copied section 523(a)(8) preserves the types of covered education obligations and hardship for the debtor and dependents; no post-2005 expansion is used. The Kontrick projection supplies only the distinction between statutory jurisdiction and the particular procedural deadlines decided there. Event: May 17, 2004. Slice: `entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK6_CASE80.md`.

The supplied figures are a $4,000 allowed secured claim, a 21 percent contract rate, and a proposed 9.5 percent rate formed from 8 percent prime plus 1.5 percentage points. They identify the dispute; they do not independently establish an adequate risk adjustment, a market rate, or feasibility.

Rash establishes adjusted replacement valuation when collateral is retained; it expressly fixes no interest rate. Nobelman concerns protected principal-residence mortgage rights and does not impose those protections on this vehicle claim. Lamie's current-term text analysis and published separate position may inform interpretive method, but establish no present-value formula.

Neutral framing qualification: the packet's reference to preserving value without unrelated lending costs or profit is a proposed construction to be tested, not an already controlling interest methodology. Rash states that dealer profit and overhead are not mechanically deducted from a supported comparable-market collateral price. That valuation holding neither requires nor forbids a comparable interest component. The model must distinguish collateral-price measurement from payment-stream discounting and must not treat a desired rate result as supplied by entering law.

Qualitative alternatives include a prime-based formula adjusted on the demonstrated record; a comparable-loan or rebuttable-contract approach justified as a measure of deferred-payment value; and a narrower text argument distinguishing compensation for delay from the risk of nonpayment of promised property. Neither selecting a starting point nor invoking feasibility supplies empirical risk findings.
