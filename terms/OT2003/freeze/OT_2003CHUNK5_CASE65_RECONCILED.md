## Reconciled commitments by component

All eight Associates retain the election judgment: affirm the injunction permitting otherwise qualified non-Hawaiians to seek elected OHA trusteeships and serve if elected. All eight retain the appointments judgment: affirm vacatur of the appointments ruling and dismissal of that component for want of an actual or imminent appointment-related injury. No election injury substitutes for injury on that separate claim.

The shared election ground is equal protection at this public-office scope. Hawaii must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its complete ancestry exclusion and that objective, accounting for actual coverage and burdens and supported practicable alternatives. Faithful administration of programs for Hawaiian beneficiaries is a legitimate serious objective. It does not itself establish that ancestry is necessary to competent and faithful trusteeship, or justify excluding all otherwise qualified candidates without that ancestry. Enforceable fiduciary duties can bind every trustee. The government, rather than the excluded candidate, retains the burden to justify the actual exclusion. No presumption against all beneficiary programs or requirement that the plaintiffs disprove Hawaiian interests is added.

## Alternative grounds and remedy

The strongest defense distinguishes representative qualifications from the broader electorate: beneficiaries may need trustees connected to them even when all voters participate. That distinction explains why Rice did not decide this question, but it does not itself meet the government's burden for this complete ancestry exclusion in a state office. The frozen record supplies an otherwise qualified candidate denied nomination papers and no additional disputed qualification requiring a new trial. None of the eight commits to a program-wide redesign or a new evidentiary remand.

The Fifteenth Amendment and section 2 theories remain alternative grounds presented below. The preserved equal-protection ground fully sustains the elected-office relief; the reconciled commitments do not adopt an independent statutory candidacy rule or a universal Fifteenth Amendment extension. Assembly must not treat judgment agreement as joins on those alternatives. The appointment component is jurisdictional and supplies no appointment-merits ruling.

The injunction retains its election-related operation. Neutral qualifications and fiduciary responsibilities remain enforceable in their own terms. The appointment component is dismissed for lack of standing. No new restriction on Hawaiian benefit eligibility, tribal membership or genuine sovereign elections follows.
