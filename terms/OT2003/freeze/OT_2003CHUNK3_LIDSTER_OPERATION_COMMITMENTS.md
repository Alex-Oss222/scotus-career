## Proposed operative rule and actual application

A checkpoint directed to obtaining witnesses' information about a specific completed crime is not automatically invalid under Edmond merely because its ultimate objective concerns criminal enforcement. Its reasonableness depends on the actual public need, the concrete relationship of place, time and questioning to likely witnesses, the full interference with motorists' liberty, and the limits on officer discretion. A general information-gathering label supplies no authority to screen motorists for their own offenses or to stop traffic for an unconnected public inquiry.

This fatal-hit-and-run inquiry satisfies that rule. A recent death supplies a grave specific need. Returning one week later to the same location and approximate time reasonably reaches persons with recurring travel patterns who may have observed the incident. That connection is an ex ante practical inference, not a finding that every driver was a witness or a numerical success quota. The same short question and flyer exchange, systematic treatment of arriving traffic and visible checkpoint limit arbitrary selection and the scope of the encounter. The restraint includes a queue of up to fifteen vehicles per lane and a total wait of a very few minutes at most. That compulsory delay and possible nighttime apprehension are real intrusions, but remain limited in this specific operation. No suspicion-directed inspection or demand for incriminating information is part of the accepted checkpoint purpose.

The State establishes the checkpoint's antecedent authority through those facts. Lidster's subsequent near-collision and signs of intoxication then permit the separately justified investigation. Reverse the Illinois Supreme Court's suppression judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with the checkpoint's constitutional validity; the checkpoint supplies no ground to exclude the resulting evidence. The remand implements this legal conclusion and preserves any genuinely independent preserved question without inventing one.

## Alternative paths and objections

**Categorical invalidity:** Edmond's concern that a secondary lawful label could swallow the individualized-suspicion rule is substantial. It fails to dispose of this record because the police were not indiscriminately seeking evidence of wrongdoing by those stopped. Their actual narrow witness inquiry has the specific place-and-time relationship stated above. The information label alone would be insufficient in a different operation; the complete functional inquiry does the limiting work here.

**Purpose-only vacatur and factual remand:** A remand would be proper if the State's witness purpose, actual selection method, total delay or relationship to likely witnesses remained materially disputed or unsupported. The accepted record now supplies those operational facts, including the queue. The lower court's use of the wrong categorical rule does not itself require a new hearing where the supported facts permit application of the correct constitutional standard. No unadjudicated material timing conflict is invented. Chandler prevents replacing the contextual inquiry with a demand for a statistical efficacy study or proof that signs, media appeals or voluntary questioning would fail.

**Complete affirmance of suppression on balance:** A brief witness request can still be an unreasonable seizure; motorists have not consented merely by using a public road. The strongest objection focuses on trapping innocent traffic for several minutes and on less intrusive possibilities. The limited total duration must be weighed, rather than redescribed as mere seconds. Here the particular serious event, predictable site-time witness relationship, neutral operation and constrained contact provide the additional justification that a generalized crime program lacked in Edmond. Reasonableness does not demand the least intrusive conceivable method.
