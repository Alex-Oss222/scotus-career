## 111. Rumsfeld v. Padilla — No. 03-1027

### Final components and remedy

**P1: ordinary domestic custodian and forum.** This petition challenges present physical military custody and was filed after the transfer. The known brig commander is the immediate custodian; the ordinary forum is the district able to reach her. The earlier material-witness proceeding is not the later military-custody petition. Endo's continuation of an already properly filed action therefore does not itself preserve the New York action. Braden concerns the custody actually challenged and does not make every superior official an acceptable respondent to a domestic physical-custody petition.

**P2: confined exceptional principal-custodian theory.** The opposing position treats the official who personally directed this particular replacement of existing judicial custody by military restraint, controls that restraint and can end it, as a reachable custodian. This is an adopted exception based on the specific transaction and control, not a finding of bad faith, a fiction that the later petition preceded the transfer, or a general rule permitting suit against any department head. Its strongest objection remains the actual filing sequence and the effective ordinary forum in South Carolina.

**P-M: merits only for those reaching them.** The supplied domestic arrest and subsequent executive designation do not establish the congressional authorization and supported belligerent predicate needed for this military imprisonment under §4001(a) and the AUMF. Section 4001(b)'s prison-management provision supplies no exception to subsection (a). This confined rejection does not hold that every domestic arrestee is outside the law of war, that battlefield capture is always indispensable, or that innocence has been adjudicated. Relief ends the challenged military custody, with ordinary implementation allowing an identified independently lawful custody basis; it does not confer immunity from prosecution.

**P-T: forum remedy.** P1 sets aside the New York merits adjudication and requires dismissal without prejudice unless lawful transfer is supported. Under §1631, a qualifying court must find the relevant want of jurisdiction, an eligible destination in which this action could have been brought at filing, and the interest of justice; when those conditions are met the statute directs transfer with preservation of the filing date. This handoff finds neither that the conditions are met nor that every possible characterization of the defect invokes §1631. Preservation of transfer consideration is not an order to transfer.

## 112. Rasul v. Bush and Al Odah v. United States — Nos. 03-334 and 03-343

### Final components and premise comparison

**R1: statutory jurisdiction at this base.** Section 2241 permits these detainees to contest the legality of United States custody at a base under complete and continuing United States jurisdiction and control when the responsible respondent is reachable. Cuba's retained ultimate sovereignty does not defeat that base-specific construction. The absence of the military adjudications present in Eisentrager is material, but allegations denying hostile conduct remain allegations. Jurisdiction permits a return and adjudication, not immediate release or a decision of every constitutional territorial question.

**R1-K: Kennedy's narrower ground.** Enduring exclusive control away from an active battlefield, coupled with potentially indefinite detention without an adjudicated status, brings these petitions within review under the continuing Eisentrager framework. He does not accept the assertion that Braden destroyed Eisentrager's statutory foundation. He can fully join R1 if its reasoning preserves that disagreement; judgment agreement cannot carry him into a general overruling rationale or a worldwide reachable-supervisor rule.

**R2: contrary foreign-custody construction.** Scalia and Thomas read Eisentrager as retaining the statutory limit for these foreign nationals held outside sovereign United States territory; Braden's domestic detainer does not remove it. On the constitutional alternative supplied by N, they also reject the asserted independent entitlement on these foreign-capture and foreign-confinement facts. That alternative is expressly identified because the historical litigation did not independently present it in the same way. No adjudicated enemy status is invented to support the territorial rule.

**R-C: independent civil branch.** N expressly retains the pleaded §§1331/1350 and APA questions. S Abebe-Jira and Kadic establish source-specific actions and distinct jurisdiction, and reject universal alien incapacity; they do not establish an ATS detention action, a waiver of United States immunity, or judicial resolution of every military decision. All eight reject alien citizenship alone as a sufficient reason to dismiss every civil claim. The six R1 supporters vacate the categorical dismissal and remand for claim-specific jurisdiction, action, immunity, reviewability and remedy analysis. Scalia and Thomas affirm rejection of a claim necessarily dependent on the constitutional territorial entitlement they reject, but remand any genuinely independent statutory branch for those inquiries. That limited remand is not permission to bypass R2 by relabeling an identical constitutional custody claim.

## 113. Hamdi v. Rumsfeld — No. 03-6696

### Final components: authority, process, proof and duration remain separate

**H-A: narrow authorization.** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer construe the AUMF to authorize detention of an actual member of opposing Taliban forces captured in the Afghanistan hostilities to prevent return to that conflict, including a citizen. Such statutory authority satisfies §4001(a), without requiring a categorical decision about every application of that subsection to military detention. Thomas supports the authorization but also a broader account of executive war powers. None treats the Mobbs declaration as a judicial finding of membership. Section 4001(b) does not itself answer the subsection (a) question.

**H-N: statutory objection.** Souter and Ginsburg reject the Government's present justification for this citizen's detention under §4001(a). Their historical reasoning does not hold that an AUMF can never authorize citizen detention or that only the literal word detention can do so. It leaves possible a demonstrated detention within the law-of-war limits implicit in the force authorized. N supplies no additional finding about Geneva compliance or a new status proceeding; this handoff therefore does not add one. Their operative objection is to sustaining the submitted detention on the asserted authorization, while retaining the stated possible further legal bases rather than announcing a categorical anti-AUMF rule.

**H-C: prosecution or suspension.** Stevens and Scalia reject substituting a judicially designed civil status hearing for ordinary criminal process or a valid suspension where a citizen's enemy allegiance is contested. Milligan and the constitutional suspension structure supply the ground; Quirin's conceded belligerency and military trial do not settle this different confinement. Their remedy calls for release from this military custody unless ordinary lawful criminal process is promptly supplied or a valid suspension changes the governing law. An undefined other-process exception cannot be used to sustain the same military detention merely after H-P. An actually independent lawful custody regime would require its own predicates.

**H-P-min: shared remand minimum.** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer directly require notice of the asserted factual basis, counsel on remand, a meaningful opportunity to answer before a neutral decisionmaker, and effective review and relief if custody is unlawful. Souter and Ginsburg support those protections as an alternative and join a vacatur/remand that gives them practical effect despite rejecting H-A. This five-Associate minimum rejects conclusive judicial acceptance of the declaration. Initial battlefield capture does not require these domestic review procedures at the point of capture.

**H-P-flex: plurality procedural permission.** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer permit appropriately justified hearsay and a genuinely rebuttable evidentiary presumption: after credible government evidence, the detainee may be required to rebut it with more persuasive evidence. That permission reaches beyond a merely nominal production obligation; it must not be rewritten as an agreed government ultimate burden. The neutral decisionmaker must still evaluate the evidence and afford a real opportunity to respond. They also permit a properly authorized military tribunal with adequate procedures to perform the initial status adjudication, with effective judicial review remaining. These are possible accommodations, not a command to employ every one or a ruling that an untested military procedure suffices.

**H-P-reserved: Souter/Ginsburg barriers.** Their agreement with meaningful process does not include assent to any presumption that casts the burden of rebuttal on Hamdi, or to military procedures that obviate or truncate the statutory habeas inquiry. Practical presentation or protection of evidence may be accommodated only without those unaccepted consequences and with a meaningful factual contest. A full H-P-flex join cannot be claimed from their support for counsel, notice or a neutral decisionmaker. Their C Demore views do not convert that distinct statutory classification and bond system into acceptance of the historical Hamdi proof accommodation.

**H-D: narrow purpose and duration.** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer tie H-A to the relevant active hostilities and prevention of return to combat; detention solely to interrogate is not authorized by this narrow ground. No current cessation date or automatic release date is found. Souter and Ginsburg support those limits as protections if detention is sustained. Thomas does not join this full formulation: his historical view recognizes intelligence gathering as a military purpose and reserves substantially greater political-branch control over wartime necessity and the duration of detention. No limitless perpetual power is decided for him, but neither the no-interrogation-alone proposition nor the active-hostilities cutoff can receive his vote by inference.

## 114. Missouri v. Seibert — No. 02-1371

### Final components and application

**S1: objective warning effectiveness.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer assess whether the later warnings actually communicated a meaningful choice in the complete sequence. The detailed initial confession, short interruption, same interrogator, overlapping content and use of prior admissions make the warned repetition ineffective here. The circumstances inform the inquiry; they are not a mechanical checklist. No independent finding of actual coercion or subjective bad faith is necessary under this ground.

**S2: deliberate circumvention and effective cure.** Kennedy excludes the second statement where a coordinated two-stage method was deliberately used to defeat Miranda unless effective intervening measures restore an informed choice. N establishes the deliberate method, so no inference of purpose is needed. A substantial break or an adequate additional explanation may work in an appropriate case; neither automatically suffices or is found here. Ordinary Elstad analysis remains for other initial omissions. The four S1 supporters affirmatively support exclusion of this deliberate, uncured sequence as a sufficient proposition, while retaining their objection to making intent necessary for every warning failure.

**S3: ordinary Elstad inquiry and remand.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject both new midstream rules and would vacate for application of ordinary Elstad principles to the actual statements. Historical Sbt-ZD expressly requests remand, not automatic admission. Actual coercion of the first statement, its possible effect on the second, the complete surrounding circumstances and a genuinely voluntary later statement remain relevant. Existing warning-and-waiver requirements continue; a signature is not a fabricated adjudication of knowing and intelligent waiver. This does not covertly assign them S1's reasonable-recipient rule or S2's intent trigger.

**S-B: Breyer's additional position.** Breyer independently favors excluding the fruits of an initial unwarned interrogation unless the omission was in good faith. He joins S1 and supports S2 only consistently with that approach. The deliberate uncured technique here is not the unresolved intent situation in Patane. His distinct formulation is preserved without making it the rationale of the other S1 supporters or a mandatory separate-writing assignment.

## 115. United States v. Patane — No. 02-1183

### Final components and corrected remedy split

**Pt-G: narrow physical-fruit rule.** Incomplete Miranda warnings alone do not require exclusion of this physical gun derived from a voluntary answer. The answer's use in the prosecution's case in chief, the physical object, actual coercion and independently unlawful acquisition are different issues. Tucker and Elstad preserve differentiated consequences; S Dickerson expressly leaves the general fruits question open. Patane's interruption does not establish a complete waiver. Reversal of warning-only suppression does not silently revive the rejected probable-cause ground or decide a new independent defect.

**Pt-duty: broader rationale remains separate.** Scalia and Thomas support the criminal-case/testimony rationale and the broader conclusion that mere omission of warnings does not itself supply the violation to deter by excluding the gun. O'Connor and Kennedy join Pt-G on its narrower remedial basis and do not join that broader characterization. O'Connor's S Chavez criminal-use position remains her own earlier position, but it does not erase her express historical reservation about the additional duty/deterrence propositions here. A statement about the scope of the Self-Incrimination Clause is not automatically a declaration that officers have no Miranda obligation.

**Pt-F: direct derivative exclusion.** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm suppression of the direct physical product to preserve the warning safeguard's practical force. They distinguish Tucker's setting and Elstad's later effective warning rather than claiming that Dickerson already resolved all fruits. Their rule does not depend on an invented bad-faith or actual-coercion finding.

**Pt-B: good-faith-dependent remand.** Breyer would vacate and remand to determine whether the failure to warn was in good faith. He supports exclusion of the physical fruit absent that exception. N establishes neither good faith nor deliberate evasion; incomplete warnings and no complete waiver do not themselves answer the question. His position supplies neither unconditional affirmance nor unconditional admission. The original model's assertion that the record supported immediate suppression without resolving that exception is corrected.
