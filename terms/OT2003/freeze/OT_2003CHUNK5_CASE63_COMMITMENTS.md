## Components and operative commitments

**Living-family privacy.** Exemption 7(C) protects a surviving relative's own substantial interest in avoiding the intrusive public dissemination of a close family member's death images. The interest is the living relative's, supported here by family declarations describing distress and unwanted publicity; it does not depend on inheritance of the deceased's extinguished personal rights. The law-enforcement compilation predicate is undisputed. The statute permits withholding when disclosure could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; it does not itself command secrecy or create a constitutional family veto.

**Public interest and burden.** When disclosure of sensitive death images is sought on a claim that officials mishandled an investigation or misrepresented its results, the requester must identify a significant governmental-transparency interest and provide an objective evidentiary foundation connecting the requested images to that asserted deficiency. Bare accusation or a wish to conduct the investigation again is insufficient to give the public-interest side substantial weight. Evidence reasonably supporting a belief that misconduct or a meaningful investigative deficiency may have occurred suffices to enter that balance; final proof of wrongdoing is not required. The agency retains the ultimate statutory burden, and courts assess the particular images and incremental transparency. Other transparency purposes do not acquire a universal misconduct prerequisite.

**Application and remedy.** Reverse the four-image disclosure judgment and remand for entry of judgment permitting withholding of those images under 7(C). The publicly available record and reports already expose the material investigative methods and disputed observations. Favish's more specific claims about the gun-hand image and shoulder stains are considered, not erased: a clearer image might enable another independent assessment, but the report actually addressed thumb entrapment, residue, spatter and transfer, and the cited discrepancies do not supply a sufficient foundation for the inference that these four photographs would reveal meaningful governmental impropriety or a concealed investigative defect. The other two photographs' asserted contribution is still more general. The family invasion remains concrete and substantial. This is a privacy/transparency determination, not an adjudication of cause of death, veracity of every witness, or personal wrongdoing.

Unauthorized prior publication of a degraded image does not itself waive relatives' remaining interest in official dissemination of the original. Reasonably segregable nonexempt material remains subject to release under §552(b); the judgment permits withholding the protected images and does not create a photographic-record exception to segregation. The source material does not establish a useful separate nonprivate portion of these four images whose release would serve the asserted inquiry independently of the protected depiction. The request concerning different, more graphic photographs is outside this four-image controversy and supplies no new enlargement of the judgment.

## Enhanced two-path comparison

**Withholding path.** The statutory text accommodates the living family's own injury; Reporters Committee, Ray and FLRA require a genuine agency-transparency contribution. The strongest counterargument is that officials can insulate an inadequate investigation by publishing their own account and then demanding proof that only the hidden material could provide. The common rule answers that objection through an objective foundation sufficient to warrant belief in a possible meaningful defect, without requiring ultimate proof, and through independent evaluation of the images' incremental contribution. Here the report contains the actual thumb, spatter, blood-transfer and witness-vantage analysis. The requester identifies differences and seeks better images but does not establish a sufficient relation between those four particular images and the claimed official impropriety to outweigh family privacy.

**Disclosure or new-balance remand path.** Favish supplies more than a bare unsupported label: he points to government documents and ties the gun-hand image and shoulder image to specific hypotheses. That is the strongest path to a renewed judicial balance and is particularly material to Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's burden/actual-evidence commitments. It fails as a basis for ordering disclosure because those hypotheses must be evaluated against the full disclosed explanations rather than accepted as findings. It fails as a basis for a fresh general remand because the legal standard can be applied to the supplied record: no new expert finding about cause of death is necessary to decide the limited transparency interest. All eight support the same ultimate balance at this scope, not a declaration that every alleged inconsistency is factually disproved.

**Segregation fork.** All eight preserve the statutory duty for genuinely separable nonexempt material. A finding that the record cannot support the stated treatment of these four images' segregation would require correction before assembly, not a categorical assumption that photographs are indivisible. An unsupported need to obtain a source does not itself create disclosure, default or a judicial production order. The actual judgment remains permitting withholding of the protected four-image material on the stated balance.

No separate writing is required if the common opinion states the limited threshold, retains agency burden, addresses the actual image-specific arguments and preserves segregation in its own terms. No Justice commits to any factual theory about murder, evidence planting or personal deception.
