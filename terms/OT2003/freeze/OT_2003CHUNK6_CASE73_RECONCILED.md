## Reconciled result and shared limits

**O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer** would vacate the appellate judgment and require prompt federal adjudication of the separately pleaded assistance claim before deciding whether to extend the innocence gateway to this noncapital enhancement. Their sufficient ground is the available ordinary constitutional route to the same sentence remedy. They do not adopt an across-the-board requirement that every innocence allegation await adjudication of every other claim.

**Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg** would affirm the existing conditional sentence writ. They adopt two distinct propositions: a confined gateway for conclusive factual ineligibility for the habitual-offender enhancement, and an independently sufficient due-process violation under the earlier no-evidence protection. They remand for implementation of lawful sentence relief, rather than for another open-ended decision whether the conceded chronology supports a constitutional claim.

All eight distinguish gateway, constitutional merits, independent collateral conditions and remedy. None grants a writ for state-law error alone. None treats the invalid habitual-offender enhancement as invalidating the theft conviction. None backdates Gatewood, invents a state merits or criminal-finality date, or treats the absence of those exact dates as an affirmative finding that a particular statutory or nonretroactivity bar applies.

The official syllabus confirms the historical disposition and joins: O'Connor for Rehnquist, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stevens dissenting with Kennedy and Souter; Kennedy also writing separately. The historical majority required consideration of nondefaulted claims for comparable relief and other cause grounds before an innocence ruling. Its constitutional discussion relied partly on the then-existing treatment of recidivist findings under historical Almendarez-Torres and distinguished the question of extending Winship. It also said the available alternative remedy independently justified avoidance. Its reported oral-argument assurances concerning assistance and reincarceration have not been admitted as verified present concessions here.

The historical Stevens dissent did not rest on state-law error alone. Its second note expressly distinguished cases requiring caution from a concededly unlawful sentence supported by no factual basis. Kennedy joined that entire constitutional analysis, then separately emphasized officials' obligation to correct punishment for a statutory violation the person did not commit. Souter also joined Stevens's constitutional and remedial analysis.

## Changes in governing law and their actual work

The canonical March 24, 1998 Almendarez-Torres decision makes the aggravated-prior fact an element of the federal aggravated-reentry offense by statutory construction. Scalia, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg joined that controlling construction. It adopts no general constitutional rule concerning every maximum-increasing fact and no constitutional prior-conviction exception. The historical comparator's description of Almendarez-Torres cannot therefore be imported as this Court's rule.

Gatewood, June 30, 2003, establishes that necessary recidivist facts increasing a binding maximum or minimum, including chronology, receive applicable element protections. Scalia, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg directly joined that constitutional holding. It governs nonfinal direct-review cases under Griffith and expressly preserves independent collateral requirements. Its present force changes the law against which the Court decides whether to recognize a new equitable gateway now; it does not automatically supply the constitutional law governing an earlier merits adjudication or resolve a collateral new-rule question.

Monge remains a bounded double-jeopardy decision permitting renewed proof of its serious-prior qualification. Its majority left independent due-process, jury and charging questions open. Neither its majority nor its dissent supplies a categorical answer to the present gateway or the earlier no-evidence argument.

Thompson and Jackson predate the identified 1997 prosecution. Thompson protects against punishment resting on a factual record entirely without support for the charged predicate; Jackson separately requires sufficient proof of actual offense elements beyond a reasonable doubt. The four granting sentence relief decide that the admitted failure of the indispensable enhancement sequence is an application of the earlier anti-arbitrariness protection. They do not use Gatewood to create a retrospective jury entitlement or resolve a disputed quantum of evidence. The four remanding recognize that argument but leave its application to this noncapital status proceeding undecided while the ordinary assistance claim is adjudicated.

The assistance claim received a state merits rejection; the sufficiency claim received a procedural rejection. Section 2254(d) therefore applies to the former on the supplied facts. No state merits adjudication of the latter may be inferred from the former. Its gateway does not remove any genuinely applicable independent collateral condition, but an unestablished condition cannot be manufactured to withhold relief.

**Historical departure:** Ginsburg supports the confined noncapital factual-eligibility gateway and sentence relief because the controlling recidivist-law premise has changed: her statutory element position became this Court's Almendarez-Torres holding, and her Gatewood join now establishes constitutional protection for indispensable recidivist chronology. Her Schlup and Monge positions connect that change to treating this admitted factual ineligibility as an exceptional miscarriage; Thompson supplies the separate earlier constitutional ground, without retroactive application of Gatewood.

**Ginsburg's alternative-remedy objection:** The historical majority's separate prudential reason for avoiding an unnecessary exception is expressly tested and does not disappear merely because the constitutional law changed. Ginsburg now regards the controlling distinction between punishment-authorizing recidivist facts and ordinary within-range sentencing information as supplying a principled boundary for the present gateway. The incontrovertible chronology meets even the demanding Sawyer quantum; a disputed credibility assessment, mitigation argument or ordinary discretionary error would not. Her separate Thompson conclusion also supplies complete constitutional entitlement on this record without deciding Gatewood's collateral reach. On that combination, she concludes that the unresolved assistance claim offers another route to an already established remedy, while requiring that route first would preserve neither a disputed factual question nor a necessary unresolved merits question. Her choice remains confined to this defined factual-ineligibility case; it is not a rule that every innocence allegation must precede an available ordinary claim.

**Historical scope difference:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer retain the historical vacatur-and-remand result but commit only to assistance-first sequencing in this case. The changed current recidivist law removes the comparator's broad premise that this Court has never established the pertinent proof protection, while its preserved collateral limits and the unresolved gateway leave a sufficient narrower reason to adjudicate the pending ordinary claim. None of their commitments supplies a universal sequencing command covering every offense- or sentence-innocence allegation.

**Provisional revision:** Kennedy changes from the original and rechecked assistance-first commitment to direct sentence relief. That revision follows the full historical writings and the corrected earlier-law analysis; it is not a response to any Chief Justice position. No concrete changed premise supported retaining his provisional departure. The historical record is used as strong evidence after, rather than instead of, the independent modeling and scoped recheck.

## Remedy, sources and remaining limits

For Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, affirm the existing sentence-only conditional writ. Implementation requires Texas, within a reasonable concrete period fixed by the lower federal court, to impose a lawful sentence without the invalid habitual-offender enhancement or terminate custody attributable to that invalid punishment. The lower court must account for service already completed and the actual present custody and judgment status. It may not use implementation to preserve confinement unsupported by a lawful sentence. The theft conviction remains valid.

This bounded implementation position differs from the historical dissents' request for immediate unconditional release. The present neutral record supplies a conditional resentencing writ and a request for resentencing without the enhancement; it does not establish a current replacement judgment, release status, independent custody basis or precise completed service. Those research limits are not findings that Haley remains incarcerated or that he has failed to prove a fact. The reconciled relief affirms his entitlement now and leaves execution of the existing sentence remedy to the court with the actual custody record. It does not authorize an additional merits delay or presume lawful continued enhanced custody. A proposed unconditional present-release command would require the pertinent neutral custody facts, not an inference from the missing source.

For O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, vacate and remand for prompt adjudication of the assistance claim under the actual dated section 2254(d) inquiry and Strickland. Success must produce the lawful sentence remedy; if assistance does not resolve the request, genuinely remaining cause, gateway, constitutional and collateral questions must be adjudicated under their distinct requirements. Neither a final denial nor an automatic extension of the invalid enhancement follows.

The exact state merits date and criminal-finality date remain unverified. No reconciled disposition depends on choosing either. A later proposal to apply Gatewood itself to this collateral claim must establish the relevant dates and actual temporal conditions first. The direct-relief coalition instead applies the earlier no-evidence rule, and the remand coalition leaves those questions open.

The oral-argument transcript has not been recovered in this work. The historical majority reports assurances about not asserting procedural impediments to assistance and not seeking reincarceration during its litigation. They are comparator descriptions, not verified concessions in the neutral case. No reconciled vote relies on either assurance, and no public projection may report either as an established current promise. The existing state merits disposition and federal nonadjudication, already in the neutral packet, support consideration of assistance without inventing an oral waiver. No new current custody fact or state resentencing event is supplied.
