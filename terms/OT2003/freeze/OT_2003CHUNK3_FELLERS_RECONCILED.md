## Rules and sufficient reasoning retained

**Initial violation, all eight Associates.** After indictment, officers violate the attached right when they deliberately elicit incriminating statements about the charged crime without counsel or a valid waiver. The Sixth Amendment inquiry does not require Miranda custodial interrogation. The officers' announced purpose of discussing the charged methamphetamine distribution and their naming of alleged associates deliberately elicited the ensuing admissions. An invitation to enter the home does not waive the right. Massiah and Brewer supply this rule, while Patterson preserves a genuinely valid waiver.

**Derivative-statement rule, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Once deliberate elicitation violating the attached right and its causal connection to a later statement have been shown, the Government must establish that the later statement resulted from a genuinely independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of the violation. Adequate later warnings and a knowing, voluntary waiver are important but do not alone resolve that inquiry. Actual temporal separation, genuine intervening circumstances, the purpose and character of the unlawful elicitation, and the later warning and waiver bear on the causal question; no factor by itself substitutes for determining independence on the established sequence.

Massiah protects the represented criminal process independently of compelled speech. Wade recognizes that a Sixth Amendment violation can have derivative evidentiary consequences. Brown separates voluntariness from exploitation of a completed constitutional violation, and Kaupp applies that distinction to unlawful arrest; the present holding expressly extends that causal principle to deliberate elicitation rather than pretending Kaupp already decided it. Patterson permits a valid later interview but does not decide whether particular evidence exploits an earlier violation. Elstad permits a later voluntary warned statement after an earlier unwarned voluntary statement in its actual Miranda setting; extending that waiver-only treatment to purposeful violation of the attached right would remove the independent causal inquiry. Dickerson preserves Elstad at its actual scope rather than establishing such an extension.

The rule gives full weight to the later waiver and permits genuinely independent later speech. The same officers' repetition of the earlier admissions warrants the inquiry but does not itself establish the complete attenuation result. Neither a permanent bar to lawful questioning nor mandatory attorney consultation is a condition of the six's rule.

## Remedy and complete resolution

Vacate the judgment sustaining admission of the jail statements and remand. Under the six-Associate rule, the lower court must determine whether the Government establishes the required causal independence on the proper record. If it fails, the derivative statements must be excluded. A properly preserved harmless-error argument then requires the Government to establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the actual trial record, that admission did not contribute to the verdict; otherwise the affected conviction must be set aside for lawful further proceedings.
