## Common ground and precise join test

The seven reversal supporters can all join this formulation: section 253's general reference to the ability of any entity does not provide the sufficiently clear congressional direction required to interpose federal authority between Missouri and its own municipal subdivisions in allocating their service powers. Gregory supplies the governing clarity requirement for this state governmental arrangement. The text's competing implications and the difference between private freedom after preemption and state-created municipal capacity explain the absence of the required clarity; they do not establish a freestanding judicial power to reject an otherwise clear enactment because its consequences seem unwise.

That formulation answers Scalia's and Thomas's actual historical objection without changing their substantive ground. ## Stevens's actual alternative and the response

Stevens reads the statutory entity language to include public utilities already authorized and capable of providing service without compelled state assistance. He distinguishes ordinary preemption of a targeted restraint on entry from federal creation of a municipality or an affirmative funding command. His distinction is not merely explicit versus implicit repeal: his actual dissent includes indirect barriers that specifically target municipal telecommunications, while allowing general redefinition of municipal powers. The resulting dissent must address the prohibition-effect language in those terms rather than caricature his view as permitting every indirectly phrased restraint.

The seven respond that identifying state economic laws as objects of preemption does not clearly identify the separate State–municipality power relationship as an object. Section 253 reaches prohibitions and prohibitory effects; it does not itself furnish an unambiguous line making a targeted withdrawal federally prohibited while a general allocation remains outside the rule. Stevens regards the statutory competition and neutrality structure as sufficient to draw that line, consistent with his Gregory/White position; the seven do not. None requires a conclusion that Congress constitutionally lacks power to enact an expressly different rule.

## Scope and remedy

The common opinion concerns Missouri's regulation of its own political subdivisions in the presented services setting. It does not adjudicate a locality's restriction of its own services, a State's direct service delivery or the distinct status of independently chartered municipal corporations. The latter limitation is explicit in the historical Court's second footnote and is consistent with the supplied neutral distinction between subdivisions and independent providers; no newly found corporate status is attributed to a party. Section 253 retains its force against covered ordinary market barriers, and generally applicable federal regulation of state activity remains distinct from this internal allocation question.

The three petitions review one judgment and receive the same reversal, leaving the FCC refusal effective for the challenged subdivision restriction. Stevens's opposite disposition would require statutory application to existing capacity, not immediate judicial creation of powers. No separate constitutional holding, policy immunity for public commerce or mandatory final competition judgment is adopted.
