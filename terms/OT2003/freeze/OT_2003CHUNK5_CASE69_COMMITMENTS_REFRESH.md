## Issues and common source constraints

**A. Statutory scope.** The Court must decide whether section 209(a)'s prohibition of state and local adoption or attempted enforcement of an emission standard for new vehicles excludes a mandatory emissions criterion merely because the command addresses purchasers. Seven Justices provisionally reject that exclusion and the further proposition that commercial availability alone removes an otherwise covered regulatory standard. A criterion may identify permitted emissions or a required emissions-control feature; commanding buyers to obtain vehicles satisfying it can enforce that criterion. The provision's actual text, including its new-vehicle and subject-to-this-part limits, must be applied. This is not a holding that every economic influence on vehicle demand is a standard.

**B. Qualifications.** Commercial availability can matter to the real operation of a rule and to the competing production-focused construction. It is not an identical, unconditional exemption in every rule. Preserve each rule's technical showing, vehicle class, credit alternative, certificate, remaining control-device/fuel duties and dates. In particular, do not equate Rule 1186.1's limited purchase-certificate route with its separate contracting route, or assume its purchase exception remains after its July 1, 2004 cutoff. Do not treat the petitioners' hypothetical-vehicle construction of the words about availability and use as an adjudicated fact.

**C. Section 246.** The program remains relevant to the statutory structure and competing understandings of standards and purchase requirements. These respondents do not defend these Rules as authorized by section 246, and the supplied primary recitation establishes that the Rules do not satisfy all of its program conditions. No Justice's disposition rests on that nonexistent authorization defense; no mandate directs a search for section 246 approval for the Rules before the Court. The case does not decide a hypothetical compliant program.

**D. Remedy and reserved applications.** A rejection of the categorical exemption does not establish that every provision or application of all six rules is preempted. The lower courts must distinguish actual sovereign regulation from any genuine proprietary purchase, new from existing vehicles under the governing statute, the actual scope of a claimed waiver, and severable applications. The Court supplies no universal market-participant safe harbor, no automatic waiver from certification of some models, and no merits ruling on an unpreserved use restriction. No injunction against every public purchase follows from the answer to issue A.

The exact entering Standards are **Cigarette-labeling preemption by statutory period and predicate duty**, **Medical-device requirements and genuinely parallel state duties**, and **Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption**. Cipollone supplies identification of the actual statutory duty and enforcement mechanism; Medtronic supplies actual statutory comparison and rejects both categorical immunity and preemption from shared subject matter alone; Boston Harbor supplies a bounded proprietary inquiry under its own statutory scheme. Section 209(a)'s different words govern here. Alaska and Environmental Defense Center supply no automatic answer.

## Stevens

**Commitment:** Vacate the judgment resting on a categorical purchase exemption and remand for rule-specific review. Join the proposition that a coercive emissions-based purchase criterion can enforce a covered standard even when compliant vehicles are commercially available. Do not join blanket invalidation of all six rules.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** Stevens's Cipollone analysis distinguishes the 1965 statute's narrow statement command from the successor clause's broader duties and prohibitions, then looks through labels to the duty imposed. A purchase prohibition based on emissions is a positive governmental command, not a voluntarily supplied warranty or a general duty independent of the regulated subject. His joins to Medtronic Parts I–III and V require the actual federal predicate and preserve genuinely parallel obligations under that statute. They do not insert a different-or-additional condition into section 209(a). The latter expressly concerns adoption or attempted enforcement and so can reach use of an existing emissions category as a new compulsory purchasing condition.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** Affirmance can be defended by reading standard in its manufacturing context and stressing that the exceptions choose among existing certified products. That is stronger than a bare buyer/seller label. He nevertheless rejects it because it makes the identity of the regulated buyer and the existing supply do work the enacted clause does not assign them. Regulatory selection is materially distinct from private choice. Uncertainty about a particular rule's operation affects its application and remedy, not a categorical exemption.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No displacement based only on pollution purpose or market effect; identify the enforceable emissions predicate. Preserve exact availability terms, public-procurement and newness issues. A narrower opinion deciding only the legal possibility of purchase-side enforcement is acceptable; an opinion treating all procurement as regulation or section 246 as a live saving defense is not. Writing function: controlling statutory explanation and bounded remand; separate clarification only if those limits are omitted.

## O'Connor

**Commitment:** Vacate and remand. Join the purchase-side enforcement rule, including its application despite existing commercial supply, with rule-specific reservations.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** Her Cipollone join accepts displacement of actual covered warning and neutralization duties despite the form of enforcement; her Medtronic Part IV join accepts common-law inclusion while Parts I–II require the statutory comparison. A directly enforceable governmental purchasing rule presents a stronger inclusion case than a damages duty. Neither the regulated person's position in a transaction nor the presence of another available vehicle changes the required emission characteristic.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** The competing path invokes the state's public health responsibilities and purchaser autonomy, reinforced by Boston Harbor. It succeeds for actual ownership choices only if the statute and proprietary character support that conclusion. It does not justify a district-wide rule binding independent private and public operators merely because some of them purchase for public purposes. The strongest production-context objection cannot overcome a clause addressing the standard adopted or enforced, rather than only the manufacturer's conduct.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** She will not substitute the labor-law proprietary holding for construction of the Clean Air Act, assume a waiver, or extend the ruling automatically to used vehicles. The availability qualifications must be acknowledged accurately but need not be adjudicated one by one to reject the categorical exclusion. Writing function: join a statutory majority and preserve targeted application on remand; no separate writing required if those limits hold.

## Scalia

**Commitment:** Vacate and remand. Join the rule that compulsory purchase criteria based on new-vehicle emissions can adopt or enforce standards regardless of which side of the transaction receives the legal command.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** His separate Cipollone express-preemption analysis, joined by Thomas, reads the enacted reach of a prohibition without making regulatory technique dispositive. His Medtronic Parts I–II and IV joins likewise distinguish statutory inclusion from the later inquiry into actual displacement. Here the state command identifies a substantive emissions criterion, while the clause does not require an additional numerical limit, a manufacturer addressee or proof that a manufacturer must invent a product. His Alaska join also insists on the precise statutory allocation; it is no general presumption against federal authority expressly granted by this different text.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** Reading standard as a production requirement has contextual support in the federal manufacturer's certification regime. But a standard and its enforcement mechanism are distinct: a government may enforce the same criterion through purchasers. Availability changes the practical burden, not that conceptual relationship. He does not need an empirical prediction that the rules will compel new manufacture.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No reliance on unspecified legislative purpose to preempt every demand effect, no reconstruction of missing pages as a concession, and no claim that every exception is identical. Section 246 cannot become an operative authorization defense in this case. A narrower remand opinion is acceptable; a categorical labor-derived procurement immunity is not. Writing function: join the textual rule; separate explanation only if the controlling writing makes policy balancing indispensable.

## Kennedy

**Commitment:** Vacate and remand. Join purchaser-directed regulatory enforcement of a covered standard, while reserving genuine proprietary and other distinct applications.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** Kennedy joined the narrower original-Act construction and broader common-law-survival position in Cipollone, and Medtronic's statutory comparison and specific-predicate analysis. Those positions prevent an inference of displacement from incidental effects. The Fleet Rules instead supply positive, compulsory emissions criteria: the distinction that mattered in those cases does not immunize them. His own Alaska dissent locates power in the precise congressional allocation, refusing to create a federal review channel from desirable outcomes. Section 209(a), unlike the disputed channel there, expressly addresses state and local adoption or attempted enforcement of new-vehicle emission standards.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** A production-focused reading could preserve local selection among certified vehicles without altering manufacturing requirements, especially where exceptions address unavailability. He rejects a categorical exemption because certification for the general market does not answer whether another government may impose a compulsory emissions condition for a segment of that market. The actual command must be identified, not inferred solely from changed demand.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No analogy from EPA's different permitting power, no assumption that all public entities act as private proprietors, and no enforcement of an exception beyond its actual date or terms. A limited decision excluding categorical immunity with a remand is preferable to an undifferentiated injunction. Writing function: join with a concise allocation and application limit; separate concurrence only if necessary to preserve those limits.

## Souter

**Commitment:** Provisionally vacate and remand on a narrower ground; withhold assent to the proposition that every mandatory selection among actually available certified vehicles is necessarily a preempted standard. This judgment commitment is the most uncertain and does not count as a join to the seven-Justice breadth of issue A.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** His Cipollone broader common-law-survival position and Medtronic Parts I–III and V joins require close attention to the specific federal manufacturing choice displaced, rather than equating every legal effect on conduct with a forbidden standard. Reading section 209 alongside the federal manufacturing/certification provisions supports a distinction between a production requirement and selection among vehicles already available under the federal/California regime. Section 246 is relevant structural evidence that purchase requirements and production standards can have distinct roles, without authorizing these Rules.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** On a record establishing that all challenged applications merely select available products, he would favor affirmance on that narrower construction, rejecting a hypothetical need to manufacture something new. The exact text now prevents treating that factual/legal premise as established across the entire prospective rule set: Rule 1186.1's purchase-certificate route ends July 1, 2004; the separate contract route is not its substitute for every purchase; other clauses retain particular certificate, vehicle-class, credit and residual-compliance conditions. The lower judgment's categorical purchase label does not perform the needed inquiry. The opposite textual objection remains substantial: the provision speaks of enforcement of any standard rather than production mandates. He does not accept it as a reason to treat actual available-product selection as categorically preempted.

**Remedy and limits:** Remand to identify the challenged applications, construe the exact qualifications and determine whether any command operates as a production requirement under his narrower reading. April 28 precedes July 1; the future cutoff is material only because the enacted prospective purchase rule is challenged, not because a post-cutoff violation or unavailable vehicle has been found. He would preserve adjudicated applications demonstrably confined to available products if they are severable. He neither finds technical infeasibility nor invalidates a provision merely because that future fact is unknown.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** Reject an unconditional all-six availability premise and the petitioners' competing claim that possible-use wording necessarily permits hypothetical products. The latter needs construction, not acceptance as fact. Reject any section-246-authorization ruling or a universal public-purchase immunity. If the final clean source interpretation establishes a legally operative availability limit for every challenged prospective purchase application, the narrower affirmance path becomes preferable; if the rule actually compels a new emissions-related production choice, preemption of that application is acceptable. Writing function: separate production-focused explanation of the judgment and the applications he would preserve; no author assigned.

## Thomas

**Commitment:** Vacate and remand. Join the compulsory purchase-criterion construction, preserving the actual statutory subject and application boundaries.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** His join to Scalia's Cipollone analysis and Medtronic Part IV supports applying express preemption to actual legal obligations regardless of their mechanism. Parts I–II of Medtronic still require the elements the particular statute contains. Section 209(a) concerns the substantive standard adopted or enforced; it supplies no buyer-only or already-commercially-available exemption. A rule can impose a criterion for acceptable emissions without itself specifying a numerical tailpipe value. His Alaska dissent join is consistent with enforcing an express allocation while refusing implied enlargement in another statutory scheme.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** Manufacturing context is the strongest narrower reading, and the availability qualifications reduce the force of any argument based on forced technological invention. He does not use that empirical argument. The legal criterion and the actor compelled to observe it are distinct; no proof of invention is needed when a command actually enforces an emissions standard for a new vehicle.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** Do not replace the text with broad regulatory-uniformity policy, decide incentive programs by analogy, infer section 246 compliance, or assume the statute covers every existing vehicle. Any proprietary exception needs statutory support and actual facts. Writing function: join a textual opinion; a short separate statement is warranted only if the majority's necessary reasoning exceeds those grounds.

## Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Vacate and remand. Join the conclusion that availability of compliant products does not by itself save a compulsory new-vehicle emissions standard. Preserve independent application defenses.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** Her Medtronic Parts I–III and V joins insist on the actual federal/state comparison and reject wholesale displacement from a shared safety subject. That approach narrows the claim here to an enforceable emissions criterion, rather than air-quality policy or public expenditure in general. Unlike section 360k, section 209(a) does not make an actual difference from a specific federal device requirement the trigger; its adoption-or-enforcement language can reach compulsory reuse of an existing standard. The statutory distinction supplies a reason for this result without retreat from her Medtronic position.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** The available-products reading protects traditional health action and avoids conflating purchasing with manufacture. It is compelling for genuinely independent purchaser choice, and the qualifications materially weaken claims that all six rules force a new vehicle design. But the Rules include governmental commands to independent operators; selecting a compulsory emissions category for them is still regulatory enforcement. The Court can recognize that while reserving genuinely proprietary procurement and refusing to assume every application is identical.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No automatic deference to a litigating position or isolated agency letter; no categorical environmental field preemption; no full-rule injunction without the remaining comparisons. Preserve section 246 as context only. Writing function: join the confined statutory opinion and remand; separate statement if necessary to distinguish actual regulation from sovereign purchasing choices.

## Breyer

**Commitment:** Vacate and remand. Join the statutory possibility and ordinary inclusion of compulsory purchase-based emissions criteria, subject to exact qualifications and unresolved applications.

**Grounds and own prior basis:** His Medtronic opinion separates inclusion of an obligation within a preemption clause from the actual comparison that determines displacement, recognizing that different enforcement methods can regulate the same substantive conduct. A purchaser command can accordingly enforce a vehicle standard even though the manufacturer receives no direct order. At the same time, commercial availability and genuine purchasing functions are operationally material and cannot be dismissed with an empirical claim that every restriction changes vehicle design. The text supplies the core rule; the actual application supplies its boundaries.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** The strongest contrary account treats the Rules as ordinary market selection among products the national scheme already permits. That is persuasive when the government acts only as a proprietor or the legal command governs a different statutory subject. It cannot justify categorical immunity for coercive rules imposed on independent operators. The section-246 program demonstrates that Congress can organize fleet purchasing within its scheme; its conditions cannot be ignored, and no compliance finding is available here.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** Do not turn Medtronic's formal FDA interpretation into deference to every agency position, treat remand as proof of invalidity, or conflate Rule 1186.1's two pathways. A narrower decision rejecting purchase-side immunity is acceptable if it preserves meaningful application review. Writing function: join the core opinion and, if needed, explain the practical difference between a regulatory command and a proprietor's choice.

## Coalition, remedies and functions frozen for reconciliation

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur and the core proposition that compulsory new-vehicle emissions criteria can be enforced through purchasers, with commercial availability alone not a categorical exclusion. Souter provisionally supports vacatur/remand on the narrower source-specific ground stated above and does not join that full breadth. All eight oppose treating section 246 as an independent authorization defense or directing a section-246-compliance inquiry on remand for these Rules. All eight require exact qualifications and refuse an undifferentiated injunction against every provision and application.

Souter's narrower remand rationale is not automatically a logical subset that narrows the seven-Justice standard-enforcement rule; the latter already has seven direct non-Chief supporters. Counts here describe only these independent commitments, not the final Court. Reconciliation may change a commitment only on a stated Justice-specific basis. The statutory writing, any separate source-sensitive/production-focused explanation, and the remand instructions are writing functions only. Assignment awaits the authorized later stage.
