## Reconciled rules and judgment

**R1 — constitutional execution.** Retain P1–P2, with the controlling explanation focused on the opportunity to destroy the cocaine. After meaningful announcement, a reasonable suspicion of imminent destruction may mature while officers wait. Here the loud daytime notice, warrant-supported presence of readily disposable cocaine, and fifteen-to-twenty-second silence together supported the inference that further waiting risked loss of the evidence. The officers did not have to hear flushing or eliminate every innocent explanation. Banks's unknown shower use supplies no contemporaneous premise. The small dwelling is context, not a rule tying destruction time to the distance to the front door, and the interval creates no universal narcotics timer.

Property damage does not require a greater quantum of exigency. Its means and amount remain separately reasonable. Without exigency, the prospect of avoidable damage can require greater patience to allow a genuine opportunity for admission; Ramirez does not make property interests irrelevant. The lower court's categorical four-part scheme cannot replace the actual circumstances. The rule concerns this warranted felony search and decides no warrantless minor-offense entry rule.

**R2 — statutory destruction qualification adopted here.** Section 3109 ordinarily requires notice of authority and purpose and refused admission; it separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate an officer or assisting person. As a codification of common-law announcement, it also preserves a specifically supported evidence-destruction exigency: after notice, officers may enter without an actual or inferred refusal when particular circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further delay will permit imminent destruction of the warranted evidence. The same common-law method that supports Ramirez's danger qualification supports this bounded qualification; it prevents the announcement rule from requiring officers to defeat the authorized search when the protected evidence is about to disappear. Miller supplies the statutory common-law background without having decided this exception; Sabbath supplies the codification analysis without making unsupported danger enough. Wilson and Richards supply the constitutional history and particularized threshold, not automatic incorporation of all constitutional rules.

This is an express extension of the statutory common-law method to the presented destruction circumstance, not a statement that Ramirez previously decided it. The facts supporting R1 satisfy R2 after actual notice. The model's P3 implied-refusal holding is replaced by R2. No independent finding of refusal is necessary or adopted. This avoids treating silence as a statutory refusal merely because disposal could be quick. The statute retains independent work: no generic drug exception, no dispensing with notice merely because drugs are sought, no automatic identity of all constitutional and statutory exceptions, and no liberation premise.

**R3 — disposition.** All eight Associates retain reversal of the Ninth Circuit's entry-based suppression judgment and remand consistent with lawful execution under both sources. The conditional conviction is not defeated on this entry ground. Independent interrogation rulings outside the grant retain their actual posture. No unpresented sentencing question or exclusionary consequence after a proven violation is decided.
