### Issue 1: Protecting federally assisted entities against corruption

* **Controlling Law:** Salinas v. United States rejects a statutory requirement that a bribe demonstrably affect federal funds but reserves constitutional outer boundaries. Fischer v. United States requires qualifying federal benefits, not every commercial payment from the Government.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a genuine federal program connection and a means reasonably adapted to protecting it. A statutory construction rejecting tracing does not itself settle every constitutional application.
* **Other Governing Law:** Spending and Necessary and Proper Clauses; 18 U.S.C. §666(a)(2), requiring corrupt intent, a covered agent and a transaction involving at least $5,000, with more than $10,000 in qualifying annual federal benefits under §666(b).
* **Material Other Authority:** McCulloch v. Maryland permits appropriate means of carrying an enumerated power into execution. South Dakota v. Dole addresses conditions on recipients, distinct from direct criminal regulation of bribery.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Salinas settles the statutory tracing question; Fischer constrains what counts as federal benefits. The constitutional power question remains open under those precedents.
* **Open Point:** Whether protecting an assisted entity’s integrity is sufficiently connected to federal expenditures without dollar-specific loss.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Eighth Circuit sustained the prohibition as protection of the federal spending program without a tracing element.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Sabri argues that the absence of a transaction-specific federal nexus turns federal aid into general authority over local corruption.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The United States argues that corrupt control of the recipient institution threatens federal program integrity even when the bribed transaction uses commingled or nonfederal dollars.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** A court may sustain the statutory mechanism while preserving a concrete challenge to an alleged application outside the identified federal program relationship.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States defends the statute under the Spending and Necessary and Proper Clauses.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Upholding the mechanism affirms and returns the indictment for trial under every statutory element. Finding the federal relationship constitutionally inadequate requires dismissal or an authorized limiting construction, not insertion of a tracing element contrary to Salinas. A narrower application challenge needs actual facts beyond this facial objection.
