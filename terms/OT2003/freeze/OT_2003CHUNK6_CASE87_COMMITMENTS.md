Alvarado was seventeen years and seven months old at the offense, and approximately one month older at the station interview. His parents transported him at the detective's request and waited in the lobby. Whether they asked to attend and were refused is disputed and supplies no finding. One detective interviewed him for about two hours in a small room, challenged his initial denials and urged honesty. Two breaks were offered near the end; he declined. He returned home. The interview was recorded with his knowledge and without Miranda warnings. His later testimony that the exchange was friendly and uncoercive does not decide objective custody. The sentence is fifteen years to life for second-degree murder.

General established law may govern without a factual twin; independent error is not enough. Apparent age differs from undisclosed experience: the former can describe the objectively understood position of the person questioned, while the latter invites an individualized psychology inquiry absent communicated circumstances. No Justice relies on a later categorical age rule or the disputed parental-refusal account.

The reversal path concludes that voluntary arrival with parents, the lack of formal arrest or physical restraint, offered breaks and departure permit the state noncustody result within the established rule's reasonable range despite station setting, duration and accusations. The strongest contrary point is that a minor brought by parents and questioned alone for two hours may understand no practical choice to leave. This is a substantial custody argument, but the established holdings did not compel giving age or private inexperience dispositive weight, and the supported objective circumstances remain susceptible to the state's conclusion.

The contrary path finds the full encounter arrest-equivalent even without the disputed parental refusal. The preexisting reasonable-person-in-the-interviewee's-position inquiry can account for obvious youth without announcing a new constitutional rule. A two-hour isolated station interrogation, escalating accusations and parents waiting outside can convey official control in a manner that voluntary arrival and ultimate release do not negate. On that view, treating the circumstances as noncustodial exceeds the permissible application range. Both statutory eligibility and actual custody are found, but a writ still requires proper admission-and-prejudice analysis not supplied by a custody finding alone.
