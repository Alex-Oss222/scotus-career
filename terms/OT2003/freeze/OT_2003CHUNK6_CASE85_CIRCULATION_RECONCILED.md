## Marks review: the two rationales actually tested

The reconciled votes remain four categorical affirmers — O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer — and two evidence-ground affirmers — Scalia and Ginsburg. Stevens and Souter remain opposed to the search-incident grounds. This receipt creates no additional direct join in either complete rationale and supplies no additional participant's position.

**Finding:** If the final decision retains those two affirmative rationales without a fifth direct join in a complete rationale, their actual bounded rules support a Marks-derived narrowest ground. It is insufficient to reject that conclusion merely because the four do not adopt the two's replacement justification. The original reconciliation properly reserved this question for an actual test; this receipt supplies that test.

Let the shared predicate be a lawful custodial arrest of a genuine recent vehicle occupant, followed by a contemporaneous search of the passenger compartment within the stated vehicle-search scope. The categorical four permit that search even after ordinary securing, without requiring an individualized offense-evidence belief. Scalia and Ginsburg permit the same bounded search only with an objective reasonable belief that the vehicle contains evidence relevant to the arrest offense. The latter ground retains the shared predicate and adds a condition.

Thus every search affirmatively authorized by the two's frozen rule is authorized by the four's frozen rule. The converse fails: a secured recent occupant arrested for an offense supplying no reasonable basis to expect evidence in the vehicle falls within the four's category but outside the two's ground. No frozen formulation permits the two to search a trunk, a remote vehicle, an unlimited time later, or a vehicle with no genuine recent-occupancy connection. Such an expansion could defeat this nesting, but it is not their recorded rule and cannot be invented to manufacture nonnesting.

This is more than accidental agreement about the handgun or the outcome of one arrest. Both groups address the same Fourth Amendment vehicle-search authorization after the same lawful arrest, on the same accepted facts and at the same temporal and spatial scope. Both accept vehicle-specific incident-search authority in this setting without a separate full automobile-exception probable-cause showing; the two add the offense-evidence condition to the circumstances in which they sustain that authority. Their differing explanations — continuity and a categorical application of Belton for four, offense-linked vehicle authority and rejection of fictional access for two — remain important disagreement, but do not create a cross-cutting class in which the two authorize a search that the four forbid.

## Why the authorities support that conclusion

Marks, 430 U.S. 188, 193–194, treated the three-Justice Memoirs position as governing although two judgment supporters rejected obscenity suppression categorically. The absence of their direct assent to Memoirs's full framework did not prevent the narrower position from controlling. Accordingly, refusal to adopt a limiting condition or its explanation cannot by itself defeat a narrowest-ground inquiry. The comparison must identify the actual decisions and premises each rule entails.

Souter's Nichols concurrence identifies the genuine problem in Baldasar: four Justices asked whether an accepted valid conviction could have a later use; Blackmun rejected original validity and did not answer the assumed-valid question. Agreement to reverse therefore could not supply five votes on that different premise. The prior public Nichols decision preserves that distinction while recognizing the narrower Guidelines permission supported by the broader four-Justice valid-use rule and Souter's added reliability-and-opportunity condition. Thornton's shared lawful-arrest and bounded vehicle setting resemble that nested permission. Scalia and Ginsburg do not deny the predicate arrest's validity or decide a different judgment's constitutional status.

Marks itself therefore answers the proposed objection based solely on disagreement over necessity, while Nichols requires a common question and compatible predicates. Both conditions are present on the frozen formulations. No later fractured-decision authority is imported.

## Authority and limits to carry forward

At the unchanged fractured topology, the controlling affirmative rule should be identified as **the offense-evidence ground derived under Marks from Scalia and Ginsburg's two-Justice rationale**, rather than a six-Justice opinion or five express joins. It permits the bounded contemporaneous passenger-compartment search after lawful arrest of a genuine recent occupant where the objective offense-evidence condition is satisfied. Thornton's supported drug arrest and immediate vehicle connection satisfy that condition without using the subsequently discovered gun to justify the search.

The four categorical opinions remain broader positions, not four extra direct adopters of a reasonable-belief replacement test. The two's general theoretical criticism of Chimel's application, broader historical explanation, or treatment of searches outside the shared domain does not acquire majority status merely from this derivation. Likewise, the shared permission must not be recast as six direct votes to invalidate every search lacking the added condition or as a new rule deciding trunks, residential searches, remote searches, unsupported alternatives or other independent exceptions. A search outside the offense-evidence ground cannot rely on this derived Thornton authorization; its treatment under otherwise governing precedent and independent justifications requires the actual distinct question and applicable authority.

This distinction is material to the final description of the resulting law: the decision is not devoid of a reusable search rule merely because neither complete opinion has five direct joins. Conversely, narrowest-ground derivation cannot supply additional opinion joins, authorship, or votes for propositions exceeding the nested ground. If final assembly changes a predicate or supplies a direct five-Justice rationale, the actual final topology must be reassessed; this receipt assumes no such new choice.
