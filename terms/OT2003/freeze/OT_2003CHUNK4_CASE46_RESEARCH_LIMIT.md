## Additional date-eligible primary authority

Chan v. Korean Air Lines, Ltd., 490 U.S. 122 (1989), official United States Reports PDF: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep490/usrep490122/usrep490122.pdf. The downloaded primary PDF and complete text are retained in terms/OT2003/tmp/olympic-foreign/chan-490122.pdf and chan-490122.txt.

Scalia wrote the Court's opinion; O'Connor and Kennedy joined. It treats the Convention's text and differences among provisions as binding, declining to create a treaty sanction for deficient ticket notice where the text does not provide it. It distinguishes defective delivery from absence of delivery; neither the goals of notice nor a related private agreement can supply the missing treaty consequence. It permits drafting history to elucidate ambiguous text and relies on Saks for that proposition, but will not amend clear treaty language. This authority does not decide whether a refusal is an accident or settle the facts here.

Stevens joined Brennan's concurrence in the judgment. That writing reads the text contextually and considers negotiating history where the text admits competing readings. It nevertheless refuses to turn an external 10-point-type standard into a treaty condition and finds the actual 8-point notice adequate even assuming a minimal-adequacy condition. This is evidence of Stevens's own method, not a controlling alternative to Chan's holding and not a finding in the present case.
