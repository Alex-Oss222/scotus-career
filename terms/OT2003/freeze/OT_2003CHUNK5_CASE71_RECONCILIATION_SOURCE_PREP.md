## Source distinctions to carry into reconciliation

The partisan branches are Congress, State House and State Senate. Majority footnote 58 expressly identifies all three. The House claim was rejected under the announced partisan test, without a separately described House evidentiary analysis. The Senate branch carries an express no-evidence concession in the second amended proposed findings and conclusions. That concession is confined to the partisan Senate claim; it does not resolve racial or process claims. Neither a legal change nor silence about House details supplies missing evidence or an affirmative no-evidence concession for another branch.

The congressional record contains local Tampa testimony concerning approximately 69,909 residents transferred from CD 10 to CD 11 and a Campbell alternative congressional plan. The lower court's use of constitutional Gingles prerequisites and its treatment of the local evidence as insufficient for a statewide theory are legal applications, not facts. The alternative exists, but its feasibility, lawful comparison value and connection to personal injury are not established merely by its 12/13 vote-index output. The separate totality discussion must be assessed under the actual effective Vieth rule; its designation as an alternative ground does not establish independence from a displaced legal premise.

The process branch concerns purpose under equal protection and the pleaded section 601 claim. The source records serious procedural criticisms and contrary participation evidence, followed by a finding against racial purpose under an ordinary motivating-factor standard. The separate bounded prospective section 602 route remains actual law but is not identified as preserved here. The earlier Count I section 2 pleading dismissal is reported in footnote 7 without a separately supplied appellate challenge or detailed reasoning; this note neither revives it nor creates a new rule to dispose of it.

The racial claims retain their district-specific proof and remedy scope. No-relief positions for SDs 1/18 and HDs 15/39/94, accepted Hispanic performance findings and the abandoned Cuban/non-Cuban theory are distinct from the actively contested districts. Candidate race, incumbency, the total number of performing districts and opportunities elsewhere cannot substitute for the actual local opportunity inquiry. HD 103/109 packing findings include specific adjacency, lawfulness and individual-opportunity analysis in addition to a district-count observation.

HD 108 requires particular care. Finding 216 accepts 47.2 percent as a reasonable conditional open-primary projection, and finding 215 separately compares combined HD 108/118 performance. The combined observation cannot cure a proved local barrier under actual De Grandy. The conditional open-primary prediction is not itself proof that such a primary will occur or that a protected candidate will lose. Its significance must be assessed with the actual nomination rules, supported local evidence and trial findings.

Florida chapter 2003-415 section 30 supplies plurality nomination for 2004, with lots resolving an equal highest vote, and continues elimination of the second primary until January 1, 2006. Section 32 makes that provision effective January 1, 2004. The lower opinion's anticipated 2004 return to majority/runoff voting therefore is not the effective legal premise for this event. Conversely, the enactment does not establish candidate entry, vote splitting, coalescence, crossover support or a qualifying write-in candidate. A plurality rule neither makes the 47.2-percent projection a proved defeat nor guarantees success. The source receipt resolves the legal chronology while leaving empirical predictions and their legal sufficiency for adjudication.

CD 23's conditional open-primary analysis is distinct: the lower court criticizes the challengers' 48.0-percent projection and supplies open-seat findings as well as incumbency support. Write-in qualification and coalescence remain predictions, not statutory guarantees or newly established candidacies. No subsequent election outcome is added.
