## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The prohibition on excluding relevant mitigation was already dictated by Lockett and Eddings. The charge and common unanimity introduction on the verdict form reasonably conveyed an impermissible mitigation barrier, and Pennsylvania's rejection failed the objective statutory standard.
* **Pennsylvania:** The later rule changes collective jury decisionmaking and was not compelled by prefinality law; no retroactivity exception applies. Independently, unanimity attached to the aggravator and death verdict, not each mitigating circumstance, and the state court reasonably read the charge. It seeks reversal.
* **Banks:** A single juror's ability to use mitigation is already protected; the instructions and form failed to communicate it. He seeks retention of sentence relief.
* **Limits:** Weight and consideration of mitigation differ. A checked mitigating factor does not establish that every juror could use every other supported factor; finding a violation does not itself complete the separate effect inquiry.
