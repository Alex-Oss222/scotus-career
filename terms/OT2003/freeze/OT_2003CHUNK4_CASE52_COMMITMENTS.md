## Judgment and full common ground

All eight Associates reverse the categorical exclusion of a working owner from ERISA participant status and remand for application of the remaining plan, anti-alienation and transfer requirements. Each joins the rule, its application to the established plan coverage, and this remedy in full.

A working owner may occupy both employer and participant capacities in an ERISA pension plan that covers one or more genuine employees other than the owner and spouse. The owner-only exclusion identifies arrangements outside the employee-plan coverage of Title I; it does not exclude a working owner from participant status once an otherwise covered employee plan exists. Yates's plan covers genuine other employees, so his ownership and control cannot alone sustain the Sixth Circuit's exclusion.

Darden supplies common-law agency as the default construction of an otherwise unhelpful employee definition. It does not require ignoring specific provisions of ERISA addressing working-owner participation. Clackamas likewise rejects ownership as a talisman and requires attention to the particular statute; its ADA classification does not resolve this distinct pension scheme. Section 1108(c)(1) permits a fiduciary to receive a benefit to which that person is entitled as a participant or beneficiary, provided the benefit is computed and paid consistently with the plan as applied to all other participants and beneficiaries. Subsection (c)(3) recognizes fiduciary service alongside service for a party in interest. Subsection (d) imposes special transaction restrictions for owner-employees, with its own defined shareholder-employee category. These provisions recognize dual capacities while regulating their risks; the decision does not find Yates within every tax-defined owner category or approve his transaction. Regulation 2510.3-3(a) identifies its plan-definition purpose, (b) contrasts owner-only Keogh arrangements with those covering common-law employees as well as self-employed individuals, and (c) confines its owner exclusion to the purposes of that section. Reading those provisions together permits dual capacity while preserving the actual nonowner-employee coverage condition. The Government's supporting litigation position is consistent with that construction, but the common ground relies on statutory and regulatory text, not on unrecovered contents or binding force of an advisory opinion.

Shumate then gives effect to an enforceable antialienation restriction protecting a beneficial trust interest under applicable nonbankruptcy law. Participant status supplies only the eligibility issue decided here. On remand, the court must decide the actual qualification and enforceability requirements and the trustee's transfer challenge, including section 547 as applicable, under their independent statutory conditions. Shumate's trust-interest exclusion and the avoidance inquiry perform different work.

## Alternative-path and join check

The strongest contrary construction treats ownership and employer control as categorically inconsistent with employee status. Darden supplies a real default for circular definitions, but its own full-relationship rule rejects a single-factor conclusion, and it cannot displace this statute's affirmative treatment of working owners in existing employee plans. The regulation's owner-only exclusion is fully operative without turning it into a ban on participation in a plan covering other employees. All eight can adopt this ground without a deference holding or a general rule equating every owner with an employee.

No Associate requires a separate writing on the supported formulation. Form and assignment remain for assembly. The remaining plan and transfer work is ordinary application on remand, not a judicial declaration that every pension or transfer question is unresolved law.
