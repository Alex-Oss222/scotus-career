## Questions and lawful alternatives

The saving clause preserves otherwise valid antitrust claims; it creates neither immunity nor an independent duty to supply a rival. The distinct questions are whether this claimant's injury permits its requested antitrust recovery and whether the pleaded conduct is unlawful monopoly maintenance independently of the interconnection mandate. The Second Circuit's free-standing leveraging theory cannot replace monopoly maintenance, or specific intent and dangerous probability for an attempt.

**Conduct path A — reverse the reinstatement.** A complaint that identifies deficient performance of an affirmative access duty created by the telecommunications scheme, together with an exclusionary purpose, does not by those allegations alone establish an independently unlawful refusal to deal. Aspen supplies an example of exclusion through a sacrificed voluntary course of dealing, not an exhaustive factual checklist. Here the supplied record identifies mandated network assistance and an actively enforced technical access regime, not comparable voluntary dealing or a separate anticompetitive restraint. Antitrust supervision of the precise quality of that compelled assistance would impose the same positive dealing duty through a second remedial structure. The saving clause preserves ordinary section 2 liability but does not generate that duty.

Strongest objection: CSU/Xerox rejects conclusively protecting a refusal by invoking another statute, and the complaint alleges purposeful discrimination rather than innocent inefficiency. Answer: neither protected entitlement nor regulatory status is conclusive; the actionable mechanism must still be independent of an asserted right to compel this assistance. Purpose and rival harm alone do not establish it. The regulatory machinery's actual response informs the risks and incremental usefulness of imposing that additional dealing duty; it is not a factual finding that every injury was compensated.

**Conduct path B — vacate and remand for element-specific testing.** CSU and Kodak disfavor categorical disposition when the actual exclusionary mechanism and business explanations require examination. Deliberately degrading competitors' orders could operate as exclusion distinct from inability to furnish perfect access. The substantial counterargument is that labeling imperfect compelled dealing deliberate does not identify the antecedent antitrust duty or harmful mechanism; no general obligation to aid competitors follows. This remand would have to identify what actual allegation makes the conduct independently exclusionary, and could not require discovery merely because the plaintiff alleges discriminatory intent.

**Plaintiff-specific path — dismiss the derivative damages claim.** Under Steamfitters and Associated General Contractors, AT&T is the direct purchaser of the allegedly degraded access and the immediate victim of its operational impairment. The customer's asserted service harm reaches it through that carrier's operations and relationship with customers; the carrier is an available direct enforcer. Duplicative recovery and allocation of this downstream service loss are material. Consumer identity alone cannot bypass this inquiry. The opposing argument is that customers are an intended target of conduct designed to make rival service unattractive, and their loss is therefore a distinct competitive injury. Intent and foreseeability nevertheless do not eliminate the intermediary or establish a sufficiently direct measure of this claimant's loss.

The plaintiff-specific route concerns Clayton section 4. Section 16 prospective relief has distinct threatened-injury and equitable predicates, and section 4 duplication concerns cannot simply be transplanted. The neutral packet does not identify a distinct preserved prospective-relief allegation. If reconciliation supplies one, the threshold-only commitments require an affected neutral scope supplement before that component can be dismissed on their rationale. This is a source-scope limit, not a conclusion that papers are absent or relief was waived.

## Proposition and remedy constraints

Five Associates support dismissal on the independent-conduct ground, with Kennedy's no-exhaustive-checklist limitation. Three prefer the section 4 directness ground and withhold a conduct join. Those grounds are not interchangeable or additive. Every Justice rejects automatic liability from breach of sections 251–252 and automatic immunity from regulation. The saving clause remains effective; CSU's rejection of conclusive exclusivity defenses remains law.

The appropriate disposition on the resolved claim is reversal and remand for dismissal of the reinstated antitrust damages theory under the sufficient ground the final opinion commands. Any separate preserved section 16 component requires the scope check stated above. The carrier's potential claim, unproved actual markets and administrative remedies are not adjudicated by this claimant's disposition. No opinion author or final Court count is inferred.
