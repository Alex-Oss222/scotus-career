## 104. Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.

### Reconciled operative ground

Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support affirmance of the Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical ineligibility and return for district-court discretion. AMD's recognized participation and review rights make it an interested person. The Commission's described first-instance adjudicative function qualifies; evidence for a reasonably contemplated dispositive proceeding need not await pending or imminent adjudication. Section 1782 imposes no categorical foreign-discoverability prerequisite or requirement that analogous domestic litigation permit the same discovery. It authorizes assistance without compelling it.

All six support consideration of the foreign body's receptivity, the character of its proceeding, the availability of evidence from a participant within its reach, actual circumvention of foreign proof-gathering restrictions or policies, and intrusive or burdensome demands. Privileges retain the statute's protection. Mere differences in discovery regimes do not themselves prove circumvention. These are relevant exercises of the statute's discretion, not new cumulative eligibility elements. The application returns for an actual determination of appropriate assistance; eligibility does not establish a present production entitlement. No private-arbitration question is presented.
