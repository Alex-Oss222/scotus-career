## Circulated language and disposition

The proposals are: prompt adjudication of interim protection before use of the disputed procedure, under actual equity without an automatic stay; a specific, properly limited injunction if relief is warranted; and a stay lasting only as long as necessary. **All eight Associates join these directions at the bounded scope below.** Their absence from an earlier formulation establishes no refusal. These directions implement the already adopted remand and independent relief conditions rather than create a new entitlement.

1. **Prompt decision.** The district court must promptly decide a properly presented request for interim protection in time to address the threatened use of the cut-down where the actual schedule permits, applying the preserved merits, exhaustion, equity and statutory requirements. It may grant or deny the request on the legally sufficient record; the instruction creates no automatic evidentiary hearing. A scheduling instruction to the court does not itself prohibit the State from acting pending a decision. Any necessary temporary restraint, including a brief restraint needed to complete adjudication, requires its own lawful equitable basis. Filing or remand alone establishes neither a stay nor priority over the State's judgment-enforcement interest.
2. **Specific and limited relief.** If the actual showing warrants an injunction, its terms must identify the prohibited procedure or conduct and the persons bound, remain within the supported injury and claim, and satisfy the applicable statutory need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness requirements. No execution-wide injunction follows merely from classifying this procedure-specific claim under section 1983. A materially broader request retains the independent channel analysis already required by the reconciliation.
3. **Necessary duration.** Any stay must last only for the period justified by its actual purpose, record and equitable findings, with reconsideration as those premises change. This imposes no invented number of days, guaranteed interval, prohibition on a supported extension, or permanent restraint. The section 3626 preliminary-relief expiration and final-finding conditions remain independently applicable. A stay's permitted duration does not establish its initial entitlement.

The phrase “before the State uses the disputed procedure” is accepted as a direction for meaningful, prompt judicial decision, not a restraint effective until the court happens to rule. All eight withhold the latter automatic-pause construction because it would collapse cognizability into interim relief. That concrete objection is answered by the express no-automatic-stay qualification above, not by denying a join in ordinary prompt adjudication.

## Actual legal support and each join

The effective Randell and Muhammad holdings require ordinary adjudication of a claim compatible with the actual custody judgment, leaving independent defenses and remedies intact. The reconciled necessary-consequence rule applies that law here. Gomez preserves consideration of unjustified delay and the enforcement interest independently of the channel; irreversible harm and a serious claim require consideration but do not alone establish a stay. The existing section 3626 rules require actual remedial fit and distinguish preliminary relief from final orders. These grounds support prompt adjudication and bounded relief together.

All eight retain reversal of the channel dismissal and remand; none supplies a protocol injunction, stay, merits violation, medical finding, newly fixed execution date or automatic hearing through this receipt.
