## Event

**Center for National Security Studies v. U.S. Department of Justice.** Decided January 6, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the D.C. Circuit's judgment, 331 F.3d 918, sustaining withholding of records about persons detained during the investigation following September 11 and their attorneys. The requests include identities, dates, locations and reasons for detention. The questions are whether FOIA supports the categorical withholding and whether the First Amendment or common law independently requires disclosure of the executive compilation.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part and vacated in part, and the case is remanded.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Holdings 1–2 are Stevens's opinion joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Holdings 3–4 have all nine Justices' direct joins.

**1. Exemption 7(A) requires a supported connection between disclosure and enforcement harm.** An agency invoking 7(A) must establish, through sufficiently specific facts or supported functional categories, how disclosure could reasonably be expected to interfere with pending or reasonably anticipated enforcement proceedings. Courts give supported security predictions substantial weight and may protect sensitive explanations, but must independently assess the connection and reasonable segregation; the undifferentiated showing here does not establish the entire withholding category.

Robbins Tire permits predictions about functional classes without proof of completed intimidation or a public explanation about each person. Landano preserves supported categorical inferences while placing the burden on the agency under 7(D); the same proof discipline applies here to 7(A)'s different statutory harm. Klamath requires the actual enacted exemption rather than confidentiality based on governmental purpose alone.

DOJ identifies serious mechanisms of harm: revealing investigative direction, identifying sources, enabling coordination or intimidation, and facilitating misleading evidence. Those mechanisms may justify properly supported categories. They do not alone explain why every withheld immigration-detention, material-witness and attorney item retains the necessary connection. Release or deportation does not automatically end investigative value, and a protected source need not be culpable. The contrary view treats official confirmation of the comprehensive investigative map as sufficient for the whole class. The Court requires support for that class's actual breadth, allowing protected common evidence rather than requiring disclosure of the intelligence being protected. A national-security label by itself cannot establish the statutory inference.

**2. Each remaining protection and reasonably segregable portion requires its own determination.** On remand, DOJ retains the burden on each independently invoked exemption, and the court must order disclosure of reasonably segregable nonexempt information. The following distinct predicates govern:

- Under **7(C)**, cognizable personal-privacy interests are balanced against disclosure's illumination of government conduct; association with a terrorism inquiry can create substantial privacy consequences.
- Under **7(F)**, the agency must support a reasonable expectation of danger to a person's life or physical safety.
- Under **Exemption 3 and Rule 6(e)**, the inquiry is whether disclosure would reveal protected grand-jury matters.
- A **sealing order** protects information within the actual order's coverage; the court must identify that order and its reach.

Landano requires real segregation, and Reporters Committee identifies the relevant public interest as information about governmental activity. The requested compilation can illuminate detention administration, while its private identifying details retain cognizable protection. Each asserted ground must be applied to its own facts. Attorney information requires its own supported connection: counsel neither automatically inherits every client exemption nor loses safety protection because of a public professional role. A material-witness label alone does not establish grand-jury secrecy or a sealing order's reach. The requested wholesale release must await these determinations, with appropriate protection during review.

**3. The First Amendment does not independently require this executive compilation.** A general request for the agency's investigative list does not establish a constitutional right merely because the list identifies detained persons or concerns proceedings to which access may attach. The asserted compilation-access claim therefore fails.

North Jersey Media Group recognizes qualified access to adversarial removal hearings with supported, appropriately limited closure; the executive list is not that adjudication. United Reporting preserves the absence of a universal right to executive information while separately governing speech-selected access conditions. The latter selection is absent here. The distinction preserves the hearing-access holding and FOIA's independent statutory duties.

**4. The asserted common-law route cannot displace FOIA for these agency records.** Congress's disclosure-and-exemption scheme governs access to the executive compilation at issue. Common law cannot override a valid FOIA exemption or create a second balancing route to the same withheld agency records.

Milwaukee v. Illinois supplies the principle that enacted federal law displaces federal common law when Congress has addressed the subject. Nixon v. Warner Communications likewise requires attention to the applicable statutory access route. This claim concerns the executive compilation; access to a particular judicial record presents a different institution and legal channel outside this question.

## Precedent Treatment

- **NLRB v. Robbins Tire & Rubber Co.:** Applied to 7(A)'s use of functional categories and predictive enforcement harm; it does not eliminate the need to support the class actually withheld.
- **United States Department of Justice v. Landano:** Its agency-burden and supported-category reasoning is extended to 7(A), while its distinct 7(D) confidentiality element remains specific to that exemption. Its segregability requirement is applied.
- **Department of the Interior v. Klamath Water Users Protective Association:** Applied to require the particular exemption's enacted conditions despite a substantial governmental confidentiality purpose.
- **Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press:** Applied for the government-conduct public interest and cognizable compilation-related privacy interests under 7(C).
- **North Jersey Media Group v. Ashcroft:** Distinguished as a hearing-access decision; its qualified access and closure safeguards remain effective.
- **Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp.:** Applied to distinguish a general information claim from speech-selected access restrictions.
- **Milwaukee v. Illinois:** Applied to the displacement of common law by the governing enacted disclosure scheme.
- **Nixon v. Warner Communications, Inc.:** Applied for attention to the statutory access route governing the requested records.

## Law After Decision

FOIA permits supported functional categories and protected predictive evidence while retaining agency persuasion, exemption-specific judicial review and reasonably segregable disclosure. The statutory inquiry turns on the actual records and asserted harms. The First Amendment and common law provide no independent route to this executive compilation on the claims presented; the qualified hearing-access rule and ordinary judicial-record channels retain their separate operation.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's statutory rules, independent-access holdings and remand. He would additionally require a prompt production-and-review schedule, a usable public explanation to the extent possible, and enforceable disclosure dates for material found nonexempt. Temporary protection should extend only as necessary to complete that review. His additional direction ensures that vacating the blanket withholding produces a timely, effective adjudication while preserving independently established protections.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The dissent accepts the agency's statutory burden and rejects secrecy based on a security label alone. It finds that the declarations support this compilation as a functional investigative-map category: official confirmation can expose selections, contacts, gaps and sources even when individual roles differ or a person has been released. Robbins Tire permits that prediction. On this record the dissent would sustain 7(A) withholding, making alternative exemptions unnecessary to its result. It joins the rejection of both independent compilation-access claims.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the actual category, residual-exemption, sealing-coverage and segregation determinations under the stated rules, followed by enforceable disclosure of nonexempt reasonably segregable material. The court may use justified protective procedures and temporary protection while resolving the claims. The independent First Amendment and common-law compilation-access claims are concluded.
