## 117. Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, No. 03-218

### Judgment and common legal premises

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg would affirm retention of the preliminary injunction and return the case for final adjudication on the appropriate record. Scalia and Breyer would reverse the determination that the filtering alternative warrants this preliminary injunction and remand for lawful disposition of remaining preserved objections. Neither disposition finally validates or invalidates COPA in every application. Breyer's position requires his national-adult-community construction; Scalia's does not.

All eight apply the following premises. COPA regulates protected adult expression by content even though its enacted commercial-purpose definition limits coverage; that definition is not the constitutional category of commercial advertising. The Government bears the ultimate strict-scrutiny justification burden. A concrete, plausibly effective alternative requires an actual comparative answer; neither a merely imagined alternative nor a demand for perfect effectiveness decides the question. Legislative judgments merit serious consideration but do not conclusively establish necessity. The Court reviews the preliminary injunction through the ordinary discretionary standard while independently reviewing its controlling legal premises and the supported record. The 2002 Ashcroft decision forecloses treating community standards alone as automatic facial invalidity and requires consideration of the full statute.

Integrated review includes the knowing conduct and character requirements, commercial business definition, under-seventeen audience, whole-material and conjunctive harmful-material elements, objective serious-value inquiry, provider and intermediary exclusions, alternative good-faith access defenses, distinct good-faith civil protection, and the statutory information-protection rule and disclosure exceptions. The credit/debit, adult-code or identification-number, digital-certificate, and other reasonable technologically feasible defenses are alternatives; their legal availability is not a factual conclusion that every adult has an affordable, anonymous and effective way to obtain access. The disclosure rule permits necessary communication, legitimate related business activity, and court-authorized disclosure; it does not promise absolute anonymity. No Justice's commitment depends on omitting those qualifications or rewriting the offense.

### Material alternative paths and their limits

**Retain preliminary protection.** Reno protects adult access against network-wide criminal restrictions and requires workable defenses. Playboy requires a supported comparative justification once a concrete lesser restraint is identified. The existing record supplies user-side filtering as such an alternative: it can target the minor's use, reach foreign-origin and non-Web material outside COPA, and avoid imposing verification costs and identity disclosures on every lawful adult reader and covered publisher. Filters' imperfections and dependence on parental action are serious comparative objections, but do not themselves prove that COPA's incremental protection warrants its demonstrated adult burdens. The alternative need not reproduce every feature of COPA or prevent every exposure. Retention rests on the Government's present failure of justification, not an assumption that filtering always works, that parents always activate it, or that COPA has no protective value.

**Remove this basis for preliminary protection.** COPA's default protection for children whose parents do not activate filters, together with its restricted harmful-to-minors category and adult-access defenses, can be considered an adequately justified incremental protection. The strongest objection is that this merely redescribes a benefit and never proves why the broader adult burden is necessary, particularly when domestic publisher controls miss foreign material and filtering reaches it. Scalia and Breyer conclude that the integrated statute and the actual activation difference supply that comparative justification at the preliminary stage. They do not move the burden to the speakers, assume filters universally fail, or import conclusive deference. Their disagreement is over the practical weight of the supported protection difference and the burden after the statutory limits are applied.

ALA supplies neither a categorical endorsement of filters nor an exemption for age checks. Its statutory disabling permission is distinct from its five-Justice effective-adult-access rule, and both concern the library service and funding structure. A lawful library adult request is not proof that publisher age verification is equally unburdensome. Conversely, that decision shows why the actual means of obtaining adult access matter. No commitment transfers the LSTA exception to E-rate, turns permission into a universal grant obligation, or treats a library's voluntary service selection as equivalent to a criminal command directed to private publishers.

## 118. Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain, Nos. 03-339 and 03-485

### Judgment and component separation

All eight associate Justices would reverse the Ninth Circuit insofar as it sustains the ATS damages judgment against Sosa and permits the asserted FTCA claim against the United States. The two components have separate sufficient grounds. Six Justices—Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—reject the asserted new received action because the proposed rule does not establish a sufficiently specific and accepted international prohibition supporting domestic damages for this less-than-one-day detention followed by lawful custody merely because the initial arrest lacked domestic legal authority. Scalia and Thomas reject creation of this additional domestic action on their preserved source-of-authority ground. They also agree that the offered declarations and treaty do not themselves supply the requested domestic action; their agreement is not a join in a general judicial license to recognize additional norms.

All eight independently conclude that section 2680(k) bars this FTCA claim for detention in Mexico notwithstanding the alleged domestic planning and direction of that same injury. The action against the United States must be dismissed at that scope. The ATS component fails on the action/norm inquiry, not on a retrospective withdrawal of district-court jurisdiction over every substantial international-tort allegation.

### Governing law and tested alternatives

**ATS route preserving the adopted actions.** Abebe-Jira affirmatively recognizes a received domestic action for deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture: severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation. Kadic/Doe additionally recognizes personally perpetrated, planned or directed genocide with its specific group-destruction intent and defined acts, and the pleaded core war crimes with armed-conflict, protected-victim, prohibited-act and conflict-connection predicates. Those actions remain law with their separate responsibility, causation and remedy requirements. Neither the statutory forum nor the seriousness of an allegation supplies all of those elements, a sovereign waiver or an action for every international wrong.

An action for the present detention could be recognized only through its own sufficiently determinate international rule and supported domestic enforcement basis. The lower court's strongest position relies on the categorical prohibitions against arbitrary arrest or detention in article 9 of the Universal Declaration and ICCPR, the illegality of the initial capture, and the absence of an express minimum duration in those instruments. On that approach, even brief detention without legal authority can be an actionable personal wrong rather than a political grievance. The strongest objection is not that a one-day detention is legally harmless. It is that the asserted transformation of any detention unauthorized by domestic law into an internationally actionable tort lacks the particular specificity and acceptance established for the adopted torture, genocide and core-war-crime actions. The Restatement's prolonged-detention formulation, the declarations' nonbinding character, and the ICCPR's non-self-executing status matter at this step. A non-self-executing instrument can inform evidence of an international norm; it does not thereby create an operative domestic damages action.

The six received-action adherents choose the confined rejection. They do not hold that only eighteenth-century labels can ever support an action, establish a universal minimum detention duration, foreclose every independently defined arbitrary-detention claim, or decide that abduction is lawful. The present sources and asserted broad norm are insufficient for this action. Scalia and Thomas would additionally refuse an extension beyond the actions already adopted unless a sufficient enacted source authorizes the new civil liability. Their position respects those precedents as existing law rather than using the word jurisdictional to erase them.

**FTCA domestic-planning route.** Sections 1346(b)(1) and 2680(k) perform different work. Identifying allegedly wrongful planning in the United States may identify conduct, an actor and a proposed local-law basis; it does not itself answer the exception for any claim arising in a foreign country. The strongest argument for the headquarters theory invokes the FTCA's act-or-omission location and Richards's place-of-law approach: genuinely domestic wrongdoing can be governed by domestic law, so the foreign-law concern may be absent. The opposing construction treats the claim for the same seizure and detention in Mexico as a foreign-country claim despite its domestic causal direction. It preserves the enacted exception rather than allowing every overseas operational injury to become domestic through a planning allegation. All eight select that latter construction for this claim. They do not treat Smith as having already resolved every mixed-location tort or manufacture a distinct injury in the United States.

Smith applies the foreign-country exception to wholly Antarctic acts and rejects residence as a substitute place-of-law source. It supports attention to the enacted waiver and exception rather than a claimant-selected law, but its factual limits remain. Mexico also is indisputably foreign sovereign territory, which distinguishes the precise premise of Stevens's Smith dissent. The law-enforcement proviso in section 2680(h) is an exception to the intentional-tort exclusion; it is not a repeal of section 2680(k). The ATS recognized actions likewise create no FTCA consent. The prior criminal Alvarez-Machain holding allows trial because the extradition treaty lacks the asserted bar; Ker–Frisbie's trial-authority rule neither authorizes capture nor decides civil liability.

## 119. Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran, No. 03-1147

### Judgment and controlling distinctions available for assembly

All eight associate Justices would affirm dismissal on the surviving Algiers Accords bar. The case-specific amendment removes the designated foreign-sovereign-immunity obstacle; it does not also repeal the independent claim-settlement bar, create a substantive cause of action, establish liability or direct a damages award. The technical case-designation correction does not add any of those consequences. The United States may protect its own agreement-related interest, and Iran's nonappearance does not forfeit that interest or establish the claim under section 1608(e).

The unresolved-damages liability default did not finally dispose of this private action through the ordinary judicial process. Consequently, Plaut's prohibition on a legislative command to reopen a completed private judgment does not supply a ground for invalidating these amendments. A targeted change of governing law in a pending case is distinct from legislation prescribing findings or liability under unchanged law. These commitments affirm on statutory grounds and do not decide every targeted-law objection or the reserved Flatow cause-of-action question. The reported Rule 60(b) treatment below is preserved rather than silently replaced with a different procedural theory.

### Enacted law and alternative paths

**Affirm on the independent bar.** The Accords' covered-claim preclusion and their implementing measures remain operative. The explicit case reference answers which acts receive the immunity exception; it does not answer every other obstacle to recovery.

**Reverse for an implied broader statutory displacement.** Petitioners' strongest position is that Congress deliberately selected this suit, knew the Government's objection, and intended meaningful recovery rather than an immunity change with no ultimate benefit. The explanatory statements concerning preservation of the judgment and the corrective amendment strengthen that contextual inference. On that reading, the amendment would displace the Accords for these claims and require further independent adjudication. Its decisive weakness is the mismatch between that comprehensive effect and the words Congress enacted: removal of one barrier is a real legal change even if another defeats relief. Explanatory statements cannot enact the omitted direction or adjudicate liability. All eight therefore reject the broader reading. Their agreement does not impose a universal magic-words requirement or hold that Congress lacks power to enact clearer domestic displacement.

**Article III alternative.** Robertson permits an actual change in substantive law governing pending litigation, even in appropriations legislation, while leaving application to the courts. Plaut protects completed private judgments against the specified legislative reopening command; Miller distinguishes prospective changed law and preserved adjudication from legislative findings. The present unresolved damages and continuing judicial proceedings do not satisfy Plaut's completed-judgment predicate. If the plaintiffs' construction merely substitutes a new legal rule, targeted identification does not alone establish legislative adjudication. If unenacted statements are treated as commands to preserve liability regardless of surviving law or proof, they both lack statutory force and disregard the independent judicial function. This distinction supports the narrow statutory resolution; it is not a separate declaration that these statutes are unconstitutional.
