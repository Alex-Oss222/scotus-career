## A. Habeas jurisdiction: supported alternatives and individual basis

**Proposition accepted by all eight:** Section 1226(e) does not withdraw independently proper section 2241 jurisdiction over this substantial constitutional and statutory challenge to Congress's mandatory custody framework. Its first sentence concerns discretionary judgment regarding application; its second protects an Attorney General action or decision under the section concerning detention, release, bond or parole. Read together, they do not clearly foreclose deciding whether the legal framework itself authorizes the contested form of custody. This construction supplies neither review of a lawful discretionary bond decision nor retrial of deportability facts. It reaches effective relief if legal authority is absent.

**Competing path:** The second sentence's broad prohibition on setting aside any detention action could be read to encompass the relief sought, even if the attack is framed as one on the statute. Its best argument is that a court cannot avoid an express action-based bar by renaming the challenge. The response is textual and channel-specific: a challenge to a mandatory congressional rule is not review of the Attorney General's protected judgment, and the combined sentences do not clearly eliminate independent review of that rule's legal validity. The retained channel is not inferred from the Government's concession. No magic reference to section 2241 is required for repeal; the requisite clarity is absent for this object of review. Because that construction resolves the statutory threshold, no Suspension Clause holding is supplied.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly supported the surviving legal-authority channel in Kolster, Goncalves, Calcano-Martinez, St. Cyr and Zadvydas. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas joined Goncalves's actual-channel holding, and applied the governing channel in St. Cyr and Zadvydas while preserving their earlier objections to Calcano's construction. Their commitment here rests on the statutory-framework distinction and those operative precedents, not an assumption that their Calcano disagreement disappeared. Their join barrier is an opinion claiming an unqualified constitutional right to review every detention decision or requiring an express section-number repeal. This new provision's second sentence is the strongest basis for a contrary jurisdiction position and must receive independent reconciliation; unanimity is not inferred merely from their earlier precedent-following joins.

## B. Pre-order coverage and Breyer's statutory alternative

All eight reject Kim's final-order-only construction. Section 1226 governs detention pending the removal decision, while section 1231 supplies the different final-order and removal-period scheme. Reading the phrase concerning deportability to require an already final order would prevent section 1226(c) from performing its evident pre-order function. Constitutional avoidance cannot erase that structure. This conclusion does not decide that either amended charge actually places Kim in the covered category.

**Breyer's proposed rule:** A resident who raises a substantial, nondilatory challenge to whether his convictions place him in section 1226(c) must receive bond consideration rather than be treated as conclusively included while that substantial question is unresolved. A substantial question is a genuinely nonfrivolous legal or factual challenge to a mandatory-custody predicate, not mere insistence on litigating removal, a discretionary plea for relief after covered removability is accepted, or a claim presented to obtain delay. Release may be conditioned on the resident establishing by clear and convincing evidence that he is unlikely to flee or endanger others. The substantial-question requirement and risk showing perform different work; neither automatically grants release. The proposed heightened detainee allocation is confined to this construction and is not a universal civil-detention burden.

The statutory basis is the difference between actual statutory deportability and a charge that remains substantially contested, read within the pre-order scheme rather than converted into a final-order requirement. National Center's individual-status protection and Breyer's Zadvydas purpose-bound construction support this distinction. The appeal-release analogy in section 3143(b), advanced and accepted as a possible resolution in the supplied argument account, supplies an administrable model that respects Congress's presumptive custody judgment while preserving meaningful adjudication. It is an analogy, not direct application of criminal bail law to immigration custody.

**Strongest contrary path:** Congress expressly supplied only the witness-protection release exception, and Joseph already adjudicates whether the charge is substantially unlikely to succeed. It is therefore argued that a court adds an exception by permitting bond on a merely substantial defense. Breyer's response is that the threshold identifies the individuals for whom mandatory classification may operate before adjudication; it does not add an equitable exception for someone conclusively within that classification. A process demanding that the alien show the Government is substantially unlikely to succeed can deny ordinary bond consideration even though a substantial, nondilatory category dispute remains. The proposed construction rejects that use of Joseph for this purpose without finally deciding removal.

**Remedy under this path:** Vacate the categorical constitutional judgment and remand for the lower court to obtain the proper agency treatment of both live charges and apply the substantial-question standard. Corona-Sanchez supplies a concrete reason to examine the original charge, but does not answer the added burglary and dual-offense grounds. Ventura bars the Court from supplying the missing classification findings. Kim's present release remains effective pending that determination. If the standard entitles him to consideration and continued custody is sought, the decisionmaker must consider release under the stated flight-and-danger allocation. If he has no qualifying substantial question, the categorical statute governs subject to any independently established constitutional defect. No final removal decision is made by this remand.

**Other Associates' precisely bounded response:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg could join this statutory disposition and rule as a sufficient, avoidance-based resolution of Kim's entitlement if it actually ensures meaningful bond consideration when the substantial-question condition is found and preserves his release pending adjudication. Each retains the broader constitutional hearing position and does not adopt the converse proposition that every resident lacking such a question may constitutionally be confined without individualized review. Their National Center and Zadvydas joins support this actual statutory alternative; it is not agreement with categorical constitutional validity. O'Connor and Kennedy agree that category objections require meaningful adjudication, but do not presently join the proposed substantial-question-to-bail construction: the distinction between testing the charge and suspending Congress's custody rule remains a material objection. Scalia and Thomas reject the proposed implied bail pathway and the imported heightened allocation; they require application of the enacted pre-order classification regime and its proper challenge mechanisms. ### Proposed common propositions

**1. Past travel of materials does not supply this constitutional connection by itself.** The fact that a camera, recording medium or other production material once crossed state lines does not independently authorize Congress to punish the ensuing wholly intrastate production. It may satisfy the connection Congress actually enacted, but statutory satisfaction and constitutional authority are separate. An equipment-only ground would connect ordinary local conduct to federal power through the provenance of ubiquitous tools, without identifying the required commerce mechanism.

**2. The actual-abuse-image production class has the necessary relation to the interstate prohibition.** The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit Congress to include the local production of actual-child-abuse recordings in the identified interstate market prohibition because the produced images constitute the very supply that the regime excludes from interstate circulation. Their reproducibility, concealability and capacity to supply the prohibited demand make a production exception a material avenue around that prohibition. Congress need not wait for a producer's individual sale or interstate transmission before addressing the creation of that supply. This is a class-and-regime connection, not a conclusion that every local offense is commercial or every valuable object may be federally regulated.

**3. The mechanism is supported on this supplied record and fits this application.** The record identifies actual recordings, actual children and a national prohibition of the corresponding image market. Local production creates additional copies or original material capable of reproduction within that same prohibited supply; excluding the productive class would leave the source of replacement and additional supply outside the restriction. The Government's anti-evasion explanation is materially supported by the characteristics of the regulated product and the operation of the identified regime. This conclusion is a legal inference from the supplied production facts and regime, not a new empirical statistic, a finding that Holston personally distributed an image or an assumption that statutory comprehensiveness proves necessity. The enacted production application reasonably fits that supported supply mechanism.

**4. The independent statutory condition remains in place.** The constitutional rationale does not remove the materials-movement requirement used in this prosecution, replace it with an uncharged sale element or add a defendant-specific interstate-impact requirement. The reserved constitutional challenge fails on the established application; the conditional plea and conviction therefore remain effective.

## Enhanced alternative-path test

### Equipment-only validity versus a constitutionally identifiable mechanism

The equipment-only path's strongest argument is that Congress wrote a concrete nexus and the plea satisfies it; the offense thus differs from the original statute in Lopez. That argument establishes a statutory distinction, but it fails as a sufficient constitutional ground under Griffin/Langley. A nexus must do actual constitutional work, and the former travel of commonplace inputs does not identify why the subsequent production class falls within interstate commerce. The same inputs could be used in local conduct with no eligible relationship to a valid federal regime. All eight reject the path on this ground. They do not invalidate the statutory nexus or deny that actual movement proves it.

### Supported necessary inclusion versus remand or invalidation for an unsupported mechanism

The supported-inclusion path identifies a valid interstate object—the prohibited market in images made through actual child exploitation—and the local conduct creating the same reproducible supply. A production exemption would permit source creation outside the national supply restriction even though the resulting material can be copied and circulated without retaining a visible marker of its local origin. The Court can recognize that relationship from the product and scheme actually presented. It need not find that every local producer will distribute, because the class's inclusion addresses a mechanism by which the interstate prohibition could otherwise be evaded or materially impaired.

The strongest challenge is that this is only marketability coupled with a prediction: no sale or intended transfer by Holston is shown, and Home Builders forbids treating a comprehensive law as its own proof of necessity. The answer must preserve that boundary. The necessary connection is not merely that an image could command money, that abuse has national social costs or that Congress wishes to eliminate it everywhere. The regulated act creates the very reproducible image stock whose interstate circulation the identified regime prohibits. The class is defined around production of that supply, rather than around an unrelated local place or use of a potentially marketable object. Hale and Kirk treat acquisition, creation, retention and circulation relationships as legitimate when the same prohibited stock is involved; they do not require an additional transaction once the class-and-regime connection is established. The actual-recording facts and supplied product characteristics support that connection here.

The competing remand path would be appropriate if the record identified only an abstract market, only equipment provenance or a different product than the interstate regime reaches. Home Builders needed no such inquiry because the taking prohibition's function within the Act's valid interstate scheme appeared from the statute's own operation. Here the material production and product identity are supplied, and the proposed mechanism is directly grounded in them. No additional finding of Holston's commercial motive, individual sale or exact contribution to national demand is legally necessary. Lack of a retrieved complete legislative file does not establish that the Government lacked support or that this litigant wins by default. No Justice votes to remand solely to obtain a preferred original research document.

The prosecution's description of the market alone would not sustain every future product prohibition. This application requires the actual-abuse-image production class, the identified valid interstate prohibition and the supported relationship between local creation and replacement or additional supply. A broad sentence that all conduct involving reproducible things is regulable would lose the recorded joins.

## Precedent treatment and law proposed

- Lopez remains the controlling connection-before-magnitude and enacted-class rule. This decision applies its necessary-inclusion route and rejects an equipment-provenance substitute; it does not narrow Lopez to statutes lacking jurisdictional words.
- Hale supplies the integrated-regime anti-evasion and material-impairment rule. The reasoning is applied to the distinct actual-abuse-image supply, with the connection independently identified; its machinegun facts and exceptions do not become facts or statutory exceptions here.
- Kirk supplies the absence of a universal individual sale or interstate-transaction prerequisite where local creation and retention are integrated with prohibited stock. Its express warning against automatic treatment of all local manufacture remains effective.
- Home Builders supplies the integral-means necessary-inclusion rule, the Government's identification of the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship, independent judicial determination of those boundaries and legislative latitude after the relationship is identified. Its wildlife application rests on the Act's integrated scheme; the relationship here rests on the directly supplied product-and-source relationship, not on general deference.
- Hiley and Franklyn/Gonzalez demonstrate the necessary-inclusion method in a distinct supply/recipient arrangement. They support the method, not a claim that firearm recipients and image producers are the same statutory class.
- Griffin/Langley controls the separation between the enacted historical-movement nexus and the independent constitutional rationale; proof of one neither supplies nor eliminates the other.

The proposed doctrinal addition is application of the established necessary-inclusion rule to local production of actual-abuse recordings within the identified interstate image-market prohibition. It supplies neither a new Commerce category nor a presumption that every comprehensive federal statute may reach all related local conduct. The conviction is affirmed because this class has the fitted mechanism, not because every recording tool previously traveled interstate.
