Law slice: `../entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK5_CASE62.md`; the complete Banks public handoff supplements Ramirez from December 2, 2003. All eight Associates participate. Research cutoff: March 30, 2004. No target comparator or Stone supplement was received. No author is assigned.

## Common sufficient ground

Reverse suppression and remand for ordinary criminal proceedings. At an actual international border, customs officers may remove, disassemble and reassemble a vehicle's fuel tank to inspect for contraband without individualized suspicion when the inspection entails the reversible, nondestructive interference shown here. Border authority rests on inspection of entering persons and effects; the mechanical complexity of this vehicle inspection does not make it equivalent to the bodily detention in Montoya de Hernandez. Ramsey supplies the border-search justification. Ramirez and Banks preserve independent review of the means and extent of property interference and do not authorize gratuitous damage simply because a search is lawful. Approximately one hour including the wait, without established serious damage or destruction, does not make this inspection unreasonable.

The Government must establish the border and inspection predicates; contraband discovered afterward supplies no retrospective justification. This record is resolved without a new finding of reasonable suspicion, the theory the Government declined below. Bodily searches retain their distinct privacy and dignity rules. A destructive search or materially different detention depends on its own facts and governing reasonableness rule; the present holding adopts no categorical immunity for all vehicle disassembly.

## Alternative-path test

The strongest suspicion-required path treats fuel-tank removal as a distinct possessory intrusion with risk of mechanical harm and delay. Its objection to the border path is that effects do not lose constitutional protection on arrival. All eight accept that objection's premise and reject its proposed consequence: independent reasonableness of the actual manner protects the possessory interest, while a speculative accident risk cannot establish an extra suspicion requirement for this reversible inspection. The contrary path would import the bodily-intrusion concern of Montoya into mechanical vehicle inspection without a comparable privacy or dignity invasion.

The strongest objection to unrestricted border power is substantial damage or delay unrelated to inspection. All eight preserve the actual-manner inquiry; none joins unrestricted destruction, an arbitrary detention rule, or suspicion supplied by hindsight. Suppression on the categorical fuel-tank rule is reversed. Independent trial issues retain their proper procedural treatment. No Associate needs a separate writing if the common explanation carries this distinction.
