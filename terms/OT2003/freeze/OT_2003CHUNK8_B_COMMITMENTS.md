## 111. Rumsfeld v. Padilla — No. 03-1027

### Supported paths and component boundaries

**P1 — ordinary physical-custody respondent and forum.** The later petition challenges present military confinement at a known South Carolina brig. The brig commander is the immediate custodian; the ordinary forum is the district able to reach that custodian. The earlier New York material-witness proceeding is not the petition challenging this custody. Endo's continuing-jurisdiction holding consequently does not itself preserve New York jurisdiction. A merits judgment entered through an unavailable forum must be set aside without adjudicating the detention's legality. The strongest contrary argument is that the Secretary's specific central control over the change from judicial to military detention makes the immediate jailer an inadequate description of the actual restraint. P1 answers that the writ can operate effectively against the actual brig custodian and that central supervision alone supplies no limiting principle for national habeas forum selection.

**P2 — confined principal-custodian exception.** In this exceptional conversion of a named citizen's existing judicial custody to personally directed military custody, the official responsible for that conversion and empowered to terminate it can count as the custodian, where that official is actually reachable. The exception rests on this specific custody transaction and respondent relationship, not rank alone or a bad-faith finding. Braden's functional attention to the particular restraint and Endo's effective-response principle support the inference; neither already holds it. The strongest contrary argument is the post-transfer filing and the availability of an ordinary South Carolina forum. P2 must therefore stand as an expressly adopted exception, not an assertion that a preexisting petition preserved jurisdiction.

**Merits if reached.** Section 4001(a)'s Act-of-Congress requirement applies to citizen military imprisonment; subsection (b)'s prison-management exception does not narrow it. Authorization of necessary and appropriate force against September 11-related actors does not, without the supported belligerent predicate, authorize this citizen's military detention following a domestic civilian arrest merely because the Executive designates him an enemy combatant. The packet supplies no battlefield capture or adjudicated unlawful-belligerent status comparable to Quirin. The narrow no-authority position requires termination of this military custody, with a reasonable lower-court implementation opportunity for a separately lawful criminal or other custody basis. It does not decide that every person arrested domestically is necessarily a civilian or exempt from the law of war.

**Transfer.** Section 1631 is not a merits remedy. A qualifying court must find a want of jurisdiction, identify a qualifying destination in which the action could have been brought at filing, and determine that transfer serves the interest of justice; then the statute directs transfer with the original filing date. Naming the brig commander and the South Carolina confinement supplies a possible destination, but not a completed interest-of-justice ruling or an answer to whether a particular characterization of the defect falls within section 1631. An instruction preserving lawful transfer consideration does not itself order transfer.

## 112. Rasul v. Bush; Al Odah v. United States — Nos. 03-334 and 03-343

### Supported paths and component boundaries

**R1 — statutory review at this controlled base.** Section 2241(c) includes detention under United States authority without a citizenship restriction. Read with subsection (a), a court able to reach the responsible official may adjudicate these statutory petitions at a base under complete, continuing United States jurisdiction and control, despite Cuba's retained ultimate sovereignty. Braden rejects a universal prisoner-presence prerequisite; the lease and absence of a domestic district prevent mechanically transplanting a domestic district-of-confinement rule. Eisentrager's alien-enemy military convictions in foreign occupied territory differ materially from these untried, disputed-status detainees. The strongest counterargument is that Eisentrager also spoke broadly about statutory territorial reach and Braden decided a domestic detainer. R1 treats the base's complete control and reachable respondent as the statutory answer; it need not claim that Braden silently overruled every part of Eisentrager.

**R2 — foreign-sovereignty limit.** Eisentrager excludes these overseas alien-prisoner petitions absent a new statutory extension; control under a lease does not establish United States sovereignty. Braden changes domestic forum allocation, not the class of persons held abroad who can invoke the writ. The strongest counterargument is the absence of a citizenship/geographic limitation in section 2241(c), complete control of the base, and the lack of any military adjudication establishing enemy status. R2 must rest on the territorial construction, not on a finding that the allegations of innocence are false. Its constitutional alternative is the view that these foreign nationals, captured and confined outside sovereign United States territory without an established voluntary domestic connection, have not shown an independent constitutional habeas entitlement under Eisentrager. That is a reached alternative ground, not an assumed disappearance of constitutional arguments.

**Civil branch.** A conclusion that the prisoners can invoke section 2241 does not itself decide Al Odah's pleaded sections 1331/1350 and APA claims. Conversely, a foreign-custody habeas limit does not by itself rewrite those statutes. S Abebe-Jira and Kadic reject a universal incapacity of nonresident aliens to bring defined claims, while keeping jurisdiction, a domestic action, official responsibility, sovereign immunity, reviewability and relief distinct. The packet supports setting aside a categorical alien-location dismissal and remanding for those actual claim-specific inquiries; it cannot establish a universal detention-damages action or sovereign-immunity waiver. A dissent may distinguish these foreign-military-restraint claims from the defined private-defendant torture and genocide actions, but must say so.

## 113. Hamdi v. Rumsfeld — No. 03-6696

### Separate propositions and alternative paths

**H-A: authorization.** One supported path reads the AUMF's necessary-and-appropriate-force language in its received law-of-war setting to authorize detention of an actual member of opposing Taliban forces captured in the Afghanistan hostilities, including a citizen, to prevent return to those hostilities. That enactment can satisfy section 4001(a) without using the word detention. Quirin supplies the belligerent/civilian distinction and the compatibility of citizenship with enemy-belligerent status. The strongest objection is that section 4001(a) requires more definite congressional authorization for citizen imprisonment and the AUMF does not name detention, citizens or its duration. The contrary statutory path accepts that objection under Endo's liberty-sensitive construction and requires release from this military custody absent an independently lawful basis. Section 4001(b)'s management exception supplies no answer to either path.

**H-C: distinct constitutional trial path.** A citizen detained domestically for alleged adherence to the enemy cannot be held indefinitely on the Executive's allegation when ordinary courts remain available and the writ has not lawfully been suspended. On this view ordinary prosecution is the lawful course; Milligan and the suspension structure cannot be replaced by a newly balanced civil hearing. The strongest counterargument is that actual battlefield belligerents may be captured and held without criminal trial, including the citizen recognized in Quirin. The response distinguishes Quirin's conceded belligerent facts and authorized military prosecution from indefinite domestic confinement of a citizen whose status is contested. This is a distinct constitutional position, not a premise that Quirin never involved a citizen or that every civilian arrest proves civilian status.

**H-P: process if H-A is accepted.** For continued domestic custody, the citizen must receive notice sufficient to understand the factual basis of the enemy-force classification and a meaningful opportunity, with counsel, to present a contrary account to a neutral decisionmaker able to determine the factual predicate and afford effective relief. Section 2243's return and fact-determination provisions and Mathews's liberty, error and governmental-interest inquiry support that minimum. Initial battlefield operations do not require a trial at capture. A sworn declaration can be evidence without being an adjudication; neither the executive label nor the father's counteraccount is conclusive. A functioning neutral military status tribunal can perform the initial determination if its actual procedures satisfy this minimum and judicial review remains effective. It is not deemed sufficient merely because it is military.

**H-E: evidence and allocation.** Three different claims must stay separate: (1) the Government must identify the actual legal category and supply credible evidence supporting membership; (2) demonstrated battlefield or security constraints can justify hearsay, protected-source handling and appropriate rebuttable evidentiary inferences, provided a real opportunity to answer remains; (3) a fixed ultimate burden of persuasion, such as a detainee burden, governmental preponderance, or governmental clear-and-convincing proof. H-P and the first two propositions do not decide the third. S Zadvydas and C Demore supply no general burden for battlefield classification. A presumption based only on the litigation label or the declaration's existence would eliminate rather than implement the required contest.

The strongest objection to accommodation is that reliance on hearsay and burden shifting may make the imprisoned citizen disprove inaccessible assertions. The answer available to H-P supporters is an actually neutral assessment of evidentiary reliability, disclosed substance adequate to answer, counsel and an effective release remedy if qualifying status is not established. The strongest objection to a fixed government clear-and-convincing rule is that Salerno and Addington address different regimes and do not settle war-status adjudication. The record supports remand under a genuine-contest rule without pretending that a precise universal quantum has already been agreed.

**H-D: purpose and duration.** The narrow H-A authority is incapacitation of an actual opposing fighter during the relevant active hostilities, not confinement solely to interrogate or permanent imprisonment by reference to an undefined worldwide threat. The purpose limit follows from the scope of the authorized force, with S Zadvydas supplying an analogy about actual statutory purpose rather than its immigration calendar. Whether these hostilities have ended, the custody has lost that relationship, or an independent authority exists requires the actual record; the packet supplies none of those findings. Remand must allow a properly presented current-authority challenge. No automatic release date is invented.

## 114. Missouri v. Seibert — No. 02-1371

### Supported alternatives

**S1 — objective effectiveness.** Later warnings and waiver must actually communicate the choice protected by Miranda in the whole interrogation setting. Where officers first obtain a detailed confession, briefly interrupt substantially continuous questioning, then use the same interrogator and the first admissions to obtain a repetition, the later recitation does not supply an effective choice. The failure here follows from the established continuity, overlap and use of prior admissions; proof of subjective intent is not an indispensable legal element. Time and place, personnel, overlap, prior completeness and treatment of the second interview as a continuation are pertinent circumstances, not a mandatory numerical checklist. **S2 — deliberate circumvention with cure.** Retain ordinary Elstad treatment of inadvertent earlier omissions, but exclude the repeated statement when police deliberately conduct a coordinated two-stage interrogation to defeat the warning safeguard unless an effective intervening cure restores the suspect's informed choice. An actually substantial break or an effective explanation distinguishing the legal significance of the earlier and later statements can matter; neither is found here. The established deliberate method satisfies the trigger without a new intent inference. The strongest objection is that two equally ineffective warning sequences should not differ solely with officer intent. S2 answers that it identifies a confined intentional evasion of a settled safeguard while leaving ordinary warning-and-waiver analysis to govern other encounters; it does not authorize ineffective warnings in inadvertent cases.

The strongest objection is that the proven coordinated tactic creates precisely that defect despite a signed form. S3 rejects making an officer's intention or a generalized continuation presumption dispositive; it requires a supported determination about the subsequent waiver. ## 115. United States v. Patane — No. 02-1183

### Supported alternatives and independent propositions

**P-G: confined physical-fruit rule.** A voluntary custodial answer obtained without complete Miranda warnings is excluded from the prosecution's case in chief, but incomplete warnings alone do not require suppression of a nontestimonial physical object discovered through that answer. Tucker and Elstad preserve differentiated consequences, and S Dickerson expressly left the general fruit question open. Reversing the warning-only exclusion permits the pistol's use if no independent admissibility defect applies; it does not establish a complete waiver from Patane's interruption.

**P-F: warning-enforcement derivative remedy.** When the prosecution uses a suspect's unwarned custodial answer to locate physical evidence, exclusion of that direct evidentiary product is necessary to preserve the constitutional warning safeguard and prevent officers from obtaining the full investigative benefit while forgoing only the words. A physical object's nontestimonial character does not eliminate the causal use of compelled testimonial disclosure protected by the safeguard. The strongest objection is that Tucker and Elstad permit some derivative use and a rule of automatic physical exclusion extends beyond the core retained in Dickerson. P-F distinguishes the earlier exceptions and the later effective waiver in Elstad rather than asserting that Dickerson already overruled them.

**Duty characterization.** One rationale for P-G treats the Self-Incrimination Clause as a criminal-case prohibition and warning omission itself as insufficient to complete that constitutional violation. Another accepts Miranda's constitutional warning obligation and reaches P-G solely because this additional suppression remedy is unnecessary for voluntary nontestimonial physical fruits. These are distinct propositions. S Chavez adopted no majority rationale resolving the earlier no-use damages theory and therefore supplies neither rationale as governing law. Any opinion claiming a broader duty rule needs its own actual joins.

**Limits.** The reviewed voluntariness premise is accepted. The record supplies no deliberate evasion finding, no actual coercion and no new Fourth Amendment ground; none is invented. The circuit's rejection of the original probable-cause suppression ground is part of the posture. A decision confined to the warning-fruit issue does not reopen that ruling by implication. The separate same-date Seibert matter supplies no authority here.
