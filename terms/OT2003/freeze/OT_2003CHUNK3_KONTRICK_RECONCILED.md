## Rule and disposition

Affirm. Bankruptcy Rules 4004 and 9006 establish a claim-processing deadline for objections to discharge and restrict enlargement. They do not withdraw the subject-matter jurisdiction Congress conferred over bankruptcy and discharge proceedings. A debtor who litigates the objection through an adverse merits determination before invoking that deadline forfeits the defense. Calling the deadline mandatory does not make it jurisdictional or perpetually available.

Sections 1334 and 157(b)(2)(J) place the proceeding within the bankruptcy court's statutory competence. Section 2075 and Rule 9030 keep procedural rules from enlarging or restricting jurisdiction. Rule 4004's ordinary sixty-day period and timely-extension requirements continue to operate, with Rule 9006(b)(3) restricting enlargement. Their strict terms govern the defense when available; they do not prescribe a subject-matter bar. Carlisle's mandatory motion restriction expressly distinguished jurisdiction and reserved forfeiture, while Henderson requires attention to the location and function of the command rather than loose jurisdictional terminology.

Kontrick's strongest argument is that a rule allowing extension only under stated conditions cannot be lost through consent or litigation conduct. That confuses the court's authority to grant an extension over a timely objection with the litigant's ability to insist on a procedural defense after merits adjudication. This judgment does not grant an extension or invoke equitable tolling. The debtor answered the family-account theory, contested summary judgment and waited until after losing to raise timeliness.

The alternative jurisdictional path would require a congressional jurisdictional limitation the relevant statutes do not contain. The alternative nonjurisdictional-but-unforfeitable path would turn a defense directed at orderly assertion of discharge objections into a postjudgment escape from merits litigation without textual support for that consequence. Neither is justified. Questions about equitable exceptions, relation back, or the exact last predecision moment for asserting the defense lie outside the accepted postjudgment question.
