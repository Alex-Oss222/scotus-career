## Controlling holdings

### I. A personal political-opportunity claim is justiciable

**Holding and operative rule:** The Equal Protection Clause permits a voter personally affected by electoral boundaries to challenge their intentional, material impairment of an existing or demonstrably attainable opportunity for effective political organization, participation, nomination or election. The claim requires the four distinct elements below, proved through an independently grounded lawful comparison, and is subject to the State's same-decision defense.

**Authority:** Parts I–III and V of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion are joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five Justices directly adopt the complete framework, evidentiary rules, burdens and remand; Part IV has all nine joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker v. Carr requires a claim-specific inquiry into whether courts can apply legal standards. Davis v. Bandemer establishes that partisan districting is not categorically beyond judicial review, but leaves the substantive standard unsettled. The claim recognized here protects an individual's effective political opportunity against purposeful governmental obstruction. It requires neither a judicially preferred party allocation nor a general assessment of political fairness.

Shaw v. Reno and Johnson v. Miller require injury independent of a district's design. Cano v. Davis preserves that requirement while recognizing that actual organization, nomination or participation can be impaired before an election is lost. The Court extends those principles to the particular purposeful partisan deprivation defined here; the earlier cases did not themselves establish this complete claim. The objection that every disappointed voter could obtain a better map fails because the claim requires an actual opportunity, substantial practical impairment, prohibited purpose and causation measured against lawful conditions independent of the desired partisan result. Gaffney v. Cummings continues to permit ordinary political accommodation.

### II. Opportunity, material impairment, prohibited purpose and causation

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish each of the following elements; evidence relevant to one does not substitute for another.

1. **Opportunity:** Identify a practical capacity, already existing or demonstrably attainable, for the affected voters or association to organize, participate, nominate or elect. Evidence must identify the people and activity involved. An emerging opportunity can qualify without a prior victory; party identity, desired additional seats and expected incumbent support alone do not establish one.
2. **Material impairment:** Show a substantial practical obstruction to that activity caused by an identified districting operation. Concentration, division, reassignment or disruption of a coalition must actually impair the opportunity. Ordinary inconvenience or an unfavorable electoral prospect alone is insufficient. Complete extinction of the opportunity, exclusion from every political activity and inevitable electoral defeat are unnecessary.
3. **Prohibited purpose:** Show that suppressing the identified opposition's effective opportunity because of its political association or participation, and thereby insulating governing power from electoral challenge, substantially motivated the decision. The purpose must actually contribute, but need be neither exclusive nor predominant. Awareness of partisan consequences or pursuit of ordinary partisan advantage alone does not establish this purpose.
4. **Material causation:** Identify how the challenged assignments materially produced the impairment, separating their effect from voter geography, ordinary competition and independently lawful requirements. The boundaries need not be the sole cause; a correlation between party preference and an unfavorable outcome is insufficient.

**Authority:** The five-Justice majority joins every element and each qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Page v. Bartels requires an identified protected interest and an operation impairing it; Robertson v. Bartels prevents preferred constituents or anticipated incumbent support from becoming the injury. Cano supplies the distinction between political design and a real interference with participation. Reynolds v. Sims supports equal participation, and White v. Regester directs attention to practical access to political processes. These principles protect effective opportunity without guaranteeing a party's preferred representation.

The substantial-impairment requirement answers the argument that any lost advantage becomes unconstitutional. A difficult election is not itself a disabled political opportunity. Conversely, an actual obstruction of organization or nomination is actionable when imposed; the affected voters need not first lose a general election. Packing, cracking, shape, departures from customary criteria and projected seats can illuminate the mechanism or purpose, but their labels and measurements do not establish the required effect. Arlington Heights supplies contextual evaluation of purpose. The rule addresses deliberate suppression producing an actual material deprivation, while leaving legitimate political considerations within lawful districting.

### III. Lawful comparison, ordinary proof and the State's defense

**Holding and operative rule:** The claimant bears persuasion by a preponderance of the evidence on all four elements, using a comparison with an independently justified, legally and practically feasible political opportunity to establish actual impairment and boundary-specific causation. Once that showing is made, the State bears persuasion by the same standard that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose; if it fails, further balancing of partisan advantages cannot excuse the violation.

A preponderance means more likely true than not. A genuinely applied, independently justified fairness policy or actual population, geographic or statutory requirements can establish the defense only when the evidence shows that they account for materially the same lawful decision. A merely conceivable explanation or another asserted political objective does not carry that burden.

No particular drawing, expert method, number of maps or type of evidence is universally mandatory. A feasible alternative district, actual organizational activity, the effects of a reassignment or another supported comparison may establish the necessary propositions. Whichever method is used must independently establish lawful feasibility and causation; past success, an old map or a preferred seat allocation alone cannot do so. A claim whose comparison depends on a different configuration must establish that configuration's feasibility, including effects necessary to make the comparison work, and may require mapping evidence for that reason.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join the complete proof and defense sequence, including the substantive comparison requirement and flexibility in methods of proof.

**Controlling explanation:** Holder v. Hall supplies the discipline of a comparison grounded independently of the desired winner count, rather than a uniquely ideal arrangement. Its statutory elements are not incorporated into this constitutional claim. Miller and Easley v. Cromartie allow varied evidence while retaining the actual claim's necessary proof. They support the distinction between a mandatory substantive showing and an invariably required cartographic exhibit.

Brooks v. Barnes and Montiel v. Davis apply the distinction between a substantially motivating forbidden purpose and the government's proof of the same lawful decision. Mt. Healthy supplies that causal sequence, and Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof rather than an invented heightened threshold. Plaintiffs must establish the prohibited contribution to the actual impairment, but need not disprove every independent explanation before the burden shifts. The State must prove its actual explanation. Consistent use of an asserted fairness policy helps establish that explanation; the defense does not impose a separate obligation to enact a comprehensive fairness code.

Act 1 was adjudged malapportioned. Its use under a stay cannot establish a lawful baseline for Act 34, and this record supplies no finding that the two plans produced the same relevant effects. The trial court must examine a supported lawful comparison for the operative assignments.

### IV. Article I does not require proportional party seats

**Holding and operative rule:** A projected congressional-seat supermajority for a party without a statewide majority of votes does not, by itself, violate Article I, sections 2 or 4. Congressional district elections create no entitlement to a proportionate statewide party delegation; such figures may serve as evidence under a separately established claim.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part IV and affirm dismissal of this independent theory.

**Controlling explanation:** Article I provides for election of Representatives; it does not turn separate district elections into a statewide proportional contest. Cook v. Gralike prohibits the official ballot penalty adjudicated there, rather than guaranteeing a party's share of seats. Reynolds and Gaffney likewise do not supply that guarantee. The voters' proposed vote-seat trigger therefore fails as an independent rule, even though the same evidence may illuminate purpose or actual operation under Parts I–III.

### V. Apply the rule to the live Act 34 claim through ordinary proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The partisan equal-protection dismissal is vacated, and the District Court must permit conforming amendment, determine personal standing and assess the live Act 34 claim under Parts I–III. If a violation is proved, the State receives the first opportunity to correct the identified deprivation; any judicial remedy must address the responsible boundaries, extend no further than necessary and comply with the other governing districting requirements.

**Authority:** The five-Justice majority joins Part V; the four dissenters would affirm the partisan dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The appeal comes from dismissal, not findings establishing the new rule's factual predicates. Ordinary notice pleading requires identification of the opportunity and challenged operation, with allegations supporting purpose and causation; it does not require merits proof or a replacement map at the pleading stage. Personal exposure, causation and effective redress remain distinct standing requirements.

The renewed challenge concerns Act 34. The lower court must evaluate the actual amended allegations rather than assume a prior plan's findings or election establish current liability. Growe v. Emison preserves the State's initial remedial responsibility. The court shall use a prompt, workable schedule consistent with the election calendar and fit any relief to the proved injury, rather than impose a proportionate delegation or guarantee a seat. The operative plan remains in effect until supported relief is entered, and the separate population disposition remains intact. An additional standalone association claim requires preservation or amendment allowed by ordinary procedure; the equal-protection holding resolves the question properly before the Court. The rule applies to this dispute and other open cases in which the claim is properly presented.

## Enhanced issue comparison

**Path A — categorical political-question disposition of the asserted partisan-dilution category.** The objection is that neither equal protection nor Article I identifies the acceptable partisan allocation from which a court can measure excess. Purpose, map shape and projected seats do not independently create the missing legal entitlement. The strongest answer is Bandemer's majority justiciability holding and the ability to adjudicate ordinary discrimination and direct political exclusion. The three supporters distinguish such independent individual wrongs from a judicially supplied entitlement to a partisan share of representation and would overrule Bandemer's contrary category-level justiciability conclusion.

**Path B — affirm this dismissal without foreclosing every future claim.** The present standards fail to identify a concrete, administrable personal injury in the operative map; that failure does not demonstrate that a workable constitutional claim is impossible. The strongest objection is that leaving the category open without a presently adequate standard may indefinitely deny relief. The response is that the Court cannot turn that concern into a new merits rule unsupported by the existing claim, while specific unequal treatment or expression burdens remain potentially adjudicable through their own elements.

**Path C — vacate the equal-protection dismissal for a confined district-specific inquiry.** Ordinary discrimination doctrine permits inquiry into intentional, materially unequal political opportunity caused by the challenged district assignment. The ground is an individual entitlement to equal participation, not a party's entitlement to seats. Purpose, departure from neutral criteria, actual electoral consequences, feasible lawful comparisons and exclusion from effective political processes must perform their distinct evidentiary functions. The strongest objection is that any preferred map can be called more neutral and any losing candidate can be described as excluded. The four remand supporters require a materially adverse personal effect and a causal comparison justified independently of the preferred seat total; they refuse liability from purpose, irregular shape, lost political advantage or statewide disproportionality alone. Their differences about the necessary adverse effect and the controlling formulation remain below.

**Article I component.** All eight reject the proposed automatic rule that a projected supermajority of seats without a statewide popular-vote majority itself violates Article I. District elections do not constitute a statewide election for a proportional delegation. Cook's official-ballot penalty rule supplies no such conversion. ## Holdings

### I. A personal political-opportunity claim is justiciable

**Holding and operative rule:** The Equal Protection Clause permits a voter personally affected by electoral boundaries to challenge their intentional, material impairment of an existing or demonstrably attainable opportunity for effective political organization, participation, nomination or election. The claim requires the four distinct elements below, proved through an independently grounded lawful comparison, and is subject to the State's same-decision defense.

**Authority:** Parts I–III and V of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion are joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five Justices directly adopt the complete framework, evidentiary rules, burdens and remand; Part IV has all nine joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker v. Carr requires a claim-specific inquiry into whether courts can apply legal standards. Davis v. Bandemer establishes that partisan districting is not categorically beyond judicial review, but leaves the substantive standard unsettled. The claim recognized here protects an individual's effective political opportunity against purposeful governmental obstruction. It requires neither a judicially preferred party allocation nor a general assessment of political fairness.

Shaw v. Reno and Johnson v. Miller require injury independent of a district's design. Cano v. Davis preserves that requirement while recognizing that actual organization, nomination or participation can be impaired before an election is lost. The Court extends those principles to the particular purposeful partisan deprivation defined here; the earlier cases did not themselves establish this complete claim. The objection that every disappointed voter could obtain a better map fails because the claim requires an actual opportunity, substantial practical impairment, prohibited purpose and causation measured against lawful conditions independent of the desired partisan result. Gaffney v. Cummings continues to permit ordinary political accommodation.

### II. Opportunity, material impairment, prohibited purpose and causation

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish each of the following elements; evidence relevant to one does not substitute for another.

1. **Opportunity:** Identify a practical capacity, already existing or demonstrably attainable, for the affected voters or association to organize, participate, nominate or elect. Evidence must identify the people and activity involved. An emerging opportunity can qualify without a prior victory; party identity, desired additional seats and expected incumbent support alone do not establish one.
2. **Material impairment:** Show a substantial practical obstruction to that activity caused by an identified districting operation. Concentration, division, reassignment or disruption of a coalition must actually impair the opportunity. Ordinary inconvenience or an unfavorable electoral prospect alone is insufficient. Complete extinction of the opportunity, exclusion from every political activity and inevitable electoral defeat are unnecessary.
3. **Prohibited purpose:** Show that suppressing the identified opposition's effective opportunity because of its political association or participation, and thereby insulating governing power from electoral challenge, substantially motivated the decision. The purpose must actually contribute, but need be neither exclusive nor predominant. Awareness of partisan consequences or pursuit of ordinary partisan advantage alone does not establish this purpose.
4. **Material causation:** Identify how the challenged assignments materially produced the impairment, separating their effect from voter geography, ordinary competition and independently lawful requirements. The boundaries need not be the sole cause; a correlation between party preference and an unfavorable outcome is insufficient.

**Authority:** The five-Justice majority joins every element and each qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Page v. Bartels requires an identified protected interest and an operation impairing it; Robertson v. Bartels prevents preferred constituents or anticipated incumbent support from becoming the injury. Cano supplies the distinction between political design and a real interference with participation. Reynolds v. Sims supports equal participation, and White v. Regester directs attention to practical access to political processes. These principles protect effective opportunity without guaranteeing a party's preferred representation.

The substantial-impairment requirement answers the argument that any lost advantage becomes unconstitutional. A difficult election is not itself a disabled political opportunity. Conversely, an actual obstruction of organization or nomination is actionable when imposed; the affected voters need not first lose a general election. Packing, cracking, shape, departures from customary criteria and projected seats can illuminate the mechanism or purpose, but their labels and measurements do not establish the required effect. Arlington Heights supplies contextual evaluation of purpose. The rule addresses deliberate suppression producing an actual material deprivation, while leaving legitimate political considerations within lawful districting.

### III. Lawful comparison, ordinary proof and the State's defense

**Holding and operative rule:** The claimant bears persuasion by a preponderance of the evidence on all four elements, using a comparison with an independently justified, legally and practically feasible political opportunity to establish actual impairment and boundary-specific causation. Once that showing is made, the State bears persuasion by the same standard that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose; if it fails, further balancing of partisan advantages cannot excuse the violation.

A preponderance means more likely true than not. A genuinely applied, independently justified fairness policy or actual population, geographic or statutory requirements can establish the defense only when the evidence shows that they account for materially the same lawful decision. A merely conceivable explanation or another asserted political objective does not carry that burden.

No particular drawing, expert method, number of maps or type of evidence is universally mandatory. A feasible alternative district, actual organizational activity, the effects of a reassignment or another supported comparison may establish the necessary propositions. Whichever method is used must independently establish lawful feasibility and causation; past success, an old map or a preferred seat allocation alone cannot do so. A claim whose comparison depends on a different configuration must establish that configuration's feasibility, including effects necessary to make the comparison work, and may require mapping evidence for that reason.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join the complete proof and defense sequence, including the substantive comparison requirement and flexibility in methods of proof.

**Controlling explanation:** Holder v. Hall supplies the discipline of a comparison grounded independently of the desired winner count, rather than a uniquely ideal arrangement. Its statutory elements are not incorporated into this constitutional claim. Miller and Easley v. Cromartie allow varied evidence while retaining the actual claim's necessary proof. They support the distinction between a mandatory substantive showing and an invariably required cartographic exhibit.

Brooks v. Barnes and Montiel v. Davis apply the distinction between a substantially motivating forbidden purpose and the government's proof of the same lawful decision. Mt. Healthy supplies that causal sequence, and Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof rather than an invented heightened threshold. Plaintiffs must establish the prohibited contribution to the actual impairment, but need not disprove every independent explanation before the burden shifts. The State must prove its actual explanation. Consistent use of an asserted fairness policy helps establish that explanation; the defense does not impose a separate obligation to enact a comprehensive fairness code.

Act 1 was adjudged malapportioned. Its use under a stay cannot establish a lawful baseline for Act 34, and this record supplies no finding that the two plans produced the same relevant effects. The trial court must examine a supported lawful comparison for the operative assignments.

### IV. Article I does not require proportional party seats

**Holding and operative rule:** A projected congressional-seat supermajority for a party without a statewide majority of votes does not, by itself, violate Article I, sections 2 or 4. Congressional district elections create no entitlement to a proportionate statewide party delegation; such figures may serve as evidence under a separately established claim.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part IV and affirm dismissal of this independent theory.

**Controlling explanation:** Article I provides for election of Representatives; it does not turn separate district elections into a statewide proportional contest. Cook v. Gralike prohibits the official ballot penalty adjudicated there, rather than guaranteeing a party's share of seats. Reynolds and Gaffney likewise do not supply that guarantee. The voters' proposed vote-seat trigger therefore fails as an independent rule, even though the same evidence may illuminate purpose or actual operation under Parts I–III.

### V. Apply the rule to the live Act 34 claim through ordinary proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The partisan equal-protection dismissal is vacated, and the District Court must permit conforming amendment, determine personal standing and assess the live Act 34 claim under Parts I–III. If a violation is proved, the State receives the first opportunity to correct the identified deprivation; any judicial remedy must address the responsible boundaries, extend no further than necessary and comply with the other governing districting requirements.

**Authority:** The five-Justice majority joins Part V; the four dissenters would affirm the partisan dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The appeal comes from dismissal, not findings establishing the new rule's factual predicates. Ordinary notice pleading requires identification of the opportunity and challenged operation, with allegations supporting purpose and causation; it does not require merits proof or a replacement map at the pleading stage. Personal exposure, causation and effective redress remain distinct standing requirements.

The renewed challenge concerns Act 34. The lower court must evaluate the actual amended allegations rather than assume a prior plan's findings or election establish current liability. Growe v. Emison preserves the State's initial remedial responsibility. The court shall use a prompt, workable schedule consistent with the election calendar and fit any relief to the proved injury, rather than impose a proportionate delegation or guarantee a seat. The operative plan remains in effect until supported relief is entered, and the separate population disposition remains intact. An additional standalone association claim requires preservation or amendment allowed by ordinary procedure; the equal-protection holding resolves the question properly before the Court. The rule applies to this dispute and other open cases in which the claim is properly presented.

## Precedent Treatment

Baker v. Carr is applied to permit adjudication through identifiable legal standards. Davis v. Bandemer's justiciability holding is retained, and the present four-element rule supplies the controlling substantive standard. Gaffney v. Cummings retains legitimate political accommodation. Reynolds v. Sims and White v. Regester support meaningful participation without a guarantee of proportional party seats.

Shaw v. Reno, Johnson v. Miller and Cano v. Davis retain the independent-injury requirement; the Court extends their participation principles to the purposeful partisan impairment defined here. Page v. Bartels and Robertson v. Bartels retain the distinction between protected political activity and favorable constituency expectations. Holder v. Hall supplies an independently grounded comparison by analogy; its statutory capacity requirements remain specific to its cause. Easley v. Cromartie and Miller preserve varied evidence subject to the actual claim's proof.

Brooks v. Barnes and Montiel v. Davis supply the distinct purpose and same-decision sequence; Brooks's nomination-opportunity holding also illustrates a political activity distinct from general-election victory. Mt. Healthy supplies the causal defense, Arlington Heights the contextual purpose inquiry, and Crawford-El the ordinary proof standard. Cook v. Gralike retains its official-ballot-penalty scope. Growe v. Emison governs initial state remedial responsibility. Cano's independent racial-opportunity protection and the Voting Rights Act's distinct statutory causes retain their own elements and remedies.

## Law After Decision

A personal equal-protection claim now lies for an identified, existing or demonstrably attainable political opportunity materially impaired by electoral boundaries substantially motivated by the defined purpose of suppressing opposition and insulating governing power. The five-Justice rule requires all four elements, an independently grounded lawful comparison and ordinary preponderance proof; the State then bears the same-decision defense. Proof may take varied forms, but must establish lawful feasibility and actual causation. Actual participation injury can arise before an electoral defeat.

Ordinary partisan considerations remain permissible, and a projected statewide vote-seat disparity supplies neither the personal deprivation nor an independent Article I violation. Courts apply the rule to properly presented open cases, determine individual standing and actual proof, and give the State the first remedial opportunity after a violation is established.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice would also ground the four-element protection in the First Amendment's guarantees of political expression and association. O'Hare Truck Service and Umbehr establish that government may burden association through a political penalty even when the affected person has no entitlement to the governmental benefit. Districting similarly cannot deliberately impose the material deprivation defined by the Court to suppress opposition and insulate governing power. The Chief Justice applies the same opportunity, substantial impairment, purpose, causation and same-decision requirements, with no automatic injury from partisan design or unfavorable seats. A standalone First Amendment claim must still be properly presented; this additional constitutional ground does not supply a finding about the Act 34 pleadings. No other Justice joins this writing.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Scalia and Thomas join.** The three join rejection of the Article I seat-ratio claim but would affirm dismissal of partisan dilution as a political question and overrule Bandemer's contrary conclusion for that category. O'Connor's Bandemer position distinguishes ordinary individual equality from a judicial allocation of partisan strength. The new opportunity and comparison language, they conclude, still requires judges to select the political arrangement against which disadvantage is measured. A lawful alternative can be feasible without being constitutionally owed, and purpose cannot supply that missing entitlement. Their Holder positions reinforce the objection to a court-selected allocation, while Growe protects legislative responsibility. They retain adjudication of independently defined exclusion, retaliation, population and racial claims under their own standards. The objection concerns the affirmative partisan-opportunity cause, not the Court's unanimous Article I rule.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Kennedy joins Part IV and would affirm this partisan dismissal because the asserted theories and proposed standards fail to identify a sufficiently manageable personal constitutional burden in Act 34. He declines to declare that every future partisan claim is nonjusticiable. Holder requires a principled basis for comparison, while Cook, Cano and the political-expression principles reflected in his McConnell position leave room for an actually defined participation or association injury. The Court's new formulation, in his view, does not yet furnish a sufficient measure for this claim. A possible future expression-based standard is therefore a reason to retain Bandemer's openness, rather than to remand this case or join the announced four-element rule. He joins neither the categorical dissent's overruling proposal nor the Court's affirmative standard.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the three-judge District Court. It shall permit conforming amendment, determine personal standing and apply the announced standard to the live Act 34 claim under ordinary pleading and proof rules, on a prompt schedule suited to the election calendar. An additional association theory remains subject to ordinary preservation and amendment rules. If the plaintiffs establish a violation and defeat the State's defense, the court shall identify the deprivation and responsible boundaries, afford the State the first opportunity to correct them and fit any further relief to that violation. The Article I dismissal and separate population disposition retain their stated force.
