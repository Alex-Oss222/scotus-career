## Controlling holdings

**Common authority:** The signed Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine participating Justices, controls each proposition below.

### I. Continued severe pressure must answer an actual continuing need

**Controlling proposition:** Officers violate the Fourth Amendment when, without a proportionate continuing safety need, they maintain substantial body-weight pressure that obstructs breathing after an unarmed person is handcuffed, controlled and nonresisting, despite known respiratory distress. The inquiry considers the complete circumstances as they develop, and an actual continuing threat may materially change the assessment.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham v. Connor requires objective assessment of the force actually used against the need confronting reasonable officers. A medical emergency can justify taking custody and obtaining control. It cannot justify avoidable, dangerous compression after control when resistance has ended and the person repeatedly reports that he cannot breathe. The supported account places this case on the latter side of that boundary. Plakas v. Drinski upheld defensive force during an immediate armed attack; it makes the actual threat material rather than supplying a general emergency exception. Holland likewise distinguishes control from needless force continued after submission. The officers' emergency-purpose argument fails because the purpose does not explain the continued degree of pressure. The court must use the supported nonmovant account at summary judgment; each defendant's participation and causal contribution remain necessary to liability.

### II. Preconduct law gave sufficiently clear warning

**Controlling proposition:** On the supported account of sustained compression after control, nonresistance and repeated respiratory warnings, the unlawfulness was apparent under law effective on March 26, 1999, and personal qualified immunity does not support summary judgment. An obvious application of an established constitutional rule needs no prior case reproducing the precise restraint, while actual contrary authority and the particular circumstances remain part of the notice inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve an objective legal defense distinct from the force merits. Graham supplied the governing force rule before this encounter. Lanier established before 1999 that an obviously covered application can provide clear warning without a factual twin, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct authority. The contrary restraint cases do not make this conduct uncertain. Phillips involved active struggle and brief, gentle shoulder pressure. Price involved a struggling, delirious person and incidental pressure for seconds on facts found at trial. Their resistance, force, duration and awareness predicates differ from sustained substantial compression after control despite repeated breathing complaints. The reported departmental warning contextualizes the danger; it does not create the constitutional right or prove any particular officer received it. Hope explains the current clarity method but supplies no retrospective notice. Saucier preserves the separate force and objective-immunity inquiries without imposing a mandatory sequence.

### III. Dependent judgments return for their independent requirements

**Controlling proposition:** The municipal and state-law judgments that rested on the absence of a constitutional violation must remain vacated once that premise is rejected on the supported summary-judgment account. Municipal liability requires an attributable policy, custom or deliberately indifferent training failure, the applicable fault, and a causal connection to the federal injury; each state-law claim remains subject to its own elements and defenses.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell makes municipal responsibility an independent inquiry, and Canton requires the necessary culpability and causal connection for a training claim. The City's lack of an officer's personal qualified immunity does not establish those elements. Because the judgments below rested on the rejected no-violation premise and the independent grounds were not resolved in this appeal, the branches return for their proper adjudication.
