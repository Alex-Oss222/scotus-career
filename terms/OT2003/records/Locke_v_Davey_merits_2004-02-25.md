### A State may decline support for the devotional ministry degree presented here

**Controlling proposition:** The Free Exercise Clause permits a State to exclude pursuit of a devotional degree preparing the student for ministry from this individual educational scholarship while allowing religious students, religious institutions, individual theology courses and secular courses of study to remain eligible. The permission concerns the devotional-degree condition as applied to Davey's undisputed pastoral training; it does not authorize disqualification merely because of religious belief, institutional affiliation or an isolated theology course.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to the rule, application and remedy. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supports reversal concerning devotional formation on a distinct ground and does not join this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Washington may make this confined choice against supporting preparation for ministry even though the Establishment Clause does not require exclusion of the student benefit. Walz recognizes room between what the Religion Clauses require and prohibit. Witters preserves individual vocational assistance as a permissible relationship; permission to include religious training does not establish an obligation to support every devotional degree.

The actual award is student assistance. Funds pass through the institution for distribution to the student and may meet tuition, room, board and other educational expenses. It is not the restricted provider purchase adjudicated in Zelman or the ordinary integrated tuition in Strout. Those decisions establish the importance of actual function but do not eliminate the separate Free Exercise question here. Washington permits religious-school attendance, religious students and theology courses. It also allows a scholarship for a secular degree at another institution while devotional study occurs elsewhere. The exclusion targets the course of devotional preparation for ministry rather than a religious identity.

The contrary equality argument correctly insists that K.D.M. and Columbia Union protect eligible secular services and work from exclusion based on religion. But this benefit is conditioned on the course of study, and the undisputed pastoral-ministry major falls within the confined condition. Davey's business double major does not convert the scholarship into the restricted secular-department grant adjudicated in Columbia Union. The State need not establish that every dollar purchases prohibited devotion before declining this particular ministry support. The Ninth Circuit's contrary Free Exercise judgment is reversed, and the State's favorable judgment on the granted claim is restored.

**Operative qualification:** The parties agree that the statute's theology-degree term covers degrees devotional in nature or designed to induce religious faith. That agreed application supplies this decision's scope.
