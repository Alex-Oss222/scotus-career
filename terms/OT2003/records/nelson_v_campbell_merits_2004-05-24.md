### I. The pleaded severable procedure challenge may proceed under section 1983

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner may proceed under section 1983 against a particular execution-related procedure when success would leave the State a lawful means of carrying out the sentence and thus would not necessarily invalidate it. At the pleading stage, Nelson's challenge to the allegedly unnecessary cut-down, supported by an alleged feasible alternative permitting lethal injection, cannot be dismissed solely as an unauthorized successive habeas petition.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join all holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Preiser v. Rodriguez identifies habeas as the channel for its core attack on custody. Heck v. Humphrey and Edwards v. Balisok require attention to the necessary legal consequence of the actual claim. Randell v. Johnson and Muhammad v. Close likewise preserve civil claims compatible with the operative custody determination. Nelson accepts the death judgment and execution by lethal injection. His complaint attacks a particular means of obtaining venous access and alleges a less invasive alternative that permits execution. ## Holdings

### I. The pleaded severable procedure challenge may proceed under section 1983

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner may proceed under section 1983 against a particular execution-related procedure when success would leave the State a lawful means of carrying out the sentence and thus would not necessarily invalidate it. At the pleading stage, Nelson's challenge to the allegedly unnecessary cut-down, supported by an alleged feasible alternative permitting lethal injection, cannot be dismissed solely as an unauthorized successive habeas petition.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join all holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Preiser v. Rodriguez identifies habeas as the channel for its core attack on custody. Heck v. Humphrey and Edwards v. Balisok require attention to the necessary legal consequence of the actual claim. Randell v. Johnson and Muhammad v. Close likewise preserve civil claims compatible with the operative custody determination. Nelson accepts the death judgment and execution by lethal injection. His complaint attacks a particular means of obtaining venous access and alleges a less invasive alternative that permits execution. Those allegations, including the supporting anesthesiologist's account, are accepted for the pleading inquiry rather than established as medical facts. Alabama argues that delay shortly before execution is functionally a sentence attack. But a temporary restraint needed to resolve a compatible civil injury differs from a claim whose necessary relief defeats lawful sentence implementation. Merits, exhaustion and equity can address an unjustified demand without changing its channel by association. If factual development establishes that the challenged procedure is necessary to carry out lawful execution, the court must examine the channel on that changed premise. A request to prohibit execution regardless of the cut-down likewise requires its own scope and channel analysis. The classification of every execution-method challenge lies outside this pleaded severable dispute.

### II. Civil cognizability and execution-stay equity are separate

**Controlling proposition:** Availability of section 1983 creates no automatic stay. A court considering interim protection must assess likelihood of success, relative harms, the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and the actual reasons for delay, applying a strong equitable presumption against a stay when the claim could have been brought early enough for merits consideration without requiring one; that presumption is contextual, with the reasons for timing and irreversible harm considered.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Gomez v. United States District Court permits a supported delay objection independently of habeas restrictions. Its principle protects the State's enforcement interest while retaining consideration of serious constitutional injury and irreversible consequences. It does not make proximity to execution proof of avoidable delay. Counsel requested protocol information and medical consultation in August 2003. The access plan described on September 10 changed materially by October 3, and the action followed the next Monday before the scheduled October 9 execution. That chronology defeats an assumption that this precise challenge was available unchanged for years, while leaving actual diligence and equitable entitlement for decision on evidence. The court must address the medical risk, alternative access and timing through ordinary adjudication and tailor any interim restraint to the lawful adjudicative need.

### III. The civil remand retains the PLRA's distinct requirements

**Controlling proposition:** A cognizable procedure-specific claim remains subject to exhaustion of actually available administrative remedies where section 1997e(a) applies and to the independent statutory screening and prospective-relief conditions. Any prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular federal-right violation, and the least intrusive necessary means; preliminary relief expires after ninety days unless the required findings are made and the order is made final within that period.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The civil channel does not erase the conditions Congress places on prison-conditions actions. The exhaustion inquiry concerns remedies actually available; the record establishes neither exhaustion nor a failure to exhaust. Section 1997e(c)(2) permits dismissal without first requiring exhaustion when the claim is facially frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim, or seeks monetary relief from an immune defendant; it does not compel that procedural shortcut. Section 3626 requires the court to give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting a state or local official to exceed lawful authority or violate state or local law additionally requires that federal law require that departure, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation, and that no other relief correct it. The court must identify the harm and scope of any preliminary order and satisfy the final-relief findings before the ninety-day period expires if continued relief is warranted. The medical merits and the findings supporting any requested injunction remain ordinary work on remand under these rules.

### IV. Meaningful prompt adjudication and the scope of any protection

**Controlling proposition:** The district court must promptly decide a properly presented interim request in time to address threatened use of the disputed procedure where the actual schedule permits, applying the medical, procedural, equitable and statutory requirements already stated. Any warranted injunction must identify the prohibited procedure or conduct and persons bound within the supported claim and injury; any stay must last only for the period justified by its purpose, record and equitable findings, with reconsideration as those premises change.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Randell and Muhammad require actual adjudication of a compatible civil injury while preserving independent defenses and relief conditions. Gomez requires attention to the State's enforcement interest, actual delay and irreversible harm. A prompt scheduling direction to the court therefore supplies no automatic restraint on the State while the court considers the request; any temporary restraint needed to complete adjudication must have its own lawful equitable basis. The court may grant or deny interim protection on the legally sufficient record. If an injunction is warranted, its precise terms must correct the supported injury and satisfy section 3626's need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness requirements. A materially broader request retains the independent channel inquiry. The period of any stay follows its actual justified purpose and may be reconsidered, including through a supported extension, as the record changes. The separate ninety-day preliminary-relief and final-finding conditions remain effective. These directions secure meaningful adjudication without turning the availability of section 1983 into entitlement to interim relief.
