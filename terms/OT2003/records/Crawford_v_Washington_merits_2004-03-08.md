## Controlling holdings

**Controlling proposition:** A formal, recorded custodial police interrogation eliciting a witness's account of past criminal events for evidentiary use against the accused is testimonial. When the prosecution offers that accusation for its truth, it must produce the declarant for constitutionally adequate confrontation or establish both unavailability and a prior opportunity for cross-examination; a hearsay label, judicial assessment of reliability or agreement with other evidence cannot replace that opportunity.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice O'Connor agrees with the judgment through the existing reliability rule and does not join this replacement rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Confrontation Clause prescribes a way to test an accusation assembled as testimony. Its protection cannot depend on a judge's assessment that the untested account is probably accurate. Mattox allows unavailable testimony that has already undergone cross-examination; the history of examinations conducted outside the accused's presence explains why police-created trial accusations require the same procedural protection even without an oath.

The State's strongest answer is that reliability rules already screen untrustworthy accusations, and Wright and Lilly could exclude this recording without changing doctrine. But that method permits another judge to admit the same class of accusation upon a different assessment of trustworthiness. Repeated reliance on that substitute does not cure its constitutional defect. The Court overrules Roberts to the extent it permits admission within this defined testimonial class through a hearsay exception or reliability finding without the required confrontation.

Sylvia's repeated, recorded custodial questioning sought an account of the completed encounter for investigation and prosecution. Crawford never had an opportunity to cross-examine her. Her accusation's asserted self-inculpatory character and its supposed agreement with Crawford's account cannot supply the missing procedure. Marital privilege does not itself forfeit his confrontation right. The recording was constitutionally inadmissible for its truth against him.

**Operative qualifications and reach:** Existing forfeiture by wrongdoing remains an independent equitable ground for loss of the confrontation claim. The rule concerns substantive truth use; genuinely nontruth use remains distinct. A declarant who appears for constitutionally adequate cross-examination can be questioned about an earlier statement. Outside the defined testimonial class, existing exception-sensitive constitutional law continues at its established scope. The new rule applies on this direct review and under ordinary direct-review principles.
