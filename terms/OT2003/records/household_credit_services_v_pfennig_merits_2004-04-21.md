## Controlling holdings

### The Board may separately classify these over-limit charges

**Controlling proposition:** Under 15 U.S.C. §1604(a)'s express classification authority, the Board may reasonably exclude fees for exceeding an agreed credit limit from TILA's finance-charge category while leaving applicable separate disclosure duties in force. Regulation Z §226.4(c)(2) validly makes that classification, and knowingly honoring an over-limit transaction does not by itself require a different classification; the statutory-conflict or unreasonableness challenge fails here.

**Authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion of the Court controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Mead requires actual delegated authority, Haggar requires independent enforcement of statutory limits, and Heimmermann keeps authorized interpretation within the underlying prohibition. Section 1604(a) expressly permits classifications, differentiations, adjustments and exceptions necessary or proper to effectuate TILA's purposes, prevent circumvention or evasion, or facilitate compliance. Its exclusion for section 1602(aa) mortgages retains its own scope; this case concerns a card account. The distinct exemption power in section 1604(f) does not supply the rule here.

Section 1605(a)'s broad definition of charges incident to credit does not unambiguously decide this contingent excess-limit category. Pfennig reasonably argues that an issuer permitting an additional advance imposes the fee because it extended credit. But permission for a transaction does not necessarily renegotiate the agreed limit, and the statutory scheme distinguishes finance charges from other open-end charges. The Board reasonably separates the agreed price of credit from a fee triggered by exceeding its terms, facilitating intelligible price comparison and compliance. Administrative convenience alone could not contradict Congress, and disclosure elsewhere alone could not validate an unlawful classification; the express delegation and permissible statutory distinction do that work.

Section 226.4(c)(2) specifically addresses exceeding a credit limit. Subsection (c)(3)'s different exclusion for paying items that overdraw an account contains a prior-written-agreement exception; that exception does not govern (c)(2). Smiley's treatment of charges as interest under another statute supplies no contrary TILA definition. The Court therefore sustains this category while requiring compliance with its actual terms and all independently applicable disclosure duties.

The category remains subject to its regulatory terms and separate disclosure obligations. Courts must identify the actual charge and apply those duties; this decision supplies no general exemption for all default or credit-related fees.
