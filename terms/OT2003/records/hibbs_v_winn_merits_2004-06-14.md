## Controlling law

##### I. Genuine court-initiated reconsideration suspends finality

**Holding and operative rule:** When an appellate court begins genuine reconsideration capable of changing the parties' adjudicated rights before the original §2101(c) period expires, the ninety-day period for Supreme Court review runs from disposition of that reconsideration. A mandate recall alone or a nominal order merely extending this Court's filing time is insufficient; this petition is timely because substantive rehearing briefing began within the original period and the petition followed within ninety days after rehearing ended.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Ginsburg's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judgment's real finality determines the start of the enacted period. Young v. Harper treated genuinely accepted reconsideration as material to finality, although that case involved a late party request rather than the court's own action. The same principle applies where the appellate court itself opens a substantive rehearing question before the existing review period ends. The Ninth Circuit recalled the mandate and ordered briefing on possible en banc rehearing, leaving a real possibility of a changed judgment. Procedural initiation by the court rather than a party does not make that possibility final. The rule enforces the statutory period from an actual final judgment; it does not grant an equitable enlargement or treat an unentertained suggestion as reconsideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Young's accepted-rehearing finality principle is extended to this timely court-initiated process.

##### II. The requested relief does not restrain protected assessment, levy or collection

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1341 bars federal restraint of a State's assessment, levy or collection of taxes when a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists; it does not bar this third-party challenge seeking disallowance of credits, the related declaration and transfer of remaining organizational funds, because those remedies do not restrain the protected imposition or collection of tax liabilities. The rule turns on the actual operation of relief, not a categorical exemption for third parties, constitutional claims, credit challenges or suits increasing revenue.

**Authority:** Part II has six direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Assessment must be read with levy and collection. Together they protect the State's imposition and obtaining of taxes from federal restraint; assessment does not encompass every operation of state tax policy. These plaintiffs seek no reduction in their own liabilities or avoidance of taxes they owe. Disallowing the challenged credits would permit collection of the full tax. The declaration supports that same relief, and transferring covered organizations' remaining funds to the general fund neither refunds the plaintiffs' taxes nor obstructs their collection.

The dissent gives assessment an independent reach encompassing a decree altering computation of liability. That construction separates the word from the collection-related setting Congress specified and would turn every tax-administration effect into the prohibited restraint. The present construction still bars relief by any plaintiff that actually restrains protected assessment or collection when the adequate-state-remedy condition holds. Grace Brethren prevents declaratory evasion where the underlying tax restraint is barred; its rule is distinguished by the operation of the relief, not by a First Amendment exception. Farm Credit involved a genuine restraint of taxation and refused to create a forum exception from substantive tax status. It does not expand the Act to this different operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Grace Brethren's declaration rule is preserved and distinguished; Farm Credit's actual tax-restraint holding remains, with substantive tax entitlement and forum availability distinct.

##### III. General interference with tax administration does not independently require dismissal here

**Holding and operative rule:** The same challenge is not barred on comity grounds merely because it affects state tax administration. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes concern efforts to arrest or countermand tax collection and do not require dismissal of this claim simply because the challenged subsidy is located in the tax code.

**Authority:** Part III has the five direct joins of Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Comity respects the State's collection process, but the argument presented would convert every objection to a tax-code subsidy into interference requiring dismissal. The requested relief here has the different operation explained above. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes do not establish that generalized bar. The ordinary Article III inquiry and ultimate constitutional adjudication remain for the lower courts; this conclusion decides the asserted generalized tax-administration ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Fair Assessment and Great Lakes are distinguished at their tax-collection scope.

##### II. The requested relief does not restrain protected assessment, levy or collection

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1341 bars federal restraint of a State's assessment, levy or collection of taxes when a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists; it does not bar this third-party challenge seeking disallowance of credits, the related declaration and transfer of remaining organizational funds, because those remedies do not restrain the protected imposition or collection of tax liabilities. The rule turns on the actual operation of relief, not a categorical exemption for third parties, constitutional claims, credit challenges or suits increasing revenue.

**Authority:** Part II has six direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Assessment must be read with levy and collection. Together they protect the State's imposition and obtaining of taxes from federal restraint; assessment does not encompass every operation of state tax policy. These plaintiffs seek no reduction in their own liabilities or avoidance of taxes they owe. Disallowing the challenged credits would permit collection of the full tax. The declaration supports that same relief, and transferring covered organizations' remaining funds to the general fund neither refunds the plaintiffs' taxes nor obstructs their collection.

The dissent gives assessment an independent reach encompassing a decree altering computation of liability. That construction separates the word from the collection-related setting Congress specified and would turn every tax-administration effect into the prohibited restraint. The present construction still bars relief by any plaintiff that actually restrains protected assessment or collection when the adequate-state-remedy condition holds. Grace Brethren prevents declaratory evasion where the underlying tax restraint is barred; its rule is distinguished by the operation of the relief, not by a First Amendment exception. Farm Credit involved a genuine restraint of taxation and refused to create a forum exception from substantive tax status. It does not expand the Act to this different operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Grace Brethren's declaration rule is preserved and distinguished; Farm Credit's actual tax-restraint holding remains, with substantive tax entitlement and forum availability distinct.

##### III. General interference with tax administration does not independently require dismissal here

**Holding and operative rule:** The same challenge is not barred on comity grounds merely because it affects state tax administration. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes concern efforts to arrest or countermand tax collection and do not require dismissal of this claim simply because the challenged subsidy is located in the tax code.

**Authority:** Part III has the five direct joins of Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Comity respects the State's collection process, but the argument presented would convert every objection to a tax-code subsidy into interference requiring dismissal. The requested relief here has the different operation explained above. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes do not establish that generalized bar. The ordinary Article III inquiry and ultimate constitutional adjudication remain for the lower courts; this conclusion decides the asserted generalized tax-administration ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Fair Assessment and Great Lakes are distinguished at their tax-collection scope.

## Precedent Treatment

- Young v. Harper: extended from accepted party rehearing to genuine court-initiated reconsideration begun within the original review period.
- California v. Grace Brethren Church, 457 U.S. 393: preserved; declaratory relief cannot evade an applicable restriction on tax collection, and a constitutional claim creates no automatic exemption.
- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas: distinguished because the requested relief there restrained taxation; a substantive tax privilege does not itself create access to a federal forum.
- Fair Assessment in Real Estate Ass'n v. McNary, 454 U.S. 100, and Great Lakes Dredge & Dock Co. v. Huffman, 319 U.S. 293: distinguished as collection-interference cases, rather than a freestanding bar to this subsidy challenge.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: its personal-standing requirements remain applicable in the restored proceedings.

## Law After Decision

Genuine timely reconsideration initiated by an appellate court makes its eventual disposition the starting point for §2101(c)'s ninety days. The tax-forum restriction requires examination of the actual restraint sought; this credit challenge may proceed because its relief does not obstruct the protected tax process. The generalized comity objection likewise fails. The lower courts must adjudicate personal standing, genuinely distinct preserved barriers, the actual financing and alleged religious use, and ultimate remedial entitlement in their proper order.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring.** Stevens joins the entire opinion and adds that settled judicial treatment of this category of challenge and Congress's failure to displace it support stability in statutory interpretation. His approach in Runyon and Fink counsels restraint before upsetting established statutory expectations. That history reinforces the contextual reading; it neither proves that every earlier case decided the present question nor permits disregard of unmistakable enacted language.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices join the timing holding but would reverse and remand for dismissal under §1341. A federal decree ordering Arizona to disallow lawful credits restrains computation and assessment of liabilities, even if receipts rise. Assessment has a statutory function separate from levy and collection; the Act contains no requirement that plaintiffs challenge their own taxes or that receipts decrease. The declaration and demand for the organizations' remaining funds depend on invalidating the same credit arrangement and supply no separate route around the bar.

Respondents admit that a state court would hear them, and a related constitutional credit challenge was heard there. The available account identifies no concrete procedural impediment to judicial consideration of these federal objections. Under Rosewell and Grace Brethren, adequacy requires an effective judicial opportunity, not identical relief, a preferred tribunal or success. The admission establishes forum availability rather than conceded coverage. The statutory condition is satisfied, and these Justices would end the federal action on that ground.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed appellate judgment returns the action to the district court. That court must resolve Article III standing and any genuinely distinct preserved threshold, then adjudicate the constitutional claim and requested remedies if the case properly reaches them. The rejected generalized fiscal-interference argument cannot be repeated as a mandatory bar to the same action.
