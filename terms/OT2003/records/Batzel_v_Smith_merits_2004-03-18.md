## Controlling holdings

### Federal appellate jurisdiction over the special-motion denial

**Holding and operative rule:** Denial of a California anti-SLAPP motion in federal court is not immediately appealable under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 merely because state law permits an appeal or describes protection from litigation. This category of merits-linked probability-of-success screening and ordinary liability defense remains effectively reviewable after final judgment, and therefore fails the cumulative collateral-order requirements; the Ninth Circuit lacked jurisdiction to decide the moderator's § 230 defense on this appeal.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion for the unanimous Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the jurisdictional rule and mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Cohen permits immediate review only within a narrow class of conclusively determined, sufficiently important and separate questions that would be effectively unreviewable after final judgment. State appeal permission cannot enlarge federal jurisdiction. The special motion's inquiry into the plaintiff's probability of success overlaps the liability issues, including the asserted publisher defense. Describing the motion as protection from litigation does not make those issues sufficiently separate and unreviewable merely because the suit continues.

California's purpose of protecting public expression is more than the private settlement interest addressed in Digital Equipment. But that public purpose cannot dispense with the remaining collateral-order conditions. Digital Equipment's independently controlling adequate-final-review holding establishes that irretrievable litigation expense and the loss of early protection do not alone make later review ineffective. Swint applies that distinction to an ordinary liability defense. Here, final review can correct an erroneous determination of the moderator's statutory protection. The possible burden or chilling effect of continued litigation does not supply the missing effective-unreviewability condition. That failure suffices for this disposition.

The sponsor's independent final appeal supplies no jurisdiction over the moderator's distinct interlocutory defense. Swint rejects adding an otherwise unauthorized appeal merely because deciding related issues would save litigation. Review of the sponsor's judgment does not require resolution of this defense.

**Question not reached:** The actual-intent, reasonable-perception and broader supplied-content constructions of § 230 cannot be adjudicated through this unauthorized interlocutory appeal. The jurisdictional ground prevents reaching those merits questions; their factual predicates return to the district court under ordinary procedure.

**Precedent treatment:** Cohen's narrow, cumulative exception is applied. Digital Equipment's adequate-final-review holding and Swint's ordinary-defense and separate-party rules supply the governing law; the Court extends their application to this category of California special-motion denial without altering their requirements.
