## Controlling holdings

### I. The announced entry was reasonable in its actual circumstances

**Controlling proposition:** After meaningful notice during execution of a search warrant, officers may force entry when contemporaneous circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further waiting will permit imminent destruction of the evidence sought. The Government must justify that exception; property damage does not require a higher suspicion threshold, but the means and extent of damage remain independently reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer’s opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices, controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson makes announcement part of reasonable home entry. Richards requires particular circumstances connecting delay to threatened evidence loss, and Ramirez rejects a higher exigency tier merely because entry causes damage. Here officers loudly announced their authority and search purpose at about 2 p.m. at a small two-bedroom apartment. A warrant supported the presence of readily disposable cocaine, and fifteen to twenty seconds passed without a response. Together those circumstances supported a reasonable suspicion that further delay would allow disposal. The officers were not required to hear flushing or eliminate every innocent explanation before acting.

Banks’s strongest objection is that an innocent occupant may need more time to reach the door. But the exigency concerns time to destroy the cocaine, not simply walking distance; the small dwelling supplies context rather than a universal measure. His shower use was unknown to the officers and cannot determine their contemporaneous justification. The necessary door entry was reasonable on this record. The lower court’s four-category scheme, including a heightened threshold for destructive entry, cannot replace that actual assessment. When exigency is absent, avoidable damage may require greater patience for admission. This holding supplies neither a fixed fifteen-second rule nor a categorical exception for narcotics warrants, and preserves independent restraint on unnecessary or disproportionate damage.

### II. Section 3109 preserves the supported destruction exception

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate an officer or a person assisting execution. Its common-law announcement rule also permits entry after notice without actual or inferred refusal when particular circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further delay will permit imminent destruction of the warranted evidence; the Government must establish those circumstances.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller supplies the statute’s common-law background, and Sabbath explains its codification of that protection. Ramirez applied that method to a supported danger exception. The same method supports the destruction qualification presented here: a rule protecting peaceful entry does not require officers, after notice, to wait while the specifically sought evidence is about to disappear. Wilson and Richards supply the historical protection and particularized threshold; the statutory conclusion rests on the statute’s common-law content rather than automatic incorporation of every constitutional exception.

The circumstances supporting the constitutional holding also establish this bounded statutory excuse after actual announcement. No inference of refusal is required. That distinction preserves the ordinary refusal requirement rather than treating silence as refusal merely because drugs can be disposed of quickly. The Court now resolves the destruction application left open in Ramirez. Generic drug risk alone remains insufficient, and the adopted exception does not dispense with meaningful notice in every drug search.

## Holdings

### I. The announced entry was reasonable in its actual circumstances

**Controlling proposition:** After meaningful notice during execution of a search warrant, officers may force entry when contemporaneous circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further waiting will permit imminent destruction of the evidence sought. The Government must justify that exception; property damage does not require a higher suspicion threshold, but the means and extent of damage remain independently reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer’s opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices, controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson makes announcement part of reasonable home entry. Richards requires particular circumstances connecting delay to threatened evidence loss, and Ramirez rejects a higher exigency tier merely because entry causes damage. Here officers loudly announced their authority and search purpose at about 2 p.m. at a small two-bedroom apartment. A warrant supported the presence of readily disposable cocaine, and fifteen to twenty seconds passed without a response. Together those circumstances supported a reasonable suspicion that further delay would allow disposal. The officers were not required to hear flushing or eliminate every innocent explanation before acting.

Banks’s strongest objection is that an innocent occupant may need more time to reach the door. But the exigency concerns time to destroy the cocaine, not simply walking distance; the small dwelling supplies context rather than a universal measure. His shower use was unknown to the officers and cannot determine their contemporaneous justification. The necessary door entry was reasonable on this record. The lower court’s four-category scheme, including a heightened threshold for destructive entry, cannot replace that actual assessment. When exigency is absent, avoidable damage may require greater patience for admission. This holding supplies neither a fixed fifteen-second rule nor a categorical exception for narcotics warrants, and preserves independent restraint on unnecessary or disproportionate damage.

### II. Section 3109 preserves the supported destruction exception

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate an officer or a person assisting execution. Its common-law announcement rule also permits entry after notice without actual or inferred refusal when particular circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further delay will permit imminent destruction of the warranted evidence; the Government must establish those circumstances.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller supplies the statute’s common-law background, and Sabbath explains its codification of that protection. Ramirez applied that method to a supported danger exception. The same method supports the destruction qualification presented here: a rule protecting peaceful entry does not require officers, after notice, to wait while the specifically sought evidence is about to disappear. Wilson and Richards supply the historical protection and particularized threshold; the statutory conclusion rests on the statute’s common-law content rather than automatic incorporation of every constitutional exception.

The circumstances supporting the constitutional holding also establish this bounded statutory excuse after actual announcement. No inference of refusal is required. That distinction preserves the ordinary refusal requirement rather than treating silence as refusal merely because drugs can be disposed of quickly. The Court now resolves the destruction application left open in Ramirez. Generic drug risk alone remains insufficient, and the adopted exception does not dispense with meaningful notice in every drug search.

## Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas: applied to announcement protection and justified countervailing circumstances during home entry.
- Richards v. Wisconsin: applied to particularized reasonable suspicion, contemporaneous justification and rejection of a categorical narcotics exception; its existing review allocation remains unchanged.
- United States v. Ramirez: applied to reject a damage-based higher exigency threshold and preserve independently reasonable force. Its statutory common-law method is extended from supported danger to the destruction circumstance decided here.
- Miller v. United States: applied for the statutory common-law background, without attributing this destruction holding to that earlier decision.
- Sabbath v. United States: applied for codification of common-law announcement; its treatment of breaking and unsupported danger retains its force.

## Law After Decision

The combined circumstances of this announced warrant execution justify the elapsed interval and necessary door entry. Courts continue to assess waiting time and force contextually. Section 3109 now expressly permits the supported post-notice destruction exception, independently of actual or implied refusal, while retaining its ordinary notice-and-refusal rule and separate liberation provision.
