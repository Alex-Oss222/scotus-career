## Controlling law

### I. Longstanding systemic pretrial conditions

**Controlling proposition:** For prospective protection against deliberately maintained, longstanding systemic pretrial conditions, detainees must prove by a preponderance a current substantial risk of serious harm and conditions that lack a reasonable relation to a legitimate nonpunitive objective or are excessive in relation to that objective. Sustained notice, a real opportunity for deliberative response and prolonged inadequate correction establish the institutional culpability present here; separate proof of every responsible incumbent's subjective appreciation is unnecessary, while ordinary negligence is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Bell v. Wolfish supplies the protection against punishment before adjudication of guilt. That protection requires examining the maintained conditions and their justification, rather than making relief turn on separately proving each incumbent's private mental state. The record establishes years of notice of specified serious conditions and prolonged inadequate responses, alongside successful measures concerning other conditions. Daniels v. Williams excludes ordinary negligence. Crawford-El v. Britton supplies ordinary civil proof for the constitutional factual elements without reducing or replacing those elements. County of Sacramento v. Lewis makes the custodial duty and genuine opportunity for deliberation material; its emergency-pursuit standard does not govern these enduring conditions. Farmer v. Brennan supplies the knowledge and reasonable-response rule for the convicted-prisoner claim it decided, but that distinct rule does not displace Bell in this institutional pretrial action. The City's objection that danger alone cannot establish culpability is sound at that level and is answered by the deliberately maintained conditions, sustained notice and opportunity for correction here. Individual damages and convicted-prisoner claims lie outside this prospective pretrial question.

### II. The OCC's actual limited functions

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3626(f) applies by actual delegated function, regardless of title. The OCC's restricted observation, information gathering, informal compliance assistance and reporting, with independent judicial determination of disputes and enforcement, do not make this continuous arrangement a special master. The party defending the appointment must produce a supported account of the actual delegated functions; the judge independently determines classification and evaluates reports as evidence subject to proper objections, and a materially expanded delegation requires a new functional assessment.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3626(g)(8) covers persons appointed under Rule 53 or inherent authority to exercise a master's powers. Ex parte Peterson recognizes judicial assistance while preserving the judge's responsibility. The OCC gathers operational information and informally assists compliance. It does not conduct adjudicatory hearings, subpoena or swear witnesses, rule on evidence, adjudicate discrete disputes or issue findings entitled to prescribed deference; the judge decides rights and enforces orders. The amended Rule 53, effective December 1, 2003, includes specified pretrial and posttrial appointments, investigation and enforcement duties, and ordinarily de novo review of objections to findings. Thus absence of binding findings or trial hearings alone cannot establish the classification. The complete, limited delegation here remains informational assistance rather than delegated judicial decision or enforcement. The City's functional objection receives that full assessment; neither the OCC's name nor a general exemption for monitors determines the result. The supported-functions requirement identifies the actual powers, terms and practice relied on; it does not allocate the ultimate risk of uncertainty in every classification dispute. The pending-case transition applies insofar as just and practicable.

### III. The continuous 1982 appointment

**Independently sufficient holding:** Section 3626(f)'s appointment machinery does not recreate the OCC's completed 1982 appointment merely because later orders renew and adjust the same continuous entity. That ground independently supports continuation of this restricted arrangement, subject to the current prospective-relief requirements of sections 3626(a) and (b).

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute's provisions governing appointments under subsection (f) address the appointment they prescribe. The OCC began in 1982, continued by consent through 1987 and thereafter under periodic orders; the 2001 order renewed and adjusted it. Treating that order as a fresh appointment would replace the actual continuous history with a different event. Section 802(b)(1) reaches all prospective relief, including future duties under old decrees, so this appointment conclusion preserves rather than displaces the independent restrictions on continued monitoring. Benjamin v. Jacobson requires present statutory fit. The restricted monitoring order has supported need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. The amended Rule 53 does not erase the completed appointment or make the OCC's origin an unlimited exemption from present-relief law.

### IV.A. Each continued duty requires current and tailored findings

**Controlling proposition:** Each continued prospective duty must rest on written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing federal-right violation and relief that is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct that violation, and the least intrusive necessary means. Supported relief may exceed the bare constitutional minimum when those findings establish its remedial necessity; unsupported or completed duties must terminate, and deficient findings require the particularized work specified in the remand.

**Authority:** Part IV.A, joined by all nine on substantive relief and its statutory conditions. The six-Justice OCC coalition also applies those conditions to continued monitoring.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin v. Jacobson separates a substantive violation from the statutory justification for each prospective obligation. The age of the decrees supplies neither current liability nor a reason to abandon effective correction. Bell supplies the pretrial right; section 3626 supplies additional remedial limits. The Constitution's protection does not itself establish a six-foot spacing or numerical-lighting minimum, and the lower judgment correctly requires adequate support for each challenged prescription. The facility-specific findings support the retained measures, while food service and the airflow alternative require the identified further work. The court must give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. A departure from state or local law additionally requires that federal law require it, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation, and that no other relief correct the violation. These findings preserve effective correction at its proved scope.

### IV.B. Effective alternatives and implementation of supported obligations

**Controlling proposition:** The City must substantiate any proposed narrower cure as adequate to correct the proved violation, while the court independently determines current necessity and statutory fit from the complete record. For supported obligations, the order must identify the responsible officials in their official capacities, the required acts and a reasonable compliance schedule, obtain verifiable condition reports within demonstrated need and lawful authority, and end duties whose current statutory justification has ceased; a valid, sufficiently definite order may be enforced through ordinary judicial procedures after the required notice and opportunity to respond.

**Authority:** Part IV.B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. O'Connor's reporting join requires a legally compliant arrangement and does not join OCC continuation.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin v. Jacobson requires current findings for retained duties and gives a section 3626(b)(2) movant the burden of establishing that termination provision's predicate. A City's asserted alternative must have support as a real cure; a merely cheaper or conceivable measure is insufficient. Adequate correction means correction of the proved violation, not guaranteed perfection or retention of every benefit of an old decree. Lewis v. Casey preserves a genuine opportunity for governmental design and requires consideration of effective alternatives before choosing the justified remedy. Rufo supplies distinct significant-change and tailored-modification burdens for its equitable route, not an additional prerequisite to statutory termination. Neither failure of the City's alternative nor its silence replaces the court's required findings. Identified responsibilities, dates and reliable, contestable reports make supported orders workable without extending them beyond the proved need. A completed act does not end a measure still justified to prevent recurrence; duties end when correction removes their current statutory basis, while completed lawful effects remain. Ordinary enforcement requires a valid definite command, notice, an opportunity to respond and judicial determination under the applicable procedures. The OCC supplies information and informal assistance; enforcement remains the judge's task.

## Competing paths: constitutional standard

Bell's protection against punishment governs pretrial detainees. A deliberately maintained institutional condition that lacks a reasonable nonpunitive justification, or is excessive in relation to that justification, cannot be sustained merely because each current official denies personal appreciation of the hazard. For the continuing systemic failures here, serious present or imminent injury, sustained notice, actual opportunity to respond and prolonged inadequate correction support prospective protection. Daniels excludes ordinary negligence. Lewis connects culpability to custodial duties and an actual opportunity for deliberation. Farmer's convicted-prisoner actual-awareness rule, including its prospective notice and reasonable-response qualifications, cannot simply be transplanted as an individual-officer prerequisite for every institutional pretrial claim.

The contrary path applies Farmer's actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard to environmental omissions even in pretrial custody. Its strongest objection is that harmful conditions alone cannot establish unconstitutional punishment, and the lower opinion's statement that imminent substantial harm alone is enough risks abolishing culpability. The bounded affirmative formulation above rejects that negligence-only or strict-liability reading. The present record also establishes notice and persistence sufficient for prospective relief on the narrower knowledge ground; effectiveness alone is not the universal measure of reasonable response, but years of known serious deficiencies and continued ineffective measures are the supported application here.

## Competing paths: OCC and transition

Section 3626(g)(8) covers an appointment under Rule 53 or inherent power to exercise a master's powers, regardless of title. Rule 53 now permits specified pretrial and posttrial work and expressly includes investigation or enforcement duties; its default review of objections to findings is de novo. Thus binding findings, trial hearings and clear-error deference are not universal prerequisites to master status. The amended rule begins with the qualification that another statute may provide otherwise and applies to pending proceedings only insofar as just and practicable.

The affirming classification nevertheless distinguishes the actual informal compliance assistant from an appointed surrogate exercising judicial decisional or delegated enforcement authority. Its reports inform the judge and the parties without deciding disputes or undertaking judicial enforcement. The possibility that a master could be assigned some information-gathering tasks does not make every court assistant a master. Ex parte Peterson recognizes assistance of more than one kind; the statute's functional qualification still has work to do. This ground concerns the actual powers, not the office's name, absence of binding finality alone, or a categorical exemption for all monitors. The amended Rule 53 does not retrospectively convert this continuous arrangement into an appointment made under that rule.

The opposing classification gives substantial weight to Rule 53's current investigation and enforcement provisions and section 3626(f)(6)(C)'s recognition of assistance in remedial plans. It sees the OCC's court-directed recurring compliance investigation and reporting as master's work despite informality. A judge cannot avoid the safeguards by withholding formal hearings or calling the reports advisory. On this view ongoing prospective use must conform to section 3626, and the all-prospective-relief transition prevents treating the 1982 origin as an unlimited future exemption. No Justice assumes a fresh 2001 appointment; the disagreement concerns the legal effect of continuing the actual delegation.

The lower court's independent temporal ground is preserved. Section 3626(f) repeatedly governs appointments under the subsection; its selection machinery does not retroactively recreate an actual appointment completed in 1982. That provides an additional ground for preserving this continuous pre-enactment arrangement for the affirming group. It is not immunity from section 3626(a) and (b): the continued monitoring order must satisfy those present-relief conditions, and the lower court actually found that the restricted OCC role did so. The opposing group distinguishes recreating the completed appointment from regulating its future operations and compensation. The official section 802(b)(1) text applies section 3626 to all prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment; the temporal debate cannot ignore it. The lower court's additional reliance on settled expectations cannot independently override express future-relief regulation.

## Constitutional rule and competing grounds

The six-Associate ground applies Bell's prohibition of punishment to deliberately maintained, longstanding institutional conditions. Serious present or imminent harm, sustained notice, an actual opportunity for deliberative response and prolonged inadequate correction support the prospective relief here; a condition without a reasonable nonpunitive justification or excessive in relation to that justification cannot be sustained merely because every current official's separate subjective appreciation has not been proved. Daniels excludes ordinary negligence. Lewis makes the genuine opportunity for deliberation material rather than extending its emergency-purpose test to all custodial conditions. Farmer governs the convicted-prisoner claim it decided and is not silently overruled.

The lower court's isolated formulation that substantial harm alone suffices must not be reproduced as the Supreme Court's unqualified rule. Its own record discusses years of notice and persistent inadequate response. Scalia and Thomas find that supported notice and knowing unreasonable nonresponse sufficient for this prospective judgment while rejecting removal of culpability. Neither treats unsuccessful protection alone as unreasonable, imputes innocent information across individuals to create damages liability, or uses notice acquired during litigation to prove past damages. Their ground does not join the six-Associate institutional proposition.

## OCC classification, alternative and transition

The five affirmers retain a function-specific distinction between this informal information and compliance assistant and an agent exercising delegated decisional or enforcement powers of a master. The OCC neither adjudicates discrete disputes nor conducts adjudicatory hearings, swears or subpoenas witnesses, rules on evidence, or produces findings entitled to prescribed deference; its actual reports inform the court and facilitate compliance. No one feature alone is dispositive. The judge retains responsibility for deciding rights and enforcing orders.

The lower opinion described the former Rule 53. The amendment effective December 1, 2003 permits specified pretrial and posttrial work and expressly contemplates investigation or enforcement duties, with de novo review of factual objections unless the permitted stipulation changes review. Thus trial-like hearings, binding findings and clear-error review are not universal prerequisites to master status. The affirmers acknowledge that change; they do not adopt a categorical rule exempting all monitors or infer that the procedural amendment changed constitutional law.

The five also accept the independent ground that section 3626(f)'s appointment machinery does not recreate an appointment completed in 1982. The OCC is a continuous entity; renewal and adjustment did not establish dissolution and reappointment. This does not immunize future monitoring from sections 3626(a) and (b). The actual order restricts resources and monitoring to current violations and includes sufficient need-narrowness-intrusiveness findings. Settled expectations alone do not override Congress's express treatment of future relief.

The three opponents consider the court-directed recurring compliance investigation and remedial assistance functionally master's work notwithstanding informal reporting. They distinguish retroactively recreating the original appointment from regulating current operation and compensation under the all-prospective-relief transition. Their remand would require future conformity with statutory appointment, power, review and compensation provisions or adoption of a lawful genuinely different assistance arrangement. It neither orders appointment of a replacement nor ends independently supported substantive relief. Rule 53's pending-case transition applies insofar as just and practicable.

## Holdings

### I. Longstanding systemic pretrial conditions

**Controlling proposition:** For prospective protection against deliberately maintained, longstanding systemic pretrial conditions, detainees must prove by a preponderance a current substantial risk of serious harm and conditions that lack a reasonable relation to a legitimate nonpunitive objective or are excessive in relation to that objective. Sustained notice, a real opportunity for deliberative response and prolonged inadequate correction establish the institutional culpability present here; separate proof of every responsible incumbent's subjective appreciation is unnecessary, while ordinary negligence is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Bell v. Wolfish supplies the protection against punishment before adjudication of guilt. That protection requires examining the maintained conditions and their justification, rather than making relief turn on separately proving each incumbent's private mental state. The record establishes years of notice of specified serious conditions and prolonged inadequate responses, alongside successful measures concerning other conditions. Daniels v. Williams excludes ordinary negligence. Crawford-El v. Britton supplies ordinary civil proof for the constitutional factual elements without reducing or replacing those elements. County of Sacramento v. Lewis makes the custodial duty and genuine opportunity for deliberation material; its emergency-pursuit standard does not govern these enduring conditions. Farmer v. Brennan supplies the knowledge and reasonable-response rule for the convicted-prisoner claim it decided, but that distinct rule does not displace Bell in this institutional pretrial action. The City's objection that danger alone cannot establish culpability is sound at that level and is answered by the deliberately maintained conditions, sustained notice and opportunity for correction here. Individual damages and convicted-prisoner claims lie outside this prospective pretrial question.

### II. The OCC's actual limited functions

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3626(f) applies by actual delegated function, regardless of title. The OCC's restricted observation, information gathering, informal compliance assistance and reporting, with independent judicial determination of disputes and enforcement, do not make this continuous arrangement a special master. The party defending the appointment must produce a supported account of the actual delegated functions; the judge independently determines classification and evaluates reports as evidence subject to proper objections, and a materially expanded delegation requires a new functional assessment.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3626(g)(8) covers persons appointed under Rule 53 or inherent authority to exercise a master's powers. Ex parte Peterson recognizes judicial assistance while preserving the judge's responsibility. The OCC gathers operational information and informally assists compliance. It does not conduct adjudicatory hearings, subpoena or swear witnesses, rule on evidence, adjudicate discrete disputes or issue findings entitled to prescribed deference; the judge decides rights and enforces orders. The amended Rule 53, effective December 1, 2003, includes specified pretrial and posttrial appointments, investigation and enforcement duties, and ordinarily de novo review of objections to findings. Thus absence of binding findings or trial hearings alone cannot establish the classification. The complete, limited delegation here remains informational assistance rather than delegated judicial decision or enforcement. The City's functional objection receives that full assessment; neither the OCC's name nor a general exemption for monitors determines the result. The supported-functions requirement identifies the actual powers, terms and practice relied on; it does not allocate the ultimate risk of uncertainty in every classification dispute. The pending-case transition applies insofar as just and practicable.

### III. The continuous 1982 appointment

**Independently sufficient holding:** Section 3626(f)'s appointment machinery does not recreate the OCC's completed 1982 appointment merely because later orders renew and adjust the same continuous entity. That ground independently supports continuation of this restricted arrangement, subject to the current prospective-relief requirements of sections 3626(a) and (b).

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute's provisions governing appointments under subsection (f) address the appointment they prescribe. The OCC began in 1982, continued by consent through 1987 and thereafter under periodic orders; the 2001 order renewed and adjusted it. Treating that order as a fresh appointment would replace the actual continuous history with a different event. Section 802(b)(1) reaches all prospective relief, including future duties under old decrees, so this appointment conclusion preserves rather than displaces the independent restrictions on continued monitoring. Benjamin v. Jacobson requires present statutory fit. The restricted monitoring order has supported need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. The amended Rule 53 does not erase the completed appointment or make the OCC's origin an unlimited exemption from present-relief law.

### IV.A. Each continued duty requires current and tailored findings

**Controlling proposition:** Each continued prospective duty must rest on written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing federal-right violation and relief that is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct that violation, and the least intrusive necessary means. Supported relief may exceed the bare constitutional minimum when those findings establish its remedial necessity; unsupported or completed duties must terminate, and deficient findings require the particularized work specified in the remand.

**Authority:** Part IV.A, joined by all nine on substantive relief and its statutory conditions. The six-Justice OCC coalition also applies those conditions to continued monitoring.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin v. Jacobson separates a substantive violation from the statutory justification for each prospective obligation. The age of the decrees supplies neither current liability nor a reason to abandon effective correction. Bell supplies the pretrial right; section 3626 supplies additional remedial limits. The Constitution's protection does not itself establish a six-foot spacing or numerical-lighting minimum, and the lower judgment correctly requires adequate support for each challenged prescription. The facility-specific findings support the retained measures, while food service and the airflow alternative require the identified further work. The court must give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. A departure from state or local law additionally requires that federal law require it, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation, and that no other relief correct the violation. These findings preserve effective correction at its proved scope.

### IV.B. Effective alternatives and implementation of supported obligations

**Controlling proposition:** The City must substantiate any proposed narrower cure as adequate to correct the proved violation, while the court independently determines current necessity and statutory fit from the complete record. For supported obligations, the order must identify the responsible officials in their official capacities, the required acts and a reasonable compliance schedule, obtain verifiable condition reports within demonstrated need and lawful authority, and end duties whose current statutory justification has ceased; a valid, sufficiently definite order may be enforced through ordinary judicial procedures after the required notice and opportunity to respond.

**Authority:** Part IV.B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. O'Connor's reporting join requires a legally compliant arrangement and does not join OCC continuation.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin v. Jacobson requires current findings for retained duties and gives a section 3626(b)(2) movant the burden of establishing that termination provision's predicate. A City's asserted alternative must have support as a real cure; a merely cheaper or conceivable measure is insufficient. Adequate correction means correction of the proved violation, not guaranteed perfection or retention of every benefit of an old decree. Lewis v. Casey preserves a genuine opportunity for governmental design and requires consideration of effective alternatives before choosing the justified remedy. Rufo supplies distinct significant-change and tailored-modification burdens for its equitable route, not an additional prerequisite to statutory termination. Neither failure of the City's alternative nor its silence replaces the court's required findings. Identified responsibilities, dates and reliable, contestable reports make supported orders workable without extending them beyond the proved need. A completed act does not end a measure still justified to prevent recurrence; duties end when correction removes their current statutory basis, while completed lawful effects remain. Ordinary enforcement requires a valid definite command, notice, an opportunity to respond and judicial determination under the applicable procedures. The OCC supplies information and informal assistance; enforcement remains the judge's task.
