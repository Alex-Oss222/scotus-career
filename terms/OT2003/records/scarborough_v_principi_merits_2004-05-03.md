## Controlling law

### A required allegation may complete this timely fee application

**Holding and operative rule:** An otherwise qualifying, timely application under 28 U.S.C. §2412(d)(1)(B) may be amended after the thirty-day filing period to supply the required allegation that the Government’s position lacked substantial justification, with the amendment relating back to the identified fee request. Demonstrated prejudice from the amendment defeats relation back; none was asserted here, and the corrected allegation must be accepted for ordinary fee adjudication.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies seven direct votes for the rule, application and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Scarborough timely identified the prevailing applicant, financial eligibility, requested amount and itemized attorney work. His prompt amendment supplied the sole omitted allegation without adding operative facts or a new demand. The fee request is ancillary to an action already within the court’s jurisdiction; completing it does not enlarge the waiver’s substantive monetary coverage. The allegation remains mandatory and is actually supplied. The Government retains its burden to establish substantial justification.

Becker v. Montgomery distinguishes correction of a timely identified filing from commencement of a late proceeding. Edelman v. Lynchburg College likewise permits required completion to relate back to timely initiation under its statute. Those authorities inform this independent construction of the fee procedure; relation back is not confined to Civil Rule 15 pleadings. The Secretary’s argument that every required item must arrive within thirty days does not establish an absolute statutory prohibition on completion of this same timely claim. Nor does counsel’s participation distinguish Becker’s correction principle, which applies to represented litigants too.

The Court can assume that the allegation shares the filing deadline and still allow relation back; it need not decide the distinct deadline reach of the subsection’s second sentence. The separate question of curing a missing first-sentence showing lies outside the presented omission. Permission to complete the application leaves substantive coverage, eligibility, reasonable amount and the statutory defenses for adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:** Becker and Edelman are applied by analogy for timely initiation and lawful later completion. Kontrick v. Ryan preserves the distinction between jurisdiction and mandatory procedure but does not itself authorize this amendment or treat the Government’s timely objection as forfeited. Ardestani’s substantive coverage boundary remains intact.

### The fee court must decide entitlement under the separate statutory conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Except as otherwise specifically provided by statute, section 2412(d)(1)(A) directs an award of reasonable fees and expenses to an eligible prevailing party other than the United States in a civil action other than tort, including judicial review of agency action, brought by or against the United States in a court having jurisdiction, unless the Government establishes substantial justification or special circumstances make an award unjust. Acceptance of Scarborough’s corrected allegation requires adjudication of those conditions and reasonable amount; it does not itself establish an award.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion controls this remand consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2412(d)(1)(A)’s fee command and its two distinct exceptions remain operative after the pleading defect is cured. Substantial justification concerns whether the Government’s position was justified to a degree that could satisfy a reasonable person, as Pierce v. Underwood explains. The separate special-circumstances exception concerns whether an award would be unjust. Neither is the prejudice-to-amendment limit governing relation back. The veterans court must hear the Government’s merits defense and decide any properly presented eligibility, amount or other statutory issue in a reasoned fee decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Pierce supplies substantial justification’s merits standard; the present correction rule changes neither that standard nor its burden.
