## Controlling holdings

**The ADEA's age-discrimination prohibition protects against disadvantage because of advancing age.** The Age Discrimination in Employment Act does not forbid an employer from favoring older workers over younger workers merely because both groups are at least forty. A claimant must first establish disadvantage of the kind the statutory prohibition covers before invoking its remedies.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg. Those six Justices adopt the directional construction, its application and the disposition.

The word age must be read within the statute Congress enacted. Its findings and structure address the obstacles older workers face because they are older. The statutory protection is therefore directional: entering the protected forty-and-over class does not make every age distinction within that class unlawful. The collective agreement's preservation of retirement health benefits for workers fifty and older favors the older group. The younger protected workers' objection to that preference alone does not state this ADEA claim.

Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins separates age motivation from other features, such as pension tenure, that may correlate with age. O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp. explains that a replacement's membership in the protected class does not defeat an older worker's claim of discrimination in favor of a substantially younger replacement. Neither decision answers the distinct directional-coverage question by treating all age differences symmetrically. Their proof rules remain consistent with the protection against disadvantage because of advancing age.

The dissent's reading gives the phrase because of age a bilateral meaning when isolated from the enacted scheme. The Court instead reads the prohibition, protected class and statutory findings together. The EEOC regulation's contrary position cannot override that clear contextual construction. The Court resolves the statutory meaning directly; choosing between different degrees of deference to an otherwise permissible agency construction is unnecessary to this conclusion.

## Event

**General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline, No. 02-1080.** Argued November 12, 2003; decided February 24, 2004. Review of the Sixth Circuit's recognition of an ADEA claim by workers in their forties challenging a benefit preference for older workers.

A collective agreement preserved retirement health benefits for employees fifty and older while withdrawing that future benefit for younger workers. The question is whether the ADEA prohibits that relative preference when both groups are within the protected age range.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The federal claim that the preference for older employees itself violates the ADEA must be dismissed. The case returns for entry of judgment consistent with that statutory ruling.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins:** Preserved for the distinction between actual age motivation and pension-related or other correlated factors; it did not settle directional statutory coverage.
- **O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.:** Preserved for older-worker discrimination relative to substantially younger replacements even within the protected class; its proof rule does not make the prohibition bilateral.

## Law After Decision

The ADEA reaches disadvantage because of advancing age. A preference for older workers over younger protected workers does not, by that feature alone, violate its age-discrimination prohibition. Courts must determine whether the challenged disadvantage falls within that protection before applying the statute's proof and remedial rules.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, joined by Kennedy, dissenting.** They read the enacted prohibition on discrimination because of age as covering either direction within the protected class. Congress specified the age-forty boundary but did not place a second, older-versus-younger restriction in the operative command. They reject narrowing that language through general statutory purpose or assumptions about the usual victim of age discrimination. They would sustain statutory coverage and leave remaining liability and defenses for ordinary proceedings.

**Scalia, dissenting.** Scalia would sustain coverage because the EEOC's bilateral interpretation is a permissible construction entitled to acceptance. His ground does not require agreement that the employees' reading is the single best interpretation. The regulation treats discrimination between protected-age workers bilaterally. Its separate additional-benefits provision says such benefits may be lawful if the employer has a reasonable basis to conclude they will counteract problems related to age discrimination, while forbidding their use to carry out otherwise prohibited practices. Scalia would preserve that qualification for application below with any statutory defenses, rather than exclude this claim categorically.
