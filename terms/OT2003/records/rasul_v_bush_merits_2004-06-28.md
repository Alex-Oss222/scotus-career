## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical dismissal of both the habeas claims and the Al Odah federal civil branch. Remand for effective statutory custody review and separate adjudication of the civil claims' actual jurisdictional, cause-of-action and remedial predicates.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a timely return identifying the legal and factual basis for each petitioner's custody, access necessary to present the challenge, and adjudication of properly preserved defenses. Al Odah's claims invoked §§1331 and 1350 and the APA; remove the categorical alien-abroad litigation disability while requiring those theories to satisfy their own statutory and remedial conditions.
* **Remedial Reach:** Persons held by the United States at Guantánamo under the control arrangement established here. The ruling does not decide jurisdiction at every foreign military installation or grant release to every detainee.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must set a prompt return and briefing schedule and resolve the actual custody claims, including appropriate protective procedures for substantiated security concerns. If detention lacks lawful authority, it must issue effective relief within its jurisdiction. No indictment, damages or particular destination for release is ordered by the jurisdictional judgment alone.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory habeas at Guantánamo

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Distinguish statutory habeas jurisdiction from constitutional entitlement and the detention merits. The government officials directing custody can be reached in the relevant action; ordinary domestic district-of-confinement assumptions cannot eliminate every forum for a base outside any domestic district. Other pleaded civil claims require their own jurisdictional and cause-of-action analysis.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether federal courts may hear the statutory habeas challenges and Al Odah's pleaded federal civil claims by foreign nationals held without trial at Guantánamo Bay, where the United States exercises complete jurisdiction and control.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open statutory application to Guantánamo; do not represent this extension as an already adopted holding.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: establish the statutory rule for the demonstrated Guantánamo control arrangement. A broader worldwide rule would disregard materially different foreign control and operational settings. A narrower citizen-only rule has no adequate basis in the statute governing this custody.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2241 reaches a substantial claim that United States officials hold a person at Guantánamo in violation of federal law when the court can reach the responsible custodian and the United States exercises the complete jurisdiction and control established by this record. Foreign citizenship and Cuba's reserved ultimate sovereignty do not alone defeat that statutory jurisdiction. The petitioner must still establish the prerequisites and merits of the requested relief.
* **Reach Within the Question:** United States detention at a base under complete and continuing United States jurisdiction and control, with an official respondent reachable by the court.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides those separate questions, courts apply the existing statutes and preserved constitutional precedents to a properly developed record; this jurisdictional holding supplies neither automatic release nor immunity from review.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The government controls access, administration and confinement at the base. Treating reserved ultimate sovereignty as conclusive would detach the statutory writ from the officials exercising the custody it reviews. Johnson v. Eisentrager involved convicted enemy nationals held in a different territorial and posttrial setting; Braden rejects an invariable requirement of the prisoner's physical presence in the forum. The narrower statutory ground suffices without a universal declaration of constitutional rights overseas.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply the effective statutory-review principles of INS v. St. Cyr and Calcano-Martinez v. INS. Distinguish Johnson v. Eisentrager on its actual predicates and recognize Braden's statutory effect; do not overrule a broader constitutional proposition unnecessarily.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits of combatant status, substantive extraterritorial constitutional rights, other foreign installations, damages and release destinations remain for a proper record.

### Issue 2: The separate federal civil branch

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Distinguish statutory habeas jurisdiction from constitutional entitlement and the detention merits. The government officials directing custody can be reached in the relevant action; ordinary domestic district-of-confinement assumptions cannot eliminate every forum for a base outside any domestic district. Other pleaded civil claims require their own jurisdictional and cause-of-action analysis.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether federal courts may hear the statutory habeas challenges and Al Odah's pleaded federal civil claims by foreign nationals held without trial at Guantánamo Bay, where the United States exercises complete jurisdiction and control.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering rules and resolve the identified open application; no undisclosed change is proposed.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: remove the categorical disability on which the reviewed dismissal rests. Broader recognition of every pleaded cause would bypass unresolved predicates. A narrower habeas-only disposition would leave a separately challenged basis for dismissal untreated.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Nonresident aliens held in United States military custody at Guantánamo are not categorically disabled from invoking an otherwise available federal civil cause and jurisdictional grant. Courts must assess the pleaded §§1331, 1350 and APA theories on their actual statutory, immunity and remedial predicates; neither alien status nor recognition of habeas jurisdiction decides those distinct conditions. The categorical dismissal must therefore be reversed without a present award of damages or other civil relief.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The actual nonhabeas federal claims pleaded in Al Odah, without determining every element or importing a general detention-damages action.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those pleaded civil theories are adjudicated, courts apply their existing jurisdiction, immunity, cause-of-action and remedy requirements separately; reversal of the categorical disability establishes none of them by itself.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Al Odah branch was rejected because the plaintiffs were treated as lacking a general privilege to litigate while held abroad, not because each pleaded statutory element had been adjudicated. The alien-tort cases already reject a universal alien inability to sue while preserving tightly defined causes. Removing the categorical premise permits the necessary individual analysis and avoids inventing an APA waiver or an ATS detention cause.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve the jurisdiction/action distinction in Abebe-Jira v. Negewo and Kadic v. Karadzic and distinguish Johnson v. Eisentrager rather than turn it into a universal litigation disability.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual APA reviewability and waiver, a cognizable ATS norm and cause, and appropriate nonhabeas relief were not adjudicated under their proper predicates below.

## Holdings

### I. Section 2241 reaches these Guantánamo custody claims

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2241 permits a substantial claim that reachable United States custodial officials hold a person at Guantánamo in violation of federal law where the United States exercises the complete and continuing jurisdiction and control established here. Foreign citizenship and Cuba's reserved ultimate sovereignty do not alone defeat that statutory jurisdiction; the prerequisites and merits of relief remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The lead's habeas portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute concerns custody exercised by officials whom the court can reach. The United States controls administration, access and confinement at this enduring base outside an active battlefield. Reserved ultimate sovereignty does not make that governmental control disappear. Eisentrager involved a different territorial and posttrial setting, including military convictions; its continuing framework does not foreclose these unadjudicated, potentially indefinite restraints under the control arrangement here. Braden rejects an invariable requirement that the prisoner be physically present in the forum; it need not be read to destroy Eisentrager's statutory foundation to reach this result. St. Cyr and Calcano-Martinez preserve effective statutory review while retaining actual channel predicates. The Government's proposed categorical alien-abroad rule would deny the writ despite this control and reachable custodian. The narrower statutory answer requires no universal decision about extraterritorial constitutional rights. Disputed innocence remains for the custody adjudication.

### II. Alien status does not replace the civil claims' actual predicates

**Controlling proposition:** Nonresident aliens held at Guantánamo are not categorically disabled from invoking an otherwise available federal civil action and jurisdictional grant. Courts must examine each pleaded §§1331, 1350 and APA theory for its own jurisdiction, cause of action, reviewability, immunity and remedy requirements; neither alien status nor recognition of habeas jurisdiction establishes or defeats those conditions by itself.

**Authority:** All nine join rejection of universal alien incapacity and remand of genuinely independent statutory claims. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also require reversal of the categorical dismissal of the whole civil branch. Scalia and Thomas preserve their different disposition for claims necessarily dependent on the constitutional entitlement they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira and Kadic distinguish jurisdiction from a defined domestic action with actual norm, actor and remedy predicates. Those decisions preclude a universal incapacity to sue but establish neither an ATS detention action nor a waiver of United States immunity. The same source-specific method applies to Al Odah's pleaded civil theories. Eisentrager is not a general litigation disability for every independent statutory claim merely because its claimant is an alien held abroad. The court below disposed of the branch categorically instead of resolving those requirements. Remand permits the necessary individual legal inquiry, including the actual APA reviewability and waiver, the claimed ATS norm and action, and available relief. A statutory label alone cannot make an otherwise identical constitutional theory independent.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Johnson v. Eisentrager, 339 U.S. 763:** Distinguished on the enduring control, absence of military convictions and actual confinement setting; its statutory framework and unaddressed constitutional questions are not generally overruled.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Applied to reject an invariable physical-presence requirement when the proper custodian is reachable.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Their effective statutory-review principles inform the actual §2241 channel and preserve its predicates.
- **Abebe-Jira v. Negewo; Kadic v. Karadzic:** Their distinct jurisdiction and defined-action holdings require claim-specific civil analysis and do not themselves establish a detention action or governmental immunity waiver.

## Law After Decision

Section 2241 now expressly reaches the defined Guantánamo control arrangement when the responsible custodian is reachable. Courts must adjudicate the actual custody basis and effective relief; the asserted substantive extraterritorial constitutional entitlement remains undecided because the statutory ground resolves the habeas question. Al Odah's independent civil theories return for their actual statutory, immunity and remedial inquiries. Whether a claim satisfies those rules is ordinary claim adjudication, not a reservation of the rule rejecting universal alien incapacity.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting.** Eisentrager retains the statutory limit for foreign nationals captured and confined outside sovereign United States territory. Braden concerns a domestic detainer and does not remove that foreign-custody limit. They also reject the independently presented constitutional entitlement on these foreign-capture and foreign-confinement facts. They therefore would affirm habeas dismissal and any civil claim necessarily dependent on that rejected entitlement. Their agreement with Abebe-Jira and Kadic's jurisdiction distinctions nevertheless prevents treating aliens as universally incapable of suit. They accept remand of genuinely independent statutory claims for jurisdiction, recognized action, immunity, reviewability and remedy analysis. That limited remand recognizes no new detention action or waiver and does not allow the identical constitutional claim to proceed through a changed label.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must set a prompt return and briefing schedule, provide access necessary for the custody challenge with supported protective arrangements, and adjudicate preserved defenses. It must separately examine the civil theories under their actual legal predicates and grant effective relief within its jurisdiction if unlawful custody or another actionable violation is established.
