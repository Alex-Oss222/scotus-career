## Controlling law kernel

### A continuing party's changed citizenship does not cure the initial defect

**Holding and operative rule:** Diversity jurisdiction depends on the citizenship of a continuing party when the action begins. A partnership's post-filing removal of nondiverse members does not cure diversity absent at commencement while that same partnership remains a party; the party invoking federal jurisdiction must establish the required citizenship at the controlling time.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices directly adopt the rule and jurisdictional remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Carden v. Arkoma Associates attributes every partner's citizenship to the partnership. Atlas therefore lacked complete diversity when it sued. The later departure of the Mexican partners changed the citizenship attributed to Atlas; it did not remove Atlas from the action. Mollan v. Torrance supplies the commencement rule. Section 1653 permits correction of defective jurisdictional allegations, not replacement of the underlying facts on which jurisdiction depended.

Newman-Green permits dismissal of an actual dispensable party, subject to dispensability and prejudice requirements. Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment when lawful dismissal of the actual nondiverse party creates complete diversity before trial and through judgment. That exception concerns changed parties, not changed citizenship of the same party. The individuals' appearance on a counterclaim does not turn their withdrawal from the partnership into a court-ordered dismissal curing the original plaintiff's citizenship.

Atlas's proposed extension treats elimination of the nondiverse membership interests as equivalent to dropping a litigant. Citizenship attribution and party status perform different work. The former identifies the citizenship of the entity that remains before the court; it does not create a Rule 21 dismissal of that entity's former members. Finality and the cost of repeating litigation cannot supply the missing jurisdictional authority. Grupo's late objection likewise cannot create jurisdiction by waiver. Kontrick distinguishes statutory jurisdiction from forfeitable procedural prescriptions.

**Material precedent treatment:** Carden and the commencement rule are applied. Caterpillar and Newman-Green retain the actual-party dismissal cure and its conditions, without extension to this continuing party's changed citizenship.

## Continuity and published separate positions

A continuing partnership cannot cure an initial diversity defect by changing its membership after filing. The recognized dismissal of an actual dispensable party remains available on its own requirements. Litigation expense, consent and delayed objection supply no independent federal jurisdiction.

**Ginsburg, dissenting, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer.** The four would affirm preservation of the litigation outcome against this jurisdictional objection. They accept Carden's attribution of the former members' citizenship at filing and expressly extend Caterpillar's cure principle to the real removal of those citizenship interests before trial, with complete diversity persisting through judgment. Because the partnership's jurisdictional citizenship is derived from its members, their genuine departure removes the precise obstacle that made the initial suit nondiverse.

The dissent accepts that the departed partners were not thereby dismissed as actual parties to the original action. It considers that formal difference insufficient to require destruction of a verdict reached after the relevant interests became diverse. Its proposed extension concerns this actual pretrial membership cure; expense or Grupo's delayed objection alone would not establish jurisdiction, and a defect persisting through trial would remain fatal. The four would preserve the outcome against this ground while leaving independent appellate questions to ordinary review.
