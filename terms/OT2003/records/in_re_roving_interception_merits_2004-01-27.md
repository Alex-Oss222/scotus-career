## Controlling holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** Stone-Zsela's opinion for a unanimous Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine Justices directly join reviewability, provider coverage, the service-interference rule and its application.

**1. The expired orders remain reviewable.** A challenged action remains reviewable when its duration is too short for full litigation before it ends and there is a reasonable expectation that the same challenger will again face it. The thirty-day assistance orders satisfy the duration requirement, and the Government's anticipated further requests involving this provider establish the required recurrence.

The short authorization period prevents ordinary appellate review before expiration, and the Government's stated expectation concerns this provider rather than possible orders against strangers. Both conditions are therefore established on the actual controversy. Expiration does not remove the Court's authority to resolve the recurring statutory dispute.

**2. Necessary technical assistance must preserve the statutory minimum-interference limit.** Section 2518(4) permits a court to require a covered provider's necessary technical assistance only when performed unobtrusively and with a minimum of interference with the services furnished to the intercepted person. The applicant must support the proposed method and its actual service effects; the least disruptive feasible technique still exceeds that authority when it materially disables the primary contracted assistance service instead of producing incidental interruption.

The provider supplies an electronic communications link and falls within the statutory assistance class under section 2510(15). Coverage establishes who may be directed, while section 2518(4) separately limits how assistance is performed. Hartford Underwriters requires both the enacted grant and its conditions to receive effect. Reading minimum interference as whatever disruption the preferred technique requires would consume the restriction whenever technology makes it consequential.

While interception operated, the ordinary information and roadside-assistance functions ceased working. An emergency button or airbag event sent a tone over the investigators' open line instead of contacting the provider's normal emergency response. An unassured signal to investigators is not continued availability of the contracted service. The loss of that availability establishes the relevant interference without requiring an attempted emergency call or a failed rescue.

The district court's finding of no unreasonable burden on the provider concerns a different object from the customer's loss of service. Reimbursement of provider costs and section 2518(5)'s minimization of intercepted conversations likewise cannot substitute for compliance with section 2518(4). New York Telephone's ancillary assistance authority does not override the specific service constraint. Bach permits technically necessary execution within a valid warrant's authorization; it does not enlarge this statutory assistance power. Ordinary incidental interruption remains governed by the enacted minimum-interference standard. The challenged method crosses that limit, so the order cannot stand.
