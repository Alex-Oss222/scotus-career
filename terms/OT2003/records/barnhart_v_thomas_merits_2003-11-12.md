## Holdings

### Retained capacity for previous work may end the inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Under the adult disability definitions in sections 423(d)(2)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(B), the agency may deny a claim at step four when the claimant retains capacity to perform qualifying past relevant work, without separately proving that the occupation currently exists in significant numbers in the national economy. The claimant retains the applicable step-four burden; the agency must support the actual capacity finding and apply its valid regulations. National availability governs the subsequent other-work step.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; the Court sustains a permissible construction, not an exclusively mandated statutory interpretation.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute distinguishes inability to perform previous work from inability, considering age, education and work experience, to perform other substantial gainful work existing in the national economy. The word connecting those categories does not unambiguously carry the later qualification backward. The overall impairment-caused-inability definition leaves room for this separate capacity screen. Congress's exclusions of a particular vacancy, hiring likelihood and the claimant's immediate locality fit that structure, although those exclusions alone do not resolve nationwide obsolescence.

Section 405(a), incorporated for SSI through section 1383(d)(1), supplies the actual statutory assignment, and the binding five-step regulations exercise it. Mead requires both delegation and its actual exercise; Walton identifies both in this disability scheme, while Heimmermann preserves the enacted limits. Expertise, complexity or administrative longevity alone would not supply that authority.

Past relevant work demonstrates demands the claimant actually performed and offers a reasonable measure of retained functional ability. Thomas's strongest objection is that ability to do a vanished occupation may not establish ability to earn income in any existing job. That exposes the proxy's imperfect fit, but does not make the permissible distinction between impairment-caused incapacity and job disappearance irrational. Administrative practicality supports a choice the statute permits; it cannot override the statute. The required actual capacity and qualifying-past-work findings remain essential. The agency need not conduct the additional occupational-existence inquiry that the Third Circuit required.
