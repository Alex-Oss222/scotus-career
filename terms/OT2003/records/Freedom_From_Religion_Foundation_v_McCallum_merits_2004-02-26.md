### Effective relief must reach the proved payment practice

**Controlling proposition:** The remand court must enjoin public payments for every established integrated devotional purchase at the supported contract and placement scope, while preserving reliably separated secular services. Responsible defendants seeking a narrower restriction must demonstrate that it fully cures the proved expenditure; public funding of a claimed secular remainder requires documented operational and accounting separation.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this implementation of the financing rule and its conditional, record-specific remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction of established financed religious use without demanding reproof or waiting for every application to be quantified. Columbia Union permits secular work on demonstrated operational controls rather than religious identity. Those rules place the remedial boundary at the actual publicly financed function: a narrower restriction is sufficient only if it stops that function, and the proof supporting any secular remainder must identify its actual separation. Shared corporate structure or staff alone does not defeat reliable secular performance.

The lower court must promptly determine the contract's covered work, payment terms and necessary scope of relief, using the existing trial record where sufficient. It must identify the current placement situation and provide a concrete lawful transition where needed, preserving secular services and voluntary private participation while stopping established prohibited payments. An unresolved allocation issue cannot justify indefinite continuation of a purchase already proved devotional. The separate Workforce Development judgment remains undisturbed.

## Law After Decision

Restricted correctional-service payments must fund lawful functions even where the beneficiary chooses freely among providers. A proved integrated devotional purchase requires effective prospective correction at its actual scope. Religious providers remain eligible for reliably separated secular work; neutral choice and religious identity alone answer neither the financing violation nor its remedy.
