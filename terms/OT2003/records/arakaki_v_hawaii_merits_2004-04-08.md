## Controlling holdings

### The complete ancestry exclusion fails equal protection

**Controlling proposition:** A State restricting eligibility for public office by race must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that exclusion and the objective, accounting for actual coverage, burdens and concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Hawaii's complete ancestry exclusion from elected OHA trusteeships fails that standard; otherwise qualified non-Hawaiians must be permitted to seek and hold the office.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full on this issue by the other seven Associates named in the opinion topology, supplies eight direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Rice establishes that OHA trustees are chosen in a public state election and that the ancestry definition operates as a racial qualification; it expressly left candidacy undecided. Adarand supplies the common substantial-justification standard, and Concrete Works requires government to justify the actual classification and burden rather than rely on purpose alone. Nixon v. Herndon supplies the equal-protection protection against state-created racial exclusion from public political participation. Faithful service to Hawaiian beneficiaries is an important lawful objective. But that objective does not establish that every person lacking the ancestry is unable to serve faithfully, or that ancestry guarantees competent trusteeship. Enforceable fiduciary duties can bind every trustee without a universal racial disqualification. Hawaii has not established the required relationship or justified rejecting that concrete alternative. Its distinction between trustee qualifications and the electorate explains why Rice left a new question; opening voting to everyone nevertheless does not justify the separate exclusion from eligibility to serve. The otherwise qualified candidate's denial of nomination papers establishes the concrete burden, and the record raises no disputed ordinary qualification requiring another trial. This application addresses the ancestry condition for public office, while preserving the distinct legal rules governing beneficiary programs and genuine tribal political membership.

### Election injury does not establish standing to challenge appointments

**Controlling proposition:** A plaintiff must establish an actual or imminent personal injury from the challenged appointment restriction to obtain a judgment on that restriction. Because these plaintiffs show only election-related injury, the appointment claim must be dismissed for lack of standing, without prejudice.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this portion of the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Warth v. Seldin requires the claimant's own injury on the claim presented. Exclusion from an election does not establish exclusion from an appointment or a sufficiently imminent prospect of one. The distinct appointment restriction therefore cannot be adjudicated at these plaintiffs' request; the lower court correctly vacated the appointment ruling and directed dismissal.

## Law After Decision

The ancestry qualification for elected OHA office is unenforceable under the governing equal-protection standard. Hawaii's beneficiary responsibilities remain lawful purposes, but they do not supply the missing justification for this complete exclusion. The appointment branch ends on standing. The Court's sufficient equal-protection ground supplies no independent Fifteenth Amendment or §2 candidacy rule.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the election judgment.** Equal protection should categorically forbid a racial-ancestry condition on eligibility for elected public state office, even when the electorate is open to everyone. A challenger must prove the public election, the operative racial qualification and personal exclusion by a preponderance of the evidence; proof that the candidate would win is unnecessary. Once those predicates are established, a racial constituency cannot justify the unequal civic status. Rice supplies the public-election premise, Nixon the equal-protection foundation and Smith v. Allwright a related participation analogy. Opening the ballot to voters does not cure exclusion from eligibility to serve. This specific civic-exclusion rule would leave Adarand's ordinary classification standard intact and would not decide genuine tribal governance or other Hawaiian programs. Stone-Zsela agrees with the election-wide relief and joins the Court's separate appointment-standing ruling.

## Procedure After Action

The district court shall maintain and enforce the elected-candidate relief and dismiss the appointment component without prejudice. The Supreme Court's merits review is concluded.
