## Controlling law

### Factual ineligibility for a statutory recidivist enhancement opens a confined gateway

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner who proves by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable factfinder could find the statutory facts authorizing a noncapital recidivist enhancement may obtain review of a procedurally defaulted federal constitutional challenge to that enhancement. The prisoner bears this demanding gateway burden; disagreement with discretionary sentencing is insufficient, and opening review preserves the separate requirements of a constitutional violation and applicable habeas restrictions.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies five direct votes for this rule and its application to Haley.

**Controlling explanation:** Sawyer v. Whitley separates facts making a defendant eligible for punishment from considerations governing discretionary selection within an authorized range. That distinction provides a workable boundary here. Conclusive factual ineligibility for an indispensable habitual-offender condition makes the additional years of imprisonment unauthorized in a way that a dispute over mitigation or sentencing discretion does not. The State’s capital-only objection identifies Sawyer’s original setting but does not explain why the same tightly confined miscarriage safeguard should stop at that line. Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing burden answers the concern that every sentencing complaint might evade default.

Texas acknowledges that Haley’s first prior conviction became final three days after he committed the second prior offense. The statute required the reverse sequence. This undisputed chronology meets the gateway burden. Schlup v. Delo supplies a distinct offense-innocence inquiry; its different probability standard is not imported here. The gateway concerns access to constitutional adjudication, rather than a freestanding right to a writ for violation of state sentencing law. The existence of a separately pleaded assistance claim does not justify withholding this confined gateway ruling.

**Precedent treatment:** Sawyer is extended from death eligibility to factual eligibility for a statutory noncapital recidivist enhancement, with its demanding burden and eligibility/discretion distinction retained. Schlup retains its separate offense-innocence function.

The existing conditional writ is vacated, and the case returns for prompt adjudication of the independently pleaded assistance claim and any properly reached constitutional sufficiency challenge, with each claim’s applicable collateral requirements preserved; once a properly adjudicated route supplies the complete sentence remedy, the court need not decide another route merely because it was pleaded.

**Controlling explanation:** An innocence gateway excuses default; a writ requires an independent constitutional violation and satisfaction of the habeas restrictions actually applicable to the particular claim. The admitted chronology establishes the gateway, but the constitutional classification and application require their own supported adjudication. ### Factual ineligibility for a statutory recidivist enhancement opens a confined gateway

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner who proves by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable factfinder could find the statutory facts authorizing a noncapital recidivist enhancement may obtain review of a procedurally defaulted federal constitutional challenge to that enhancement. The prisoner bears this demanding gateway burden; disagreement with discretionary sentencing is insufficient, and opening review preserves the separate requirements of a constitutional violation and applicable habeas restrictions.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies five direct votes for this rule and its application to Haley.

**Controlling explanation:** Sawyer v. Whitley separates facts making a defendant eligible for punishment from considerations governing discretionary selection within an authorized range. That distinction provides a workable boundary here. Conclusive factual ineligibility for an indispensable habitual-offender condition makes the additional years of imprisonment unauthorized in a way that a dispute over mitigation or sentencing discretion does not. The State’s capital-only objection identifies Sawyer’s original setting but does not explain why the same tightly confined miscarriage safeguard should stop at that line. Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing burden answers the concern that every sentencing complaint might evade default.

This undisputed chronology meets the gateway burden. Schlup v. Delo supplies a distinct offense-innocence inquiry; its different probability standard is not imported here.
