## Controlling law of the decision

### I. Municipal discharge regulation may offer a genuine alternative to third-party administration

**Controlling proposition:** The Federal Government may regulate a municipality's own covered pollutant discharges and offer an optional management program when a lawful, available alternative permits compliance through direct regulation of those discharges instead of compulsory administration of third-party rules. The Government must identify that operative alternative when relying on choice, and the challenger may establish that its availability is illusory; ordinary costs of valid own-operation regulation do not become federal-assistance reimbursement claims.

**Authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the entire opinion, including both holdings and their explanations at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** The municipal operators' concrete obligations concerning their systems and work supply injury, traceability and redressability. Lomont v. O'Neill rejects a categorical state-authorization barrier to that own-work claim. The merits then depend on what the rules actually require.

Reno v. Condon permits direct regulation of a State's own activity despite the employee work needed for compliance. New York v. United States requires a real alternative to compulsory sovereign administration. Some listed stormwater measures do require regulation of third parties; sewer ownership does not itself justify those commands. But sections 122.33(b)(2)(ii) and 122.34(a) provide a different-program application under section 122.26(d), excepted from the listed minimum controls. The permission to omit legal-authority information and the demonstrated alternative experience support that route's availability here.

The municipalities argue that a demanding individual application and compliance expense make the alternative illusory. Those burdens do not establish that every available route requires the same unwanted third-party administration. Lawful control of the operator's own discharge may remain compulsory and costly. Lomont's freedom to decline assistance to private applicants does not confer a right to refuse that direct regulation. The alternative's substance and actual access, rather than a permit label, supply the answer; the application provisions guarantee neither a particular final permit nor a numerical end-of-pipe limit.

Printz and Mack forbid Congress to command unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons, while preserving direct regulation of a State's own activity. The defect they identify is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and they create no compensation rule. A genuine alternative to third-party administration is not such compulsion, and ordinary municipal operating costs do not become reimbursable federal assistance. On this facial record the genuine direct-discharge route defeats the commandeering claim. A specific permit that eliminates that route must be judged from its actual terms and record under the same anti-command and genuine-choice rules.

**Regulatory terms material to the application:** A regulated small municipal separate storm sewer system must seek discharge authorization from its authorized State, Tribe or Territory, or from the EPA Regional Office where no authorized program operates. Federal permitting remains available when a State declines administration. Under section 122.33(b)(2)(ii), an operator seeking a different individual program uses section 122.26(d)'s application requirements. It may omit the legal-authority information described in sections 122.26(d)(1)(ii) and (d)(2), unless it wants the permit writer to consider that information in developing other permit conditions. This is permission to omit that material; the remaining applicable application requirements continue. Section 122.34(a) excepts that route from its listed minimum controls, while lawful information requirements and final discharge conditions remain separately applicable.

For the minimum-control route, section 122.34 requires a program designed to reduce pollutants to the maximum extent practicable, protect water quality and satisfy appropriate Clean Water Act requirements. The permitting authority specifies up to five years from permit issuance for development and implementation. The six measures are public education and outreach, public involvement, illicit-discharge detection and elimination, construction-site controls, post-construction controls, and pollution prevention and housekeeping for municipal operations. Public involvement must at least comply with State, Tribal and local public-notice requirements. Municipal housekeeping includes an operation and maintenance program with employee training to prevent and reduce runoff from activities such as park, fleet, building, construction and stormwater-system maintenance.

The third-party controls have distinct operative conditions:

- **Illicit discharges:** The operator must develop, implement and enforce a detection-and-elimination program, prepare the required outfall and receiving-water map if not already completed, and develop a plan to detect and address non-stormwater discharges, including illegal dumping. Its effective prohibition through ordinance or another regulatory mechanism, with enforcement procedures and actions, operates to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law.
- **Listed flows:** The operator need address the following flows only if it identifies them as significant contributors of pollutants to the small system: water-line flushing, landscape irrigation, diverted stream flows, rising groundwater, uncontaminated groundwater infiltration, uncontaminated pumped groundwater, potable-water discharges, foundation drains, air-conditioning condensation, irrigation water, springs, crawl-space pump water, footing drains, lawn watering, individual residential car washing, riparian-habitat and wetland flows, dechlorinated pool discharges, and street wash water. Firefighting discharges are separately excluded from the effective non-stormwater prohibition and need be addressed only when identified as significant pollutant sources to waters of the United States. The latter condition concerns the receiving waters, not the small-system destination used for the listed flows.
- **Construction controls:** The program covers construction disturbing at least one acre, including smaller disturbances forming part of a common development or sale plan disturbing at least one acre. It includes an ordinance or regulatory mechanism requiring erosion and sediment controls and compliance sanctions to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law; appropriate erosion and sediment practices; control of site waste capable of harming water quality; review of plans for water-quality effects; receipt and consideration of public information; and inspection and enforcement procedures. When the permitting authority grants the incorporated small-construction waiver, the municipality need not develop, implement or enforce the corresponding program for the waived sites.
- **Covered small construction and waiver:** Section 122.26(b)(15)(i) covers clearing, grading and excavation disturbing at least one but less than five acres, including smaller components of a common development or sale plan ultimately disturbing at least one but less than five acres. Routine maintenance preserving the original line and grade, hydraulic capacity or original purpose is excluded. The Director may waive otherwise applicable general-permit requirements for construction disturbing less than five acres through either of the two paths below; qualifying acreage alone does not grant a waiver. Section 122.26(b)(15)(ii) separately permits designation of other construction by the Director or, in an approved State program, the Director or EPA Regional Administrator, based on potential contribution to a water-quality-standard violation or significant pollutants to covered waters.
- **Rainfall path:** The rainfall erosivity factor must be less than five during construction, calculated under Chapter 2, pages 21–64, of the incorporated January 1997 Agriculture Handbook 703 RUSLE method. The operator must certify to the Director that construction will occur during a period meeting that criterion.
- **Water-quality-analysis path:** Controls must be unnecessary under an EPA-approved or EPA-established total maximum daily load addressing the pollutants of concern. For non-impaired waters that do not require such a load, an equivalent analysis must allocate pollutants to small construction sites or determine that allocations are unnecessary to protect water quality after considering existing in-stream concentrations, expected growth in pollutant contributions from all sources and a margin of safety. Pollutants of concern include sediment or a sediment measure, such as suspended solids, turbidity or siltation, and every other pollutant identified as causing impairment of a receiving water body. The operator must certify to the Director that construction and stormwater discharges will occur within the analysis's drainage area. The permitting authority's waiver remains permissive under either path.
- **Post-construction controls:** New development and redevelopment disturbing at least one acre, including smaller projects within a larger common development or sale plan, are covered when they discharge into the small system. The operator must ensure controls preventing or minimizing water-quality impacts, use appropriate structural or nonstructural practices, employ an ordinance or regulatory mechanism to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law, and ensure adequate long-term operation and maintenance. The construction-site waiver is not an exception to this separate provision.

Text labeled Guidance recommends methods; it does not make each suggested civic project, outreach medium, planning strategy, inspection technique or enforcement example compulsory. The minimum-control duties themselves remain duties within that route. These actual qualifications define the program considered here; the different-program route, rather than the State-law qualifications alone, provides the genuine alternative to compulsory third-party administration.

### II. Factual information serving the operator's pollution-control function may be required within defined expression safeguards

**Controlling proposition:** Assuming municipal operators may assert the claimed speech protection, a lawful pollution-control program may require truthful, nonideological information about the system's discharges, runoff reduction and disposal hazards when the agency justifies a substantial health or environmental purpose, a direct practical connection to the regulated operation, and a confined obligation with meaningful flexibility that is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. The requirement may not compel falsehood, political allegiance, ideological or personal endorsement, or suppression of contrary views; public usefulness, a factual label or presumed agreement alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly supports this defined operational-information rule and its application, independently of the permit-choice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The physical discharge-control program exists independently of the required communication. Community inputs and improper disposal affect the pollutants the operator's system discharges. Information about those effects and practical reduction therefore serves an existing operating function, rather than making the message its own justification.

Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's private-subsidy ruling rests on an independently justified function, nonattribution of the promotional message to dissenting producers, and freedom to advertise independently and criticize the program. United States v. United Foods bars selected promotion from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. The present duty concerns factual pollution operations, with freedom to identify the federal requirement and criticize its policy. The municipality may also be identified as the information source; the decision does not assume that all communications are unattributed.

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell supplies a bounded analogy for justified product-and-hazard disclosures and undue-burden review. Municipal outreach is not automatically commercial product speech. The Court adopts this distinct operational application because the lawful physical program, substantial environmental purpose, direct practical connection, confined nonideological content and flexible means supply the required independent justification. It does not infer a general factual-speech exception from the program's breadth.

The municipalities correctly observe that direct communication is speech and that a permit cannot compel unrelated advocacy. But the actual provisions require runoff-impact and disposal-hazard information, not agreement with EPA's policy. Barnette and Wooley's protections against coerced belief remain effective. The flexible methods and ability to use existing materials bear on burden without establishing that all implementation is inexpensive. The facial challenge establishes neither compelled ideology or falsehood nor an unjustified or undue burden in these limited duties. The same justification, fit and burden requirements govern an actual different implementation. Municipal constitutional personhood generally is unnecessary to this disposition and is not decided.

**Required communications and application:** Section 122.34(b)(1)(i) requires distribution of educational materials or equivalent outreach about stormwater discharges' effects on water bodies and the steps the public can take to reduce runoff pollutants; it does not require both methods. Section 122.34(b)(3)(ii)(D) separately requires informing public employees, businesses and the general public about hazards from illegal discharges and improper waste disposal. These confined obligations satisfy the operational rule, and their facial speech challenge fails. The alternative permit route does not eliminate every information requirement and is not the independent ground for this speech holding.

## Precedent treatment

- **Reno v. Condon, January 12, 2000:** Applied to direct regulation of municipal discharge operations and the employee work required for compliance. Its own-activity distinction does not itself validate administration of rules against third parties.
- **New York v. United States, June 19, 1992:** Applied to require a lawful, genuine alternative to compulsory sovereign responsibility; two compulsory third-party programs would not supply that choice.
- **Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States, June 27, 1997:** Distinguished. Its rule that Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons does not reach ordinary compliance with direct regulation of a municipality's own operations or a genuine alternative to third-party administration. The defect it identifies is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and it creates no compensation rule.
- **Lomont v. O'Neill, March 11, 2004:** Applied to concrete own-work injury and examination of actual legal choices. Its unconditional refusal of private-applicant assistance is distinguished from compliance with valid own-discharge controls.
- **Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc., June 25, 1997:** Applied for an independently justified regulatory function and communication germane to it, with independent criticism preserved. Its private promotional-subsidy and attribution conditions retain their own scope.
- **United States v. United Foods, Inc., June 25, 2001:** Applied to prevent a selected message from creating its own compulsory justification. The separate physical pollution-control function distinguishes these operational duties from advertising-only promotion.
- **National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, March 17, 2003:** Its justification, practical fit and undue-burden limits inform the confined operational application here. Its commercial product-and-hazard category is preserved rather than treating municipal outreach as commercial by definition.
- **West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette, 319 U.S. 624:** Preserved against compelled ideological affirmation; operational pollution facts must remain within the adopted nonideological limits.
- **Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705:** Preserved against compelled personal ideological endorsement; the factual-information duty allows attribution to the federal requirement and independent disagreement.

## II. Federalism: alternative paths and controlling ground proposed

**Proposed rule.** A municipality remains subject to valid federal regulation of the discharges from its own storm-sewer operation. A facial challenge that third-party minimum measures commandeer its legislative or executive authority fails when a legally available, operative individual-permit route lets it comply through regulation of those own discharges without requiring the challenged administration of third persons. A nominal choice between two compulsory third-party programs would not suffice. Lawful direct compliance may cost money and remain compulsory without becoming involuntary assistance to federal administration.

Condon supplies the own-activity distinction: the work government employees perform to comply with direct regulation does not by itself turn the regulated government's own activity into conscription to regulate others. New York requires a lawful alternative rather than a choice between two forbidden duties. Lomont confirms that actual legal consequences control over the optional label, but it involved assistance to private applicants. Its unconditional right to refuse that assistance does not become a right to refuse lawful regulation of the municipality's own discharges.

**The strongest contrary path** treats the regulations as a practical command to enact ordinances and police private construction, since individual permitting may be more expensive and section 122.26(d) entails a demanding application. The objection correctly identifies some minimum measures as third-party administration. It fails on this facial record because section 122.33 authorizes a different program, allows omission of legal-authority material unless the applicant wants it considered, and section 122.34 expressly excludes that route from the listed minimum controls. The record's alternative experience supports the textual availability. Costs and inconvenience of lawful own-discharge compliance do not prove that the municipality must assume federal responsibility for regulating other persons.

The Court does not infer validity from the word permit. It distinguishes the application from the eventual conditions and requires a genuinely operative own-discharge alternative. A record establishing that the authority denied the different program solely because the municipality refused third-party legislative administration, or imposed equivalent compulsory third-party duties in the final alternative, would present a different concrete application. No such final condition or universal practical barrier is established here. That limitation prevents facial affirmance from becoming advance approval of every permit.

**The fiscal argument** supplies no independent basis for invalidation. Printz's controlling rule forbids commanding unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons; it locates the defect in compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and creates no compensation rule. This case's own-system costs are direct regulatory compliance, and the choice of minimum measures does not itself establish compelled executive administration. Neither Printz nor Lomont grants an entitlement to payment for those costs. No compensation measure, cause of action or appropriation is created or assumed.

## III. Speech: content, independent function and burden

The speech component is decided independently of whether the alternative route defeats the commandeering claim. An optional route does not establish a universal waiver of constitutional expression objections, and the alternative application does not make every information obligation disappear.

Section 122.34(b)(1)(i) requires educational materials **or equivalent outreach** concerning stormwater discharges' effects on water bodies and steps the public can take to reduce pollutants in runoff. It does not require both distribution and a separately prescribed campaign. Section 122.34(b)(3)(ii)(D) requires informing public employees, businesses and the general public about hazards associated with illegal discharges and improper waste disposal. The first concerns runoff impacts and practical reduction; the second concerns the hazards of illegal discharge and disposal. Neither is rewritten as an obligation to profess a belief or to sponsor every activity in the guidance.

**Proposed bounded rule.** Even assuming the municipal operators may assert the claimed First Amendment protection, a valid discharge-control regime may require them to provide truthful, nonideological information about their systems' pollution impacts and practical ways to avoid harmful inputs when that communication serves the independently justified operating and pollution-prevention function and is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. The government must justify that function and fit from the actual obligation; usefulness, a factual label and presumed agreement alone are insufficient. This holding concerns the defined system-related hazard information, not all public messages a regulator regards as beneficial.

Here the substantive program regulates pollutant discharges, independently of any desire to disseminate an opinion. Community inputs and improper disposal affect what the operator's system discharges. Informing people of those effects, reduction measures and disposal hazards directly serves that physical pollution-control purpose. The actual requirements permit flexible factual materials or equivalent outreach rather than prescribing ideological language. Suggestions to participate in particular civic activities are labeled guidance. The provisions contain no command to endorse EPA's policy judgments or restriction on independent criticism of the program.

The communications may identify the municipality as the operator and source of the information. The Court does not rely on an invented guarantee that no message will be attributed to it. Communicating factual hazards associated with its regulated operation is different from being required to adopt someone else's ideological belief. The absence of prescribed ideology is examined together with the real operating function and scope, not treated as sufficient by itself.

NEMA supplies a close, bounded analogy, not an automatic classification. Its rule concerns truthful, noncontroversial **commercial product** and disposal facts, justified by a substantial health or environmental purpose and protected against undue burdens. Municipal outreach has not been shown to be commercial product speech merely because it occurs within a regulatory program. The present ground extends the functional principle only to the actual system-related factual hazard communication: lawful substantive pollution controls, a substantial health/environmental objective, a direct practical connection, and a confined nonideological obligation whose method retains meaningful flexibility. It does not apply reduced protection to every municipal statement or announce a general factual-speech exception.

Glickman shows why a germane communication expense may serve an independently justified regulatory function, but its private promotional-subsidy rule is not imported wholesale. United Foods forbids letting the selected message supply its own independent justification; the pollution controls and operational responsibilities here exist independently of the public-information requirement. Barnette and Wooley protect against coerced ideological affirmation. These provisions require hazard information and practical pollution reduction, not a declaration of personal or political allegiance. Presumed municipal agreement with environmental protection is no ground for the decision.

**The competing invalidation path** emphasizes that information and education are speech, that direct communication is more than paying a fee, and that the government cannot compel advocacy simply by attaching it to a valid program. All eight accept those premises. They reject the facial conclusion because the actual duties are confined to information about the regulated system's discharges and harmful inputs, serve a separate substantive pollution-control function and leave the operator a choice of factual means. The requirements do not prescribe the municipality's position on a disputed policy or silence contrary views.

The rule's flexible methods and permission to use existing materials bear on practical burden; they do not prove that every implementation is inexpensive. There is no supported facial showing that the actual limited communications require unjustified expense, misleading content or ideological endorsement. A regulator could not sustain a materially different message or undue burden by pointing only to today's judgment. This is a bounded merits rejection on the actual provisions, without deciding municipal constitutional status generally or requiring an advertising-effectiveness trial.

## Holdings

### I. Municipal discharge regulation may offer a genuine alternative to third-party administration

**Controlling proposition:** The Federal Government may regulate a municipality's own covered pollutant discharges and offer an optional management program when a lawful, available alternative permits compliance through direct regulation of those discharges instead of compulsory administration of third-party rules. The Government must identify that operative alternative when relying on choice, and the challenger may establish that its availability is illusory; ordinary costs of valid own-operation regulation do not become federal-assistance reimbursement claims.

**Authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the entire opinion, including both holdings and their explanations at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** The municipal operators' concrete obligations concerning their systems and work supply injury, traceability and redressability. Lomont v. O'Neill rejects a categorical state-authorization barrier to that own-work claim. The merits then depend on what the rules actually require.

Reno v. Condon permits direct regulation of a State's own activity despite the employee work needed for compliance. New York v. United States requires a real alternative to compulsory sovereign administration. Some listed stormwater measures do require regulation of third parties; sewer ownership does not itself justify those commands. But sections 122.33(b)(2)(ii) and 122.34(a) provide a different-program application under section 122.26(d), excepted from the listed minimum controls. The permission to omit legal-authority information and the demonstrated alternative experience support that route's availability here.

The municipalities argue that a demanding individual application and compliance expense make the alternative illusory. Those burdens do not establish that every available route requires the same unwanted third-party administration. Lawful control of the operator's own discharge may remain compulsory and costly. Lomont's freedom to decline assistance to private applicants does not confer a right to refuse that direct regulation. The alternative's substance and actual access, rather than a permit label, supply the answer; the application provisions guarantee neither a particular final permit nor a numerical end-of-pipe limit.

Printz and Mack forbid Congress to command unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons, while preserving direct regulation of a State's own activity. The defect they identify is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and they create no compensation rule. A genuine alternative to third-party administration is not such compulsion, and ordinary municipal operating costs do not become reimbursable federal assistance. On this facial record the genuine direct-discharge route defeats the commandeering claim. A specific permit that eliminates that route must be judged from its actual terms and record under the same anti-command and genuine-choice rules.

**Regulatory terms material to the application:** A regulated small municipal separate storm sewer system must seek discharge authorization from its authorized State, Tribe or Territory, or from the EPA Regional Office where no authorized program operates. Federal permitting remains available when a State declines administration. Under section 122.33(b)(2)(ii), an operator seeking a different individual program uses section 122.26(d)'s application requirements. It may omit the legal-authority information described in sections 122.26(d)(1)(ii) and (d)(2), unless it wants the permit writer to consider that information in developing other permit conditions. This is permission to omit that material; the remaining applicable application requirements continue. Section 122.34(a) excepts that route from its listed minimum controls, while lawful information requirements and final discharge conditions remain separately applicable.

For the minimum-control route, section 122.34 requires a program designed to reduce pollutants to the maximum extent practicable, protect water quality and satisfy appropriate Clean Water Act requirements. The permitting authority specifies up to five years from permit issuance for development and implementation. The six measures are public education and outreach, public involvement, illicit-discharge detection and elimination, construction-site controls, post-construction controls, and pollution prevention and housekeeping for municipal operations. Public involvement must at least comply with State, Tribal and local public-notice requirements. Municipal housekeeping includes an operation and maintenance program with employee training to prevent and reduce runoff from activities such as park, fleet, building, construction and stormwater-system maintenance.

The third-party controls have distinct operative conditions:

- **Illicit discharges:** The operator must develop, implement and enforce a detection-and-elimination program, prepare the required outfall and receiving-water map if not already completed, and develop a plan to detect and address non-stormwater discharges, including illegal dumping. Its effective prohibition through ordinance or another regulatory mechanism, with enforcement procedures and actions, operates to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law.
- **Listed flows:** The operator need address the following flows only if it identifies them as significant contributors of pollutants to the small system: water-line flushing, landscape irrigation, diverted stream flows, rising groundwater, uncontaminated groundwater infiltration, uncontaminated pumped groundwater, potable-water discharges, foundation drains, air-conditioning condensation, irrigation water, springs, crawl-space pump water, footing drains, lawn watering, individual residential car washing, riparian-habitat and wetland flows, dechlorinated pool discharges, and street wash water. Firefighting discharges are separately excluded from the effective non-stormwater prohibition and need be addressed only when identified as significant pollutant sources to waters of the United States. The latter condition concerns the receiving waters, not the small-system destination used for the listed flows.
- **Construction controls:** The program covers construction disturbing at least one acre, including smaller disturbances forming part of a common development or sale plan disturbing at least one acre. It includes an ordinance or regulatory mechanism requiring erosion and sediment controls and compliance sanctions to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law; appropriate erosion and sediment practices; control of site waste capable of harming water quality; review of plans for water-quality effects; receipt and consideration of public information; and inspection and enforcement procedures. When the permitting authority grants the incorporated small-construction waiver, the municipality need not develop, implement or enforce the corresponding program for the waived sites.
- **Covered small construction and waiver:** Section 122.26(b)(15)(i) covers clearing, grading and excavation disturbing at least one but less than five acres, including smaller components of a common development or sale plan ultimately disturbing at least one but less than five acres. Routine maintenance preserving the original line and grade, hydraulic capacity or original purpose is excluded. The Director may waive otherwise applicable general-permit requirements for construction disturbing less than five acres through either of the two paths below; qualifying acreage alone does not grant a waiver. Section 122.26(b)(15)(ii) separately permits designation of other construction by the Director or, in an approved State program, the Director or EPA Regional Administrator, based on potential contribution to a water-quality-standard violation or significant pollutants to covered waters.
- **Rainfall path:** The rainfall erosivity factor must be less than five during construction, calculated under Chapter 2, pages 21–64, of the incorporated January 1997 Agriculture Handbook 703 RUSLE method. The operator must certify to the Director that construction will occur during a period meeting that criterion.
- **Water-quality-analysis path:** Controls must be unnecessary under an EPA-approved or EPA-established total maximum daily load addressing the pollutants of concern. For non-impaired waters that do not require such a load, an equivalent analysis must allocate pollutants to small construction sites or determine that allocations are unnecessary to protect water quality after considering existing in-stream concentrations, expected growth in pollutant contributions from all sources and a margin of safety. Pollutants of concern include sediment or a sediment measure, such as suspended solids, turbidity or siltation, and every other pollutant identified as causing impairment of a receiving water body. The operator must certify to the Director that construction and stormwater discharges will occur within the analysis's drainage area. The permitting authority's waiver remains permissive under either path.
- **Post-construction controls:** New development and redevelopment disturbing at least one acre, including smaller projects within a larger common development or sale plan, are covered when they discharge into the small system. The operator must ensure controls preventing or minimizing water-quality impacts, use appropriate structural or nonstructural practices, employ an ordinance or regulatory mechanism to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law, and ensure adequate long-term operation and maintenance. The construction-site waiver is not an exception to this separate provision.

Text labeled Guidance recommends methods; it does not make each suggested civic project, outreach medium, planning strategy, inspection technique or enforcement example compulsory. The minimum-control duties themselves remain duties within that route. These actual qualifications define the program considered here; the different-program route, rather than the State-law qualifications alone, provides the genuine alternative to compulsory third-party administration.

### II. Factual information serving the operator's pollution-control function may be required within defined expression safeguards

**Controlling proposition:** Assuming municipal operators may assert the claimed speech protection, a lawful pollution-control program may require truthful, nonideological information about the system's discharges, runoff reduction and disposal hazards when the agency justifies a substantial health or environmental purpose, a direct practical connection to the regulated operation, and a confined obligation with meaningful flexibility that is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. The requirement may not compel falsehood, political allegiance, ideological or personal endorsement, or suppression of contrary views; public usefulness, a factual label or presumed agreement alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly supports this defined operational-information rule and its application, independently of the permit-choice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The physical discharge-control program exists independently of the required communication. Community inputs and improper disposal affect the pollutants the operator's system discharges. Information about those effects and practical reduction therefore serves an existing operating function, rather than making the message its own justification.

Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott's private-subsidy ruling rests on an independently justified function, nonattribution of the promotional message to dissenting producers, and freedom to advertise independently and criticize the program. United States v. United Foods bars selected promotion from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. The present duty concerns factual pollution operations, with freedom to identify the federal requirement and criticize its policy. The municipality may also be identified as the information source; the decision does not assume that all communications are unattributed.

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell supplies a bounded analogy for justified product-and-hazard disclosures and undue-burden review. Municipal outreach is not automatically commercial product speech. The Court adopts this distinct operational application because the lawful physical program, substantial environmental purpose, direct practical connection, confined nonideological content and flexible means supply the required independent justification. It does not infer a general factual-speech exception from the program's breadth.

The municipalities correctly observe that direct communication is speech and that a permit cannot compel unrelated advocacy. But the actual provisions require runoff-impact and disposal-hazard information, not agreement with EPA's policy. Barnette and Wooley's protections against coerced belief remain effective. The flexible methods and ability to use existing materials bear on burden without establishing that all implementation is inexpensive. The facial challenge establishes neither compelled ideology or falsehood nor an unjustified or undue burden in these limited duties. The same justification, fit and burden requirements govern an actual different implementation. Municipal constitutional personhood generally is unnecessary to this disposition and is not decided.

**Required communications and application:** Section 122.34(b)(1)(i) requires distribution of educational materials or equivalent outreach about stormwater discharges' effects on water bodies and the steps the public can take to reduce runoff pollutants; it does not require both methods. Section 122.34(b)(3)(ii)(D) separately requires informing public employees, businesses and the general public about hazards from illegal discharges and improper waste disposal. These confined obligations satisfy the operational rule, and their facial speech challenge fails. The alternative permit route does not eliminate every information requirement and is not the independent ground for this speech holding.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Reno v. Condon, January 12, 2000:** Applied to direct regulation of municipal discharge operations and the employee work required for compliance. Its own-activity distinction does not itself validate administration of rules against third parties.
- **New York v. United States, June 19, 1992:** Applied to require a lawful, genuine alternative to compulsory sovereign responsibility; two compulsory third-party programs would not supply that choice.
- **Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States, June 27, 1997:** Distinguished. Its rule that Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons does not reach ordinary compliance with direct regulation of a municipality's own operations or a genuine alternative to third-party administration. The defect it identifies is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and it creates no compensation rule.
- **Lomont v. O'Neill, March 11, 2004:** Applied to concrete own-work injury and examination of actual legal choices. Its unconditional refusal of private-applicant assistance is distinguished from compliance with valid own-discharge controls.
- **Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc., June 25, 1997:** Applied for an independently justified regulatory function and communication germane to it, with independent criticism preserved. Its private promotional-subsidy and attribution conditions retain their own scope.
- **United States v. United Foods, Inc., June 25, 2001:** Applied to prevent a selected message from creating its own compulsory justification. The separate physical pollution-control function distinguishes these operational duties from advertising-only promotion.
- **National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, March 17, 2003:** Its justification, practical fit and undue-burden limits inform the confined operational application here. Its commercial product-and-hazard category is preserved rather than treating municipal outreach as commercial by definition.
- **West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette, 319 U.S. 624:** Preserved against compelled ideological affirmation; operational pollution facts must remain within the adopted nonideological limits.
- **Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705:** Preserved against compelled personal ideological endorsement; the factual-information duty allows attribution to the federal requirement and independent disagreement.

## Law After Decision

Effective March 25, 2004, municipal own-discharge regulation may employ the challenged permit architecture when a lawful and actually available route permits direct compliance without compulsory administration of third-party rules. Government must identify that route when relying on choice; an operator may show it to be illusory. Ordinary costs of valid own-operation compliance are not converted into assistance reimbursement claims. Application requirements and final permit obligations remain distinct, and the retained administrative remands continue to govern permitting.

The decision also establishes a confined operational-information application: factual pollution-impact, reduction and disposal-hazard information may be required within the independent physical function, substantial purpose, direct practical fit, flexibility and justification-and-burden limits stated in Holding II. This governing permission extends beyond automatic classification as commercial product speech while retaining the compelled-belief and independent-criticism protections. The validity of a materially different final message, burden or permit condition is assessed under those existing limits on its own record.
