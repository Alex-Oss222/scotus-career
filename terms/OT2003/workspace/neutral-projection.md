### Demore v. Kim — 2003-10-14

Effective October 14, 2003, section 1226(e) preserves independently proper habeas review of the legality of the mandatory framework, with effective relief and the ordinary limits on discretionary review and initial agency factfinding. Section 1226(c) may operate before a final removal order, subject to actual consideration of disputed statutory membership. Lawful permanent residents with a category question that is not insubstantial and not interposed solely for delay have a statutory entitlement to individual flight-risk and danger consideration, with each independently sufficient mandatory ground addressed.

These jurisdiction and statutory-threshold propositions are direct majority holdings. Kim's remand applies the detainee clear-and-convincing risk showing and preserves his current release through the threshold determination and any consequent bond decision under a five-vote judgment. The burden rationale has four joins and supplies no general majority proof rule. The independent constitutional hearing and continued-detention formulations remain without a majority at the necessary level of generality; a distinct duration claim also requires findings about the course of detention. Classification and individual-risk findings on remand are applications of the stated instructions, not unresolved statutory standards.

Published separate positions:

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** He joins Parts I, II-A and II-B and accepts the entire vacatur, remand and interim-release remedy so Kim can receive the individual assessment available under the statutory pathway. He withholds assent from the allocation of persuasion to Kim. In his view, when genuinely contested inclusion or constitutionally insufficient continuing authority requires individual review, the Government should prove by clear and convincing evidence that reasonably available conditions, separately or together, cannot adequately address flight risk or danger; he would also accept governmental preponderance with the same object of proof and effective relief. Mathews directs attention to liberty and error; Salerno and Addington offer regime-specific support for the preferred quantum; Cooper illustrates the consequences of a burden that wrongly allocates uncertainty. Krimstock supports meaningful review and consideration of alternatives, while his separate preponderance position there remains his own. Kim seeks liberty in a civil immigration proceeding, not release pending appeal of his criminal convictions, so section 3143(b) does not persuade him to place the risk of uncertainty on Kim. The neutral decisionmaker should receive contrary evidence, assess residence, family and community ties, convictions, release conduct, appearance evidence and feasible conditions, and give reviewable reasons; failure of the governmental showing should permit conditional release.

He also explains that lawful initial detention does not justify continuation after its nature, duration or circumstances lose a reasonable relationship to removal proceedings pursued with reasonable diligence. A resident would identify a concrete continuing-custody concern; the Government would supply the chronology, basis, delay explanations and anticipated course and prove disputed diligence facts by preponderance. Legitimate substantial defenses are not automatically misconduct; solely dilatory conduct may count against the resident, and unjustified governmental or institutional delay weighs against categorical continuation. If that authority fails, individualized justification and effective relief are required; material change permits renewed review. Gilbert, Fairley, Foucha and Jackson support the continuing-authority inquiry; Flores preserves lawful temporary custody, and Zadvydas contributes the removal-purpose relationship without its separate calendar or burden. The present statutory holding leaves this constitutional limit open. His proposals are noncontrolling, supply no finding that Kim's past detention crossed the limit, and neither condition the entered judgment nor alter its remand instructions.

**Justice Souter, concurring, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** They join the entire lead opinion because its substantial-question pathway affords genuine individual consideration and protects Kim's existing release during the inquiry. They retain the broader view that a lawful permanent resident must receive a reasonably prompt, impartial opportunity to contest the necessity of custody on flight-risk and danger grounds. A past criminal category cannot conclusively establish present need throughout the proceeding. Carlson retained individual discretion, Flores retained real release and review avenues, and Zadvydas ties detention to its purpose; National Center and the continuing-deprivation decisions support effective inquiry into the actual justification. A rebuttable initial category presumption may be permissible, but meaningful consideration and effective release authority are essential. Their statutory join accepts Part III's allocation within that pathway, without making it a universal burden or approving categorical constitutional validity outside the threshold. That broader constitutional position is noncontrolling.

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** They accept pre-order operation of section 1226(c), but would reverse the resident-wide constitutional hearing requirement and remand for implementation in the actual custody and removal posture. Congress may make a properly established criminal category sufficient during reasonably dispatched removal proceedings. Flores supports temporary categorical arrangements; the statutory classification, rather than risk alone, supplies the substantive predicate. Gilbert, Krimstock and Fairley require testing the actual legal justification, not adding a new risk exception to a valid category. Joseph and the proper review channels must address genuine membership disputes. A substantial challenge does not itself suspend Congress's custody rule, and the express witness-protection exception does not authorize the Court's implied bond pathway. The disputed charges require their own lawful treatment; the requested continuance and elapsed six months do not establish the exceptional constitutional defect asserted here. O'Connor and Kennedy join the Court's jurisdiction holding; Scalia and Thomas reach these merits on the Court's jurisdiction determination. This proposed reversal is noncontrolling.

**Justice Kennedy, additionally concurring in part and dissenting in part.** His disagreement with the statutory pathway preserves individual protection against unreasonable, unjustified or arbitrary continuing detention and denial of prescribed review. The Government must have actual support for mandatory category membership; unreasonable delay or failure of that justification can require individual flight-risk and danger consideration. His Flores and Zadvydas positions and Krimstock's inquiry into continuing authority support that distinction. The present record, including Kim's requested continuance and incomplete findings, does not establish that constitutional defect from elapsed time alone. This qualification of his reversal position is noncontrolling.

**Justice Scalia, additionally concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They read section 1226(e)'s second sentence as a bar on setting aside an executive detention or release action, including through this challenge to the mandatory framework. A legal or constitutional label cannot evade the action-based language. Their application of retained channels under other statutes does not settle this text. They decline Part I and proceed to the statutory merits on the Court's contrary jurisdiction determination, without announcing a comprehensive Suspension Clause rule. Their jurisdictional objection is noncontrolling.

Public procedure: The mandate returns the case for the lower court to obtain proper agency consideration of the current removal and custody posture and every asserted mandatory category, including the original theft theory and the added burglary and two-crimes grounds. The decisionmaker applies Part II-B's not-insubstantial and not-solely-for-delay conditions to each independently sufficient ground. If those conditions are met, the consequent individual risk assessment applies Kim's clear-and-convincing showing of unlikely flight or danger and effective authority to grant lawful conditional release. If mandatory membership fails, ordinary section 1226(a) procedures govern; if the substantial-question condition fails while mandatory coverage remains, section 1226(c) applies subject to an independently established constitutional defect. Kim's existing $5,000 bond release remains effective pending the threshold determination and any consequent lawful bond decision. The lower court implements and enforces these instructions through the retained legal-authority channel; the agency performs the initial determinations assigned to it.

### Virginia v. Maryland — 2003-12-09

The protected Potomac withdrawals and appurtenant improvements rest on the interstate settlement rather than Maryland’s unilateral permission. The declaration governs the covered class of Virginia activities, while navigation, proper Maryland use and applicable federal law continue to limit their exercise. Prior cooperation has not transferred the reserved authority. Concrete disputes about those continuing limits remain subject to ordinary federal interstate remedies.

Published separate positions:

Kennedy, joined by Stevens, would sustain Maryland’s regulatory-authority exception and dismiss the broad exemption claim. They read the instruments as securing an enforceable riparian entitlement within Maryland’s river sovereignty, subject to compatible regulation. A later determination of title can confirm preexisting sovereignty; historical disagreement does not itself show that Maryland lacked regulatory authority. The Award’s full dominion over the shore and qualified use beyond it are distinct.

The dissent emphasizes that protected property and use ordinarily coexist with police-power regulation. An advance permit can test compliance with navigation and proper-use limits while remaining subordinate to the federal entitlement. The specific mutual-consent provisions also show that regulatory authority could be expressly allocated. Maryland therefore could not use a permit to nullify protected access, suppress Virginia’s development for its own sake or impose an unrelated policy veto, and judicial relief would remain available against such interference. But those constraints do not invalidate every compatible licensing requirement. Nor does ordinary interstate-water apportionment automatically answer the regulatory question for a river within one State’s territorial title.

They agree only conditionally that the supported permit history would not independently establish surrender if the instruments created the exemption the Court finds. Because they reject that antecedent construction, this observation supplies no join in the Court’s sovereignty or acquiescence opinion.

Public procedure: The Court enters this decree, effective upon entry:

1. Virginia, its governmental subdivisions and its citizens exercising the riparian rights reserved by the governing instruments may withdraw water from the Potomac and construct and maintain improvements appurtenant to the Virginia shore without obtaining the challenged Maryland permits. Maryland must cease conditioning those covered activities on its unilateral permit requirement.
2. Exercise of those rights remains subject to the prohibition on obstructing or injuring navigation, the Award’s restriction against interfering with Maryland’s proper use, and applicable federal law and reserved federal rights and jurisdiction.
3. The Court retains jurisdiction to give effect to and enforce this decree, including adjudication of supported disputes concerning compliance with its terms. Concrete navigation or proper-use disputes must be resolved under those limits rather than through the rejected unilateral permit condition.

**Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer.** These Justices join the entire Court opinion and would specify additional terms for the loss inquiry. ### Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. — 2004-06-21

Authority: [Record](../records/intel-corp-v-advanced-micro-devices_merits_2004-06-21.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. Section 1782 permits this application for a contemplated adjudicative proceeding

**Holding and operative rule.** A district court may provide assistance under 28 U.S.C. §1782(a) upon an interested person’s application for evidence from a person residing or found in the district, for use in a reasonably contemplated proceeding before a foreign governmental body exercising adjudicative authority. The applicant need not await a pending or imminent adjudication; practical rights to submit evidence and obtain review may establish the required interest without formal litigant status.

**Authority.** The seven Justices joining the Court’s opinion support every holding and explanation in Parts I–III.

**Controlling explanation.** The enacted language reaches a foreign tribunal, and Congress removed the former restriction to pending judicial proceedings. That change permits assistance before an adjudication has begun, provided the expected proceeding rests on more than speculation. AMD can submit evidence and seek review of Commission action or inaction. Those rights give it a practical legal interest in the proceeding. The Commission’s ability to make a first-instance competition determination, subject to judicial review, supplies the adjudicative function relevant here. Its investigative directorate’s refusal to request the materials does not eliminate AMD’s separate statutory right to apply. The Commission’s combined functions warrant attention to the actual use of the evidence; they do not support categorical exclusion of its adjudicative work.

### II. Foreign discoverability and domestic analogues are not additional statutory prerequisites

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 1782 does not require the applicant to show that the evidence would be discoverable under foreign law or that an analogous domestic proceeding would permit the same discovery. Differences in national procedures do not alone establish an effort to evade foreign restrictions.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute states who may apply, where the evidence provider must be found and the proceeding for which assistance may be given. It adds an express protection for legally applicable privileges, but no foreign-discoverability or domestic-analogue condition. Requiring either would substitute an extra eligibility rule for Congress’s discretionary authorization. A foreign body’s practices, policies and views remain important when the district court decides whether assistance is appropriate. The dissent’s concern that discovery litigation itself can cause expense and regulatory interference is substantial, but supports a genuine discretionary assessment rather than categorical conditions absent from the statute. Neither a foreign body’s refusal to request evidence nor the absence of comparable foreign procedures is a statutory veto.

### III. Eligibility leaves a genuine, application-specific discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** An eligible application does not compel production. The district court must exercise reasoned discretion in light of the contemplated proceeding and requested assistance, considering the foreign body’s receptivity, the nature and character of its proceeding, access to evidence through a participant within that body’s reach, actual circumvention of foreign proof-gathering restrictions or policies, and undue intrusion or burden; no order may violate a legally applicable privilege.

**Controlling explanation.** The word permitting assistance leaves the district court responsible for the actual production decision. Evidence from a participant subject to the foreign tribunal’s authority may present a different need from evidence outside that authority. The court must consider the Commission’s position and distinguish rejection of a particular assistance mechanism from a legally operative restriction. It may narrow, condition or deny a request with reasons, and use appropriate confidentiality protection. These considerations guide discretion; they are not new cumulative eligibility elements. The application therefore returns for identification of the materials, their relevance to the reasonably contemplated proceeding, and the protections and burdens that the actual request presents.

F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A. supplies respect for foreign regulatory interests through the statute actually at issue. Its construction of antitrust coverage does not eliminate Congress’s affirmative authorization of foreign evidentiary assistance.

Interested complainants with practical participation and review rights may seek assistance for reasonably contemplated governmental adjudication. Neither foreign discoverability nor a domestic discovery analogue is a categorical condition. The district court retains responsibility for a proportionate order consistent with privileges, foreign receptivity, lawful restrictions and actual burdens.

### Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws — 2004-06-23

Authority: [Record](../records/duckworth-v-state-administration-board-of-election-laws_merits_2004-06-23.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. Ordinary pleading weakness does not dispense with the statutory tribunal

**Holding and operative rule.** When a plaintiff presents a constitutional challenge to congressional apportionment within federal jurisdiction, §2284 requires a three-judge district court unless the constitutional claim is wholly insubstantial, obviously frivolous or clearly foreclosed. A single judge may not equate ordinary failure to state a claim with that exceptional category and enter a merits dismissal.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join the Court’s statutory holding and the directions applying existing law in Part II. That application does not reconsider or enlarge the constitutional holding of Vieth.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2284 allocates covered apportionment litigation to three judges and expressly restricts a single judge’s authority to enter judgment on the merits. Goosby v. Osser distinguishes a wholly insubstantial constitutional claim from one that is merely doubtful or may fail under ordinary merits rules. Treating Rule 12(b)(6) insufficiency as the substantiality test erases that distinction and permits the single judge to exercise the very authority Congress assigned elsewhere. The earlier map’s unsuccessful merits litigation supplies no categorical pleading bar for the changed map. Duckworth’s allegation of purposeful impairment of Republican participation presents the kind of equal-protection claim recognized by Vieth. Whether his allegations and evidence satisfy that rule belongs to the designated tribunal.

### II. The panel applies Vieth’s actual rule and ordinary pleading procedure

**Holding and operative rule.** The required panel must apply Vieth’s partisan equal-treatment rule: identified political capacity, material impairment, prohibited purpose as a substantial motivating factor, and boundary-specific causation, supported by an independently lawful comparison and subject to the State’s same-decision defense. Irregular shape or electoral disadvantage alone does not establish those elements; ordinary notice pleading does not require merits proof, an expert model or a replacement map.

**Controlling explanation.** Vieth supplies the law for the preserved Fourteenth Amendment claim, and Martinez v. Bush illustrates its claim-specific application. Division among four districts and alleged purposeful degradation of political participation make this a substantial constitutional claim for tribunal allocation. The panel must permit the ordinary process for seeking conforming amendment and evaluate pleading, proof and any remedy under the actual rule. The Court’s reliance on that rule does not revive the Article I or independent First Amendment theories abandoned on appeal. The State continues lawful election administration subject to any separately justified order entered by the proper tribunal.

Goosby v. Osser is applied to distinguish constitutional insubstantiality from ordinary merits insufficiency. Vieth v. Jubelirer supplies the controlling partisan equal-treatment rule; its constitutional coalition and scope remain unchanged. Martinez v. Bush supplies the requirement to apply that rule to the particular claim and proof. Shaw v. Reno does not convert unusual district shape into a substitute for the injury required by the preserved partisan claim.

A single judge cannot bypass §2284 by using ordinary Rule 12(b)(6) weakness as constitutional insubstantiality. A substantial congressional-apportionment claim receives merits adjudication from the statutory panel, which applies the existing substantive elements, lawful comparison, ordinary proof rules and State defense.

### Beard v. Banks — 2004-06-24

Authority: [Record](../records/beard-v-banks_merits_2004-06-24.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. An individual-juror mitigation veto is forbidden by law dictated before finality

**Holding and operative rule.** A capital instruction may not condition an individual juror’s use of supported mitigating evidence on unanimous agreement that the circumstance exists. Applying that prohibition to a judgment final after the effective-mitigation requirement was established does not announce a new rule merely because the barrier takes the form of a unanimity requirement.

**Authority.** The five Justices joining the Court’s opinion support Parts I–IV, including application of the existing effect rule in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation.** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma forbid legal exclusion of relevant mitigation from the sentencing decision. A juror who accepts mitigating evidence but is legally forbidden to use it because another juror disagrees encounters the same functional exclusion. Graham v. Collins explains that an effective route for independent culpability mitigation was dictated before its 1984 finality. Its later decision confirms that earlier rule; it is not backdated as a new command. The dissent distinguishes usable information from the agreement required within a collective sentencer. That distinction cannot sustain a legal veto over evidence an individual sentencing juror accepts. The rule leaves the jury’s ultimate verdict requirements and each juror’s assessment of weight intact. Horn required the preserved Teague defense to be answered, and this comparison supplies the answer. Because the claim applies old law, the Court need not extend the watershed exception.

### II. The actual charge and form create the forbidden barrier

**Holding and operative rule.** A capital charge violates the effective-mitigation requirement when, read with the verdict form and trial context, it creates a reasonable likelihood that a juror cannot use supported mitigation without unanimous agreement. Banks’s combined charge and form create that likelihood.

**Controlling explanation.** The charge required death upon unanimous findings of an aggravating circumstance without mitigation, or aggravating circumstances outweighing mitigation, and directed life in other cases. It stated different proof burdens—beyond a reasonable doubt for aggravation and preponderance for mitigation—while repeatedly requiring agreement on findings and the ultimate verdict. The form then placed the circumstance findings under a common unanimity introduction. Nothing in that mechanism distinguished a juror’s individual ability to use mitigation. Different proof burdens do not explain a different voting rule. The jury’s acceptance of extreme mental or emotional disturbance does not establish that every juror could use the separate impaired-capacity and catchall categories. The reported polling supplied no cure. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood, not a merely possible restrictive reading; Middleton v. McNeil requires the complete charge and context, not isolated wording. The combined mechanism meets that standard. ### III. The state application was objectively unreasonable

**Holding and operative rule.** Under §2254(d)(1), the 1995 state adjudication unreasonably applied the clearly established mitigation rule to this combined charge and form. The petitioner satisfies the statutory merits gateway in addition to establishing the constitutional violation.

**Controlling explanation.** Williams v. Taylor makes unreasonable application an objective legal inquiry measured against this Court’s holdings at the state merits decision. Mills v. Maryland and McKoy v. North Carolina were available by 1995, as was Graham’s explanation of the effective-use requirement. The state analysis relied on previously accepted wording without confronting how the common unanimity requirement operated across the distinct mitigation findings. This is more than ordinary disagreement about an isolated ambiguity. The form and repeated instruction create the operative veto the governing rule forbids. Yarborough v. Alvarado preserves room for reasonable application of general rules but does not demand an earlier factual twin or turn judicial disagreement into the statutory test. The state resolution falls outside that permissible range. This statutory conclusion remains separate from the effect of the error on the death sentences.

### IV. Sentence relief requires the separate State-borne effect inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** For this properly available original-charge mitigation restriction, Brecht’s governing rule, as applied in Penry v. Johnson, requires the State to prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. Failure requires conditional sentence relief permitting lawful capital resentencing within a reasonable court-fixed period or a lawful noncapital sentence.

**Controlling explanation.** Constitutional error and satisfaction of §2254(d)(1) permit the inquiry; neither resolves actual contribution. Penry expressly separates these steps for an original-charge mitigation restriction, and Mitchell v. Esparza rejects treating a defect as structural merely because the sentence is capital. The full penalty evidence must be assessed under the State’s burden. The instructional account establishes the legal mechanism but does not supply the complete evidentiary basis for deciding effect here. The court on return must conduct that confined inquiry while treating the old-rule, constitutional-error and statutory conclusions as settled. Brecht and Penry govern present federal review; their effect procedure is not used as a later substantive command at the 1995 state-law cutoff.

Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma are applied to a legal barrier preventing individual use of supported mitigation. Graham v. Collins confirms the prefinality force of that protection. Horn v. Banks is followed by resolving the preserved Teague defense; its reserved question is answered without changing its sequencing rule. O’Dell v. Netherland retains its distinct old-rule and confined watershed grounds.

Mills v. Maryland and McKoy v. North Carolina are applied to the unanimity mechanism. Boyde v. California and Middleton v. McNeil supply contextual reasonable-likelihood review. Williams v. Taylor and Yarborough v. Alvarado supply objective statutory review rather than factual identity or a count of disagreeing judges. Brecht v. Abrahamson and Penry v. The effective-mitigation rule already dictated before Banks’s 1987 finality forbids a unanimity veto on an individual juror’s use of supported mitigation. This actual instruction/form mechanism violates that rule, and the 1995 state rejection was objectively unreasonable. Those conclusions bind the remand; the complete penalty-record effect inquiry determines whether conditional sentence relief must issue.

### Blakely v. Washington — 2004-06-24

Authority: [Record](../records/blakely-v-washington_merits_2004-06-24.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. The binding range identifies the punishment authorized by the plea or verdict

**Holding and operative rule.** A present-offense fact legally necessary to increase a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; a lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder while retaining that burden. **Authority.** The six Justices joining the Court’s opinion support the rule, application and remedy in Parts I and II.

**Controlling explanation.** Apprendi v. New Jersey supplies the requirement of element protection for a fact necessary to authorize greater punishment. United States v. United States confirms that sentencing labels and the distinction between increasing a floor and increasing a ceiling do not remove the protection. The relevant question is what punishment the actual adjudication authorizes, not the largest term the statute could allow after additional facts are established. The dissenters’ structured-sentencing concerns address whether Angle should stand; they supply no distinction under its controlling rule. Judicial evaluation remains permissible within independently authorized punishment. Here the plea supports a maximum of 53 months, so the additional cruelty finding was necessary to authorize the 90-month term.

**Holding and operative rule.** An unadmitted and untried deliberate-cruelty finding cannot authorize the exceptional sentence imposed here. Arizona preserves the distinction between identifying a constitutional factfinding requirement and deciding an error’s consequences on the actual record. Nothing here permits retention of the 90-month sentence on a hypothetical replacement verdict. Resentencing must respect the plea and conviction that remain in force. Apprendi v. New Jersey is applied to the fact legally necessary for greater punishment and to the distinction between notice and valid admission. United States v. Buckland preserves analysis of the punishment actually authorized, rather than assumed exposure. Ring v. Arizona retains its separate capital-eligibility and qualified remedy rules. The Court applies these decisions without changing their operative rules.

The existing element-protection rule governs materially equivalent binding sentencing schemes, including Washington’s exceptional-range mechanism. Judges retain discretion within punishment independently authorized by the actual verdict or valid admissions. ### Cheney v. United States District Court for the District of Columbia — 2004-06-24

Authority: [Record](../records/cheney-v-united-states-district-court_merits_2004-06-24.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. Extraordinary appellate relief and statutory enforcement have separate requirements

**Holding and operative rule.** Appellate mandamus under §1651 requires absence of an adequate alternative, a clear and indisputable entitlement, and a determination that issuance is appropriate in the circumstances. The plaintiffs’ separate §1361 theory requires a legally enforceable, sufficiently clear nondiscretionary duty and its other prerequisites; any APA claim requires an eligible agency defendant and satisfaction of that statute’s independent conditions.

**Authority.** Part I is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** Kerr v. United States District Court supplies the exceptional-remedy conditions and the importance of adequate protection in the district court. An appeal deadline does not mechanically govern a mandamus petition, although unreasonable delay and resulting prejudice may bear on equitable relief. Appellate mandamus is not a substitute for establishing the plaintiffs’ underlying enforcement route. The official’s title also supplies neither a categorical immunity nor an automatic interlocutory appeal. The inquiry addresses the function involved, the actual demands and their supported burdens. These distinctions preserve both independent judicial enforcement of federal law and the protections necessary for executive responsibility.

### II. Concrete institutional burdens need not await a document-by-document privilege confrontation

**Holding and operative rule.** A court considering extraordinary protection against compulsory civil discovery directed into presidential advisory work must assess demonstrated separation-of-powers burdens from the scope and operation of the demands. A formal assertion of executive privilege is not an invariant prerequisite to addressing those distinct burdens; particular claims of privilege retain their own requirements.

**Authority.** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** Clinton v. Jones requires accommodation of concrete interference with official responsibilities while rejecting immunity based simply on presidential status. The appellate court therefore erred in treating the absence of a formal privilege confrontation as sufficient to avoid examining these burdens. The availability of narrower district-court remedies remains important, as the dissent urges, but must be evaluated as an actual alternative to the asserted intrusion rather than presumed adequate from the possibility of future privilege objections.

### III. Actual integration into collective advisory work can establish membership

**Holding and operative rule.** Under FACA’s definition and its exclusion for a body composed wholly of federal officers or employees, a private person may be a functional member when sustained participation integrates that person into an established body’s internal collective deliberative and advisory work. Formal appointment, voting, veto or final governmental decisionmaking authority is not indispensable; access, attendance, lobbying, consultation, influence or outside recommendations without that integration is insufficient.

**Authority.** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These are direct joins to the statutory distinction, independent of the disagreement over appellate relief.

**Controlling explanation.** Public Citizen v. Department of Justice construes FACA with attention to ordinary executive consultation and serious constitutional concerns; it does not exempt every presidential advisory arrangement. Association of American Physicians & Surgeons v. Clinton persuasively distinguishes regular work functionally indistinguishable from membership from episodic advice. Relevant facts include regular internal meetings, continuing assigned responsibilities, participation in developing collective recommendations or drafting the group’s report, and work materially indistinguishable from that of recognized members. No single fact automatically establishes membership. An executive label cannot conclusively exclude an integrated private member, and allegations of influence cannot conclusively establish one. The distinction concerns an actual organized advisory body, not every person whose views officials hear. Here private integration remains disputed. The governing inquiry concerns participants’ functions; coverage and any final enforcement require factual and claim-specific adjudication through an available legal route.

### IV. The proper mandate is appellate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court of Appeals must reconsider the petition by applying all mandamus requirements to the actual structural burdens and available protective alternatives. Its former privilege-only approach cannot sustain the judgment; whether a writ should issue requires that complete inquiry.

**Authority.** Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** The identified appellate error does not establish that every extraordinary-relief condition is satisfied. The court must consider the breadth and necessity of the demands, whether narrower inquiry and district-court supervision adequately protect the asserted interests, the clear-duty and enforcement limitations, and the appropriateness of intervention. Thomas and Scalia would order the writ now because the uncertain duty cannot support discovery that may deliver the ultimate disclosure sought. Those concerns require consideration, but the present record leaves the complete alternatives and entitlement inquiry for appellate adjudication. Any legally authorized further process must respect the Court’s functional-membership rule and the distinct protections for particular privileged materials.

Kerr v. United States District Court is applied to the full extraordinary-writ inquiry and adequate protective alternatives. Clinton v. Jones is extended through its concrete-accommodation principle to official advisory discovery while retaining its actual unofficial-conduct holding. United States v. Nixon retains qualified protection and the distinction between identified criminal evidence and broad civil demands. Nixon v. Fitzgerald retains its function-based official-act damages immunity, a different question from statutory-compliance inquiry.

Public Citizen v. Department of Justice supplies contextual statutory caution without a blanket presidential-advice exemption. The functional reasoning of Association of American Physicians & Surgeons v. Clinton is adopted to distinguish integrated members from outside consultants. The statutory inquiry must preserve the independent requirements of the asserted enforcement channel.

Concrete institutional discovery burdens must receive consideration under the full mandamus standard without an automatic privilege-first barrier. FACA membership turns on sustained integration into an actual advisory body’s collective work, rather than formal voting authority or private influence alone. The Court’s mandate requires appellate reconsideration under the complete extraordinary-relief standard.

**Holding and operative rule.** Ring’s allocation to the jury of an otherwise required statutory aggravating-fact determination is a new procedural rule for a judgment final in 1984. ### Hamdi v. Rumsfeld — 2004-06-28

Authority: [Record](../records/hamdi_v_rumsfeld_merits_2004-06-28.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. Congress authorized detention of the defined battlefield category

**Controlling proposition:** The AUMF authorizes military detention of an actual member of opposing Taliban forces captured in the authorized Afghanistan hostilities as an incident of the force Congress authorized, including when that person is a citizen. That Act supplies the congressional authority required by 18 U.S.C. §4001(a) for this category.

**Authority:** Lead Part I; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer directly adopt this proposition. Thomas joins this statutory proposition despite disagreeing with the remand and the additional limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress authorized necessary and appropriate force against the September 11-related forces identified by the AUMF. Capturing and incapacitating an opposing battlefield fighter is an established incident of that force. Citizenship does not categorically exempt an actual opposing fighter; Quirin supplies that limited law-of-war proposition. Section 4001(a) requires an Act of Congress, and this authorization supplies it for the defined category. Section 4001(b)'s military-institution exception concerns prison management and supplies no independent exception to subsection (a). The conclusion establishes authority for a category; the contested declaration still must be tested to determine whether Hamdi belongs to it.

### II. A disputed citizen classification requires an effective factual contest

**Controlling proposition:** A citizen held in prolonged domestic military custody on this disputed battlefield classification must receive counsel, notice of the asserted factual basis, a meaningful opportunity to answer before a neutral decisionmaker, and effective judicial review and relief if custody is unlawful. Judicial acceptance of the executive declaration as conclusive does not satisfy that requirement; initial battlefield capture does not require the same procedures at the point of capture.

**Authority:** Lead Part II; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt the stated minimum and its remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews requires attention to the citizen's loss of liberty, the risk of an erroneous classification, and the Government's demonstrated military interests. The authority to detain an actual fighter cannot establish that a particular prisoner is one. Counsel, adequate notice and neutral assessment allow the factual dispute to be answered while preserving room for supported security accommodations that leave the contest meaningful. St. Cyr supplies the importance of effective legal-authority review; Demore distinguishes classification from the consequences of a classification actually established. The declaration here records allegations disputed by the petition; treating it as conclusive would eliminate the inquiry. The Court therefore rejects the existing restricted review and requires prompt proceedings giving this common minimum practical effect.

**Unresolved fractured question:** No majority adopts a complete burden of persuasion or the additional presumption and military-procedure permissions. The controlling minimum remains enforceable without converting any of those proposals into law.

**Controlling proposition:** Detention under this narrow battlefield authorization lasts only while the relevant active-hostilities justification for preventing return to combat continues. The authorization does not permit detention solely for interrogation; a changed purpose or end of the relevant justification requires renewed lawful authority.

**Authority:** Lead Part III; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer adopt the limitation directly; Souter and Ginsburg adopt it as a protection if the detention is sustained. These six support the same operative limit. Thomas does not join it.

Zadvydas supplies the method of relating detention to its statutory purpose, while its distinct immigration calendar and express security reservation remain intact. The present record establishes no cessation date; the lower court must examine the current hostilities-related basis as part of lawful-custody review. Interrogation alone cannot sustain detention under this construction after that basis fails. The statutory limit does not depend on treating the immigration regime as controlling wartime custody.

### Missouri v. Seibert — 2004-06-28

Authority: [Record](../records/missouri_v_seibert_merits_2004-06-28.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. Midstream warnings must communicate a genuine choice

**Controlling proposition:** When a confession follows unwarned custodial questioning, the prosecution must establish a knowing, voluntary and effective warning waiver by a preponderance of the evidence on the complete circumstances. In a coordinated question-first sequence, courts must assess the initial questioning's completeness, overlapping content, timing, setting, personnel, references to prior admissions and meaningful corrective measures; deliberate evasion is powerful evidence but is not necessary to find objectively ineffective warnings. **Authority:** The lead's rule portion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join at this generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda protects an informed choice in custodial interrogation, and Dickerson prevents replacing its constitutional safeguard with voluntariness alone. The relevant circumstances determine whether the second interview offered an intelligible fresh choice or instead continued a confession already treated as irrevocable. The factors are considerations, not a numerical test. No fixed waiting period or additional sentence supplies a categorical safe harbor. A prior unwarned statement is also not an automatic permanent bar to a later confession: genuine separation and an effective waiver remain legally significant.

### II. This continuous, deliberate sequence requires exclusion

**Controlling proposition:** The warned repetition obtained through this detailed initial confession, short break, same officer, overlapping content and use of prior admissions must be suppressed. The established deliberate method lacked effective intervening measures that restored an informed choice.

**Authority:** The lead's application; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly support this sufficient ground. Kennedy's application join does not extend to Part I's intent-independent rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The officer first obtained the developed confession and then used it to elicit its repetition. The record supplies no meaningful change that conveyed a new choice to decline. Elstad's transition from a brief unwarned exchange to an effective warned interview is materially different. An intent requirement would not alter this result because the officer established deliberate coordination himself. The warning form consequently did not make the repeated statement admissible, and Missouri's requested reinstatement of the conviction fails. The state suppression and conviction-reversal judgment is affirmed.

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436:** Applied to effective warnings and knowing, voluntary waiver in the whole custodial sequence.
- **Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Its constitutional safeguard governs; voluntariness alone cannot substitute for effective warnings and waiver.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Distinguished on the actual continuity and lack of effective separation; its rule for a genuinely effective subsequent warning and waiver remains.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157:** The ordinary prosecution burden to establish waiver remains distinct from proving actual coercion.

A direct five-Justice majority establishes objective effectiveness for later warnings following unwarned custodial questioning. Courts apply the complete-sequence rule without making subjective intent indispensable or treating any earlier omission as permanent taint. No Marks synthesis is needed. Suppression of this repeated confession follows under both the controlling objective rule and the six-Justice deliberate-uncured application.

### Rasul v. Bush — 2004-06-28

Authority: [Record](../records/rasul_v_bush_merits_2004-06-28.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. Section 2241 reaches these Guantánamo custody claims

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2241 permits a substantial claim that reachable United States custodial officials hold a person at Guantánamo in violation of federal law where the United States exercises the complete and continuing jurisdiction and control established here. Foreign citizenship and Cuba's reserved ultimate sovereignty do not alone defeat that statutory jurisdiction; the prerequisites and merits of relief remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The lead's habeas portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute concerns custody exercised by officials whom the court can reach. The United States controls administration, access and confinement at this enduring base outside an active battlefield. Reserved ultimate sovereignty does not make that governmental control disappear. Eisentrager involved a different territorial and posttrial setting, including military convictions; its continuing framework does not foreclose these unadjudicated, potentially indefinite restraints under the control arrangement here. Braden rejects an invariable requirement that the prisoner be physically present in the forum; it need not be read to destroy Eisentrager's statutory foundation to reach this result. St. Cyr and Calcano-Martinez preserve effective statutory review while retaining actual channel predicates. The Government's proposed categorical alien-abroad rule would deny the writ despite this control and reachable custodian. The narrower statutory answer requires no universal decision about extraterritorial constitutional rights. Disputed innocence remains for the custody adjudication.

### II. Alien status does not replace the civil claims' actual predicates

**Controlling proposition:** Nonresident aliens held at Guantánamo are not categorically disabled from invoking an otherwise available federal civil action and jurisdictional grant. Courts must examine each pleaded §§1331, 1350 and APA theory for its own jurisdiction, cause of action, reviewability, immunity and remedy requirements; neither alien status nor recognition of habeas jurisdiction establishes or defeats those conditions by itself.

**Authority:** All nine join rejection of universal alien incapacity and remand of genuinely independent statutory claims. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also require reversal of the categorical dismissal of the whole civil branch. Scalia and Thomas preserve their different disposition for claims necessarily dependent on the constitutional entitlement they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira and Kadic distinguish jurisdiction from a defined domestic action with actual norm, actor and remedy predicates. Those decisions preclude a universal incapacity to sue but establish neither an ATS detention action nor a waiver of United States immunity. The same source-specific method applies to Al Odah's pleaded civil theories. Eisentrager is not a general litigation disability for every independent statutory claim merely because its claimant is an alien held abroad. The court below disposed of the branch categorically instead of resolving those requirements. Remand permits the necessary individual legal inquiry, including the actual APA reviewability and waiver, the claimed ATS norm and action, and available relief. A statutory label alone cannot make an otherwise identical constitutional theory independent.

- **Johnson v. Eisentrager, 339 U.S. 763:** Distinguished on the enduring control, absence of military convictions and actual confinement setting; its statutory framework and unaddressed constitutional questions are not generally overruled.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Applied to reject an invariable physical-presence requirement when the proper custodian is reachable.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Their effective statutory-review principles inform the actual §2241 channel and preserve its predicates.
- **Abebe-Jira v. Negewo; Kadic v. Karadzic:** Their distinct jurisdiction and defined-action holdings require claim-specific civil analysis and do not themselves establish a detention action or governmental immunity waiver.

Section 2241 now expressly reaches the defined Guantánamo control arrangement when the responsible custodian is reachable. Courts must adjudicate the actual custody basis and effective relief; the asserted substantive extraterritorial constitutional entitlement remains undecided because the statutory ground resolves the habeas question. Al Odah's independent civil theories return for their actual statutory, immunity and remedial inquiries. Whether a claim satisfies those rules is ordinary claim adjudication, not a reservation of the rule rejecting universal alien incapacity.

### Rumsfeld v. Padilla — 2004-06-28

Authority: [Record](../records/rumsfeld_v_padilla_merits_2004-06-28.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. A new domestic physical-custody petition ordinarily follows the immediate custodian

**Controlling proposition:** A new petition challenging present physical confinement at a known domestic facility ordinarily must name the immediate custodian and proceed in a district with authority over that custodian. Senior supervisory control alone does not replace those requirements; a properly supported continuing-jurisdiction or exceptional-forum claim requires its own predicates.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2242 and 2243 direct the petition and return to the person exercising the custody challenged. The known brig commander exercises Padilla's present physical custody in South Carolina. Braden concerns the restraint actually challenged and does not make every supervising official a domestic custodian; Endo preserves an already properly filed proceeding despite a later transfer. Padilla's earlier material-witness challenge was a different proceeding, and this military-custody petition followed the transfer. It therefore cannot borrow an earlier habeas filing that did not occur. St. Cyr protects effective review of authority, while Calcano-Martinez preserves the predicates of the actual channel. An effective South Carolina forum answers the claimed absence of review without treating the Secretary's supervisory authority as sufficient everywhere. The record establishes no bad-faith transfer exception. Detention authority remains outside the Court's reach in this forum posture.

### II. The forum remedy must preserve an available lawful route

**Controlling proposition:** On identifying this forum defect, the lower court must promptly consider an authorized transfer and apply the actual conditions of that authority; otherwise it must dismiss without prejudice to a properly filed challenge. Section 1631 requires a qualifying court's finding of want of jurisdiction, an eligible destination where the action could have been brought when filed, and the interest of justice; when those conditions are met, transfer is mandatory and the original filing date is preserved.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; the same five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The transfer statute supplies an alternative to requiring a new filing only when its text applies. The court must identify the character of the defect and the available destination rather than assume that every custodian or forum problem falls within §1631. Consideration does not establish eligibility, but an available mandatory transfer cannot be disregarded. If the conditions are absent and no other lawful route permits transfer, dismissal without prejudice allows immediate proper refiling. The receiving court must address properly presented requests for interim protection and custody review within its own lawful authority. That arrangement enforces the actual forum rules while preserving the effective review channel.

- **Ex parte Endo, 323 U.S. 283:** Its continuation of already attached jurisdiction is distinguished because the military-custody petition here followed the transfer.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Its focus on the challenged custody and reachable custodian does not replace the ordinary domestic immediate-custodian rule with senior supervision.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Applied to preserve effective statutory authority review with actual custody, respondent and forum predicates.
- **Zadvydas v. Davis:** Its removal-purpose detention holding supplies no domestic military-detention merits answer in this forum disposition.

New domestic physical-custody petitions follow the ordinary immediate-custodian and territorial forum requirements, with actual continuing-jurisdiction or other exceptions considered on their established predicates. Conditional transfer consideration and dismissal without prejudice preserve the proper route. The AUMF's application to this domestic arrest and the constitutional detention question await an action in a forum able to decide them.

### United States v. Patane — 2004-06-28

Authority: [Record](../records/united_states_v_patane_merits_2004-06-28.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

**Controlling proposition:** Failure to give complete Miranda warnings restricts case-in-chief use of the resulting statement but does not by itself require suppression of nontestimonial physical evidence derived from a voluntary answer. When voluntariness is properly contested, the prosecution must establish it under the applicable burden and satisfy other independent admission requirements; an unwarned answer is not thereby a valid waiver, and the rule does not extend to evidence produced by actual coercion.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas directly join the narrow remedial proposition and reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** Dickerson preserves Miranda's constitutional warning-and-waiver safeguard and prevents voluntariness alone from authorizing the answer's ordinary case-in-chief use. It left the physical-fruits question open. Tucker and Elstad show that constitutional status does not require identical exclusion for an unwarned answer and every derivative evidentiary product. Here the offered item is a pistol, the reviewed answer is assumed voluntary, and the challenge rests on the incomplete warning itself. The direct gain to investigators identified by the dissent does not eliminate those distinct remedial categories. Admitting a voluntary answer's physical product neither establishes a knowing waiver nor resolves an actual-coercion or independent acquisition claim. Chavez's fractured no-use damages disposition supplies no controlling theory of physical-fruit admissibility; its independent due-process inquiry remains distinct. The appellate court therefore erred in treating Dickerson's constitutional characterization alone as a sufficient fruits rule. The warning-only ground for suppressing this pistol must be reversed, with any genuinely preserved independent objection considered in its proper scope.

- **Miranda v. Arizona; Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Their constitutional case-in-chief warning-and-waiver core is preserved; Dickerson's open physical-fruits question is resolved at the voluntary, nontestimonial scope here.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433; Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Their differentiated consequences support this physical-remedy distinction without replacing the original statement's warning requirement.
- **Chavez v. Martinez, 538 U.S. 760:** Its no-use damages judgment had no controlling Fifth Amendment rationale and does not decide admissibility; its separate interrogation-abuse inquiry survives at its actual scope.

Incomplete warnings alone no longer justify excluding nontestimonial physical fruits of a voluntary answer. Statements, actual coercion and independently unlawful acquisition retain their own rules and properly preserved remedies. An incomplete warning does not establish waiver; the dispute decided concerns this evidentiary category.

### Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union — 2004-06-29

Authority: [Record](../records/ashcroft_v_american_civil_liberties_union_merits_2004-06-29.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. The Government must justify the complete restriction against concrete alternatives

**Holding and operative rule:** When a content-based restriction burdens protected adult Internet expression and a concrete, plausibly effective less restrictive alternative is identified, the Government must establish why that alternative's comparative inadequacy warrants the broader restriction in serving the compelling child-protection interest. At the preliminary stage, an injunction may remain when the supported record shows a likelihood that the Government cannot meet that burden and the other equitable requirements are satisfied; appellate review respects the district court's discretion while independently correcting legal error.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, supplies the common framework.

**Controlling explanation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union protects adults' access to lawful Internet expression and requires workable safeguards against a criminal restriction's actual burdens. United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group places the comparative justification burden on the Government after a concrete lesser restraint is presented. Legislative predictions deserve serious consideration, but do not settle that inquiry. Neither a merely imagined alternative nor a demand for perfect filtering supplies the proper comparison.

The earlier Ashcroft decision requires the statute to be considered as a whole. COPA reaches knowing World Wide Web communications in interstate or foreign commerce, made for commercial purposes with knowledge of their character, available to persons under seventeen and harmful to minors. Its commercial category concerns a regular business course and profit objective, with knowing posting or solicitation; it requires neither actual profit nor a sole or principal business. That category does not turn protected literature into commercial advertising.

Harmful material is obscenity or material satisfying all three additional elements: taken as a whole, prurient appeal or pandering with respect to minors under contemporary community standards; specified patently offensive sexual depictions, descriptions or representations with respect to minors; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for minors. The content element includes actual or simulated sexual acts or contacts and lewd displays of genitals or post-pubescent female breasts. The community-standards language expressly appears in the prurience element; serious value remains objective under Pope v. Illinois.

The defenses, exclusions and privacy conditions stated below matter to practical adult access. Their availability reduces coverage and potential liability, but does not prove every reader has affordable, anonymous and effective access. Courts must evaluate their operation, rather than add missing offense limits or treat material's lack of value for minors as loss of adult protection.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 231(b) excludes telecommunications carriers, Internet-access providers and information-location services to the extent they act in those capacities. It separately protects intermediaries transmitting, storing, retrieving, hosting, formatting or translating another's communication without selecting or altering its content; deletion consistent with §231(c) or §230 does not defeat that protection. Section 231(c)(1)'s alternative good-faith access defenses are credit card or debit account use, adult access code or adult personal identification number, an age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable measures feasible with available technology. Section 231(c)(2) separately protects good-faith implementation or restriction activity against a cause of action only if the activity violates no law carrying a criminal or civil penalty. Section 231(d) requires protection against unauthorized access to collected information and prior written or electronic consent for disclosure from the adult concerned or the parent or guardian of a person under seventeen; disclosure is permitted when necessary to communicate, to conduct legitimate related business activity, or under an authorizing court order. These provisions protect information without guaranteeing absolute anonymity.

### II. The present record supports continued protection and a prompt merits adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The district court did not abuse its discretion by preserving the injunction because the supported comparison shows a likelihood that the Government cannot justify COPA's adult-speech burdens against user-controlled filtering. The injunction continues while the district court promptly resolves final validity through the integrated statutory inquiry; supported findings remain unless genuinely new evidence or legal error warrants reconsideration, and challengers need not disprove every conceivable alternative.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, controls by six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Filtering acts at the receiving end. It can address foreign-origin and non-Web material outside COPA's coverage while permitting adults to choose lawful reading without publisher-imposed verification or identification. COPA's defenses do not eliminate the record's expense, anonymity and access concerns, and its verification methods remain subject to circumvention.

The dissent correctly identifies the statute's narrower harmful-material category and its added protection for children whose parents do not activate filters. Those are relevant advantages. But a beneficial increment does not itself demonstrate why this broader adult burden is necessary. The Government's evidence does not adequately answer filtering's wider reach and reduced interference with adult choice. The Court neither assumes universal parental participation nor requires flawless filtering; it requires the actual comparative justification that Playboy assigns to the Government.

United States v. American Library Association concerns an assisted library service, distinct LSTA and E-rate disabling permissions, and effective adult access in that setting. Its statutory permission is not a finding that every request is honored; its constitutional protection concerns prompt whole-session disabling or an equally effective method without disclosure of protected reading or an approved reason, subject to reasonable technical, supervision and resource procedures. That decision establishes neither universal filter effectiveness nor justification for criminal restrictions on private publishers.

Preserving relief avoids the demonstrated chill while the court develops any material changes in date-appropriate evidence. The preliminary posture leaves final facial validity and supported particular applications for the merits record. Any severance must follow an actual statutory division, rather than create a new offense. The district court must set a prompt schedule, identify genuinely disputed updated facts and state the enforceable scope of its final decree.

- Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, 535 U.S. 564, is applied: rejection of community standards as an automatic ground of invalidity requires integrated review and preserves consideration of actual adult burdens.
- Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union is applied to protected adult Internet access, workable safeguards and genuine textual severance; COPA's different elements receive their own examination.
- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group is applied to the Government's comparative justification burden and the need for concrete alternatives rather than perfect protection.
- Pope v. Illinois is applied to the objective serious-value inquiry; community approval does not supply that element.
- United States v. American Library Association is distinguished at its library-service, funding and effective-access scope. Its distinct statutory disabling provisions retain their own terms.

Courts considering a preliminary challenge to a content-based adult-speech restriction must compare the complete law with concrete lesser restraints under the Government's continuing justification burden. COPA remains enjoined on the present record. Genuine changes in evidence may be considered at final adjudication under that same rule; the preliminary judgment does not resolve final facial validity.

### Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain, No. 03-339 — 2004-06-29

Authority: [Record](../records/sosa_v_alvarez_machain_merits_2004-06-29.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. A federal forum, a domestic action and sovereign consent remain separate

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350 gives district courts jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort claim alleging a violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty, but does not itself convert every alleged violation into a domestic damages action or waive sovereign immunity. The international norm, its actor coverage, the domestic action, personal responsibility, causation and authority for relief must each be established under the governing law; the existing specifically adopted actions retain their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, applies these common distinctions and preserves the existing holdings without making their original recognition rationales unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira v. Negewo separates §1350's forum from the received domestic action for personal official torture. Kadic v. Karadzic and Doe v. Karadzic apply the same separation to defined genocide and core war crimes. The jurisdictional character of the statute cannot silently erase those holdings. Conversely, their recognition of particular actions does not establish liability for a different asserted norm or supply the United States' consent to suit.

The official-torture action reaches deliberate participation in or personal supervision of severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation. The existing genocide action runs against an individual for personally perpetrating, planning, or directing genocide, whether or not that individual acted under state authority. The plaintiff must establish intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group as such, through killing members, causing serious bodily or mental harm, deliberately imposing conditions calculated to bring about the group's physical destruction in whole or in part, imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group, or forcibly transferring the group's children to another group, together with the defendant's qualifying responsibility, causation, and a lawful basis for relief. The core-war-crime action reaches personally perpetrated, planned or directed deliberate killing, torture and grave sexual violence, with the relevant armed conflict, protected victim, prohibited act and connection to the conflict. Each retains its responsibility, causation and remedy requirements; official position alone is insufficient.

Those defined actions furnish the existing law against which this asserted addition must be examined. United States v. Alvarez-Machain decided the treaty-based objection to criminal trial. Its trial-authority holding neither authorized the capture nor supplied a civil action, and it does not resolve the independent predicates now presented.

### II. The asserted brief-detention norm does not support this additional action

**Holding and operative rule:** Recognition of an additional received action requires a specifically defined, widely accepted and obligatory international prohibition whose content and actor coverage support judicial application, together with a sound domestic remedial basis. The asserted rule that any detention unauthorized by domestic law is an internationally actionable tort does not establish those predicates for the detention of less than a day in Mexico followed by lawful custody here.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins supply the controlling ground for rejecting this new action.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira and Kadic/Doe recognize determinate personal torts through a confined domestic reception of international law. They support a disciplined inquiry into an additional action, rather than either automatic incorporation of every international objection or categorical withdrawal of that received route. Their existing conduct, actor and remedial requirements explain why the source objection in the separate opinion does not justify closing the inquiry altogether.

Alvarez relies on article 9 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, article 9 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, other international statements and the absence of an express minimum detention period. Those materials do not establish the particular customary rule and domestic enforcement predicate his broad theory requires. The Declaration does not itself impose binding treaty obligations. The Covenant's non-self-executing status prevents it from itself supplying this damages action, although a treaty with that status may still inform evidence of customary law. General language against arbitrary detention is not enough to make every violation of local arrest law an internationally actionable tort.

The Restatement's treatment of prolonged detention as state policy illustrates the specificity missing from this claim; it does not create a duration safe harbor. The defect concerns the proposed norm and its domestic enforcement basis. The existing official-torture, genocide and core-war-crime actions remain available on their established terms. This award rests on the asserted brief arbitrary-detention action and must be set aside.

### III. Domestic planning does not relocate the same foreign-detention claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim seeking compensation for the detention injury occurring in Mexico arises in a foreign country within 28 U.S.C. §2680(k), even when domestic planning or direction is alleged as a cause of that same detention. The claimant must establish a waived claim on its actual injury and statutory predicates; a domestic causal link alone does not avoid the exception, and §2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso does not displace it. A genuinely independent domestic injury falls outside this holding.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion is joined by all nine Justices at this bounded foreign-detention scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The asserted false detention took place and endured in Mexico; its liability-creating detention and injury were there. Planning in the United States may have causal importance, but it does not change the claim for which Alvarez seeks compensation. If an allegation of domestic direction alone removed that claim from the exception, nearly every overseas operation could be recast through a headquarters instruction and §2680(k) would lose its enacted function.

Smith v. United States enforced the foreign-country exception together with the FTCA's liability and place-of-law provisions for acts wholly in Antarctica. The present decision applies that statutory structure to the additional domestic-planning argument; Smith did not itself decide this mixed-location question. Richards v. United States identifies the governing law under §1346(b), but that choice-of-law inquiry operates within the waiver and its exceptions. Even a domestic causal wrong subject to domestic law does not alone establish consent for the same excluded Mexican detention.

Section 2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso removes specified intentional torts from that subsection's exclusion when its own conditions are satisfied. It does not repeal the separate foreign-country exception. The asserted detention claim therefore must be dismissed. The Court resolves a claim whose detention and injury occurred together abroad; a separately supported domestic injury requires its own waiver and exception analysis.

- Abebe-Jira v. Negewo is applied and preserved: its jurisdiction/action distinction, confined official-torture action and distinct remedy rules remain effective.
- Kadic v. Karadzic and Doe v. Karadzic are applied and preserved at their defined genocide, core-war-crime, actor, responsibility and remedy predicates; they do not make every international norm privately enforceable.
- United States v. Alvarez-Machain is distinguished as a treaty-based criminal trial-authority decision. Its holding supplies neither capture authorization nor this civil remedy.
- Smith v. United States is extended from its wholly Antarctic acts to the domestic planning of the same Mexican detention; its territorial and statutory-waiver analysis remains confined to its actual work.
- Richards v. United States is applied within the FTCA's consent structure. Selection of domestic law does not remove a claim from an independently applicable exception.

The specifically adopted international-tort actions remain enforceable on their existing terms. A proposed additional action must satisfy the norm, actor and domestic remedial requirements stated here; §1350 jurisdiction alone is insufficient. The broad brief-detention theory presented fails that inquiry. Under the FTCA, domestic planning or direction of the same foreign detention does not avoid §2680(k), which operates independently of §2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso.

### Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran — 2004-06-30

Authority: [Record](../records/roeder_v_islamic_republic_of_iran_merits_2004-06-30.md); [public handoff](../render-inputs/OT_2003CHUNK8.md).

### I. The immunity amendments leave the independent claim bar in place

**Holding and operative rule:** A statute removing a specified sovereign-immunity obstacle extinguishes an independent bar to the plaintiff's claim only if its enacted text or necessary legal effect accomplishes that further displacement; legislative explanations cannot supply an omitted operative rule or direct entry of judgment. Section 626(c) changes the state-sponsor-designation condition for this identified litigation and §208 corrects its designation, but neither displaces the Accords' covered-claim prohibition; the claims against Iran and its Ministry therefore remain barred.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion directly controls this holding and the following two holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The amendment does real legal work. Section 626(c) of Public Law 107-77 inserts this designated litigation into former §1605(a)(7)(A), removing the state-sponsor-designation obstacle. Section 208 of Public Law 107-117 corrects the judge's initials in that case designation. Neither enactment supplies a rule inconsistent with the Accords' separate prohibition of these hostage-related claims.

The plaintiffs emphasize that Congress knew of the Government's objection, deliberately identified this suit and expressed a desire to preserve their judgment. That context supports taking the enactments seriously; it cannot change their subject from immunity to every obstacle to recovery. Removal of one legal barrier is meaningful even when another remains decisive. An explanatory statement favoring recovery does not itself enact abrogation, liability or a judgment.

Trans World Airlines v. Franklin Mint, Cook v. United States and Weinberger v. Rossi require a genuine legislative basis for displacing international commitments. No talismanic phrase is necessary: courts examine the operative text and its necessary legal effect. Congress may change domestic law concerning an executive agreement within its constitutional authority. These enactments simply do not accomplish the additional displacement asserted here. Dames & Moore v. Regan sustains the relevant claims-settlement framework within its authorization and historical-practice setting, rather than establishing an executive power to disregard later legislation.

The Ministry is Iran itself for these covered claims, so naming it does not avoid the same bar. That independent ground completely resolves the presented statutory-abrogation question. The broader Flatow cause-of-action objection is unnecessary because resolving it could not overcome the surviving bar.

### II. Intervention, default and payment each retain their independent conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The United States may protect its own concrete agreement-related interest through authorized intervention and judicial reconsideration; the foreign state's nonappearance neither forfeits that interest nor establishes entitlement to judgment. Under §1608(e), the claimant must establish the claim or right to relief by evidence satisfactory to the court, and a payment provision conditioned on a qualifying judgment supplies payment authority only after a legally supportable judgment exists.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government undertook its own obligation to bar these claims. Its intervention after learning of the conflict protects that obligation, and Iran's nonappearance does not adequately protect the Government's interest. On this record, its timely intervention and request for judicial reconsideration were proper. The courts independently considered the legal bar under the procedural rules, including the Rule 60(b) motion as presented.

Argentine Republic v. Amerada Hess and Republic of Austria v. Altmann distinguish the FSIA's adjudicative-immunity framework from substantive entitlement. The claimant must establish the invoked exception and the separate right to relief. Section 1608(e) preserves that latter inquiry even upon default. The Accords defeat the covered claim despite the amended immunity condition. Public Law 106-386's judgment-payment mechanism presupposes the qualifying judgment and does not create the missing entitlement to obtain one.

### III. The amendments change law for a pending case and leave adjudication to the courts

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may enact a new legal rule governing a pending controversy, including an identifiable controversy, subject to its lawful temporal reach and constitutional limits, while courts independently apply the rule to facts and claims. The present amendments fall on that pending-case side of Plaut's distinction: the unresolved-damages liability default did not finally dispose of the action, and the enacted immunity change prescribes neither findings nor liability. Unenacted explanations cannot become binding adjudicative commands.

**Controlling explanation:** Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society permits genuine governing-law changes in pending litigation while preserving independent judicial application. United States v. Klein protects the adjudicative function from legislative prescription of findings or liability. The distinction concerns what a statute does, not merely whether its beneficiaries or case can be identified.

Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm supplies the completed-judgment rule: once a private judgment becomes final through the ordinary judicial process, Congress may not, solely by changing the previously applicable law, require its vacatur and reinstate the same litigation. Later independent adjudication of eligibility and merits cannot restore the conclusiveness of the displaced judgment. That rule preserves Congress's authority to change law while a case remains pending and preserves constitutionally permissible judicial relief, including valid Rule 60(b) grounds; Klein and McCullough v. Virginia continue to forbid dictated findings and predetermined liability.

Here damages remained unresolved when Public Laws 107-77 and 107-117 were enacted. The liability default therefore was not the completed private judgment Plaut protects. The provisions remove the specified immunity obstacle and correct the designation while leaving courts to apply the remaining law. Judicial reconsideration on the Government's motion is likewise distinct from a legislative command reopening a completed action.

Miller v. French and Plaut's Wheeling Bridge qualification distinguish new law governing future enforcement of continuing relief from displacement of completed private judgments; meaningful adjudication of the governing predicates remains necessary. Landgraf v. USI Film Products separately addresses temporal operation: express retrospectivity does not itself establish constitutional validity. Morgan Stanley's affirmance by an equally divided Court supplies no contrary rule.

The asserted broader command to preserve liability appears in explanations, not the enactments. Applying the actual amendments therefore preserves independent adjudication and leaves the Accords bar decisive.

- Trans World Airlines v. Franklin Mint, Cook v. United States and Weinberger v. Rossi are applied to require an enacted basis for displacement of the international commitment; explanatory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Dames & Moore v. Regan is applied within its claims-settlement authorization and historical-practice scope. Congress's power to enact contrary domestic law remains distinct from what it enacted here.
- Argentine Republic v. Amerada Hess and Republic of Austria v. Altmann are applied to preserve the separation between present immunity, a substantive claim and independent defenses.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society is applied to a genuine law change in pending litigation with independent judicial application.
- Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm is applied at its pending-case boundary. Its completed-private-judgment rule, prospective-relief qualification and preservation of constitutionally permissible judicial procedures retain their existing force.
- United States v. Klein and McCullough v. Virginia supply the continuing protection against legislative findings or prescribed liability.
- Miller v. French and Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co. retain their distinct treatment of changed prospective law and meaningful judicial adjudication.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products is applied to distinguish enacted temporal effect from constitutional permission.
- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., affirmed by an equally divided Court, establishes no precedent.

The designated immunity amendments retain their actual effect, but the Accords continue to bar these hostage-related claims against Iran and its Ministry. Courts must examine each independent jurisdictional and substantive predicate before treating a limited amendment as entitlement to judgment. A real law change governing a pending controversy receives independent judicial application; legislative explanations cannot supply an unpassed command preserving liability. Plaut's separate completed-judgment rule remains unchanged.

They would reverse the determination that filtering establishes a sufficient likelihood of success to retain this injunction basis. Their disagreement concerns the supported comparison, not the governing standard.

COPA's conjunctive harmful-material requirements, commercial-coverage limits and alternative good-faith adult-access defenses materially reduce the burden compared with the broader restriction in Reno. The dissent accepts verification costs, anonymity concerns and the possibility of circumvention; the privacy provision does not erase those effects. It nevertheless finds the remaining burden sufficiently fitted to a protection filtering does not supply by itself: children whose parents do not obtain or activate household controls receive the benefit of COPA's default restriction. The comparative position stated in the Playboy dissent gives that activation gap substantial weight while recognizing that filters reach foreign and non-Web sources and preserve adult choice.

In these Justices' assessment, the narrower statutory category and access routes, considered together with that additional protection, carry the Government's burden at this preliminary stage. They would remand for lawful disposition of other preserved grounds. Breyer's conclusion further depends on the national-community construction stated below; O'Connor and Scalia join the comparative reasoning without adopting that construction.

Justice Breyer additionally requires the national adult community to supply the governing community standard. His earlier Ashcroft position reads the phrase in statutory context and in light of the serious difficulty caused when restrictive local standards govern nationally distributed speech. Different jury applications of one national standard differ from different substantive standards. That construction is necessary to his conclusion that the narrower offense and access defenses sufficiently answer the filtering comparison. O'Connor and Scalia do not join this construction.

Part II has seven direct joins; Parts I and III have nine at their stated scopes. Judgment agreement is not combined into a broader recognition or territorial rule.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, joins Parts I and III and concurs in the ATS reversal on an additional-action source ground. They distinguish an international prohibition, federal jurisdiction and domestic authority to impose damages. Section 1350 supplies a forum, while the offered Declaration and Covenant do not themselves enact this private action. The received common-law setting is the strongest argument for the Court's method, but in their view it does not supply sufficient congressional authorization for the additional arbitrary-detention liability.

Their earlier writings in Abebe-Jira and Kadic/Doe preserve that source objection. Suter requires an actual source of private liability; O'Melveny and Atherton reject federal rules derived merely from a general federal interest; Meyer keeps forum, consent and substantive liability separate. Central Bank's established securities action and Musick, Peeler's treatment of incidents of recognized liability do not independently authorize this different extension. They respect the already adopted official-torture, genocide and core-war-crime holdings as precedents at their exact scopes, but decline to extend them to the present detention action without a sufficient enacted source. That independently sufficient ground explains their ATS judgment agreement and their refusal to join Part II's recognition methodology.

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justice Breyer, joins the Court's opinion and explains the conduct-based reason for the bounded FTCA result. Section 2680(k) must be read with §1346(b)(1), which locates the governing law by the relevant act or omission. For this claim, Mexico was the place of the last significant act necessary to create the alleged tort: the seizure and detention took place there, and the detention injury accrued there. Domestic planning was causally relevant but did not by itself complete the false-detention claim or convert its Mexican core into a domestic injury.

Richards supplies the place-of-conduct inquiry within the FTCA's statutory structure. Smith enforced the exception for acts wholly in Antarctica; its actual scope does not require an injury-only test for every combination of domestic and foreign conduct. These Justices therefore support the Court's common disposition of the same Mexican detention notwithstanding headquarters planning, while retaining a construction attentive to liability-creating conduct. Their direct join in Part III does not adopt a categorical rule that any injury abroad is conclusive regardless of where the tortious conduct occurred.

Justice Breyer joins the Court's opinion and adds a comity concern. International acceptance of a substantive prohibition does not necessarily establish agreement that the courts of every nation may enforce it against foreign persons for foreign conduct. Territorial or nationality connections ordinarily reduce that concern. For certain grave international crimes, acceptance of universal criminal jurisdiction supports related civil recovery because many legal systems combine criminal and civil adjudication; torture, genocide and core war crimes fit that established inquiry. He identifies no comparable procedural consensus for this brief arbitrary arrest. This further reason supports rejection here while preserving the existing actions and his join in Part II; it supplies no general controlling universal-jurisdiction or exhaustion rule.
