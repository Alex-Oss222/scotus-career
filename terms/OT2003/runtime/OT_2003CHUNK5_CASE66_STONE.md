## 66. United States v. Lara

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the double-jeopardy judgment and remand for further federal proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit litigation of the federal charge and independently preserved defenses. Award no conviction or validation of the tribal proceeding’s unreviewed procedural details.
* **Remedial Reach:** Lara’s federal prosecution; the judgment determines sovereign source, not blanket immunity for every tribal prosecution.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must reinstate the federal case and resolve ordinary charging, proof and defense questions. The Government must prove its federal offense beyond a reasonable doubt.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Restoration of inherent authority and separate sovereigns

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may recognize and restore a tribe’s inherent criminal jurisdiction over nonmember Indians, so that a subsequent federal prosecution is not barred by double jeopardy.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open source-of-authority question while preserving separate individual constitutional claims.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A recurring nonmember-Indian jurisdiction question requires a source rule. All persons and all tribal criminal powers are beyond this record. Treating Congress’s express restoration as an unexplained case exception would not answer double jeopardy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may lift a federal common-law restriction on a tribe’s retained criminal authority over nonmember Indians, and a prosecution under that restored inherent authority is not a federal prosecution for double-jeopardy purposes. Section 1301(2) does so; the defendant therefore cannot bar this federal case solely by identifying the earlier tribal case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The statutory restoration for Indians in the presented reservation setting; the rule does not determine non-Indian jurisdiction or dispense with applicable procedural protections.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the reserved individual-rights challenge, courts apply the Indian Civil Rights Act and existing jurisdictional and review requirements; this sovereign-source ruling supplies no procedural exemption.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wheeler makes sovereign source, not the amount of congressional supervision, decisive. Duro’s limit concerned the recognized scope of retained authority, not an irrevocable constitutional transfer of all Indian prosecutions to the United States. Congress’s express recognition restores that scope without turning each tribal charging decision into federal action.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Wheeler is applied. Duro v. Reina’s former restriction is recognized as legislatively altered within Congress’s authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Tribal-trial due process and equal protection were not presented for adjudication; non-Indian criminal jurisdiction lies outside this record.
