# Norton: objective plan and posture source extraction for fresh validation

Source: Norton v. Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance, No. 03-101, published recitations of the parties' claims and plan language, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/03-101.ZO.html . The source is a reported recital, not recovery of the original plans, declaration or briefs. Only the objective points below are supplied to neutral review. This supplement supplies no Supreme Court disposition, vote, judicial construction of the plan or commitment.

## Pleaded plan provisions and performance

The plan-compliance count was reduced during the litigation to provisions of the 1991 San Rafael resource management plan and the 1990 Henry Mountains off-road-vehicle implementation plan.

The San Rafael route-designation plan had been completed. The Henry Mountains use-supervision files for designated areas had been created. The complaint's possible demand for continuing maintenance of those files must be distinguished from a demand for their initial creation. The supplied recital identifies the supporting sources as the 2003 San Rafael Route Designation Plan; the relevant BLM field manager's declaration at volume 3 of the appellate appendix, page 771; and SUWA's merits brief, page 12, acknowledging completion.

The remaining Henry Mountains claim concerned continuing intensive monitoring of ORV use, particularly in the Factory Butte area. The plan stated that that area “will be monitored and closed if warranted.” Its general use-supervision discussion provided that resource damage would be documented and recommendations made for corrective action; monitoring in open areas would focus on damage potentially warranting a designation change; and supervision would emphasize limited and closed areas. The source cites the plan at the Supreme Court appendix, pages 140 and 148–149.

BLM stated in its appellate brief that informal monitoring had occurred for some years, and the published recitation says SUWA did not contest that statement. SUWA acknowledged in its merits brief, page 12, that a monitoring program had recently begun. The demand was for continuing implementation of the program, not solely an initial act already performed.

The Henry Mountains plan had no express statement that implementation was subject to budget constraints. Whether such a limitation follows from the plan's legal character is a legal issue, not a supplied factual premise.

## Planning framework and NEPA claim

The plans at issue had already been approved. SUWA relied on increased ORV use as new environmental circumstances or information and alleged a failure to examine whether the existing environmental statements required supplementation. The legal issue remains whether completed approval or ongoing management supplies a remaining proposed major federal action under the enacted and regulatory terms. This extraction supplies no finding of a separate pending project or plan amendment.

The contemporaneous regulation in 43 C.F.R. §1601.0-5(k) describes a resource-management plan as not itself a final implementation decision for actions requiring further specific plans, process steps or decisions under particular laws and regulations. Section 1610.5-3(a) requires subsequent authorizations, actions and more detailed planning to conform to the approved plan. Section 1712(e) permits the Secretary to issue management decisions implementing land-use plans. These source terms must be read with the approved statutory and environmental-regulatory supplements, not taken as a predetermined characterization of every provision.

An independently imposed monitoring duty under 43 C.F.R. §8342.3 is distinct from the plan-language theory presented here; the supplied source identifies enforcement of that regulatory duty as outside the question presented. This is a review-scope recital, not a conclusion that such a duty is unenforceable.

Neutral validation should determine the source status, exact preserved scope and whether the completion/continuing-maintenance distinction is material. Fresh modeling then applies the existing section 706(1) alternatives to the actual pleaded terms and completed-plan posture.
