# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Engine Manufacturers Association v. South Coast Air Quality Management District
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1343; 541 U.S. 246.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-04-28
* **Question Presented:** Whether Clean Air Act §209(a) preempts local fleet-purchase requirements based on vehicle emissions merely because those requirements govern buyers rather than manufacturers or sellers.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming judgment sustaining the Fleet Rules on the purchase-versus-sale distinction.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturers seek invalidation of the conflicting rules; the district seeks to preserve its air-quality program.
* **Material Record:** Six rules govern vehicle purchases or leases by specified fleets in the South Coast air basin. Some reach private operators and all reach public operators. They select vehicles through fuel or emission requirements. The lower courts did not resolve every rule-specific defense.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged premise is categorical nonpreemption of purchase restrictions. Proprietary public purchases, existing vehicles and any applicable waiver require separate statutory and factual analysis.
* **Relevant Dates:** The district adopted the rules in 2000; review is confined to the enactments and record preceding the April 2004 event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation addresses a different Clean Air Act permitting relationship; Environmental Defense Center concerns municipal discharge regulation, not motor-vehicle preemption.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Purchase restrictions as emission standards

* **Controlling Law:** Cipollone v. Liggett Group and Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr require attention to the actual preemption text and predicate duty. Building and Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders and Contractors distinguishes genuine proprietary action in its own statutory field.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the federally displaced standard and the conduct it governs. An enforcement technique does not necessarily change the substantive criterion; proprietary status is source-specific, not a universal exemption.
* **Other Governing Law:** Clean Air Act §§209 and 246, including 42 U.S.C. §7543(a), with any actually applicable waiver provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory use of emission standards governs; federal authority over manufacturers is not itself a prohibition of every local fleet choice.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires text-specific preemption but leaves these purchase mandates unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether directing fleet purchasers to meet emission criteria enforces a prohibited standard, and which separable applications survive.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The lower courts considered rules governing purchases outside §209 because the vehicles remained certified for sale.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Manufacturers argue that a mandatory emissions criterion is a standard regardless of which side of the sale receives the command.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The district argues that choosing among already certified vehicles does not establish a competing manufacturing standard and invokes its air-quality and purchasing responsibilities.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Genuine procurement, rules about vehicles already in use and statutory waivers may present distinct grounds not adjudicated below.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Rejecting a categorical purchase exemption vacates the judgment and requires rule-specific preemption review. Accepting that exemption affirms on the reviewed premise. Neither course justifies declaring every public purchase or every Fleet Rule invalid without examining its operation and any independent exception.
