## Objective scope checks for the fresh validator

The compact neutral packet does not itself reproduce the full statute or every separately challenged clause. The exact source selections permit fresh validation of at least these distinct subjects, without any predicted vote or disposition:

1. FECA §323(a), (b), (c), (d), (e) and (f), enacted by BCRA §101; each different actor, act, definition, exception and federal-election-activity category. Section 323(b)'s Levin-fund conditions and §301(20)'s four affirmative categories and four exclusions require their own text.
2. Section 201 primary and expressly conditional fallback definitions; media/debate/expenditure exceptions; relevant-electorate rule; ordinary disclosure; donor-account alternatives; the separate contracts-as-disbursements clause. The lower court did not treat the disclosure subsection and the definition as one indivisible issue.
3. Sections 203 and 204's different text and interaction, including the statutory nonprofit exception, its source/account restrictions, targeted-communication rule and the separate constitutional nonprofit issue.
4. Sections 202, 211, 212, 213 and each subsection of 214. The lower court separately addressed §212 reporting ripeness and §214(b)/(c) reviewability. The January 2003 regulations are saved for checking the operative administrative predicates.
5. Sections 304, 305, 307, 316 and 319's distinct standing questions; any separately presented §102/indexing claim must be checked against the actual appeal papers rather than assumed identical. Section 305's certification includes express television and radio alternatives; the statute does not simply ban mentioning an opponent.
6. Sections 311 and 504's distinct sponsor-identification and broadcaster-record duties; §504 covers purchase requests, including rejected requests, and separately identifies candidate and national-importance categories.
7. Section 318's complete age-based text; the lower judges agreed on judgment but offered differing scrutiny accounts. Their grounds are lower-court evidence, not a predetermined Supreme Court result.
8. The Paul parties' Press Clause and media-exemption theory appears expressly in their questions and in the per curiam discussion; it is not fully stated in the compact neutral packet.
9. Sections 401–403's severability, effective-date and review provisions.
