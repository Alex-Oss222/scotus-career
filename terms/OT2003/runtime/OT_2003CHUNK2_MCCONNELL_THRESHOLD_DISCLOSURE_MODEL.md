## 2. Statutory conditions and actual lower judgment

### Reporting, identification and broadcast records

**Section 201 ordinary reporting.** The primary category is a broadcast, cable or satellite communication identifying a federal candidate within 60 days of a general, special or runoff election, or 30 days of a primary, preference election, nominating convention or caucus. Nonpresidential coverage additionally requires the relevant-electorate condition: reception by at least 50,000 persons in the specified House district or Senate State. News/commentary/editorial communications have the party/committee/candidate ownership-control qualification; expenditures and independent expenditures, qualifying debates/forums and their solely promotional communications, and lawful regulatory exemptions are distinct exclusions. The statute forbids a regulatory exemption for an otherwise covered section 301(20)(A)(iii) communication. The conditional fallback requires both promotion/support/attack/opposition and no plausible meaning other than an exhortation to vote; the district's deletion of the latter condition is a disputed remedy, not enacted text.

For direct production/airing costs aggregating **more than $10,000** in a calendar year, a statement is due within 24 hours of each disclosure date, including each later additional over-$10,000 aggregate. It identifies the disburser, persons directing or controlling its activity, records custodian and nonindividual principal place of business; each disbursement of **more than $200** and its recipient; and pertinent elections and candidates known or to be identified. Section 304(f)(2)(E) confines donor names and addresses to donors of **$1,000 or more** to a qualifying segregated account, measured from the first day of the preceding calendar year through disclosure. That account must consist solely of direct contributions for electioneering communications by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents; it may also be used for other purposes. Without that account, (F) reaches $1,000-or-more contributors to the disburser over the same period. It is inaccurate to say every entity need disclose only earmarked advertising donors or only donors to a special account. Reporting is additional to other reporting duties; the tax-law definitions remain unaffected.

**Section 304(f)(5).** Executed contracts are deemed disbursements. Section 104.20 starts reporting upon public distribution when the aggregate exceeds the threshold, but its report of obligations can include other contracted communications that have not aired. Thus the existence of that rule does not itself dispose of this challenge. The district sustained ordinary disclosure but severed the contracts clause in full.

**Section 212.** The statutory independent-expenditure report is due within 24 hours for $1,000-or-more aggregates after the twentieth day but more than 24 hours before an election; within 48 hours for $10,000-or-more aggregates up to and including the twentieth day; and again upon each corresponding additional aggregate for the same election. Contracts are included in the statutory wording; receipt is filing for the $1,000 report. The effective section 109.10(c)–(d) rule measures those report deadlines from public distribution/dissemination. The live challenge attacks compelled advance reporting, and the district dismissed it as unripe. That is different from a merits approval of every section 212 duty.

**Section 311.** The challenged change extends source identification to electioneering communications and other covered non-express advocacy. The additional printed-box/type/contrast, candidate approval, image, voice and four-second specifications are enacted context; the district identifies no independent preserved attack on each such detail. Candidate/authorized-speaker identification differs from the other-speaker responsible-for-content statement and connected-organization identification. Neither is section 305's price condition or section 504's public file. The district rejected the extension challenge.

**Section 504.** A licensee must keep a public record of a request to buy broadcast time by or for a legally qualified candidate for public office, or communicating a message relating to a political matter of national importance, including a legally qualified candidate, a federal election, or a national legislative issue of public importance. Accepted **and rejected** requests are covered. The record states acceptance/rejection, charged rate, airing date/time, purchased class, and pertinent candidate/office, election or issue. Candidate requests identify candidate, authorized committee and treasurer; other requests identify purchaser, a contact person's name/address/telephone, and chief executive officers or executive-committee/board members. Information is placed in the file as soon as possible and retained at least two years. The section is not restricted to completed broadcasts, electioneering communications, federal-candidate purchases or a $10,000 threshold. The district invalidated it entirely.

### Threshold subjects

**Section 305.** The lowest-unit-rate certification is made at purchase. A candidate may refer to another candidate for the same office while keeping eligibility by satisfying the television or radio alternative. Television requires the candidate image and readable identification/approval/authorized-committee-payment statement simultaneously for at least four seconds at the end; radio requires the candidate's personal identifying, office and approval statement. A noncompliant reference loses the rate for that and subsequent covered broadcasts in the applicable 45-/60-day periods. The district dismissed for lack of standing. It described Pence's actual testimony about his advertisements and future campaign practice, Barr's showing, and McConnell's future opponent-critical plans and 2008 election. These reported features support a bounded threshold decision; they do not authorize saying civil price consequences can never injure a speaker.

**Section 307.** Beginning with contributions made January 1, 2003, the individual candidate ceiling rises from $1,000 to $2,000, the national-party ceiling from $20,000 to $25,000, the aggregate becomes a two-calendar-year allowance of $37,500 for candidates plus $57,500 for other contributions with the $37,500 nonnational-party sublimit, and the senatorial committee allowance becomes $35,000. The specified ceilings receive the enacted inflation adjustments, $100 rounding and odd-year/two-year operation where applicable; the text retains different 1974 and 2001 base years. Unchanged nonparty-PAC ceilings do not become amended provisions merely because other ceilings were changed. The lower court dismissed the increased-limit/indexing challenges, without separately resolving Paul's operative candidate-ceiling injury on its actual theory.

**Sections 304/316/319.** These are distinct candidate-response mechanisms, not Daggett's public-grant program. The Senate threshold formula is $150,000 plus four cents per voting-age resident, with the enacted twice/four-times/ten-times bands, threefold or sixfold contribution allowances, aggregate-limit exceptions and the highest band's coordinated-party relaxation. Opposition personal funds compare the opposing and recipient candidates' personal expenditures, with section 316's half-gross-receipts-advantage adjustment and specified June 30/December 31 measurements. The House mechanism uses opposition personal funds over $350,000, tripled individual limits, aggregate exception and coordinated-party relaxation, its own gross-receipts rule, and a 100-percent cap; the Senate acceptance cap is 110 percent. Receipt of notification precedes increased-limit acceptance, attributable permission ceases when the relevant opponent withdraws, and unspent excess contributions must be returned within 50 days. The distinct declarations, initial/additional notices, recipient rules and excess reports remain part of the statute. Section 304 also contains the personal-loan repayment limitation above $250,000 from post-election contributions. The presented self-financing challenge and lower threshold disposition do not establish a claimant subject to that separate loan limit. No merits ruling on it is manufactured. The district dismissed the actual self-financing claims for lack of standing.

## 3. Threshold commitments and adversarial alternatives

### A. Section 305: dismissal, without adopting the district's criminal-sanction premise

Each Associate would **affirm the dismissal without prejudice** of this facial challenge. The sufficient common ground is Lujan's concrete, actual-or-imminent personal application: the supplied candidate-specific posture does not establish that a plaintiff presently must choose between the challenged certification/identification conditions and the preferential rate in a covered election. McConnell's identified 2008 exposure is too remote on this showing; the reported Pence and Barr posture does not supply the required present conflict. No new factual conclusion about absent documents is necessary.

The opposing path is immediate pre-enforcement review under Lee and Maine Right to Life: the purchase-time certification itself can constrain preparation, and a civil financial consequence can chill speech. This is a legally valid path when concrete application is shown, not an exception reserved for criminal statutes. It does not follow here merely from future candidacy and an intention to criticize an opponent; criticism can comply with the statute, and the actual temporal and conduct nexus must be established. Section 403(c)'s congressional-plaintiff authorization cannot substitute for Article III injury. The dismissal resolves no First Amendment validity or entitlement to the lowest unit price.

### B. Section 307: three claims, not one equal-influence claim

**Paul's amended individual-to-candidate ceiling:** Each Associate would **reverse the no-standing disposition on this component**, recognize the candidate's concrete restraint, and **reject the preserved categorical Press Clause exemption on its merits**. Paul's paragraph 14 describes actual donors willing to give beyond the limit even as amended and continued interference with his own candidate communication. Lujan permits the ordinary favorable inference from that specific declaration. Enforcement of the operative ceiling directly prevents those receipts; removing it would redress that restriction. Congress's decision to make the ceiling less restrictive does not make a candidate unable to challenge the remaining restraint, and success on the constitutional theory is not an element of injury.

The rejected path would treat all section 307 plaintiffs as seeking restoration of lower ceilings or equal electoral influence. That is factually and legally wrong for this claim. The alternative **merits** path would remand a preserved independent close-fit challenge for Russell/Shrink/Lamb analysis of actual amount, fundraising scale, cumulative burden and justification. The present appealed question and pp. 45–50 identify a Press Clause theory of candidate publishing immunity and unequal treatment versus the institutional press. They do not establish that every operational claim concerning the new ceiling has independently been submitted for merits resolution. Rejecting that categorical theory requires no new empirical finding. It also supplies no adjudication that $2,000 is invariably closely fitted. Remand should direct disposition of the presented Press Clause theory; a genuinely preserved independent application claim, if identified by the operative pleadings, must receive its own ordinary adjudication under the stated contribution standard rather than an invented finding here.

**Unchanged nonparty-PAC ceilings:** Each Associate would **leave the challenge to the unchanged ceilings outside this section 403 direct-review merits judgment**, and remand for dismissal from that special proceeding without prejudice to the proper FECA review route. The specific maximum-giving evidence defeats a casual assertion of no injury, but it cannot expand the statute conferring this expedited review. Section 403 covers BCRA or its amendments; the old receipt/giving ceilings are analytically distinct. Mariani supplies the actual section 437h eligibility, substantiality and certification discipline; it is not a command to certify every plaintiff or every claim automatically. The relief must not direct a newly filed action or decide a claim by an ineligible claimant.

**Amended indexing/classification as such:** A claim that BCRA's increased/indexed allowances for other recipients necessarily violate the Press Clause does not confer an entitlement to have an old PAC ceiling judicially increased or indexed. To the extent the challenge attacks that new classification independently and is within section 403, reject the categorical press-equality ground for the reasons in section 6 below. This is separate from declaring the underlying old ceiling valid or assuming Congress must give every financial channel identical limits.

### C. Sections 304, 316 and 319: threshold dismissal of the presented self-financing claims

Each Associate would **affirm dismissal without prejudice**. The lower court's reported claimant posture supplies no present triggering race for these plaintiffs; the RNC actors are not themselves the asserted candidate class. The Adams claim remains the increased-influence theory, and the party's asserted ability to support its candidates equally does not establish a current forced transfer or actual adverse treatment in a qualifying election. The direction of the alleged burden matters: a self-financing candidate subject to an opponent's responsive allowance, a candidate entitled to an increased limit, an additional opponent and a party committee are not interchangeable claimants.

The contrary path uses Day and Daggett to recognize that a statutory response to protected expenditures can produce injury before money is actually transferred. That principle does not dispense with an actual or imminent application of this different formula. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their Day/Daggett objection to adverse financing consequences; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their distinct bounded-public-program position. None can use that merits disagreement to invent a claimant subject to this scheme. The declaration, notice, personal-loan and return provisions are not given unpresented independent constitutional judgments. The section 305 Justice-specific Article III authorities above apply to each Associate here; the differing Day/Daggett authorities explain why they stop at the threshold despite genuinely different possible merits views.

### D. Section 212: dismiss only the presented advance-reporting challenge

Each Associate would **affirm dismissal without prejudice** of the advance-disclosure challenge as unripe. The effective rule requires reporting following public distribution/dissemination and supplies the practical protection sought by that challenge. The claim that the FEC might later abandon the rule or that Congress might disapprove it does not establish current threatened advance reporting. This is not a decision that the regulation overrides every statutory duty or is substantively valid under every theory; it is a conclusion about present exposure under the existing binding administrative regime.

The competing path emphasizes the mandatory statutory words concerning contracts and Lee's present civil duties. It would be persuasive if current law in operation compelled a report before dissemination despite the rule. Unlike section 201's report of multiple outstanding obligations after one airing, section 109.10's identified independent-expenditure reporting trigger supplies no present advance-disclosure demand in the reviewed challenge. No advisory-opinion exhaustion is imposed. A concrete change or enforcement conflict remains reviewable in its proper channel.

Stevens grounds the disposition in absent present exposure, consistently with his National Park Hospitality standing position and Lee opinion. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg may join the fitness/hardship rationale in light of their National Park Hospitality joins, while preserving the substantial difference between its nonbinding policy and this effective protection. O'Connor and Breyer join this narrower no-current-advance-reporting result on Lee and Maine Right to Life's actual-controversy premise; they need not adopt every rationale of National Park Hospitality, which they did not join. All eight would reject describing section 212 as facially sustained on the merits.

## 4. Ordinary section 201 reporting and section 311 extension

### Common operative commitment

Each Associate would **reject the facial categorical challenge to ordinary section 201 reporting** and **affirm rejection of the preserved section 311 extension challenge**. The primary category can constitutionally define these disclosure duties even if it cannot define the whole permissible reach of a treasury prohibition. Large, objectively identified candidate-referencing broadcasts close to an election have a direct electoral-information connection. Identifying their payer and significant financing sources enables voters to assess organized election-period advocacy and trace financing through intermediary names. It neither makes the expenditure a candidate contribution nor requires a showing of corruption by every independent speaker.

The actual section 201 thresholds, finite reporting event, segregated-account alternative and donor amount/period limitations do constitutional work. The account option confines disclosure to that pool if its actual source conditions are met; the alternative all-qualifying-contributor rule cannot be silently rewritten as earmarked-donor-only reporting. On this facial attack the substantial relation of the financial pool to the purchased candidate-focused communication, together with the lawful segregation option, is enough. The rationale is not permission to publish every member or unrelated person associated with an issue organization. A supported reasonable probability of threats, harassment or reprisals remains governed by Buckley/Brown and Mariani; a general desire for privacy does not establish the exception. These commitments rely on the district's adjudicated facial posture and the legal rule, not a new categorical assertion that every cited reprisal account is false or absent.

For section 311 the factual source designation remains distinct from an ideological pledge. Identifying the sponsor of the objectively covered communication does not force the sponsor to endorse a governmental position. Stengel's organizational-finance rationale and McIntyre's reserved distinctions permit rejecting the specific assertion that identification necessarily fails whenever the message lacks express advocacy. The additional typography, image and timing details are not independently adjudicated beyond the presented extension claim.

The strongest alternative would import the expressive-content boundary governing prohibitions or full political-committee status into every report, or apply McIntyre's ordinary individual leaflet rule to all paid broadcast finance. Bartlett itself preserves distinct sponsor-disclosure predicates, Stengel distinguishes financed organizational candidate advocacy, and Mariani establishes an independent source-attribution justification. Conversely, these authorities cannot justify an unrestricted donor registry: the actual covered communication, reporting scale, funding-pool relationship, statutory account choice and supported reprisal protection are necessary limits of this conclusion.

## 5. Contracts and broadcaster files: separate, formulation-sensitive commitments

### A. Section 304(f)(5)

The common threshold commitment is to **reach this challenge**. The first airing may trigger a statement embracing outstanding contracts for other communications, so section 104.20 does not supply section 212's complete answer. The principal alternatives are (1) sustain reporting of real financial obligations as materially connected to election-period spending; or (2) preserve disclosure for aired communications, including unpaid contractual obligations, while invalidating compelled public disclosure of a separate communication's still-unbroadcast contractual plans. Entire-clause severance is broader than the second path requires because the clause also captures deferred payment for an aired communication.

### B. Section 504

**Candidate-request files:** Each Associate would **reverse the district's complete invalidation** insofar as it prevents the candidate-request file. Records of the licensee's acceptance/rejection, rates, time class and placement serve a separate verification function concerning access to a licensed broadcast facility. They also trace the actual sponsor. A purchaser's section 201 report does not record all rejected requests, station treatment or candidate-purchase circumstances, so overlap does not make the file pointless. The statute requires transaction records rather than agency approval of political content. No finding of actual past discrimination is necessary to sustain an appropriately fitted verification duty.

**Aired noncandidate political messages:** Each Associate would also **reject the facial challenge to the source/transaction file for aired covered messages**, including the national-legislative-issue class. Buckley's electoral-corruption interests are not the exclusive possible information interests in paid mass broadcasting; Bellotti's surviving source-information observation and Stengel's distinction between individual leaflets and organized paid advocacy support the result. Publicly identifying the purchaser and responsible organizational leadership differs from a general donor or membership register. The ordinary meaning of the enumerated political categories gives the professional transaction-record duty sufficient content for this facial claim; no ruling approves every conceivable marginal application.

**Requests for separate noncandidate messages never yet aired, including rejected requests:** The alternatives remain distinct. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would **reverse the district's invalidation of this application as well**. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would **retain a narrow injunction against mandatory public disclosure of these unbroadcast noncandidate requests**, while reversing the rest of the blanket injunction. The latter application is severable under section 401; they do not rewrite the statute's request language into a universally completed-purchase rule. ## 6. Independent Press Clause and media-exemption theory

Each Associate would **affirm rejection of the categorical claim that describing candidate campaigns, political organizations and all communicators as the general press immunizes them from otherwise valid contribution, source-reporting or sponsorship rules**. Press freedom is not confined to a privileged institutional caste. That does not mean actual candidate receipts become the publisher's own independent expenditure, or that every regulation of a communicative transaction is an impermissible editorial license. Buckley, McIntyre, Stengel, Akins, Clifton and Mariani require examination of the actual regulated activity, not a choice between absolute general-press immunity and no press protection.

Each would also **reject the asserted automatic invalidity of BCRA because it preserves a news/commentary/editorial exemption**. Bellotti protects noninstitutional speakers against exclusion from First Amendment protection. It does not require treating a candidate's campaign receipt or paid advocacy purchase as identical to a broadcaster's independent news function. The statutory ownership/control limit and the actual news function must be acknowledged; the exemption does not itself confer a universal license to favor media owners or define away protected independent advocacy. A concrete discriminatory application or unjustified expenditure prohibition must be decided on its own proper component, and the parent analysis of treasury and issue-speech applications remains independent.
