## I. The actual regulatory choice and its qualifications

The rule requires a regulated small municipal separate storm sewer system to obtain discharge authorization. In an authorized State, Tribe or Territory the authorized government is the permitting authority; otherwise the EPA Regional Office performs that role. The municipality is not compelled to make its State administer the federal program, and a State's refusal to administer does not eliminate federal permitting.

Under section 122.33(b)(2)(ii), an operator seeking an individual permit for a program different from section 122.34 uses the application requirements in section 122.26(d). The operator may omit the information in section 122.26(d)(1)(ii) and (d)(2) concerning its legal authority unless it wants the permit writer to consider that information when developing other permit conditions. This is permission to omit that information, not a command to withhold it and not an exemption from every part of the application. Section 122.34(a) expressly makes the listed minimum controls inapplicable when the operator applies under section 122.26(d).

Those provisions create a distinct program route; they are not a guarantee of particular final permit terms. The route may still require information and lawful conditions governing the operator's own discharge. A final condition that actually requires an unwilling municipality to administer a third-party program needs its own lawful basis and cannot be sustained merely by the individual-permit label. The supplied facial record does not establish that every available individual permit imposes the same disputed third-party duties or that the route is otherwise unavailable. The alternative-permit experience identified through City of Abilene illustrates availability without becoming controlling law or proof of every future permit's validity.

The minimum-control route is substantive. Section 122.34(a) calls for a program designed to reduce pollutants to the maximum extent practicable, protect water quality and satisfy appropriate Clean Water Act requirements. The permit writer supplies a development and implementation period of up to five years from issuance. The program includes education/outreach, public involvement, illicit-discharge controls, construction-site controls, post-construction controls and municipal housekeeping. This description is not a finding that all of those measures concern only municipal property.

The third-party measures retain their actual qualifications:

- The ordinance or other regulatory mechanism against non-stormwater discharges, construction erosion-and-sediment controls and sanctions, and the mechanism for post-construction runoff operate to the extent allowable under State, Tribal or local law. Those qualifications limit the regulatory command; they do not themselves supply a constitutional cure for a genuinely compulsory third-party program.
- The listed non-stormwater flows need to be addressed only when the operator identifies them as significant contributors of pollutants to the small system: water-line flushing; landscape irrigation; diverted stream flows; rising groundwater; uncontaminated groundwater infiltration; uncontaminated pumped groundwater; potable-water discharges; foundation drains; air-conditioning condensation; irrigation water; springs; crawl-space pump water; footing drains; lawn watering; individual residential car washing; riparian-habitat and wetland flows; dechlorinated pool discharges; and street wash water. Firefighting discharges are separately excluded from the effective non-stormwater prohibition and need be addressed only where identified as significant sources of pollutants to waters of the United States. The two conditions and their respective pollution destinations must not be collapsed.
- Construction-site control reaches disturbance of at least one acre and smaller disturbance that forms part of a common development or sale plan disturbing at least one acre. If the permitting authority waives small-construction discharge requirements under section 122.26(b)(15)(i), the municipal operator need not develop, implement or enforce the corresponding program for those sites. The waiver is permissive, not mandatory or automatic from acreage.
- The incorporated small-construction definition covers clearing, grading and excavation disturbing at least one but less than five acres, and less-than-one-acre components of a common plan ultimately disturbing at least one but less than five acres. It excludes routine maintenance preserving the facility's original line and grade, hydraulic capacity or original purpose. Other construction may be designated on the potential to contribute to a water-quality-standard violation or contribute significant pollutants to covered waters; small size alone is not an exemption.
- For the under-five-acre general-permit waiver, the Director may act when the rainfall erosivity factor is below five during construction under the rule's incorporated January 1997 RUSLE method and the operator certifies that timing. Alternatively, stormwater controls must be unnecessary under an EPA-approved or EPA-established total maximum daily load addressing the pollutants of concern, or, for non-impaired waters not requiring such a load, an equivalent analysis allocating pollutants to small construction sites or finding allocations unnecessary after considering existing in-stream concentrations, expected growth in contributions from all sources and a margin of safety. Pollutants of concern include sediment or a sediment measure and every other pollutant identified as causing impairment of a receiving water body. The operator must certify that construction and discharges will occur within the analysis's drainage area. No waiver or satisfaction of these predicates is found for an actual site here.
- Post-construction measures concern new development and redevelopment disturbing at least one acre, including smaller projects within a larger common plan of development or sale, that discharge into the small system. They require appropriate structural or nonstructural practices, the state-law-bounded regulatory mechanism and adequate long-term operation and maintenance. The separate construction waiver is not silently extended to this provision.

Recommendations expressly labeled Guidance remain recommendations. Suggested stream or beach projects, school programs, particular outreach media, planning strategies, inspection methods and enforcement examples are not each mandatory federal prescriptions merely because the rule recommends them. The minimum-control program nevertheless contains real duties; describing its guidance accurately does not turn all required measures into options.

These qualifications identify the scheme actually challenged. They supply neither a universal exception from lawful discharge limits nor the Court's independent basis for calling every third-party measure voluntary. That basis is the genuine different-program route.

## II. Federalism: alternative paths and controlling ground proposed

**Proposed rule.** A municipality remains subject to valid federal regulation of the discharges from its own storm-sewer operation. A facial challenge that third-party minimum measures commandeer its legislative or executive authority fails when a legally available, operative individual-permit route lets it comply through regulation of those own discharges without requiring the challenged administration of third persons. A nominal choice between two compulsory third-party programs would not suffice. Lawful direct compliance may cost money and remain compulsory without becoming involuntary assistance to federal administration.

Condon supplies the own-activity distinction: the work government employees perform to comply with direct regulation does not by itself turn the regulated government's own activity into conscription to regulate others. New York requires a lawful alternative rather than a choice between two forbidden duties. Lomont confirms that actual legal consequences control over the optional label, but it involved assistance to private applicants. Its unconditional right to refuse that assistance does not become a right to refuse lawful regulation of the municipality's own discharges.

**The strongest contrary path** treats the regulations as a practical command to enact ordinances and police private construction, since individual permitting may be more expensive and section 122.26(d) entails a demanding application. The objection correctly identifies some minimum measures as third-party administration. It fails on this facial record because section 122.33 authorizes a different program, allows omission of legal-authority material unless the applicant wants it considered, and section 122.34 expressly excludes that route from the listed minimum controls. The record's alternative experience supports the textual availability. Costs and inconvenience of lawful own-discharge compliance do not prove that the municipality must assume federal responsibility for regulating other persons.

The Court does not infer validity from the word permit. It distinguishes the application from the eventual conditions and requires a genuinely operative own-discharge alternative. A record establishing that the authority denied the different program solely because the municipality refused third-party legislative administration, or imposed equivalent compulsory third-party duties in the final alternative, would present a different concrete application. No such final condition or universal practical barrier is established here. That limitation prevents facial affirmance from becoming advance approval of every permit.

**The fiscal argument** supplies no independent basis for invalidation. Printz's controlling rule forbids commanding unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons; it locates the defect in compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and creates no compensation rule. This case's own-system costs are direct regulatory compliance, and the choice of minimum measures does not itself establish compelled executive administration. Neither Printz nor Lomont grants an entitlement to payment for those costs. No compensation measure, cause of action or appropriation is created or assumed.

## III. Speech: content, independent function and burden

The speech component is decided independently of whether the alternative route defeats the commandeering claim. An optional route does not establish a universal waiver of constitutional expression objections, and the alternative application does not make every information obligation disappear.

Section 122.34(b)(1)(i) requires educational materials **or equivalent outreach** concerning stormwater discharges' effects on water bodies and steps the public can take to reduce pollutants in runoff. It does not require both distribution and a separately prescribed campaign. Section 122.34(b)(3)(ii)(D) requires informing public employees, businesses and the general public about hazards associated with illegal discharges and improper waste disposal. The first concerns runoff impacts and practical reduction; the second concerns the hazards of illegal discharge and disposal. Neither is rewritten as an obligation to profess a belief or to sponsor every activity in the guidance.

**Proposed bounded rule.** Even assuming the municipal operators may assert the claimed First Amendment protection, a valid discharge-control regime may require them to provide truthful, nonideological information about their systems' pollution impacts and practical ways to avoid harmful inputs when that communication serves the independently justified operating and pollution-prevention function and is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. The government must justify that function and fit from the actual obligation; usefulness, a factual label and presumed agreement alone are insufficient. This holding concerns the defined system-related hazard information, not all public messages a regulator regards as beneficial.

Here the substantive program regulates pollutant discharges, independently of any desire to disseminate an opinion. Community inputs and improper disposal affect what the operator's system discharges. Informing people of those effects, reduction measures and disposal hazards directly serves that physical pollution-control purpose. The actual requirements permit flexible factual materials or equivalent outreach rather than prescribing ideological language. Suggestions to participate in particular civic activities are labeled guidance. The provisions contain no command to endorse EPA's policy judgments or restriction on independent criticism of the program.

The communications may identify the municipality as the operator and source of the information. The Court does not rely on an invented guarantee that no message will be attributed to it. Communicating factual hazards associated with its regulated operation is different from being required to adopt someone else's ideological belief. The absence of prescribed ideology is examined together with the real operating function and scope, not treated as sufficient by itself.

NEMA supplies a close, bounded analogy, not an automatic classification. Its rule concerns truthful, noncontroversial **commercial product** and disposal facts, justified by a substantial health or environmental purpose and protected against undue burdens. Municipal outreach has not been shown to be commercial product speech merely because it occurs within a regulatory program. The present ground extends the functional principle only to the actual system-related factual hazard communication: lawful substantive pollution controls, a substantial health/environmental objective, a direct practical connection, and a confined nonideological obligation whose method retains meaningful flexibility. It does not apply reduced protection to every municipal statement or announce a general factual-speech exception.

Glickman shows why a germane communication expense may serve an independently justified regulatory function, but its private promotional-subsidy rule is not imported wholesale. United Foods forbids letting the selected message supply its own independent justification; the pollution controls and operational responsibilities here exist independently of the public-information requirement. Barnette and Wooley protect against coerced ideological affirmation. These provisions require hazard information and practical pollution reduction, not a declaration of personal or political allegiance. Presumed municipal agreement with environmental protection is no ground for the decision.

**The competing invalidation path** emphasizes that information and education are speech, that direct communication is more than paying a fee, and that the government cannot compel advocacy simply by attaching it to a valid program. All eight accept those premises. They reject the facial conclusion because the actual duties are confined to information about the regulated system's discharges and harmful inputs, serve a separate substantive pollution-control function and leave the operator a choice of factual means. The requirements do not prescribe the municipality's position on a disputed policy or silence contrary views.

The rule's flexible methods and permission to use existing materials bear on practical burden; they do not prove that every implementation is inexpensive. There is no supported facial showing that the actual limited communications require unjustified expense, misleading content or ideological endorsement. A regulator could not sustain a materially different message or undue burden by pointing only to today's judgment. This is a bounded merits rejection on the actual provisions, without deciding municipal constitutional status generally or requiring an advertising-effectiveness trial.

## V. Precedent treatment and final compatibility instructions

- Condon applies to direct regulation of the municipal operator's own covered activity and the employee work needed for compliance. It does not automatically sustain ordinances governing third persons.
- New York applies to genuine legal choice and rejects two compulsory alternatives. The present available individual route is evaluated by its actual terms and operation, not the desirability of federal environmental policy.
- Printz/Mack retains its rule against commanding unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons, which preserves direct regulation of state activity and turns on compulsory assignment of executive responsibility rather than reimbursement. No compensation holding is transferred to the ordinary costs of municipal discharge compliance.
- Lomont applies to concrete own-work standing and the need to examine real legal consequences. Its unconditional freedom to refuse applicant assistance is distinguished from the lawful obligation to control the operator's own discharge.
- Glickman supplies the relevance of an independently justified function and germaneness, with its particular subsidy and attribution conditions confined to its own setting.
- United Foods preserves the requirement that communication not justify its own compulsory association. The independent physical pollution-control function distinguishes this case from advertising-only promotion.
- NEMA supplies the bounded factual-hazard analogy and real justification/burden limits. The present holding does not automatically characterize municipal operating communications as commercial product speech or create a universal rule for government-required facts.
- Barnette and Wooley retain protection against compelled ideological affirmation; the actual information requirements concern factual runoff effects, reduction methods and disposal hazards.

A signed plenary opinion is appropriate. No author is chosen. All eight Associates support one full opinion on the actual grounds above; their differing prior positions concerning compulsory assistance and private advertising do not create a new fracture here. Any formulation treating the individual application as a guarantee of every final term, omitting the genuine-alternative requirement, creating a compensation entitlement, making outreach commercial by fiat, or relying on presumed agreement would require reconsideration rather than a claimed existing join.

The mandate affirms the Ninth Circuit's rejection of the municipal Tenth and First Amendment challenges and denies their requested constitutional invalidation at this facial scope. It leaves the independent general-permit remands for agency review and public participation exactly as they stand. The lower court and EPA continue that administrative work under the existing mandate; this decision grants no blanket validation of the entire rule or future permit conditions. The Supreme Court's review of the granted questions concludes with its ordinary mandate.
