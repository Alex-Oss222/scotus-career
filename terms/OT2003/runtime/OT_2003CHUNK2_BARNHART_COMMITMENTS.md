## Common supported propositions

P1. Under §§423(d)(2)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(B), the agency may treat ability to perform past relevant work as a separate capacity condition and deny the initial adult disability claim at step four without first establishing that such work currently exists in significant numbers in the national economy. The sustained construction is permissible within the delegated scheme; it is not declared the only construction Congress allows.

P2. Congress placed the national-economy language with other substantial gainful work and distinguished that inquiry, including age, education and work experience, from inability to perform previous work. The word other connects the work categories but does not unambiguously carry every later qualification backward. The overall impairment-caused-inability definition does not eliminate that separate previous-work condition. Congress's exclusion of a particular vacancy, likely hiring and immediate local availability is consistent with a capacity inquiry, although those exclusions alone do not answer nationwide obsolescence.

P3. Section 405(a), incorporated for SSI through §1383(d)(1), and the binding five-step regulations supply actual authority and its exercise. Mead and Heimmermann require both that delegation and conformity with enacted limits. Walton applies those requirements in this very disability scheme. Neither expertise, complexity, longstanding practice nor a policy-ruling label alone supplies controlling force. The operative step-four regulations assess residual capacity against the physical and mental demands of prior relevant work; the national-economy inquiry belongs at step five when the claimant cannot do that work.

P4. The choice is reasonable. Past work demonstrates actual demands a claimant has performed and supplies an administrable measure of retained working ability. Requiring the impairment to prevent that work reasonably separates loss of functional capacity from job disappearance. The strongest objection is a claimant able to perform only a vanished occupation but unable to perform any existing job. That is a serious limit of the proxy, but Congress has not unambiguously required an individualized job-market determination after retained previous-work capacity is established. A reasonable categorical screen need not perfectly track every claimant's present prospects. Administrative efficiency is supporting reason within this statutory distinction, not authority to override it.

P5. Reverse the Third Circuit's judgment and remand to implement the valid step-four rule on the established capacity finding. Thomas cannot require the additional national-economy inquiry as a prerequisite to that denial. The judgment does not award benefits or establish either actual obsolescence or ability to perform an unidentified other job. It resolves the presented legal entitlement to a further inquiry; ordinary processing gives effect to the denial under the sustained rule. Existing requirements that work qualify as past relevant work and that the claimant actually retain its required functional capacity remain.

## Alternative paths and individual commitments

The alternative affirmance path rests on treating previous work as necessarily a subset of currently existing national work and reading impairment-caused inability as the overriding statutory aim. It would send Thomas back to prove disappearance and, if established, require step five rather than award benefits. The objection to that path is that it makes the separate previous-work condition subordinate to the distinct other-work inquiry and rejects an authorized reasonable choice. The textual reading is plausible but not exclusive, and the proxy is not irrational. The alternative of declaring the agency's reading statutorily compulsory is unnecessary and does not command these commitments; the full delegated-choice holding decides the actual question.
