## Merits: six full rule joins and two independent grounds

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain P2–P3 in full.** Before a court makes a pro se filing bearing another label count as the prisoner's first §2255 motion for later successive-motion restrictions, it must give notice of the intended conversion, warn about the restrictions on subsequent motions, and provide an opportunity to withdraw or amend to include the §2255 claims the prisoner wishes to raise. Without those protections the converted filing does not count for that later restriction. This is an exercise of federal judicial supervision of courts' own pleading administration in the statutory setting, not a new exception allowing a concededly successive motion through AEDPA's gates.

The rule operates here despite the earlier conversion's 1994 date: it governs use of that unwarned conversion as the predicate for barring the 1997 motion, and the supervisory rule applies at least to the case in which it is announced. It neither reopens the old Rule 33 denial nor declares all pre-enactment conversions exempt from statutory consequences. Haines supports liberal reading, not secret exhaustion of a statutory first opportunity. Lonchar and Stewart support attention to the actual dismissal and classification predicates without already having established these exact warnings.

Failure to appeal the label does not cure the omitted warning. Notice serves informed amendment or withdrawal and informed challenge to conversion, including appeal. The absence of an appeal therefore cannot establish the informed choice that the missing warning was designed to permit. Even assuming law of the case applies, it describes a judicial practice rather than an absolute restriction on power; it does not bar correction of this later counting consequence. The Court need not decide that every earlier recharacterization judgment is void.

**Scalia and Thomas support noncounting and the corrective remedy on an independent ground, not P2's affirmative permission-and-warning framework.** Liberal construction of allegations does not justify overriding a prisoner's chosen procedural vehicle and silently imposing the costs of a first §2255 motion. Their party-control objection survives warnings: a court cannot reliably predict every adverse consequence, and even an apparent immediate benefit may disappear on appeal. They would avoid involuntary conversion into a first §2255 motion where that risk cannot be removed, rather than treat three warnings as a sufficient license to convert. Their ground is the impropriety of imposing the unchosen procedural vehicle, not an equitable exception to a properly triggered statutory gate.

Their historical concurrence also found a narrower sufficient reason: conversion offered Castro no benefit because Rule 33 validly presented a claim no weaker on its merits under that vehicle. That historical application confirms the strength of their objection but is not needed to add a new factual premise to this frozen packet. The reconciled commitment rests on the broader party-control objection already tested in independent modeling, applied to the supported fact that Castro selected and reiterated Rule 33 and the judge imposed the additional label. No exact filing-date finding or new assessment of the Rule 33 claim's substantive strength is adopted. If assembly instead wishes to make the historical no-benefit application a separate operative ground, the exact procedural and merits equivalence premise requires bounded neutral source validation before its adoption; absence of that additional finding does not block the present independent ground.

Both join the jurisdiction holding and agree that law of the case neither bars nor automatically resolves the question. They refuse full merits-opinion joinder even if its practical remedy is identical. Their noncounting result is not an endorsement of every warned conversion, and their broader proposed restriction is not controlling merely because they agree with the other six on this disposition.
