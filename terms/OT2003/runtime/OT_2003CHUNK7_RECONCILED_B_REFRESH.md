## 97. Hibbs v. Winn

### Final operative commitments

**H1 — Timely review.** All eight Associates hold the petition timely. Before the original ninety-day period expired, the court of appeals recalled its mandate and ordered briefing on genuine possible en banc rehearing. That authorized reconsideration was capable of changing the judgment; the petition followed within ninety days after reconsideration ended. Section 2101(c)'s final-judgment period therefore runs from the latter event. Young's March 18, 1997 holding concerned an accepted late party request; its finality principle supports this express extension to genuine court-initiated reconsideration. The rule does not rest on mandate recall alone, an unentertained suggestion, an unauthorized late paper, a nominal order extending this Court's time, or equitable enlargement of the statutory period.

**HA — The collection-related statutory construction.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the judgment reversing the section 1341 dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings. Read together, assessment, levy and collection protect the State's imposition and obtaining of tax liabilities from the prohibited federal restraint; assessment does not mean every aspect of state tax policy. These plaintiffs seek no reduction of their own assessments or avoidance of taxes owed. Prospective disallowance of the challenged credits would permit collection of the full tax rather than impede the protected collection process. The requested declaration supports that same relief, and payment of covered organizations' remaining funds into the general fund is not a refund of the plaintiffs' taxes or a restraint on their collection.

This construction turns on the actual operation of the relief. It creates no exemption for every third-party plaintiff, credit challenge, constitutional claim or revenue-increasing suit. A third party's relief that actually restrains the protected assessment or collection remains within the Act if the adequate-state-remedy condition is satisfied. Grace Brethren forbids declaratory evasion of an otherwise applicable bar; its holding is preserved and distinguished by the different operation of the relief here, not by the religious character of the claim. Farm Credit separates substantive tax entitlement from eligibility for a federal forum; it involved an actual tax restraint. Galletti's federal collection-period and partnership-liability decision supplies no universal definition controlling this different statute.

HA also rejects the generalized argument that interference with state tax administration alone requires dismissal under comity. Fair Assessment and Great Lakes concern efforts to arrest or countermand state tax collection, not a freestanding prohibition of this challenge merely because the State places its subsidy in its tax code. That conclusion does not adjudicate an unidentified independent abstention issue. Article III standing, actual religious uses, the constitutional merits and entitlement to particular ultimate relief remain for ordinary adjudication. The federal forum is not a merits award.

**HB — The independent assessment construction.** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse the appellate judgment and remand for dismissal under section 1341. A decree requiring the State to deny legally enacted credits restrains the computation and assessment of tax liabilities under the State's system even if receipts increase. The statutory protection of assessment has work distinct from levy and collection; no own-tax or revenue-decrease condition appears in the enacted restriction. The declaration seeks the equivalent federal direction, and the demand for the organizations' remaining funds depends on invalidating the same credit arrangement. It does not create a separately maintainable route around the tax bar on this theory.

The plain, speedy and efficient state-remedy condition is satisfied on the supported account. Respondents admit they can be heard in state court, a related constitutional credit challenge was heard there, and no concrete procedural impediment to judicial determination of these federal objections is identified. Rosewell and Grace Brethren require an adequate judicial opportunity, not identical relief, success or a preferred tribunal. The admission concedes availability of a state hearing, not applicability of the Tax Injunction Act. Kotterman's described similarity does not establish identical pleadings or every remedy. Those limits do not create an otherwise unsupported adequacy controversy. HB therefore directs dismissal, not a conditional future inquiry based solely on unrecovered briefing; it does not reach the Establishment Clause merits.

**HS — Stevens's additional statutory-stability position.** Stevens joins HA without reservation and additionally emphasizes the longstanding judicial treatment of this category of challenge and Congress's failure to displace it. His published approach in Runyon, June 25, 1976, and Fink, June 22, 1987, supports restraint before upsetting settled statutory expectations. He treats the history as support for the contextual reading, not as proof that a prior decision actually adjudicated every present Tax Injunction Act issue or as a license to disregard unmistakable text. His historical Hibbs concurrence confirms this additional position on unchanged premises. His contribution is a concurrence accompanying HA; no other Associate is assigned a join to HS, and it is not an independently controlling rationale.

## 95. Norton v. Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance

### Final operative commitments

All eight Associates reverse the Tenth Circuit judgment insofar as it restored the challenged section 706(1) theories, and remand to implement the following component dispositions. The generalized nonimpairment demand, the pleaded continuing monitoring-program demand and the demand for supplementation of these already approved plans fail. Requests solely to compel completion of the San Rafael route-designation plan and creation of the Henry Mountains use-supervision files are moot because those initial acts have been completed. Completion does not adjudicate a different continuing statutory or regulatory duty; none is invented from the source's tentative discussion of possible pleading scope.

**N1R — Discrete and legally required action.** Sections 551(13) and 706(1) permit compulsion of a discrete agency act the agency is legally required to take and has unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed. The listed forms of agency action and their failures concern circumscribed acts; a general demand for a better administrative program is not such an act. Where a decision is legally required but its permissible substance is discretionary, the court may compel the decision while leaving that substance to the agency. A specific statutory deadline is not an indispensable condition of every unreasonable-delay claim. Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation supplies the prohibition on wholesale program correction; Lincoln and Bennett preserve the distinction between legal duties, administrative discretion and the appropriate review channel. The complete section 706(1) formulation is adopted here rather than falsely attributed to those earlier cases.

**N2R — Nonimpairment is binding but does not specify the demanded act.** Section 1782(c) requires management without impairing wilderness suitability during review and until Congress determines otherwise. Its exception permits continued existing mining, grazing and mineral leasing in the manner and degree conducted on October 21, 1976. Its proviso separately requires action against unnecessary or undue degradation or to afford environmental protection. Unless already withdrawn, the lands remain open to appropriation under mining laws during review, subject to a withdrawal under section 1714 for reasons other than preservation of wilderness character. After congressional wilderness designation, the Wilderness Act's national-forest wilderness rules apply, including their mineral-survey, development, access, exchange and claimant ingress-and-egress provisions. These qualifications prevent turning the provision into an unqualified prohibition of every land use or alteration.

The nonimpairment obligation is a real management standard, but it does not specify the particular omitted agency act sought in this program-wide demand. Leaving nominal choice of means to BLM would still require a court continually to decide whether its management was good enough. The generalized section 706(1) claim therefore fails. Final agency action inconsistent with a binding legal boundary remains subject to its proper review channel. Section 1732(a)'s plan-conformity duty, including the governing law for tracts dedicated to particular uses, and section 1732(b)'s unnecessary-or-undue-degradation duty remain distinct provisions; one provision's exception is not transferred to another.

**N3R — The actual plan provisions supply no binding intensive-program command.** Plan-conformity law prevents BLM from taking actions inconsistent with an operative plan until the plan is amended; review of such an action under section 706(2), subject to the applicable review requirements, is distinct from compelling every projected operation under section 706(1). A plan's statement that an activity will be undertaken does not by itself establish a binding affirmative commitment. A plan may reiterate an independently binding duty or contain language clearly making a particular act obligatory. The plan's source, language, function and context must establish that commitment.

The Henry Mountains supervision and monitoring projections do not establish the binding intensive continuing program claimed here. The Factory Butte provision calls for monitoring and closure if warranted; the related provisions concern documenting damage, recommending corrective action, identifying damage that may warrant designation changes, and prioritizing supervision in limited and closed areas. These are planning directions, not a clear commitment to the demanded legally compulsory operational program. Neither future-tense wording nor the general plan-conformity command supplies that missing legal force. The plan remains guidance and a constraint on contrary action without converting every projection of future activity into an enforceable schedule of agency operations.

The reported informal monitoring and acknowledgment that a program commenced do not establish satisfactory management and do not make the continuing-program claim moot. They distinguish it from the completed requests for initial acts. The continuing claim fails because the relied-upon planning projections are not the binding affirmative command alleged. It is unnecessary to decide whether every element of such a monitoring program, if actually made mandatory by another legal source, would be sufficiently discrete. This corrects any suggestion in the fresh commitment that an intensive or continuing program is necessarily nondiscrete or that ongoing activity automatically satisfies an enforceable duty.

Closure remains conditional; neither a distinct preserved closure claim nor an established triggering determination is supplied. The Court does not order closure, find environmental damage absent, or decide enforcement of a regulation-based monitoring duty under 43 CFR 8342.3. Any asserted continuing-file demand founded only on these same nonbinding program projections would meet the same legal answer; this conditional legal consequence neither finds that such a separate claim was pleaded nor decides an independently sourced duty. No historical inference concerning the pleading or a particular budget clause becomes a new factual finding.

**N4R — Completed approval and supplementation.** Mandatory EIS supplementation under 40 CFR 1502.9(c)(1) applies when relevant major federal action remains and either the agency makes substantial changes to the proposed action relevant to environmental concerns, or significant new circumstances or information relevant to those concerns bear on the proposed action or its impacts. These are alternative triggers. Section 1502.9(c)(2) separately permits supplementation when the agency determines it would further NEPA's purposes; that permission creates no mandatory duty by itself.

Approval of the plans at issue is complete. Continued management under those approved plans does not perpetually keep plan approval proposed, and the asserted increase in ORV use identifies no distinct remaining major federal action to which the demanded supplement attaches. This supplementation theory therefore fails. Marsh concerns significant new information while relevant major action remains; completed preparation of an EIS for an unfinished project is not completion of the project. Future amendments or site-specific authorizations retain their own NEPA obligations. The Court does not find the increased-use information insignificant or hold every later implementation decision exempt.

The June 7 Public Citizen ruling reinforces the need to identify the actual agency act and available choices. It does not establish that BLM lacks environmental authority merely because FMCSA could not veto the President's border-entry decision. No alternatives forfeiture from that different case is imported. The present missing predicate is a remaining relevant major action, not an independent actor's controlling choice or a lack of BLM authority over land management.

## 98. Pennsylvania State Police v. Suders

### Common rule and procedural commitments

**SC — Eight direct Associate joins.** An employee claiming hostile-environment constructive discharge must establish actionable discrimination because of sex and working conditions so objectively intolerable that a reasonable employee would feel compelled to resign, together with causation between those actionable conditions and the resignation. Harris and Oncale retain the distinct objectively and subjectively severe-or-pervasive environment and because-of-sex requirements; an actionable environment alone does not establish the additional compulsion threshold. Duncan, decided May 27, 2003, already governs objective compulsion: realistic corrective opportunities matter without becoming an exhaustion condition. The employee need not endure danger, futile procedures or indefinite delay, and announcement of an investigation alone is no safe harbor.

Attribution remains separate. Under the actual-authority route, the plaintiff must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of the actionable environment. Title, rank and workplace proximity alone are insufficient. A completed personnel action, economic injury, higher-management notice or an express threat of discharge is not invariably required. Apparent authority requires employer manifestations and reasonable reliance, while negligence retains its own requirements; neither replaces actual-authority proof by assertion.

The plaintiff-favorable account includes sustained alleged supervisory harassment, inadequate assistance in obtaining a complaint route, the examination-paper episode, the supervisory arrest and resignation during it. Those matters support genuine inquiries concerning environment, compulsion, authority aid, practical corrective opportunity and causation. They are not conclusive findings of liability, unlawful arrest, effective protection or unreasonable nonuse. All eight reject restoration of employer summary judgment on the supplied categorical grounds. Neither resignation nor police rank automatically proves an official employment act.

Duncan did not decide a universal independent intent-or-foreseeability formulation, and its noncontrolling complete-rule proposal is not adopted here. Stevens and Ginsburg had joined only its reasonable-jury application, not that separate complete proposal. The present defense question can be answered without resolving every possible independent mental-state formulation. Any genuinely preserved, distinct causation or mental-state issue retains its ordinary procedural footing; no unpresented element is invented or found unsatisfied. This limit does not make the present defense question unresolved.

### SA — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: compensation follows proved avoidability

These four affirm the judgment reversing employer summary judgment and remand under SC and SA. They reject the Third Circuit's reasoning that constructive discharge automatically establishes an official act or employer responsibility, but retain the judgment because the claimed complete defense is not the governing consequence of prevention and nonuse under their rule.

After the employee proves an actionable, employer-attributable wrong and otherwise compensable injury, the employer must prove by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use them; and that the particular claimed loss consequently could have been avoided. Recovery is reduced or barred only to that proved extent. This is a causal limitation on loss recovery, not a complete defense extinguishing the discrimination.

A paper policy, nonreporting alone, an unsafe or futile channel, correction after injury or the mere possibility of a complaint does not satisfy the rule. The employee need not complain to the harasser. Injury incurred before a reasonable protective opportunity and injury the procedure could not prevent remain recoverable if otherwise established. Proof that all compensable loss was avoidable can eliminate that loss recovery but does not by itself eliminate the violation, decide other relief or determine fees. The plaintiff retains the actual-aid burden; the employer bears the avoidability burden.

SA creates neither special immunity when no official employment act occurs nor a categorical exception from ordinary avoidability whenever an official act occurs. An official coercive act may make effective protection or causal avoidability impossible to prove on the facts. Duncan's earlier inquiry whether realistic options defeated compulsion remains distinct from this later compensation inquiry. Failure to prove constructive discharge does not itself extinguish an otherwise established harassment claim; discharge damages cannot be relabeled to evade a failed discharge predicate.

The complete-defense argument stresses employer diligence and incentives to seek prompt correction. SA gives those considerations real work through compulsion and proved avoidability, while refusing to extinguish a plaintiff-proved abuse of actual delegated power or unavoidable injury. Its material-aid attribution requirement is narrower than automatic supervisory responsibility. Once that requirement is proved, general prevention policy and unreasonable nonuse do not change the historical fact that employer-delegated power aided the discrimination.

### SB — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: a newly adopted complete defense

These four vacate the appellate judgment and remand under SC and SB. The Third Circuit's categorical rule that every constructive discharge excludes a complete prevention/nonuse defense must be removed. The employer is not entitled to reinstatement of summary judgment, because the defense's factual predicates and the official-act issue have not been conclusively established in its favor.

For a supervisor-created hostile environment, including one culminating in an objectively compelled resignation, the employer may establish a complete defense by proving by a preponderance both that it exercised reasonable care to prevent and promptly correct the harassment and that the employee unreasonably failed to use the preventive or corrective opportunities provided or otherwise to avoid harm. The opportunities must be practically accessible and reasonably effective. Unsafe or futile channels and paper policies are insufficient. No particular written-policy form is invariably required, and nonreporting alone establishes neither element. The plaintiff separately retains the actual-authority/material-aid burden.

The defense is unavailable when an official discriminatory employment act precipitates the compelled resignation. Such an act uses delegated employer authority to effect a significant employment decision, such as a demotion, an extreme cut in pay or a transfer imposing unbearable working conditions. Authority to make the decision does not mean the employer authorized its discriminatory motive. The plaintiff must establish the asserted official employment act and its causal connection; the employer retains persuasion on the two affirmative-defense elements. A resignation does not become that official act merely because it qualifies as constructive discharge. The arrest allegations bear on coercion, actual authority and the safety of complaint routes, but this record neither conclusively classifies the arrest as the requisite employment act nor conclusively excludes that possibility.

SB is a new resolution of a question expressly reserved in the current Faragher, Harrison and Ellerth decisions. It is not an automatic importation of their historical namesakes, a statement that a complete defense already existed, or a replacement of the governing actual-authority rule with supervisory-status liability. Its agency distinction is between authority materially aiding an environment and delegated authority consummating an official discriminatory employment decision. The former threshold establishes a relationship sufficient for presumptive attribution; the latter makes the employer's official decision the immediate source of the compelled resignation and defeats the asserted diligence defense. Genuine preventive care and unreasonable failure to use practical protection perform a separate function at the former stage.

SA's strongest objection is that this approach can eliminate liability and unavoidable loss even after actual delegated aid is proved. SB answers that Title VII agency responsibility permits a bounded affirmative defense promoting prevention and reasonable use of real corrective opportunities, while withholding it where an official discriminatory act precipitates resignation. Its supporters accept that the rule does more than reduce particular proved avoidable losses. They do not claim SA is a logical subset, that its causal restriction has been satisfied whenever SB is satisfied, or that ordinary negligence alone governs all supervisory harassment. A negligence-only rule, compulsory exhaustion and automatic immunity from a policy's existence are rejected.

### Coalition and remedy audit

SC has eight direct Associate joins. SA has Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: four votes for affirmance and the complete loss-specific rule. SB has O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: four votes for vacatur and the newly adopted complete-defense rule. These are firm competing legal commitments, not a numerical compromise or a research reservation. The historical alignment of O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy in result does not mean that historical Faragher/Ellerth automatically entered present law; each expressly adopts SB within the different current actual-aid framework.

The two frameworks cannot be combined into eight votes for a defense or into a Marks controlling rationale. SA preserves a violation and unavoidably incurred injury that SB may defeat; SB's official-act exception is not part of SA. The Chief's eventual compatibility testing and the resulting controlling coalition remain outside this file. No hypothetical additional join is supplied.

Under either position, ordinary lower-court proceedings address genuinely disputed facts and preserved independent grounds. No trial, discovery, production of additional papers, liability finding or award is automatically required. Harassment injury, resignation injury, backpay, other loss, statutory remedy limitations and fees retain their distinct predicates; no double recovery or punitive-damages prerequisite is presumed. The final mandate must identify which framework actually controls rather than leave the defense question generally open. This reconciliation is complete for assembly.
