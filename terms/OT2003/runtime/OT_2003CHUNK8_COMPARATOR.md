## 104. Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 7 to 1 among participating Justices.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Ginsburg wrote for six Justices; Scalia concurred in the judgment; Breyer dissented. O'Connor did not participate.
* **Historical Holding:** AMD and the Commission proceeding satisfied the relevant statutory requirements; no categorical foreign-discoverability or pending-proceeding requirement applied, and discretionary consideration remained.
* **Historical Authority:** Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., No. 02-572; 542 U.S. 241 (2004).

## 109. Cheney v. United States District Court for D.C.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded. Five Justices joined the actual remand for reconsideration; Thomas and Scalia also rejected the appellate disposition but sought reversal with instructions to issue the writ. Ginsburg and Souter would leave the denial intact.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer throughout and for Scalia and Thomas in Parts I-IV. Stevens concurred. Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurred in part and dissented from the remedial Part V; Ginsburg dissented with Souter.
* **Historical Holding:** The appellate court had to consider separation-of-powers objections under the mandamus framework rather than end review because executive privilege had not first been asserted.
* **Historical Authority:** Cheney v. United States District Court for D.C., No. 03-475; 542 U.S. 367 (2004).

## 113. Hamdi v. Rumsfeld

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded. Eight Justices rejected leaving the Fourth Circuit judgment intact, but there was no eight-Justice common rationale.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O'Connor's plurality, joined by Rehnquist, Kennedy and Breyer, recognized narrow AUMF detention authority and required a meaningful status hearing. Souter, joined by Ginsburg, rejected the asserted statutory authority but joined the remand judgment and supported process. Scalia, joined by Stevens, dissented and required criminal process or a valid suspension of habeas. Thomas dissented in support of the government.
* **Historical Holding and Limits:** The controlling disposition required further proceedings; the plurality contemplated flexible evidentiary procedures, including hearsay and a rebuttable presumption.
* **Historical Authority:** Hamdi v. Rumsfeld, No. 03-6696; 542 U.S. 507 (2004).

## 114. Missouri v. Seibert

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5 to 4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote a plurality for Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy concurred in the judgment under a narrower deliberate two-step rule with curative measures. Breyer separately concurred. O'Connor dissented with Rehnquist, Scalia and Thomas.
* **Historical Holding and Limits:** The postwarning repetition was inadmissible on this interrogation record; the plurality's objective approach and Kennedy's intent-based limitation were distinct.
* **Historical Authority:** Missouri v. Seibert, No. 02-1371; 542 U.S. 600 (2004).

## 118. Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded unanimously in the judgment, with differing rationales.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote the opinion. Parts I and III were unanimous; Part II had seven joins, excluding Ginsburg and Breyer; Part IV had six, excluding Rehnquist, Scalia and Thomas. Scalia, joined by Rehnquist and Thomas, concurred in part and in the judgment. Ginsburg, joined by Breyer, separately addressed the FTCA ground; Breyer also wrote separately.
* **Historical Holding:** The FTCA foreign-country exception barred the foreign-injury claim despite domestic planning, and the asserted brief arbitrary detention did not support an ATS damages action. The historical opinion left a limited category of specifically defined international-law actions available.
* **Historical Authority:** Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain, Nos. 03-339 and 03-485; 542 U.S. 692 (2004).

## 119. Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No Supreme Court merits decision exists in this matter. The D.C. Circuit unanimously affirmed dismissal on July 1, 2003; no Supreme Court merits vote is assigned to that affirmance.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Randolph wrote the D.C. Circuit opinion for Judges Henderson, Randolph and Garland.
* **Historical Holding:** The case-specific amendments addressed Iran's immunity but did not enact abrogation of the Algiers Accords' independent bar. The United States properly intervened to protect its own obligations and could seek judicial vacatur. The Ministry was treated as Iran itself. The court reserved the broad Flatow cause-of-action question and the amendments' separation-of-powers validity.
* **Historical Authority:** Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran, 333 F.3d 228 (D.C. Cir. 2003), No. 02-5145; district-court opinion, 195 F. Supp. 2d 140 (2002).
* **Historical Sources:** D.C. Circuit opinion, Parts II–III and footnotes 3 and 5.

## B. PETITION COMPARATOR

* **Historical Petition Treatment:** Certiorari denied; 542 U.S. 915 (2004), No. 03-1147.
* **Date and Material Procedural Detail:** June 14, 2004. The order announces no merits holding and records no dissent in this matter. The petition had been filed February 5, 2004, after denial of rehearing on November 7, 2003.
* **Sources:** Supreme Court order list, June 14, 2004, entry No. 03-1147; 542 U.S. 915; United States' opposition, jurisdiction statement.
