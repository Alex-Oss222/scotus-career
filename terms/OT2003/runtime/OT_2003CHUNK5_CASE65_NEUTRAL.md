* **Question Presented:** Whether Hawaii may restrict candidacy for elected Office of Hawaiian Affairs trusteeships to persons of Hawaiian ancestry, and whether these plaintiffs may challenge the separate appointment restriction.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming invalidation of the elected-candidate restriction under the Fifteenth Amendment and Voting Rights Act §2, and vacating the appointment ruling for lack of standing.

* **Material Record:** The elected OHA trustees administer a state office serving Hawaiian beneficiaries. An otherwise eligible non-Hawaiian candidate was refused nomination papers. * **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Standing must be assessed separately for election and appointment. The publicly elected state board must not be assumed to be a sovereign tribe.

### Issue 1: Ancestry eligibility in a public election

* **Controlling Law:** Rice v. Cayetano establishes the public state-election setting and invalidates the ancestry electorate, but expressly reserves candidacy. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña supplies the common substantial-justification rule for ordinary governmental racial allocations, not a historical strict-scrutiny rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish political eligibility from ordinary allocation of benefits and distinguish ancestry-based civic exclusion from genuine tribal political membership. Candidate exclusion and appointment standing require separate analysis.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifteenth and Fourteenth Amendments; Voting Rights Act §2; Hawaii Constitution article XII, §5, and Hawaii Revised Statutes §13D-2.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Herndon supplies a Fourteenth Amendment public-election exclusion principle; Smith v. Allwright concerns public political participation. Neither alone settles every candidacy qualification or establishes a general Fifteenth Amendment candidacy rule.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Rice settles the public character and ancestry operation, but not the candidate question. Ordinary Article III injury requirements independently govern appointments.
* **Open Point:** Whether equal protection independently forbids the ancestry qualification for this public office; Rice reserved the precise question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The court treated candidate exclusion as abridging voters’ effective choices, but found no standing to adjudicate appointed trusteeships.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Hawaii argues that beneficiaries may select representatives with the qualifying relationship and that unrestricted voting defeats a Fifteenth Amendment claim.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Voters and excluded candidates argue that a state cannot achieve through racial candidate eligibility the electoral exclusion Rice forbids directly.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Equal protection may support the elected-office judgment without adopting a broader Fifteenth Amendment candidacy theory or revising appointment standing.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Equal protection, the Fifteenth Amendment or a properly supported §2 interpretation could sustain the elected-office result on distinct rationales. A candidacy/electorate distinction requires consideration of the independently preserved equal-protection ground. Appointment relief fails without injury regardless of the merits. No path decides tribal membership or every Hawaiian beneficiary program.
