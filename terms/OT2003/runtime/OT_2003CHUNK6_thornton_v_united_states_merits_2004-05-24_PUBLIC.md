## Event

Thornton v. United States; No. 03-5165; 541 U.S. 615. Argued March 31, 2004; decided May 24, 2004. On review of the Fourth Circuit's affirmance of the denial of suppression and the resulting convictions, the Court considers vehicle search incident to arrest after first police contact outside the car and after the recent occupant has been handcuffed and placed in a patrol car. Thornton produced marijuana and crack cocaine during the encounter before his arrest. The ensuing passenger-compartment search revealed a handgun under the driver's seat. Thornton seeks suppression and relief from affected convictions; the United States defends the search and an alternative inventory or inevitable-discovery basis.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Affirmed, 6–3; the bounded offense-evidence permission controls under Marks from the two-Justice concurrence and four broader categorical positions; seven direct joins support the limited first-contact proposition.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Souter, who would vacate and remand for the independent admissibility ground and the resulting conviction consequences.

The Fourth Circuit's judgment sustaining admission of the handgun and the convictions is affirmed.

## Holdings

### First police contact outside the vehicle

**Controlling proposition:** The fact that police first approach a recent occupant after he has left the vehicle does not itself defeat search-incident authority. The validity of the ensuing search turns on its constitutional justification rather than that contact-timing distinction alone.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins in this limited proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** A moment's difference in first contact does not determine the constitutional interests underlying the vehicle-search exception. Belton addresses a search associated with an actual lawful arrest; a genuine recent occupant does not become categorically immune from that doctrine merely by exiting before an officer speaks. For the same reason, first contact inside the car cannot by itself answer whether the separate, later search is justified after the occupant has been secured. The controlling contact proposition therefore requires an actual search justification. Knowles preserves the distinction between a citation and custodial arrest; Florida v. Thomas supplies no merits answer because its disposition concerned finality.

### A contemporaneous search for evidence of the arrest offense

**Controlling proposition:** Following the lawful custodial arrest of a genuine recent vehicle occupant who has been secured away from the vehicle, a contemporaneous search of the passenger compartment within the vehicle-search scope is permissible when there is an objective reasonable belief that the vehicle contains evidence relevant to the offense of arrest. This affirmative permission sustains Thornton's search on the supported drug arrest and immediate vehicle connection.

**Authority:** Scalia's concurrence, joined by Ginsburg, supplies the controlling narrowest ground under Marks. O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer authorize every search within the same lawful-arrest, genuine-recent-occupancy, contemporaneous passenger-compartment category without requiring the additional offense-evidence condition. The concurrence retains all those shared predicates and adds that condition, making its affirmative permission a logical subset of the four's broader permission. For example, a secured recent occupant arrested for an offense giving no reasonable basis to expect evidence in the car satisfies the broader category but falls outside the concurrence's permission; both rules retain the same temporal, spatial and passenger-compartment boundaries, so the narrower permission contains no search excluded by the broader rule within that common domain. This is two express joins in the concurrence, not six express adoptions of a replacement test.

**Controlling explanation:** The offense-evidence ground supplies a vehicle-specific justification where the secured arrestee cannot actually reach a weapon or destroy evidence in the car. Chimel supplies the immediate-control rationale whose factual premise is absent here; Robinson's categorical search of the arrested person does not by itself explain a search of an inaccessible area. The narrower ground retains Belton's vehicle setting and lawful-arrest predicate while requiring an objective connection to evidence of that arrest offense. Thornton produced marijuana and crack cocaine before arrest and had just left his car; those facts support the required connection without using the later discovery of the gun to justify the search. The dissent would require an independently satisfied existing exception, including automobile probable cause. The controlling permission instead rests on the bounded offense-linked arrest ground; its required connection prevents mere arrest from authorizing an exploratory search. Houghton's automobile rule retains its separate probable-cause basis. Marks permits a narrower position to govern despite broader judgment supporters' rejection of its limiting condition. Nichols requires compatible predicates and a common question: here both affirming groups accept the lawful arrest and address the same bounded vehicle search, rather than disagreeing about the validity of a necessary prior judgment. Their differing justifications do not create a class of searches the narrower ground allows but the broader ground forbids.

The derived ground governs the secured recent-occupant search at those shared predicates. Independently established automobile-probable-cause, inventory and Chimel grounds retain their own factual and legal requirements.

## Precedent Treatment

- New York v. Belton: applied to a genuine recent occupant first contacted outside the vehicle; the offense-evidence permission controls for the secured recent occupant at the shared contemporaneous passenger-compartment scope under Marks, while the four-Justice lead retains its broader categorical position.
- Chimel v. California: supplies the immediate-control rationale distinguished by the controlling offense-evidence ground when the arrestee is secured away; its application to other area searches retains its own scope.
- United States v. Robinson: retains the categorical rule for searches of the person after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest.
- Knowles v. Iowa: preserves the actual-custody prerequisite and the need for an independently justified additional search; its citation holding remains distinct.
- Florida v. Thomas: retains its finality holding, which supplied no recent-occupant merits rule.
- Marks v. United States: applied to the two-Justice narrower affirmative permission logically encompassed by the four broader positions at shared predicates and scope.
- Nichols v. United States: applied to require a common legal question and compatible predicates for the narrowest-ground derivation, distinguishing agreement resting on incompatible views of an underlying judgment.
- Wyoming v. Houghton: preserves the distinct automobile-exception permission grounded in vehicle probable cause.

## Law After Decision

First contact after a recent occupant leaves a car is not an independent constitutional bar to a search with a valid arrest-related justification. Under the controlling ground derived through Marks, lawful arrest of a genuine recent occupant secured away from the car permits a contemporaneous passenger-compartment search when an objective reasonable belief connects the vehicle to evidence of the arrest offense. Thornton's search and convictions stand under that rule. The four broader categorical positions remain separate opinions from the concurrence's two express joins. Independent automobile-probable-cause, inventory and Chimel grounds retain their own applicable conditions.

## Separate Writings

The four-Justice lead applies Belton's contemporaneous passenger-compartment rule to a genuine recent occupant and includes containers within that compartment capable of the search's scope. Ordinary securing during the arrest does not, for those Justices, end the categorical authority. The rule avoids making the officer choose between promptly securing a suspect and undertaking the authorized arrest search. They acknowledge that Thornton could not actually reach the car and rely on the established category, not a finding of escape danger. O'Connor recognizes the force of the contrary access argument, but, like Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, retains the category here rather than adopting a replacement evidence standard. Genuine recent occupancy and contemporaneity bound their rationale.

Scalia, joined by Ginsburg, agrees that first contact outside is not decisive but rejects the categorical justification once Thornton is secured away. Chimel's immediate-control premise cannot explain an inaccessible area, and Robinson's personal search does not authorize every nearby intrusion. Their controlling narrower ground permits a contemporaneous search of the passenger compartment after lawful arrest of a recent occupant secured away from the car when it is reasonable to believe that the vehicle contains evidence relevant to that arrest offense. The drugs produced before arrest and the immediate association with Thornton's car supply that connection; finding the gun later supplies no earlier justification. Their narrowest ground retains Belton at this vehicle- and offense-specific scope rather than creating general exploratory authority. Its controlling status follows from Marks; the four categorical Justices do not expressly join its replacement rationale. Houghton's probable-cause automobile rule concerns a distinct exception. Ginsburg's protection of an identified passenger's belongings there does not decide this search of the arrested suspect's own recent vehicle for evidence connected to his offense.

Stone-Zsela, joined in full by Stevens and Souter, would require a reasonable possibility at the time of search that the arrestee can reach the searched area to obtain a weapon or destroy evidence. Chimel supplies that purpose, and Knowles rejects a categorical additional intrusion when its justification is absent. A first-contact line would leave the same defect whenever the officer spoke a moment earlier. Thornton's handcuffing and confinement in the patrol car defeat the access ground. They reject the new reduced-suspicion evidence rationale and preserve independently established probable cause and other exceptions on their own terms. The Government must prove the necessary factual predicates of its asserted warrantless incident-search exception by a preponderance under Matlock; the court determines the legal scope, and that factual quantum does not replace an exception's substantive probable-cause or suspicion requirement. They would remand for actual lawful impoundment, standardized inventory criteria under Florida v. Wells, and inevitable lawful discovery under Nix v. Williams. A possible inventory is insufficient. If no independent lawful basis makes the gun admissible, it must be excluded, followed by applicable error review of materially affected convictions and appropriate further proceedings. This conditional direction first resolves available independent grounds and does not order automatic relief from every count. They would apply their proposed access limit to Thornton and other still-open criminal direct-review cases under Griffith v. Kentucky, preserving the separate operation of finality, preservation, harmless-error and collateral-review requirements.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's affirmance remains operative. Admission of the handgun and the resulting convictions stand under the Court's judgment.
