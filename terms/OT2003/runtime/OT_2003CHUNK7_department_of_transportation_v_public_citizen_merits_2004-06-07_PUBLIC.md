## Event

Department of Transportation v. Public Citizen, No. 03-358. Argued April 21, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which required the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration to prepare an environmental impact statement and a full Clean Air Act conformity determination addressing increased Mexican truck operations. The Department seeks reversal; respondents seek the broader review before the rules operate. The question is whether the effects of the President's independent decision to lift the cross-border moratorium must be treated as effects of the agency's registration and safety rules.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requirements for an environmental impact statement and a full conformity determination predicated on the independently authorized increase in cross-border traffic are set aside.

## Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of a unanimous Court.

## Holdings

**Authority:** The entire opinion, including each holding and its operative qualifications below, is joined by all nine participating Justices.

### 1. NEPA connects the required analysis to the agency's own action and authority

**Holding and operative rule:** NEPA requires a reasonably close causal relationship between the reviewed agency action and the environmental effects to be studied. Where an independent governmental decision produces the challenged increase and the agency lacks statutory authority to prevent that increase, the agency's necessary implementation step does not by itself make those effects attributable to its rules; effects of the agency's own available choices remain subject to the applicable review requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Metropolitan Edison requires legally relevant causation, beyond a foreseeable sequence of events. Robertson makes NEPA an informed-decision procedure operating within the agency's lawful authority. FMCSA must register carriers satisfying its safety and financial conditions; it cannot deny otherwise qualified carriers admission on environmental grounds. The President controls lifting the moratorium. The additional entry attributed to that choice therefore is not an effect for which these rules require an environmental impact statement.

Respondents stress that the rules enable traffic and that environmental information serves the public as well as the agency. Both points preserve the value of NEPA procedure but do not transfer the President's decision to FMCSA. The relevant statutory allocation must substantiate the agency's asserted lack of control. Here it does. The assessment addressed the agency's rules, and the disputed omission concerns the independent increase in entry. The Court's rule continues to require assessment of environmental consequences caused by discretionary safety choices within the agency's authority.

### 2. Cumulative-impact analysis and preservation retain their distinct requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 40 CFR §1508.7, the agency must consider its action's incremental impact together with past, present and reasonably foreseeable future actions, regardless of actor; that requirement does not turn another actor's independent action into an effect of the agency rule. A party seeking review based on an omitted regulatory alternative must meaningfully present that alternative while the agency can address it, unless an obvious defect supplies the requisite notice without a specific comment.

**Controlling explanation:** The cumulative-impact provision expressly includes actions of other actors, but its starting point remains the incremental impact of the action being reviewed. The President's entry decision supplies context without becoming an increment caused by FMCSA's rule. Vermont Yankee separately requires meaningful participation sufficient to identify an alternatives objection. Respondents identified no additional regulatory alternative in their comments, and the asserted omission presents no supported obvious alternative the agency should have examined despite that silence. This forfeiture concerns those unpresented alternatives. It does not dispose of the independently preserved causation and conformity objections. An alternative premised on an environmental entry veto also lies outside FMCSA's statutory choices.

### 3. The conformity regulations separately require direct emissions or practicable continuing control

**Holding and operative rule:** For the conformity inquiry under 40 CFR §93.152, direct emissions must be caused or initiated by the federal action and occur at the same time and place. Indirect emissions may occur later or farther away, but must be caused by the action, reasonably foreseeable, and emissions the agency can practicably control and will maintain control over because of its continuing program responsibility; the independent traffic increase satisfies neither the direct nor the indirect definition here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Clean Air Act conformity regulation uses its own definition. Its caused-by requirement permits a but-for connection, so the Court does not simply transplant NEPA's close-causation rule. Even assuming that connection, truck emissions do not occur at the same time and place as the promulgation of these rules. For indirect emissions, reasonable foreseeability alone is insufficient: practicable control and continuing program responsibility are additional conditions. FMCSA cannot prevent the President's admission decision and does not possess continuing environmental entry-control authority. Its safety responsibilities do not establish control over the independently determined increase. The demanded full conformity determination therefore lacks the required regulatory predicate.

## Precedent Treatment

- Metropolitan Edison Co. v. People Against Nuclear Energy supplies NEPA's reasonably close causal relationship and defeats attribution based only on a necessary implementation sequence.
- Robertson v. Methow Valley Citizens Council supplies the procedural duty to make an informed choice within the agency's actual authority; it supplies no additional power to prevent the President's entry decision.
- Vermont Yankee Nuclear Power Corp. v. Natural Resources Defense Council supplies meaningful presentation of an alternatives objection, applied here subject to the supported-obvious-defect qualification.

## Law After Decision

NEPA review follows the legally relevant effects of the agency's action under its governing authority, with cumulative analysis of that action's incremental impact. The conformity inquiry separately applies the regulation's same-time-and-place or foreseeable-emissions-plus-continuing-control conditions. These rules remove the challenged review requirements for the President's independent entry increase while preserving review of effects attributable to the agency's actual discretionary choices.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion accompanies the unanimous decision.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit to implement reversal and remove the environmental-impact-statement and conformity requirements based on the independent traffic increase.
