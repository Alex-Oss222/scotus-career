## 40. Leebaert v. Harrington

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the federal judgment on the curricular and free-exercise claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No federal opt-out injunction or damages on these claims. Preserve the independent state-law dismissal without prejudice.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parent, student, and challenged attendance requirement on this record; no general immunity for public-school conduct.
* **Practical Relief:** The school may enforce the neutral attendance rule subject to independently applicable exemptions and protections. No new educational program or compelled personal affirmation is authorized.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The boundary of the parental curricular claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The stipulated attendance dispute is ready for adjudication. Nothing in the record establishes a forced profession, treatment, or targeted religious disability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether parental objection plus a religious-exercise label establishes a right to veto this ordinary public-school health instruction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing separation between instruction and independently actionable coercion; do not create a higher tier by combining unsuccessful claims.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the recurring rule covers neutral ordinary instruction while preserving distinct personal coercion. Broader denial of all parental protection at school would contradict the reserved rights. A narrower rule confined to the course title would fail to explain the constitutional boundary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A parent’s right to direct upbringing does not include a general constitutional right to exempt a child enrolled in public school from neutral ordinary instruction, and coupling that objection with a free-exercise claim does not itself trigger heightened scrutiny. Each claim must establish its own protected burden; coercion, targeting, or a discriminatory exemption scheme remains independently reviewable. The stipulated objection here establishes none of those predicates, and the conceded rational relation supports the attendance rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Ordinary curricular exposure without compulsory affirmation, treatment, intimate disclosure, or religious discrimination.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine what the student must do, not merely whether the lesson conflicts with a parent’s beliefs.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. rejects curricular veto on actual compulsory-presentation allegations while refusing blanket school immunity. Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth addresses a voluntary program and cannot alone decide compulsory attendance, but reinforces the need to identify actual interference. Wisconsin v. Yoder’s distinct community and compulsory-schooling record does not convert every instructional objection into that deprivation.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. applied; Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth and Wisconsin v. Yoder distinguished at their actual factual reach.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different compelled affirmation, treatment, and targeting are absent facts, not silently rejected claims. Until presented, their existing constitutional doctrines govern.

## 41. United States v. McFarland

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm against the reviewed Commerce Clause challenge.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The convictions and sentence receive no relief on this ground. No new sentencing or retrial direction follows from resolving it.
* **Remedial Reach:** McFarland’s reviewed robbery convictions and any expressly derivative component; unrelated defects remain outside the judgment.
* **Practical Relief:** The existing judgment remains enforceable against the stated commerce objection. Future prosecutions must still prove the enacted nexus and actual business connection.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Direct business depletion under the settled rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The operating-store record presents the commercial-asset question already resolved in United States v. Robinson. The equally divided appellate judgment creates no new substantive burden.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a substantial effect must be proved for each individual robbery despite a direct operating-business connection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Robinson without expanding a personal-victim depletion theory.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the class is direct interference with operating commercial assets. Broader coverage of personal robbery would erase the mechanism. Narrower insistence on a substantial individual loss would contradict class aggregation already permitted by entering law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Hobbs Act may reach robbery of operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce when the prosecution proves the Act’s commerce element and the taking directly impairs those commercial operations; each robbery need not have a separately substantial interstate effect. The criminal predicates remain the Government’s burden beyond a reasonable doubt. The established store robberies fall within that rule, not a generic theory that all stolen money eventually affects commerce.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Actual operating receipts and inventory-related capital; it excludes reliance solely on a victim’s employment, consumption, or an object’s remote interstate origin.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The factfinder must determine the enacted nexus; the court must identify the commercial operation the robbery directly impaired.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Robinson specifically adopts this business-function connection and permits slight individual magnitude. United States v. Lopez prohibits aggregation from creating the missing relationship. Those rules answer both sides: local geography does not defeat direct commercial impairment, and a commerce label cannot replace proof of the assets’ operating function.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Robinson reaffirmed and applied; United States v. Lopez preserved without a new category.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Robbery of a private person based only on employment or future consumption is outside this record. Until such a case is decided, the connection requirements of United States v. Robinson govern and no all-money rule exists.

## 42. Illinois v. Fisher

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the federal due-process dismissal and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The state court must reconsider under the potentially-useful-evidence rule and any properly preserved independent state-law ground. No new finding that an unperformed test would inculpate or exculpate Fisher is permitted.
* **Remedial Reach:** Fisher’s prosecution; no general order approving the police department’s retention practices.
* **Practical Relief:** The reviewed dismissal cannot stand on the rejected discovery-request exception. The state court must specify any independent basis for relief; otherwise the conviction may proceed under ordinary appellate rules.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Preservation and the actual evidentiary category

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The record expressly lacks bad faith and supplies four inculpatory tests. The federal issue can be resolved without deeming Fisher’s absence a forfeiture of all fair-trial rights.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether request or centrality alone changes the federal rule for potentially useful evidence.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received preservation rule rather than infer an exception from the desirability of additional testing.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: reject a categorical discovery-request exception across equivalent potentially useful evidence. Broader approval of every destruction policy would go beyond the case. Narrower reliance on Fisher’s fugitive status would leave the legal ground below unresolved and confuse preservation with punishment for absence.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A pending discovery request or the centrality of evidence does not by itself establish a due-process violation when the destroyed material was only potentially useful and bad faith is not shown. The defendant must satisfy Arizona v. Youngblood’s bad-faith requirement for that category; California v. Trombetta continues to govern evidence whose exculpatory character was apparent and which could not be comparably replaced. Fisher’s existing showing does not meet the applicable federal condition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Unknown-result testing material; the rule excludes destruction of apparently exculpatory evidence from this categorical treatment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A request may support an inference about knowledge and purpose, but courts must make that inference from evidence rather than replace the bad-faith element automatically.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Four inculpatory tests do not make a fifth test impossible to challenge, but they do not establish apparent exculpatory character either. Arizona v. Youngblood distinguishes those propositions. Kyles v. Whitley does not authorize presuming favorable undisclosed content where the actual issue is loss of an opportunity to conduct a test. A State remains free to impose an enforceable retention obligation beyond the federal floor.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Arizona v. Youngblood and California v. Trombetta applied without expansion. Kyles v. Whitley distinguished.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent Illinois sanctions and any inadequacy of the general destruction policy are not resolved on the federal grant. Existing state law governs those questions.

## 43. Groh v. Ramirez

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the reinstatement of the Fourth Amendment claim against Groh.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return for ordinary adjudication of the remaining individual damages issues; immunity is unavailable on the accepted facts. No damages sum or liability of other officers is determined.
* **Remedial Reach:** The plaintiffs and the reviewed individual defendant only; no proved ongoing agency policy is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must adjudicate disputed facts, causation, and compensable injury without treating the unincorporated application or magistrate’s signature as a cure for the established facial defect.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A warrant must supply the limitation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The warrant’s omission is undisputed. The asserted oral explanation is neither an accepted finding nor a substitute for judicial authorization.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the preparing and executing officer may search a home under a warrant that names nothing to be seized and invokes no incorporated list.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing particularity and notice principles; no general replacement of qualified-immunity doctrine is necessary to deny protection here.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: complete facial omission by the preparer presents an administrable boundary. Broader treatment of every clerical error would ignore different incorporation and reliance facts. A narrower rule confined to this form would understate the constitutional written-authorization requirement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A home-search warrant that identifies no things to be seized and incorporates no document identifying them fails the Fourth Amendment’s particularity requirement, and its preparer cannot claim objectively reasonable reliance on that obvious omission when executing it. The plaintiffs must establish the deficient search and the defendant’s participation; the ordinary causation and damages requirements remain. The officer’s private knowledge of the application does not supply a judicial limit omitted from the warrant.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The complete omission and the responsible preparing officer; other officers’ participation and materially different incorporation facts remain distinct.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must inspect the actual warrant and any valid incorporation before considering what an officer intended to seek.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wilson v. Layne confirms that a warrant’s scope limits execution rather than following the officer’s preferences. United States v. Leon had already identified facial deficiency as incompatible with reasonable reliance. Hope v. Pelzer explains the governing notice method but supplies no retroactive 1997 warning. The particularity text and preconduct authority provide the warning; restraint actually exercised cannot retrospectively enlarge authority.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Wilson v. Layne, United States v. Leon, and the notice doctrine are applied. Massachusetts v. Sheppard is distinguished by the preparing officer’s direct knowledge of the complete omission.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Valid cross-reference arrangements and unreviewed officers’ defenses require different facts. Until presented, existing warrant and individual-immunity law governs them.

The statutory findings and structure give age its operative sense. An agency construction cannot override that contextual reading.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins and O’Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp. preserved; their proof rules are not converted into answers to an unpresented coverage question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other ERISA, contract, state-law, and benefit-plan defenses are outside this coverage issue. Until resolved, their own sources govern.

## 45. Doe v. Chao

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand to restore Doe's $1,000 statutory minimum on the adjudicated violation, culpability and adverse effect. If no five-vote judgment for reversal on any ground exists, affirm under the actual-damages prerequisite without adopting a pecuniary-only definition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate Doe's minimum award and determine the costs and reasonable attorney's fees authorized by §552a(g)(4)(B). Leave the prospective order and judgments concerning nonpetitioning plaintiffs undisturbed. Under the fallback, deny the minimum on the accepted absence of qualifying actual damages.
* **Remedial Reach:** Doe's monetary claim and its statutory incidentals; no award to other claimants and no enlargement of the separate prospective order.
* **Practical Relief:** Doe receives the statutory floor for the established personal injury even without separately quantified actual damages. Additional compensation requires proof under the actual-damages clause. Under the fallback, the damages denial stands while the agency's prospective obligations continue.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A remedy must satisfy its statutory conditions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Court can decide the minimum-recovery question without equating privacy injury with abstraction or reopening the unchallenged liability predicates. Only Doe sought review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a claimant establishing an intentional or willful violation and a causally resulting adverse effect must additionally prove actual damages before receiving the statutory minimum.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt the minimum as an independently specified measure of recovery once the statutory liability predicates are proved. If no five-vote judgment for reversal on any ground exists, apply the actual-damages prerequisite to affirm while reserving the compensability and proof of nonpecuniary loss.
* **Reach Calibration:** Resolve the presented minimum-recovery issue fully. The proposed floor does not authorize damages for a harmless technical violation, presume injury in every disclosure case or define every form of actual damages. The fallback preserves the separate monetary prerequisite without diminishing the Act's personal-information protections.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under §552a(g)(1)(D) and (g)(4), a claimant who proves by a preponderance a statutory violation, an intentional or willful agency act and a genuine adverse effect caused by that act is entitled to the $1,000 minimum. The liability clause identifies the persons entitled to recovery; the actual-damages clause permits compensation for proved loss and the minimum supplies an express floor. A separately established dollar amount of actual damages is not an additional condition of that floor. Doe's uncontested distress and the adjudicated violation and culpability satisfy the premises accepted on this review. This holding does not award the minimum on a bare violation or establish entitlement for the other plaintiffs.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The minimum under the presented Privacy Act damages provision. Correction, access and independently authorized equitable remedies retain their own statutory limits.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Decide violation, intentional or willful conduct, causation and genuine adverse effect separately. Once those predicates are established, award the minimum without demanding proof of an additional amount of actual damages. A claimant seeking more must prove entitlement under the actual-damages clause. Under the fallback, some qualifying actual damages must first be established; this case supplies no rule excluding adequately proved nonpecuniary injury.
* **Transition:** Apply the construction to Doe's pending claim and other claims governed by ordinary rules for statutory decisions. No closed judgment or nonpetitioning claimant's loss is reopened.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation separates a personal information invasion from final monetary entitlement and reserves this question. The statute expressly makes the United States liable when the stated culpability and injury conditions are satisfied, then specifies damages and a minimum for a person entitled to recovery. Reading that last phrase as an unstated requirement to prove some additional actual damages makes the floor depend on a condition Congress could have named directly. The contrary reading draws support from the placement of the minimum after actual damages and Congress's deletion of general-damages language. But a fixed, expressly enacted minimum is different from unlimited presumed general damages. Sovereign immunity requires the enacted monetary limits to be respected; it does not justify withholding the floor within an express waiver. This interpretation creates neither a new cause of action nor a judicially selected sum. The district court accepted Doe's uncontroverted evidence of distress and awarded the statutory amount; the appellate reversal turned on an additional actual-damages prerequisite. Restore that award without presuming harm for plaintiffs whose proof failed.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation at its adverse-effect scope and resolve its minimum-recovery reservation. Neither the proposed construction nor the fallback treats that decision as having already awarded statutory damages.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The full definition of actual damages, the proof necessary for compensable emotional harm and any award above the minimum remain outside the granted question. Costs and reasonable fees require determination under their statutory provision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Confrontation before admission of testimonial accusations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Both admissibility and reconsideration of the Roberts framework are within the granted questions. The recording's custodial investigative setting, substantive use and absence of prior cross-examination permit decision of the testimonial core without defining every out-of-court statement.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a testimonial accusation deliberately elicited in police interrogation may be admitted for its truth against an accused without producing the witness or establishing both unavailability and a prior opportunity for cross-examination.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Establish confrontation as the required procedure for testimonial accusations and displace reliability-based admission within that category. If the Court declines this change, apply Wright and Lilly to reject the State's existing-law rationale. Preserve existing protection outside the category rather than confine the entire Clause to formal testimony.
* **Reach Calibration:** Adopt a categorical protection within the question's established testimonial core. A narrow rejection of corroboration would leave judges free to substitute other reliability assessments for confrontation of the same investigative testimony. A declaration that all other hearsay is outside the Constitution would unnecessarily contract White's protection.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When the prosecution offers a testimonial accusation for its truth, it must produce the declarant for constitutionally adequate confrontation or establish both the declarant's unavailability and a prior opportunity for cross-examination. A recorded narrative deliberately elicited through custodial police questioning about a completed alleged crime is testimonial: it supplies an account assembled for use in investigating and prosecuting the offense. A hearsay label, asserted intrinsic trustworthiness or agreement with other evidence cannot replace the required confrontation. Sylvia's recorded accusation falls within this category, and Crawford never had the opportunity to cross-examine her. Marital privilege does not itself forfeit the accused's confrontation right. Its substantive admission on this record violated that right.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Testimonial accusations offered for their truth, including the established custodial-interrogation setting. The holding supplies a constitutional minimum for that category, not a new declaration that all remaining statements lack confrontation protection.

A prosecution cannot cure missing confrontation merely by proving the statement accurate. Where the testimonial classification is not resolved by this holding, existing exception-sensitive law continues to govern unless a further holding changes it. Hearsay restrictions remain independently applicable.
* **Transition:** Apply the rule on this direct review and under ordinary direct-review principles. Collateral retroactivity is not presented or decided.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Clause protects the accused's opportunity to test the witnesses supplying the prosecution's evidence. For an accusation assembled as testimony, judicial confidence in accuracy is not the procedure the Constitution prescribes. Mattox permits previously cross-examined testimony upon the witness's unavailability; it does not equate untested investigative testimony with testimony subjected to adversarial questioning. Roberts allows the same constitutional prerequisite to turn on variable assessments of trustworthiness. Lilly and Wright demonstrate the problem here: labeling an accusation against interest and matching parts of it to the defendant's account do not test the accusation about the critical encounter. The proposed rule replaces that discretionary admission route for testimonial accusations while retaining White's refusal to make formal affidavits the entire reach of confrontation. Under the fallback, the State must establish particularized guarantees arising from the statement-making circumstances themselves. External corroboration and interlocking accounts cannot supply them, and the State's penal-interest label does not establish a firmly rooted exception for this accusation.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Overrule Ohio v. Roberts insofar as it permits testimonial accusations to be admitted through a hearsay category or judicial reliability assessment without the required confrontation. Qualify White's preservation of that framework to the same extent for testimonial statements offered for their truth. Preserve its exception-specific protection outside that category and its rejection of a formal-affidavit-only constitutional limit; a spontaneous-declaration or medical-treatment label does not exempt a statement that otherwise meets the new testimonial rule. Lilly and Wright remain independently sufficient to reject the admission rationale under the fallback. Mattox supports the required prior testing of unavailable testimonial evidence. Coy and Craig retain their distinct rules for constitutionally adequate trial-witness presentation; Chapman governs the available direct-review harmlessness inquiry.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The outer testimonial boundary, informal statements, any historically grounded dying-declaration exception and a properly established forfeiture claim are not decided. No such alternative supports this admission. Harmlessness requires the State's actual-record showing below; the error is not declared automatically structural.
