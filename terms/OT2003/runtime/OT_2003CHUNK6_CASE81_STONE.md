## 81. Tennessee v. Lane

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of Tennessee’s immunity motion for the court-access claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for statutory coverage, accommodation, causation and remedy findings under Title II; preserve defenses that do not recreate the rejected categorical immunity bar.
* **Remedial Reach:** The plaintiffs’ live claims. If a challenged access policy or practice is proved unlawful, relief must correct that policy for those it governs unless Tennessee demonstrates that narrower relief completely cures the violation.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify each inaccessible service, available effective accommodation and current risk, then set enforceable access measures and deadlines for any proved violation. Damages require the applicable independent prerequisites and proof; immunity denial alone establishes neither liability nor a sum.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Section 5 enforcement of equal court access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress validly authorized private Title II ADA enforcement against a State for disability-based denial of access to its courts.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Popovich's settled court-participation principle to physical access to judicial services and state the supported scope of that application.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Court access is a coherent constitutional and statutory category. All public services would exceed the presented evidence. Limiting relief to independently proven constitutional torts would discard Congress’s legitimate preventive function.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may authorize private Title II enforcement against States for disability-based exclusion from judicial services because reasonable, nonfundamentally altering access duties congruently prevent and remedy constitutional denials of access to courts. The plaintiff must prove the statutory violation and applicable remedy requirements; the State must establish a properly supported fundamental-alteration defense. Sovereign immunity does not bar these pleaded court-access claims.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Title II obligations concerning participation in judicial services; the rule does not automatically validate every application to every public program or decide Jones’s individual statutory coverage.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides another public-service application, courts apply the §5 framework to the identified right, evidence and statutory fit. Individual claims here proceed under Title II’s elements and defenses.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Popovich already rejects categorical immunity for bounded Title II court-access duties: reasonable accommodation can prevent denial of a meaningful hearing without requiring a separately proved constitutional tort in every claim. Physical barriers to judicial participation present the same enforcement concern. Garrett's fit analysis concerned Title I's general employment requirements and left room for fitted prevention, while Boerne and the reasonable-modification, fundamental-alteration and undue-burden limits constrain its fit. Neither the authority to sue nor this category-level conclusion establishes Lane's or Jones's individual statutory elements or damages.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas to physical judicial access, together with Boerne, distinguishing Garrett's Title I employment holding. Distinguish Olmstead's separate integration question. No heightened disability classification rule is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other public-service applications lie outside the question; individual liability, Jones’s coverage and damages require facts and issues not resolved at this stage.
