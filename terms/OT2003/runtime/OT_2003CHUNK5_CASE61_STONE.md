## 61. California ex rel. Lockyer v. FERC

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the presented procedural challenge on the actual notice and completed reconsideration record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the approvals in force on the issues reviewed; require no additional evidentiary hearing where no material factual dispute demanding one is identified. Do not adjudicate independent creditor, fraudulent-transfer, or bankruptcy claims.
* **Remedial Reach:** These approvals and these procedural objections; no general exemption from notice or hearing requirements for urgent energy transactions.
* **Practical Relief:** FERC’s approvals remain operative. The objectors may pursue independent claims in their proper forum, but this judgment supplies no second administrative hearing merely to repeat fully considered legal and policy objections.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A complete meaningful process, not a formal cure label

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The notices, submissions, and reconsideration orders permit review of what the agency actually considered. No record finding establishes an excluded material evidentiary dispute or an irretrievable procedural injury requiring a new hearing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this expedited procedure and subsequent full consideration met the actual statutory and constitutional hearing requirements.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply functional notice and meaningful-opportunity principles to the record; reject a universal cure rule while sustaining this result.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record: adequacy depends on the content of the notices, nature of the objections, urgency, and genuine reconsideration. A broader rule that any rehearing cures defective notice would permit irreversible evasion of participation. A narrower automatic invalidation based only on the number of days would ignore the actual issues and complete process.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An abbreviated FERC approval process does not require invalidation solely because no trial-type hearing occurred when notice identifies the essential transaction, the objectors receive meaningful consideration of all material submissions, and no material factual dispute or irretrievable procedural prejudice requires additional process. The agency must identify its actual consideration and reasons; an objector claiming prejudicial inadequacy must identify the lost opportunity or material issue, not only disagreement with the result. This administrative record sustains the approvals on that basis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The identified transaction approvals and record-supported procedural treatment; no fixed minimum comment period or general postapproval cure is established.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Test whether later consideration was real and capable of meaningful relief. Agency assertions of urgency or unchanged outcome do not independently prove adequate process.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews v. Eldridge directs attention to actual interests, error risk, useful safeguards and government needs, rather than treating every hearing as a trial. National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. requires the agency’s actual ground, so the reviewing court cannot manufacture responsive consideration. Arkansas v. Oklahoma preserves the difference between supported findings and a competing policy preference. Here the notices described the ownership changes, and the agency addressed the substantive creditor and public-interest objections in its reconsideration orders. Closure alone does not show that these objections became legally unreviewable or that a material fact hearing would change the procedure needed.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Mathews v. Eldridge applied; National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. and Arkansas v. Oklahoma applied to actual agency reasoning and record review. No categorical rehearing-cure precedent is created.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent creditor and bankruptcy consequences are outside this administrative procedural grant. Until adjudicated in their proper forum, those claims retain their own statutory and factual requirements.
