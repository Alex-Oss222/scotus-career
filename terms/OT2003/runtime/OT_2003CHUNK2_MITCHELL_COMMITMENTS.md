## Component positions shared by all eight

C1. Reject the categorical proposition that this indictment/eligibility omission is necessarily immune from harmless-error review because the sentence is capital. The statutory gateway requires a contrary governing rule or objectively unreasonable application of this Court's law at the relevant state adjudication, not merely a later court's disagreement or a later rule. Sullivan's invalid reasonable-doubt instruction infected the standard for every finding; it does not automatically classify omission of one eligibility issue as the same defect. Pre-divergence Chapman, Rose, Carella, Fulminante and Satterwhite establish meaningful error review, including in capital proceedings. The absence of a fact-identical case does not exclude a genuinely governing general rule.

C2. Separate Ohio's statutory specification requirement, due-process notice, Eighth Amendment narrowing and the later Sixth Amendment eligibility-allocation rule. The principal-offender alternative requires the actual-killer culpability specified by Ohio law; prior calculation and design is a different statutory alternative and cannot be substituted without its own valid basis. The State's construction that a sole-defendant indictment implicitly alleges principal-offender status, reinforced by its death-specification cover language, is relevant to actual notice and state-law sufficiency. It does not alone establish a jury finding or resolve the federal narrowing/actual-verdict inquiry. Presnell prevents sustaining death on a materially unsubmitted aggravating basis; Lowenfield permits constitutionally adequate narrowing at the guilt stage and does not require a duplicative label where the necessary determination was actually made.

C3. Reverse the Sixth Circuit's legal ruling that harmless review is categorically unavailable. Do not affirm a conditional sentence writ merely on that categorical ground. The absence of that categorical bar does not itself establish that the particular state disposition satisfies §2254, that any available constitutional error was harmless, or that the death sentence may be implemented. Those remain distinct legal inquiries with distinct supported records and dates.

C4. The convictions and already rejected guilt-phase claims remain outside this sentence-review grant. The district court's other sentence grounds and the preserved alternatives—including sentencing assistance, denial of sentencing-preparation continuance and cumulative sentencing error—must receive their own adjudication. Rejection of one ground cannot erase every independent basis for the existing sentence writ.

## Competing actual-application positions

### A. Foreclose the reviewed omission theory under the statutory gateway

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the sentence-writ affirmance insofar as it rests on this omission and remand for the independent sentence grounds. They join C1–C4 and A1–A3 below.

A1. On the state adjudication and trial account supplied, the state result is not contrary to or an objectively unreasonable application of the contemporaneous federal law. The jury was instructed on purposefully causing death while committing aggravated robbery and separately on the beyond-reasonable-doubt robbery specification; the prosecution's trial theory identified one robber/shooter. The indictment's death-specification cover text supplied notice of capital exposure, and the state's sole-defendant construction did not create an entirely different offense after trial. Those circumstances permitted the state court to regard the omitted principal-offender words as implicit in the adjudication rather than an absence of every narrowing determination.

A2. The state court's reference to manifest weight/sufficiency is not the ideal expression of actual-verdict noncontribution, and sufficient evidence alone is not a complete constitutional harmlessness test. Whole-adjudication review nevertheless considers the state principal-offender construction, actual trial theory and charge together. The reasonable state application survives; these Justices do not independently find what a new jury would decide. Neither the single-defendant fact alone nor overwhelming confession evidence alone would suffice for every case.

A3. Later federal evidence suggesting a second participant and withheld inconsistent descriptions must be assessed through its own legally available claim and factual-development rules; it cannot silently rewrite the historical state adjudication record. These Justices do not reject a properly available sentencing-disclosure or other independent ground. They reject using the later evidence, without those intervening legal determinations, to preserve the categorical omission-based writ. Their judgment conclusively rejects the reviewed omission theory under §2254 on the supplied state record while preserving independent sentence relief and any legitimately distinct later-evidence theory.

The strongest objection is that actual principal-offender identity remained unasked, and the state applied sufficiency instead of noncontribution; the possible second participant sharpens the difference between participation and actual killing. These four answer that the gate concerns objective reasonableness under the earlier law and the actual single-offender trial adjudication, not direct application of later Ring. They accept the supplied charge's purposeful-killing finding as adequate support for the state result within that range, without a new appellate witness-credibility finding.

### B. Vacate the categorical affirmance but retain the actual gateway/application inquiry on remand

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the affirmance of sentence relief and remand for application of C1–C4 and B1–B3 below; they refuse A1's definitive foreclosure.

B1. The lower court decided the case primarily on a categorical structural premise, while the record contains a material distinction between the 1992 notice construction and the 1994 manifest-weight/sufficiency response. Proper §2254 review must identify the actual federal component adjudicated, its date and contemporaneous governing holdings, then assess the full state rule, reasons and record. Presnell and Lowenfield supply real preexisting constraints; the categorical argument's failure does not settle whether the state supplied a different aggravator or substituted hypothetical guilt for a necessary actual determination. These four do not grant relief now or assume the statutory gate is satisfied.

B2. If a constitutional violation is legally available after the separate gateway and nonretroactivity inquiries, the governing simulated Chapman/qualified actual-verdict law requires the State to establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt. The court must examine the charge, actual required findings, evidence directed to the jury and material conflicts. Principal-offender status is not automatically established by sole indictment or conviction of the murder charge. The specific charge quoted by the dissent strongly supports Ohio, but it must be evaluated with the full supported instructions and adjudication rather than treated as a conclusive new finding here. The sufficiency of a hypothetical properly instructed conviction does not substitute for this inquiry.

B3. The federal discovery suggesting a second participant and the withheld descriptions are materially different from the trial's single-offender account. The lower court must determine the evidence's lawful role under the applicable review, factual-development and claim rules before using it; it cannot pretend the state court had it or erase it solely because the guilt-phase Brady claim failed. Participation sufficient for the underlying conviction is not necessarily the actual-killer alternative of capital eligibility. If a legally available error fails the State's proper harmlessness showing, conditional sentence relief follows with a reasonable period for a lawful penalty disposition; if the reviewed theory fails its gate or merits showing, it cannot support the writ, and independently remaining sentence grounds must still be resolved.

The strongest objection to remand is that the state record permits a definitive reasonable-application determination and the four favoring A have made it. These four find the state reasoning's conflation of sufficiency and actual adjudication, together with the distinction between state and newly developed federal evidence, material enough that the categorical appellate rationale cannot be replaced with final foreclosure without the proper inquiry. A source limitation does not itself create the merits doubt: the quoted state reasoning and identified federal evidence do. Remand uses the already developed lawful record and does not command a new evidentiary hearing.
