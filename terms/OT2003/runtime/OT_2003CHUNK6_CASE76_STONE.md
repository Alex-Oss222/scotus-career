# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit on the instructional habeas claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate denial of relief on this ground, leaving any independently preserved claim to its own adjudication.
* **Remedial Reach:** McNeil’s instructional claim; no approval of using the mistaken language in future trials.
* **Practical Relief:** The federal court must apply §2254 and deny this ground rather than order a new trial based solely on its preferred reading of the disputed phrase.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Contextual instructional review under §2254

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied clearly established federal law when it assessed a mistaken phrase in an imperfect-self-defense instruction in the context of the full trial charge.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering objective habeas standard and whole-charge instruction rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record. The contextual question turns on what the jury heard. A categorical rule that repetition cures every instruction would be unsound. Treating the single phrase as dispositive would omit the governing contextual inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A mistaken phrase in a jury instruction warrants §2254 relief only when the complete charge and trial context make the state court’s rejection of the asserted constitutional defect objectively unreasonable under clearly established law. The prisoner bears that showing. The repeated distinction between honest and reasonable fear here permits the state court’s conclusion, so this claim does not support a writ.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This habeas challenge to the overall instructional treatment of imperfect self-defense; trial courts remain obliged to give accurate instructions.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Williams keeps constitutional error distinct from objectively unreasonable state adjudication. Boyde and Estelle require the entire charge, so the erroneous qualifier cannot be treated as the sole instruction. The surrounding explanations preserved the relevant distinction sufficiently to sustain the state court under that demanding review standard.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Williams v. Taylor, Boyde v. California and Estelle v. McGuire are applied without creating a categorical cure rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
