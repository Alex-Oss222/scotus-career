## III. Actual Vieth applies to the three partisan branches

### The complete rule and its limits

The current law is a direct five-Justice majority, not a narrowest-ground inference. The claimant must identify an existing or demonstrably attainable practical opportunity for the affected voters or association to organize, participate, nominate or elect; show a substantial practical obstruction produced by an identified districting operation; establish a substantially motivating purpose to suppress that opposition's effective opportunity because of its association or participation and thereby insulate governing power; and prove material boundary-specific causation. Party identity, a preferred seat allocation, ordinary inconvenience, a difficult election, partisan awareness or ordinary political advantage does not substitute for any element. Complete extinction, exclusion from every political activity and inevitable electoral defeat are not necessary.

The claimant bears preponderance on all four elements through an independently justified, legally and practically feasible comparison. Different evidence methods are permitted. A replacement map is not universally necessary, but a comparison that depends on a different configuration must establish that configuration's feasibility, including effects needed to make it work. Once the plaintiff carries that burden, the State must prove by a preponderance that materially the same lawful decision would have occurred absent the forbidden purpose. Actual lawful constraints or a genuinely applied independently justified fairness policy can support that defense; a conceivable explanation or another political objective cannot. No further balancing excuses a proved violation after the defense fails.

Neither the lower court's statutory political-cohesion gate nor a new mandatory-map rule can be inserted into that constitutional cause. Conversely, the new rule does not dispense with comparison, personal standing, practical injury or causation. ## II. The proposal's actual doctrinal change

The proposed right concerns control of an elected chamber by an already existing party or coalition despite reliably demonstrated opposition majorities across recorded conditions, caused materially and avoidably by the assignments. It is narrower than a general proportional-representation entitlement: it uses an absolute majority, a real governing body, persistence beyond an isolated condition, personal causal exposure, and an independently justified comparison. Its exclusion of congressional delegations is meaningful. The proposal should not be rejected by simply pretending those limits are absent or by calling every majority-control claim a demand for an exact proportional seat share.

Those limits nevertheless do not make this an application of existing Vieth. The new formulation would establish a separate constitutional entitlement to avoid the defined retained-control result without a prohibited-purpose showing. Its defense asks whether consistently applied lawful constraints made that result unavoidable. Vieth instead requires a defined purposeful suppression of actual personal opportunity, followed by a State showing that materially the same lawful decision would have occurred without the forbidden purpose. A plan can be one permissible, independently justified choice among alternatives without its partisan-control consequence being unavoidable. The two defenses therefore have different reach.

Vieth's unanimous Article I rejection does not itself decide the proposed distinct House equal-protection theory. Nor does the Court's adoption of a purposeful opportunity cause necessarily hold that no other independently established constitutional right can ever operate without purpose. Population equality is a familiar example of a different right with different elements. But recognizing a new governing-control entitlement requires an additional constitutional judgment; the existence of population equality, §2 results liability or a valid statutory effects regulation does not perform that work. The proposed cause cannot acquire its missing authority merely by borrowing their absence of an intent requirement.

The comparison requirement helps separate assignment effects from geography, turnout, candidates and lawful requirements. It does not by itself establish why a court may require one lawful control result over another. Likewise, standing, reliable measurement and repeated effects identify important factual limits; they do not independently create the asserted substantive right. The audit preserves this distinction rather than treating additional proof detail as a substitute for adopting the new constitutional principle.

### Measurement clauses at their actual scope

The absolute-majority requirement is more than half, not merely the largest share. A coherent coalition must be independently established; unrelated votes cannot be combined simply because their aggregation exceeds half. The whole-membership requirement for a staggered body is material, though no staggered-body finding is supplied here. Disclosed and tested treatment of uncontested races is essential because candidate votes are not automatically a reliable measure of all voters' preferred governing coalition. Recorded conditions beyond an isolated point address persistence, but their representativeness and actual limits still require adjudication. No fixed election count is added by this audit.

The proposal does not require a completed election under the challenged map, and the four Vieth majority Associates already accept that actual participation injury need not await electoral defeat. That existing proposition does not establish that old elections or another map automatically prove this new chamber-control injury. Applying recorded votes to a comparison requires a supported inference about candidate choice, turnout, coalition coherence and the scope of the changed assignments; the source must establish the inference rather than assume fixed behavior. No such evidence is fabricated here.

These are recognizable proof disciplines and do not themselves supply additional-rule joins. A Justice can agree that any proposed theory would require such care while declining the theory's constitutional premise, its defense or its adoption in this case.
