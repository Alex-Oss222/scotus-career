## 75. Johnson v. California

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case to the state appellate process without a federal merits mandate.
* **Remedial Reach:** This premature attempt at Supreme Court review only; no declaration upholding the challenged Batson rule.
* **Practical Relief:** The California courts must complete the unresolved appeal under their procedures. Johnson may seek review after an adverse final state judgment through the applicable filing process; this dismissal neither restarts nor extends those eventual deadlines.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Final state judgment and preserved federal review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The record shows unresolved substantive grounds and an available path to later review. Stone reaches only mandatory jurisdiction and cannot adjudicate the jury-selection merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Court has jurisdiction to review a state Batson ruling while other grounds for reversal of the conviction remain unresolved on remand; the underlying merits concern the first-stage showing of discriminatory peremptory challenges.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Florida v. Thomas and the existing Cox framework.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The statutory finality defect fully controls this proceeding. A general restriction on all interlocutory review would erase the preserved exceptions. A discretionary dismissal without the finality rule would obscure why the Court lacks power.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state judgment rejecting one federal ground for reversal is not final under §1257 when independent appellate claims remain unresolved and the defendant can obtain federal review after an adverse final disposition, absent a demonstrated Cox exception. The party invoking jurisdiction must establish such finality or an exception; Johnson has not done so, so the case must be dismissed.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Substantive state remands with available later federal review; established Cox exceptions remain available on their own facts.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court lawfully decides the merits after a final judgment or established exception, courts apply existing Batson law; this jurisdictional dismissal endorses no additional first-stage burden.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida v. Thomas rejected immediate review of a federal issue embedded in unfinished state proceedings. Johnson’s unresolved claims can change the conviction’s disposition, and an adverse final judgment would permit renewed review. The importance of nondiscriminatory jury selection supplies no special finality exception common to every rejected Batson claim.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Florida v. Thomas is applied; Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn is reaffirmed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Batson merits cannot be reached without statutory finality; no jurisdiction exists to resolve them here.
