## 80. Till v. SCS Credit Corp.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for a prime-plus-risk determination under the statutory present-value and feasibility requirements.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a reasoned rate finding; reinstate the proposed 9.5 percent only if the record supports the risk adjustment and feasibility.
* **Remedial Reach:** The Tills’ proposed treatment of SCS’s allowed secured claim. No cancellation of the secured principal or automatic approval of every formula plan.
* **Practical Relief:** The bankruptcy court must identify the prime benchmark at confirmation, resolve evidence concerning term, collateral and repayment risk, and enter findings before confirming the payment stream. If the plan cannot meet the lawful rate and feasibility requirements, confirmation must be denied or the plan amended.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Present-value interest under Chapter 13

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** How a Chapter 13 plan must set the interest rate on deferred payments that preserve the present value of a secured creditor’s allowed claim over its objection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve an open methodology question with a transparent statutory formula.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A reusable method is necessary for Chapter 13 administration. A fixed national premium would disregard proved differences. A case-only rate with no allocation of burdens would reproduce the dispute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Chapter 13 cramdown rate begins with the national prime rate and adds the risk adjustment supported by the proposed payment stream, collateral and debtor’s demonstrated circumstances. The debtor bears the ultimate confirmation and feasibility burden; a creditor demanding a higher adjustment must substantiate the additional risk by a preponderance of the evidence. The contract rate is evidence, not a presumption or automatic entitlement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Deferred payment of an allowed secured claim under §1325(a)(5)(B)(ii); statutory protections for other claims remain separate.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rash values the retained collateral but leaves compensation for deferred payment unanswered. Prime supplies an observable baseline; a supported adjustment connects compensation to the actual risk imposed. The contract embeds origination costs and negotiated profit that the present-value command does not automatically preserve. A debtor cannot shift the ultimate feasibility burden merely because the creditor must substantiate a requested premium.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash is applied to the valuation prerequisite; Nobelman v. American Savings Bank is distinguished by statutory protection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
