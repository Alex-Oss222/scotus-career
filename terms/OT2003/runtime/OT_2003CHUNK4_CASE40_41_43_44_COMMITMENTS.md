The prosecution retains its beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden on the enacted commerce element. The evenly divided en banc court supplied an appealable judgment but no majority rationale entitled to precedential treatment.

## 44. General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline — February 24, 2004

**Disposition commitments:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the statutory-coverage ruling and restore dismissal of the ADEA claim. Scalia and Thomas affirm recognition of coverage and would remand for the unresolved defenses and ordinary liability questions. All agree that the cutoff expressly uses age; the disagreement is which direction of disadvantage the statutory prohibition covers.

**Competing legal paths:** The employees' path reads section 623(a)(1)'s reference to any individual and treatment because of that individual's age with section 631(a)'s express forty-year boundary. The policy uses exactly that criterion against persons Congress placed inside the protected class. The EEOC's 2003 section 1625.2(a) interprets the Act to prohibit preference either way among protected persons, while subsection (b) separately allows additional older-worker benefits when the employer reasonably concludes they counteract age discrimination and forbids using them to accomplish otherwise prohibited practices. The employer's path reads the operative prohibition in the context of section 621's disadvantage to older workers and its purpose to promote employment of older persons; membership in the forty-and-older group identifies eligible claimants without transforming a protection against advancing-age disadvantage into a symmetrical ban on older-worker preferences.

**Strongest objections:** A purely purposive reading could erase unqualified operative language and allow an employer to invoke beneficial motives against an enacted command. Conversely, a purely symmetrical reading could ignore what discrimination because of age signifies in this statute's express setting, treating claimant eligibility as the complete definition of the wrong. Hazen Paper answers actual motivation, not this directional coverage question. O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers rejects a protected-group replacement requirement and recognizes substantially younger replacement evidence; it does not directly resolve preferences for the older group. Neither side may convert those holdings into an already-decided answer.
