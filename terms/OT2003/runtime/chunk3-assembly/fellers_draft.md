## Controlling holdings

**1. The home discussion violated the attached right to counsel.** Once prosecution has begun, officers may not deliberately elicit incriminating statements about the charged crime without counsel or a valid waiver of the Sixth Amendment right. The officers' directed discussion of Fellers's indicted methamphetamine conspiracy deliberately elicited his admissions; the absence of custodial interrogation under Miranda does not answer that distinct constitutional question.

**Authority:** This portion of {{AUTHOR}}'s opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; the rule, application and correction of the lower court's Fifth-Amendment-only analysis have nine direct votes.

Massiah and Brewer protect the accused against deliberate elicitation after the right attaches. The officers came to discuss the charged distribution and named alleged associates, inviting the incriminating response they obtained. Permission to enter the home did not waive the attached right. Patterson permits a genuinely valid waiver, but no valid home waiver is established. Cobb preserves the independent Sixth Amendment inquiry; this conversation concerned the indicted conspiracy itself, so its broader inseparable-occurrence boundary does no additional work. The lower court's interrogation label therefore cannot sustain its treatment of the home encounter.

**2. Later warnings and waiver do not alone establish causal independence.** After the defendant establishes deliberate elicitation violating the attached right and a causal connection to a later statement, the Government must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the later statement resulted from a sufficiently independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of the violation. The court considers timing and continuity, genuine intervening circumstances, the purpose and character of the unlawful elicitation, actual use of the first account, and later warnings and a knowing, voluntary waiver; the Government must show independence is more likely than not.

**Authority:** {{AUTHOR}}'s causal-independence rule, proof standard and defined remand have seven direct supporters. That coalition consists of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas join the judgment without this rationale.

Massiah protects the represented criminal process independently of compelled speech, and Wade recognizes derivative consequences of a Sixth Amendment violation. Brown separates voluntary speech from exploitation of an actual constitutional wrong; Kaupp applies that distinction to unlawful arrest. The Court extends the causal principle to deliberate elicitation. Patterson gives later waiver real force, but it does not make that waiver an answer to derivation. Elstad addresses a voluntary statement following an earlier warning omission, and Dickerson preserves that setting. A waiver-only extension would erase the distinct consequences of the completed violation here. The rule instead permits genuinely independent later speech while requiring proof that it was not obtained by exploitation.

Nix, Matlock and Lego support the ordinary preponderance standard for admissibility facts. Wade's clear-and-convincing standard addresses the special reconstruction difficulty when an uncounseled lineup fixes an identification in memory. The present inquiry examines the actual interview sequence, conduct, warnings and intervening events, so that distinctive identification concern does not require a heightened quantum here. An evenly balanced showing fails the Government's burden.

The short sequence, same officers and repetition of the home account require the separate causal inquiry; the lower courts must determine independence on the relevant record. The home statements remain excluded. If the Government fails to prove independence, the jail statements must also be excluded. A properly preserved harmless-error contention requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt that their admission did not contribute to the actual verdict; otherwise the conviction must be vacated and a new trial may proceed with independently admissible evidence. These are factual and remedial determinations under the announced rule, which governs this case and similarly situated pending direct-review cases.

## Public Projection

## Event

**Fellers v. United States, No. 02-6320.** Argued December 10, 2003; decided January 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. The questions are whether officers deliberately elicited statements at Fellers's home about an indicted charge without counsel or waiver, and whether the later warned jail statements require a Sixth Amendment derivative-evidence inquiry.

The same officers discussed the charged methamphetamine conspiracy at the home, then obtained warnings, a signed waiver and further admissions at the jail, including repetition of the earlier account. The district court excluded the home statements but admitted the jail statements, and the Court of Appeals affirmed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The admission-based affirmance is set aside. The original exclusion of the home statements remains in force. The lower courts must apply the causal-independence rule and the conditional exclusion, harmlessness and new-trial directions stated in Holding 2. Scalia and Thomas agree with reversal and remand on the erroneous Sixth Amendment framework while withholding the new causal rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Massiah v. United States and Brewer v. Williams:** Applied to directed elicitation concerning the indicted conspiracy without counsel or waiver.
- **Texas v. Cobb:** Applied for the distinct attached-right inquiry; its inseparable-occurrence extension is unnecessary for questioning about the indicted charge itself.
- **Patterson v. Illinois:** Preserved for genuinely valid later waiver, which remains distinct from exploitation of an earlier violation.
- **Oregon v. Elstad and Dickerson v. United States:** Distinguished at the voluntary unwarned-statement setting; later warnings alone do not answer fruits of this completed Sixth Amendment violation.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Its distinction between voluntariness and causal attenuation is extended to deliberate elicitation after attachment.
- **Kaupp v. Texas:** Its unlawful-arrest application supports the causal distinction without itself deciding this Sixth Amendment application.
- **United States v. Wade:** Applied for derivative consequences of a Sixth Amendment violation; its heightened identification-proof rule is distinguished by the reconstruction problem of an uncounseled lineup.
- **Nix v. Williams, United States v. Matlock and Lego v. Twomey:** Applied for preponderance as the ordinary suppression-fact standard; Nix's inevitable-discovery setting is distinct from the actual-statement inquiry here.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applied to any properly preserved claim that erroneous admission was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt on the actual trial record.

## Law After Decision

Causally connected later statements following deliberate elicitation in violation of the attached right require proof of genuine independence, even after valid warnings and waiver. The Government bears preponderance on that admissibility question; any properly preserved constitutional harmlessness claim carries the separate beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. The legal rule governs pending direct-review cases, while lower courts determine the actual causal sequence and verdict consequence under those standards.
