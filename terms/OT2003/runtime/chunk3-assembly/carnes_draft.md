## Controlling holdings

**Congress may protect covered calls carried by an interstate telephone network.** Congress may prohibit unauthorized interception of covered telephone communications carried through an interstate communications system even when the particular call begins and ends in one State. Direct interference with that instrumentality supplies the constitutional connection; the prosecution must prove the statutory interception and network-related predicates beyond a reasonable doubt, but need not prove an additional interstate endpoint or a substantial individual economic effect.

**Authority:** {{AUTHOR}}'s opinion, joined by {{OTHER_JOINERS}}, has all nine Justices' direct support for the instrumentality rule, its application and the limited disposition.

Lopez preserves Congress's power to protect actual channels and instrumentalities of interstate commerce. Harris applies that power to a direct intrusion upon an identified transportation instrumentality, and Guillen confirms that a confined protective mechanism need not be recast as a substantial-effects case. Here the conduct is interception of communications actually carried by the telephone system. Local endpoints do not sever that direct relationship, and physical damage to the network is unnecessary to its protection.

Holston distinguishes a statutory reference to equipment's past interstate movement from an adequate constitutional connection. Carnes's case rests on the communication's actual use of the network, not the recorder's remote origin. Home Builders supplies additional means for different regulated classes; those routes do not displace the direct-instrumentality ground. The statutory definition, intentional-interception requirement, exceptions and criminal proof remain independently operative. The local-call objection therefore supplies no constitutional basis to dismiss the reviewed wiretap count.

The independent suppression reversal, new-trial direction on the wiretap count and associated sentencing mandate remain effective. The Court's rejection of the commerce objection does not reinstate the reversed conviction.

## Event

**United States v. Carnes.** Decided February 9, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the Sixth Circuit's judgment, No. 00-2103, 309 F.3d 950. The granted question is whether federal power to protect interstate instrumentalities permits application of 18 U.S.C. §2511 to unauthorized interception of telephone calls whose endpoints are within one State.

Recordings were obtained through a wiretap installed on telephone lines at Carnes's girlfriend's residence. The Court of Appeals rejected the commerce objection but independently reversed the wiretap conviction on suppression grounds and directed a new trial.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied for protection of actual interstate instrumentalities and identification of a real constitutional connection.
- **United States v. Harris:** Applied for direct protection of an identified instrumentality even against intrastate conduct.
- **Pierce County v. Guillen:** Applied for a fitted protective mechanism without requiring a substantial-effects showing for each instance.
- **United States v. Holston:** Applied to distinguish remote equipment provenance from an actual constitutional mechanism.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Distinguished as identifying additional routes for different regulated classes; those routes are not exclusive of instrumentality protection.

## Law After Decision

Federal protection of covered telephone communications extends to local calls actually carried through an interstate telephone system. The enacted interception and network predicates remain subject to the criminal burden. The direct-instrumentality ground defeats the local-endpoints objection while the independent suppression and retrial mandate governs further proceedings in Carnes's case.
