## Controlling holdings

**Authority for both holdings:** {{AUTHOR}}'s opinion for the unanimous Court, joined by {{OTHER_JOINERS}}. All nine Justices directly adopt both constitutional grounds, the statutory qualifications stated here and the limited disposition.

**1. The federal commencement rule is a valid means of regulating commercial hazardous-waste activity.** Congress may displace an earlier state limitations commencement date for covered hazardous-substance exposure claims when that displacement is reasonably adapted to preserving remedies for latent harm within its valid regulation of commercial hazardous-waste generation, handling, disposal and response. Section 9658 satisfies that requirement because premature commencement can defeat exposure-based liability before causal discovery and thereby impair the cost allocation and preventive and remedial incentives of the regulated waste activity.

Lopez requires a real constitutional connection before the magnitude of downstream consequences can support federal power. Home Builders requires identification of an independently valid interstate regime, the actual covered class and a genuine functional relationship. Those requirements are met by commercial hazardous-waste regulation and the specific latent-exposure mechanism. The timing rule keeps remedies meaningful when injury and its cause may emerge only after a state's ordinary commencement date. Its source is that adapted relationship, not environmental importance or a statutory label.

Guillen shows that a confined litigation rule can implement an identified interstate program. Jinks likewise confirms that a claim-linked federal timing rule can be necessary and proper, though its source is authorized federal adjudication rather than the commerce power employed here. A separate federal cleanup remedy does not constitutionally foreclose additional adapted means. The defendants' procedural characterization therefore does not require restoring the earlier limitations dismissal. Courts independently decide the legal connection while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about adapted means; the Constitution requires neither indispensability nor the least restrictive method.

**Statutory operation.** Section 9658 applies to state-law personal-injury or property-damage actions caused or contributed to by exposure to a hazardous substance, pollutant or contaminant released into the environment from a facility. It substitutes the federal commencement date only when state law supplies an earlier date; otherwise state limitations law governs. Actions under section 9607 are excluded. The ordinary federal date is when the plaintiff knew or reasonably should have known that the injury or damage was caused or contributed to by the substance concerned. The party invoking the section must establish its applicable predicates under ordinary civil rules. Paragraph (b)(4)(B) states the following distinct qualifications:

> (B) Special rules
> In the case of a minor or incompetent plaintiff, the term “federally required commencement date” means the later of the date referred to in subparagraph (A) or the following:
> (i) In the case of a minor, the date on which the minor reaches the age of majority, as determined by State law, or has a legal representative appointed.
> (ii) In the case of an incompetent individual, the date on which such individual becomes competent or has had a legal representative appointed.

The underlying tort elements and the otherwise applicable period remain operative. These express statutory conditions define the upheld rule; the separate application questions remain governed by the Second Circuit's existing remand.

**2. State judges must apply the valid federal commencement rule.** Article VI binds state courts adjudicating covered private claims to section 9658's valid federal rule of decision even though the cause of action arises under state law. That judicial obligation does not commandeer state legislatures or executive officers to establish or administer a federal regulatory program.

Testa supplies the duty of competent state courts to apply controlling federal law. New York and Printz distinguish that duty from compelled political-branch administration. Section 9658 changes governing law in an existing judicial action; it does not demand a new state code or field-enforcement program. Calling commencement procedural cannot create an exception to the Supremacy Clause. Jinks directly confirms this distinction for a valid federal timing rule, while requiring the distinct enumerated-power source established in the first holding here. Guillen's reserved Tenth Amendment question supplies no contrary holding. Article VI follows the valid exercise of enumerated power; it is not itself a free-standing source of regulatory power.

## Public Projection

## Event

**Freier v. Westinghouse Electric Corp.** Decided January 22, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 303 F.3d 176, Nos. 00-7724(L) and 00-7728(XAP). The questions are whether the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses authorize CERCLA section 309, 42 U.S.C. §9658, to displace earlier state limitations commencement dates for covered toxic-exposure claims, and whether requiring state courts to apply that rule violates the Tenth Amendment.

The consolidated suits allege injury and death from industrial waste deposited at the Pfohl landfill near Buffalo. The Second Circuit rejected the constitutional objections and vacated limitations summary judgment because discovery-of-cause facts remained disputed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in the constitutional respects under review.

The defendants' request to invalidate section 9658 and restore a limitations dismissal on that ground is denied. The Second Circuit's separate vacatur of limitations summary judgment and discovery-of-cause remand remain in force.

## Law After Decision

Section 9658 validly supplies its enacted causal-discovery commencement floor for covered industrial toxic-exposure claims. Its functional relationship to commercial hazardous-waste regulation supplies federal power, and state courts must apply it under Article VI. Each claimant must establish the statutory predicates; tort liability and the remaining limitations rules retain their own requirements.
