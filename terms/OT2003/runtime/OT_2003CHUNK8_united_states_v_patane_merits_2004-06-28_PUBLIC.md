## Event

United States v. Patane, No. 02-1183; merits decision, June 28, 2004. Argued December 9, 2003. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 304 F.3d 1013, which rejected the district court's probable-cause rationale but affirmed suppression of a pistol as the fruit of incomplete Miranda warnings. Officers investigating a restraining-order violation arrested Patane and began warnings; he interrupted by saying he knew his rights. A subsequent answer disclosed the pistol's location. The reviewed question assumes the answer was voluntary and asks whether the incomplete warning alone requires suppression of its physical fruit.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses suppression insofar as it rests solely on the Miranda physical-fruits ground and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm suppression. Breyer would vacate and remand to determine whether the warning omission was in good faith, with exclusion absent that exception.

The automatic Miranda-fruits bar to the pistol is removed. The lower court must apply any genuinely preserved independent admission ground; the appellate rejection of the probable-cause rationale remains part of the posture. The pistol may be admitted if otherwise lawful, while case-in-chief use of the unwarned answer remains governed by the separate statement rule.

## Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, J., SCALIA, J., KENNEDY, J. and THOMAS, J. joined. Thomas filed a concurring opinion, in which Scalia joined. Souter filed a dissenting opinion, in which Stevens and Ginsburg joined. Breyer filed a separate dissenting opinion.

The Court's opinion adopts the narrow physical-evidence remedy. The Thomas concurrence's broader duty and deterrence theory has only its two named supporters. The separate dissents seek different judgments.

## Holdings

**Controlling proposition:** Failure to give complete Miranda warnings restricts case-in-chief use of the resulting statement but does not by itself require suppression of nontestimonial physical evidence derived from a voluntary answer. When voluntariness is properly contested, the prosecution must establish it under the applicable burden and satisfy other independent admission requirements; an unwarned answer is not thereby a valid waiver, and the rule does not extend to evidence produced by actual coercion.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas directly join the narrow remedial proposition and reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** Dickerson preserves Miranda's constitutional warning-and-waiver safeguard and prevents voluntariness alone from authorizing the answer's ordinary case-in-chief use. It left the physical-fruits question open. Tucker and Elstad show that constitutional status does not require identical exclusion for an unwarned answer and every derivative evidentiary product. Here the offered item is a pistol, the reviewed answer is assumed voluntary, and the challenge rests on the incomplete warning itself. The direct gain to investigators identified by the dissent does not eliminate those distinct remedial categories. Admitting a voluntary answer's physical product neither establishes a knowing waiver nor resolves an actual-coercion or independent acquisition claim. Chavez's fractured no-use damages disposition supplies no controlling theory of physical-fruit admissibility; its independent due-process inquiry remains distinct. The appellate court therefore erred in treating Dickerson's constitutional characterization alone as a sufficient fruits rule. The warning-only ground for suppressing this pistol must be reversed, with any genuinely preserved independent objection considered in its proper scope.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Miranda v. Arizona; Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Their constitutional case-in-chief warning-and-waiver core is preserved; Dickerson's open physical-fruits question is resolved at the voluntary, nontestimonial scope here.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433; Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Their differentiated consequences support this physical-remedy distinction without replacing the original statement's warning requirement.
- **Chavez v. Martinez, 538 U.S. 760:** Its no-use damages judgment had no controlling Fifth Amendment rationale and does not decide admissibility; its separate interrogation-abuse inquiry survives at its actual scope.

## Law After Decision

Incomplete warnings alone no longer justify excluding nontestimonial physical fruits of a voluntary answer. Statements, actual coercion and independently unlawful acquisition retain their own rules and properly preserved remedies. An incomplete warning does not establish waiver; the dispute decided concerns this evidentiary category.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, concurring.** They also rely on the Self-Incrimination Clause's criminal-case and testimonial focus. In their view, mere omission of warnings does not itself supply the constitutional violation that excluding this physical evidence would deter. Their broader characterization is unnecessary to the five-Justice narrow remedy and is not joined by O'Connor, Kennedy or Stone-Zsela. It provides no permission for actual coercion.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The gun is the direct investigative product of unwarned testimonial disclosure. Allowing that benefit weakens the constitutional safeguard in practice and encourages bypassing warnings. Tucker's setting and Elstad's effective intervening warning do not resolve this direct-fruit problem. They would affirm suppression without requiring a new finding of deliberate evasion or actual coercion.

**Justice Breyer, dissenting.** He would exclude fruits of unwarned questioning unless the warning omission was in good faith. Because this record establishes neither good faith nor deliberate evasion, he would vacate and remand for that inquiry. Incomplete warnings and the absence of a complete waiver do not themselves decide whether the exception applies; he does not vote for unconditional affirmance or unconditional admission.

## Procedure After Action

The prosecution returns to the lower court with the warning-only physical-fruits bar removed. The court must apply any genuinely preserved independent admission requirement, including properly contested voluntariness under its applicable burden, while respecting the existing appellate disposition of the probable-cause ground.
