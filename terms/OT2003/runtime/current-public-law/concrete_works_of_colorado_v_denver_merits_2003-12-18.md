## Event

**Concrete Works of Colorado v. City and County of Denver.** Decided December 18, 2003, on merits submission. Review of the Tenth Circuit's judgment, 321 F.3d 950, directing judgment for Denver after a bench trial invalidated its contracting program. The questions concern the evidence supporting the minority- and women-owned-business provisions and the effect of an earlier unchallenged determination that the program fit its remedial objective.

The 1998 ordinance supplies the live prospective claim; the 1990 ordinance supplies the damages claim. The record includes marketplace studies, testimony about exclusion, evidence connecting city spending to private discrimination, and the earlier appeal's treatment of fit.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: 9–0 on each component. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The direction to enter judgment for Denver on the live racial and sex contracting claims remains in force, including rejection of the 1990 damages theory on its constitutional merits. The injunction and declarations founded on the rejected constitutional grounds must be removed. The mootness dispositions for prospective relief under the superseded 1990 and 1996 ordinances and declaratory relief under the 1996 ordinance are preserved. The 1990 damages claim is resolved on its merits rather than extinguished by repeal.

## Holdings

**Authority for all three holdings:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**1. Denver establishes the required racial justification.** A public contracting classification satisfies Adarand when the government proves an important lawful remedial objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, accounting for its actual coverage and burdens and answering supported, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Linked statistical and anecdotal evidence can establish local exclusion to which public spending contributes without a prior discrimination judgment against each contractor or individual proof of injury to every beneficiary; Denver's developed evidence and preserved fit determination meet that burden here.

Adarand supplies the common substantial-justification rule. Engineering Contractors requires a supported response to concrete alternatives, and City of Dallas requires a demonstrated local remedial connection. The accepted findings connect city contractors and city spending to actual exclusion. The studies are considered with their limitations, contracting experience and testimony. The developed response to the size-and-capacity criticism addresses how firms hire, subcontract and obtain equipment rather than simply assuming that every disparity proves discrimination.

The ultimate burden remains Denver's. The Court does not rest validity on CWC's failure to commission a regression or disprove every finding. Gratz requires attention to the mechanism's actual coverage and burdens; Grutter requires consideration of genuinely practicable alternatives rather than exhaustion of every imagined method. The record and the binding fit determination satisfy those inquiries. A prior adjudication against every beneficiary or contractor would replace the governing evidentiary inquiry with a prerequisite that these precedents do not impose.

**2. The women-owned-business provisions receive a separate constitutional assessment.** Government must establish an actual important objective and a substantial relationship between the sex classification and that objective under the demanding governmental proof requirement. Denver satisfies that standard through the women-specific utilization, earnings and exclusion evidence and the program's preserved fit; proof supporting the racial provisions does not by itself establish this separate justification.

City of Dallas preserves the distinct sex inquiry. The Court evaluates the evidence concerning women-owned firms in its own terms, including their opportunities and the relationship of the remedy to demonstrated exclusion. A shared remedial setting cannot replace that proof with stereotypes or evidence about different groups. The separate showing supports the same judgment for Denver.

**3. The unchallenged fit determination retains its effect.** An earlier determination in the same litigation ordinarily governs a later stage when the available appeal left it unchallenged and the challenged program has not materially changed. Substantially different evidence, contrary intervening controlling law, or clear error producing manifest injustice may justify departure; none is established for this preserved fit issue.

Law of the case is a practice governing reconsideration, not a restriction on subject-matter jurisdiction. CWC had the opportunity to challenge the earlier determination and has identified no material amendment reopening it. The actual Adarand standard requires substantive justification, but replacing the lower court's scrutiny vocabulary does not itself reopen an already established, more demanding fit determination. The settled issue supplies its proper part of the constitutional analysis while Denver retains the burden on the live questions. The developed record permits judgment without a new appellate factual finding.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Adarand Constructors v. Peña:** Applied as the controlling common substantial-justification rule for racial classifications; its governmental burden and actual fit requirements retain force.
- **Engineering Contractors Association v. Metropolitan Dade County:** Applied to the evidence, actual burdens and supported practicable alternatives; supported local remediation remains subject to those requirements.
- **City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters:** Applied for a demonstrated local remedial connection and independent sex-classification proof. The evidence here supplies the connection and distinct showings the governing inquiries require.
- **Gratz v. Bollinger:** Applied for attention to a remedial mechanism's actual operation, coverage and burdens rather than general historical exclusion alone.
- **Grutter v. Bollinger:** Applied for meaningful consideration of concrete, practicable alternatives without a requirement to exhaust every conceivable method.

## Law After Decision

The existing racial and sex contracting standards remain distinct and government-borne. Denver's developed evidence and preserved fit adjudication satisfy them for the live 1990 and 1998 claims. Combined evidence may demonstrate connected local exclusion; each classification still requires its own supported justification. Law of the case preserves an unchallenged fit determination subject to materially changed issues and the stated exceptions.
