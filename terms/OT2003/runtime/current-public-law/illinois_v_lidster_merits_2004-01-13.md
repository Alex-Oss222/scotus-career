## Event

**Illinois v. Lidster.** No. 02-1060; 540 U.S. 419. Decided January 13, 2004, on merits submission. The Illinois Supreme Court sustained suppression by treating this checkpoint as categorically barred by City of Indianapolis v. Edmond. The question is whether a brief, systematic checkpoint seeking witnesses to a specific fatal hit-and-run has an adequate independent justification and is reasonable in its actual operation.

Police returned one week after the fatal incident to the same place and approximate time, asked motorists a brief question and handed them a flyer. Flashing lights and partial lane obstruction produced queues of up to fifteen cars per eastbound lane. Total waiting was a very few minutes at most; contact with an officer lasted ten to fifteen seconds. Lidster's near collision with an officer and observed signs of intoxication led to a later individualized investigation.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded with this checkpoint's reasonableness established.

## Holdings

**1. A specific witness inquiry requires its own operational justification.** Edmond does not categorically bar a genuine checkpoint seeking possible witnesses' information about another person's specific serious incident. The government must establish the actual purpose and a concrete connection between the incident, the selected place and time, and the likely information sought; Brown then requires consideration of the gravity of the concern, how the seizure advances it, and the full interference with liberty, including duration and limits on officer discretion.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion on this rule and framework, joined by all eight other Justices.

Edmond invalidated an ordinary drug-detection program directed at the stopped motorists' own offenses. Its antecedent-authority requirement remains effective. A defined inquiry addressed to possible witnesses has a different relation to those stopped, but an information-gathering label cannot itself authorize a seizure. Chandler requires actual justification in the program's operation. Brown supplies the contextual balance once that concrete need is identified. The rule accordingly permits a genuinely incident-linked, proportionate interruption; an investigation of a motorist's own wrongdoing requires its separate justification.

**2. This complete operation is reasonable.** The same-place, same-time inquiry one week after a fatal hit-and-run, its systematic and narrowly limited question and flyer, and its brief total delay establish a proportionate witness checkpoint. The whole detention—including the queue rather than just officer contact—satisfies Brown, so the checkpoint supplies no basis for the ordered suppression.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The fatal event supplies a grave public concern. The place and approximate weekly timing reasonably connect the inquiry to travelers who might have useful information; the justification does not depend on every driver having witnessed the collision. Uniform, short contacts limit discretion. The compulsory queue and nighttime encounter impose real interruption and apprehension, but the total wait remains small in relation to the confined inquiry.

The competing remand position gives that intrusion and uncertain information yield greater weight and would have Illinois perform the contextual assessment first. The accepted record permits the Court to resolve this particular balance now through Brown's practical assessment of the established operation. The later intoxication investigation supplies no retrospective justification for initiating the checkpoint; the incident-linked operation stands on its own grounds.

## Precedent Treatment

- **City of Indianapolis v. Edmond:** Distinguished by the genuine witness purpose; its prohibition of ordinary suspect-screening roadblocks and antecedent-justification requirement remain effective.
- **Chandler v. Miller:** Applied to require a supported operational need rather than a label or symbolic purpose; the actual incident connection and constrained operation supply that justification here.
- **Brown v. Texas:** Applied to the public concern, advancement of that concern, full intrusion and constraints on discretion. That framework governs both the defined inquiry and this operation's reasonableness.

## Law After Decision

Courts must distinguish a genuinely incident-linked witness inquiry from a program investigating the stopped motorists for ordinary crimes. The former still requires a concrete place-and-time connection and proportionate, constrained operation under Brown. This fatal-hit-and-run checkpoint satisfies that standard on its complete queue-inclusive record.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three Justices agree that Edmond's categorical rule does not dispose of this witness inquiry and join the governing Brown framework. They give substantial weight to the involuntary nighttime delay and the uncertain likelihood of useful information from the motorists. Because Illinois relied on a categorical rule, they would return the case for its initial contextual application. Their proposed remand does not find this checkpoint unreasonable, require a new hearing or rest on a disputed longer wait; it preserves their view that this close balance should be applied below first.

## Procedure After Action

The ordinary mandate returns the case to Illinois with the categorical suppression ruling reversed and the checkpoint's reasonableness settled. The state court continues the prosecution consistently with that determination.
