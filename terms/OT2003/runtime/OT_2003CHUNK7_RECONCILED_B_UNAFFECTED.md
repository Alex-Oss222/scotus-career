## 94. Elk Grove Unified School District v. Newdow

### Resolved commitments and grounds

The independent N1 and N2 commitments are retained with the following complete boundaries.

**N1 — The confined threshold ground.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the appellate constitutional judgment and remand for dismissal on prudential standing grounds. Newdow abandoned representation of his daughter and asserts his own parental interest. The operative custody arrangement gives both parents consultation rights but gives Banning final authority over substantial health, education and welfare decisions when agreement fails; Newdow remains free to teach his own views. The suit's requested judicial control of this disputed school experience depends on the opposed parental authority that the custody arrangement allocates. Those five decline adjudication of the claimed parental entitlement in that setting.

This is neither a categorical bar on joint-custody parents nor a holding that all family disagreements defeat Article III injury. It neither determines custody nor treats the domestic-relations exception as a general exclusion of federal constitutional claims. Warth's own-right limitation, the distinct representation issue preserved in Raines, and Ankenbrandt's confined treatment of domestic-relations jurisdiction supply the boundaries. The possibility of a genuinely personal injury from government interference with parental religious instruction is acknowledged; it does not allow this abandoned representative claim and disputed authority to be relabeled as an unrestricted individual entitlement to control the child's school exposure. No view of the child's religious preferences is found. Those five reach no Establishment Clause merits question in this case.

**N2 — Personal standing and the existing civic-practice rule.** O'Connor and Thomas recognize an adequately alleged personal injury to Newdow's continuing religious instructional relationship with his child. Ankenbrandt allows adjudication of a federal question without alteration of a custody decree; the legal allocation of final school decisions does not itself negate that asserted personal injury. They reverse on the merits under Sherman, decided February 12, 1996, whose concrete civic-practice ground both joined and O'Connor authored. The materially equivalent government-led Pledge is permitted where children may decline words and gestures, remain in place, and incur no punishment; the supplied record establishes no different compelled profession, worship program, devotional instruction or penalty. Agostini, decided June 23, 1997, preserves this established permissible relationship within its unified inquiry. Both joined that controlling modification.

Sherman concerns governmental speech, not a private-speech forum, and does not require the religious acknowledgment to become meaningless to believers. Protected abstention matters within this civic exercise; it is not a universal cure for official prayer. Lee and Santa Fe remain controlling for government-organized worship and its mechanisms. N2 supplies no universal historical exemption and no separate revival of Lemon's former independent prongs. O'Connor and Thomas would restore the dismissal of the reviewed constitutional challenge on these merits grounds; they do not rewrite the federal Pledge statute or adjudicate a different compelled practice.

## 96. United States v. Dominguez Benitez

### Corrected operative commitments

**D1R — Prejudice from the omitted personal warning.** A defendant seeking relief for the unpreserved omission of the required personal warning that rejection of a nonbinding sentencing recommendation does not permit withdrawal must show a reasonable probability that, had the warning been given, he would not have pleaded guilty. The probability must undermine confidence in the plea decision; it is less than a more-likely-than-not requirement. It concerns the defendant's choice, not proof of probable acquittal at trial. The defendant must still establish the other Rule 52(b) conditions, and the independent fourth condition governs discretionary correction after substantial-rights effect has been shown.

Vonn, decided March 4, 2002, supplies the ordinary forfeiture framework and defendant burden; Rule 11(h) supplies no exception for this omission. Strickland and Bagley supply the familiar reasonable-probability formulation for outcome uncertainty. The Court now applies that measure to the counterfactual plea decision rather than to a hypothetical trial verdict. The question concerns a nonstructural federal-rule omission on direct review. It does not decide the consequences of a constitutionally invalid plea where the record fails to establish the constitutionally indispensable waiver, or a collateral challenge.

**D2R — Whole-record application without automatic substitutes.** The reviewing court considers all relevant portions of the existing record, including the agreement, actual advisements, demonstrated comprehension, sentencing expectations, available plea alternatives and consequences of choosing trial. A signed agreement neither conclusively establishes comprehension nor retroactively performs the judge's personal duty. A disappointed expectation of safety-valve relief may bear on the decision but does not alone prove that the omitted withdrawal warning caused the plea. Representation and available evidence of guilt may be relevant without becoming conclusive presumptions or an innocence requirement. No additional historical recital about the defendant's consultations, translation, admissions or strength of evidence is imported as a present factual finding.

**D3R — Reversal and defined remand.** Reverse the appellate judgment and remand for application of D1R and D2R, including each distinct Rule 52(b) condition. The appellate court used an incorrect prejudice rule. Correcting that legal ruling supports reversal; the need for record-specific proceedings does not require a different formal disposition. The mandate neither grants withdrawal nor conclusively reinstates the plea and sentence. Former Rule 11(e)(2) governs the omitted warning; later numbering as Rule 11(c)(3)(B) is a cross-reference, not substitution of later text.

All eight Associates join D1R, D2R and D3R in full. The provisional Stevens alternative DS is withdrawn. The provisional vacatur is corrected to reversal. Scalia's full join is retained for the concrete changed-prior ground below.

## 99. F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A.

### Corrected operative commitments

**E1R — Statutory coverage and the claimant's connection.** Section 6a's general exclusion reaches nonimport foreign commerce, including wholly foreign transactions; it is not limited to American exports. Its exception requires a direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable effect of the statutory kind, and that effect must give rise to the claim for which the plaintiff seeks relief. Where foreign anticompetitive conduct produces domestic injury and an independent foreign injury, a foreign purchaser cannot obtain coverage for the independent foreign injury merely because the domestic effect gives another purchaser a claim. Import trade remains outside the general exclusion. Where coverage is restored only by the export-commerce branch, recovery remains limited to injury to export business in the United States.

**E2R — The construction and its limits.** The indefinite phrase concerning a claim can grammatically bear the purchasers' reading, but it also permits reference to the claim being litigated. The statutory structure, the prescriptive-comity principle of avoiding unreasonable interference with foreign sovereign regulation, and the enactment's clarification rather than substantial expansion of preexisting geographic coverage favor the latter construction. The 1982 change from export-only wording to nonimport foreign commerce confirms the general exclusion's coverage of wholly foreign transactions. The cited legislative history supports that statutory context; it does not erase a contrary enacted term.

The domestic-effects exception protects claims arising from the qualifying domestic injury. Extending it to independent foreign injury would regulate foreign commercial affairs without that claim connection. The construction does not call for an empirical finding of incompatible foreign commands, an open-ended country-by-country balancing trial, or an automatic foreign-permission defense. Government enforcement cases involving broad remedial decrees do not establish a private claimant's right to damages solely for independent foreign harm. The distinct examples of foreign injury dependent on domestic harm do not decide the assumed independent-injury question.

Current Hartford Fire preserves this result. Its American-market effects gave rise to the claims it allowed, and it expressly reserved section 6a's precise operation while preserving its effect-and-claim conditions. Its rejection of the particular foreign-permission defense did not reject the retained Charming Betsy interpretive canon. Trinko supplies no geographic rule and cannot substitute for construing section 6a.

**E3R — Vacatur and the alternative theory.** On the assumed independence of these foreign injuries, the statutory exception is unavailable. Vacate the appellate judgment and remand. The purchasers may seek consideration of their alternative contention that the domestic effects were necessary to sustain the foreign price increases and therefore gave rise to their own losses. The appellate court must determine whether that contention was preserved and, if so, consider its legal sufficiency under the statute and any properly required further proceedings. The contention is not a finding of dependence, a successful causal showing or a grant of antitrust liability. No final merits disposition of that unaddressed alternative is made here. The geographical coverage holding does not decide whether section 6a is jurisdictional in every procedural sense.

**EC — The narrower sufficient rationale.** Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on the ground that the statutory text readily permits the claim-specific construction and that this is the interpretation consistent with customary deference to foreign countries' regulation within their own territory. They accept E1R's operative construction, the independent-injury result and E3R's defined remand, but do not join E2R's legislative-history and broader explanatory route. Their position is not a rejection of comity. Their canon is a rule of statutory interpretation, not a discretionary defense based on proved incompatibility of foreign law. Their support for the same operative result cannot be represented as a full join in every reason supplied by the five.
