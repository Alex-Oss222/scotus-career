## 85. Thornton v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand; reject search-incident authority on this secured-arrestee record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the Fourth Circuit to address the preserved independent inventory or inevitable-discovery rationale under its actual requirements. If none sustains admission, exclude the gun and determine the effect on each affected conviction under applicable error review.
* **Remedial Reach:** Thornton’s challenged evidence and resulting convictions; no automatic invalidation of the lawful arrest or independently obtained drugs.
* **Practical Relief:** The Government must identify an actual lawful alternative and supporting findings, including standardized inventory procedures where relied on. The court must resolve that basis rather than hypothesize one. If admission fails, it must vacate the convictions materially dependent on the gun and order the appropriate further proceedings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Limits of vehicle search incident to arrest

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Belton authorizes a vehicle search incident to arrest when police first contact the recent occupant after he has left the vehicle, including when he is secured away from it before the search.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose limiting Belton’s categorical authority by the actual protective purpose of the exception; do not portray this limitation as already adopted.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring secured-arrestee problem requires a rule beyond a contact-timing distinction. Reworking all automobile searches would exceed the incident-search question. A first-contact line would preserve an equally purposeless search whenever an officer spoke a moment earlier.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A vehicle search incident to arrest requires a reasonable possibility, at the time of search, that the arrestee can reach the searched area to obtain a weapon or destroy evidence; recent occupancy alone is insufficient once the arrestee is secured away from the vehicle. The Government bears the burden to establish the exception by a preponderance. Thornton’s handcuffed placement in the patrol car defeats this ground, without foreclosing an independently proved lawful basis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Passenger-compartment incident searches of current or recent occupants; person searches and independent automobile, inventory or other exceptions retain their own requirements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides an additional evidence-gathering exception, courts apply existing probable-cause automobile rules and the access rule stated here; a mere reason to suspect evidence is not an adopted new exception.
* **Transition:** Apply the limitation to Thornton and cases still on direct review under Griffith v. Kentucky. Final convictions remain subject to the existing collateral-review rules; no new retrospective exception or later historical good-faith doctrine is imported.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Chimel identifies the source of the exception, and Knowles refuses a categorical search when its rationale is missing. Belton’s broad vehicle proxy has become disconnected from that rationale when access is eliminated. First-contact timing does not repair the defect. A candid access limitation does, while preserving the Government’s ability to establish a genuinely independent inventory or probable-cause basis.
* **Precedent Treatment:** New York v. Belton is limited insofar as it permits an automatic passenger-compartment search after the arrestee is secured beyond access. Chimel v. California and Knowles v. Iowa supply the retained purpose. Florida v. Thomas supplies no merits rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The alternative inventory/discovery basis needs review of its supporting findings; any other exception requires preservation and record support.
