## 56. Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm vacatur of this preliminary anti-suit injunction and its dependent surviving civil-contempt relief.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Dissolve the challenged restraint and vacate coercive civil consequences dependent on its validity. Do not disturb the separate recognition judgment or decide the pending enforcement questions.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parties and foreign proceedings covered by this injunction; no worldwide prohibition or immunity from domestic enforcement is entered.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must remove the invalid restraint and dependent civil enforcement measures. It may continue authorized recognition or execution proceedings and independently determine the domestic effect of any foreign order under the Convention.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Necessity within the treaty’s distributed enforcement system

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The injunction and civil consequences present a live appeal separate from award recognition. The record permits review of the asserted need without resolving every Convention defense.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether protecting this enforcement judgment justifies barring the Indonesian proceedings on the showing made.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** State a bounded equitable rule grounded in the Convention’s structure, rather than invent a categorical absence of anti-suit authority.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: ordinary parallel annulment or restraint proceedings do not alone justify interference. A broader ban on all foreign anti-suit relief could leave a court powerless against a demonstrated obstruction. A narrower fact-only vacatur would fail to identify why enforcement preference cannot itself establish necessity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court enforcing a foreign arbitral award may not enjoin a party’s parallel foreign annulment or enforcement-related proceedings merely because they contest the award or seek a different outcome. The injunction applicant must establish a concrete threat to the domestic court’s lawful adjudication or effective judgment that the Convention’s ordinary recognition rules cannot adequately address, together with the usual equitable prerequisites and a restraint no broader than necessary. This record does not justify the challenged injunction on that basis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Foreign anti-suit relief ancillary to Convention enforcement; the domestic court retains its authority to determine the effect, if any, of foreign orders.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A party seeking a later restraint must identify the actual obstruction and why ordinary domestic adjudication cannot address it; disagreement among national courts is insufficient by itself.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Articles V and VI contemplate that foreign proceedings or annulment may become relevant to recognition without automatically depriving another court of its task. A policy favoring enforcement cannot erase that allocation. The applicant must therefore explain what cannot be protected through the domestic court’s own adjudication and lawful execution. The district court’s broad restraint prevents a foreign adjudication without the demonstrated necessity required for that interference.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No controlling Convention decision is overruled. The circuit’s necessity and comity analysis is adopted only at the stated scope, without importing all its alternative discussion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The separate recognition appeal, the definitive allocation of primary annulment jurisdiction, and the effect of an actual foreign annulment are outside this injunction judgment. Until decided in a proper posture, courts apply the Convention’s recognition provisions to the orders actually presented.

## 57. Batzel v. Smith

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the circuit’s interlocutory anti-SLAPP and §230 disposition and remand with instructions to dismiss that appeal for want of appellate jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the moderator’s defenses to the district court without a Supreme Court construction of the disputed §230 issue. Preserve the distinct final sponsor disposition to the extent independently before the circuit and outside the questions.
* **Remedial Reach:** The premature appeal and its merits ruling, not the final disposition of every defendant or any damages award.
* **Practical Relief:** The circuit must remove the unauthorized interlocutory decision; the district court may adjudicate the defenses and factual disputes through ordinary procedure. Later final review or a properly established statutory interlocutory route remains available.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal finality precedes the content-immunity question

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenged order denies a special motion in an ongoing federal action. No independent authorized route makes its merits reviewable merely because a related final appeal exists.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this anti-SLAPP denial satisfies the federal collateral-order exception.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing federal finality law; reach no §230 merits holding after finding the appeal unauthorized.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: reject immediate review for this category of special-motion denial based on ordinary probability-of-success and liability issues. A broader rule against every statutory immunity appeal would disregard established exceptions. A narrower unexplained dismissal would fail to address the state-created appeal and trial-avoidance arguments.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Denial of a California anti-SLAPP motion in federal court is not immediately appealable under §1291 merely because state law permits an appeal or describes protection from litigation. The appellant must satisfy every collateral-order condition; the merits-linked probability inquiry and ordinary liability defenses here do not become sufficiently separate and effectively unreviewable because litigation will continue. The circuit therefore lacked authority to decide the moderator’s §230 defense on this interlocutory appeal.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The identified special-motion denial and independent liability defenses within it; a genuinely separate authorized final appeal remains governed by its own scope.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A party invoking §230 may pursue the defense below and seek review through a valid final or independently authorized interlocutory route. A state appeal rule does not enlarge federal appellate jurisdiction.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. rejects trial expense and a promised escape from litigation as sufficient reasons to bypass federal finality. Swint v. Chambers County Commission applies adequate final review to an ordinary liability defense and refuses to append independent questions for efficiency. The anti-SLAPP inquiry here tests the plaintiff’s prospect of success and overlaps the disputed §230 defense. The public value of protected expression does not dispense with the cumulative jurisdictional conditions.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. and Swint v. Chambers County Commission applied; Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. retained at its narrow scope. No §230 precedent is created or overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The sender-intent and reasonable-perception questions cannot be adjudicated on this unauthorized appeal. Until reached through a valid route, lower courts apply §230 and independently applicable authority without treating this jurisdictional disposition as a choice among its disputed constructions.

## 58. South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe of Indians

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject the claim that the pump must originate pollutants, vacate the premature permit summary judgment, and remand the genuinely disputed distinct-water issue.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require application of the conveyance rule and factual determination of whether the pump adds pollutants to a meaningfully distinct water body. Permit the properly presented broader statutory theory to be addressed on remand without adopting it here.
* **Remedial Reach:** The S-9 permit dispute; no blanket exemption or permit order governing all engineered transfers is entered.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must resolve the material hydrological evidence and issue a permit-compliance judgment if the statutory elements are proved. If no addition to distinct waters is established, it must reject this permit claim. This Court fixes no effluent limit or new factual boundary.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Conveying pollution can constitute the point-source act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The pump’s operation presents the granted source question; the litigated relationship between source and receiving areas determines whether summary judgment can stand. Resolving that factual uncertainty does not require accepting the newly elaborated unitary theory.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether existing pollutants conveyed by a pump are excluded solely because the pump did not generate them, and whether this record conclusively establishes the necessary addition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted definitions and remand the material factual premise; propose no general transfer exemption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: settle the recurring conveyance issue and preserve the distinct addition element. A broader all-transfer rule would decide a materially underdeveloped theory and erase the factual distinction. A narrower source-only affirmance would sustain an operative permit judgment despite its disputed necessary premise.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A point source may discharge pollutants by conveying them from elsewhere; the Clean Water Act does not require the source to generate the pollutants. A claimant must still establish an addition to covered receiving waters, so movement within a single water body does not become an addition merely because a pump is used. The party seeking summary judgment must show the absence of a genuine material dispute about those predicates, which this hydrological record does not establish.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Conveyances such as pumps, pipes, and ditches that transport pollutants; this holding retains the separate covered-water and addition requirements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish point-source conveyance from proof of an addition. Hydrological connection is relevant evidence, not an automatic exemption or automatic showing of one water body.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory list describes transport mechanisms, and treating each as a pollutant generator would exclude the ordinary function Congress named. Arkansas v. Oklahoma confirms that permit consequences turn on actual statutory water-quality relationships, not an invented universal rule. The canal and impoundment evidence nevertheless requires assessment of their actual connection and distinction. Neither separate names nor some shared groundwater conclusively answers that question. Borden Ranch confirms that a point-source instrument need not manufacture the material it conveys, while requiring an actual addition rather than environmental harm alone. Its §404 soil-deposit and farming-recapture holding does not settle this §402 water-transfer application. The distinction reinforces the need to resolve the disputed relationship between the canal and receiving conservation area.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Arkansas v. Oklahoma preserved at its permit and supported-record scope; no adopted water-transfer precedent is displaced.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Government’s broader unitary-waters construction requires proper presentation and development; the factual distinction requires findings. Until this Court decides that broader theory, courts apply the enacted discharge elements and the conveyance rule stated here, considering preserved arguments without treating all covered waters as one by fiat.

## 59. United States v. Galletti

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the rule requiring a separate timely assessment against each general partner and remand the bankruptcy claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore consideration of the Government’s claims under the collection period applicable to the timely assessed partnership tax. Require proof or resolution of the remaining liability and claim prerequisites; award no fixed sum here.
* **Remedial Reach:** The IRS claims against these debtors for the identified partnership tax debt, not their unrelated obligations.
* **Practical Relief:** The bankruptcy court may not disallow these claims solely because the IRS did not duplicate the partnership assessment in each partner’s name. It must adjudicate the claims and remaining defenses under the correct limitations premise.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: One assessed debt may have several legally responsible obligors

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Timely assessment of the partnership is established for the presented issue. The legal source of the partners’ responsibility is sufficiently identified to review the categorical assessment requirement.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether another assessment is indispensable to use the collection period against general partners liable for the same employment-tax debt.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the statutory question by separating assessment of the obligation from collection against its legally responsible obligors.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: settle the recurring general-partner collection issue. Broader permission to collect any asserted federal liability without its own prerequisites would exceed the reasoning. A narrower debtor-specific ruling would leave the same statutory mistake intact elsewhere.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A timely assessment of a partnership’s employment tax preserves the statutory collection period for that tax against general partners independently liable for the partnership debt; the Code does not require duplicate assessments solely because collection is sought from those partners. The Government must still establish the assessment’s timeliness and amount, the applicable basis of partner liability, and the requirements of the collection proceeding. The absence of a second assessment therefore does not by itself defeat these bankruptcy claims.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The same assessed debt and partners liable for it under applicable law; independent personal taxes and separately created penalties retain their own assessment rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify whether the Government is collecting the same assessed obligation or asserting a different liability that has its own statutory prerequisites.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The assessed item is the partnership’s tax obligation. California liability supplies a route to persons responsible for that obligation, not a new federal employment tax in each partner’s name. The distinction recognized in United States v. Updike supports reading the collection period as attached to the assessed debt while preserving defenses belonging to the person pursued. An extra assessment requirement cannot be inferred merely from the availability of an additional obligor.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Updike applied at the obligation-versus-obligor distinction; no adopted partner-assessment rule is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual partner liability, claim amount, and independently applicable collection safeguards remain for the proper record. Until resolved, the Government must establish those predicates under their own law; this holding supplies no automatic levy or payment entitlement.

## 60. Nixon v. Missouri Municipal League

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s preemption judgment in all three consolidated dockets.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Sustain the FCC’s refusal to displace Missouri’s allocation of municipal telecommunications authority under this provision. Enter no federal authorization for the municipalities to operate.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged Missouri restriction and the consolidated petitions; independent private-provider protections remain governed by §253.
* **Practical Relief:** The state restriction remains effective against the municipal respondents unless changed through an independently lawful route. The FCC need not reopen the proceeding to create municipal powers from the general entity language.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: General market-entry language does not reassign municipal powers

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual state restriction and the FCC’s asserted power present a statutory question. No hypothetical federal enactment expressly conferring local power needs adjudication.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §253 authorizes displacement of this State’s decision about the activities its political subdivisions may undertake.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply contextual statutory interpretation and the clear-statement principle without deciding an unnecessary constitutional limit on Congress.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: cover state allocation of municipal provider authority under this provision. Broader immunity for every regulation affecting public providers would exceed the governmental-capacity rationale. A narrower exception turning only on the order of state enactments would make federal power depend on drafting sequence despite the effects language.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 253 does not clearly authorize the FCC to displace a State’s allocation of telecommunications-service authority to its own political subdivisions merely through its reference to the ability of any entity. A claimant seeking that alteration must identify an unmistakable congressional direction in the statute’s text and structure, not only a competitive benefit. The provision therefore does not preempt Missouri’s restriction on the municipal respondents’ entry.
* **Reach Within the Question:** State assignment or withdrawal of municipal authority to offer the service; this holding does not exempt independent private market barriers from federal preemption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Separate regulation of a provider’s conduct from whether a State has empowered its subdivision to be that provider. General competition policy cannot substitute for the necessary statutory direction.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gregory v. Ashcroft requires clear direction before construing general statutory language to alter important state governmental arrangements. New York v. United States explains why regulation and assignment of governmental decision-making are distinct. AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board recognizes federal administration within the enacted telecommunications field, but does not convert every broad noun into authority to reconstruct municipal powers. The proposed existing-authority distinction would make results turn on formal state drafting while disregarding the federal statute’s attention to prohibitory effects.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Gregory v. Ashcroft applied; New York v. United States and AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board preserved at their actual scopes. No constitutional immunity from an expressly authorized enactment is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The constitutional validity of a different, explicit congressional command is outside the statute presented. Until such a case arises, courts apply the actual enacted preemption authority and the clear-statement principle.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this expedited procedure and subsequent full consideration met the actual statutory and constitutional hearing requirements.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply functional notice and meaningful-opportunity principles to the record; reject a universal cure rule while sustaining this result.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record: adequacy depends on the content of the notices, nature of the objections, urgency, and genuine reconsideration. A broader rule that any rehearing cures defective notice would permit irreversible evasion of participation. * **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews v. Eldridge directs attention to actual interests, error risk, useful safeguards and government needs, rather than treating every hearing as a trial. National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. requires the agency’s actual ground, so the reviewing court cannot manufacture responsive consideration. Arkansas v. Oklahoma preserves the difference between supported findings and a competing policy preference. Here the notices described the ownership changes, and the agency addressed the substantive creditor and public-interest objections in its reconsideration orders. Closure alone does not show that these objections became legally unreviewable or that a material fact hearing would change the procedure needed.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Mathews v. Eldridge applied; National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. and Arkansas v. Oklahoma applied to actual agency reasoning and record review. No categorical rehearing-cure precedent is created.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent creditor and bankruptcy consequences are outside this administrative procedural grant. Until adjudicated in their proper forum, those claims retain their own statutory and factual requirements.

## 62. United States v. Flores-Montano

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the suppression judgment and remand for further criminal proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject suppression based solely on the absence of reasonable suspicion for this fuel-tank disassembly. Preserve any genuinely independent, properly raised issue; order no conviction or sentence.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged search and resulting evidence in Flores-Montano’s prosecution; no damages or broader policy injunction is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must resume proceedings without excluding the seized contraband on the rejected categorical ground. The prosecution retains its burden to prove the charged offenses.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reversible vehicle inspection at entry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The border location, inspection purpose and method are established. The record presents no serious destruction requiring resolution of a different manner-of-search question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether taking apart and restoring this fuel tank categorically requires reasonable suspicion.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received border-inspection authority while retaining independent limits on unreasonable execution.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: cover reversible fuel-tank inspection and comparable vehicle-compartment examination at entry. A broader approval of every destructive vehicle search would disregard the independent manner limitation. A narrower result dependent on an unrelied-upon suspicion theory would leave the actual categorical question unanswered.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Officials conducting a lawful international-border inspection may remove, disassemble and reassemble a vehicle’s fuel tank to inspect for contraband without reasonable suspicion when the method does not inflict serious destruction or otherwise make the search unreasonable. The Government must establish the border and inspection predicates; mechanical complexity alone does not add a suspicion requirement. This reversible procedure therefore was not invalid solely because the Government did not establish individualized suspicion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Entering vehicles and reversible compartment inspections within a genuine border examination; bodily searches and materially different destructive or prolonged procedures are excluded from this holding.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Determine the actual border function and effects of the method. Neither a complicated procedure nor use of specialized labor alone decides reasonableness.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Ramsey grounds inspection authority in the actual crossing. United States v. Montoya de Hernandez’s personal-intrusion concerns do not turn use of a mechanic into a bodily search. United States v. Ramirez nevertheless prevents lawful search authority from becoming permission for gratuitous or disproportionate destruction. Those principles permit this inspection while retaining a meaningful method constraint. Soldal v. Cook County’s home-possession holding is not generalized into a fuel-tank suspicion rule.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Ramsey and United States v. Montoya de Hernandez applied at their border and personal-intrusion scopes; United States v. Ramirez preserved as an independent execution limit; Soldal v. Cook County distinguished at its actual home-seizure holding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Destructive drilling, serious damage, bodily searches, and materially prolonged detention require facts absent here. Until such a method is adjudicated, courts apply existing border authority together with the independent Fourth Amendment reasonableness limits on manner and detention.

## 63. National Archives and Records Administration v. Favish

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the disclosure judgment and remand with instructions to enter summary judgment permitting withholding of the four disputed photographs under Exemption 7(C).
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the request to compel release of those images. Order no wider secrecy decree and no finding that all surrounding investigative records are exempt.
* **Remedial Reach:** The four photographs and the present FOIA disclosure demand; other released or unchallenged records remain outside this judgment.
* **Practical Relief:** The custodian may withhold the four images from this FOIA demand. The judgment does not convert the exemption into an independent prohibition on every lawful disclosure route or reopen material already released.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Real family privacy and a supported public-interest claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The family’s own privacy evidence and the remaining images present a concrete balance. The request’s stated purpose requires evaluating its evidence of agency error without trying the underlying death investigation anew.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these relatives’ privacy may be protected and whether unsupported allegations justify compelled release of these photographs.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the actual privacy-and-transparency balance to living relatives’ death-image interests; reject both an automatic family veto and a universal prior-proof-of-misconduct rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: recognize a recurring protected interest and an evidence-based disclosure balance. A broader requirement to prove wrongdoing before any FOIA inquiry would contradict the limits of United States Department of State v. Ray. A narrower image-by-image aesthetic judgment would fail to identify the family’s own privacy and the actual governmental-transparency inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Exemption 7(C) protects living relatives against an unwarranted invasion of their own privacy through disclosure of a family member’s death-scene images. The agency must establish the law-enforcement and privacy predicates and justify withholding; when the asserted public benefit is exposing official misconduct, the requester must provide a reasonable evidentiary foundation for that inquiry and a plausible connection between the requested material and the governmental issue, without first proving misconduct itself. Speculation alone supplies no counterweight sufficient to compel these four photographs on this record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Law-enforcement death images implicating demonstrated surviving-family privacy; the rule does not create a perpetual proprietary right in every fact about a deceased person or a prerequisite for unrelated transparency requests.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A credible factual inconsistency tied to requested images can justify a different balance; bare suspicion cannot. Other demonstrated transparency purposes must be assessed on their own terms.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States Department of State v. Ray requires demonstrated incremental value, while preserving the possibility that derivative investigation matters. Bibles v. Oregon Natural Desert Association keeps the balance focused on government conduct and retains the agency’s ultimate burden. Those bounded rules can be restated as one inquiry into the actual privacy intrusion and supported incremental transparency, with Exemption 7(C)’s distinct statutory threshold. The family evidence establishes a concrete intrusion; unsupported doubts about the official conclusion do not establish how these images would materially illuminate an agency failure. Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority further prevents permission to withhold from being mislabeled a universal legal ban.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States Department of State v. Ray and Bibles v. Oregon Natural Desert Association applied and extended to the demonstrated family interest; Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority preserved at the permission-versus-prohibition distinction. No presumption that official conduct is infallible is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unrequested records, different supported investigative evidence, and independent disclosure prohibitions are outside this record. Until such a claim is decided, courts apply the statutory exemption and actual evidence-based balance, with the agency retaining its withholding burden.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain declaratory and injunctive relief requiring officials to accept otherwise eligible candidates without the ancestry condition; dismiss the uninjured appointment claim without prejudice.

## 70. Vieth v. Jubelirer

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE AND REMAND. Reject categorical nonjusticiability and vacate the judgment dismissing the partisan-gerrymandering claim. Claims under the First and Fourteenth Amendments are justiciable when they allege that the State selected district boundaries to impose a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of their political affiliation or activity. If the Court adopts Stone's reasoning, Stone writes the opinion.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case to the three-judge District Court with instructions to permit amendment of the pleadings and determine whether the plaintiffs can establish the injury, purpose and causation defined below, subject to the State's same-decision defense. The District Court must determine personal standing and apply the test to the challenged boundaries. The mandate neither invalidates Pennsylvania's congressional map on the present record nor prescribes replacement districts.
* **Remedial Reach:** The plaintiffs' adequately pleaded claims of material electoral disadvantage intentionally imposed because of political affiliation or activity. Relief following proof of a violation and failure of the State's defense must fully correct the established deprivation and extend no further than necessary. The State receives the first opportunity to adopt lawful replacement boundaries and need not adopt the claimant's evidentiary comparator. No party receives a right to a proportional seat allocation, a particular district or electoral success.
* **Practical Relief:** Permit conforming amendment and set a prompt schedule for pleading, proof and findings consistent with the election calendar. An adequately pleaded claim proceeds without requiring a subsequent electoral defeat. The existing map remains operative unless and until the court grants properly supported relief; this remand supplies no finding of liability and no immediate redraw.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Justiciability and personal partisan injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve justiciability and the governing constitutional standard on review of the dismissal. Personal standing, the sufficiency of amended allegations and ultimate liability remain distinct inquiries. No missing finding of deprivation, purpose or causation is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether intentionally imposing a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of their political affiliation presents a justiciable claim under the First and Fourteenth Amendments; what personal political injury, governmental purpose and causal connection establish that claim; and whether partisan predominance or a projected delegation-wide seat majority without a statewide vote majority suffices by itself.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Recognize a constitutional claim when the State selects electoral boundaries that materially impair identified voters' existing or demonstrably attainable capacity to organize, participate, nominate or elect, substantially because of their political affiliation or activity. Apply four elements and a State-proved same-decision defense. The equal-protection rule is informed by the First Amendment's protection of political belief, expression and association, and the same proved conduct independently violates the First Amendment. This is an affirmative extension of independent-injury and political-association principles; neither Bandemer nor Cano already establishes this complete partisan equal-treatment rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad as to the constitutional rule, limited as to the present judgment. The rule protects equal treatment of an existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity without requiring prior victory, a lost election, complete political exclusion, statewide entrenchment, a particular vote share, inability to elect in every destination district, a prescribed historical range or a separate durability threshold. It does not constitutionalize ordinary partisan disappointment, district shape, every departure from traditional districting principles or projected seat distributions. Political awareness, past electoral performance, continuity of representation, genuine political fairness and other independently legitimate districting objectives remain permissible considerations. Deliberately weakening identified voters because they support political opponents is not excused as partisan advantage or incumbent protection.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The First Amendment protects political belief, expression and association; the Fourteenth Amendment protects equal participation in the political community. A State may be aware of politics in drawing districts, but may not select electoral boundaries to impose a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of their political affiliation or activity. The constitutional wrong is intentional discriminatory treatment causing material impairment of an identified political capacity through a concrete electoral operation. Partisan design alone is insufficient, and material impairment does not require complete destruction of the capacity or elimination of a realistic possibility of victory. A plaintiff must establish four elements:

  **1. Identified political capacity.** Identify the affected voters and an existing or demonstrably attainable practical capacity to organize, participate, nominate or elect, supported by evidence of the voters, association or coalition and the activity at issue. An electoral claim requires evidence of the voters' capacity to translate their support into representation under lawful conditions. Prior electoral victory is not indispensable; a supported emerging coalition may qualify. A party label, desire for additional seats, expected support for an incumbent, a favorable hypothetical result or preferred statewide partisan distribution does not by itself identify the protected capacity.

  **2. Material impairment.** Show that the challenged boundaries materially impair that capacity through a demonstrated electoral operation. Packing concentrates voters in a district in a way that impairs their electoral effectiveness, even when they continue to elect a preferred candidate; cracking divides them among districts in a way that impairs their capacity to act together. Displacement, division of an effective coalition or another proved districting mechanism may also cause the impairment. These descriptions do not establish liability without proof of the resulting impairment. Materiality is a demonstrated, nontrivial reduction in the identified voters' effective ability to organize, nominate or elect preferred representatives, assessed under ordinary electoral conditions, rather than a nominal, speculative or practically insignificant effect, ordinary disappointment, inconvenience or a less favorable electoral prospect alone. A change in predicted vote share, without reliable evidence of its practical electoral consequences, does not independently establish materiality. District shape, partisan composition, predicted seat totals and departures from traditional criteria are evidence; none alone constitutes the injury. The plaintiff need not show complete destruction of the identified capacity, elimination of a realistic possibility of victory, complete political exclusion, statewide entrenchment, that every possible political activity has been eliminated or that the affected voters could never elect their preferred candidate.

  **3. Prohibited purpose.** Establish that imposing the identified electoral disadvantage on the affected voters because of their political affiliation or activity was a substantial motivating purpose of the challenged decision. The objective must actually contribute to the decision, but need not be exclusive or predominant. Knowledge of partisan effects or an incidental partisan benefit alone does not establish the prohibited purpose. Deliberately weakening the identified voters because they support political opponents is not an independently lawful purpose merely because officials call it partisan advantage or incumbent protection. An effort to entrench governing power may be powerful evidence, but statewide entrenchment is not an additional element. Purpose is evaluated from the decision's context and evidence of what the officials sought to accomplish.

  **4. Causation.** Establish that the challenged boundaries materially caused the impairment, rather than merely reflecting voter geography, ordinary electoral competition or independently lawful districting requirements. Identify the assignments or electoral operation responsible for the impairment. The boundaries need not be the only contributing cause, but a correlation between party preference and an unfavorable electoral result is insufficient.

  **Proof and same-decision defense.** Plaintiffs must prove each element by a preponderance of the evidence, meaning that it is more likely true than not. Once they establish the protected capacity's material impairment, prohibited purpose and causal connection, the State bears the burden of proving by a preponderance that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose. A genuine policy of partisan fairness or compliance with legitimate districting criteria may establish that defense if it independently accounts for the challenged result. An independently established and consistently applied fairness policy, or genuine population, geographic or statutory requirements, must actually account for materially the same lawful decision. A purpose to secure power by materially weakening the identified voters because of their affiliation cannot itself establish the defense. Merely naming an additional political objective, offering a conceivable justification or stating that politics always enters districting does not defeat the claim. Once the four elements are proved and that defense fails, no additional balancing of partisan advantages excuses the violation.

  **When injury occurs.** The injury is complete when the plan imposes the proved deprivation. A plaintiff need not wait for a preferred candidate to lose a later election. Organization, participation and nomination are protected political capacities in their own right; a claim concerning one of those activities does not require proof of a lost general election.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The stated rule governs personal claims that electoral districting intentionally imposed a material electoral disadvantage because of political affiliation or activity. Subordination of neutral criteria to political aims, unusual boundaries and a projected seat supermajority without a statewide vote majority do not alone establish a violation of the First Amendment, the Fourteenth Amendment or Article I. There is no entitlement to proportional party representation, competitive elections in every district, a guaranteed nomination or electoral success. Independent constitutional racial-opportunity claims retain their own elements and defenses.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Standing requires personal exposure to the challenged electoral operation, a causal connection to the claimant's injury and relief capable of redressing it. Statewide vote-seat disparities alone establish neither personal injury nor a violation. Under the ordinary notice-pleading rules governing this case, the complaint must identify the protected political capacity and the districting operation alleged to impair it, with allegations supporting the claim's purpose and causation requirements; merits proof is not required at the pleading stage. The amendment ordered here allows plaintiffs to present their claims under the announced standard.

  At the merits stage, comparative maps, voting patterns, organizational activity, district boundaries, legislative evidence and electoral history may illuminate capacity, impairment, purpose and causation. No single kind of evidence is mandatory or dispositive. A comparison must illuminate a real political capacity and the challenged operation; a map producing the plaintiff's preferred partisan outcome does not establish injury simply because it can be drawn. Historical success may support a capacity, but is not a prerequisite. No alternative map or prescribed number of comparison maps is mandatory. Examine the operative congressional plan and any legally permissible comparison without treating a prior malapportioned plan as a presumptively lawful baseline; Act 1's use under a stay does not cure its adjudicated population defect.

  If a violation is proved, identify the deprivation and the boundaries materially responsible for it. Give the State the first opportunity to adopt a lawful correction under a workable election timetable. Judicial relief, if required, must remove the established deprivation and extend no further than necessary, while respecting other governing districting requirements. It may not impose proportional partisan representation or guarantee a candidate, party or coalition a seat. A separate racial-opportunity claim must be assessed independently even when the same boundaries also support a partisan claim.
* **Transition:** Apply the announced rule to this live dispute and other open cases in which the claim is properly presented. Permit amendment here under the remand mandate, while retaining ordinary jurisdictional and procedural requirements. Announcing the rule does not reopen final judgments or invalidate completed elections.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Political awareness does not confer a power to impose a material electoral disadvantage on voters because they support political opponents. Equal participation, informed by freedom of political belief, expression and association, requires a distinction between an unfavorable political arrangement and government-imposed material impairment of an identified political capacity. The absence of an entitlement to a particular constituency does not authorize that disadvantage. Requiring political capacity, material impairment, prohibited purpose and material causation supplies that distinction. The same-decision defense protects independently lawful choices without allowing an additional political explanation to conceal an established constitutional injury.

  Pennsylvania's asserted pursuit of Republican electoral advantage and the alleged statewide disparity between votes and seats do not, without more, invalidate its congressional plan. Susan Furey alleges that the redistricting reassigned her residence from former Congressional District 13 to reconfigured District 6, affecting her political association and representation. That allegation identifies a concrete setting in which the existence of an identified political capacity, its impairment, the decisionmakers' purpose and map causation can be examined. It is not itself proof of any element or a finding that the assignment is unconstitutional. The District Court dismissed the partisan claim without applying this standard; vacatur and permission to amend allow the required inquiry without declaring an unproved deprivation or ordering a replacement map.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Baker v. Carr establishes that constitutional districting claims are not categorically barred by the political-question doctrine. Davis v. Bandemer's justiciability holding is retained; this decision supplies the more precise substantive standard. Gaffney v. Cummings preserves room for legitimate political accommodation; it does not immunize intentional disadvantage imposed on voters because they support opponents. Reynolds v. Sims supports meaningful, equal representation without guaranteeing proportional party seats, and White v. Regester directs attention to genuine access to political processes beyond formal eligibility.

  Page v. Bartels requires an actual protected political interest and a governmental operation materially impairing it; design and political disappointment are insufficient. Robertson v. Bartels prevents expected incumbent support or electoral preferences from becoming automatic constitutional injuries. Montiel v. Davis supplies the substantial-motivating-purpose principle and confirms that a partisan explanation does not automatically excuse an independent racial deprivation. Cano v. Davis distinguishes independent injury from design and recognizes that interference with organization, nomination, candidacy or participation need not await electoral defeat. Cano expressly left a standalone First Amendment claim outside its appeal. The present partisan equal-treatment rule extends those principles; it is not a holding already compelled by Cano.

  Brooks v. Barnes illustrates why nomination opportunity matters independently of ultimate general-election victory. Its Section 2 holding supplies an analogy, not a partisan districting test or an automatic incorporation of statutory racial-dilution elements. O'Hare Truck Service v. City of Northlake and Board of County Commissioners v. Umbehr show that a governmental penalty on political association can violate the First Amendment even without an entitlement to the governmental benefit at issue. That principle supports the present extension and the independent First Amendment ground without making their distinct factual settings or doctrinal requirements identical to districting. Police Department of Chicago v. Mosley supplies the method of deciding an equal-protection claim informed by closely connected First Amendment interests; it does not equate districting with exclusion from a speech forum.

  Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp. supports evaluating governmental purpose through contextual evidence. Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle supplies the motivating-factor and State-proved same-decision framework. These authorities support the announced elements and burdens without turning every political consideration into a constitutional violation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The present record does not establish liability or the boundaries needed to cure a violation. Those questions remain for proceedings on remand. Preserve Cano's separate intentional racial-opportunity claim, with its own deprivation, purpose, causation and same-decision requirements: a State cannot defeat that claim merely by relabeling its racial objective as partisan strategy. The partisan rule neither displaces that independent protection nor makes racial and partisan claims interchangeable. Sections 2 and 5 of the Voting Rights Act retain their own statutory elements and remedies. Structural Entrenchment remains a separate proposed claim concerning control of an elected governing body despite an opposing voting majority, without a prohibited-purpose requirement. This opinion does not adopt that claim, and Pennsylvania's congressional delegation is not such a governing body. Evidence of an effort to entrench power within the four-element claim does not dispense with any element or decide that separate structural question.

Material impairment means a concrete and practically significant reduction of the identified capacity, not a nominal, speculative or practically insignificant effect, mere disappointment or a less favorable electoral prospect. It does not require complete destruction, elimination of every realistic chance of success, complete political exclusion, statewide entrenchment, a particular vote share, inability to elect in every district or circumstance, a prescribed range of election results, or a lost general election before organizational injury can be recognized. Knowledge of political consequences or an incidental partisan benefit alone does not establish the prohibited purpose, but deliberately weakening identified voters because they support political opponents is not an independently lawful purpose merely because officials call it partisan advantage or incumbent protection. A supported emerging coalition may qualify on concrete evidence without a prior victory.

  **Proof and defense.** Preponderance means more likely true than not. Once all four elements are established, the State bears the same standard of persuasion that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose. A genuine, independently established and consistently applied fairness policy or lawful population, geographic or statutory requirement must actually account for that decision; a conceivable alternative justification is insufficient. A purpose to secure power by materially weakening the identified voters because of their affiliation cannot itself establish the defense. If the defense fails, no additional balancing of partisan advantages excuses the violation. Personal standing requires the claimant's own injury, traceability and redressability and remains separate from proof of the merits. Map-caused injury is complete when the proved deprivation occurs; no completed-election waiting rule applies.

  **Governing control.** For a preserved legislative claim, propose an additional rule: district assignments may not insulate an existing governing party or coalition from a reliably demonstrated opposition vote majority across the recorded conditions through a material, avoidable effect of those assignments. This rule applies separately to an elected legislative chamber or other district-elected governing body, never to a State's congressional delegation. It requires no discriminatory-purpose showing.

  The claimant must prove by a preponderance: (1) an independently existing governing party or coalition; (2) recorded conditions in which a coherent opposition receives more than half the reliably measured votes for the whole body; (3) retention of a seat majority by the governing coalition in those conditions; (4) retention across the recorded opposition-majority conditions rather than at one isolated point; and (5) a material causal contribution from the challenged assignments beyond geographic concentration, turnout, candidates and lawful constraints. No fixed number of elections or cycles controls.

  **Measurement and comparison.** An absolute majority means more than half, not a plurality. A staggered body requires evidence encompassing its whole membership. Use votes actually cast, accounting for uncontested races through disclosed, tested evidence rather than assumed votes. The lawful comparison must be workable, satisfy governing legal requirements and rest on independent legal, institutional or practical considerations rather than a desired partisan result. Applied to the same recorded votes, it must support the inference that the challenged placements cause retained control; the court must state the inference's limits. A completed election under the challenged map is unnecessary if reliable evidence establishes the elements.

  **Lawful-constraints defense.** The State may prove by a preponderance that consistently applied lawful constraints made the retained-control result unavoidable. Ordinary partisan advantage, voter approval or commission authorship is insufficient. This defense concerns unavoidable lawful constraints, not the purpose-based same-decision defense governing the unified equal-treatment claim.

  **Standing and application.** The claimant's own assignment must contribute materially to the body-wide injury, with relief likely to redress it. Congressional claims cannot invoke this route. No partisan liability is found here. Preserved legislative claims proceed under the proposed governing-control rule only if the Court adopts it; the independent racial and §2 dispositions remain governed by their separate law and findings.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Preserved congressional claims and procedurally available amendments; legislative chamber theories only if actually presented. No missing claim or adopted Court rule is invented.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply an effective Vieth holding at its actual scope. A possible electoral victory does not by itself defeat proof that boundaries materially impair an identified political capacity. Conversely, partisan design, irregular shape or an unfavorable seat projection does not establish the four elements without concrete injury and causal evidence. Governing-control claims require whole-body measurement and reliable treatment of uncontested races; neither a congressional delegation nor projected party advantage alone supplies that claim. A remand permits only preserved claims and amendments allowed by ordinary procedure, with no automatic revival of abandoned theories.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gingles governs statutory racial opportunity and cannot supply the elements of a separate partisan constitutional claim. The unified rule protects an identified political capacity from material impairment deliberately imposed because of political affiliation; it does not demand electoral impossibility, statewide entrenchment or complete exclusion, or split organizational and electoral injuries into incompatible tests. The separate proposed governing-control claim concerns insulation of a district-elected governing body and requires its own whole-body causal proof. The Florida finding of partisan purpose is relevant to Vieth's prohibited purpose but does not by itself establish that the challenged assignments were selected to impose the identified disadvantage, the required impairment or causation; if they were, the label partisan advantage supplies no independent justification. Party advantage neither establishes racial purpose nor excuses racial injury. Assess the supported findings under the effective rule, allowing only procedurally available further proof and amendments and imposing no categorical waiting period for an election under the challenged plan.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Davis v. Bandemer and the independently governing racial precedents retain their actual scope. No proposed Vieth formulation is treated as an effective holding before adoption.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any legislative partisan claim not preserved or properly added is outside the remand; remaining liability and remedial facts require adjudication under the actual controlling rule.
