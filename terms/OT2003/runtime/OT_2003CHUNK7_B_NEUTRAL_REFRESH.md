## Norton: neutral effects and remaining questions

Reported completion of the San Rafael route-designation plan and creation of Henry Mountains use-supervision files bears on requests to compel those initial acts. Completion does not establish satisfaction of a distinct continuing duty or mootness of all relief. Identify each actual remaining claim and remedy before drawing a completion-based consequence. The possibility of a maintenance claim is not an established pleading fact; this uncertainty does not prevent resolution of other supported plan claims.

The Factory Butte language distinguishes monitoring from closure when warranted. Informal monitoring and a recently commenced program are reported litigation facts, not findings of adequate compliance. The quoted provisions separately address documenting damage, recommendations for corrective action, identifying damage that may necessitate a designation change, and emphasis on supervision in limited and closed areas. A duty to document or recommend does not automatically become a duty to close or achieve a particular environmental condition. Conversely, discretion about means does not itself erase a specifically binding command.

The original packet's Lincoln and Bennett analogies preserve discretion without an enforceable standard while permitting enforcement of specific nondiscretionary duties. They do not already decide every component of a universal section 706(1) rule. The source of obligation, required act, obligatoriness, completion, and relief remain distinct. General wilderness nonimpairment, particular plan commitments, and NEPA supplementation require separate analysis.

Completed plan approval does not itself determine whether another major federal action remains. Identify the agency act to which supplementation would attach. The [regulatory copy](../runtime/OT_2003CHUNK7_ENVIRONMENT_REGS_NEUTRAL.md) preserves both mandatory triggers: substantial changes in the proposed action relevant to environmental concerns, and significant new circumstances or information relevant to environmental concerns and bearing on the proposed action or its impacts. Discretionary supplementation when NEPA's purposes would be furthered remains permission. Increased ORV use is SUWA's contention about new information, not a finding of legal significance or a new agency authorization. Completed approval and ongoing implementation therefore require distinct assessment.

The relevant earlier current-term projection entries preserve their confined statutory settings: Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation addresses bounded BACT enforcement; Lockyer addresses meaningful participation and review of supported agency reasons; Barnhart addresses a valid disability sequence; Environmental Defense Center addresses its permit architecture and constitutional questions. None supplies a previously decided answer to these plan terms or section 706(1) components.

### Earlier effective Public Citizen law

[Public Citizen's authorized public law](../runtime/OT_2003CHUNK7_department_of_transportation_v_public_citizen_merits_2004-06-07_PUBLIC.md), decided June 7, 2004, enters Norton. It requires a reasonably close causal relationship between the reviewed action and environmental effects. A necessary implementation step alone does not attribute an independently authorized increase to an agency that lacks statutory power to prevent it; the agency's own available choices remain subject to review. Cumulative analysis begins with the reviewed action's incremental impact while considering other actors' past, present and reasonably foreseeable actions. Its alternatives-preservation rule requires meaningful presentation, subject to notice from an obvious defect.

For Norton, identify BLM's actual statutory choices and the action said to require review. Do not assume BLM lacks control over ORV management merely because FMCSA lacked environmental authority over the President's entry decision. No independent actor's legally controlling decision is established by this supplement. Public Citizen does not settle whether a plan commitment is discrete and legally required, whether a relevant major action remains, or whether a supplementation trigger is met. Its distinct conformity-emissions rule is not a new NEPA supplementation test. Nor does its case-specific failure to present alternatives establish any Norton forfeiture.

## Suders: actual prior Duncan law and separate issues

The [Duncan controlling-law copy](../entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK7_DUNCAN.md), effective May 27, 2003, supplements the original packet's incomplete account of entering law. Its [prior public entry](../entering-law/OT_2003CHUNK7_DUNCAN_PUBLIC.md) supplies distinct earlier public positions, not current-case commitments.

Duncan already requires objectively intolerable conditions that would compel a reasonable employee to resign. Realistic corrective opportunities bear on that inquiry without becoming an exhaustion prerequisite. An employee need not endure danger, futile procedures or indefinite delay, and announcing an investigation alone creates no safe harbor. Duncan's concrete management response, unfinished promised account and actual alternative location are its own facts; they cannot be transplanted into Suders. A supported hostile environment does not itself establish compelled resignation.

Duncan leaves the circuit's intent-or-foreseeability condition distinct and adopts no universal formulation of that independent element. The original packet's statement that the constructive-discharge threshold is open therefore needs this qualification: objective compulsion is governing law; its application and any independently supported intention, foreseeability, causation, attribution or remedial requirements remain separate. The complete proposal in Stone-Zsela's Duncan Part B is noncontrolling. Stevens and Ginsburg joined Part A only; their public disagreement about the factual compulsion inference does not adopt Part B's complete rule.

Actual-authority attribution separately requires plaintiff-proved delegated supervisory power materially aiding harassment, by a preponderance. Title or rank alone is insufficient; policy knowledge and nonreporting alone supply no complete defense. Preserved negligence theories retain their own requirements. Duncan does not supply the general prevention/nonuse defense, its burdens or a tangible-action exception reserved in Faragher and Ellerth. Corrective opportunities may bear on compulsion without furnishing a complete defense; any new defense or remedy limitation requires its own legal rule and predicates.

Suders remains a summary-judgment case. Alleged harassment, deficient complaint response, examination-paper episode, supervisory arrest and resignation receive the applicable plaintiff-favorable treatment, not final findings establishing liability. Objective compulsion, any independent mental-state requirement, resignation causation, delegated-authority aid and injury require separate analysis. Negligence, actual authority and apparent authority are not interchangeable. Harassment and resignation losses require claim-specific causation; failure of one component neither defeats another automatically nor permits relabeling damages.
