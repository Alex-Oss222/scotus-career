## 83. Sabri v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment reinstating the prosecution and remand for trial.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the facial no-tracing objection; require proof of all enacted elements and permit independently preserved defenses.
* **Remedial Reach:** Sabri’s indictment. No conviction or automatic federal jurisdiction over every local offense follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must permit the case to proceed and instruct on qualifying benefits, agency, transaction value and corrupt intent. The prosecution must prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt; program participation cannot substitute for any element.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Protecting federally assisted entities against corruption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may criminalize bribery of agents of entities receiving qualifying federal assistance without requiring proof that each bribe affected particular federal dollars.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the constitutional point Salinas reserved, using the statute’s actual institutional connection.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Congress must be able to protect an assisted program’s institutional integrity without tracing fungible dollars. General policing of anyone associated with a funded community would be too broad. A specific-dollar loss requirement would disable protection of the resource pool Congress supports.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may prohibit corrupt payments concerning substantial transactions of an entity receiving qualifying federal assistance without tracing the payment to particular federal dollars, because corruption of that recipient’s authorized agents threatens the integrity of the assisted institution. The Government must prove every §666 element beyond a reasonable doubt, including the actual benefits, agency, value and corrupt-intent requirements; the statute’s lack of dollar tracing does not make it facially invalid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The enacted assisted-entity bribery mechanism; mere geographic proximity to federal spending or ordinary commercial receipts are insufficient.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides such a concrete application, courts enforce all §666 elements and the actual assistance-and-agency connection stated here; federal spending somewhere in the community does not suffice.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Salinas establishes what Congress enacted, while Fischer prevents treating all federal payments as assistance. The remaining connection is concrete: corruption of those administering a funded institution can divert its resources and decisions. Protecting that institution is adapted to the spending program without asserting a general federal police power.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Salinas v. United States is applied on statutory meaning and extended to the presented constitutional question. Fischer v. United States retains its benefits limitation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A fact-specific claim that a different prosecution lacks the identified institutional relationship is outside this pretrial facial record.
