## 88. Williams v. Morton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the meat-based equal-protection disposition; vacate and remand the free-exercise summary judgment for the specified material factual inquiry.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require findings concerning the proposed supply method, scale, security and cost under Turner and Overton. No immediate damages or meal injunction is entered. Any later prospective decree must meet 18 U.S.C. §3626's need, narrowness and intrusiveness requirements, with the required public-safety weight and an effective means of correcting the proved violation.
* **Remedial Reach:** The live policy challenge and named plaintiffs. If the policy is proved unlawful, correction must reach affected prisoners unless officials show that a narrower remedy fully cures it; damages require individual proof and defenses.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must promptly identify the genuinely disputed operational facts, permit targeted proof and decide whether the actual proposal is reasonably rejected. If a violation is proved, it must set a workable meal or equivalent lawful accommodation schedule and compliance reporting; it cannot direct procurement on assumed feasibility.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Religious diet and the Turner inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a prison violates Free Exercise or Equal Protection by providing kosher meat meals to Jewish prisoners while offering Muslim prisoners only vegetarian meals despite their asserted religious need for halal meat. The question’s kosher-meat premise is contested and, on the verified appellate record, unsupported.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Turner differently from the lower court, preserving deference while requiring a supported connection to the particular proposal.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record. Competing operational evidence determines summary judgment. A constitutional entitlement to any requested meal would exceed the test. Accepting an administrative label despite material contrary evidence would remove judicial review.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under Turner, a prison may restrict a sincere religious diet for supported legitimate penological reasons, but summary judgment cannot rest on disputed operational assumptions material to that connection. The prisoners bear the ultimate burden to show the restriction unreasonable; officials seeking summary judgment must show the absence of a genuine material dispute. This record requires assessment of the competing supply and security evidence before the free-exercise claim is finally resolved.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The proposed addition of halal meat under the constitutional prison standard; no automatic requirement to purchase it and no determination of religious orthodoxy.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a properly presented statutory accommodation claim, courts apply Turner to this constitutional claim; damages and immunity retain their independent requirements.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Turner allows substantial administrative latitude and consideration of cost. The prisoners nevertheless offered testimony that an outside supply could avoid the supposed operational obstacle. Whether that proposal actually addresses scale and security matters to the rational relationship and ready-alternative factors. Acknowledged added cost is relevant, but not a categorical answer without considering the concrete accommodation. Overton confirms Turner's inquiry into rational connection to legitimate neutral objectives, alternative ways to exercise the right, consequences for guards, other prisoners and resources, and obvious ready alternatives. This is not a least-restrictive-means test. The prisoners retain the ultimate burden; the summary-judgment court must still resolve a genuinely disputed operational premise through lawful procedure rather than presume it in officials' favor.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Turner v. Safley and O’Lone v. Estate of Shabazz are applied, not replaced by strict scrutiny.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual accommodation feasibility, any personal damages liability and immunity require further findings; contamination and RLUIPA claims are outside the presented case.

### Issue 2: The alleged meat-based religious comparator

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a prison violates Free Exercise or Equal Protection by providing kosher meat meals to Jewish prisoners while offering Muslim prisoners only vegetarian meals despite their asserted religious need for halal meat. The question’s kosher-meat premise is contested and, on the verified appellate record, unsupported.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the evidentiary prerequisite rather than adopt the question’s inaccurate assumption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The claim’s identified comparator fails on this record. A general rule authorizing unequal religious accommodations would exceed the facts. Assuming kosher meat to answer the abstract question would decide a different case.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An equal-protection claim based on provision of meat to one religious group and denial to another cannot succeed without evidence of that differential benefit. The claimant bears that predicate burden by a preponderance; the corrected, corroborated record describes both religious diets as meatless. The asserted meat-based comparator therefore does not establish a triable equal-protection violation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The identified differential meat claim; this ruling does not authorize discrimination proved through another actual policy or record.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The original certification was corrected, and additional witnesses confirmed the absence of kosher meat. The Court must resolve the case the record presents. The separate free-exercise claim survives or fails under its own standard, regardless of whether another faith receives meat.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No precedent is changed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A different proved religious preference would require a different factual claim; none is assumed here.

## 89. Republic of Austria v. Altmann

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the temporal applicability ruling and remand for further proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the FSIA to the immunity claim; award no property or damages at this threshold stage.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Temporal reach of the immunity statute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act governs a post-enactment suit based on conduct predating both the 1976 Act and the 1952 restrictive-immunity policy.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the statute’s temporal operation without creating a universal exception for jurisdictional enactments.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A uniform rule for present FSIA immunity determinations is required. Retroactive alteration of every substantive liability would exceed the statute. Exception-specific dates absent from its text would fracture the integrated regime.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The FSIA governs immunity in a suit filed after its enactment even when the alleged conduct predates 1976 and the 1952 restrictive policy; its present immunity framework does not itself create the underlying substantive liability. The claimant must establish an applicable statutory exception under the ordinary immunity procedure, and the sovereign retains all independent merits defenses. The pre-enactment date alone does not bar this suit.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Present immunity adjudication under the FSIA; substantive causes, act of state and attachment or execution retain separate requirements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides those distinct issues, courts apply the relevant FSIA exception, existing act-of-state law and governing property law independently; temporal applicability supplies no merits presumption.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Landgraf demands attention to legal consequences, not a label. Here Congress directed a comprehensive present determination of foreign immunity while leaving the wrongfulness of the old conduct to independent law. Hughes Aircraft involved a change opening substantive exposure through a newly available claimant route; it does not require preserving executive immunity practice as a vested entitlement for every later suit. Dole Food's filing-time status rule is consistent with applying the present statutory immunity framework, but does not itself resolve all retrospective effects. The decisive distinction is between present amenability to suit and the separate law governing ownership, wrongful conduct and liability. No expropriation exception or title entitlement is established merely by applying the FSIA.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products is applied contextually. Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer is distinguished, not overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The particular expropriation exception, act-of-state defense and property title are outside the limited grant or require unmade merits findings.

Jacobson distinguishes the defined benefit from surplus ownership; these retirees claim the defined promised payment itself. Section 203’s permission for an original suspension term does not authorize later reduction contrary to §204. Keeping the nominal figure while withdrawing the right to receive it is a cutback in substance and operation.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Lockheed Corp. v. Spink and Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson are applied; no precedent is overruled.

## 93. Belitskus v. Pizzingrilli

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm relief for Stith, Linzey and the Pennsylvania Green Party and dismissal of Donovan's moot claim; reverse the remedial limitation to the two named candidates and remand for a specific injunction curing the proved mandatory-fee policy for financially excluded candidates.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Replace the vague general command with an injunction specifying a sworn hardship application, decision criteria, a realistic nonmonetary alternative where offered, written reasons for denial and prompt review. Officials may verify a material factual dispute but may not demand unavailable records or set the decision after the relevant ballot deadline. Preserve the claimant-specific dispositions and any independently available fee award; no damages are ordered.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged mandatory policy governs all otherwise qualified candidates who establish inability to pay without material financial hardship. The responsible officials must show why a narrower remedy would fully cure that policy violation; people able to pay receive no automatic exemption.
* **Practical Relief:** Before the next relevant filing deadline, officials must publish the application and objective hardship criteria, identify the deciding official, and provide decisions and review early enough for a successful candidate to appear on the ballot. The district court must set dates tied to the actual election calendar and a provisional-filing procedure when official delay would otherwise defeat relief. Compliance requires accepting qualifying filings and maintaining reasons sufficient for review. A petition route is an alternative only if its deadlines and support requirements make it realistically usable. The district court retains enforcement authority; the order awards no ballot place to a candidate failing independent lawful qualifications.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Unaffordable ballot-access conditions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Candidate-specific injury, organizational standing, election-related mootness, and the fit between the proven exclusion and injunction are material. The record supports standing and recurring injury for Stith, Linzey and the party; Donovan's individual claim is moot. The action was litigated as applied, not as a challenge to every lawful fee. A policy remedy still must state concrete hardship criteria and operative duties under Rule 65(d).
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a State may require ballot filing fees without a realistic nonmonetary alternative for otherwise qualified candidates unable to pay without financial hardship.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering rules and resolve the identified open application; no undisclosed change is proposed.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the recurring mandatory fee barrier requires a class-wide operational rule. A broader ban on all fees would exceed the injury. A plaintiff-only exception would leave the proved policy operating against identically excluded candidates.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not make a filing fee the exclusive ballot-access route for an otherwise qualified candidate who cannot pay it without material financial hardship. The claimant proves that inability by a preponderance through a sworn account of available income, reasonably available assets, necessary living expenses and dependent obligations; poverty-line status alone is neither required nor conclusive. Material hardship means sacrificing ordinary necessities or assuming an unreasonable financial burden to pay the fee, rather than preferring not to pay. The State must justify the resulting ballot burden under Bullock, Lubin and Cartwright's three levels of calibrated review; excluding an otherwise qualified candidate for inability to pay requires an actually usable alternative. The rule does not excuse independent, lawful qualification requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Otherwise qualified candidates excluded for inability to pay and the associated voter and party interests; lawful support requirements and fees that do not produce that exclusion remain possible.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bullock v. Carter rejects wealth-based electoral exclusion unsupported by the screening rationale, and Lubin v. Panish requires another route for indigent candidates. The affidavits and injunction show that a hardship mechanism is administrable, although each disputed claimant still needs evidence. The rule protects participation without making public financing of all candidacies compulsory.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Bullock v. Carter and Lubin v. Panish at their inability-to-pay scope; extend no general wealth suspect-class rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 94. Elk Grove Unified School District v. Newdow

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Recognize Newdow's personal standing on the retained parental interest identified in the record and affirm the Ninth Circuit's rejection of the official classroom religious affirmation on that claim. Remand to enter relief consistent with his personal claim and the actual custody orders; no representative claim for the daughter remains for adjudication.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct a declaration and prospective order ending the district's organized religious affirmation while preserving secular civic instruction and private speech. The order rests on Newdow's established personal standing, not authority to represent his daughter. Resolve a demonstrated material custody ambiguity through prompt, limited proceedings; enter no federal custody decree and award no damages.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief concerns the district's proved official classroom practice and those it governs. A mere individual opt-out does not cure governmental organization of the religious affirmation; officials bear the burden of showing a narrower effective cure. Newdow receives no power to override the mother's lawful custody decisions.
* **Practical Relief:** Require school administrators to remove the official religious affirmation from the organized classroom exercise and communicate compliant instructions to teachers before the next such exercise under the remedial order. Enforce the order without restricting voluntary private religious speech. Any demonstrated custody ambiguity must receive prompt, limited clarification and cannot become an unsupported reason to postpone relief on the retained personal interest.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Personal standing and authority to represent a child

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Article III injury, Newdow's retained personal parental interest and the actual custody orders come first. The daughter's representative claim is no longer pursued. Ankenbrandt v. Richards establishes no general federal-question domestic-relations exception. A merits decision is unavailable if the personal claim lacks standing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Newdow has standing to challenge his daughter's public-school Pledge policy and, if so, whether teacher-led recitation including “under God” violates the Establishment Clause.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Article III's personal-injury requirements and Ankenbrandt's limited domestic-relations exception to this retained parental interest. Recognizing injury from the official school exercise is the proposed application; the right to instruct one's child alone does not establish a general right to control public-school speech.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: distinguish personal injury from representation across similar custody settings. Broader automatic parental standing would ignore state-defined interests. Narrower automatic dismissal would convert disputed custody into categorical immunity for school conduct.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A custody restriction on representing a child does not by itself extinguish a parent's distinct, legally retained interest injured by government action. The plaintiff must establish that retained interest, concrete interference and redressability. Adjudicating that personal claim gives him no authority to represent the child or amend custody under the guise of constitutional relief.
* **Reach Within the Question:** A parent's own legally retained relationship to the school practice; claims asserted for the child require independent authority.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides any distinct child claim properly brought by a representative, courts apply ordinary standing and representation rules without inferring authority from parenthood alone.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ankenbrandt excludes a categorical family-law jurisdiction bar, while Lujan still demands a concrete personal injury. The custody orders preserve Newdow's personal role in his daughter's religious upbringing but withhold authority to represent her. The proposed standing conclusion rests on the official school exercise's direct operation within that retained relationship, not mere disagreement with another parent's beliefs or with ordinary curricular exposure. Relief against the school's own practice neither changes custody nor restricts the mother's private religious instruction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Ankenbrandt v. Richards and Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife; add no general domestic-relations abstention rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The abandoned representative claim and final custody allocation are outside the question presented for adjudication.

### Issue 2: Official school religious affirmation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Article III injury, Newdow's retained personal parental interest and the actual custody orders come first. The daughter's representative claim is no longer pursued. Ankenbrandt v. Richards establishes no general federal-question domestic-relations exception. A merits decision is unavailable if the personal claim lacks standing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Newdow has standing to challenge his daughter's public-school Pledge policy and, if so, whether teacher-led recitation including “under God” violates the Establishment Clause.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Overrule Sherman's permission for a teacher-led classroom Pledge asking pupils to affirm 'under God'; retain its separate protection against compelled personal recitation. This changes the governing establishment rule rather than merely applying Lee or Santa Fe.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within official elementary-school affirmation. Sherman squarely authorizes the material practice and must be reconsidered openly. The rule reaches school-organized professions of religious belief, while historical instruction, private speech and unrelated governmental ceremonies present different questions. A claimant-only opt-out would leave the official religious affirmation intact.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public elementary school may not organize a classroom exercise asking pupils to affirm a religious proposition as part of its own civic program. Overrule Sherman's contrary permission for the teacher-led Pledge containing 'under God.' A claimant with standing must establish the official practice and its religious content; formal abstention does not change the government's role in organizing that affirmation. The school may retain a secular patriotic exercise, teach about religion and history, and protect genuinely private religious speech.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Teacher-led elementary classroom affirmation of the religious proposition; private recitation and secular patriotic exercises remain outside the prohibition.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides other ceremonial settings, courts apply the existing establishment rules to the actual speaker, program and audience.
* **Transition:** Apply the replacement rule to this live case and other open claims. Prospective correction does not reopen final judgments or itself establish damages; immunity and other damages prerequisites remain separate.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sherman treated the religious words as a permissible component of a voluntary civic exercise; that concrete permission remains governing until overruled. Agostini's unified method did not erase it. The proposed change rejects the operative distinction: teachers direct children to affirm the religious proposition itself within an official classroom exercise. Lee and Santa Fe identify the constitutional significance of school sponsorship and pressure, supporting reconsideration rather than silent displacement. Longstanding civic use and administrative reliance deserve weight but do not justify continuing that official profession of belief. A secular Pledge and historical instruction preserve the civic function without it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Overrule Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21 only insofar as it permits this teacher-led religious affirmation. Preserve Sherman and Barnette on protected abstention. Apply Agostini's unified inquiry and extend Lee and Santa Fe's official-school-exercise reasoning.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other governmental ceremonies are outside the question; no merits order may issue before personal standing is established.

Resolve Duncan's equitable reservation for the specified court-induced impediment without altering §2244(d)(2). Any effective Castro holding governs only its actual recharacterization issue; the limited warning for mixed-petition dismissal is an express supervisory extension, not an already established rule attributed to Castro. No general stay-and-abeyance holding is made.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** General authority and standards for mixed-petition stays remain open. The particular notice, causal prevention, diligence, any basis for reopening and each petition's ultimate timeliness require findings. Passing the timing inquiry establishes no constitutional error in the underlying convictions.
