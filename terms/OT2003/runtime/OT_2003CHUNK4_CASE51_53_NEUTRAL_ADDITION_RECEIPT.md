## Case 53: Guinn scope and qualifications

Guinn v. Cupp, 304 Or. 488, 747 P.2d 984, was decided December 22, 1987, and is date-eligible state judicial authority. The full opinion's initial petition expressly invoked both federal and Oregon constitutional assistance rights. The court discussed adequate appellate counsel under Article I, section 11, or the Sixth Amendment and then stated a two-part standard for failure to assert a claimed appellate error: a competent appellate lawyer would have raised it, and the outcome more probably than not would have differed. The application concerned an omitted shackling issue; relief required examining the probable result of that appeal and the permissible postconviction remedy.

That formulation may bear on how Oregon lawyers and judges understand an appellate-assistance allegation. It does not establish that every Oregon assistance claim is necessarily federal, that each state and federal element is identical in every context, or that Reese cited Guinn or incorporated the earlier federal argument in his highest-court petition. Guinn's more-probable-than-not formulation must not be relabeled as a different federal prejudice quantum. Its discussion of a notice-of-appeal failure, and its particular shackling proof sequence, remain distinct from a universal rule for all appellate representation.

The existing screened preservation recital still states that Reese's equivalence argument first appeared in his Supreme Court merits brief and was not addressed below.
