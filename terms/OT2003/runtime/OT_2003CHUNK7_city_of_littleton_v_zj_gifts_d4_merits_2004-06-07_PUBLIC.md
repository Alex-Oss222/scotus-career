## Event

City of Littleton v. Z. J. Gifts D-4, L.L.C.; No. 02-1609. Argued March 24, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit. The bookstore opened without applying for the required adult-business license and challenged the ordinance on its face. The Tenth Circuit held the ordinance deficient because it failed to assure a prompt final judicial determination. The questions are whether the scheme must assure a prompt judicial decision and whether ordinary Colorado procedures can supply that assurance without a special fixed decision deadline. The city seeks reversal; the bookstore seeks facial invalidation on judicial timing.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Tenth Circuit's judicial-timing facial ruling is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's decision.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The facial timing claim must be resolved under the prompt-review rules stated below. The ordinance remains operative against this objection; the Justices reach the judgment through the distinct grounds identified in the opinions.

## Holdings

### I. Judicial review must protect against suppression through delay

**Holding and operative rule:** An adult-business licensing scheme governing protected expression must assure a judicial determination promptly enough to prevent delay from suppressing that expression. Prompt access to file a challenge alone does not satisfy this requirement if the challenged restraint can remain effective indefinitely without a decision.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those seven direct joins make it controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas protects against the risk that a licensing process will suppress expression through delay. That risk continues after the applicant reaches the courthouse: an unresolved challenge can leave an erroneous denial in force as effectively as an unreviewable administrative refusal. The city's access-only position therefore does not supply the required safeguard. Thomas v. Chicago Park District distinguishes objective administration from censorship but expressly leaves judicial timing open. The Court now resolves that issue for the adult-business scheme presented. Objective criteria affect how the required protection may be furnished; they do not make the consequence of judicial delay irrelevant. Freedman v. Maryland supplies the stronger safeguards applicable to direct censorship. Recognizing the prompt-determination protection here preserves that distinction because the mechanisms needed to secure it may differ.

### II. Effective ordinary procedures satisfy this facial challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** An objective, nondiscretionary adult-business licensing scheme need not prescribe a special fixed judicial deadline when existing court procedures provide effective expedition and interim protection against suppression through delay. Colorado's identified ordinary procedures satisfy that facial requirement on this record; courts must use their available tools when delay threatens significant First Amendment injury.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those six direct joins make its rationale and application controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The licensing criteria concern objective qualifications, not official approval of expressive content. Such questions ordinarily permit relatively prompt adjudication. Colorado courts possess procedures for expedition and interim relief, and their ordinary administration may be expected to protect the constitutional interest when needed. The government has identified those operative mechanisms; the absence of a bespoke numerical timetable does not establish their inadequacy. The bookstore brought a facial challenge without applying for a license, so this record supplies no delayed licensing appeal showing that the procedures cannot perform that function.

The bookstore's proposed fixed-deadline rule would prescribe one procedural mechanism without establishing that Colorado's existing alternatives are constitutionally ineffective. Thomas supplies the distinction between neutral administration and the complete censorship procedure, while FW/PBS requires effective protection against delay. Their combined application permits the ordinary tools identified here. The disagreement about whether licensing receives a special judicial priority does not alter the reason for the safeguard: a restraint on protected expression can continue through an unresolved erroneous denial, and expedition or interim protection can prevent that harm without a special judicial code. A demonstrated delay is addressed by applying the prompt-decision rule with available expedited and interim remedies. Content censorship remains subject to Freedman's stronger safeguards.

## Precedent Treatment

- FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas is clarified: the core protection against licensing delay extends to judicial determination, while ordinary procedures can supply effective protection in this objective scheme.
- Thomas v. Chicago Park District is extended to the judicial-timing issue it reserved. Its distinction between objective administration and the complete censorship procedure remains effective.
- Freedman v. Maryland is distinguished as direct censorship; its stronger procedural safeguards continue to govern that setting.

## Law After Decision

Objective adult-business licensing must provide effective protection against suppression through judicial delay. A special fixed judicial-decision deadline is unnecessary when ordinary procedures furnish prompt adjudication, expedition and interim protection as required. Colorado's scheme survives the facial timing challenge on that basis. Concrete delay invokes application of this governing protection rather than a new unresolved timing rule.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens joins Part II and the judgment. He distinguishes ministerial adult-business licensing from the direct content censorship addressed in Freedman. In his reading, FW/PBS requires a prompt administrative decision and the possibility of prompt judicial review, rather than imposing on the licensor the full censorship-system duty to ensure a prompt judicial determination. Ordinary Colorado procedures supply an adequate possibility of promptness on this facial record. He withholds Part I because equating the two safeguards risks either burdening ordinary licensing with censorship procedures or weakening Freedman's stronger protection.

Justice Souter, joined by Justice Kennedy, joins Part I's prompt-determination rule and agrees that the facial challenge fails. Although the denial criteria are objective, adult-business licensing is triggered by the expressive materials sold, so its administration can expose unpopular sellers to delay. The mere availability of discretionary judicial tools does not by itself settle practical adequacy. Actual judicial practice must provide prompt disposition, with immediate intervention against foot-dragging and expeditious review at every stage. A rigid censorship timetable is unnecessary, and this record does not establish that the available procedures fail to furnish that protection. This is their sufficient adequacy ground in place of Part II's reliance on ordinary administration and available tools.

Justice Scalia concurs in the judgment. Even assuming the activity is protected, a general permit requirement unrelated to censorship of expressive content does not give the business a special constitutional priority in the courts. Ordinary Colorado procedures therefore suffice against the demand for special timing rules without his joining the affirmative prompt-determination entitlement. His conditional ground resolves this facial challenge without a finding that the bookstore's materials are obscene or that its actual business satisfies a distinct unprotected category.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Tenth Circuit for disposition of the facial timing claim consistently with the Court's ruling. The ordinance remains operative against that claim. A court reviewing a concrete denial must provide expedition or interim protection when delay threatens suppression of protected expression.
