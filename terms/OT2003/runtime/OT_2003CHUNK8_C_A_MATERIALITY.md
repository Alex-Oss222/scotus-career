## Intel — foreign evidentiary assistance, June 21

The controlling opinion permits a §1782 application for a reasonably contemplated foreign governmental adjudication upon a practical interested-person showing, rejects foreign discoverability and domestic analogues as additional categorical eligibility conditions, and preserves genuine discretion concerning receptivity, actual circumvention, burden and applicable privileges. Its remedy returns the particular request for a reasoned assistance decision. Breyer's dissent would impose the stated tribunal-character and dual-unavailability limits, based on statutory purpose or supervisory authority.

**Sosa:** This is the closest A decision by subject matter, but the legal predicates differ. Intel construes Congress's express evidentiary-assistance authorization. It does not determine whether §1350 supplies a domestic damages action, establish the particular customary detention norm, or waive the United States' immunity under the FTCA. Foreign receptivity and procedural interests under §1782 are consistent with Breyer's frozen Sosa distinction between substantive prohibition and international acceptance of adjudication; neither his Intel dissent nor the Court's discretionary rule changes his specific Sosa comity qualification. Scalia's delivery of Intel's enacted-source opinion supplies no join in new received ATS actions. The frozen Abebe-Jira/Kadic premises, seven-Justice recognition inquiry and bounded §2680(k) rule therefore remain intact.

**Ashcroft:** Intel's express discretionary assistance statute supplies no speech-scrutiny or preliminary-relief rule. Its treatment of evidence availability does not change COPA's integrated operation, the Government's comparative burden or the supported filtering evidence.

**Roeder:** Intel creates no substantive entitlement from procedural access. Its separation of eligibility from a production order is compatible with the frozen distinction between immunity, an action, the independent Accords bar and relief, but it does not construe these enactments or affect the Government's intervention and judicial-reconsideration posture. No new authority is required in the Roeder holding.

## Beard — capital mitigation, collateral gateway and effect, June 24

The five-Justice opinion treats the individual-juror mitigation rule as dictated before Banks's finality, finds the actual instruction/form mechanism unconstitutional and the 1995 state application objectively unreasonable, and requires the separate State-borne whole-penalty-record effect inquiry before sentence relief. The dissent would terminate the claim under Teague. Stevens joins application of the existing effect rule while retaining his different preferred standard.

**Ashcroft and Sosa:** The capital mitigation entitlement, §2254(d)(1) gateway and collateral effect burden do not govern preliminary speech protection, an ATS action or the FTCA exception. The State's criminal-collateral effect burden neither changes the Government's distinct speech-justification burden nor supplies an international-tort cause or waiver.

**Roeder:** Banks's collateral challenge and the Court's adjudication under existing habeas authority do not create a legislative command reopening a completed private judgment. Beard changes no Plaut predicate, immunity text or claim bar. Its treatment of a Justice's application of binding law without renewed rationale endorsement is compatible with Roeder's tested description of prior Plaut disagreements; it is not a new rationale commitment for any Justice.

## Blakely — punishment authorized by a plea and binding range, June 24

The six-Justice opinion applies Angle, Apprendi and Harris to a fact necessary to raise a binding sentencing range. The actual plea authorizes at most 53 months; the 90-month sentence is vacated, and any further aggravation proceeding depends on independent state authority and valid procedures. The opinion expressly applies existing doctrine without changing its operative rules.

**All three C matters:** COPA's criminal penalties create the speech burden under review, but Ashcroft is a pre-enforcement preliminary challenge, not a case of increased punishment based on an unadmitted aggravating element. Blakely creates no fact about COPA's operation or the filtering comparison. Sosa seeks civil remedies, and Roeder concerns a nonfinal civil liability default and an independent legal bar. Neither presents Blakely's jury, admission, binding-range or resentencing predicates. Its requirement of independent remedial authority is consistent with the existing C distinctions but changes none of them.

## Schriro — confined capital watershed availability, June 24

All nine classify Ring as a new procedural rule for Summerlin's 1984 finality. Five permit the indispensable present-offense death-eligibility protection under the confined watershed exception, with actual error and effect left for the applicable collateral process. The separate writings preserve different actual-record methods and capital-specific grounds. The decision does not establish a general reopening entitlement.

**Roeder:** Schriro adjudicates a capital prisoner's collateral claim under the legal regime governing the petition. The current Roeder record presents neither circumstance: its damages were unresolved and Congress made a specific immunity amendment during the pending action. Schriro's confined collateral availability thus neither expands nor displaces Plaut's completed-private-judgment rule, its pending-case qualification or its preservation of constitutionally permissible judicial procedures. No prior Plaut dissent or present Roeder join changes by inference from a criminal retroactivity vote.

**Ashcroft and Sosa:** Neither event concerns a final capital judgment, Ring eligibility or a Teague exception. Schriro supplies no new speech burden, international prohibition, received action, foreign-country waiver or remedy for the claims before the Court.

## Tennard — mitigation relevance and appellate certification, June 24

The seven-Justice opinion rejects the uniquely severe permanent-handicap and offense-nexus gates for supported mitigation, directs an issue-specific certificate under Slack and Miller-El, and preserves separate merits, statutory and effect inquiries. Scalia and Thomas would reconsider the underlying mitigation entitlement rather than treat the displaced gate as law.

**All three C matters:** None seeks a certificate of appealability or presents capital mitigation. The distinction between threshold access and substantive entitlement is already established in the C entering law; Tennard's particular certification threshold does not become the standard for COPA preliminary relief, ATS action recognition or a foreign-state default. Its preservation of separate merits and remedies supports no new C vote or legal condition.
