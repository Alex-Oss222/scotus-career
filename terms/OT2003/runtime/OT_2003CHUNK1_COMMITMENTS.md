## A. Habeas jurisdiction: supported alternatives and individual basis

**Proposition accepted by all eight:** Section 1226(e) does not withdraw independently proper section 2241 jurisdiction over this substantial constitutional and statutory challenge to Congress's mandatory custody framework. Its first sentence concerns discretionary judgment regarding application; its second protects an Attorney General action or decision under the section concerning detention, release, bond or parole. Read together, they do not clearly foreclose deciding whether the legal framework itself authorizes the contested form of custody. This construction supplies neither review of a lawful discretionary bond decision nor retrial of deportability facts. It reaches effective relief if legal authority is absent.

**Competing path:** The second sentence's broad prohibition on setting aside any detention action could be read to encompass the relief sought, even if the attack is framed as one on the statute. Its best argument is that a court cannot avoid an express action-based bar by renaming the challenge. The response is textual and channel-specific: a challenge to a mandatory congressional rule is not review of the Attorney General's protected judgment, and the combined sentences do not clearly eliminate independent review of that rule's legal validity. The retained channel is not inferred from the Government's concession. No magic reference to section 2241 is required for repeal; the requisite clarity is absent for this object of review. Because that construction resolves the statutory threshold, no Suspension Clause holding is supplied.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly supported the surviving legal-authority channel in Kolster, Goncalves, Calcano-Martinez, St. Cyr and Zadvydas. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas joined Goncalves's actual-channel holding, and applied the governing channel in St. Cyr and Zadvydas while preserving their earlier objections to Calcano's construction. Their commitment here rests on the statutory-framework distinction and those operative precedents, not an assumption that their Calcano disagreement disappeared. Their join barrier is an opinion claiming an unqualified constitutional right to review every detention decision or requiring an express section-number repeal. This new provision's second sentence is the strongest basis for a contrary jurisdiction position and must receive independent reconciliation; unanimity is not inferred merely from their earlier precedent-following joins.

## B. Pre-order coverage and Breyer's statutory alternative

All eight reject Kim's final-order-only construction. Section 1226 governs detention pending the removal decision, while section 1231 supplies the different final-order and removal-period scheme. Reading the phrase concerning deportability to require an already final order would prevent section 1226(c) from performing its evident pre-order function. Constitutional avoidance cannot erase that structure. This conclusion does not decide that either amended charge actually places Kim in the covered category.

**Breyer's proposed rule:** A resident who raises a substantial, nondilatory challenge to whether his convictions place him in section 1226(c) must receive bond consideration rather than be treated as conclusively included while that substantial question is unresolved. A substantial question is a genuinely nonfrivolous legal or factual challenge to a mandatory-custody predicate, not mere insistence on litigating removal, a discretionary plea for relief after covered removability is accepted, or a claim presented to obtain delay. Release may be conditioned on the resident establishing by clear and convincing evidence that he is unlikely to flee or endanger others. The substantial-question requirement and risk showing perform different work; neither automatically grants release. The proposed heightened detainee allocation is confined to this construction and is not a universal civil-detention burden.

The statutory basis is the difference between actual statutory deportability and a charge that remains substantially contested, read within the pre-order scheme rather than converted into a final-order requirement. National Center's individual-status protection and Breyer's Zadvydas purpose-bound construction support this distinction. The appeal-release analogy in section 3143(b), advanced and accepted as a possible resolution in the supplied argument account, supplies an administrable model that respects Congress's presumptive custody judgment while preserving meaningful adjudication. It is an analogy, not direct application of criminal bail law to immigration custody.

**Strongest contrary path:** Congress expressly supplied only the witness-protection release exception, and Joseph already adjudicates whether the charge is substantially unlikely to succeed. It is therefore argued that a court adds an exception by permitting bond on a merely substantial defense. Breyer's response is that the threshold identifies the individuals for whom mandatory classification may operate before adjudication; it does not add an equitable exception for someone conclusively within that classification. A process demanding that the alien show the Government is substantially unlikely to succeed can deny ordinary bond consideration even though a substantial, nondilatory category dispute remains. The proposed construction rejects that use of Joseph for this purpose without finally deciding removal.

**Remedy under this path:** Vacate the categorical constitutional judgment and remand for the lower court to obtain the proper agency treatment of both live charges and apply the substantial-question standard. Corona-Sanchez supplies a concrete reason to examine the original charge, but does not answer the added burglary and dual-offense grounds. Ventura bars the Court from supplying the missing classification findings. Kim's present release remains effective pending that determination. If the standard entitles him to consideration and continued custody is sought, the decisionmaker must consider release under the stated flight-and-danger allocation. If he has no qualifying substantial question, the categorical statute governs subject to any independently established constitutional defect. No final removal decision is made by this remand.

**Other Associates' precisely bounded response:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg could join this statutory disposition and rule as a sufficient, avoidance-based resolution of Kim's entitlement if it actually ensures meaningful bond consideration when the substantial-question condition is found and preserves his release pending adjudication. Each retains the broader constitutional hearing position and does not adopt the converse proposition that every resident lacking such a question may constitutionally be confined without individualized review. Their National Center and Zadvydas joins support this actual statutory alternative; it is not agreement with categorical constitutional validity. O'Connor and Kennedy agree that category objections require meaningful adjudication, but do not presently join the proposed substantial-question-to-bail construction: the distinction between testing the charge and suspending Congress's custody rule remains a material objection. Scalia and Thomas reject the proposed implied bail pathway and the imported heightened allocation; they require application of the enacted pre-order classification regime and its proper challenge mechanisms. No automatic migration of any Justice to Breyer's ground is authorized.

## C. Individualized risk review: enhanced comparison

### Stevens

His Flores dissent, full Zadvydas join, Foucha joins and authorship of unanimous Fairley establish concern with continued restraint whose individual justification can be tested without displacing legitimate initial authority. He affirms Kim's as-applied hearing judgment because a lawful permanent resident's criminal history may justify taking custody but cannot conclusively establish present flight risk or danger throughout a contested removal process. The strongest opposing path invokes congressional authority over removal and a finite adjudicative endpoint. It fails for him because even a proceeding with an eventual end can needlessly imprison a particular resident for months; Carlson retained individual discretion, Flores retained actual release avenues and requested review, and Salerno coupled preventive custody with an individual hearing. He does not turn Zadvydas's six months into the operative trigger here. He accepts the statutory alternative above if it resolves Kim's case, while retaining his broader constitutional ground.

### O'Connor

Her full Flores join and concurrence with Souter protect liberty while accepting categorical custodial arrangements that serve a legitimate temporary purpose. Her Zadvydas majority join requires custody to remain attached to removal; it does not erase Congress's distinct pending-proceeding authority. She reverses the premise that permanent residence alone requires an individual risk hearing in every section 1226(c) case. The strongest contrary path applies Gilbert, Krimstock and Fairley to the six-month deprivation. She distinguishes those cases without denying them: they concern continuing disputed predicates or custody necessity under the actual governing regime; here Congress may make qualifying status sufficient during the reasonably dispatched removal process. Connecticut Department of Public Safety forbids using procedural due process alone to insert a substantive risk exception. Genuine inclusion errors and unreasonable or arbitrary administration remain reviewable. She does not treat Kim's requested continuance as waiver, but the record does not establish the exceptional defect needed to affirm on that separate ground.

### Scalia

His Flores opinion supports legislative and regulatory use of temporary category rules, subject to the statute and real review avenues. His Zadvydas dissent rejects the claimed general constitutional release entitlement while his actual jurisdiction join accepts governing legal-authority review. He reverses the individual-risk-hearing requirement. The best contrary argument is his Krimstock concurrence: prolonged deprivation warrants a meaningful later adversary contest. His response is that such a contest addresses the governing legal predicates; it does not authorize redesigning a substantive mandatory-custody classification through Mathews balancing. Here a genuine disputed qualifying conviction or category must be cognizable, but current dangerousness is not itself a statutory release predicate outside the separate witness-protection provision. Joseph's existence does not prove adequacy in every application, and the absence of a request proves no waiver; this record supplies no adjudicated failure of that process warranting a general bail rule. He refuses a universal process-driven danger hearing and a six-month rule borrowed from section 1231(a)(6).

### Kennedy

His Flores join, Zadvydas statutory dissent and distinct constitutional discussion support temporary immigration custody while preserving protection against arbitrary detention and denial of prescribed review. His Krimstock opinion requires meaningful testing of the actual continuing legal justification and allows effective release when that justification fails. He reverses the resident-wide constitutional risk-hearing rule but requires that mandatory custody remain connected to adjudicating and implementing removal with reasonable dispatch. Unreasonable delay attributable to administration, or an actual failure of the required legal-predicate review, can make individual relief necessary. ### Souter

His Foucha joins, full Zadvydas join and Flores concurrence establish both continuing substantive authority and a serious liberty interest even within permissible regulatory custody. He affirms a reasonably prompt individualized hearing for a detained permanent resident. The best categorical argument is that Congress has already found the criminal class dangerous and unlikely to appear, with Joseph guarding only membership. For Souter that substitutes a past classification for the constitutionally relevant justification of prolonged physical restraint; it cannot explain holding a resident who can show he poses neither risk. Flores's real release possibilities and Carlson's discretion are missing from the absolute application asserted here. Connecticut Department of Public Safety does not answer the substantive challenge to making the risk presumption irrebuttable; it rejects procedural proof about an immaterial fact under an unchallenged substantive rule. He does not universalize Foucha's civil-commitment burden or propose Zadvydas's calendar for this statute. He can adopt Breyer's statutory alternative within the limits above.

### Thomas

His Flores and Zadvydas positions support Congress's pre-order categorical custody authority. He joined Scalia's constitutional ground in Zadvydas, not Kennedy's distinct procedural-liberty analysis. ### Ginsburg

She rejects its sufficiency because Joseph answers inclusion rather than whether a resident actually needs to remain confined; neither a criminal conviction nor an unresolved removal case demonstrates that bond cannot protect attendance and safety. ### Breyer

His own Zadvydas and Gilbert opinions distinguish lawful initial action from continued restraint and use statutory purpose and practical administration without assuming one universal rule. ## D. Hearing scope, proof, remedies and compatibility limits

For Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, the constitutional hearing must permit the resident to contest whether present flight risk or danger makes physical custody necessary, and the neutral decisionmaker must be able to order release on effective conditions if custody is unnecessary. Their commitments do not require a separate new tribunal if the existing bond process can provide the actual review. They do not adopt an automatic release deadline or a universal governmental clear-and-convincing burden. The present record supports affirming the actual reasonably-prompt-hearing judgment; it supplies no adjudicated proof-allocation dispute requiring them to prescribe a general quantum beyond meaningful individual risk consideration. The party's argument accepting detainee persuasion cannot be represented as a concession to any particular unstated quantum. If assembly proposes a new constitutional quantum or an obligation beyond the individual review required here, these commitments do not supply that join.

Breyer's expressly proposed heightened detainee risk showing is different and confined to his statutory approach. It is not silently combined with the constitutional votes as an independently controlling proof rule. O'Connor, Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas do not join a routine risk hearing, so their acceptance of meaningful category review cannot be counted as support for one.

For reversal, the Court removes the constitutional hearing condition but does not issue an immigration custody order itself. ## Provisional commitments

### Proposed common propositions

**1. Past travel of materials does not supply this constitutional connection by itself.** The fact that a camera, recording medium or other production material once crossed state lines does not independently authorize Congress to punish the ensuing wholly intrastate production. It may satisfy the connection Congress actually enacted, but statutory satisfaction and constitutional authority are separate. An equipment-only ground would connect ordinary local conduct to federal power through the provenance of ubiquitous tools, without identifying the required commerce mechanism.

**2. The actual-abuse-image production class has the necessary relation to the interstate prohibition.** The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit Congress to include the local production of actual-child-abuse recordings in the identified interstate market prohibition because the produced images constitute the very supply that the regime excludes from interstate circulation. Their reproducibility, concealability and capacity to supply the prohibited demand make a production exception a material avenue around that prohibition. Congress need not wait for a producer's individual sale or interstate transmission before addressing the creation of that supply. This is a class-and-regime connection, not a conclusion that every local offense is commercial or every valuable object may be federally regulated.

**3. The mechanism is supported on this supplied record and fits this application.** The record identifies actual recordings, actual children and a national prohibition of the corresponding image market. Local production creates additional copies or original material capable of reproduction within that same prohibited supply; excluding the productive class would leave the source of replacement and additional supply outside the restriction. The Government's anti-evasion explanation is materially supported by the characteristics of the regulated product and the operation of the identified regime. This conclusion is a legal inference from the supplied production facts and regime, not a new empirical statistic, a finding that Holston personally distributed an image or an assumption that statutory comprehensiveness proves necessity. The enacted production application reasonably fits that supported supply mechanism.

**4. The independent statutory condition remains in place.** The constitutional rationale does not remove the materials-movement requirement used in this prosecution, replace it with an uncharged sale element or add a defendant-specific interstate-impact requirement. The reserved constitutional challenge fails on the established application; the conditional plea and conviction therefore remain effective.

## Enhanced alternative-path test

### Equipment-only validity versus a constitutionally identifiable mechanism

The equipment-only path's strongest argument is that Congress wrote a concrete nexus and the plea satisfies it; the offense thus differs from the original statute in Lopez. That argument establishes a statutory distinction, but it fails as a sufficient constitutional ground under Griffin/Langley. A nexus must do actual constitutional work, and the former travel of commonplace inputs does not identify why the subsequent production class falls within interstate commerce. The same inputs could be used in local conduct with no eligible relationship to a valid federal regime. All eight reject the path on this ground. They do not invalidate the statutory nexus or deny that actual movement proves it.

### Supported necessary inclusion versus remand or invalidation for an unsupported mechanism

The supported-inclusion path identifies a valid interstate object—the prohibited market in images made through actual child exploitation—and the local conduct creating the same reproducible supply. A production exemption would permit source creation outside the national supply restriction even though the resulting material can be copied and circulated without retaining a visible marker of its local origin. The Court can recognize that relationship from the product and scheme actually presented. It need not find that every local producer will distribute, because the class's inclusion addresses a mechanism by which the interstate prohibition could otherwise be evaded or materially impaired.

The strongest challenge is that this is only marketability coupled with a prediction: no sale or intended transfer by Holston is shown, and Home Builders forbids treating a comprehensive law as its own proof of necessity. The answer must preserve that boundary. The necessary connection is not merely that an image could command money, that abuse has national social costs or that Congress wishes to eliminate it everywhere. The regulated act creates the very reproducible image stock whose interstate circulation the identified regime prohibits. ## Common disposition and complete propositions

All eight Associates recognize the officer plaintiffs' standing to present their own concrete official-injury claim and affirm the rejection of the granted constitutional challenge. Each joins the following rationale in full. The regulations remain effective against this Tenth Amendment attack. There is no required remand or severance, and no additional hearing or fiscal proceeding follows.

**Official standing.** An officer who asserts a concrete burden on his own office from the challenged federal certification arrangement may challenge that asserted burden without separate authorization to represent the State. The officials here identify the work and demands directed to their offices through the certification mechanism and seek relief that would remove that mechanism. Their claim is neither abstract disagreement with federal policy nor a purported suit on behalf of the State as an institutional whole. The merits conclusion that federal law leaves refusal unconditional does not itself erase the concrete controversy concerning the arrangement's alleged imposition on their offices.

This is not standing from official title or a nonfrivolous legal label alone. The officials' own asserted work burden, connection to the specific certification process and requested relief supply the stake. The private applicants' inability to obtain certification does not substitute for that official injury. Because the officers support the same requested relief, the case needs no separate determination of the applicants' standing.

**Voluntary certification.** A federal condition requiring an applicant to obtain a local certification does not commandeer the local officer when federal law leaves that officer free to refuse unconditionally and imposes no penalty, mandatory investigation or other adverse federal consequence on the officer or office for refusal. On the supplied historical arrangement, the officer may decline the requested certification. The applicant's federal obligation does not become an official's duty simply because satisfying it would require the official's cooperation.

Constituent pressure does not change that result. The federal program may cause residents to ask their officials for assistance, and declining may be politically unpopular. Those consequences leave the officer's actual choice intact; they do not transfer the federal obligation to the local government. New York's valid access incentives likewise imposed consequences on private parties without commanding the State's regulatory response. This differs from offering two alternatives that both require an unwilling State to undertake federal responsibility.

The Court therefore affirms the judgment rejecting the challenge. The rule permits the optional certification arrangement on this record; it does not rest on a conclusion that all federal assistance commands are valid or that voluntary cooperation can supply statutory authority an agency otherwise lacks. No independent delegation, tax, privacy or compulsory-compensation question is within the supplied grant.

## Supported alternatives and response

### Standing: official injury versus an unauthorized institutional suit

The jurisdictional objection's strongest version is that the State did not authorize this suit, the officers cannot be compelled to sign and applicants' difficulties belong to someone else. Printz distinguishes those questions. State authorization would matter to a claim purporting to represent the State's institutional interests; it is not a prerequisite when the plaintiff contests a burden directed at his own office. Raines denied injury for abstract dilution after fully effective legislative votes, while expressly preserving concrete official injuries.

The officer plaintiffs' asserted certification work and demands establish the concrete stake at the supplied scope. Relief against the challenged arrangement would remove the disputed federal certification role. The Court decides the legal existence and coercive quality of the asserted duty on the merits, rather than presuming the answer against jurisdiction. No Justice dispenses with Article III, converts constituent disagreement generally into injury or uses a merits-first sequence to avoid a real standing question. Dismissal would require an independent absence of concrete injury, traceability or redressability; the supplied facts and official claim do not establish such a defect.

### Merits: functional conscription versus genuine choice

The officers' strongest merits argument is that a federal application system deliberately relies on their certification, creating requests and local political pressure that make refusal practically difficult. Because constitutional structure concerns actual operation, a court must examine the legal alternatives rather than accept an optional label. Here that examination confirms that the officer may refuse without a federal sanction or substitute mandatory task. The federal requirement falls on the applicant; federal law supplies no legal demand that the officer produce a signature, investigate or answer the request by performing the certification.

New York's take-title provision failed because neither branch left a State free to decline federal program responsibility. Its access incentives survived because the consequences of refusal fell on private generators while the State retained its choice. The present arrangement is within that latter distinction. Political accountability for exercising a genuine choice is different from federal legal compulsion.

An actual mandatory duty, sanction for refusal or materially different established practice would require examination of that burden. None is supplied here, and the Court cannot invent one to preserve the claim. Printz's prohibition on compelled executive administration does not reach a genuinely optional arrangement. Printz involved actual compulsory work and located the defect in that compulsion, not in the absence of reimbursement. Neither its judgment nor Souter's dissenting fair-value view creates a payment condition for a voluntarily undertaken certification.

## III. Speech: content, independent function and burden

The speech component is decided independently of whether the alternative route defeats the commandeering claim. An optional route does not establish a universal waiver of constitutional expression objections, and the alternative application does not make every information obligation disappear.

Section 122.34(b)(1)(i) requires educational materials **or equivalent outreach** concerning stormwater discharges' effects on water bodies and steps the public can take to reduce pollutants in runoff. The government must justify that function and fit from the actual obligation; usefulness, a factual label and presumed agreement alone are insufficient. This holding concerns the defined system-related hazard information, not all public messages a regulator regards as beneficial.

Here the substantive program regulates pollutant discharges, independently of any desire to disseminate an opinion. The State must determine, after notice and an opportunity for hearing under state procedures, that the individual cannot reasonably be expected to be discharged and return home. These are statutory predicates, not findings that any particular beneficiary satisfies them.
- For assistance received at age 55 or older, subsection (b)(1)(B) makes estate recovery mandatory for nursing-facility and home-and-community-based services and related hospital and prescription-drug services. Recovery for other plan items or services is the State's option; it is not a universal mandatory recovery command.
- Subsection (b)(1)(C) separately addresses recipients or persons entitled to long-term-care insurance benefits connected to disregarded assets or resources and requires estate recovery for nursing-facility and other long-term-care assistance. That requirement does not apply when assistance was received under a state plan with an amendment approved as of May 14, 1993 providing disregard of assets or resources to the extent of insurance payments or because the individual received or was entitled to those insurance benefits. This exception is not the general hardship exception.
- Under subsection (b)(2), recovery may occur only after any surviving spouse's death and when no surviving child under 21 remains. A surviving child also prevents recovery if blind or permanently and totally disabled in a State eligible to participate in the State program established under subchapter XVI, or blind or disabled as defined in §1382c in a State not eligible for that program. For recovery under an institutional home lien, recovery must additionally wait while either of the following qualifying occupants lawfully resides in the home and has continuously resided there lawfully since admission: a sibling who resided there for at least the year immediately before admission; or a son or daughter who resided there for at least the two years immediately before admission and establishes to the State's satisfaction that the care provided allowed the individual to remain home rather than enter an institution. The recovery-stage sibling protection does not require an equity interest; the separate pre-lien sibling protection below does. These protections restrict recovery, not the occupants' right to remain in the home, and do not exempt every adult heir.
- Subsection (b)(3) requires the state agency to establish procedures in accordance with the Secretary's standards, under which it shall waive application of the subsection, except paragraph (1)(C), when application would cause undue hardship determined under the Secretary's criteria. It is a mandatory beneficiary-level safeguard, not a discretionary excuse for the State to omit its entire recovery program.
- Subsection (b)(4)(A) includes all property and other assets within the state-probate estate. Subsection (b)(4)(B) permits inclusion of other real and personal property and assets in which the individual had a legal title or interest at death, to the extent of that interest, including conveyance to a survivor, heir or assign through joint tenancy, tenancy in common, survivorship, life estate, living trust or another arrangement. That broader inclusion becomes mandatory for an individual to whom paragraph (1)(C)(i) applies. Mandatory and optional inclusions remain distinct.
- Subsection (a)(1) generally forbids a predeath lien on account of assistance paid or to be paid under the plan. Its distinct exceptions permit a lien pursuant to a court judgment concerning incorrectly paid benefits, or a lien on the institutionalized individual's real property under the predicates in paragraph (1)(B) described above. The court-judgment exception for incorrectly paid benefits is not the estate-recovery rule for correctly paid assistance. Subsection (a)(2) separately forbids the institutional home lien while a spouse, child in the age or blindness/disability categories just stated, or a sibling with an equity interest who resided there for at least the year immediately before institutional admission lawfully resides in the home. Under subsection (a)(3), the institutional lien dissolves upon discharge and return home. These predicate provisions are described because subsection (b) incorporates the institutional class; they do not enlarge this case into a separate lien challenge.

Section 1396c requires reasonable notice and a hearing opportunity for the state agency administering or supervising the approved plan. The Secretary must then find either a changed plan no longer complying with §1396a or failure in plan administration to comply substantially with a provision of that section. On that finding, the Secretary shall notify the state agency that further payments will not be made, or, in the Secretary's discretion, that payments will be limited to categories under or parts of the plan not affected by the failure. Payments must then stop or remain so limited until the Secretary is satisfied that there will no longer be that failure to comply. The statute does not mandate a particular lesser sanction, cap withholding at administrative funds, or establish that the Secretary has exercised the discretion here. The discretionary alternative concerns continued payments to unaffected categories or parts; it does not mean withholding those unaffected payments while leaving the affected categories paid.

The estate hardship waiver and category-limited funding discretion operate at different levels. Beneficiary protection helps describe the condition's actual demand; it does not prove that a threatened statewide cutoff is noncoercive. Likewise, a possible lesser sanction defeats the claim that the statute inherently requires total withdrawal, but it is not a guarantee that every future exercise will be lawful.

## Adversarial paths

**Affirmance path.** Program-related recovery with its actual qualifications and a sanction scheme permitting lawful narrower applications does not establish facial compulsion on the supplied record. Kansas rejects substituting substantial dollar amounts or political difficulty for an examination of actual choice. The strong counterargument is that a threat to end an established health program can itself compel compliance before any formal sanction, and statutory discretion offers cold comfort if the credible governmental demand is total withdrawal. That objection is legally sound in principle; it does not become an established fact here. The supplied warning is all-or-part, the statute retains the category route, and the record does not establish that HHS has closed that route or made a present demand that practically destroys choice. No statistics-only evidentiary demand is imposed.

**Invalidation or remand path.** A supported present demand leaving no genuine practical choice could warrant relief despite formal voluntariness, clear notice, relatedness, or a small recovery requirement. The strongest basis would be concrete operational evidence of the threatened funding consequence and practical inability to refuse; a compelled total loss would be especially material, but total loss is not a categorical prerequisite to coercion. The counterargument is that the Court would be deciding a different sanction application and assuming facts not supplied in this facial case. The present record does not establish such a demand. A remand merely to search for a different theory would convert an operator's limited source account into an invented evidentiary entitlement. These commitments therefore reject that path for this judgment while preserving the applicable constitutional rule.

An interpretation limiting §1396c to administrative funds is not a third lawful route: it lacks support in the enacted alternatives. Nor may Kansas's different statute supply a numerical Medicaid cap. Judicial review of a concrete sanction is the relevant limit, not textual rewriting.
