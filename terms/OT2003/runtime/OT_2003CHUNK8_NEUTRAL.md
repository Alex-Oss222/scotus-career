* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-06-21

## 105. Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws
* **Citation or Docket:** 332 F.3d 769 (4th Cir. 2003)
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment, including any necessary threshold disposition.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-06-23
* **Question Presented:** Whether a single district judge may dismiss a constitutional congressional-apportionment claim for failure to state a claim without convening the three-judge court required by §2284, and whether alleged district shapes adequately support the preserved partisan claim.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit judgment affirming a single judge's dismissal of the Fourteenth Amendment partisan-gerrymandering claim.
* **Relief Requested:** The opposing dispositions and their concrete consequences are stated in C and D below.
* **Material Record:** Duckworth challenged Maryland's 2002 congressional map, alleging that division of Anne Arundel County among four unusually shaped districts intentionally impaired Republican participation. The district judge compared a prior challenge to the earlier map and dismissed without convening three judges. The court of appeals rejected aspects of that comparison but equated failure to state a claim with insubstantiality and affirmed. Article I and First Amendment theories were not pursued on appeal.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Only the Fourteenth Amendment claim remains preserved on appeal; Article I and independent First Amendment theories were abandoned. Section 2284's tribunal requirement precedes ordinary merits sufficiency unless the constitutional claim is wholly insubstantial or jurisdiction otherwise absent. Vieth v. Jubelirer, decided April 28, 2004, governs the preserved claim at its actual scope.
* **Relevant Dates:** Lower-court judgment: June 19, 2003. Supplied simulation event: June 23, 2004, after Vieth v. Jubelirer was decided on April 28, 2004.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Court reviews the actual judgment and preserved questions. Historical lower-court reasoning is a record fact; its account of Supreme Court doctrine does not displace controlling law.

### Issue 1: Statutory tribunal and insubstantiality

* **Controlling Law:** the special-tribunal rules distinguish a claim plainly foreclosed by controlling law from a live substantial question expected to fail. Shaw v. Reno does not create a freestanding racial-design claim in this Court, and district shape alone cannot establish the independent partisan injury. Vieth v. Jubelirer holds the partisan equal-protection claim justiciable: the claimant must establish an identified political capacity, a material impairment of it, a substantial motivating purpose to disadvantage the affected voters because of their political affiliation, and boundary-specific causation, subject to the State's same-decision defense. Political design or irregular shape alone remains insufficient, and ordinary notice pleading requires no merits proof, expert model or replacement map.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Test jurisdiction and constitutional substantiality before allocating the merits. A merits prediction cannot substitute for the tribunal Congress required. Preserve the actual claim and existing pleading rules.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2284, particularly the limitation on a single judge's merits authority; Fourteenth Amendment; Goosby v. Osser; then-operative civil pleading rules; Davis v. Bandemer's justiciability holding as retained in Vieth v. Jubelirer.
* **Material Other Authority:** The public authorities identified in E perform the historical or statutory work specified here; their account of displaced Supreme Court doctrine is not imported.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Substantial constitutional challenges to congressional apportionment; jurisdictionally defective, wholly frivolous or clearly foreclosed claims remain outside compulsory three-judge merits adjudication.
* **Open Point:** Whether ordinary failure to state a claim suffices for one-judge dismissal, and whether this particular theory is substantial under Vieth's rule.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** Alleged shape and party composition did not adequately establish the required discriminatory effect; a claim failing Rule 12(b)(6) was treated as insubstantial for §2284.
* **Duckworth:** Congress assigned substantial constitutional apportionment challenges to three judges; a single judge cannot decide the merits by relabeling an arguably deficient pleading jurisdictionally insubstantial. He seeks proper tribunal adjudication.
* **Officials:** The claim merely alleges that one party will lose in oddly drawn districts and is foreclosed or insufficient under controlling law. They seek affirmance.
* **Limits:** Assignment to three judges establishes neither a viable gerrymandering claim nor entitlement to redistricting.

* **Solicitor General, Amici, and Concessions:** No independently verified amicus proposal distinct from the parties is relied upon; no oral-argument concession is attributed without a transcript.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Consequences:** If the constitutional claim is substantial, vacatur and convening the required tribunal preserves merits review without finding discriminatory intent or effect. A genuinely frivolous or clearly foreclosed theory can be dismissed by one judge. Ordinary Rule 12(b)(6) weakness is not automatically that threshold. Vieth's justiciability holding and elements identify which category this claim occupies.

* **Record and Legal Uncertainty:** Vieth forecloses treating partisan districting claims as categorically nonjusticiable; whether these allegations adequately plead its elements is a merits question unless the theory is wholly insubstantial. Ordinary merits weakness alone does not answer that jurisdictional question.

Landgraf requires attention to enacted temporal direction and separate constitutional constraints. Wheeling Bridge concerns future operation of prospective relief, not an automatic power to cancel completed damages judgments.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Plaut's finality rule is confined to legislative commands requiring an Article III court to vacate a private judgment that has become final through the ordinary judicial process and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed. Congress may still change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, and change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, as Wheeling Bridge illustrates. It may not require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability in any case; Klein and McCullough v. Virginia forbid that legislative adjudication. Relief from a final judgment through constitutionally permissible judicial procedures, including valid Rule 60(b) grounds, remains available. Section 27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review and due process remain undecided.
* **Open Point:** Whether the plaintiffs' reading depends on an enacted change of law or instead treats unenacted statements as judicial commands. This record does not present application of §27A(b) or a new congressional command to vacate a completed final judgment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The amendments remove the case-specific sovereign-immunity obstacle but do not enact abrogation of the Accords. The United States may intervene to protect its own international obligations and seek vacatur. The Ministry is treated as Iran itself, so naming it does not avoid the agreement's bar. Explanatory statements cannot supply statutory language that Congress did not enact. The court reserved broader cause-of-action and separation-of-powers questions unnecessary to affirmance.
* **Petitioners:** Congress identified this litigation and sought to permit recovery; construing the amendments to leave the Accords dispositive allegedly defeats their intended operation. The explanatory statements concerning the existing judgment support reading the statutes as removing the Government's entire obstacle to relief. Petitioners seek restoration of the suit and an opportunity to obtain damages rather than merely a declaration that one immunity condition has changed.
* **United States:** The immunity amendment does the specific work its text prescribes without extinguishing an independent agreement-based bar. A technical correction and unenacted explanatory statement cannot abrogate an international commitment or command preservation of a default. The Government's intervention protects its own obligations. The Flatow cause-of-action question supplies an additional, distinct objection; it need not be reached if the Accords remain dispositive.
* **Alternative Grounds and Limits:** The claim against the Ministry does not rest on a legally distinct private actor. A statute authorizing payment of a qualifying judgment does not establish a right to obtain that judgment. A failure to find statutory abrogation does not hold that Congress lacks power to enact it. Neither the text nor the procedural record supplies a legislative command reopening a completed judgment finally disposing of the action.
* **Solicitor General, Amici, and Concessions:** The United States' opposition in No. 03-1147 supplies its statutory and agreement-based position, including the separate cause-of-action objection. No oral-argument concession is attributed without a verified transcript, and no independent amicus proposal is treated as controlling.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Consequences:** Affirmance is available if the immunity amendments leave the Accords' independent bar operative; that path preserves dismissal without adjudicating every alternative cause-of-action issue or the constitutional validity of every targeted statute. Reversal on statutory construction would require identifying enacted language that displaces the Accords and would ordinarily return remaining jurisdictional, cause-of-action and damages questions for independent adjudication, rather than automatically entering a money judgment. A separately reached Article III challenge must distinguish a genuine change in law from a command to decide the pending case under unchanged law. Plaut's finality rule protects a private judgment that has become final through the ordinary judicial process against a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation; it preserves Congress's power to change the law governing pending cases, subject to the bar on legislatively dictated findings or liability, and is not a general prohibition on legislation affecting identified litigation. The liability default and unresolved damages here do not themselves present that completed-judgment question.
