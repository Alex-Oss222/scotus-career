## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Vieth v. Jubelirer
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1580; 541 U.S. 267.
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-04-28
* **Question Presented:** Whether partisan congressional-districting claims are justiciable, whether subordinating neutral criteria to partisan advantage presumptively violates equal protection, and whether a projected supermajority of seats without a statewide vote majority violates Article I.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Three-judge Middle District of Pennsylvania, dismissing the partisan claim; the revised map’s population issue was separately resolved.
* **Relief Requested:** The voters seek reversal, adjudication of the partisan claim and a lawful replacement map if a violation is proved.
* **Material Record:** The appeal concerns Act 34, the replacement for Act 1. Act 1 was held malapportioned but governed the 2002 election under a stay; Act 34 was approved for subsequent elections. Plaintiffs renewed their partisan challenge to Act 34. The dismissal record establishes no constitutional partisan injury or continuity finding between the plans.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must identify personal district-related injury and a live claim to Act 34. First Amendment association theories require preservation or an amendment allowed by ordinary procedure. A stayed election under an adjudged unlawful map does not make that map a lawful comparison.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged maps followed the 2000 census; the final lower disposition was entered January 24, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Martinez v. Bush follows the next day on the calendar and concerns a trial record involving Florida, racial dilution and partisan claims. Its facts cannot establish Pennsylvania liability.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Justiciability and personal partisan injury

* **Controlling Law:** Davis v. Bandemer preserves justiciability without a comprehensive majority merits rule. Shaw v. Reno and Miller v. Johnson reject district design alone as injury. Holder v. Hall requires an independently grounded lawful comparison in its statutory context; Cook v. Gralike rejects official ballot penalties for political positions, not every partisan district.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep standing, a legally recognized injury, purpose, map causation and remedy separate. Gaffney v. Cummings permits ordinary political consideration; seat disproportionality alone supplies no right to a different delegation.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §§2 and 4; First and Fourteenth Amendments; 28 U.S.C. §§1253 and 2284.
* **Material Other Authority:** Baker v. Carr supplies justiciability. Mt. Healthy City School District v. Doyle provides a causation and same-decision analogy; its precise extension to districting remains open.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law permits judicial adjudication of actual civic injury and protects political participation, but does not already adopt the proposed representation or association tests.
* **Open Point:** The administrable elements of a personal congressional-districting injury, and their application to the operative plan and lawful historical comparison.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The court found the allegations insufficient under the existing partisan-dilution formulation.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Voters seek a rule keyed to predominant partisan purpose, packing and cracking, and interference with translating electoral support into representation.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Officials argue that neither proportionality nor judicial assessment of acceptable partisan advantage supplies a legal standard and seek dismissal.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** A claim may be justiciable yet insufficiently pleaded or proved. Population equality is analytically distinct and cannot alone dispose of partisan injury.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** A justiciable rule can allow an adequately pleaded claim to proceed while leaving proof and remedy for the trial court. A failure-of-proof or failure-to-state-a-claim ruling need not foreclose the entire category. A categorical political-question ruling would dismiss all such constitutional claims, leaving distinct racial, population and statutory protections intact. No path supports an immediate Court-drawn map on the present factual record.
