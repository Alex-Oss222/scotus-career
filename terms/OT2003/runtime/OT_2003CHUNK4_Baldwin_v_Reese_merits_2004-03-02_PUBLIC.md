## Event

Baldwin v. Reese, No. 02-964; merits. Argued December 8, 2003; decided March 2, 2004. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which found fair presentation because the Oregon Supreme Court could consult a lower opinion when considering discretionary review. The question concerns federal notice of Reese’s appellate-counsel claim in the proper state submissions. His federal habeas application was filed in 1998.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit fair-presentation judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 8–1. Supporting: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Justice Stevens. The appellate court must apply the actual procedural record and existing presentation, availability, default and gateway rules to any remaining properly preserved issue.

## Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Justice Stevens filed a dissent on fair notice and the Court’s discretionary refusal to decide the late equivalence contention.

## Holdings

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner ordinarily fails to fairly present a federal claim when the petition, brief or properly incorporated submission does not identify its federal basis and the state court would have to search a separate lower-court opinion to discover it. Clear federal notice may be supplied by a constitutional provision, pertinent federal authority or an understandable description of the claim; no particular citation or verbal formula is indispensable.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Stevens dissents on the application to Reese's actual petition.

**Controlling explanation:** Picard requires a fair opportunity to decide the federal claim itself. Boerckel carries that claim-specific obligation through ordinary available state review, and Castille distinguishes proper presentation from a claim's mere presence elsewhere in a record. A discretionary-review court must be able to identify the claims it is asked to decide from the papers properly submitted.

Reese's petition mentions trial and appellate assistance, but its express Fifth, Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment references concern coercion to waive a jury. It leaves the distinct appellate-assistance allegation without federal identification. The postconviction opinion's Jones v. Barnes citation does not cure that omission: the intervening appellate order was a summary affirmance, and the requirement to attach that appellate decision does not establish attachment or incorporation of the underlying postconviction opinion.

The dissent reads the petition's constitutional references collectively with its description of counsel's failings. The Court instead reads the specific references in their stated setting. Treating possible access to another opinion as enough would replace meaningful notice with possible discovery and require the court to reconstruct the federal claim. The record establishes neither proper incorporation nor actual judicial consideration supplying the missing notice.

**Preserved scope:** The argument that identical state and federal assistance standards independently established notice was first advanced in the merits brief here and was not addressed below. The Court recognizes discretion to consider an included legal argument but declines to decide this newly presented equivalence contention under Rule 15.2. Its complexity, broader implications and absence of lower-court treatment support that choice; the Court does not decide Guinn’s ultimate effect on equivalence or fair notice. The remand applies the actual presentation and state-remedy record, and ordinary default and gateway law, to any remaining properly preserved issue.

## Precedent Treatment

Picard v. Connor is applied to meaningful notice of the federal claim. O’Sullivan v. Boerckel is applied to claim-specific presentation through ordinary available review without importing its preenactment posture into this 1998 petition. Duncan v. Henry retains the federal-nature notice requirement. Castille v. Peoples is applied to proper presentation rather than mere inclusion elsewhere in a record. United States v. X-Citement Video and Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corporation retain permission to consider an included legal defense of the same judgment; neither compels consideration of the late equivalence argument here.

## Law After Decision

State courts must receive clear federal notice through the properly presented papers, including valid incorporation. They need not reconstruct an unstated federal theory by searching a separate opinion. Clear substance controls over a mandatory citation formula; the governing procedural consequences remain those of the actual state-review record.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents. He would exercise the Court’s discretion to consider Guinn and finds its Oregon-law context sufficient for fair notice on these papers. Oregon’s highest court discussed appellate assistance under both constitutions and supplied a unified formulation for failure to raise an appellate issue. A short petition expressly complaining of trial and appellate assistance, within a federal constitutional challenge to representation and conviction, would naturally alert that court to the recognized claim. Guinn itself involved express dual pleading and an omitted-shackling issue, but those qualifications do not defeat the contextual notice Stevens finds here. He does not require universal identity of every state and federal element or a search through all lower opinions. He would affirm federal consideration of the appellate-assistance claim.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for further proceedings consistent with the fair-presentation ruling and the actual closed state-review route. Existing default and gateway rules govern any remaining properly preserved issue. The late equivalence contention remains undecided on this review.
