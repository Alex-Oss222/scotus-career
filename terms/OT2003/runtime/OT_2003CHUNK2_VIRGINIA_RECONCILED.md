The historical Court granted Virginia's relief, seven to two. O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the Court; Stevens and Kennedy dissented. The short comparator supplement omits a material topology detail: there were two dissents, Stevens joined by Kennedy and Kennedy joined by Stevens. Both substantive objections must be represented accurately if they remain relevant in assembly.

The majority construed the Award's reservation as Virginia's sovereign right, not merely the ordinary private riparian entitlement of individual owners; the navigation and proper-use limitations did not confer a unilateral Maryland licensing power. It rejected the asserted acquiescence on the supported protest history. Its footnote 4 expressly recognized that the 1958 Compact superseded the older agreement while preserving Article Seventh rights. The independent model's explicit enacted savings-clause analysis is therefore compatible with the complete historical opinion, despite the short supplement's omission.

Stevens argued that ordinary riparian property rights coexist with the river sovereign's police power, and that Fairfax's municipal-supply use is not equivalent to an individual owner's domestic withdrawal. Kennedy argued that a later determination of title can confirm its earlier existence, so the historical dispute itself did not eliminate Maryland's sovereignty; the Compact guaranteed access but did not unmistakably surrender reasonable regulation, and the Award distinguished full dominion over the shore from qualified use beyond it. He rejected automatic application of ordinary interstate-water apportionment doctrine to a river owned wholly by one State. Both would dismiss the broad complaint, while Kennedy expressly preserved review of regulation that effectively excludes Virginia or improperly regulates its out-of-state development.

These are legally distinct emphases in the shared alternative construction, not a reason to add unpresented claims. The reconciled decision uses the frozen record and instruments; it does not promote new comparative historical recitations into factual findings or import an unvalidated project-use adjudication from the dissents.

## Retained components

**Regulatory authority.** O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer retain V1. Maryland's riverbed title and Virginia's instrument-secured use coexist. The Award reserves Virginia itself the use necessary to enjoyment of riparian ownership, rather than a privilege dependent on Maryland's prior permission. The distinct allocation of authority in the historical Compact and its preserved shore rights reinforce the conclusion. The 1958 savings clause preserves rather than enlarges these rights. The six support a declaration that Virginia, its subdivisions and citizens may make the protected withdrawals and appurtenant improvements without the challenged Maryland permit requirement, subject to navigation, Maryland's proper use and applicable federal authority. This does not allocate a volume for every future withdrawal or authorize harm to Maryland.

Stevens and Kennedy retain M1 and refuse V1. They would sustain the regulatory-authority exception and dismiss the requested categorical exemption claim. The protected entitlement constrains compatible Maryland regulation but does not make every advance permit requirement invalid. A permit decision cannot nullify access or serve as an extraterritorial veto over Virginia's development. Their disposition does not resolve the validity of an unpresented particular future regulation or create an unbounded Maryland power.

**Acquiescence.** The six V1 adherents retain A1: the supported applications and administrative cooperation, considered with official awareness and protests, do not establish accepted displacement of Virginia's reserved entitlement. New Jersey supplies the supported adverse-exercise, notice and acquiescence analysis; the present ruling does not declare every administrative application irrelevant, prescribe a universal formal-protest requirement or invent a minimum period. The historical majority's preponderance formulation describes that asserted affirmative defense, but the model need not create a new universal burden for every regulatory dispute to conclude the supplied facts fail it. No result turns on choosing a higher proof burden.

Stevens and Kennedy retain their conditional agreement that this record does not independently establish surrender of an exemption if the instruments supplied one. They deny that antecedent construction. Do not turn their conditional observation into a full join of the majority's sovereignty or acquiescence opinion, or describe the final authority as unanimous on an exemption they reject.

**Instrument and remedy limits.** Preserve each separate clause: the shore-owner improvement right and navigation condition; the distinct common-fishing right and its hindrance/disturbance and nets/seines qualifications; the Award's navigation and proper-use constraints; the 1958 Article VII section 1 preservation, distinct sections 2–3 fisheries provisions, Article VIII no-unilateral-alteration condition, Article IX replacement, and the federal reservations. Historical Article Eighth informs interpretation but does not establish a new present mutual-consent rule for every subject. The original proceeding resolves the continuing permitting controversy; the eventual individual permit does not dispose of it. Mootness and nontidal coverage were not additional exceptions pursued here. No quantitative or damages remedy is invented.
