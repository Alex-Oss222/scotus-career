## 69. Engine Manufacturers Association v. South Coast Air Quality Management District

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for application-specific preemption analysis.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the purchase-versus-sale exemption; require the lower courts to distinguish regulatory mandates from genuine proprietary purchases and decide preserved exceptions.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged Fleet Rules and their proved preempted applications. The initial legal correction does not establish that every rule must be enjoined.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify each operative criterion, affected vehicle category and asserted exemption, then enjoin enforcement of any conflicting regulatory standard. It must explain which provisions remain enforceable and why.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Purchase restrictions as emission standards

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Clean Air Act §209(a) preempts local fleet-purchase requirements based on vehicle emissions merely because those requirements govern buyers rather than manufacturers or sellers.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply enacted preemption without expanding a rule directed at state regulation automatically to every purchasing choice.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutory standard needs a rule independent of enforcement technique. An all-purchases ban would outrun the preserved questions. A single-fleet exception would leave the dispositive legal error intact.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A mandatory emission criterion for new vehicles does not cease to be a standard under §209(a) because the command is directed to purchasers. Courts must examine the criterion and each challenged application, including any genuine proprietary choice or statutory exception, before determining the proper injunction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Regulatory fleet-purchase mandates tied to emissions of covered new vehicles; proprietary and other statutory defenses retain independent analysis.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the reserved proprietary applications, courts apply §209 to actual regulatory standards and separately determine whether its text reaches the challenged governmental purchase.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cipollone and Medtronic require analysis of the legal duty displaced, not its caption. A command to buy only vehicles satisfying a locally selected emission criterion can enforce the same criterion as a sales restriction. The proprietary distinction preserved in Boston Harbor does not decide the Clean Air Act question without examining this statute and transaction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Cipollone v. Liggett Group and Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr are applied. Building and Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders and Contractors is used only for the genuine-proprietor distinction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unresolved proprietary and rule-specific exceptions require findings and were not adjudicated below.
