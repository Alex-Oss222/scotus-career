## Judgment and full common ground

All eight Associates reverse the Iowa Supreme Court's federal mandatory-warning ruling and its resulting direction to exclude the first conviction and enter the lesser judgment. They remand for proceedings under the correct waiver rule. Each joins this rule, the disposition of the presented collateral attack and the remedy in full.

The Sixth Amendment requires a knowing, intelligent and voluntary relinquishment of counsel before an uncounseled guilty plea leading to imprisonment. The defendant must understand the right to counsel at the plea stage, the nature of the charge and the consequences of the plea; the necessary advice depends on the stage and actual circumstances. It does not invariably require the two additional warnings that a lawyer may discover an overlooked defense and may offer an independent view of whether to plead guilty. Omitting those prescribed warnings is therefore not itself a federal constitutional basis to exclude Tovar's first conviction from enhancement.

Godinez separates capacity from actual informed waiver. Johnson v. Zerbst requires intentional relinquishment of the known right; Patterson makes the adequacy of information sensitive to the proceeding in which counsel is waived. Faretta's warning about the dangers of conducting one's own trial addresses the distinct tasks of trial representation and does not prescribe these two warnings for every guilty plea. The meaningful constitutional requirement is understanding the counsel right and the plea, not an invariant prediction of every assistance counsel might provide. The two warnings may be useful and a State may require more protective colloquies, but usefulness does not establish their constitutional indispensability.

The strongest contrary argument is that a defendant cannot decide to forgo counsel without being told that counsel evaluates defenses and the plea decision independently. That concern supports an actual-understanding inquiry; it does not show that every defendant, regardless of the charge and circumstances, lacks the requisite understanding without these particular warnings. The rule continues to require knowledge of counsel's availability before and at the plea. A warning about trial counsel cannot simply be declared equivalent to that knowledge in every case.

## Application and collateral posture

Tovar's first conviction imposed two days of jail, so valid waiver matters. But his presented collateral theory does not assert that he actually lacked awareness of the right to counsel before and at arraignment; it suggests only a possible mistaken belief. The established omission of the two nonmandatory warnings does not prove that possibility. His written brief describes an initial defendant showing that the prior uncounseled conviction lacked a valid waiver, distinguishes direct review, and invokes a preponderance requirement for asserted facts on collateral review. Watts independently supplies Iowa's preponderance rule for postconviction factual assertions, while its counseled-plea facts are different. Neither source converts a speculative possibility into an established waiver defect.

The disposition accordingly resolves the actual warning-based attack and removes the resulting exclusion and lesser-judgment direction. It does not reopen an unasserted actual-unawareness theory merely because the Court rejects the categorical rule. The lower court proceeds with the enhancement case under the correct constitutional rule and its otherwise applicable law. This is not an appellate factual finding that every aspect of the old colloquy was ideal or a universal allocation of all enhancement burdens.

Parke permits its actual collateral burden sequence but concerns an earlier counseled conviction and does not automatically validate an uncounseled plea. Its former section 2254 factual-presumption regime does not govern this direct review of the present state enhancement proceeding. The present rule instead decides the legal insufficiency of the warning-based objection on the supported submissions, preserving actual waiver as a distinct constitutional requirement.
