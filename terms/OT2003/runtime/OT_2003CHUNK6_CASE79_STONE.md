## 79. Tennessee Student Assistance Corporation v. Hood

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the immunity motion and remand for the dischargeability determination.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow Hood to prove undue hardship under §523(a)(8), with notice and an opportunity for TSAC to contest the statutory showing.
* **Remedial Reach:** Hood’s debt and bankruptcy discharge. No affirmative damages or recovery of state property is awarded.
* **Practical Relief:** The bankruptcy court must schedule the hardship adjudication, permit the creditor to present its position, and enter a discharge determination binding as bankruptcy law authorizes. The loan remains subject to the statutory discharge exception unless Hood meets the applicable burden.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Bankruptcy discharge and state immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether state sovereign immunity bars a bankruptcy proceeding to determine undue hardship and discharge a state-held student-loan debt.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply controlling Article I law and recognize the independent in rem character of the relief; do not reopen broader immunity precedent unnecessarily.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. Debt discharge is the actual requested relief. A rule authorizing every affirmative action against a state creditor would exceed it. Treating this particular summons as dispositive would mistake procedure for the jurisdictional character of discharge.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A bankruptcy court may determine whether a state-held student loan is discharged under §523(a)(8) without the State’s consent when the requested relief determines the debtor’s discharge rather than an affirmative personal recovery. The debtor must establish undue hardship under the governing statutory standard; immunity does not replace that merits inquiry. Congress’s express authorization also fits the Article I enforcement rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Dischargeability of the state-held debt in the bankruptcy case; an affirmative treasury claim requires its own source and remedy analysis.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a distinct affirmative-recovery claim, courts apply the Article I enforcement holding and the relevant statutory authorization, without treating this discharge ruling as a damages award.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida Prepaid permits clear private enforcement of valid Article I obligations in this Court, and §106 expressly addresses bankruptcy provisions. More immediately, the federal court administers the discharge of an estate subject to bankruptcy jurisdiction. The creditor’s decision not to participate cannot create a different discharge rule, although the debtor must still prove the exception’s requirements.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank is applied. No historical limitation on Article I abrogation is imported.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Affirmative monetary recovery against the State is outside the requested relief; actual hardship remains to be found below.
