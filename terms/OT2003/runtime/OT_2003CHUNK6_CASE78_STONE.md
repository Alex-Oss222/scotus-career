## 78. Lipscomb v. Federal Labor Relations Authority

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment permitting FLRA’s representation proceeding.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested injunction against the election and apply federal labor law to the civilian employment function.
* **Remedial Reach:** The covered technicians, their employing organization and the official acting in that federal capacity. No damages judgment or command to regulate private state residents is authorized.
* **Practical Relief:** The Guard and adjutant general must cooperate with the lawful representation process on FLRA’s schedule. Objections to a particular bargaining subject must use the applicable statutory procedure and identify an actual military or management exclusion.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal civilian employment and official capacity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Federal Labor Relations Authority may require a state National Guard organization to permit a union-representation election among civilian technicians designated by federal law as federal employees, and whether applying those labor obligations violates the Tenth Amendment or state sovereign immunity.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the own-activity principle to the federal statutory capacity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The dual-capacity distinction must govern the employment relationship consistently. General federal control of state military operations would exceed the record. An election-only exception without identifying the employer’s capacity would leave the source of the obligation unexplained.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state National Guard organization and adjutant general administering federally designated civilian technicians act in the federal employment capacity Congress created and must comply with the applicable federal labor statute in that capacity. Neither the Tenth Amendment nor state sovereign immunity defeats this representation proceeding merely because the employer also holds state military authority. The objector must identify a specific statutory exclusion or distinct sovereign function; none defeats this election.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Federal civilian technician employment and the presented representation process; military command and particular bargaining exclusions retain their statutory limits.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a particular military-management bargaining restriction, courts apply the express labor statute and its exclusions without treating civilian representation as authority over military command.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reno v. Condon permits regulation of a government’s covered operations; Printz forbids a different act, compelling sovereign administration for others. Congress defined these workers as federal employees, and the challenged direction governs that accepted employment role. The official does not acquire immunity from federal employer obligations by also being a state official.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reno v. Condon is applied; Printz v. United States is distinguished by capacity. Feres v. United States does not govern civilian representation rights.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The legality of particular bargaining demands is outside this election record.
