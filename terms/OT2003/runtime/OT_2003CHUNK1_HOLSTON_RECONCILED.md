## Confirmed propositions and support

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each directly join all four propositions and affirmance:

1. Prior interstate movement of commonplace recording equipment or media does not alone provide constitutional authority for prosecuting the later local production. It can satisfy the statutory condition Congress enacted, but the Constitution requires its own eligible mechanism.
2. Congress may include local production of recordings of actual child abuse in the identified interstate image-market prohibition when that production supplies the same reproducible image stock, and its exclusion would permit evasion or materially impair the valid interstate restriction. The supplied actual-product, reproduction and concealment relationship supports that conclusion for the presented class.
3. The necessary relationship is materially supported here by the actual recordings, actual minors, identified corresponding interstate market and the source role of local creation. This is a legal inference from the supplied facts and regime, not a new finding of Holston's sale, distribution, intent, individual interstate impact, market share or the accuracy of a legislative statistic. Neither general harmfulness, marketability alone nor the enactment's comprehensiveness is the ground.
4. The enacted materials-movement element remains independently required and is established in this application. The constitutional ruling neither deletes it nor inserts a new defendant-specific sale, transmission or substantial-effect requirement. The reserved challenge fails; the judgment and conviction are affirmed.

The adopted common inference requires the production of the actual regulated supply, not simply local activity associated with a potentially traded item. The opinion must explain how excluding that source class would impair the identified interstate prohibition. Its conclusions do not extend automatically to every manufactured object, all local possession, virtual imagery, every expressive product or all crimes Congress wishes to suppress.
