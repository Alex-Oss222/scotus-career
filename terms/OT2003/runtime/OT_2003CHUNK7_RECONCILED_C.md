## 103. Pliler v. Ford

### Why the provisional alignment changes

The full historical writings expose two material discrepancies in the provisional commitments. First, Stevens and Souter historically agreed with the substantive views of both dissents and joined only the equitable-tolling remand judgment. They did not join the no-warning rationale. Second, Ginsburg and Breyer sought affirmance to the extent the Ninth Circuit had vacated the second-petition dismissals; disagreement with the Ninth Circuit's relation-back mechanism did not make them supporters of the Court's vacatur of that relief.

The historical premises survive the governing law and the original predecision record. Duncan still excludes federal pendency from section 2244(d)(2), but leaves lawful equity and retention open. Its published Stevens/Souter concurrence affirmatively distinguishes exhaustion from a categorical prohibition on protecting a timely opportunity; its Breyer/Ginsburg dissent emphasizes the arbitrary forfeiture that nonmerits federal delay can cause and preserves lawful equity even when the statutory majority controls. Castro regulates unsolicited first-section-2255 conversion, not every chosen section 2254 filing. Its current narrow holding neither automatically imposes Ford's warnings nor prevents an analogous court-accuracy argument. Its scope is not a materially different premise sufficient to explain a new Stevens/Souter rejection of every pertinent notice duty.

The preserved record already includes Ford's stay requests, the two options concerning dismissal or deletion, the five days remaining when the first petitions were filed, and expiration before the later dismissal orders. It separately identifies Loguercio's affirmative election and Weed's nonresponse. Missing original district papers are not evidence that the court's described communications did not occur; the published recitations support the bounded review. Conversely, those recitations do not establish every element of eventual equitable relief. There is no new record, source gap or changed preservation premise sufficient to turn Ginsburg and Breyer's partial-affirmance position into the provisional vacatur position.

Accordingly, the unsupported departures are corrected. Stevens and Souter retain their judgment but withdraw the provisional no-duty rationale and proposed six-Associate rationale coalition. Ginsburg and Breyer change their provisional remedy position to partial affirmance of the vacatur of the repeated-claim dismissals and replace the generic-risk-warning formulation with the more specific available-option and misleading-election objection. These changes are reasoned reconciliations of unchanged premises, not automatic substitution of a historical vote.

### Precisely separated issues

The two particular advisements required below were: the court could consider Ford's requested stay only if he deleted the unexhausted claims; and, where the period had expired, a dismissal followed by state exhaustion would make the federal return untimely absent equitable tolling. The four no-required-advisement Associates reject making those two advisements compulsory. They do not resolve the general legality or conditions of stay-and-abeyance because it was not employed and their warning disposition does not depend on its validity.

The contrary substantive position distinguishes counseling from a court's accurate presentation of available procedures. Ford requested preservation of a timely federal opportunity while completing state review. The proposed three-step route deletes unexhausted claims, retains and stays the exhausted claims, and permits lawful amendment after exhaustion; its central difference from the Ninth Circuit's remedy here is that an original petition remains pending. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the legal availability of this protective procedure within ordinary case-management and amendment rules and oppose treating Rose's comity requirement as an absolute bar. They would require disclosure of that available requested option when the court presents the prisoner's alternatives. No compulsory stay in every mixed case, unlimited amendment, or immunity from independent timing requirements follows. Abusive delay may be controlled by conditions on the stay.

For those four, the already elapsed period makes a court-presented dismissal-and-return option materially misleading without an accurate explanation of that risk. This is more specific than a universal obligation to calculate deadlines or recommend which claims to abandon. It is also materially different from the four opposing Associates' conclusion that accurate nonmerits terminology and omitted advice alone do not establish misleading conduct.

The merits of the two advice requirements do not themselves establish a still-pending first application, an actual stay or a lawful reopened proceeding. The no-advisement path removes the lower judgment's stated improper-dismissal predicate and therefore its resulting automatic relation-back relief. It does not decide whether a later petition could relate back after an independently established improper dismissal and a lawful procedural correction. The historical Court expressly left that latter relation-back question unanswered. Neither the provisional file nor assembly may turn this scoped consequence into a categorical holding against every such relation-back route.
