## Event

Norton v. Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance, No. 03-101; 542 U.S. 55. Merits decision, June 14, 2004. Argued March 29, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, which restored challenges to BLM inaction concerning wilderness protection, land-use-plan commitments and supplemental environmental analysis of off-road-vehicle use. The organizations seek orders under APA §706(1). The San Rafael route-designation plan and the Henry Mountains use-supervision files have been completed. A distinct claim seeks continuing intensive monitoring under the Henry Mountains plan, and the organizations also seek supplementation of environmental analysis for already approved plans.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment restoring the live §706(1) theories is reversed. The case is remanded for their dismissal and dismissal as moot of the two completed initial-act demands.

## Holdings

##### I. Section 706(1) requires a discrete act that governing law makes obligatory

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 706(1) authorizes a court to compel a discrete agency act that governing law requires and the agency has unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed. The claimant must identify the act and establish that legal obligation and withholding or delay; a court may compel a required decision while leaving its permissible substance to the agency, but may not undertake continuing supervision of an administrative program.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Parts I–IV and the live-claim mandate of Thomas's opinion. The same coalition supplies authority for the live-claim holdings below.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 551(13) identifies agency action through circumscribed forms—rules, orders, licenses, sanctions, relief and their equivalents, denials or failures. Section 706(1) reaches an unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed act of that kind, not every asserted shortcoming in administration. Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation rejects wholesale improvement of a program through judicial review. Lincoln distinguishes legal constraints from discretionary management, and Bennett requires the identified legal duty and appropriate review channel. Together those sources support the rule stated here, rather than a commission to improve administration.

Discretion over a required decision's content does not erase the duty to decide. Conversely, leaving nominal choice of means to the agency does not make a decree lawful if a court must continually decide whether an entire program is satisfactory. A specific statutory deadline is not indispensable to every unreasonable-delay claim. The decisive questions remain what act governing law requires and whether that act has unlawfully been withheld or delayed.

**Precedent treatment:** National Wildlife Federation's action-specific review boundary is applied; Lincoln and Bennett's source-and-duty distinctions are developed into the stated §706(1) rule.

##### II. The wilderness standard does not specify the act demanded by this program-wide claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1782(c)'s binding obligation to preserve wilderness suitability does not identify a discrete omitted act that can sustain the organizations' demand for judicial supervision of BLM's wilderness-management program. That §706(1) demand must be dismissed, while the statutory management standard retains its force.

The standard governs during review and until Congress determines otherwise. It permits continued mining, grazing and mineral leasing in the manner and degree existing on October 21, 1976, while separately requiring action against unnecessary or undue degradation or to afford environmental protection. Unless previously withdrawn, the lands remain open to appropriation under mining law during review, subject to withdrawal under §1714 for reasons other than preservation of wilderness character. Following congressional wilderness designation, the Wilderness Act's national-forest wilderness rules govern, including mineral surveys, development, access, land exchange and claimant ingress and egress.

**Controlling explanation:** A legal standard can constrain agency conduct without identifying the particular act a court may compel. The nonimpairment provision sets the required management objective with its stated qualifications; it leaves BLM choices about the measures needed. The organizations' proposed decree would require continuing judicial evaluation of management across the program. Describing the result as mandatory while leaving the means nominally open does not cure that defect. Final agency action violating a binding boundary remains subject to the appropriate review channel. Section 1732(a)'s requirement of plan-conforming multiple-use and sustained-yield management, with separately dedicated tracts governed by their dedicating law, and §1732(b)'s unnecessary-or-undue-degradation command retain their distinct operation.

**Precedent treatment:** National Wildlife Federation's prohibition on wholesale program review controls the demanded form of relief; Lincoln preserves the distinction between an enforceable standard and judicial selection of management measures.

##### III. These planning projections do not create the compulsory continuing program alleged

**Holding and operative rule:** Plan conformity prevents agency action inconsistent with an operative land-use plan until amendment, but a plan's prediction of future activity does not by itself create an affirmative duty enforceable under §706(1). A plan can reiterate an independently binding duty or clearly commit the agency to a particular obligatory act; the source, language, function and context must establish that commitment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Factory Butte provision contemplates monitoring and closure if warranted. Related Henry Mountains provisions contemplate documenting damage, recommending correction, identifying damage that may warrant designation changes and giving priority to supervision in limited and closed areas. In context these are planning directions, not a clear legal commitment to the intensive continuing operational program demanded. The general conformity command does not convert every projected activity into an enforceable schedule. This live claim therefore fails for lack of the asserted binding affirmative command.

Reported informal monitoring and commencement of a program do not make the continuing claim moot; they distinguish it from the completed requests to create files and finish route designation. Plan conformity remains a constraint on contrary action, reviewable through the proper §706(2) channel.

**Precedent treatment:** Bennett's identified-duty requirement and Lincoln's separation of law from administration are applied to the actual plan terms.

##### IV. Supplementation requires relevant major action remaining to occur

**Holding and operative rule:** When relevant major federal action remains, 40 C.F.R. §1502.9(c)(1) requires an EIS supplement if either substantial changes to the proposed action are relevant to environmental concerns or significant new circumstances or information relevant to those concerns bear on the action or its impacts. Section 1502.9(c)(2) separately permits supplementation when the agency determines it would further NEPA's purposes; that permission alone imposes no mandatory duty.

**Controlling explanation:** The approval of these management plans is complete. Continued management does not indefinitely keep the approval itself proposed, and the asserted increase in off-road-vehicle use identifies no distinct remaining major federal action to which this demanded supplement attaches. The supplementation claim therefore fails. Marsh concerns significant new information while relevant major action remains; finishing an EIS for an unfinished project does not finish that project. Later plan amendments and site-specific authorizations remain subject to their own NEPA requirements. Public Citizen reinforces identification of the actual action and legally available choices. Here the missing predicate is remaining relevant major action, not an absence of BLM's environmental authority.

**Precedent treatment:** Marsh's remaining-action rule is applied; Public Citizen's action-specific approach is preserved at its actual scope.

##### V. Completion ends the two requests directed solely to initial acts

**Holding and operative rule:** Requests solely to compel completion of the San Rafael route-designation plan and initial creation of the Henry Mountains use-supervision files are moot because those acts have been completed. They must be dismissed on that ground.

**Authority:** Part V's mootness rationale has the eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela concurs in those two component dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** The particular acts sought through these initial-completion requests now exist. A judicial command to perform them would supply no remaining relief under those requests. The continuing monitoring-program demand is a different live claim and receives the legal-force disposition stated above. Completion of an initial act does not establish fulfillment of every distinct continuing duty.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871: applied against wholesale program supervision, while permitting review directed to an identifiable agency act.
- Lincoln v. Vigil: applied to distinguish enforceable law from administrative discretion; the present decision specifies the complete discrete-act rule for §706(1).
- Bennett v. Spear: applied for an identified binding duty and the proper review channel.
- Marsh v. Oregon Natural Resources Council, 490 U.S. 360: applied to supplementation where relevant major federal action remains; completed plan approval differs from an unfinished project whose EIS has already been prepared.
- Department of Transportation v. Public Citizen: applied at its requirement to identify the agency action and available choices; BLM's authority over management remains governed by its own statutes.

## Law After Decision

Courts may compel discrete legally obligatory agency acts unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed, leaving lawful substantive choices to the agency. A broad management standard does not itself identify every act that may be compelled. Plans constrain inconsistent action, while affirmative enforcement requires an independently binding duty or clear obligatory commitment. The Henry Mountains projections fail that requirement for the program claimed. Mandatory supplementation attaches to relevant major action remaining to occur and either regulatory trigger; discretionary supplementation retains its permissive character. The component dismissals implement those rules and the separate completion-based mootness conclusions.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must dismiss the generalized nonimpairment, nonbinding continuing monitoring-program and completed-plan supplementation theories on the grounds stated. They must dismiss as moot the requests solely for San Rafael route-plan completion and Henry Mountains file creation. The remand implements these component outcomes under ordinary procedure.
