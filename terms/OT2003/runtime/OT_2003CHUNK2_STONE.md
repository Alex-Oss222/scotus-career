* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand to deny relief on that confined claim; resolve any independently pending, preserved ground under its own requirements. No retrial or release is ordered.

* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment governs Gentry's reviewed habeas claim and supplies no relief against a policy or class.

* **Practical Relief:** The court of appeals must implement the rejection of this claim and identify any genuinely surviving claim before disposing of the application. The conviction remains operative on this ground.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Contextual review of closing advocacy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this summation establishes constitutionally deficient performance under the applicable habeas limitation.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. Bell v. Cone requires contextual review of actual advocacy. Broader immunity for tactical labels would shield deficient representation; a phrase-by-phrase rule would ignore the trial setting.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal habeas court may not invalidate a state conviction merely because another lawyer could have delivered a stronger closing argument, when the state court reasonably found that counsel presented a coherent defense grounded in the record. Gentry bears the statutory and constitutional burdens. Read as a whole, counsel pursued accidental injury and resisted propensity reasoning; the identified omissions do not establish an objectively unreasonable rejection of deficient performance.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers represented defendants challenging choices within a substantive closing presentation; it does not immunize uninformed investigation or complete abandonment.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bell v. Cone distinguishes particular omissions from failure to test the prosecution at all. Here counsel identified the disputed intent, attacked observation evidence and separated past misconduct from guilt. Williams v. Taylor requires review of the supported record and does not convert debatable presentation choices into objectively unreasonable state adjudication.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Bell v. Cone and Williams v. Taylor are applied without extension of their habeas limits.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 11. Boca Investerings Partnership v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the D.C. Circuit's judgment and remand for application of the genuine-partnership inquiry without a freestanding requirement that use of a partnership have a separate nontax business purpose.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require consideration of the district court's findings on contributions, control, risk, profit sharing and the participants' actual relationship under Culbertson, with clear-error review of factual findings. Only then may the tax court or district court determine the resulting adjustments under the applicable procedure; no amount or penalty is fixed here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Partnership recognition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether genuine investment transactions compel tax recognition of this claimed partnership.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statutory partnership inquiry and existing source-based tax interpretation.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The entity-recognition standard must govern recurring partnership disputes. It neither validates all tax shelters nor makes tax motivation conclusive; the trial record and each independent statutory qualification still control.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Federal partnership recognition turns on whether the participants genuinely intended to join in conducting an enterprise and sharing its economic results, not on an additional requirement that their choice of partnership form serve a separate nontax purpose. Under Culbertson, contributions, control, risk, economic participation and the entire actual relationship determine qualification. Tax avoidance may be evidence of an artificial relationship but is not itself disqualification. Apply the ordinary tax burdens and clear-error review to the trial findings. Vacate because the appellate rationale treated a separate business purpose for partnership form as necessary without completing that inquiry.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The claimed joint enterprise and its partnership allocations, not every investment loss or any new penalty.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those computations are resolved, the lower court applies the Code's ordinary allocation and procedural rules without treating this holding as authority for unrequested penalties.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Culbertson examines the substance of the joint undertaking. Real trades alone do not establish a partnership, but neither does a tax motive negate real capital participation, risk or shared economic activity. The district court made detailed findings after trial; the court of appeals must identify any legal deficiency or clear error rather than substitute the requirement that the partners explain why they needed partnership form. Gitlitz requires enacted tax consequences when statutory qualifications are met. It does not decide qualification here or defeat a separately established sham-transaction ground.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Commissioner v. Culbertson and Commissioner v. Tower to the actual relationship; apply Gitlitz v. Commissioner against an extra-statutory anti-benefit condition. Reject the D.C. Circuit's separate nontax-purpose prerequisite to partnership form.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Transaction-level alternative theories and precise tax computations require the applicable assessment record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Soft money and party connections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** How far Congress may regulate party and officeholder fundraising to protect valid federal contribution rules.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the preventive contribution and circumvention principles of Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb, Colorado II and Mariani to the demonstrated national-party system, preserving government-borne close fit and meaningful political association. Neither equalization of influence nor ordinary access alone supplies the required justification.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad across the integrated national-party channel and the federal-election activities of state and local parties because the record demonstrates how candidate-linked financing moves between them. A broader rule governing genuinely independent state political activity would lose the necessary connection. A receipt-by-receipt exemption for funds nominally designated nonfederal would leave the demonstrated national-party channel intact.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may dismantle a demonstrated system for circumventing lawful candidate-contribution restrictions through party fundraising when the government establishes an objectively grounded important corruption or circumvention interest and a close fit preserving meaningful association. The government bears that justification burden on the supported record; no completed bribe, completed local scandal, mandatory new local study or proof of legislators' subjective reliance on particular evidence is required. Relevant experience outside the jurisdiction may establish a preventive justification. Uphold §323(a)'s restrictions on national parties receiving and spending soft money, including receipts nominally designated for state or local elections, because the proved fundraising system is structurally integrated with federal candidate financing. Uphold §323(b)'s federal-election-activity limits, including appropriately allocated registration and turnout activity, with the statutory Levin-fund accommodation material to fit. Sustain §323(d)'s restrictions on party fundraising used to channel prohibited money through §501(c) and §527 organizations while preserving lawful hard-money donations and genuinely independent organizational activity. Sustain §§323(e) and (f) within their candidate, officeholder and federal-election connections. Preserve concrete challenges demonstrating disabling associational burdens. Party affiliation, ordinary meetings or access, lobbying, policy agreement, donor prominence and unequal influence alone establish neither corruption nor a general power to restrict political resources.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The complete national-party soft-money receipt and spending channel, including nominally nonfederal receipts; state and local party federal-election activities within §323(b); the nonprofit and political-organization channels covered by §323(d); and the candidate-linked solicitation and financing covered by §§323(e) and (f). Genuinely independent organizational activity and state politics outside those statutory connections receive no additional restriction from this holding.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Relevant outside experience may support preventive regulation when it bears on the identified mechanism; a local scandal or fresh local study is not invariably necessary. National-party designation of a receipt as nonfederal does not defeat the integrated-channel holding. A materially different application must still satisfy the government's objective-justification and close-fit burden and preserve meaningful association. Voluntary capped responsive public grants may be lawful under Daggett, but that permission supplies neither an entitlement to equal campaign resources nor authority to suppress competing private speech.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb supplies the central preventive principle: relevant experience can establish an objective justification without a completed local scandal, a mandatory new local study or proof of legislative subjective reliance, but the actual restriction must remain closely fitted and preserve meaningful association. Its recognition of tacit donor-party-candidate understandings and fundraising credits is directly relevant. The record here shows national-party arrangements through which large donors obtain candidate-connected benefits and furnish support unavailable as direct candidate contributions. Colorado II recognizes donor understandings, fundraising credits and allocation arrangements as evidence of circumvention through genuinely coordinated party financing; Mariani supports enforcement of actual source and channel limits. Those mechanisms justify controlling the national-party channel as a whole, including nominally nonfederal receipts, and preventing migration to covered state-party and outside-organizational channels. Levin funds and lawful hard-money transfers preserve activity while closing the demonstrated substitutes. Buckley and Shrink Missouri require actual justification and fit; political responsiveness, ordinary access and unequal influence cannot replace them. Daggett's approval of voluntary capped responsive grants identifies a permissible financing alternative, not a command that Congress establish one or a basis for suppressing private competitors.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb's objective preventive justification and meaningful-association limits as the central rule; apply Buckley, Shrink Missouri, Mariani and Colorado II to the proved fundraising and circumvention mechanisms. Preserve Colorado I's genuinely independent spending and Day's voluntary-association protection. Preserve Daggett's voluntary public-financing permission without turning it into an equalization power or funding mandate.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different applications alleging demonstrably disabling associational burdens or restrictions beyond the statutory federal-election connections require their own facts. Creation of a public-financing system is outside the question presented.

### Issue 2: Treasury funding of electioneering communications

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The presented facial challenges permit adjudication of the electoral-advocacy core and the governing independent-speech boundary. No particular future issue advertisement or nonprofit financing record is conclusively adjudicated. Reporting coverage, treasury-financing restrictions and constitutional exemptions remain separate questions.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §§203 and 204 may restrict corporate and union treasury financing of actual electoral advocacy while preserving genuine independent issue discussion and the functional protections of qualifying ideological nonprofit associations.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Sustain the valid electoral-advocacy core under surviving Austin and MCFL principles while applying Bartlett's genuine-independent-issue-advocacy boundary and Day's functional nonprofit protection. Do not convert Beaumont's direct-contribution holding into a rule equating independent expenditures with contributions.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad enough to sustain the presented facial operation and state a usable independent-speech boundary. A broader prohibition on every communication naming a candidate in the statutory window would disregard Bartlett and Day. A narrower rigid list of electoral words would fail to reach actual electoral campaigning that uses other language.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may require genuine electoral advocacy by covered corporations and unions to be financed through lawful segregated funds supported voluntarily rather than their treasuries, consistently with surviving precedent, but may not suppress genuine independent issue advocacy merely because a communication falls within the statutory electioneering definition. Sustain §§203 and 204 against the presented facial challenges to their valid electoral-advocacy core without declaring every covered application constitutional. The definition's candidate identification, broadcast, cable or satellite medium, 30-day primary or 60-day general-election period, and applicable electorate-targeting requirements provide objective statutory coverage; they do not conclusively establish constitutionally regulable electoral advocacy. Under Bartlett, examine the communication as a whole, including what it actually asks the audience to do; a reasonable nonelectoral interpretation remains constitutionally material. An approaching election, candidate identification, potential influence on voters or opposition to the candidate's legislative position cannot alone defeat a genuine-issue-advocacy claim. No rigid 'vote for' or 'vote against' vocabulary is required for actual electoral advocacy. The government must justify the electoral and financing connection. Under MCFL and Day, assess a nonprofit's actual organization, resources and financing: incidental commercial activity or imperfect formal compliance does not alone remove independent-expression protection, while material corporate treasury financing or use as a commercial conduit may change the result. The entity claiming the functional nonprofit protection must establish its actual qualifying characteristics. Preserve genuine press activity, individual independent expenditures and effective as-applied review.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Actual electoral advocacy financed through the covered treasury channels within the statutory time, medium and electorate boundaries. The holding preserves genuine independent issue discussion, functionally qualifying ideological nonprofits, genuine press activity and natural persons' independent political expenditures. Disclosure duties require their separate justification and do not establish power to prohibit the underlying message.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** A communication asking voters to replace a senator in November presents electoral advocacy; a communication asking the audience to contact that senator about an impending Medicare vote presents a materially different legislative appeal. Courts must consider the actual message, context, audience request and financing rather than decide either application from candidate naming or timing alone. A reasonable nonelectoral interpretation must receive constitutional consideration without automatically resolving every future dispute. Apply Day functionally: incidental commercial activity and formal defects are not conclusive, but actual material treasury financing and conduit use matter. Until a particular application is adjudicated, courts apply Bartlett's issue-advocacy boundary, MCFL and Day's functional association protections, and the government's required justification for the restriction.

* **Transition:** Apply these boundaries in the pending actions and prospective enforcement. The facial disposition neither reopens final judgments nor creates retrospective penalties through a newly enlarged construction; it does not extinguish a properly supported as-applied claim.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Austin remains effective for the valid treasury-financed electoral-advocacy core, but that permission does not eliminate the independent protections recognized in Bartlett, MCFL and Day. Bartlett rejects prohibition of genuine independent issue advocacy under an indefinite electoral-influence rationale and requires a sufficiently definite distinction between issue discussion and electoral campaigning without a mandatory word list. Objective dates and candidate references identify statutory coverage but can also describe a real legislative controversy, so expected electoral effects cannot replace examination of what the communication asks its audience to do. Day protected an ideological nonprofit's independent expenditure despite incidental commercial activity and imperfect formal compliance, making actual structure and financing decisive rather than corporate status alone. Material business financing or conduit use remains relevant; incorporation creates neither automatic prohibition nor universal exemption. Beaumont concerned direct candidate contributions, where candidate control and the actual source-and-voluntary-channel record justified the restriction. It supplies no general equation of independent expenditures with contributions. The valid electoral core and a meaningful genuine-issue-advocacy challenge can therefore coexist without deciding unpresented advertisements or funding arrangements.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply surviving Austin at the treasury-financed electoral-advocacy core; reaffirm North Carolina Right to Life v. Bartlett's genuine-issue-advocacy boundary and the functional association protection of MCFL and Day v. Holahan. Preserve Buckley and Colorado I on independent expenditures. Distinguish Beaumont and Stengel's direct-contribution holdings from a general restriction on independent expression.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of particular future issue advertisements and materially different nonprofit financing arrangements requires a concrete communication and record. The facial disposition grants no blanket advance exemption and forecloses no properly supported challenge under the stated boundaries.
