## 71. Martinez v. Bush

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the independently unsupported intentional-racial and §2 claims. If an effective Vieth judgment rests on a five-Justice holding that adopts a different partisan standard or forecloses partisan claims, apply that holding to the preserved partisan portion; otherwise vacate the preserved partisan portion for application of the unified four-element rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve supported findings on the independently resolved racial claims. Remand only preserved partisan claims and amendments available under ordinary procedure, using the rule that governs under the disposition above; impose no completed-election waiting requirement.
* **Remedial Reach:** Only the adjudicated branches and districts affected by any violation proved on remand. No violation is newly found and no present statewide redesign is ordered.
* **Practical Relief:** The three-judge court must identify what the actual Vieth mandate governs, evaluate the remaining partisan claim under that law, and issue concrete correction only upon supported liability. If no injury is established, enter judgment for officials on that branch.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Intentional exclusion from the redistricting process

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Florida’s redistricting process and plans unlawfully excluded racial minorities, diluted protected voters’ electoral opportunity under §2, or imposed unconstitutional partisan entrenchment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the ordinary intent rule to the claim actually pleaded; no preserved §602 effects claim under Powell's bounded prospective action is before the Court, and §1983 supplies no regulation-created effects right.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record. The trial findings decide the intentional claim. Deciding the validity of §602 effects regulations or their agency enforcement would exceed Sandoval, which assumes the first and preserves the second. Reopening the purpose finding merely because participation was imperfect would omit clear-error review.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A claim that redistricting procedures intentionally excluded racial minorities requires proof by a preponderance that racial discrimination was a motivating factor, and an appellate court must respect supported findings absent clear error. The findings here do not establish that purpose. This conclusion does not affect agency enforcement of §602 effects regulations.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The preserved constitutional and §601 intentional-process claims.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trial court examined notice, hearing arrangements, software access and actual participation. Its no-purpose finding has not been shown clearly erroneous. Powell's bounded prospective action, which Sandoval applies, enforces only a valid applicable §602 regulation, no such claim is identified here, and Powell and South Camden hold that §1983 supplies no regulation-created effects right; the private Title VI claim here is the §601 intent claim, which fails on the same finding. Because Sandoval assumes the regulations' validity and preserves their agency enforcement, it does not support the further proposition that every Title VI claim necessarily requires intent; that broader historical rationale is not adopted.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Powell v. Ridge, Alexander v. Sandoval and South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection are applied: §602 effects regulations are privately enforceable only through Powell's bounded prospective action, which is not invoked here, and not through §1983, while agency enforcement and the §601 intent action remain; Washington v. Davis and Village of Arlington Heights govern the intentional claim.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No identified §602 regulatory-effects claim is before the Court.

### Issue 2: Section 2 electoral opportunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Florida’s redistricting process and plans unlawfully excluded racial minorities, diluted protected voters’ electoral opportunity under §2, or imposed unconstitutional partisan entrenchment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing functional-opportunity rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The supported district-performance findings resolve the claim. A statewide proportionality safe harbor would exceed De Grandy. A rigid racial-headcount requirement would contradict Valdespino.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2 does not require replacement of districts that provide reliably demonstrated equal opportunity to elect protected voters’ preferred candidates merely to maximize racial population percentages or seat totals. Plaintiffs retain the preponderance burden on unequal opportunity; proportionality is evidence rather than a defense by itself. The district-specific findings support judgment for defendants here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged Florida districts and the proved opportunity comparisons.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** De Grandy rejects automatic proportionality defenses, but the findings here examine actual minority-choice performance. Valdespino permits dependable crossover support within a supported comparison. Neither authorizes treating candidate race alone as proof of whom protected voters prefer.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Voinovich v. Quilter, Johnson v. De Grandy and Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District are applied.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 3: Conditional application of the proposed partisan rules

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Florida’s redistricting process and plans unlawfully excluded racial minorities, diluted protected voters’ electoral opportunity under §2, or imposed unconstitutional partisan entrenchment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the unified four-element political-opportunity rule unless a five-Justice holding in an effective Vieth judgment adopts a different partisan standard or forecloses partisan claims, in which case apply that holding. Organization, participation, nomination and election are opportunities protected by that single rule, not separate liability branches. For a properly presented legislative governing-control claim, propose the separate purpose-free rule stated below; a congressional holding does not itself adopt that rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate for application of the unified opportunity rule; broad within any properly presented governing-control claim. That additional whole-body claim remains distinct from the four-element purpose-based claim. Congressional delegations are not governing chambers, projected seat shares alone do not prove injury, and no completed-election prerequisite is added.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Unless a five-Justice holding in an effective Vieth judgment adopts a different partisan standard or forecloses partisan claims, a claimant must prove four elements by a preponderance: (1) an identifiable existing or demonstrably attainable opportunity for effective political organization, participation, nomination or election; (2) material deprivation of that opportunity through a demonstrated districting operation; (3) a substantial motivating objective to suppress identifiable political opposition's effective opportunity because of the affected voters' political association or participation and thereby preserve governing power against electoral challenge; and (4) a material causal contribution from the challenged boundaries rather than voter geography, ordinary competition or independently lawful requirements. Material deprivation means a substantial practical obstruction to the identified activity, not mere disappointment or a less favorable electoral prospect. It does not require complete destruction, inability to elect in every district or circumstance, a prescribed range of election results, or a lost general election before organizational injury can be recognized. Ordinary partisan advantage alone does not establish the prohibited purpose. An emerging opportunity may qualify on concrete evidence without a prior victory.

  **Proof and defense.** Preponderance means more likely true than not. Once all four elements are established, the State bears the same standard of persuasion that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited entrenchment objective. A genuine, independently established and consistently applied fairness policy or lawful population, geographic or statutory requirement must actually account for that decision; a conceivable alternative justification is insufficient. If the defense fails, no additional balancing of partisan advantages excuses the violation. Personal standing requires the claimant's own injury, traceability and redressability and remains separate from proof of the merits. Map-caused injury is complete when the proved deprivation occurs; no completed-election waiting rule applies.

  **Governing control.** For a preserved legislative claim, propose an additional rule: district assignments may not insulate an existing governing party or coalition from a reliably demonstrated opposition vote majority across the recorded conditions through a material, avoidable effect of those assignments. This rule applies separately to an elected legislative chamber or other district-elected governing body, never to a State's congressional delegation. It requires no discriminatory-purpose showing.

  The claimant must prove by a preponderance: (1) an independently existing governing party or coalition; (2) recorded conditions in which a coherent opposition receives more than half the reliably measured votes for the whole body; (3) retention of a seat majority by the governing coalition in those conditions; (4) retention across the recorded opposition-majority conditions rather than at one isolated point; and (5) a material causal contribution from the challenged assignments beyond geographic concentration, turnout, candidates and lawful constraints. No fixed number of elections or cycles controls.

  **Measurement and comparison.** An absolute majority means more than half, not a plurality. A staggered body requires evidence encompassing its whole membership. Use votes actually cast, accounting for uncontested races through disclosed, tested evidence rather than assumed votes. The lawful comparison must be workable, satisfy governing legal requirements and rest on independent legal, institutional or practical considerations rather than a desired partisan result. Applied to the same recorded votes, it must support the inference that the challenged placements cause retained control; the court must state the inference's limits. A completed election under the challenged map is unnecessary if reliable evidence establishes the elements.

  **Lawful-constraints defense.** The State may prove by a preponderance that consistently applied lawful constraints made the retained-control result unavoidable. Ordinary partisan advantage, voter approval or commission authorship is insufficient. This defense concerns unavoidable lawful constraints, not the purpose-based same-decision defense governing the unified political-opportunity claim.

  **Standing and application.** The claimant's own assignment must contribute materially to the body-wide injury, with relief likely to redress it. Congressional claims cannot invoke this route. No partisan liability is found here. Preserved legislative claims proceed under the proposed governing-control rule only if the Court adopts it; the independent racial and §2 dispositions remain governed by their separate law and findings.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Preserved congressional claims and procedurally available amendments; legislative chamber theories only if actually presented. No missing claim or adopted Court rule is invented.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply an effective Vieth holding at its actual scope. A possible electoral victory does not by itself defeat proof that boundaries materially obstruct an identified political opportunity. Conversely, partisan design, irregular shape or an unfavorable seat projection does not establish the four elements without concrete injury and causal evidence. Governing-control claims require whole-body measurement and reliable treatment of uncontested races; neither a congressional delegation nor projected party advantage alone supplies that claim. A remand permits only preserved claims and amendments allowed by ordinary procedure, with no automatic revival of abandoned theories.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gingles governs statutory racial opportunity and cannot supply the elements of a separate partisan constitutional claim. The unified rule protects an identified political opportunity from intentional material deprivation; it does not demand electoral impossibility or split organizational and electoral injuries into incompatible tests. The separate proposed governing-control claim concerns insulation of a district-elected governing body and requires its own whole-body causal proof. The Florida finding of partisan purpose does not by itself establish Vieth's prohibited entrenchment objective, the required deprivation or causation. Party advantage neither establishes racial purpose nor excuses racial injury. Assess the supported findings under the effective rule, allowing only procedurally available further proof and amendments and imposing no categorical waiting period for an election under the challenged plan.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Davis v. Bandemer and the independently governing racial precedents retain their actual scope. No proposed Vieth formulation is treated as an effective holding before adoption.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any legislative partisan claim not preserved or properly added is outside the remand; remaining liability and remedial facts require adjudication under the actual controlling rule.
