## Event

Central Laborers' Pension Fund v. Heinz; No. 02-891. Argued April 19, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, which reversed judgment on the pleadings for the pension plan, 303 F.3d 802. The question is whether ERISA's anti-cutback rule prohibits expanding postretirement employment restrictions to suspend early-retirement benefits already accrued. The retirees accrued benefits under terms permitting supervisory construction work; the plan's 1998 amendment added that work to disqualifying employment and suspended their payments. The plan seeks approval of that restriction; the retirees seek restoration of the payments withheld.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed, and the case is remanded for proceedings restoring benefits withheld under the unlawful added restriction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Seventh Circuit's reversal of judgment on the pleadings for the plan stands. The added restriction may not be applied to the protected accruals; payments withheld solely under that restriction must be restored, with the amounts calculated below.

## Holdings

### I. Adding a payment restriction reduces the accrued benefit

**Holding and operative rule:** Under ERISA §204(g), a plan amendment reduces an accrued early-retirement benefit when it adds a condition withholding payment for employment the terms governing that accrual permitted. The reduction occurs when the added condition diminishes the entitlement, even though the nominal monthly amount remains unchanged; lawful changes to terms governing future accruals remain possible.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls this rule and the following statutory distinction and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The benefit is the promised entitlement under its governing conditions, not an abstract figure payable only if a later-added condition is satisfied. Lockheed Corp. v. Spink preserves sponsor authority to design plans together with independent statutory limits. Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson protects the defined accrued entitlement while rejecting ownership of all plan surplus; these retirees seek the promised payment itself. They earned the benefit under terms permitting supervisory employment, and the 1998 amendment reduced that right by making continued payment depend on abandoning previously permitted work. The plan's distinction between suspension and reduction fails because the added condition itself diminishes the accrued entitlement; actual suspension demonstrates the injury here. Treating the unchanged nominal figure as conclusive would permit the plan to withhold the protected value while retaining its name. Section 204(g)'s early-retirement protection reaches benefits attributable to service before the amendment.

The corresponding formal rule in 26 CFR §1.411(d)-4 distinguishes objective conditions specified before accrual, under A-6, and changes to benefits not yet accrued, under A-2(a)(1), from added conditions or further restrictions on an already accrued protected benefit, prohibited by A-7. That formal regulation confirms the statutory reading. An inconsistent unreasoned manual statement or informal approval practice cannot displace it.

### II. Permission for original suspension terms does not authorize this cutback

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 203(a)(3)(B)'s permission to suspend specified benefits during covered postretirement employment does not authorize a later amendment reducing an already accrued benefit contrary to §204(g). For the multiemployer provision invoked here, work in the same industry, trade or craft, and geographic area covered by the plan may be made an original suspension condition; attaching an additional restriction after accrual remains subject to the separate anti-cutback command.

**Authority:** The unanimous Court adopts both grounds in the explanation below.

**Controlling explanation:** First, §203 governs vesting and forfeiture, while §204 protects benefit accrual. The absence of prohibited forfeiture does not establish the absence of a prohibited reduction. Second, §203(a)(3)(B) describes conditions that may form part of the benefit package offered before accrual. It does not grant permission to rewrite a previously earned entitlement. An original suspension term is accounted for in the benefit when earned; an added term diminishes what was already earned. Each provision therefore performs its own work. Section 203 remains available for lawful future-accrual terms, while §204 protects accrued payment rights from this added supervisory-employment restriction.

The 1998 amendment violates that protection as applied to the retirees' accrued benefits. The restoration and calculation directed by the judgment follow from the invalid condition. The separate statutory mechanisms for reductions based on substantial business hardship under §302(c)(8), 29 U.S.C. §1082(c)(8), or terminated multiemployer plans under 29 U.S.C. §1441 are outside this claim. Plan tax qualification and the authority to adopt a prospective regulatory exception are outside the participant-benefit question decided here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lockheed Corp. v. Spink is applied to preserve sponsor design power subject to independent statutory limits; sponsor status supplies no answer to the accrued-benefit violation.
- Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson is applied for enforceable accrued payment rights, distinct from ownership of plan surplus; its rejection of the surplus claim remains intact.

## Law After Decision

Section 204(g) protects the conditions of an earned early-retirement payment entitlement as well as its nominal amount. A plan must respect those conditions when amending disqualifying employment; §203's separate suspension permission does not override that protection. The rule governs the challenged accrued benefits and pending benefit claims, while lawful terms for future accruals retain their separate operation.

## Separate Writings

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices O'Connor and Ginsburg, joins the Court's opinion on the understanding that its construction of the existing statute and regulation does not foreclose a legally authorized, explicit regulation by the Secretary of Labor or the Secretary of the Treasury permitting an expansion of disqualifying employment for already earned benefits. The concurrence leaves the existence and exercise of that agency power open. No such operative regulation permits this amendment, so the qualification does not alter the present benefit-restoration result.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the anti-cutback holding and restoration judgment. He would require the plan to identify the protected accruals, stop applying the new restriction, calculate the sums withheld solely because of it and resume payment, with the district court setting accounting and payment deadlines and retaining enforcement authority. A remedy confined to selected participants would require the plan to show that it fully cures the same unlawful condition within the affected live claim. He regards any authorized Treasury determination under 26 U.S.C. §7805(b)(8) about retroactive tax consequences as separate from participants' established payment rights. His concurrence addresses implementation of the statutory entitlement rather than replacing it with a fiduciary or surplus-ownership theory.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for calculation and restoration of payments withheld solely because of the 1998 added employment restriction and enforcement of the protected accrued terms. The plan must administer those benefits without the invalid condition.
