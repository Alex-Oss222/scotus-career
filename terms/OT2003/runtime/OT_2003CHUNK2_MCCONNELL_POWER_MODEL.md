# McConnell — independently modeled power and structural components

This supplement forms part of the combined independent commitments. It uses the validated power preflight, the exact power entering-law selections and the programmatically copied predecision source supplement. The political-party brief's characterization of the September 19, 2002 interrogatory response remains advocacy: the underlying response was not inspected, and no exclusive-Elections-Clause concession, factual admission, waiver or stipulation is adopted. ## A. Presented claims and the actual lower disposition

The Title I affirmative-power challenge covers the national-party off-year/state-election restrictions, state/local federal-election-activity categories and Levin conditions, federal-officeholder fundraising for other elections, and state-candidate federal-candidate communications. The party appellants also invoke reserved state electoral authority and the Tenth Amendment against their own financing restrictions. Section 318 has a short separate state-funds federalism alternative. These are existing questions, not new grants.

Kollar-Kotelly's Part II.D rejected the Title I claims through an expressly **prudential third-party-standing** rationale: no State was a plaintiff, and she regarded the substance as assertion of absent States' rights. That is not an Article III finding that regulated parties suffer no injury and is not a panel-wide merits holding. Henderson rejected the power objection to §323(e) and reserved the others; Leon rejected the power objection to class-(iii) communications and reserved classes (i), (ii) and (iv) while resolving speech. The final decree must therefore be reviewed provision by provision, without inventing a separate unanimous lower federalism judgment.

## B. Directly regulated parties may raise the presented allocation objection

**Selected commitment of each Associate:** Reach the affirmative-power objection and the associated reserved-power objection insofar as they challenge the federal restrictions imposed on these parties' own fundraising and spending. Their regulated conduct and exposure supply personal injury, enforcement supplies causation, and relief from their own restriction supplies redressability. State representation is unnecessary to that claim. Do not adopt Kollar-Kotelly's prudential bar or recast it as an Article III dismissal. No decision is required on a private claimant seeking relief solely for a State's independent institutional injury without injury to that claimant.

Lujan requires actual personal injury, not a particular constitutional label; the supplied direct-regulation posture supplies it here. Lopez adjudicated the regulated person's source objection. Printz's unanimous jurisdictional part likewise distinguished the officers' own commands from absent dealers' different claims. New York's actual division-of-authority rule protects an allocation that state consent cannot cure; the present challengers invoke that allocation to resist their own legally binding restrictions. The Court's actual Guillen reserved private Tenth Amendment standing and merits; it establishes neither a categorical bar nor a categorical private cause of action. Tennessee Electric concerned competition with nonexclusive franchises, not a prohibition directly binding the challenger's conduct. Its absent-State passage is not extended beyond that setting to foreclose this challenge.

**Strongest contrary path:** Treat the state-election framing as an effort to vindicate absent States' sovereign preferences, especially where some States support the Act. That could support a prudential refusal on a genuinely state-only claim. It does not answer these plaintiffs' own asserted freedom from allegedly unauthorized federal commands. Their claim succeeds or fails on the allocation and the enacted mechanism, not on whether their State authorizes the same conduct or joins the suit. The narrower accepted position reaches only directly injured regulated parties; no general taxpayer, competitor, voter or representative standing rule follows. No Associate joins a conclusion that a federalism label alone establishes injury, or that an adverse merits conclusion defeats standing retroactively.

## C. Affirmative authority, by statutory component

The selected source rule is common to the eight Associates at this scope: Article I, §4 supplies congressional-election authority, including fitted financial safeguards protecting those elections; presidential safeguards rest separately on the national protective authority recognized in Burroughs and applied in Voting Rights Coalition, implemented through the Necessary and Proper Clause. Necessary implementation may reach a supported circumvention channel or shared federal-election operation even when it also serves state elections. It supplies no general power over state elections, no power to determine state-office qualifications, and no authority from the mere fact that state politics can eventually influence national politics.

Voting Rights Coalition, authored by O'Connor and joined by each Associate, supplies the actual congressional/presidential distinction and the preservation of state qualifications. Smiley and Siebold there support electoral administration and overlapping machinery; Burroughs supplies the distinct national-executive source. Thornton and Cook distinguish lawful administration from manipulating the constitutional representative relationship; neither supplies immunity for every private transaction associated with a state campaign. The current Lopez rule concerns commerce, not an extra interstate element in an election law. Its source-before-consequences discipline, reinforced by Holston and Ho, prevents substituting remote political effects for the actual constitutional mechanism. No new Commerce Clause ground is needed or adopted for Title I.

**The source holding and the First Amendment holding do different work.** An enacted class may fall within a constitutionally eligible federal subject and reasonably implement its protection while imposing a speech or associational burden that fails the independently demanding campaign-finance standard. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas therefore can reject the party-expression sweep without denying power to regulate supported federal financing mechanisms. O'Connor and Souter can require a separate full-payroll speech-fit inquiry without holding that Congress lacks power over employees materially engaged in federal-election work. The government's claim that its First Amendment answer automatically answers power is rejected; the following independent source analysis is required.

## D. Reserved state sovereignty and the Tenth Amendment

**Selected commitment of each Associate:** Reject the distinct claim that the otherwise authorized parts of Title I are invalid merely because they restrict private financial conduct that States allow in state campaigns. No state legislature must enact a federal code; no state executive must administer a general federal program against others; no state candidate qualification, voter qualification, election calendar or allocation of state offices is replaced. The restrictions directly bind the identified parties, candidates and fundraising actors. Existing state restrictions may continue; a State's permission to give more does not defeat a valid federal restraint. The holding is conditional on the independently established affirmative source and the surviving speech limits, not a theory that regulation of private people is always valid.

New York and Condon distinguish direct regulation supported by a federal source from compulsory state government of others. The actual Printz majority permits a confined form of temporary records assistance, while O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject that compelled executive work on structural grounds. Neither position condemns this different private-finance arrangement. Voting Rights Coalition separately confirms that election duties derive from their own constitutional grants; no such state-officer implementation command needs sustaining here.

The strongest contrary theory is that state electoral finance is so fundamental to state self-government that even valid federal protection must yield or be balanced against it. All eight preserve the State's own electoral machinery and qualifications, but decline an absolute immunity for a private transaction with a supported federal-election or federal-official connection. Supremacy resolves a real conflict only after federal validity is established. Mere public importance cannot establish either power or immunity. This is a merits rejection of the directly injured parties' presented structural theory, not a dismissal for lack of a State plaintiff and not a ruling on every conceivable claim to another sovereign's rights.

## E. Equality and clarity alternatives at their presented scope

The party/classification objection remains distinct from the claim that ordinary access or influence equality justifies financing restrictions. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the facial equal-protection objection to the **surviving** party-specific rules for the same supported actual financing-channel differences described above: the institutional relationship, donor-credit and transfer mechanisms justify different rules without declaring all parties intrinsically corrupt or requiring every other organization to receive identical regulation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas obtain their selected relief from the independent-expression/chosen-channel grounds and do not add a broader equal-protection invalidation of every differential party rule. They join the narrower proposition that genuine direct/conduit differences can justify different treatment and that the source label alone is insufficient. Their equality disposition therefore leaves no additional requested remedy unresolved.

All eight reject the Thompson objection insofar as it rests on the alleged disproportionate effect of the uniform federal-candidate solicitation rule on minority communities or on a right to equal financial advantages. Valeria's unanimous rule requires attention to an actual racial classification, deliberate proxy or racially selective political barrier; absence of hostility alone would not answer such a mechanism. The presented §323(e) theory identifies the same federal-candidate fundraising restriction across candidates, not a race-defined relocation of access or a supported deliberate proxy. Disproportionate consequences alone do not establish that different trigger. This does not adopt a categorical rule requiring animus in every structural racial claim, use the historical formulation of Adarand, or supply an unpresented racial factual finding.

On the facial clarity theories, each Associate accepts that ascertainable medium, time, candidate and electorate conditions are not vague merely because some covered speech is constitutionally protected. The primary-category breadth objections remain independently live. For party communications and §323(f), the adopted candidate-for-office meaning and the distinction between electoral promotion and mere policy discussion supply the operative limit; the three broader speech-protective positions do not require a separate declaration that every ordinary use of promote, support, attack or oppose is unknowable. Coordination requires an actual spending relation; absence of a formal agreement requirement does not eliminate that limiting criterion. The fallback retains its entire cumulative limiter. No Associate joins a judgment sustaining an undefined all-influence test or a boundless contact rule. Bartlett, Maine and Clifton supply the relevant campaign-finance limits; Greenville's contextual-notice and independent-missing-standard holdings reinforce the distinction without importing medical professional context. To the extent the selected invalidation already removes a challenged speech restriction, no separate broader vagueness remedy is ordered.

## G. Remedy and integration

These source/structural commitments reject a proposed alternative basis for wholesale Title I invalidation and reject the prudential standing premise used by one lower judge. They do not reverse a nonexistent panel-wide federalism dismissal. The appellate mandate resolves the actual Title I decree through the main component dispositions: where a source challenge accompanies a provision otherwise sustained, reject that alternative challenge; where speech relief is granted or fit remanded, preserve it without an additional source injunction. No State is added, no litigation default is inferred, and no remand to obtain a new State plaintiff is permitted. Section 318's alternative federalism/equality questions are unnecessary because complete speech relief already supplies the requested result.
