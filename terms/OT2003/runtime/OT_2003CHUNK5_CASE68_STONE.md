## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Delegated classification of over-limit charges

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Federal Reserve Board may exclude credit-card over-limit fees from the Truth in Lending Act’s finance-charge category.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the delegation framework to a permissible statutory classification.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A uniform fee category is the issue presented and supports administrable disclosure. Broader immunity for default fees would exceed this text. A merchant-authorization distinction would create uncertainty without a statutory command.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Board may classify an over-limit fee as an other charge rather than a finance charge when Regulation Z reasonably implements TILA’s express classification authority and the fee remains subject to applicable disclosure duties. The challenger must identify a statutory conflict or legally unreasonable classification; the disclosed charge here supplies neither.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Credit-limit excess fees under the challenged Regulation Z provision, not every fee connected with consumer credit.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Heimmermann does not allow uncertainty to create agency power. Here §1604(a) expressly assigns implementing classifications, and the statute distinguishes finance charges from other charges while specifically contemplating over-limit fees. Independently construing that assignment permits this uniform classification. The agency must still comply with enacted limits and the separate disclosure duties; the result supplies no general immunity for default fees.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Mead Corp. and United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. are applied; no precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
