## 89. Republic of Austria v. Altmann — June 7, 2004

### Proposition sets and disposition

**A1 — Statutory reach.** The FSIA governs this post-enactment suit's immunity determination even though the alleged underlying conduct preceded both 1976 and the 1952 restrictive-immunity policy. Sections 1330, 1602 and 1604 establish a comprehensive current immunity regime, with the exceptions doing their own statutory work. Section 1602's direction that courts henceforth decide immunity within that scheme is read with the present immunity command and jurisdictional structure.

**A2 — Temporal explanation.** The conclusion rests on this scheme's function, not its jurisdictional label alone. The FSIA allocates the present availability of a United States forum under a subject previously governed by changing executive and judicial immunity practice; it does not supply the property rule or determine wrongful acquisition. The older practice did not fix the merits liability or guarantee that later immunity determinations would preserve the policy prevailing when the conduct occurred. Landgraf's provision-specific inquiry permits this application. Hughes Aircraft's removal of a complete statutory restriction on private enforcement does not make every current forum determination a newly created claim. Dole Food supports examination of present statutory status but does not itself decide this temporal question.

**A3 — Judgment and reach.** Affirm denial of immunity on the granted temporal question, leaving the separately determined expropriation-exception ruling in its existing posture. The property suit proceeds. Taking and ownership remain allegations; title, act of state, limitations and execution are outside this temporal question and retain their own operation. Reject the United States' proposed exception-by-exception historical cutoff as a temporal construction: the integrated scheme supplies no general division of current immunity determinations according to each exception's historical origin.

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support A1–A3 at this common level, subject to Scalia's method qualification below. Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the temporal ruling and remand for the proper earlier-immunity determination. Their position does not establish dismissal before that inquiry.

### Adversarial paths

The application path faces the argument that withdrawing immunity creates exposure where none existed, as in Hughes Aircraft. Its answer is specific to this statute and the former regime: present diplomatic and forum rules, rather than an enacted conduct-date substantive defense, govern whether a United States court may hear the otherwise existing claim.

The contrary path regards the expropriation exception's loss of immunity as a new disability attached to completed conduct. Its strongest objection is section 1602's direction to courts and the integrated current framework. Kennedy and Thomas read the language as identifying future courts' source of law, not clearly extending every new exception to earlier conduct. They require the earlier-immunity inquiry rather than assume a title determination or automatic historical immunity.

No row adopts the United States' entire historical-origin schedule. Kennedy and Thomas accept its requested reversal for this expropriation application without deciding every proposed commercial-activity or waiver cutoff. The six affirming Justices reject a parallel historical schedule absent an enacted basis.

## 93. Belitskus v. Pizzingrilli — June 10, 2004

### Common merits and threshold propositions

**B1 — Access rule.** An otherwise qualified candidate unable to pay the required ballot fee without financial hardship must have a realistic nonmonetary route. A mandatory fee without such a route imposes severe exclusion on that candidate and associated electoral interests. Under Anderson–Burdick as stated in Cartwright, the exclusion requires narrow tailoring to a compelling interest. Ordinary administrative expense or screening seriousness does not justify selection by ability to pay where a meaningful support-based or hardship alternative serves the legitimate objective. Reasonable fees for those able to pay and valid qualification and support requirements remain available.

**B2 — Application.** Sustain the inability-to-pay challenge supported by Stith, Linzey and the Pennsylvania Green Party. Bullock and Lubin supply the protection; actual financial exclusion differs from ordinary inconvenience or a preference for a cheaper route. Reasonable proof of hardship may be required, but the alternative must be usable. The Court supplies no unsupported financial threshold or affidavit form.

**B3 — Justiciability.** The recurring candidate and party injuries support adjudication for Stith, Linzey and the party. Donovan's individual claim is moot because the required likelihood of recurrence to him is absent; affirm the direction to dismiss it. Schrader protects a continuing party's recurring election controversy, not every voter's completed-election claim.

All eight support B1–B3. The disagreement concerns the permanent injunction, not permission to exclude a demonstrated financially unable candidate by mandatory fee.

### Remedial positions

**B4 — Effective relief for established plaintiffs.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the substantive ruling and Donovan disposition, vacate the appellate injunction restriction insofar as it limits enforceable relief to Stith and Linzey without securing the party's established recurring injury, and remand for a specific order protecting the actual candidate and party interests proved. Under Rule 65(d), the lower court must state the hardship condition and officials' concrete duties, allow the State to choose a meaningful lawful alternative, and fit relief to the proven operation of the policy. The district court's general protection of every candidate unable to afford the fee is not restored without that fit and specificity work. Party standing is not class certification or automatic universal relief.

**B5 — Named-candidate limitation.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the appellate limitation to Stith and Linzey upon demonstrated hardship, together with its supported candidate-and-party merits relief and Donovan dismissal. The action was as applied; these Justices regard the named candidates' continuing protection as adequate on the established scope. Specificity remains required. They would allow a broader operative order only after a concrete determination that distinct proved party injury remains unremedied by those terms. Standing alone does not establish that remedial predicate.

The sides share the constitutional rule and specificity requirement but differ on whether the appellate cap should stand now. Neither limits the merits rule's precedential force to two candidates.

### Adversarial comparison

The officials' strongest merits argument invokes screening and administration. It fails because ability to pay does not establish public support and the mandatory policy supplies no realistic alternative to severe exclusion. The candidates' protection does not dispense with lawful support and qualification rules.

B4 faces the objection that an as-applied action cannot automatically produce an injunction for unnamed candidates. It requires an injury-specific order for these plaintiffs, including the party, and justification of any policy-wide scope by remedial necessity. B5 faces the objection that protecting two names may leave the successful party injured by the same recurring policy. It treats the supplied candidacies as the established scope while permitting an adequately supported extension for remaining concrete injury. This is remedial factual work under the settled inability-to-pay rule.

## Full propositions supported

**PC1 — NEPA's causal boundary.** NEPA requires review of environmental effects having a reasonably close causal relationship to the agency action under review. The contemporaneous section 1508.8 includes effects caused by that action at the same time and place and effects caused by it later or farther away that are reasonably foreseeable. Foreseeability and a necessary place in a sequence do not alone make the agency legally responsible for the effects of an independently controlled decision. Where FMCSA cannot prevent otherwise qualified carriers' entry on environmental grounds, its registration and safety rulemaking is not the relevant cause of the additional entry that the President independently authorizes by lifting the moratorium. NEPA does not require FMCSA to prepare an environmental impact statement on that entry increase as an effect of these rules.

Metropolitan Edison supplies the reasonably close causal relationship; Robertson supplies NEPA's informed-decision procedure rather than a freestanding environmental veto. The agency's lack of an environmental veto does not by itself remove all procedure: the point is that the particular admission decision and resulting increase belong to a separate decisionmaker, while this agency's actual choices must still be examined for the effects they cause. The record supports that allocation, not an immunity for any agency with limited powers. Lincoln and Bennett reinforce respecting statutory decisionmaking boundaries while enforcing actual legal duties.

**PC2 — Cumulative effects and alternatives.** Section 1508.7 requires the reviewed action's incremental impact to be considered with other past, present and reasonably foreseeable future actions regardless of actor. It does not dispense with identifying an incremental impact of the reviewed action or make every consequence of another actor's decision a consequence of the agency rule. The President's distinct action therefore does not become attributable to FMCSA merely by relabeling its entry effects cumulative. Effects caused by FMCSA's actual discretionary safety choices retain their ordinary NEPA treatment; this record's assessment addressed the agency rules, and the challenged omission is the independent traffic increase.

The commenters' failure to identify additional regulatory alternatives when FMCSA could consider them defeats the request to require an unpresented specific alternative on the account supplied. Vermont Yankee requires meaningful presentation of such an objection rather than forcing an agency to anticipate every unarticulated proposal. That conclusion concerns these missing alternative proposals; it is not forfeiture of every environmental objection, a demand for technical precision beyond fair notice, or a finding that a supported obvious defect could never be reviewed. No supported obvious omitted alternative is supplied here. A court cannot create an environmental exclusion alternative the agency lacks statutory authority to choose.

**PC3 — Clean Air Act conformity.** The conformity issue has its own regulatory terms. Under section 93.152, direct emissions of a criteria pollutant or its precursors must be caused or initiated by the federal action and occur at the same time and place. Indirect emissions must be caused by the action, may occur later or farther away but must remain reasonably foreseeable, and must be emissions the agency can practicably control and will maintain control over because of its continuing program responsibility. The challenged increased cross-border traffic emissions are not direct emissions of issuing the rules. They do not satisfy the indirect-emissions definition because FMCSA does not control the independent decision to admit otherwise qualified carriers and lacks a continuing environmental entry-control responsibility. The order demanding a full conformity determination for that traffic increase cannot stand.

The conformity regulation's explicit practicable-control and continuing-program-responsibility conditions do separate work. They are not silently inserted as identical wording into NEPA. The possibility that safety standards may have some emission consequences does not establish continuing control over the distinct traffic increase attributed to lifting the moratorium. The rule applies the supplied allocation of authority to the actual demanded determination, rather than declaring every FMCSA activity exempt from conformity review.

**PC4 — Judgment and remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it requires the environmental impact statement and full conformity determination for the independent increase in Mexican truck operations. This resolves the presented review requirements rather than issuing an advisory approval of every environmental feature of the rules.

## 95. Norton v. Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance

### Operative commitments

**P1 — Section 706(1).** An order compelling agency inaction requires an identifiable agency act that governing law requires the agency to take. Section 551(13)'s listed forms of action and their failures, read with section 706(1)'s unlawfully-withheld or unreasonably-delayed language, do not authorize a continuing judicial decree to improve an entire administrative program. When law requires an act but leaves its lawful content to the agency, the court can require action without selecting the discretionary content. Lincoln supplies the distinction between enforceable legal boundaries and an allocation committed to agency discretion; Bennett preserves review of legally consequential action and source-specific nondiscretionary duties. Neither earlier decision alone already established this complete section 706(1) construction.

**P2 — Wilderness nonimpairment.** Section 1782(c)'s obligation to manage the study lands without impairing their suitability is binding, with its actual statutory qualifications. It applies during review and until Congress determines otherwise, subject to continued existing mining, grazing and mineral leasing in the manner and degree conducted on October 21, 1976; the separate proviso requires action against unnecessary or undue degradation or to afford environmental protection. Those qualifications are not interchangeable with section 1732's management provisions. The general nonimpairment command leaves BLM choices about management means and does not identify the discrete omitted act necessary for a program-wide section 706(1) decree. A final management decision alleged to violate the binding boundary presents its proper review channel, rather than making the boundary meaningless.

**P3 — Plans.** Section 1732(a)'s management-in-accordance-with-plans command does not turn every plan objective into an immediately enforceable promise to perform a particular act. A plan term can support section 706(1) when its actual language, legal force and trigger impose a specific required act; aspiration, prediction and discretionary planning language cannot. The court must construe each pleaded term and its operative context. The legal rule does not find an unidentified plan term mandatory or permissive and does not presume that a source unavailable to this preparation is absent from the judicial record.

**P4 — Supplementation.** The continuing existence of an adopted plan does not itself make completed plan approval a still-proposed agency action. For an identifiable proposed action remaining to be undertaken, section 1502.9(c)(1) requires a supplement to the draft or final statement if either the agency makes substantial changes relevant to environmental concerns, or significant new circumstances or information relevant to those concerns bear on the proposed action or its impacts. These are alternative triggers. Subsection (c)(2) separately permits a supplement when the agency determines that doing so will further NEPA's purposes; permission supplies no mandatory section 706(1) duty. A court must identify the relevant remaining action and the mandatory trigger before compelling supplementation. Continued land management alone does not establish both.

**P5 — Judgment and remand.** Vacate the judgment reinstating these claims under an undifferentiated mandatory-management theory and remand under P1–P4. The general program-supervision demand fails as such. The lower courts must separately classify the pleaded plan duties and the asserted supplementation duty, using the actual terms, remaining proposed action and triggering facts; enforce a supported required act through the proper channel and dispose of a theory lacking those predicates. This is ordinary application under complete legal standards, not permission for a general obey-the-law decree and not conclusive dismissal of unidentified binding terms.

### Alternative-path comparison

The organizations' strongest competing path reads Congress's mandatory nonimpairment outcome and plan-compliance command as sufficient for a decree while leaving selection of means to BLM. It correctly identifies real statutory duties; its defect is equating a legally binding program boundary with the omitted agency act that section 706(1) authorizes a court to compel. The remedy would require the court continually to decide whether BLM had managed well enough, without an identified required act. Conversely, the Secretary's broadest possible reading—that all plan language and every continuing land-use effect are beyond review—goes farther than the statutory text. P3 preserves genuinely binding specific terms and P4 distinguishes completed planning from a still-proposed action. The uncertainty about particular terms and triggers warrants their application below, not a fabricated factual finding or a categorical immunity.

## 97. Hibbs v. Winn

### Timeliness commitment H1

### Tax-bar paths

**H2 — Collection-related reading.** Section 1341 does not bar this third-party Establishment Clause challenge at the requested scope. Read together, assessment, levy and collection refer to the imposition and obtaining of tax liability; the statute does not make every federal challenge to a measure appearing in a state tax code a forbidden restraint. These plaintiffs seek no reduction of their own assessments or avoidance of taxes owed. Prospective disallowance of credits would allow collection of the full tax; the declaration supports that same relief; the requested payment of covered organizations' remaining funds into the State's general fund likewise does not suspend a tax assessment or collection. The rule follows from the relief's legal operation, not a universal revenue-increase exception or an exemption for any suit labeled third-party.

**H3 — Consequence of H2.** Affirm the appellate judgment reversing the section 1341 dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings. Federal adjudication still requires Article III standing, consideration of a genuinely applicable independent comity ground and an available remedy. Those matters must be resolved at their actual preserved scope before the constitutional merits and any ultimate relief. This procedural decision establishes neither the actual financed function nor an Establishment Clause violation. Farm Credit governs an actual effort to avoid tax assessment and collection, while separating substantive tax rights from a federal forum; it does not answer this distinct credit challenge. Galletti's federal collection-period holding supplies no controlling definition of section 1341, and Zelman supplies no jurisdictional or merits shortcut.

**HD — Tax-administration reading.** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas read assessment to include the legally required calculation of liability with the credits the State has enacted. An injunction disallowing those credits restrains that assessment process even if receipts may increase. The declaratory request obtains the same prohibited federal direction; a request for payment of the resulting organizational funds cannot sever away the predicate invalidation of the credit system on this theory. Section 1341 uses no taxpayer-identity or anticipated-revenue condition. If a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists, the requested federal adjudication must be dismissed. Because adequacy of the actual state remedy is not established by the supplied legal account, these three would vacate the appellate judgment and remand for that condition to be resolved, followed by dismissal if satisfied. They do not invent a conclusive finding of adequacy.

## 98. Pennsylvania State Police v. Suders

### Common sufficient ground S1–S3

**S1 — Constructive discharge.** A hostile-environment resignation amounts to constructive discharge when discriminatory working conditions become so objectively intolerable that a reasonable employee in the employee's position would feel compelled to resign, and those conditions cause the resignation. Establishing a Harris hostile environment does not automatically establish that further severity and causal connection. The employee must prove the actionable sex-based conditions, subjective experience required for the hostile-environment claim, the objective compelled-resignation threshold and causation. A formal firing or completed personnel decision is not indispensable to this form of discharge.

**S2 — Attribution and procedures.** Constructive discharge does not itself attribute every supervisor's act to the employer. Under Faragher, Harrison and Ellerth, the plaintiff invoking actual-authority attribution must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of the actionable environment. A resignation neither replaces that proof nor converts supervisory rank into liability. Conversely, a paper policy, nonreporting, absence of a formal personnel action and later correction do not by themselves negate proved actual aid or erase prior incurred harm. The summary-judgment account alleges persistent supervisory harassment, inadequate assistance in accessing a complaint route, and an arrest episode carried out by the supervisors using their positions. That account supports genuine inquiry into objective intolerability, cause and actual-authority aid; it does not establish an operationally effective protective route that Suders unreasonably bypassed.

**S3 — Judgment and limit.** Affirm the judgment reversing employer summary judgment and remand for ordinary proceedings under S1–S2. Reject both automatic employer responsibility from the label constructive discharge and the employer's requested immunity merely because resignation is not a formal personnel act. The Third Circuit's categorical treatment of constructive discharge as itself resolving the defense question is not the governing rationale. The general question whether proved effective protection and unreasonable nonuse can supply a complete liability defense remains unadjudicated because those disputed predicates are not supplied as established facts here. That reservation preserves the earlier reservation; it creates no defense on remand and does not convert the contested complaint mechanism into a finding. The court must apply the settled elements and ordinary summary-judgment or trial rules, resolving properly available further questions when supported.

The employer's strongest alternative would treat an effective prevention system plus unreasonable nonuse as a complete defense whenever no official personnel act caused departure. The Court's actual-authority precedents deliberately did not adopt that regime. Its proposed factual predicates also cannot be found against the plaintiff on this record. The employee's strongest alternative would treat an objectively compelled resignation as sufficient official action to foreclose every defense. That collapses discharge, attribution and compensation, which Title VII and the entering cases keep separate. S1–S3 answer the present dispute without either categorical leap. No supervisor intent specifically to cause resignation is added: the required discrimination, attributable intolerable conditions and causal resignation do the work, rather than a second motive element unsupported by Harris and the actual-authority precedents.

### Additional recoverability position SR

Ginsburg, Souter and Breyer continue the position they published in Ellerth. After the employee establishes the underlying claim and otherwise compensable harm, the employer should bear the burden of proving by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use them; and that the particular loss consequently could have been avoided. Recovery would be reduced or barred only to that proved extent. A safe, effective opportunity and causal avoidability are necessary; the employee need not complain to the harasser or use an unsafe route. Harm before a reasonable protective opportunity and harm the measures could not prevent remain compensable if otherwise proved.

SR is a proposed limit on loss recovery, not a complete defense erasing the discrimination, a shift of the plaintiff's attribution burden, or a formal-personnel-action exception. Even proof that every compensable loss was avoidable would not by itself determine other relief or fees. The alleged failed assistance and supervisory arrest foreclose applying SR conclusively for the employer now. The three join S1–S3 and would additionally instruct the lower courts to apply SR when its predicates are supported. Their proposal is not treated as preexisting controlling law.

## 99. F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A.

### Operative commitments E1–E3

**E1 — Claim connection.** For nonimport foreign-commerce conduct subject to section 6a, its domestic-effects exception requires that the qualifying direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable effect give rise to the claim for which the plaintiff seeks relief. A foreign purchaser's independent foreign injury does not satisfy that condition merely because the same conduct produced a domestic effect supporting someone else's claim. The statute first excludes the covered foreign-commerce conduct and then restores application on specified effects and claim conditions; reading a claim to mean any unrelated person's claim would disconnect the second condition from the cause being adjudicated. The export-only branch retains its separate restriction to injury to export business in the United States. The import exclusion and the qualifying-effects requirement retain their actual statutory operation.

**E2 — Application and authority.** On the assumed independence of these purchasers' foreign injuries, the domestic-effects exception does not restore Sherman Act coverage for their claims. Hartford Fire involved American-market effects giving rise to the claims allowed there and expressly preserved section 6a's connection requirement. Its rejection of a foreign-permission defense does not answer this different statutory connection question. Trinko's separate treatment of injury and remedy supports keeping the actual claimant's harm distinct, but supplies no geographic coverage rule. The statute's limited claim connection also avoids converting a domestic injury to one purchaser into general regulation of every independently injured foreign purchaser; this observation confirms the enacted limit, without adopting a free-standing comity-balancing rule or finding a particular conflict of foreign laws.

**E3 — Remedy and alternative theory.** Reverse the appellate any-claim construction and remand. The lower court must determine whether the separately advanced causal-dependence theory was preserved and, if so, whether the alleged domestic effect actually gives rise to these plaintiffs' claims under E1. The assertion that maintaining domestic prices was necessary to sustain foreign prices is a contention requiring legal and factual examination, not an accepted causal fact. If the theory was not preserved or fails the claim-connection requirement, the reviewed independent-foreign-injury claims must be dismissed. The construction does not conclusively resolve cartel liability, damages or the separate jurisdictional characterization of section 6a, which the confined statutory coverage disposition does not require.

### Alternative-path comparison

The purchasers' strongest reading emphasizes Congress's phrase a claim rather than the plaintiff's claim and the deterrent benefit of addressing a worldwide cartel. That reading supplies a grammatically possible indefinite reference but would allow a claim independent of the very effect Congress made the condition of restored coverage. Statutory structure and the connection requirement outweigh a general enforcement objective. Conversely, the manufacturers' request for immediate dismissal of every foreign-purchaser theory overreaches the stipulated independence premise: the connected-effects contention must receive its proper preservation and sufficiency treatment. No Justice infers that the contention is preserved, successful or barred merely from its mention. This distinction permits reversal of the legal rule with a remand that does real, defined work.

## 102. Aetna Health Inc. v. Davila, including the Calad companion

### Supported common rule and actual claims

All eight Associates would reverse the Fifth Circuit judgments in both granted dockets and remand for proceedings implementing complete preemption and the actual district dispositions. The common rule has two required conditions: the plaintiff could have sought the disputed plan entitlement through ERISA section 502(a)(1)(B), and the particular state-law claim rests on no legal duty independent of that entitlement or the administration of it. A tort label and a request for personal-injury damages do not create an independent duty; neither does plan involvement alone establish complete preemption of an actually independent duty.

Davila challenges the administrator's denial of coverage for the prescribed drug and the injury following use of the covered alternative. Calad challenges the coverage determination concerning further inpatient care and the injury following discharge. On the supplied allegations, both challenged functions are benefit determinations, rather than the treating physician's inseparable delivery of medical care. Whether the plans were required to supply the refused benefits supplies the premise of the challenged coverage wrong. The plaintiffs could have sought their asserted plan entitlement through section 502(a)(1)(B). The ordinary-care characterization does not furnish an independently operating treatment duty by these administrators on these claims.

Metropolitan Life's retained ERISA complete-preemption rule supplies the specific removal basis; Beneficial supplies the current exclusive-cause principle. Pegram requires analysis of the challenged function and preserves independent treatment duties. Rush Prudential saves a qualifying substantive coverage condition while keeping enforcement within section 502; it does not create an additional consequential-damages action. Miller's insurance-saving framework does not erase that enforcement boundary. Eichorn preserves genuinely equitable remedies under section 502(a)(3) on their own predicates, without converting a personal damages claim into equity.

The strongest contrary path characterizes Texas's ordinary-care requirement as a bodily-injury duty independent of contractual benefits. That path would survive for a genuine treatment wrong or other duty applicable independently of the benefit decision. It fails for these claims because the complained-of conduct is refusal of plan coverage, the defendants' relevant role is administration, and changing the loss claimed does not change the duty on which liability depends. The fact that section 502 may not furnish equivalent compensation is a serious remedial consequence, but it does not enact another cause of action or change original federal jurisdiction.

## 103. Pliler v. Ford

### Separate petitions, warnings and surviving judgments

The two repeated-claim components require separate application. Both first petitions were delivered to prison officials on April 19, 1997, against the supplied April 24 transitional deadline. Loguercio involved an express dismissal election on July 28, a September 11 dismissal and September 29 state filing. Weed involved failure to notify the court that the unexhausted claims would be deleted, an October 14 dismissal and October 24 state filing. Both state petitions ended March 25, 1998; the federal returns were filed April 7. A failure to respond is not silently transformed into an affirmative informed choice.

The Ninth Circuit required two particular advisements: first, that the court could consider the requested stay only if Ford deleted the unexhausted claims; second, that the apparent expired period meant returning after dismissal would be time-barred absent equitable tolling. It treated the repeated claims as timely by relation back, effectively assuming an amended retained petition, a granted stay and a timely return. That remedy is not entailed merely by finding inadequate advice.

The lower judgment separately affirmed dismissal of two new Loguercio assistance claims and directed factual development concerning access to legal papers for five new Weed claims. Those branches are outside the specified-warning question. Every Associate preserves both branches, including the actual evidentiary-development direction for the new Weed claims; no disposition of the repeated claims silently vacates them or converts them into merits judgments. The denial of automatic relation back for new claims is likewise not reopened here.

### Governing paths and limits

Duncan excludes federal pendency under section 2244(d)(2) and preserves independently available grounds on their own terms. Carey applies only to qualifying state pendency and intervals. Slack's non-successive classification does not make a separate later application timely. Castro regulates a court's unsolicited conversion into a first section 2255 motion; it does not automatically determine the treatment of a prisoner who selected section 2254 and submitted a mixed petition. Its supervisory protection nonetheless supplies a serious analogy when a court directs a procedural choice that can destroy an existing opportunity.

One path rejects the two mandatory warning requirements. Courts may explain procedures, but neither the statutory scheme nor the existing supervisory rule makes them counsel responsible for explaining the deletion/stay strategy and determining the deadline's consequence before a mixed-petition dismissal. An accurately described nonmerits dismissal can still leave a later filing untimely. Actual misleading statements remain analytically different from omission of advice, and independently available relief must receive the correct petition-specific inquiry.

The strongest alternative adopts a narrower supervisory notice duty: when a court presents a pro se prisoner with dismissal and return after exhaustion as one of its options, it must warn neutrally that the federal limitation continues during federal pendency and dismissal may foreclose a later federal return. That rule need not promise a stay, calculate the precise last day or advise which claims to abandon. Its strongest objection is that it converts application of a chosen vehicle into legal counseling and supplies a procedural duty the statute does not state. Its supporters answer that the duty governs the court's own invited election, as Castro regulates the court's own conversion, rather than guaranteeing advice on every filing rule.

Neither path itself establishes a still-pending original action, a granted stay, the legal predicates for reopening, timely amendment or equitable tolling. The exact warning/stay question can be resolved without adjudicating the general availability of mixed-petition stays, which Duncan leaves open and which this record's specified-warning review does not require. Independent relief for affirmative misdirection remains subject to preservation, an applicable legal basis, the actual communication, causal reliance and the diligence required by that basis. The lower majority's footnote discussing equity did not decide those matters for the repeated claims; the dissent's preservation objection is an issue for proper determination, not a found waiver.

### Component result and compatible remand

All eight support vacating the repeated-claim reinstatement and returning those claims for further proceedings; their grounds are not wholly shared. The common remedial requirements are: distinguish Loguercio's express election from Weed's failure to respond; remove automatic relation back founded solely on the two omitted advisements; decide any properly presented independent ground according to its actual source and facts; and leave the new Loguercio dismissal and new Weed evidentiary-development direction undisturbed. A remand to assess an actual misleading communication does not presuppose it occurred. Any reopening, amendment, tolling or other relief must have its own lawful predicate. If no available ground establishes timeliness for a repeated claim, the statutory time bar governs it.

The six-Justice no-duty position can state that a court must not mislead without deciding that accurate without-prejudice language alone establishes misdirection. It cannot be reconciled with the two-Justice notice proposal simply by relabeling the latter an accuracy requirement: the latter imposes affirmative information even when the existing description is legally accurate. That material difference remains frozen for reconciliation and assembly.
