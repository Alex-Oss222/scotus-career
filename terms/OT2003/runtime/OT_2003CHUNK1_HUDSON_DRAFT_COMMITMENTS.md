### Delegation, scope and conflict

The actual statutory scheme supplies more than general administrative responsibility. Section 1103(a)(1) assigns immigration administration and enforcement except where the laws assign powers, functions or duties to the President, Secretary of State, State Department officers or diplomatic or consular officers; subsection (a)(2) gives control of Service files and records; subsection (a)(3) authorizes regulations and other necessary implementing acts. Subsection (a)(9)(A) specifically authorizes payments from immigration-administration appropriations for necessary clothing, medical care, guard hire, housing, care and security of persons detained by the Service under federal law in nonfederal institutions under state or local agreements. The Commissioner's subsection (c) powers operate through authority delegated by the Attorney General or prescribed through that statutory structure. Taken together, these provisions authorize a rule retaining federal control over public release of detainee information acquired in carrying out federal detention. That conclusion comes from the enacted subject and record-control provisions, not from a county's consent or the importance of national security.

The rule covers governmental and private custodians holding detainees on the Service's behalf and persons who obtain the information by virtue of an official or contractual relationship with those custodians. It prevents those holders from making the covered information public outside the governing federal disclosure channels. Names and other covered information remain subject to applicable federal laws, regulations and executive orders. The rule is therefore not an independently sufficient exemption defeating every federal access request. The supplied rule explanation preserves detainees' ability to identify themselves, communicate and seek counsel.

For the records in this custody relationship, a state-law duty to release and the valid federal restriction cannot both be performed. The federal restriction controls that conflict. A voluntary contract cannot expand the rule to unrelated sovereign records or create statutory power that Congress withheld. The pending-request clause states the regulation's intended reach; it does not by itself establish lawful retroactivity. The supplied pending request seeks a public release that the governing rule now forbids outside federal disclosure law. Applying the valid rule to that future release does not impose liability for a completed past disclosure. The affirmed appellate judgment concerns that live disclosure remedy; the earlier trial order remained subject to appellate review and was reversed after promulgation. No separate damages claim, completed-disclosure sanction or final unreviewable disclosure judgment appears in the granted record, and none is adjudicated.

Mead requires actual delegated force, and Haggar keeps statutory authority distinct from procedural form and reasonableness. The detailed custody-and-records scheme and exercise through a generally binding rule distinguish this mechanism from Mead's revisable tariff letter. The confidentiality rule's federal-law channel and connection to records of federal detention keep it within that delegation. No Justice need adopt a proposition that mere notice and comment proves authority.

### Interim APA procedure

Section 553(b)(B) permits omission of notice and public procedure when the agency finds, and incorporates a brief statement explaining, impracticability, lack of necessity, or conflict with the public interest. Section 553(d)(3) separately permits good cause, found and published with the rule, for dispensing with the ordinary 30-day period before effectiveness. The commitment addresses the public-interest justification rather than inventing a different exception under §553(a).

The interim notice identified possible disclosure of investigative methods, sources, witnesses, investigative direction and scope; intimidation and harm; retaliation against detainees or their associates; and impairment of cooperation in the continuing emergency. These are the agency's published predictions and reasons, not adjudicated findings about every detainee. The proposed conclusion is that the described irreversible loss of control through public disclosure supports immediate central federal management while public comments are obtained. That management preserves federal-law disclosure rather than conclusively classifying every detainee or record as secret.

The strongest contrary path is that a government cannot invoke an emergency in general terms to defeat ordinary rulemaking during inconvenient litigation, particularly months after the triggering attack and through a rule covering all immigration detainees. On this text, the affirmative justification is more specific: disclosure by many custodians could irreversibly expose the listed information before the responsible federal authority can apply federal disclosure law. The same impending disclosure risk bears independently on why a 30-day delay would defeat the protective function. An imminent adverse disclosure order is relevant to timing, but winning that litigation is not itself good cause.

The conclusion sustaining §553(b)(B) does not automatically establish §553(d)(3). The first exception is satisfied because public disclosure during advance notice and comment would irreversibly defeat the described control; the second is independently satisfied because the same irreversible disclosures during an otherwise required 30-day effectiveness delay would defeat that function. Neither conclusion rests on litigation inconvenience alone. Nor does a valid immediate rule authorize punishment for conduct before notice or settle every retroactive application of its pending-request language. Those applications are outside the supplied requested relief.

### Structural objection

Condon directly distinguishes compliance with regulation of a State's own covered handling of information from a demand that it legislate or administer federal regulation against unrelated private persons. The counties voluntarily entered paid federal custody arrangements. Restricting release of the resulting federal detainee information regulates that accepted operational activity; the required custodial and records work does not turn this rule into a separate state regulatory program.

Lomont's real-refusal inquiry remains relevant but does not create an unconditional right for an existing federal custodian to disregard lawful rules for its custody records. Its officers were asked to help private applicants and could refuse without any federal consequence. Here the relevant predicate is an established voluntary paid custody relationship and regulation of its resulting records. No contract-termination term, release entitlement after termination, or duty to accept future detainees is invented. Condon's express reservation about a law regulating States alone remains intact because this rule also governs private custodians and related holders.

Printz's differing public positions remain distinct. Appropriate temporary compelled assistance, its unresolved fiscal issue and O'Connor's structural objection concern an unwilling office assigned affirmative federal administration. Neither a new compulsory-assistance permission nor a constitutional compensation right is needed for this case. O'Connor's express preservation of voluntary funded contracts is particularly close; the other structural objectors' Condon and Lomont joins supply their own support without attributing that additional Printz reservation to them.

EDC's March 25 holding requires a lawful and actually available direct-compliance route when the Government relies on an optional third-party-management program. It does not infer freedom from commandeering from municipal ownership alone. That distinction strengthens, rather than eliminates, the need to identify Hudson's actual regulated object: the public handling of information obtained through the federal custody operation. Section 236.6 regulates that handling, including private custodians' handling; it does not assign counties an unwanted general program for regulating unrelated private persons. The Government therefore does not need to invent an alternative permitting scheme or a right to ignore record restrictions while continuing the operation. The supplied voluntary paid custody relationship supports entry into that function. EDC's alternative-identification requirement supplies no finding about unsupplied contract-exit terms, and this commitment depends on none.

EDC also confirms that ordinary employee costs of lawful own-operation compliance do not become constitutional reimbursement claims. That point is consistent with the distinct Condon ground and preserves Printz's unresolved fiscal question rather than answering it. EDC's speech permission is limited to justified factual pollution-control information and does not authorize confidentiality restrictions, supply a FOIA exemption or decide the requester's access claim. Its assumed municipal protection and own-work standing analysis are unnecessary to this requester's established review posture. All eight Associates joined EDC's complete public holdings; their current structural commitments account for that actual join without attributing any new APA or immigration-record position to it.
