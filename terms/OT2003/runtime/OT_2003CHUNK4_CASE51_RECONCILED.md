## Comparator and actual changed legal premise

There is no historical Supreme Court merits disposition supplying a target vote or opinion for any Associate. The comparator is the Seventh Circuit's April 2, 2003 affirmance; May 22 was the rehearing denial. The lower court analogized the payments to vouchers and treated voluntary selection among religious and secular providers as constitutional insulation. That legal premise relies on the historical Zelman rule. Current Zelman instead preserves scrutiny of what a restricted provider payment buys even when the beneficiary chooses the provider. Current Agostini, Mitchell and unanimous Columbia Union also require actual funded-use proof, effective separation where claimed, and relief fitted to the proved defect. Those are concrete changes to the legal premise supporting the judgment below, not invented historical Supreme Court votes.

The completed February 25 Locke decision does not revive the lower premise. Its controlling permission concerns a confined devotional-ministry-degree exclusion from an independently directed student scholarship, including living and educational expenses. It expressly distinguishes that relationship from restricted provider purchases. The Locke dissent's objection to excluding the independent student benefit likewise does not establish that a State's selected contracted rehabilitation placement is an unrestricted personal grant. Beneficiary choice remains relevant to the actual relationship, but neither a paper voucher nor its absence decides constitutional function.

## Standing, scope and source-supported facts

The accepted taxpayer-standing predicate has an identified expenditure and a claimed Establishment Clause financing defect: Wisconsin pays part of placement costs to Faith Works under its correctional-services contract, and the taxpayer plaintiffs seek prospective relief against those payments as public financing of religious treatment. This is a challenge to identified state spending under the Religion Clause's limitation on governmental financing, not a generalized ideological objection or a claim resting only on displeasure with recommendations. On the supplied characterization of this tried taxpayer expenditure action, all eight accept the necessary taxpayer-spending/Establishment nexus and proceed to the granted merits. No particular appropriations enactment, tax payment, appropriation amount or legislative earmark is supplied or invented. The ruling does not create standing for every disagreement with an executive recommendation. Inventory authorization settles the review route; the described expenditure nexus supplies the separate substantive threshold premise.

The source describes a contract with Faith Works and reimbursement of part of the cost when an offender enrolls. Faith Works incorporates Christianity into its treatment, encouraging a relationship with God through Jesus Christ. Its program addresses employment, addiction and parental responsibility. These facts identify a real financing question; they do not establish that each reimbursed dollar pays for devotion, that every activity is inseparable, or that any particular secular cost category is independently funded. The supplied account gives no exact reimbursable-cost schedule, secular-use clause, allocation, accounting system or findings establishing the full scope of operational separation. Those cannot be filled by presumption in either direction.

The accepted findings establish secular alternatives, nonbinding recommendations, notice of the program's Christian character, and no advice to convert. The nine-month residential program differs from the three-month secular alternatives; greater attractiveness does not itself undo those findings. The bid waiver was attributed to the program's longer rehabilitative structure, not proved religious favoritism. Those findings remain operative. The opinion's general therapeutic assertions and incentive hypotheticals are not independent clinical or theological findings adopted here. Its reported argument acknowledgment is not treated as a separately verified transcript concession. The separate Workforce Development judgment is outside this review and remains undisturbed.

## Definitive common rule and complete remedy

A State may not use a correctional-services contract to purchase devotional formation integrated into the publicly financed treatment. Voluntary selection, a secular alternative and a secular rehabilitative objective do not cure an established purchase of that religious function. The court must identify the work actually purchased and its operational character. Independently identifiable secular services at a religious provider remain permissible when effective controls and reliable accountability preserve that work; affiliation, worship elsewhere, hypothetical diversion, fungibility and incidental savings do not themselves establish the contrary.

The lower courts must apply that rule to the actual contract and existing trial record, determine the funded work and supported scope of separation, and enter effective prospective relief against every proved devotional purchase. Existing findings establishing a funded use need not be reproved. Additional proceedings are required only when a material application or remedial fact remains unresolved; a new hearing, new audit, separate corporation or wholly different staff is not categorically mandatory. Any payment scope already shown to be unlawful must receive effective correction rather than continuation merely because the full program has not been quantified. Independently lawful secular applications and valid neutral program requirements remain operative.

This remand does not hold that some specific segregated secular reimbursement has already been proved, nor that all placements must stop. It does not award damages, order recoupment, close the provider, reopen the Workforce Development matter or invalidate every contract with a religious institution. The constitutional analysis of an identified public purchase differs from a restriction on privately financed voluntary religious rehabilitation or a properly supported accommodation for private exercise under custody; no such distinct claim is decided by assumption.

## Formal disposition and precedent treatment

**Formal flexibility:** Each of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer accepts either vacatur and remand or reversal of the private-choice-only sustainment with the identical definitive function rule and payment/remedial remand. No Associate conditions assent on the exact verb. A disposition conclusively declaring every payment unlawful, sustaining the program on choice alone, reopening established voluntariness findings without a supported basis, or merely suggesting the function rule for optional consideration would change the substance and fall outside these commitments.

Agostini governs through the modified unified Lemon inquiry; no separate old-prong test is revived. Mitchell governs actual-use proof, effective correction and preservation of lawful applications, without transferring its established funded-use finding to this record. Strout and Zelman defeat the lower choice-only premise at the restricted-purchase scope and retain their actual limits. K.D.M. and Columbia Union preserve equal eligibility for controlled secular services and require an operational connection before devotional financing is attributed to the payment. Locke retains its particular individual-scholarship permission and express distinction from restricted purchases. Witters remains an established individual-benefit relationship, not automatic validation of a selected provider contract. No precedent is newly overruled here.
