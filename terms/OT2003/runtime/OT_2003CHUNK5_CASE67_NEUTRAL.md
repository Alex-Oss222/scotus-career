* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-04-20
* **Question Presented:** Whether the prior judgment rejecting Customs’ original prohibition on duty-free fuel sales precludes a later revocation based on federal excise-tax treatment, and whether contempt is available.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit, affirming the Court of International Trade’s refusal to enforce the earlier judgment through contempt against the new revocation.

* **Material Record:** Ammex operates a bonded store at the Ambassador Bridge. Its first action defeated a Customs rationale under one statutory provision. After a requested IRS determination concerning excise tax, Customs initiated a new revocation under the statutory definition of duty-free merchandise.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The contempt motion cannot become review of every substantive tax issue. The preclusive scope of the first judgment must be determined from what it actually adjudicated and the operative transaction.
* **Relevant Dates:** The first authorization followed the 2000 litigation; the later revocation followed the 2001 IRS response. Federal Circuit judgment: July 1, 2003.

### Issue 1: Preclusion and later agency action

* **Controlling Law:** Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. distinguishes the source and scope of preclusion, but expressly does not decide all federal-question judgments. No holding makes successful review of one agency rationale a perpetual license against a later distinct action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the judgment’s actual preclusive scope and distinguish claim preclusion from issue preclusion. A new rationale alone does not necessarily create a new claim; the operative administrative action and facts matter.
* **Other Governing Law:** 19 U.S.C. §§1555(b), 1557(a); 26 U.S.C. §4081; the final federal judgment and ordinary federal preclusion rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lawlor v. National Screen Service distinguishes claims based on later conduct; Montana v. United States concerns matters actually determined.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Final judgments bar relitigation within their proper claim or issue scope, while genuinely later agency decisions may be challenged through their own review channel.
* **Open Point:** Whether the new tax-dependent revocation is the same adjudicated claim, and whether it violates a sufficiently definite prior command.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The first administrative determination and the later tax-dependent revocation involved distinct operative grounds and proceedings; contempt was not an appropriate substitute for fresh review.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** Ammex characterizes the case as a renewed attempt to deny the same duty-free fuel operation on a ground available in the first case.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The Government characterizes the revocation as a later decision prompted by the IRS response, outside the earlier judgment’s adjudicated scope.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** The revocation’s substantive tax validity can be litigated in a properly brought challenge even if contempt fails.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States defends the distinction between the two administrative proceedings.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Affirmance permits the later revocation to be challenged independently and denies contempt. Reversal would require identifying an actually precluded claim or command violated, followed by properly confined enforcement; it would not automatically establish an excise-tax exemption never adjudicated.
