## Event

Aetna Health Inc. v. Davila, No. 02-1845, and CIGNA HealthCare of Texas, Inc. v. Calad, No. 03-83; 542 U.S. 200. Argued March 23, 2004; decided June 21, 2004. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, 307 F.3d 298. The consolidated question is whether the patients' state-law claims against benefit administrators for injuries allegedly caused by coverage denials fall within ERISA §502(a)(1)(B), producing complete preemption and removal. Davila alleges injury following denial of coverage for a prescribed drug and use of a covered alternative. Calad alleges complications following denial of coverage for further inpatient care. Each sued under Texas's ordinary-care duty. The federal district courts dismissed after removal and each patient's decision not to amend to assert an ERISA cause; the Fifth Circuit reversed. The administrators seek reversal, and the patients seek state adjudication.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed in each docket, and both matters are remanded.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** All nine participating Justices directly adopt the Opinion of the Court, the common test, both applications and the mandate.

### I. A claim duplicating the federal benefits cause is completely preempted

**Controlling proposition:** A state-law claim is completely preempted by ERISA when the claimant could, at some point, have pursued the disputed plan benefit under §502(a)(1)(B) and no legal duty independent of the plan entitlement supports the challenged conduct. The removing defendant must establish federal jurisdiction and both predicates; a later bodily injury or different damages demand does not change the source of the duty, while an independently operating treatment duty remains distinct.

**Controlling explanation:** Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Taylor supplies the established removal rule for claims governed by ERISA's exclusive benefits cause. Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson distinguishes replacement by a federal cause from an ordinary federal defense, which does not alone permit removal. The inquiry therefore turns on the actual entitlement and source of duty, not the plaintiff's chosen label.

Pegram v. Herdrich requires examination of the function challenged. Inseparable medical treatment by a treating physician differs from a discretionary determination applying plan-coverage terms, even when the latter uses medical information. Rush Prudential HMO, Inc. v. Moran permits a saved substantive coverage condition while preserving enforcement through §502. Miller retains that enforcement boundary alongside its insurance-saving method. A tort claim cannot evade the benefits channel merely by requesting consequential damages unavailable through the benefits action. Nor does insurance involvement convert independently negligent treatment into plan administration. These distinctions answer the patients' reliance on a state ordinary-care duty without treating every claim against an HMO as federal.

### II. Both pleaded coverage-denial claims satisfy that rule

**Controlling proposition:** Davila's challenge to the administrator's drug-coverage denial and Calad's challenge to the administrator's further-hospitalization coverage denial each could have pursued the asserted plan entitlement under §502(a)(1)(B), and neither identified claim rests on an independently operating treatment duty. Both claims are therefore completely preempted, removal was proper in each case, and the contrary Fifth Circuit judgments must be reversed.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged wrong in each complaint is the administrator's denial of coverage, not treatment delivered by that administrator as the patient's physician. Liability for that wrong depends on the asserted entitlement under the plan. Davila's ensuing use of a covered alternative and Calad's ensuing discharge explain the alleged injuries but do not supply a different source of obligation. The Texas ordinary-care characterization and additional injury element likewise do not detach these claims from coverage entitlement.

The seriousness of the alleged harm and absence of equivalent federal compensation cannot enact a competing enforcement cause. Rush preserves federal enforcement even when insurance regulation supplies a coverage condition, and Eichorn requires relief to fit the invoked statutory channel rather than relabeling personal monetary liability as equity. The plaintiffs chose not to amend to assert an ERISA cause after removal. The cases return for implementation consistent with that actual dismissal posture and ordinary treatment of any preserved procedural matter.

## Precedent Treatment

- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Taylor: applied as the specific ERISA complete-preemption and removal rule.
- Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson: applied to distinguish replacement by an exclusive federal cause from ordinary defensive preemption.
- Pegram v. Herdrich: applied to distinguish treatment from administration of plan coverage; medical information alone does not make administration treatment.
- Rush Prudential HMO, Inc. v. Moran: applied to preserve §502 enforcement despite a saved substantive coverage condition.
- Kentucky Association of Health Plans, Inc. v. Miller: preserved in its separate insurance-saving inquiry and federal-enforcement boundary.
- Eichorn v. AT&T Corp.: applied to require an authorized remedial channel and the actual predicates of genuine equitable relief.

## Law After Decision

State-law claims satisfying both the benefits-entitlement and no-independent-duty conditions fall within the exclusive federal benefits cause and may be removed on that basis. The source and function of the alleged duty control despite a tort label or consequential-injury demand. Independently operating treatment obligations remain outside this rule, and saved substantive coverage conditions remain subject to ERISA's enforcement limits.

## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Breyer.** The exclusive federal benefits route may displace a state claim for serious consequential injury while supplying no equivalent compensation. Pegram's treatment boundary and Rush's preservation of substantive insurance regulation do not eliminate that gap when the challenged function is administration of coverage. The concurrence joins the Court because the remedial shortfall cannot itself change jurisdiction or create a statutory cause. It identifies a substantial reason to reconsider the remedial regime through a lawful case or legislation. Mertens holds that §502(a)(3) permits appropriate monetary restoration traditionally available in trust litigation, including surcharge against a fiduciary for losses its breach causes, so the gap concerns consequential injury beyond that restoration. Genuine equity retains the predicates recognized in Eichorn; concern about incomplete compensation is not adjudication of a different fiduciary claim or a new equitable category.

## Procedure After Action

The Fifth Circuit must implement reversal separately for Davila and Calad, treating removal as proper and respecting the district dismissals following each patient's refusal to amend. Any preserved procedural question proceeds under the applicable ordinary rules.
