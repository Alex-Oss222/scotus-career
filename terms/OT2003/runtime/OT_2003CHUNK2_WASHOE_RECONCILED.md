## Reconciliation result and objections

Retain all eight full P1–P6 joins: the claim is sufficiently final; neither physical appropriation nor the asserted regulatory taking is established; affirm summary judgment for the United States and rejection of claimants' partial-summary-judgment motion. The compensation case concludes under that judgment.

The strongest contrary ground is functional destruction of the only feasible municipal project. The complete source confirms the project's dependence on a federal route but supplies no prior entitlement to that route, loss of access to wells or restriction on extraction. Dugan and International Paper concern actual interference with a water entitlement; the present inability to obtain another owner's land interest is materially different. The proposed affirmance therefore does not require a finding that the water right is valueless or that Nevada's approval has no legal significance.

The narrower partial-taking remand argument is also tested. The actual Palazzolo and Tahoe-Sierra holdings forbid resolving every partial-taking theory solely because some use remains. P4 supplies an independent property/action ground here; P5 preserves contextual analysis when a cognizable regulatory burden is established. There is no supported valuation issue whose resolution could create the absent crossing right. The lower opinion's proprietary-capacity language is not enlarged into categorical immunity for every act government calls proprietary.

The lower opinion's footnote 4 also preserves a source distinction: Laney ordered further factual development rather than finding an actual taking. The frozen commitments do not misstate it as a controlling taking result. The separate APA processing question remains outside the compensation question supplied, and finality does not adjudicate that claim.
