## 74. Scarborough v. Principi

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for consideration of the amended application.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Accept relation back of the omitted allegation; determine substantial justification, eligibility and reasonable fees under the statute.
* **Remedial Reach:** Scarborough’s application and resulting authorized award, if established; no reopening of unrelated judgments.
* **Practical Relief:** The veterans court must docket the corrected application as timely, give the Government an opportunity to establish its merits defense, and enter a reasoned fee decision. The ruling itself orders no fee payment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Curable pleading omission in a timely fee request

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a timely Equal Access to Justice Act fee application may be amended after the filing period to add the omitted allegation that the Government’s position lacked substantial justification.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the correction principle to an ancillary fee proceeding rather than create an exception to an unfulfilled jurisdictional grant.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The identified omission determines this case. Excusing every application defect would outrun the statutory distinctions. Refusing cure because counsel rather than a layperson erred would give formal placement more force than the requirement’s function.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A timely EAJA application that identifies the applicant, judgment and fee request may be amended to add the required allegation that the Government’s position lacked substantial justification. The applicant must establish a timely qualifying application and required eligibility; the Government retains its burden to show substantial justification. This amendment relates back and requires merits consideration, not an automatic award.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The omitted allegation in an otherwise timely application; wholly late applications and missing substantive eligibility are not decided.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the treatment of a wholly untimely application, courts enforce the statutory filing requirement and any independently applicable tolling law; this cure rule supplies no automatic extension.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Becker preserves a timely appeal despite a curable signature omission; Edelman does the same for later verification. Here the additional sentence neither identifies a new claim nor changes the Government’s proof burden. The waiver authorizes fees already, and the amendment completes the mode of requesting them.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Becker v. Montgomery and Edelman v. Lynchburg College are applied by analogy to EAJA pleading.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Wholly untimely applications and tolling lie outside the presented facts.
