## 57. Batzel v. Smith

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the circuit’s interlocutory anti-SLAPP and §230 disposition and remand with instructions to dismiss that appeal for want of appellate jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the moderator’s defenses to the district court without a Supreme Court construction of the disputed §230 issue. Preserve the distinct final sponsor disposition to the extent independently before the circuit and outside the questions.
* **Remedial Reach:** The premature appeal and its merits ruling, not the final disposition of every defendant or any damages award.
* **Practical Relief:** The circuit must remove the unauthorized interlocutory decision; the district court may adjudicate the defenses and factual disputes through ordinary procedure. Later final review or a properly established statutory interlocutory route remains available.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal finality precedes the content-immunity question

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenged order denies a special motion in an ongoing federal action. No independent authorized route makes its merits reviewable merely because a related final appeal exists.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this anti-SLAPP denial satisfies the federal collateral-order exception.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing federal finality law; reach no §230 merits holding after finding the appeal unauthorized.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: reject immediate review for this category of special-motion denial based on ordinary probability-of-success and liability issues. A broader rule against every statutory immunity appeal would disregard established exceptions. A narrower unexplained dismissal would fail to address the state-created appeal and trial-avoidance arguments.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Denial of a California anti-SLAPP motion in federal court is not immediately appealable under §1291 merely because state law permits an appeal or describes protection from litigation. The appellant must satisfy every collateral-order condition; the merits-linked probability inquiry and ordinary liability defenses here do not become sufficiently separate and effectively unreviewable because litigation will continue. The circuit therefore lacked authority to decide the moderator’s §230 defense on this interlocutory appeal.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The identified special-motion denial and independent liability defenses within it; a genuinely separate authorized final appeal remains governed by its own scope.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A party invoking §230 may pursue the defense below and seek review through a valid final or independently authorized interlocutory route. A state appeal rule does not enlarge federal appellate jurisdiction.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. rejects trial expense and a promised escape from litigation as sufficient reasons to bypass federal finality. Swint v. Chambers County Commission applies adequate final review to an ordinary liability defense and refuses to append independent questions for efficiency. The anti-SLAPP inquiry here tests the plaintiff’s prospect of success and overlaps the disputed §230 defense. The public value of protected expression does not dispense with the cumulative jurisdictional conditions.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. and Swint v. Chambers County Commission applied; Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. retained at its narrow scope. No §230 precedent is created or overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The sender-intent and reasonable-perception questions cannot be adjudicated on this unauthorized appeal. Until reached through a valid route, lower courts apply §230 and independently applicable authority without treating this jurisdictional disposition as a choice among its disputed constructions.
