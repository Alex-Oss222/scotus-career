## Event

Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co., No. 02-1205, 541 U.S. 369. Argued February 24, 2004; decided May 3, 2004. Certiorari to the Seventh Circuit. Former employees assert section 1981 hostile-environment, termination and transfer claims made possible by the Civil Rights Act of 1991. The question is whether section 1658’s four-year limitation covers those claims although section 1981 itself predates December 1, 1990.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The judgment rejecting section 1658’s four-year period for amendment-created claims is reversed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Claims wrongly excluded under the shorter borrowed period return for application of the federal period and ordinary claim adjudication.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is published.

## Holdings

### The four-year period follows the enactment that made the claim possible

**Holding and operative rule:** Except where law otherwise supplies a limitation, 28 U.S.C. §1658(a) requires a claim made possible by an Act of Congress enacted after December 1, 1990 to be commenced within four years after accrual, whether the Act creates a new statute or amends an existing one. The party invoking that period must identify the new substantive basis for the claim against the defendant; an amendment immaterial to an already available claim does not change that claim’s limitation.

**Authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** An amendment is an Act of Congress. Restricting section 1658 to newly numbered statutory provisions would make limitations turn on codification rather than on the legal liability Congress added. Rivers v. Roadway Express identifies section 101 of the 1991 Act as a substantive expansion of section 1981 into contractual performance, modification, termination and enjoyment of the relationship’s benefits. Patterson v. McLean Credit Union supplies the construction of the earlier text against which that expansion is identified. The employees’ hostile-environment, termination and transfer claims fall within section 1658 insofar as the amendment supplied their actionable basis.

The employer’s continuity objection has force for claims already available under the older law, but it does not exclude claims the later Act newly authorized. The dependency inquiry preserves that distinction: the same amended statute may support both old claims governed by their otherwise applicable limitations and newly enabled claims governed by section 1658. Goodman v. Lukens Steel continues to supply borrowing where no applicable federal period displaces it. The date of the complaint or citation of amended text alone cannot establish the new period. The Seventh Circuit’s categorical reliance on section 1981’s original enactment therefore requires reversal, with claim-specific timeliness and the surviving claims resolved below.

**Precedent treatment:** Rivers is applied for the 1991 substantive expansion and its temporal boundary; Patterson remains the earlier-text construction. Goodman retains its borrowing role outside section 1658’s displaced category.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Applied to identify the new substantive contractual coverage supplied by the 1991 Act; its prospective temporal operation remains intact.
- **Patterson v. McLean Credit Union:** Retained as the construction of the earlier statutory coverage against which amendment-dependent claims are identified.
- **Goodman v. Lukens Steel Co.:** Its state-borrowing rule remains for claims without a displaced limitation category.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Its provision-specific temporal inquiry remains distinct from this construction of the enacted section 1658 date criterion.

## Law After Decision

Section 1658 follows the post-1990 enactment that supplies a new claim, including an amendment to an older statute. A mixed complaint can contain claims with different applicable periods. The employees’ amendment-dependent claims receive the four-year period; identifying accrual and resolving any actually disputed tolling, timeliness and discrimination issues are ordinary application of that rule.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must identify the claims whose substantive basis depends on the 1991 expansion, restore those improperly barred under the shorter borrowed period, apply four years from accrual and adjudicate the surviving claims through ordinary procedure. Any properly certified class receives the same claim-specific analysis.
