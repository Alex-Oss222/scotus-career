## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for application of the four-year period to claims made possible by the 1991 amendment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore claims wrongly dismissed under the shorter borrowed period; resolve accrual, tolling and merits only as actually disputed.
* **Remedial Reach:** The employees’ covered claims and any properly certified class; no finding of discrimination or damages.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify the amendment-dependent claims, apply the four-year statute and adjudicate surviving claims through ordinary procedure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Claims made possible by post-1990 enactments

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1658’s four-year limitation governs §1981 claims made possible by the Civil Rights Act of 1991’s amendment of an existing statute.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the recognized statutory expansion to an open limitations construction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutory trigger warrants a uniform rule for amendments. Every claim under an amended statute would be too broad. A §1981-only exception would ignore the general text.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A claim arises under a post-December 1, 1990 Act for §1658 when that enactment makes the claim possible, whether it creates a new statute or amends an existing one. A party invoking the four-year period must identify the new substantive basis; the asserted termination, hostile-environment and transfer claims qualify insofar as the 1991 amendment supplies that basis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Newly authorized claims, not unchanged causes merely pleaded after an amendment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rivers establishes that the amendment expanded the actionable contractual relationship. An amendment is itself an Act of Congress, so restricting §1658 to newly numbered statutory sections would disconnect the limitation from the liability Congress actually added.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc. is applied. Goodman v. Lukens Steel Co. remains for claims outside §1658.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Factual innocence of statutory sentencing eligibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether actual innocence of a noncapital habitual-offender enhancement permits review of a procedurally defaulted constitutional claim challenging that enhancement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose extending the eligibility gateway, while retaining the independent constitutional and statutory requirements for habeas relief.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Statutory eligibility supplies an administrable distinction tied to authorized punishment. A gateway for any sentencing error would undo default rules. A death-only line would leave conceded factual ineligibility for years of imprisonment without the same safeguard.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Clear and convincing proof that no reasonable factfinder could find the statutory facts authorizing a noncapital recidivist enhancement excuses procedural default of a federal constitutional challenge to that enhancement. The prisoner bears that gateway burden; discretionary sentencing disagreement is insufficient. Haley’s undisputed chronology meets it, but a writ still requires an independently established constitutional violation and satisfaction of applicable habeas restrictions.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Factual eligibility for a statutory recidivist enhancement; it does not create a federal remedy for every state-law sentencing error or revise ordinary sentencing discretion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the unresolved recidivist-proof question, courts apply existing constitutional proof rules and §2254 without treating gateway innocence as a new substantive right; a properly available Strickland claim remains independently adjudicable.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sawyer separates legal eligibility from discretionary selection and applies a demanding factual burden. That distinction remains usable when the unauthorized additional punishment is imprisonment. Schlup’s offense-innocence standard need not be transplanted wholesale. Edwards preserves a separate counsel-based route but does not make an unadjudicated claim an established violation. The admitted chronology opens review; it does not supply the missing constitutional analysis.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Sawyer v. Whitley is extended beyond capital eligibility. Schlup v. Delo and Edwards v. Carpenter retain their distinct functions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise underlying constitutional basis requires adjudication not adequately established by the gateway finding; no independent freestanding innocence claim is presented.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Best ability and evidentiary support for adverse inferences

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether 19 U.S.C. §1677e(b) permits the Department of Commerce to draw adverse inferences against an exporter that failed to provide requested information to the best of its ability without finding intentional concealment, willfulness, or bad faith.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the statutory intent question while enforcing an evidence-based ability requirement.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring information rule needs an administrable standard. Automatic adverse treatment of any error would erase the cooperation finding. An intent prerequisite would rewrite best ability as good faith.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1677e(b) permits an adverse inference without proof of intentional concealment when substantial evidence shows that the respondent could reasonably obtain the requested information but failed to make the effort within its ability. Commerce must identify the information gap and supported capability-and-effort findings; a deficient response alone is insufficient. The repeated requests and belatedly produced factory information support that determination here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Adverse inferences following supported noncooperation, subject to the then-effective information and deficiency provisions; no requirement of perfection or production of genuinely unavailable data.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a properly presented currency-methodology challenge, courts apply the governing valuation statute and ordinary agency review; this adverse-inference holding changes no currency rule.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The operative phrase measures performance against ability. Nippon’s prompt later production and failure to inquire at the relevant facilities support an inference that more could have been done earlier. Deference does not supply those facts; the agency record must. Adding willfulness would allow avoidable investigative failure to disable a statutory mechanism designed for inaccessible foreign records. Ventura also preserves the separate currency-methodology remand: the court cannot replace Commerce's unresolved statutory calculations with its own facts. Affirming the supported adverse inference leaves that distinct component of the mandate intact.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No precedent is overruled; the governing administrative-review principles are applied.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The separate starting-price currency dispute is outside the question.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may prohibit corrupt payments concerning substantial transactions of an entity receiving qualifying federal assistance without tracing the payment to particular federal dollars, because corruption of that recipient’s authorized agents threatens the integrity of the assisted institution. The Government must prove every §666 element beyond a reasonable doubt, including the actual benefits, agency, value and corrupt-intent requirements; the statute’s lack of dollar tracing does not make it facially invalid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The enacted assisted-entity bribery mechanism; mere geographic proximity to federal spending or ordinary commercial receipts are insufficient.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides such a concrete application, courts enforce all §666 elements and the actual assistance-and-agency connection stated here; federal spending somewhere in the community does not suffice.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Salinas establishes what Congress enacted, while Fischer prevents treating all federal payments as assistance. The remaining connection is concrete: corruption of those administering a funded institution can divert its resources and decisions. Protecting that institution is adapted to the spending program without asserting a general federal police power.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Salinas v. United States is applied on statutory meaning and extended to the presented constitutional question. Fischer v. United States retains its benefits limitation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A fact-specific claim that a different prosecution lacks the identified institutional relationship is outside this pretrial facial record.

### Issue 1: Pretrial conditions and continued prospective relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether pretrial detainees challenging longstanding, systemic jail conditions must prove officials’ actual knowledge and disregard of health risks, and whether a nonadjudicative compliance monitor is a special master under 18 U.S.C. §3626(f).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Bell and the existing PLRA rule; resolve the distinct prospective institutional question without extending Farmer’s individual-awareness requirement wholesale.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Systemic pretrial confinement requires a usable institutional rule. Eliminating fault requirements for every damages case would exceed the question. Requiring proof of each official’s private awareness would frustrate correction of demonstrable continuing institutional conditions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For prospective relief from longstanding systemic pretrial confinement conditions, detainees must prove by a preponderance that current conditions expose them to a substantial risk of serious harm and are not reasonably related to, or are excessive in relation to, legitimate nonpunitive objectives. * **Reach Within the Question:** Prospective institutional relief for the pretrial classes, not ordinary negligence, convicted-prisoner Eighth Amendment claims or individual damages liability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a distinct individual-damages claim, courts apply existing due-process and personal-liability law; Farmer continues to govern its Eighth Amendment setting.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bell asks whether pretrial detention conditions amount to punishment without adjudication. Farmer concerns a different constitutional and liability setting. Benjamin v. Jacobson prevents this distinction from turning historic decrees into permanent supervision: present risk and each remedial provision still require supported findings.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Bell v. Wolfish is applied to systemic pretrial conditions; Farmer v. Brennan is distinguished. Benjamin v. Jacobson remains controlling on continued relief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual damages liability and convicted-prisoner claims are outside the presented prospective pretrial dispute.

### Issue 2: Monitoring versus adjudicative mastery

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether pretrial detainees challenging longstanding, systemic jail conditions must prove officials’ actual knowledge and disregard of health risks, and whether a nonadjudicative compliance monitor is a special master under 18 U.S.C. §3626(f).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the functional statutory category, preserving supervision without evasion.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The OCC’s specified powers supply the boundary. Exempting every entity called a monitor would invite circumvention. Treating every information-gathering agent as a master would erase the functional limitation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court-appointed body limited to observing and reporting compliance, without adjudicative powers or findings entitled to judicial deference, is not a special master under §3626(f). The party defending the appointment must establish its actual functions; a body exercising a master’s powers remains subject to the statute regardless of title. The OCC’s identified functions fall outside that category.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Nonadjudicative monitoring only; judicial adoption of binding or presumptively controlling findings requires a separate functional assessment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a materially expanded appointment, courts assess its actual duties under §3626(f) and may not exempt quasi-judicial authority merely by using the monitor label.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute refers to the work of a master, not every person who assists a judge. The OCC supplies operational information while the court independently determines violations and relief. That distinction preserves both useful monitoring and the statutory safeguards against delegated adjudication.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No governing precedent is overruled; the appointment is classified under the statutory definition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A future expansion of the OCC’s actual powers would require facts absent from this record.

### Issue 1: Limits of vehicle search incident to arrest

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Belton authorizes a vehicle search incident to arrest when police first contact the recent occupant after he has left the vehicle, including when he is secured away from it before the search.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose limiting Belton’s categorical authority by the actual protective purpose of the exception; do not portray this limitation as already adopted.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring secured-arrestee problem requires a rule beyond a contact-timing distinction. Reworking all automobile searches would exceed the incident-search question. A first-contact line would preserve an equally purposeless search whenever an officer spoke a moment earlier.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A vehicle search incident to arrest requires a reasonable possibility, at the time of search, that the arrestee can reach the searched area to obtain a weapon or destroy evidence; recent occupancy alone is insufficient once the arrestee is secured away from the vehicle. Thornton’s handcuffed placement in the patrol car defeats this ground, without foreclosing an independently proved lawful basis.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides an additional evidence-gathering exception, courts apply existing probable-cause automobile rules and the access rule stated here; a mere reason to suspect evidence is not an adopted new exception.
* **Transition:** Apply the limitation to Thornton and cases still on direct review under Griffith v. Kentucky. Final convictions remain subject to the existing collateral-review rules; no new retrospective exception or later historical good-faith doctrine is imported.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Chimel identifies the source of the exception, and Knowles refuses a categorical search when its rationale is missing. Belton’s broad vehicle proxy has become disconnected from that rationale when access is eliminated. First-contact timing does not repair the defect. A candid access limitation does, while preserving the Government’s ability to establish a genuinely independent inventory or probable-cause basis.
* **Precedent Treatment:** New York v. Belton is limited insofar as it permits an automatic passenger-compartment search after the arrestee is secured beyond access. Chimel v. California and Knowles v. Iowa supply the retained purpose. Florida v. Thomas supplies no merits rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The alternative inventory/discovery basis needs review of its supporting findings; any other exception requires preservation and record support.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied clearly established Miranda custody law when it found that the seventeen-year-old Alvarado was not in custody during a stationhouse interview.
