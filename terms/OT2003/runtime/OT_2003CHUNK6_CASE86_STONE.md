## 86. Nelson v. Campbell

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical dismissal and remand for the §1983 claim and any necessary interim-relief decision.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require prompt medical and procedural findings concerning alternative access, the claimed risk, available administrative requirements and the stay equities.
* **Remedial Reach:** Nelson’s challenged venous-access procedure and necessary interim relief; no vacatur of the death judgment or general injunction against lethal injection.
* **Practical Relief:** Before the State uses the disputed procedure, the district court must promptly decide whether interim protection is justified and then resolve the supported claim. Any injunction must identify the forbidden procedure and permissible scope; a stay issues only under ordinary equitable requirements and must last no longer than necessary for lawful adjudication.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Procedure-specific challenge and the habeas boundary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a condemned prisoner may use §1983 to challenge an allegedly unnecessary surgical venous-access procedure without necessarily challenging the lawfulness of execution by lethal injection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the necessary-invalidity boundary to the actual relief requested.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. A discrete procedure can be adjudicated without remaking the capital sentence. All method-of-execution challenges would exceed this record. Treating any temporary delay as habeas would make judicial protection of a severable right impossible.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prisoner may proceed under §1983 against a particular execution-related procedure when success would leave the State a lawful means of carrying out the sentence and therefore would not necessarily invalidate that sentence. The prisoner must establish the constitutional claim and separately satisfy applicable procedural and equitable requirements; the availability of §1983 supplies no automatic stay. Nelson’s identified alternative-access claim cannot be dismissed solely as successive habeas.
* **Reach Within the Question:** A severable venous-access challenge with an available lawful execution alternative; a claim necessarily foreclosing the sentence remains governed by habeas law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a challenge necessarily foreclosing every lawful implementation of the sentence, courts apply the existing habeas boundary and determine the actual effect of the requested relief.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Randell requires a claim-specific comparison, reinforcing Heck and Edwards rather than treating every custody-related injury as habeas. The proposed surgery can be separated from the death judgment if another lawful access method exists. Preiser therefore does not compel reclassification merely because a court may need time to adjudicate the issue. The State’s timing objection belongs in the independent equitable analysis.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Randell v. Johnson, Heck v. Humphrey, Edwards v. Balisok and Preiser v. Rodriguez are applied to the requested remedy; the former-prisoner exception is unnecessary here.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The medical merits and justification for a stay require findings absent from this threshold judgment; methods that leave no lawful execution alternative lie outside the presented claim.
