## 2. Historical positions verified from the assigned writings

The historical joint opinion's Title II reasoning is that of Stevens and O'Connor, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, as the authorized comparator records. Additional judgment concurrence does not become an additional join in that reasoning. Scalia's opening paragraph is indispensable: he joins Kennedy except insofar as Kennedy upholds section 323(e) and section 202; he joins Thomas only in Parts I, II-A and II-B. Kennedy's asterisk identifies Thomas as joining his section 213 analysis only. Do not convert these partial joins into a common all-purpose dissent.

### Primary definition, treasury restriction and nonprofits

The joint opinion treats the primary definition as definite, rejects a constitutional magic-words prerequisite, and separately sustains section 203's treasury restriction against the facial overbreadth challenge. It relies on Austin's corporate-structure concern, the voluntary-fund route, the record's concentration of election-directed advertisements, and the relative scope of protected applications. It does not require a literal electoral slogan. Note 88 expressly assumes that interests supporting regulation of campaign speech may not apply to genuine issue advertisements. Notes 77–78 supply the consultant and Yellowtail examples; they are judicial record discussion, not findings that every issue advertisement is election advocacy.

The five invoke MCFL's three historical characteristics and preserve constitutional nonprofit protection by construction of section 204. Note 90 distinguishes the section 203 statutory exception from section 204's targeted-communication withdrawal. Neither the statutory exception nor the Court's constitutional protection is a general exemption for every organization with nonprofit tax status.

Kennedy would overrule Austin and invalidate section 203. Independently, he would invalidate it even under Austin because its candidate/time/geography proxies suppress substantial genuine issue speech and the segregated-fund alternative imposes organizational and practical burdens. Scalia joins these grounds and separately explains why corporate identity and resource pooling do not remove speech protection. Kennedy would not save section 204 by attributing an MCFL exception to its text; he regards the enactment as deliberately expansive, while maintaining the constitutional rights of all speakers. Scalia's Kennedy join extends to that analysis.

### Ordinary section 201 reporting and section 304(f)(5)

The joint opinion sustains ordinary reporting on electoral information, anticorruption and enforcement grounds, while preserving the reasonable-probability threats/harassment/reprisals exception. It rejects the current facial record's claimed need for a blanket donor exception, not every possible future supported exemption. It reverses the district's entire contract-clause severance: actual obligations and deferred payment can otherwise prevent timely preelection information, and the record does not establish the speculative disadvantages asserted for advance disclosure. Note 84 specifically identifies the thin contract-performance evidence and the absence of record support for Kennedy's speculation about disadvantage.

Kennedy accepts ordinary disclosure on its informational ground alone. He rejects the majority's anticorruption/enforcement explanation insofar as it depends on invalid restraints on independent soft-money activity. He would invalidate advance disclosure because publication of an unbroadcast contractual plan can expose strategy and facilitate pressure against its airing; requiring disclosure when the advertisement runs supplies a less burdensome response to deferred payment. Scalia joins this distinction. This historical position already combines strong independent-expression protection with acceptance of ordinary disclosure. Current ordinary-report precedents therefore do not, by themselves, explain any new vote by either Justice to sustain a materially different disclosure burden.

Thomas's independent disclosure grounds are outside this helper's source partition and must come from the parent's full ZX1 verification. His joins cannot be inferred from Kennedy's ordinary-reporting concurrence.

### Sections 202 and 214

The joint opinion sustains section 202 because actual coordinated disbursement can function as a contribution even when the communication lacks express advocacy. It sustains section 214(a)'s party coordination rule, rejecting a constitutional formal-agreement prerequisite: complying with a request or suggestion can confer the same practical benefit without prior agreement. It also rejects vagueness based on the familiar statutory cooperation/consultation/concert/request/suggestion language. Its evidence discussion and holding do not equate ordinary policy agreement with coordination. It leaves attacks on implementing regulations to an appropriate separate proceeding and affirms nonripeness only to that extent for sections 214(b) and (c).

Kennedy's distinctions are different and must be preserved. He would invalidate section 214(a)'s restriction on party-coordinated activity that remains independent of a candidate. He would sustain section 202 for candidate coordination, sever its party-coordination text, and independently reject its advance-contract aspect. The latter timing objection is not erased by his acceptance of candidate coordination. He agrees that sections 214(b) and (c) are not presently justiciable. Scalia withholds the part of Kennedy's section 202 conclusion that sustains candidate coordination; his historical position therefore cannot be described as Kennedy's complete candidate-versus-party rule.

The assigned writings do not provide a freestanding historical section 211 vote or a separate section 214(d) treatment. Section 211 is nevertheless live in the validated question map. Note 95 reproduces its amended independent-expenditure definition. Preserve its express-advocacy element and the absence of candidate/party cooperation; rejection of a definition challenge does not decide that every excluded communication may constitutionally be suppressed. The section 214(d) result must rest on the verified amendment and review scope, not a historical lineup inferred from silence.

### Section 212

The joint opinion recounts the district's ripeness dismissal under the dissemination-trigger regulation but explicitly doubts whether an agency rule can make nonjusticiable a facial challenge to a concededly broader speech-burdening statute. It then declines a separate section 212 determination because the section 201 decision makes the issue essentially moot. That is not a pure ripeness affirmance and must not be summarized as one.

Kennedy says the claim is ripe: the statutory command remains despite the agency's failure to implement its entire reach. He would invalidate the advance-reporting requirement for the same reasons as section 201. His initial description of the majority as finding nonripeness is qualified by his ensuing citation recognizing the majority's actual refusal to decide ripeness. Scalia's Kennedy join includes this analysis. No independent Thomas section 212 conclusion is inferred here.

### Section 213

The joint opinion affirms invalidation of the reciprocal choice. It carefully distinguishes the special party allowance from the ordinary $5,000 contribution/coordinated-spending route that remains after independent spending. The independent-expenditure definition covers express advocacy, so exercising the special allowance forfeits that category rather than every conceivable independent communication. The burden nevertheless reaches protected speech. The justification is undermined both by the narrow magic-words coverage and the committee aggregation that prevents a genuinely independent voluntary choice.

Note 96 reserves the national-versus-state aggregation interpretation: even the narrower grouping permits one local committee to bind other committees within its State. A disposition need not settle that interpretive puzzle to invalidate the forced choice. Note 93 includes the transfer/assignment/receipt rule; the judgment affirms invalidity of section 213 as a unit.

Kennedy concurs in judgment on a different ground: parties possess First Amendment interests in both independent and coordinated expression, so the provision forces surrender of one constitutional right for another. Scalia and Thomas join that section. The principal opinion's statutory-benefit rationale is not a complete description of their historical grounds.

## 3. Reconciliation assessment against actual law and frozen commitments

### A. Supported differences in the primary/source and nonprofit reasoning

The five's frozen facial result can survive verification, but its current-law grounds must remain narrower than a transplant of historical Beaumont. The actual Beaumont adjudication concerns NCRL's direct contributions, requires a cumulative source/channel justification and leaves independent spending outside review. Day protects actual voluntary associations beyond a literal MCFL checklist. Bartlett independently protects genuine issue discussion and requires definite expenditure coverage. Those are concrete changed legal premises for all five, supported by their actual joins. They explain express constitutional limits and the actual-characteristics nonprofit protection without requiring reversal of the five's basic facial result. The historical footnote 88 also means that preserving genuine-issue applications is not wholly a new historical departure.

For Scalia and Kennedy, their frozen refusal to extend treasury restrictions follows both historical concerns and their actual Maine/Bartlett and chosen-channel positions. The frozen refusal to overrule Austin differs from their historical preferred breadth, but actual Day, Maine, Mariani and Beaumont dispositions preserved Austin's surviving setting and developed a narrower basis for resolving the newly expanded reach. A judgment confined to that expansion has a supported current-law/remedial ground; it must not be reported as their historical Austin position. Thomas requires his own parent-verified historical comparison.

### B. Fallback is a material issue requiring a more exact answer

The five who sustain the primary scheme have the historical note 73 reason not to reach fallback. Removing the lower court's unnecessary fallback declaration, including its deletion of the last limiter, is consistent with that result.

The three contrary commitments require more work. Kennedy historically accepted ordinary primary-definition reporting while still concluding that invalidating section 203 under the primary definition required him to address backup. The survival of reporting alone therefore supplies no changed premise for his frozen nonactivation conclusion. Scalia joins Kennedy's fallback rejection. A narrower remedy against only newly expanded nonexpress treasury coverage, leaving lawful existing categories and the primary definition operative elsewhere, may differ from Kennedy's preferred total section 203 invalidation. But section 401 cannot defeat the express fallback activation clause if the final judgment actually finds the primary definition insufficient to support the relevant regulation. The parent should state precisely what application is severed and why that judgment does or does not satisfy the trigger; a definition-versus-application label is insufficient by itself.

The conditional constructions are also not historical matches. Kennedy expressly rejected the enacted no-plausible-meaning limiter as insufficient to cure backup vagueness and breadth; Scalia joined him. Actual Maine's majority construction is a changed authoritative treatment of cumulative language, while their own Maine dissent continues to reject context supplying an absent electoral command. The frozen narrow message-supplied-electoral-direction construction can draw on that current dissent and the newly enacted statutory words, but it must expressly distinguish their historical fallback objection. No conditional branch becomes current law merely because it appears in the commitment table.

### C. Coordination departures have support, with two necessary limits

Actual Lamb supplies an unusually concrete changed premise for Scalia and Thomas: their full joins accept regulation of candidate-controlled resources and genuine tacit conduits under the contribution standard, while their actual Colorado II/Lamb separate positions preserve the party's own joint message. This differs from the historical wholesale attack on contribution restraints joined by Scalia. It can support retaining real-resource coordination controls without resurrecting a formal-agreement requirement.

Kennedy's actual Colorado I/II and Lamb positions likewise supply a finer resource-versus-message distinction than the historical section 202 candidate-versus-party split. His historical candidate-coordination acceptance does not automatically resolve an actual own-message claim under those intervening public commitments. Conversely, the actual three-Justice expression position is expressly party-specific in Colorado and Lamb; Clifton protects policy inquiry and independent editorial control, not every third party's jointly arranged campaign service. The frozen phrase protecting an association's own jointly expressed message should not become universal immunity from contribution treatment merely because an outside spender describes campaign work as its expression.

A second unresolved comparison concerns section 202 contracts. Kennedy and Scalia historically reject the advance-contract aspect separately. Frozen section 5.A and the remedy table do not separately explain it. The parent should test whether an executed candidate-requested obligation already supplies a genuine financial resource, unlike merely reporting an unbroadcast message. Lamb could support that distinction, but it is not a reason to infer a new join without answering the specific objection. Equally, the advance-disclosure result cannot mechanically decide contribution classification: information exposure and transfer of a financial benefit have different triggers and interests.

The five's section 202/214 sustaining ground, with actual expenditure connection and inquiry exclusions, is compatible with their current holdings. Section 214(b)/(c)'s actual regulatory-vacuum and future-application limits remain distinct from a facial merits declaration about every regulation. Section 211 should remain expressly resolved at its independent definitional scope.

### D. Section 201 ordinary and contract reporting

Scalia and Kennedy's ordinary-reporting commitments match the historical informational acceptance in result, with actual Stengel and their disclosure alternatives providing independent current support. Their narrow application remedy for contracts is also defensible: their historical objection is to advance exposure, not to reporting a debt after the corresponding speech is public. Application severance preserves the anti-evasion function. The same statute permitted application severance historically, so the change should be described as a more precisely delimited remedy supported by their actual ordinary-disclosure acceptance, not as newly discovered severability language.

The five's contract result matches history. Their current explanation must preserve the actual binding obligation, finite disclosure category and difference between financial information and publication of a script. It cannot find the feared disadvantage nonexistent merely because the historical opinion called the evidentiary basis thin. Thomas's reporting and attribution departures are for the parent to reconcile from his complete writing and actual Stengel position.

### E. Section 212: frozen unanimity is not supported by the historical comparison as presently explained

The regulatory public-distribution trigger, mandatory statutory contract language and distinction from section 201 were all present historically. The new opinion cannot treat discovery of that regulation as a changed factual premise. National Park Hospitality is expressly about a nonbinding policy that changes no legal duties or review rights; it is not authority that an agency rule can eliminate a clear statutory duty. Actual Lee permits review of presently applicable civil obligations, and Maine/Bartlett reject postponement of supported recurring speech disputes without an effective binding answer to the threat. These sources sharpen, rather than answer away, Kennedy's historical statutory-command objection.

For Kennedy and Scalia, the current freeze gives no concrete change that adequately explains abandonment of their historical reach-and-invalidate position while retaining their advance-disclosure objection under section 201. A no-present-demand theory is arguable only after resolving why the regulation lawfully removes the asserted statutory obligation; saying the claim is not about the regulation's ultimate validity leaves that necessary answer missing. Their National Park joins do not supply it because the distinction between nonbinding policy and operative duty is explicit. Their narrow contract-remedy approach supports reaching only the asserted advance application, without deciding the entire reporting system.

For Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, the historical opinion supplies no affirmative ripeness commitment. Their current acceptance of section 201 may make section 212's parallel constitutional objection fail or unnecessary to analyze separately, as historically; adopting a distinct universal nonripeness holding needs an independently sufficient explanation under Lee and the actual statute. Stevens's National Park standing-only position, and O'Connor/Breyer's absence from that rationale, preclude treating all eight as already committed to one ripeness theory. Thomas must be checked independently against ZX1; his current ordinary-disclosure acceptance changes some merits premises but does not itself erase a statutory duty.

Recommendation to parent: do not certify the section 212 row as historically confirmed. Reconcile its current legal obligation and remedy explicitly; if retained as threshold dismissal, supply the actual source of binding protection and a Justice-specific explanation. Otherwise preserve the historically supported reach of the advance issue and test the already frozen narrow merits alternatives. No new factual default or lack of claimant evidence is established by this verification.

### F. Section 213 remains a sound common judgment with distinct grounds

The all-eight invalidation is supported. Actual Colorado I protects true independence; actual Colorado II does not authorize a conclusive choice eliminating it after permitted coordination. The three's current joint-expression position strengthens their objection to the reciprocal condition, while preserving separate regulation of actual resources. The same entire-section remedy is consistent with the historical judgment and the integrated function of its aggregation and transfer provisions. Retain the ordinary $5,000 route, the special-allowance distinction, and the unresolved breadth of committee aggregation as an unnecessary interpretive question, not an assumed nationwide first-mover rule.

## 5. Recommended formulations supplied to parent

**Fallback:** The three's preferred section 203 disposition should acknowledge that finding the primary definition constitutionally insufficient to support the expanded treasury regulation requires examining the complete backup for that regulation. Vacate the lower deletion of the final no-plausible-meaning limiter. Their actual Maine/Bartlett grounds can support backup coverage only where the communication itself supplies an explicit or functionally explicit exhortation to vote for or against the identified candidate; ordinary context may identify the candidate or explain the words but may not supply an absent electoral command. Any plausible nonelectoral meaning excludes coverage. The enacted word plausible should not be silently replaced by reasonable or by a regulator's preferred contextual reading. The backup has no imported primary time or geographic limit. Primary section 201 reporting remains independently valid, and no extension of disclosure to the backup's different category is inferred from the source-regulation ruling.

That is a concrete alternative disposition of the source restriction, not a declaration that fallback is unnecessary whenever reporting survives. Its operative use depends on the final judgment actually rejecting the primary definition for that regulation. If the final Court judgment sustains the primary scheme, fallback remains inactive. The parent's historical-departure explanation must acknowledge that Kennedy and Scalia historically rejected even the complete backup limiter; their actual Maine dissent's preservation of message-supplied functional electoral commands, together with Bartlett's definite issue-speech boundary, supplies the materially narrower construction now proposed. Neither primary-reporting survival nor section 401 alone supplies that changed premise.

**Section 212:** A five-Justice rejection of the duplicate advance-timing objection on their section 201 actual-obligation ground, without adopting the lower ripeness rationale, accords with the historical comparison. For the three with an advance-exposure objection, reach the asserted statutory contract obligation and preserve only relief against compelled reporting before public dissemination; the effective rule already protects that conduct and need not itself be invalidated. No broader reporting invalidation follows from their timing objection. Each Justice's actual grounds remain necessary, with Thomas's historical source verified by the parent.

**Section 202 timing:** The parent's proposed resolution is supportable if it expressly concerns an enforceable purchase commitment, actually requested or coordinated, that supplies a candidate or party with a genuine campaign financial resource before airing. Actual Lamb accepts that functional category, including tacit conduits, and preserves the contribution standard. Disclosure of an unrelated future private message under sections 201/212 has a different information-exposure burden. For Kennedy and Scalia this is a real departure from their historical advance-contract objection and requires that specific explanation. The three's own-party-message distinction and Clifton's independent issue/editorial inquiry limit must remain intact; the word contract does not convert protected independent preparation into a transferred campaign resource.
