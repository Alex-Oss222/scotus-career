## 89. Republic of Austria v. Altmann — June 7, 2004

### Reconciled common rationale

**A1 — Reach of the current immunity scheme.** The FSIA governs this post-enactment suit's claim to sovereign immunity although the alleged conduct preceded both 1976 and the 1952 restrictive-immunity policy. Sections 1330, 1602 and 1604 form an integrated current regime; the relevant exceptions retain their own predicates. Section 1602's direction that courts henceforth decide immunity claims within that regime concerns the immunity assertion now presented, not a newly created substantive property claim.

**A2 — Text, function and temporal analysis.** The answer does not follow from calling the statute jurisdictional, nor from the word henceforth taken alone as an express command covering past conduct. Landgraf's provision-specific inquiry and Hughes Aircraft's warning about newly enlarged enforcement exposure remain effective. Foreign-state immunity, however, supplies present protection from the inconvenience of a United States suit in a field historically governed by changing political-branch decisions; it did not guarantee that the forum policy existing when the conduct occurred would remain fixed. The FSIA leaves the substantive source of the property claim intact. Its present-immunity function, text and comprehensive structure together resolve the uncertainty; no general exception for governmental defendants or all immunity statutes is announced. Dole Food supports attention to filing-time statutory status and present forum protection without itself deciding this temporal question.

**A3 — Disposition and scope.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit on the temporal issue and allow the property litigation to proceed. The separate determination below that the expropriation exception applies remains outside the granted question. Ownership and taking are allegations at this stage. The grant does not decide title, act of state, limitations or execution. Reject the proposed exception-by-exception historical cutoff as the governing temporal construction: the enacted integrated scheme contains no general schedule dividing its present immunity determinations according to each exception's historical origin.

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support A1–A3 in full at this level. The common rationale recognizes the force and limits of Landgraf rather than treating forum terminology as a complete answer. No concurrence's additional rationale is silently counted as a common holding.

## 90. Central Laborers' Pension Fund v. Heinz — June 7, 2004

### Reconciled operative rule and application

**H1 — Protected payment entitlement.** Under section 204(g)'s anti-cutback rule, a plan amendment reduces an accrued early-retirement benefit when it adds an employment condition withholding payments that the participant could receive under the terms governing accrual. The nominal amount does not preserve the benefit when a new condition reduces the right to receive it. The loss occurs when the added restriction diminishes the accrued entitlement; an actual payment suspension demonstrates the injury here but is not what alone makes the amendment a cutback.

**H2 — Separate statutory commands.** Section 203(a)(3)(B) permits specified original suspension terms without treating the benefit as forfeitable; it does not authorize adding such a term after accrual contrary to section 204(g). In the multiemployer setting described, the suspension permission concerns work in the same industry, trade or craft and geographic area covered by the plan. Accrual and vesting are distinct statutory inquiries: absence of prohibited forfeiture does not establish absence of prohibited cutback. Lawful terms may govern future accruals, while already earned benefits retain their protected terms.

**H3 — Application and remedy.** The 1998 addition of previously permitted supervisory construction work to disqualifying employment violates the current anti-cutback command as applied to these accrued benefits. Affirm the Seventh Circuit's reversal of the plan's judgment on the pleadings. The case returns for restoration of payments withheld solely under the added restriction and the necessary benefit calculation.

All eight Associates support H1–H3. Spink and Jacobson retain sponsor design authority together with independent accrued-benefit limits; neither grants authority for this amendment. Section 204(g)'s exceptions for substantial business hardship under section 302(c)(8), 29 U.S.C. §1082(c)(8), and terminated multiemployer plans under 29 U.S.C. §1441 are outside the supplied claim. The holding applies to the challenged added employment condition, not an amendment invoking those separate statutory mechanisms.

The source's formal-regulation discussion confirms the same distinction already identified in the packet: 26 CFR §1.411(d)-4, A-6 permits objective conditions specified before accrual; A-2(a)(1) allows changes concerning benefits not yet accrued; A-7 prohibits adding conditions or further restrictions to an already accrued protected benefit. Its force is distinct from an unreasoned manual statement or inconsistent informal approval practice. The reconciled statutory ground does not depend on finding a new administrative reliance fact or deciding plan tax qualification. No post-event administrative measure enters.

## 92. City of Littleton v. Z. J. Gifts D-4, L.L.C. — June 7, 2004

### Reconciled propositions and distinct grounds

**L1 — Prompt judicial determination.** For the protected-expression licensing scheme presented, the safeguard against undue delay includes a prompt judicial determination, not merely an opportunity to file suit promptly. A potentially erroneous licensing denial cannot suppress protected expression indefinitely while the proceeding remains unresolved. FW/PBS's core protection against delay includes the judicial stage. Six Associates adopt this proposition: O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**L2 — Facial sufficiency of ordinary procedures.** A special fixed judicial deadline is not required for this scheme's objective, nondiscretionary licensing criteria. The available ordinary Colorado procedures supply powers of expedition and interim protection; their ordinary administration can produce prompt decisions, with judicial intervention when necessary to avoid significant First Amendment injury. Judges may be expected to use those tools consistently with the constitutional protection, and this facial record supplies no actual delayed appeal establishing their inadequacy. These considerations sustain the ordinance against the judicial-timing facial objection without placing a separate numerical judicial timetable in the ordinance. O'Connor, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this reasoning together with Stevens, subject to his different understanding of the underlying safeguard. This is a five-Associate proposition coalition.

**L3 — Judgment and next stage.** Reverse the Tenth Circuit's facial invalidation on judicial timing and remand consistently with the governing decision. The ordinance remains operative against that objection. A concrete application involving undue delay may require expedition, interim protection or other appropriate relief. That is application of the governing protection, not a claim that delay occurred here. All eight Associates reach this judgment, but their sufficient grounds differ.

**L-S — Stevens's distinct procedural ground.** Objective adult-business licensing is ministerial regulation rather than the direct content censorship addressed in Freedman. FW/PBS requires a prompt administrative decision and the possibility of prompt judicial review, but does not put the full censorship-system burden of ensuring a prompt judicial determination on the licensor. Ordinary court procedures supply the adequate possibility of promptness for this facial challenge. Stevens rejects equating that protection with Freedman's stronger requirement, because doing so risks either imposing censorship burdens on ordinary licensing or weakening the censorship safeguard. He joins L2 and L3, not L1 as formulated.

**L-SK — Souter's and Kennedy's distinct adequacy ground.** These Justices join L1 and the judgment and agree that a rigid Freedman-style timetable is unnecessary. They withhold assent from an adequacy explanation resting on the mere availability of judicial tools and an undifferentiated characterization of the scheme as neutral. Although the denial criteria are objective, adult-business licensing is triggered by the expressive materials to be sold, which creates a risk that unpopular sellers will encounter delay. The procedures must be accompanied by actual judicial practice providing prompt disposition; evidence of foot-dragging requires immediate intervention, and judicial oversight or review must be expeditious at every stage. The facial challenge fails without evidence that the available procedures cannot furnish that protection. Their ground is not a vote that ordinary procedures fail here.

**L-Sc — Scalia's sufficient conditional ground.** Even assuming protected activity, a general permit requirement unrelated to censorship of expressive content gives the business no special constitutional priority in the judicial process; the supplied ordinary procedures defeat the asserted demand for special timing rules. He therefore joins the judgment, without L1 or the lead opinion's affirmative timing entitlement. His earlier FW/PBS position additionally denies constitutional protection to the defined category of sex-pandering business. The approved packet does not supply the detailed ordinance definition or an adjudicated pandering predicate needed to make that broader ground an operative factual premise here. His historical Littleton writing expressly supplies the conditional ground first, which is sufficient without adding that premise. This is preservation of an independently stated sufficient ground, not adoption of protected status by Scalia or invention of an obscenity finding.

## 93. Belitskus v. Pizzingrilli — June 10, 2004

### Frozen common propositions retained

**B1 — Actual financial exclusion.** An otherwise qualified candidate unable to pay the ballot fee without financial hardship must have a realistic nonmonetary route. Mandatory payment without such a route severely excludes that candidate and associated electoral interests; under Anderson–Burdick as stated in Cartwright, the restriction requires narrow tailoring to a compelling interest. Administrative expense and screening seriousness do not justify choosing candidates by ability to pay when a usable support-based or hardship alternative serves the legitimate interest. Reasonable fees for candidates able to pay and lawful qualification and support requirements remain available.

**B2 — Application.** Sustain the inability-to-pay challenge of Stith, Linzey and the Pennsylvania Green Party. Bullock and Lubin protect against actual inability-to-pay exclusion, not ordinary inconvenience or a preference for a cheaper route. Reasonable proof of hardship may be required; the alternative must be usable. No financial threshold or affidavit form is invented.

**B3 — Justiciability.** The recurring candidate and party injuries support adjudication for Stith, Linzey and the party. Donovan's individual claim is moot because the requisite likelihood of recurrence to him is absent; affirm the direction to dismiss his claim. Schrader's continuing-party holding does not preserve every voter's completed-election claim.

All eight Associates support B1–B3. The legal rule's precedential reach is distinct from the permissible scope of the injunction.

**B4 — Fitted candidate-and-party relief.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the substantive ruling and Donovan disposition, vacate the appellate injunction restriction insofar as it caps enforceable relief at Stith and Linzey without securing the party's established recurring injury, and remand for a specific order addressing those actual plaintiff interests. Rule 65(d) requires concrete hardship conditions and official duties. The State may choose a meaningful lawful alternative; the order's scope must fit the proven operation and remedial need. The district court's open-ended protection of every financially unable candidate is not automatically restored, and party standing is not class certification.

**B5 — Current named-candidate limitation.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the appellate limitation to Stith and Linzey when they demonstrate the relevant hardship, together with the candidate-and-party merits disposition and Donovan dismissal. They regard those terms as adequate on the established as-applied record. A wider operative order requires a concrete finding that distinct proved party injury remains unremedied; standing alone does not supply that remedial necessity. Specificity remains required.
