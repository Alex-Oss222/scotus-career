## Components and operative ground

Affirm the free-and-clear sale as applied to the represented EEOC claimants' pre-sale employment claims and the represented travel-voucher class. Under section 363(f), an asserted successor obligation can be an interest in transferred property when it would attach to the purchaser through acquisition and continuation of the transferred operating enterprise; the statute is not confined to liens or strictly in-rem interests. Its application still requires the actual property connection and an independent enacted condition. Here paragraph (5) is satisfied because the identified obligations could be compelled into monetary satisfaction in bankruptcy liquidation, not simply because money would be more convenient. The voucher entitlement is a travel benefit capable of monetary replacement; the reviewed pre-sale employment remedies likewise are reducible to monetary claims on this record. Valuation and distribution remain subject to ordinary claim and priority law.

The disposition adopts neither a release of all general unsecured debts against every purchaser nor the lower court's suggested priority-policy alternative. Dow Corning's prohibition of releases through general plan powers remains effective. Sections 105(a), generalized equity, the priority scheme and the prospect of preserving employment cannot independently dispense with the specific requirements of section 363(f). The validity of nonbankruptcy successor liability is assumed for this review, as below. No property connection is inferred solely because every employer necessarily owns assets: the decisive relation is the successor claim asserted against acquisition and continuation of this enterprise. No finding of adequate notice binds an absent person whose notice was not established. Purchaser misconduct after closing and future statutory compliance duties are distinct.

The ancillary appeal from refusal of a limited stay is moot upon the final merits disposition. No new injunction, independent application or immediate claim distribution is ordered. The consummated sale remains effective within the affirmed scope.

## Enhanced comparison of lawful paths

**Statutory affirmation:** The words any interest and the separate reference to an interest that is a lien in paragraph (3) reject an equation of interest with lien. The actual successor theory follows acquisition of the operating enterprise, and paragraph (5) supplies a separate legally available monetary-satisfaction route. Its strongest objection is that property interests include nonlien rights without encompassing personal employment liabilities. The answer is confined to the asserted transfer-dependent successor interest, not the general debt: the acquisition is the proposed source of American's obligation. General insolvency alone could not reach an independent nondebtor claim.

**Reversal under a property-specific construction:** This path would distinguish leaseholds, servitudes and liens from a personal employment obligation and preserve the successor claim subject to its actual nonbankruptcy elements. Dow Corning and Hartford make this a serious reading rather than a policy objection. Its strongest difficulty is the enacted breadth of interest, the transfer-dependent nature of the particular successor claims and the independent paragraph (5) mechanism. On this record the Associates choose the bounded statutory construction above. Their affirmance does not adopt the lower opinion's broader but-for-assets language as a universal test.

**Predicate remand:** A remand would be appropriate if the legal availability of money satisfaction or transfer connection were genuinely unresolved. The lower recitations establish these predicates for the actual claimant groups, including conversion in liquidation and valuation of vouchers. The proponent cannot substitute the favorable purchase price for either predicate. No remand is ordered merely to restate established facts. The order is read in the scope of the claims before the Court; a future absent person's objection remains independently available.

## Components, evidence and shared legal limits

The unresolved identities principally concern immigration detainees and material witnesses; criminal-defendant names and counsel were already released. Requested dates, locations, detention reasons and attorney identities are distinct information categories. Reynolds's declarations predict intimidation, impaired cooperation, misleading evidence and revelation of investigative direction. These are predictive submissions, not findings that every detainee had terrorist connections. Release or deportation does not necessarily terminate enforcement relevance, but historical inclusion alone does not establish its continuation. Watson's discussion of evidence aired at hearings does not by itself establish that every name presents that same risk.

FOIA assigns DOJ the exemption burden under §552(a)(4)(B). Exemption 7(A) asks whether production could reasonably be expected to interfere with enforcement proceedings. Robbins Tire permits functional categories; Landano's exact confidentiality holding concerns 7(D), and its separate agency-burden and supported-category principles remain relevant without converting 7(A) into 7(D). O'Connor's unanimous Klamath opinion requires the actual exemption conditions rather than an important governmental purpose alone. Predictive security expertise deserves substantial weight, including cumulative effects, without a new national-security exemption or a requirement to prove completed injury.

The opposing statutory paths are (A) accept these declarations and the investigative compilation as a sufficient functional category, or (B) reject the blanket coverage and remand for a supported connection between the retained information categories and interference, with appropriate protected proceedings and segregability review. A categorical showing may avoid individual public descriptions. Protected submissions are available when justified, not universally mandatory. The objection to A is that it treats inclusion in the initial investigation as sufficient indefinitely and fails to account for admitted variation among persons and information. The objection to B is that disclosing which persons remain useful can itself expose investigative knowledge. B can answer that objection through protected review and categories defined by actual common characteristics; it need not compel the disclosure used to establish an exemption.

Neither path justifies immediate wholesale release. Exemption 7(C) privacy, 7(F) safety, exemption 3 with Rule 6(e), and an actually applicable sealing order require their separate predicates. A material-witness label is not itself a finding that every requested item reveals grand-jury proceedings or falls within a seal. The existence and scope of a sealing order may be established without disclosing the protected material. Segregability applies to nonexempt portions and does not defeat established protection merely because names can be removed.

On independent access, United Reporting distinguishes speech-selected access conditions from a general information entitlement; this request presents no speech-selected allocation. North Jersey protects adversarial removal hearings on their history and adjudicative function, not every executive record related to those hearings. The asserted common-law route to this same agency compilation cannot bypass FOIA's enacted disclosure and exemption arrangement. # OT2003 chunk 3 — Nebraska v. EPA — frozen non-Stone commitments

**Event:** Merits review, January 8, 2004.
**Inputs:** Approved neutral packet; task A preflight; `OT_2003CHUNK3_A.md`; 331 F.3d 995. Research cutoff: January 8, 2004. No target comparator or private Stone position received. Eight Associates participate. These are provisional commitments, not a Court judgment or assignment.

## Proposed component dispositions and rules

Affirm denial of the petition for review. Reject the facial Commerce Clause attack on the Safe Drinking Water Act's regulation of noncommunicable contaminants because the covered water systems include established interstate utility supply and sales. Congress may prescribe safety standards for water supplied in interstate commercial transactions; the noncommunicability of the contamination does not remove the commodity or transaction from commerce. A challenge demanding categorical invalidation cannot disregard those valid applications. No general adjudication of a particular wholly local system is necessary or supported by the presented as-applied record.

Reject the anti-commandeering challenge. The Act regulates public and private suppliers in their capacity as water-system operators. It does not require States to enact federal policy or direct their officers to enforce federal rules against unrelated persons. Actual work and expense of complying with a valid rule governing one's own operation do not alone constitute commandeering. Condon, New York and Printz retain their distinct boundaries; the government's authority to regulate an activity is separate from authority to conscript sovereign enforcement machinery.

Preserve the treatment of the rule-specific objections as unpreserved. An agency's lack of power to invalidate the governing statute permits the facial legislative challenge without futile presentation. That does not excuse failing to present objections to the agency's own regulatory implementation when it could respond, interpret its mandate or develop pertinent evidence. The preserved constitutional question does not reopen the scientific selection of the arsenic limit. This is an application of the actual failure to present and conceded waiver; it creates no universal exhaustion rule for all constitutional objections.

The .01 mg/L rule's 2006 compliance date remains what the applicable rule provides. Affirmance creates no finding of current violation, new duty, penalty or accelerated deadline.

## Alternative-path test

**Direct-commercial ground:** Interstate water supply and sales furnish a concrete connection before aggregation. The strongest contrary argument invokes local distribution and noncommunicable contaminants. Those features could matter to a properly developed different application but do not invalidate direct regulation of an interstate commodity. No chain through generalized healthcare costs is needed. The record source identifies the interstate systems, including the appellate judicial-notice basis; the Court need not invent a new market.

**Broader class-wide or necessary-inclusion ground:** The utility market and prevention of regulatory evasion might support some wholly intrastate applications on an appropriate record. Holston, Ho and Deaton illustrate independently identified mechanisms, but none supplies a substitute water-market or hydrological finding here. The proposed judgment rests on the sufficient interstate-transaction ground; it does not add a statewide or nationwide holding about every private well or local system. The alternative cannot justify a broader rule without its own application and support.

**Commandeering invalidation:** Petitioners' strongest position is that public employees must implement federal water policy at local expense. Condon and Baker distinguish compliance in one's own covered activity from compulsory government of others. No challenged duty here commands a state legislature or recruits state officers to regulate third parties; the asserted burden is therefore ordinary operator compliance. Expense alone cannot transform that duty into the command New York and Printz prohibit.

**Excused rule objection:** Futility would matter if EPA could do nothing pertinent with the objection. Its inability to invalidate Congress's statute does not establish that premise for the rule. Implementation and supporting evidence remain agency functions. The decision preserves the statutory challenge while retaining the specific procedural bar on regulatory objections, rather than equating the two.

## Proposed disposition and controlling scope

Reverse the Eleventh Circuit's categorical exclusion and remand. An investment contract can promise a fixed return. Howey's expected profits encompass the investment income sought by a person who commits money to a common enterprise and relies on the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts of others; the return need not vary with the enterprise's earnings. A contractual entitlement to payment does not by itself eliminate that reliance: the economic arrangement and the activity on which performance depends must be examined as a whole. The statutory investment-contract inquiry does not turn on calling the acquired object a payphone or separating its nominal purchase from the marketed management-and-leaseback arrangement.

Howey applied the investment-contract term to a combined property and service arrangement; Forman distinguished obtaining a consumptive benefit from seeking an investment return. Reves's treatment of the separately enumerated note category does not establish that only variable earnings can be profits under Howey. A guarantee defines the promisor's obligation; it does not establish that the promised return is independent of the managerial activity of the enterprise responsible for paying it.

The decision removes the fixed-return and contractual-entitlement exclusions. It does not hold that every equipment lease, loan, contract bearing interest or ordinary consumer purchase is an investment contract. Money investment, common enterprise, the required expectation and dependence on others' efforts remain separate requirements; the other statutory categories and their exceptions retain their terms. The promised return and economic structure permit application of the securities laws, but the preliminary record does not conclusively prove fraud or any disputed enforcement element. No ultimate finding that payments came from later investors is made from the SEC's allegation.

Remand requires application of the correct definition and ordinary proof and remedial requirements. Reversal does not itself restore all preliminary relief, determine final liability, award recovery or conclusively resolve every Howey element. The lower courts must decide the surviving matters within the actual scope of the enforcement action.

## Alternative-path assessment

**Include fixed income within expected investment profits:** A return to an investor for committing capital is investment income whether fixed or contingent. The strongest contrary argument is that Forman described capital appreciation or participation in earnings. That language identifies investment benefit in contrast with consumption; it did not adjudicate or exclude every other form of investment income. Howey's whole-arrangement approach and its rejection of a speculative-enterprise prerequisite supply the relevant rule.

**Categorically exclude a contractual fixed return:** This path would treat an enforceable promise as severing dependence on the manager and would treat profits as necessarily variable. Its strongest support is formal certainty of the debt and the wording concerning profits in prior descriptions. The obligation's enforceability does not supply the money needed for performance, and Howey itself concerns contractual arrangements. The proposed exclusion confuses legal entitlement with the economic activity supplying investment income and has no basis in the enacted category or the surviving test.

**Declare every remaining element established:** The offered payphone arrangement places operational responsibilities with the promoter and is marketed for income, so it may satisfy the complete test when the actual elements are shown. That does not enlarge the granted categorical question into a conclusive finding on all elements or fraud. Zandford's adopted separation of a corrected legal standard from final proof and relief supports remand at this posture. No Justice commits to treating an accusation or a preliminary finding as a final merits adjudication.

## Three separate legal inquiries and competing routes

### 1. Section 2: unlawful monopoly maintenance, not the regulatory violation alone

The saving clause prevents automatic displacement of existing antitrust law; it does not enlarge that law. Monopoly power and unlawful acquisition or maintenance remain distinct. The disputed conduct must impair competition rather than merely injure a competing carrier. A use of power in one market to obtain an advantage elsewhere is not a free-standing offense. If attempt is pursued, Spectrum Sports requires anticompetitive conduct, specific intent and independent dangerous probability in the properly defined market. Neither intent nor a leverage label supplies that last element.

The remand route treats the alleged deliberate discriminatory degradation of rival-carrier orders, intended to deter customers and impair rival market participation, as a potentially independent monopoly-maintenance mechanism requiring ordinary section 2 adjudication. Its strongest support is Kodak's actual conduct-and-market inquiry and CSU's refusal to make a statutory legal setting a conclusive defense while preserving substantial legitimate justifications. Its strongest objection is that the allegation may describe only nonperformance of compelled sharing, dressed in the language of anticompetitive purpose. The remand must therefore identify an exclusionary mechanism and actual monopoly maintenance beyond violation of a regulatory access standard, give investment, efficiency and operational reasons substantive weight, and permit dismissal where the allegations fail the applicable elements. It awards no presumed discovery, trial or access.

The dismissal route concludes that the supplied access-performance allegations do not establish an independent antitrust duty to provide the rival inputs or equal treatment the complaint demands. Compulsory access and a purpose to keep customers do not themselves establish unlawful maintenance. Its strongest support is the no-general-duty limitation preserved by Kodak and CSU, with the distinction between regulatory breach and injury to competition in NYNEX. Its strongest objection is that it can turn the regulated setting into the categorical shield CSU rejects and disregard the pleaded customer-diversion mechanism. A lawful dismissal must therefore rest on insufficiency of this specific conduct theory, not automatic immunity for regulation or the saving clause's irrelevance.

Aspen remains an example of unjustified exclusion. A discontinued voluntary course of dealing may be powerful evidence, but its absence is not an exclusive, universally required pleading element. None of the commitments below adopts every fact of Aspen as an exhaustive checklist. Intellectual-property exclusion in CSU and compelled interconnection are different legal settings; CSU's ordinary-elements rule and substantial-justification approach apply by reasoned comparison, not by pretending CSU decided telecommunications.

### 2. Section 4: actual personal injury and the proper damages plaintiff

Steamfitters, applying Associated General Contractors, requires more than consumer status or an asserted causal connection. The actual causal relation, intermediaries, more direct claimants, potential duplicate recovery and workable allocation must be assessed as relevant. The injury to AT&T's ability to obtain and deliver network service is not automatically the same as Trinko's recoverable business or property loss.

The dismissal route characterizes the pleaded customer damage as downstream of the competitive carriers' operational injury. It gives weight to the carriers as direct claimants and the need to distinguish their loss from consequent customer effects before treble damages can follow. The strongest contrary route treats discriminatory handling of end-user orders as the very instrument of exclusion, making those customers directly injured in a manner analogous to McCready's reimbursement denial. The disagreement below is about that actual causal characterization; it is not a rule that only direct purchasers can ever sue or that all consumers automatically have standing.

This is the existing pleaded claim. No Justice infers a particular pricing decision, invoice, passed-on charge, quantifiable loss or contractual arrangement that the neutral packet does not establish. The damages-dismissal position rests on the claim's asserted relation to the carrier-access injury, not an invented finding about those matters or a missing archive treated as failure to plead.

### 3. Section 16: threatened personal antitrust harm and a fitted decree

Cargill Part II requires threatened injury of the type antitrust law protects against, flowing from what makes the challenged conduct unlawful. Section 16 does not repeat §4's actual-business-or-property-loss requirement. Cargill notes 5 and 6 also prevent importing damages duplication, allocation difficulty or the mere presence of a more direct damages claimant as conclusive barriers to an injunction. Those differences do not eliminate the need for a personal threat and an adequately connected antitrust offense.

On the remand route, the existing allegation of a systematic strategy degrading service to customers of rival carriers, coupled with Trinko's actual customer position, permits further adjudication of a personally threatened exclusion injury. The court must apply Cargill and ordinary equity to determine a significant threat from conduct continuing or likely to recur; regulatory relief is material to that inquiry but is not established complete redress. A decree may prohibit only a proved antitrust wrong and the causal threat it poses. The court may not impose the requested equal-access regime merely because §§251 and 252 prescribe access duties. If no narrower effective antitrust remedy is legally supportable on the existing claim, or no sufficient personal prospective threat exists, §16 relief must be denied.

On the dismissal route, failure of the independent §2 claim defeats both remedies: §16 does not furnish an offense that §2 lacks. That conclusion is separate from a §4 directness defect. The three Justices taking this route still accept Cargill's distinct equity standard; they do not deny equity because AT&T is a more direct damages claimant or because allocation would be difficult.

Section 9658 covers an action under state law for personal injury or property damage caused or contributed to by exposure to a hazardous substance, pollutant or contaminant released into the environment from a facility. ## Competing constitutional paths

Path A sustains the provision as a reasonably adapted component of the commercial hazardous-waste regime. Congress validly regulates commercial production, handling, disposal and response to hazardous waste. The covered class is the specified exposure-based state damages action, and the operative relationship is preserving meaningful consequences of that waste activity when latent injury would otherwise be barred before reasonable discovery. Preserving those liability consequences supports the cost allocation and incentives of the underlying waste regime. It is not enough that §309 appears in CERCLA; the limited covered subject and the direct latency problem supply the functional relationship. Home Builders supplies identification of the regime, enacted class and actual fit, with independent judicial determination of those boundaries and respect for reasonable legislative implementation. Guillen confirms that a confined litigation rule can implement an identified interstate program. Jinks confirms that a properly sourced federal limitations rule may govern state claims, but its Article III/federal-adjudication source is not the source here.

Path B invalidates the displacement as regulation of state tort procedure disconnected from any federal cause of action or adjudication. Its strongest point is that §9658 can govern local injuries and affects claims Congress did not create; merely describing litigation as useful to pollution control would furnish a limitless rationale. A answers with the actual exposure, release, facility and earlier-commencement conditions and the latency mechanism: the federal rule preserves consequences of the very hazardous-waste activity the interstate regime regulates. # Fellers — clean proof-quantum addendum

**Scope:** Supplemental independent assessment of the proof standard within the already frozen six-Justice causal-independence rule, Fellers v. United States, No. 02-6320, January 26, 2004. The neutral packet, C entering law and original `FELLERS_COMMITMENTS` were expressly supplied for this limited audit. No comparator, target Supreme Court outcome, Stone material, assignment or current assembly was received. The original commitment and remedy split remain preserved.

## Supported proposed formulation

Once the defendant establishes the original deliberate-elicitation violation and its causal connection to the later statement, the Government must establish by a preponderance of the evidence that the later statement was obtained through a genuinely independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of that violation. The factual basis must make causal independence more likely than not. Valid warnings and waiver receive their full evidentiary significance but do not alone satisfy this separate showing. The actual sequence, temporal separation, intervening circumstances, and purpose and character of the earlier elicitation remain the governing considerations already frozen.

This proof standard concerns admissibility, not guilt, and does not replace the separate beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard for any properly preserved Chapman harmlessness inquiry. The defendant's established initial violation and causal nexus are the trigger; this addendum does not create a new numerical threshold for the defendant's production or proof that the original record did not address. No lower-court finding of actual attenuation follows merely from specifying the burden.

## Sources and comparison of alternatives

Brown v. Illinois, 422 U.S. 590, 603–604, assigns the prosecution the burden of establishing admissibility and distinguishes voluntariness and warnings from attenuation. Brown does not itself fix the numerical quantum for this later Sixth Amendment application.

Nix v. Williams, 467 U.S. 431, 444 and n.5, applies preponderance to inevitable discovery after a Sixth Amendment violation. Its operative doctrine is inevitable discovery of physical evidence, not later-statement attenuation; it nevertheless supplies directly relevant proof-standard reasoning and rejects a universal rule that every derivative-evidence question after a Sixth Amendment violation demands clear and convincing proof. Its footnote relies on United States v. Matlock, 415 U.S. 164, 178 n.14, and Lego v. Twomey, 404 U.S. 477, 488–489, for the ordinary preponderance treatment of suppression facts. Lego independently permits that quantum for determining a confession's voluntariness, while preserving a State's ability under its own law to demand more.

The strongest competing path is clear-and-convincing proof by analogy to United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218, 240. Wade addresses the independent origin of an in-court identification after an uncounseled lineup. Nix expressly distinguishes that setting's crystallized identification and the special difficulty of reconstructing an untainted recollection. Those concerns do not make clear-and-convincing proof the general Sixth Amendment derivative-evidence standard. Here the court examines the actual later interview, warnings, waiver, interval, officers' conduct and intervening events. Credibility and inference remain necessary, as they also do in ordinary voluntariness adjudication under Lego; that uncertainty alone does not establish Wade's distinctive identification problem.

The present conclusion is a reasoned application of the ordinary suppression-proof rule to the proposed causal-independence inquiry. It is not a claim that Nix previously decided Fellers's question or that Lego equates voluntariness with attenuation. Equally, preponderance does not dilute the substantive requirement by permitting a finding of independence whenever voluntariness is more likely than not: independence itself is the proposition the Government must establish. The inquiry concerns the actual later choice and sequence; speculation that officers inevitably would have obtained an identical future confession is not this causal-independence showing.

All precede the divergence. Their actual contexts and distinct doctrinal functions are retained.

## Supported record and two paths

The lower opinion establishes that recovery mode prevents the information and roadside-assistance buttons from providing their functions. An emergency button or airbag event emits a tone on the existing FBI connection rather than opening the ordinary provider connection. The record does not establish an attempted rescue, an unanswered actual emergency or a physical injury. The Court can decide the order's interference with provided services without inventing any of those events. The district court's finding that assistance did not impose an unreasonable burden on the company concerns a different object from the customer-service limitation.

**Substantive-limit path:** Read the affirmative grant and its conditions together. The phrase protects the service that the provider is according the customer. Treating any indispensable disruption as minimal would allow the means' necessity to displace that additional restriction. A compensated, brief technician task can nevertheless eliminate the service for the entire interception period. An emergency-response subscription includes availability before an emergency occurs, so interference does not require a completed failed rescue. New York Telephone's distinction between assistance and unreasonable burdens supports bounded compulsion, while its All Writs holding does not replace this specific enacted condition.

The strongest objection is that minimum ordinarily means the least possible amount, so the only feasible method necessarily satisfies it; the service might never have been requested while surveillance operated, and the emergency tone still reached the FBI. The answer is that the entire phrase describes the permissible interference with the customer's service, not only a competition between technical methods. In context, a minimum of interference does not authorize complete displacement of the service merely because interception is otherwise impossible. An unassured investigative audio tone is not the provider's ordinary emergency-response service. No conclusion depends on investigators actually ignoring a tone.

**Relative-minimum path:** Reverse because the statute requires necessary assistance performed in the least disruptive feasible manner, not maintenance of all contracted services. The remaining emergency signal and district court's no-undue-burden finding reinforce that construction. Its strongest challenge is textual: it makes the customer-service condition ineffective whenever a high-disruption design happens to be the only one technically available, and transfers protection of the customer's service to a provider-burden doctrine addressing another question. All eight reject that construction for this complete operational substitution while preserving its valid point that the statute does not require literally zero interference.

An equipment-redesign order is not a narrower available remedy on this record. The Government did not pursue the separate §2522/CALEA basis on appeal, and §2518(4) does not itself order an otherwise unsupported redesign. Takings and due-process issues were not continued on appeal. The Fourth Amendment can remain undecided because the specific assistance order exceeds the statute on the established mechanics.

# United States v. Carnes — frozen neutral non-Stone commitments

**Event:** Merits, 2004-02-09; Sixth Circuit No. 00-2103; judgment below 309 F.3d 950.

## Common judgment and rule

All eight Associate Justices would affirm the rejection of the Wiretap Act commerce objection, leaving the lower court's independent reversal of the wiretap conviction, new-trial direction and associated sentencing proceedings in effect. This is not wholesale affirmance of the conviction or unrelated appellate components.

Congress may protect the interstate telephone system by prohibiting unauthorized interception of covered wire communications carried through it, even when the intercepted call begins and ends in one State. The relationship is present use of and intrusion into an interstate communications instrumentality, not remote interstate manufacture of a recorder or a generalized effect of local crime. Congress need not prove that the particular conversation crossed a border or separately had a substantial interstate effect. The statutory definition, intentional interception requirement and applicable statutory exceptions remain independently necessary; the constitutional ruling supplies no missing element or finding.

## Alternative-path comparison

Lopez preserves channels and instrumentalities as direct bases of regulation. Harris applies instrumentality protection without reducing every individual event to an independent substantial-effects inquiry. Guillen likewise permits confined protection of an actual interstate transportation mechanism. Unauthorized acquisition of communications carried through the interstate telephone network directly implicates the network's secure operation, including communication confidentiality; physical damage or interruption of a call is not an additional constitutional prerequisite.

Carnes's strongest objection is that personal local conversations and a locally installed recording device are not commercial transactions. That objection would defeat a theory relying only on the device's manufacturing history, which Holston rejects, or on an unsupported chain from crime to national economic costs. It does not sever the intercepted wire communications from the actual network carrying them. Section 2510(1)'s facilities/provider relationship supplies the statutory limit relevant here. No new element requiring a completed interstate call is inserted.

A second lawful framework would ask whether inclusion of local calls is integral to a valid interstate telephone regime under Home Builders and, by February 9, Rancho Viejo. That route remains available on its own predicates, but the direct instrumentality ground already resolves this case; the opinion need not turn practical enforcement concerns into an independently sufficient holding. The constitutional power over face-to-face speech or stand-alone recorders is outside this narrow question. The Roving Interception matter concerns the conditions on a Government assistance order and supplies no exception to Congress's power to protect covered telephone communications from private interception; no uncompleted Roving decision is treated as law in this commitment.
