## Event

Holland v. Jackson, No. 03-1200; merits decision, June 28, 2004. On review of the Sixth Circuit, 80 F. App'x 392, which reversed denial of habeas relief from Jackson's murder conviction and life sentence. The question concerns reliance on the later Gooch witness proffer and whether the state court applied a prejudice standard contrary to Strickland. Hughes, the principal eyewitness, placed Gooch at the scene; seven years after conviction Jackson proffered her denial. The state court rejected the later request on a procedural basis and also addressed limited impeachment value. The court of appeals relied on that proffer and isolated preponderance or probability language despite the state court's statement of the reasonable-probability standard. The State preserved its objection to reliance on the new material.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Sixth Circuit judgment granting habeas relief and remands for proper treatment of the evidentiary record and the statutory and constitutional requirements.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine support the stated correction and remand.

The appellate court must identify the state merits record, resolve the lawful basis for considering the late proffer and any properly presented procedural barrier, and then apply the proper statutory and Strickland inquiries if they remain available. An independently sufficient established ground may dispose of the claim without unnecessary further adjudication. Necessary factfinding returns to the district court. The conviction remains operative unless a lawful grant of relief follows.

## Holdings

### I. Evidence development and statutory review have distinct gates

**Controlling proposition:** A federal habeas court may not deem a state merits decision objectively unreasonable by relying on material not properly before that court without first identifying a lawful statutory and procedural basis for considering it. Section 2254(e)(2) applies when deficient diligence or greater fault is attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the state factual basis; an incomplete record alone does not trigger it.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Michael Wayne Williams makes attributable failure the trigger. If that trigger is established, the claim must rely either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or on a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence. It must also satisfy the cumulative requirement that the facts would establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Those alternatives and the cumulative requirement cannot be replaced by a seven-year rule. Terry Williams and Lockyer preserve the separate objective statutory inquiry into the state adjudication, using governing substantive holdings established at that adjudication; §§2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) retain their separate factual roles. The Sixth Circuit did not resolve the state rejection of the proffer or the necessary diligence predicates before relying on Gooch's account. Potential impeachment value does not cure that omission. An alternative state merits discussion does not itself eliminate an independent procedural basis for rejecting material, while a record-admission restriction does not automatically establish an adequate and independent default of the underlying claim. Any such default and applicable exception need their own predicates.

### II. The actual prejudice analysis controls

**Controlling proposition:** A state court acts contrary to Strickland when it actually requires a more-likely-than-not different outcome instead of a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the result. Isolated shorthand or a burden for proving underlying facts does not establish that substitution when the complete opinion applies the proper legal standard; a correct citation does not cure contrary actual reasoning.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Visciotti requires examination of the whole state opinion and supported record. References to preponderance, disbelief of an eyewitness or probability must be read in their function rather than treated as automatic legal error. Strickland separates proof of what occurred from the legal assessment of prejudice. Here the state court expressly stated reasonable probability, and its other language can be read consistently with that rule; detached phrases therefore cannot sustain the writ. Conversely, the opening citation alone does not establish a reasonable application. Lockyer requires the independent statutory threshold as well as an underlying constitutional violation, and Gentry's contextual performance analysis does not admit the new proffer or decide prejudice. If the claim remains available after the applicable gates, the appellate court must perform those inquiries on the evidence that may lawfully be considered.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420:** Applied to applicant-attributed failure, diligence and the separate statutory exceptions for factual development.
- **Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362:** Applied to the independent contrary-to and objectively-unreasonable-application clauses.
- **Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668:** Its reasonable-probability standard governs constitutional prejudice; a factual proof burden is a different question.
- **Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19:** Applied to the complete state reasoning and supported record, preserving both its rejection of isolated-word analysis and its substantive reasonableness inquiry.
- **Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63:** Preserves the state-adjudication substantive cutoff, separate statutory threshold and underlying constitutional requirement.
- **Yarborough v. Gentry:** Its contextual performance review remains distinct from admission of new material and prejudice.

## Law After Decision

The governing Williams, Strickland, Visciotti and Lockyer rules remain in force and require setting aside this inadequately grounded grant. Lawful consideration of the proffer, attributable diligence, any established default and its exception, and final constitutional prejudice are separate applications to be resolved on remand. Permission to develop evidence does not itself satisfy §2254(d) or establish a new claim.

## Procedure After Action
