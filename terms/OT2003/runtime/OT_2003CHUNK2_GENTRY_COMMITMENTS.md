## Common proposed propositions and remedy

P1. Section 2254(d)(1) requires either a contrary governing rule or an objectively unreasonable application of clearly established Supreme Court law. An independently preferable federal assessment is insufficient. Strickland supplies the underlying performance and prejudice rules, and its generality does not remove it from clearly established law. Williams, Bell, Visciotti and Andrade retain their existing scope; no exclusive allocation between the statutory clauses is newly resolved.

P2. A closing argument must be assessed in the context of the supported defense and the argument as a whole. Counsel may concentrate on the disputed intent, concede damaging background facts and use candor to resist improper character reasoning. Neither omission of every available point nor offensive language considered alone establishes deficient performance. The strategy characterization remains subject to substantive examination and cannot supply an investigation that the record does not establish. Herring protects the opportunity for closing advocacy; it supplies no mandatory script or requirement to exhaust every evidentiary inference.

P3. The state court could reasonably conclude that this actual summation remained within Strickland's permissible range. Counsel identified accidental stabbing as the defense, challenged the eyewitness's changing description of the lighting, argued that prior theft and other misconduct did not establish an intentional stabbing, explained the defendant's confusion about convictions, and urged consideration of the evidence. The derogatory descriptions served that character-versus-intent distinction in the argument actually given. The transcript permits that assessment without a finding about counsel's private motives, tone or unrecorded investigation.

The strongest contrary evidence is substantial: counsel omitted the victim's inconsistent, drug-affected recollections, the single wound despite her account of two stabbings, the corroboration that a third man was present, the absence of Pedro, and affirmative details consistent with accident. He also told jurors that disbelief of Gentry required conviction. That last formulation is unsound if understood as a legal statement of the prosecution's burden: disbelief alone does not establish every element. Nevertheless, reading the complete summation, the state court could treat it as a compressed presentation of the central factual dispute rather than surrender of the defense or displacement of the governing burden. The identified intent argument and impeachment make the state performance determination reasonable. No missing jury instruction is reconstructed to cure the phrasing. The omitted points do not make concentration on the supported core constitutionally indefensible. The trial judge's favorable assessment and six-hour deliberation are neither decisive proof of competence nor proof of prejudice.

P4. Reverse the Ninth Circuit's grant of habeas relief on the closing-argument claim and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of that claim under §2254(d)(1). The district court's denial of relief on this claim stands; the conviction is not displaced by this claim. The sufficient performance-gateway ground makes an independent actual-prejudice determination unnecessary. This is a supported stopping ground within the question decided, not a reservation of the legal test. No separate investigation claim is decided. The abandoned Cronic theory is not revived; substantive advocacy in this record also distinguishes the asserted complete-failure category. No new trial or conditional writ follows.
