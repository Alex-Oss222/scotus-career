## 67. Ammex, Inc. v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the contempt-based attempt to stop the revocation.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave Ammex free to obtain ordinary review of the later administrative decision; enter no tax exemption through contempt.
* **Remedial Reach:** Ammex’s enforcement motion and the adjudicated preclusive effect of the first judgment.
* **Practical Relief:** The trade court must enforce its actual judgment but cannot expand it to prohibit the distinct later tax-based decision. Ammex must use the appropriate statutory review procedure to challenge that decision.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Preclusion and later agency action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the prior judgment rejecting Customs’ original prohibition on duty-free fuel sales precludes a later revocation based on federal excise-tax treatment, and whether contempt is available.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply preclusion and judgment-enforcement limits without immunizing agencies from their final judgments.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The operative decisions and judgment language resolve this dispute. A blanket right to recycle rejected decisions would undermine finality. A result without specifying the distinct later action would permit evasion by relabeling.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A judgment invalidating a particular Customs determination does not preclude a genuinely later administrative revocation based on a subsequently developed tax determination that the judgment did not resolve. The party asserting preclusion must establish identity within the prior judgment’s scope; Ammex has not done so here. Contempt cannot enlarge that judgment.
* **Reach Within the Question:** These successive administrative actions and the requested enforcement remedy; claim preclusion remains available against relitigation of the same operative determination.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Semtek requires identifying the actual preclusion rule rather than treating an adverse judgment as unlimited substantive immunity. The later IRS determination and new revocation present a different administrative action. Ammex retains review of that action, so refusal of contempt does not insulate the new statutory interpretation from judicial scrutiny.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. is distinguished as to its diversity-specific borrowing rule; ordinary finality remains intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
