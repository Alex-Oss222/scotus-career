## Event

Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. McCallum; review of Seventh Circuit No. 02-3102, 324 F.3d 880; merits; decided February 26, 2004 after plenary review. The Seventh Circuit affirmed the bench-trial rejection of the taxpayer challenge to Department of Corrections payments for Faith Works rehabilitation placements. The question is whether voluntary selection from secular and religious alternatives sustains payment for integrated devotional treatment. The separate Workforce Development judgment is outside this review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining the challenged Department of Corrections funding is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The lower court must determine the supported payment scope and enter effective prospective relief against established integrated devotional purchases, preserving genuinely separated secular services and the existing Workforce Development judgment.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivered the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

## Holdings

### The identified expenditure challenge supplies the standing predicate

**Controlling proposition:** The taxpayer plaintiffs may pursue this challenge to identified state contract payments as public financing of religious treatment. The accepted taxpayer-spending and Establishment Clause nexus rests on the Department of Corrections reimbursements and the prospective expenditure relief sought, rather than a generalized objection to religious recommendations.

**Authority and explanation:** All nine Justices directly accept this threshold on the tried expenditure claim. The challenged governmental action is payment of part of placement costs under the Faith Works contract, and the asserted constitutional limit concerns that use of public funds. That concrete relationship permits the Court to reach the granted financing question.

### Contract payments are governed by the work the State purchases

**Controlling proposition:** Government may not finance devotional formation integrated into the rehabilitation service it purchases through restricted contract payments merely because an offender voluntarily selects the provider from options including a secular alternative. A claimant must establish the actual financed religious function; religious affiliation, a religious theme elsewhere in the institution or speculative freeing of other resources is insufficient. Independently identifiable secular services remain eligible when documented operational separation and reliable accounting preserve their secular function.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct joins to the financing rule, the rejection of the private-choice-only rationale and the payment-specific remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini directs attention to the governmental function, Mitchell requires actual-use proof and effective relief at the proved scope, and Columbia Union protects genuinely separated secular work. Strout and Zelman establish that private selection does not change the character of an integrated devotional service government actually buys. These rules apply to correctional treatment as to the public purchases they already govern.

Wisconsin reimburses part of the cost of a selected placement under its contract with Faith Works, whose treatment integrates Christianity. The offender selects among contracted providers rather than receives an independently distributed grant for broad personal expenses. Locke's individual scholarship and its confined permission not to support ministry preparation therefore supply no private-choice immunity for this contractual expenditure.

The accepted findings of secular alternatives, nonbinding recommendations and absence of religious favoritism remain effective. The longer program's attractiveness does not itself establish coercion; voluntariness, however, answers a different question from what government purchases. The State's secular rehabilitative purpose cannot authorize a proved devotional purchase.

The appellate account does not settle the contract's reimbursable cost categories, operative secular-use restrictions or full extent of actual separation. The lower courts must apply the financing rule to the trial record rather than presume either that every provider activity is publicly financed or that the reimbursements pay only secular care. Established findings of a particular financed use remain binding for this work; further findings are required only to resolve a material application gap.

### Effective relief must reach the proved payment practice

**Controlling proposition:** The remand court must enjoin public payments for every established integrated devotional purchase at the supported contract and placement scope, while preserving reliably separated secular services. Responsible defendants seeking a narrower restriction must demonstrate that it fully cures the proved expenditure; public funding of a claimed secular remainder requires documented operational and accounting separation.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this implementation of the financing rule and its conditional, record-specific remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction of established financed religious use without demanding reproof or waiting for every application to be quantified. Columbia Union permits secular work on demonstrated operational controls rather than religious identity. Those rules place the remedial boundary at the actual publicly financed function: a narrower restriction is sufficient only if it stops that function, and the proof supporting any secular remainder must identify its actual separation. Shared corporate structure or staff alone does not defeat reliable secular performance.

The lower court must promptly determine the contract's covered work, payment terms and necessary scope of relief, using the existing trial record where sufficient. It must identify the current placement situation and provide a concrete lawful transition where needed, preserving secular services and voluntary private participation while stopping established prohibited payments. An unresolved allocation issue cannot justify indefinite continuation of a purchase already proved devotional. The separate Workforce Development judgment remains undisturbed.

## Precedent Treatment

Agostini v. Felton is applied through its unified governmental-function inquiry. Mitchell v. Helms is applied to actual-use proof and effective relief at the demonstrated scope. Strout v. Albanese and Zelman v. Simmons-Harris are extended to the analogous restricted purchase of correctional treatment; private selection does not alter the function purchased. Columbia Union College v. Oliver and K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District retain equal treatment for reliably separated secular work at religious institutions. Locke v. Davey retains its confined ministry-degree nonfunding permission and distinct individual-assistance classification; it supplies no immunity for this provider-reimbursement contract. Witters’s independent vocational-benefit relationship remains distinct. Lee v. Weisman retains its coercion inquiry, which does not exhaust the financing question.

## Law After Decision

Restricted correctional-service payments must fund lawful functions even where the beneficiary chooses freely among providers. A proved integrated devotional purchase requires effective prospective correction at its actual scope. Religious providers remain eligible for reliably separated secular work; neutral choice and religious identity alone answer neither the financing violation nor its remedy.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for prompt application of the actual-function rule to the trial record, payment-specific findings where materially needed, and effective prospective relief. Established devotional purchases must cease; a claimed secular remainder requires documented separation. The court must provide any necessary lawful transition in light of actual current placements. The Workforce Development judgment remains undisturbed.
