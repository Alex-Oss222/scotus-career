## 65. Arakaki v. Hawaii

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the election-related judgment and the dismissal of the appointment branch for lack of standing.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain declaratory and injunctive relief requiring officials to accept otherwise eligible candidates without the ancestry condition; dismiss the uninjured appointment claim without prejudice.
* **Remedial Reach:** The unlawful election qualification itself and everyone subject to it. Individual permission would leave the same public ballot restricted; Hawaii has shown no narrower complete cure.
* **Practical Relief:** Before the next candidate-filing period, election officials must remove the ancestry prerequisite from forms and eligibility instructions and process qualifying nominations. The district court may enforce compliance; it must issue no appointment merits decree.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Ancestry eligibility in a public election

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Hawaii may restrict candidacy for elected Office of Hawaiian Affairs trusteeships to persons of Hawaiian ancestry, and whether these plaintiffs may challenge the separate appointment restriction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose the identified Fourteenth Amendment extension to public candidacy; apply settled appointment standing.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Equal public eligibility requires a rule for the ancestry bar, not proof the candidate would win. A universal rule for tribal membership or beneficiary programs would exceed this public-office setting. A voter-only rule would leave the distinct exclusion from service unanswered.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Equal protection forbids a racial-ancestry condition on eligibility for an elected public state office even when every voter may cast a ballot. The challenger must prove the public election, operative racial qualification and personal exclusion by a preponderance; proof that the candidate would win is unnecessary. Once established, the exclusion is not justified by the racial constituency the office serves. Those predicates invalidate this condition; the uninjured appointment claim remains dismissed.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Eligibility to serve in elected public state office. This proposed categorical civic-exclusion rule does not replace Adarand’s ordinary classification standard or determine genuine tribal governance.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides those separate questions, courts apply their existing standing, tribal and statutory rules. The present Fourteenth Amendment ruling supplies no broader Fifteenth Amendment candidacy holding.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rice establishes that OHA is a public state election but left candidate eligibility open. Nixon supplies the equal-protection anchor for exclusion from public electoral participation; Smith supplies a related participation analogy. Opening the electorate does not cure legal exclusion from eligibility to serve. The proposed extension treats that unequal civic status as complete without an election-loss requirement, and does not claim the Fifteenth Amendment already resolved candidacy.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Rice v. Cayetano is extended only on the reserved candidacy issue through equal protection. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña remains the common standard outside this specific political-exclusion category.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Appointment standing is absent; sovereign tribal membership, other Hawaiian programs and a broader Fifteenth Amendment candidacy theory are outside the necessary disposition.
