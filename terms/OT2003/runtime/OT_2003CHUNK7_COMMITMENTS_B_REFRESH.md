## 95. Norton v. Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance

### Committed disposition and distinct grounds

All eight Associates would reverse the Tenth Circuit's restoration of the challenged section 706(1) theories and remand to implement the following dispositions. The live generalized nonimpairment demand, demanded intensive monitoring program, and demand to supplement the already approved plans do not support the compulsory relief sought. Requests solely to compel completion of the San Rafael route-designation plan and initial creation of Henry Mountains supervision files have no remaining object after the reported completion of those acts and should be dismissed as moot. Completion of those initial acts does not decide an independently pleaded continuing breach; no such additional file-maintenance claim is established by this packet, and this decision neither creates nor adjudicates one. The remand implements identified legal outcomes; it is not a direction to seek missing research documents or reconsider the resolved intensive-program claim.

**N1 — Statutory enforcement.** Section 706(1), read with section 551(13), authorizes a court to compel an identifiable agency act that law requires and that has been unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed. A legally required decision can be compelled while leaving its legally discretionary substance to the agency. A court may not select a discretionary management program or supervise compliance with a broad statutory objective simply by calling the program a failure to act. This is a construction adopted here; Lincoln and Bennett support its source-and-duty distinctions but did not already decide the complete section 706(1) rule.

FLPMA section 1782(c) imposes a real nonimpairment obligation during wilderness review and until Congress determines otherwise. It also preserves existing mining and grazing uses and mineral leasing in their October 21, 1976 manner and degree, requires protection against unnecessary or undue degradation and environmental protection, and retains its stated mining-appropriation and withdrawal conditions. Those qualifications cannot be replaced with a universal prohibition on every land use or every environmental change. The broad management command identifies a required standard, but the present demand to improve wilderness protection does not identify a particular legally mandatory omitted agency act. The Court therefore rejects this section 706(1) claim without suspending the statute or immunizing reviewable final decisions inconsistent with it.

Section 1732(a) separately requires management in accordance with available land-use plans, subject to the governing law for tracts dedicated to particular uses. Section 1732(b)'s unnecessary-or-undue-degradation command is a distinct obligation. Neither becomes a judicial warrant to choose the amount of monitoring, a closure, or a comprehensive enforcement program absent a legally prescribed act and its trigger. Review of final agency decisions under section 706(2), compliance with section 704, and compelling an actual mandatory decision remain distinct channels.

**N2 — The actual plan claims.** A plan can contain a binding, discrete commitment; its source, wording, context, and required act must establish that character. Plan conformity does not, without more, make every description of intended management a judicially enforceable schedule of affirmative operations. Nor is every statement in a plan categorically unenforceable.

The two initial acts identified above have been completed. The surviving Factory Butte claim demands an intensive continuing monitoring program. The supplied language promises monitoring and closure if warranted; it does not prescribe the demanded intensity, frequency, staffing, methodology, or particular additional output. The related language about documenting resource damage, making recommendations, determining damage that may require a designation change, and emphasizing supervision in limited and closed areas distinguishes several management functions. It does not convert documentation into closure or supply the intensive-program specification sought here. Informal monitoring occurred, and SUWA acknowledged commencement of a monitoring program. Those facts are not findings that BLM's management was adequate; they do foreclose treating the pleaded demand as a wholly omitted initial monitoring act. The remaining dispute seeks judicially chosen improvement of an ongoing program rather than performance of the identified, legally prescribed omitted act required by N1.

Conditional closure remains conditional. The packet supplies neither a distinct preserved claim to compel a particular closure decision nor an established triggering determination that closure is warranted. The Court orders no closure, invents no negative finding about environmental damage, and does not hold that a genuinely binding documentation or decision duty could never be enforced in a proper claim. It resolves the actual intensive-monitoring demand on the supplied terms.

**N3 — NEPA.** Mandatory supplementation under the operative regulation has two triggers: substantial changes in the proposed action relevant to environmental concerns, or significant new circumstances or information relevant to environmental concerns and bearing on that proposed action or its impacts. The relevant major federal action must remain to occur. Discretionary supplementation when it would further NEPA's purposes remains permission. The approval of the plans at issue is complete. Continuing administration under an approved plan does not by itself keep that approval perpetually proposed. The present theory identifies no distinct remaining major federal action to which the demanded supplementation attaches; increased ORV use, even if environmentally important, does not alone supply that missing action. The asserted requirement to supplement these approved plans therefore fails.

This conclusion does not equate completion of an EIS with completion of an unbuilt project, immunize a later site-specific authorization, or find the increased-use information insignificant. Marsh addresses significant information while relevant major action remains. Public Citizen requires the actual agency action and choices to be identified; its absence-of-control result for FMCSA does not establish that BLM lacks ORV-management authority. BLM's future choices remain subject to applicable review. No preservation failure is imported from Public Citizen.

## 97. Hibbs v. Winn

### H1 — Review is timely

All eight Associates would hold the petition timely. Before the original ninety-day period expired, the court of appeals recalled its mandate and ordered briefing on genuine possible en banc rehearing. This reconsideration was capable of changing the judgment, and the petition was filed within ninety days after reconsideration ended. Young's actual holding concerns an expressly accepted late party filing. Its finality principle applies here because the court itself placed its judgment under genuine reconsideration; there is no statutory reason to make finality turn on who initiated the authorized reconsideration. The conclusion applies section 2101(c), not an equitable waiver or extension.

The contrary path emphasizes Rule 13's reference to a rehearing petition and section 2101(c)'s fixed period. Its force is preserved for an unauthorized late paper, an unentertained en banc suggestion, a mandate date alone, or a nominal order whose only work is extending Supreme Court time. Those are not the supplied facts. No Justice would dismiss this petition as untimely or ask for documents to reestablish the already supported sequence.

### H2 — Two distinct tax-forum constructions

**Construction A — Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.** Section 1341 bars relief that restrains assessment, levy, or collection in the Act's revenue-collection setting, not every injunction affecting a state's tax administration. These plaintiffs do not seek to reduce their own tax liability or prevent collection of taxes owed. They challenge an asserted subsidy through credits and seek prospective restraint, a declaration, and payment of covered organizations' remaining funds into the general fund. Those requests do not obstruct the collection protected by the Act; the payment demand does not become a tax-refund request merely because it involves money. The judgment reversing the section 1341 dismissal should be affirmed, and the case should proceed under the remaining ordinary jurisdictional and merits requirements.

This is not a categorical exemption for third-party plaintiffs, constitutional claims, credits, or suits that would increase aggregate revenue. Relief by a third party that actually prevents protected assessment or collection remains barred when the state-remedy condition is met. The requested relief's operative effect, not its caption or constitutional basis, controls. Grace Brethren's prohibition on declaratory evasion remains applicable when the underlying restraint is within the Act. Neither Galletti's federal partnership-assessment holding nor the unadjudicated constitutionality of this credit answers the forum question.

**Construction B — Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.** A federal decree forbidding allowance of the credits restrains the State's assessment of tax liabilities under the enacted system, even if it would increase receipts. Section 1341 separately protects assessment, levy, and collection of any state tax; it contains no plaintiff-own-liability or revenue-decrease limitation. The requested declaration is the equivalent restraint, and the remaining-funds demand is part of undoing the same credit arrangement rather than a basis to retain this tax-administration challenge in federal court. These Justices would reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand for dismissal under section 1341, without reaching the Establishment Clause merits.

For B, the adequate-state-remedy condition is satisfied on the supported record: respondents admit that their challenge would be heard in state court, a related constitutional credit challenge was actually heard there, and no concrete procedural obstacle to presentation and judicial decision of these federal objections is identified. Rosewell and Grace Brethren make the condition one of an adequate opportunity for judicial determination, not a guarantee of identical relief, success, or a preferred tribunal. The reported admission is not a concession that section 1341 applies. The characterization of Kotterman as similar does not establish identical pleadings or every requested remedy. Those distinctions do not manufacture an outcome-material inadequacy dispute absent any identified impediment. These three therefore commit to dismissal, not a conditional vote awaiting unknown briefing.

Construction A answers B's strongest textual argument by reading assessment with levy and collection and refusing to expand the enacted restraint to all state-tax policy. Construction B answers A's strongest revenue-purpose argument by preserving the independent work of assessment and rejecting an unstated direction-of-revenue limitation. Each construction keeps state-remedy adequacy separate from the conduct covered by the bar.

## 98. Pennsylvania State Police v. Suders

### Common commitments: existing law and the actual summary-judgment posture

All eight Associates accept Duncan as governing the objective-compulsion component. Suders must establish working conditions so objectively intolerable that a reasonable employee would feel compelled to resign, and an actual causal connection between the actionable conditions and her resignation. A supported hostile environment alone does not prove the greater compulsion requirement. Realistic corrective opportunities are relevant but are not an exhaustion condition: no employee must remain in danger, use futile procedures, or wait indefinitely, and announcing an investigation is not a safe harbor. Duncan's removed planter, promised account, and offered alternative location are not facts in Suders.

All eight separately retain Harris and Oncale: actionable sex-based hostility must be objectively and subjectively severe or pervasive, with discrimination because of sex independently shown. On the actual-authority route, Suders bears the preponderance burden of proving that delegated supervisory power materially aided the harassment. Rank, title, or proximity alone is insufficient; a completed personnel action, management notice, economic injury, or an express discharge threat is not invariably necessary. Apparent authority requires employer manifestations and reasonable reliance; negligence has its own requirements. Neither is silently substituted for actual authority.

The plaintiff-favorable record contains a sustained alleged supervisory course, inadequate assistance when Suders sought a complaint channel, the examination-paper episode, a supervisory arrest, and resignation during that episode. It supports genuine issues concerning hostility, objective compulsion, practical corrective opportunity, delegated-authority aid, and causation. It does not establish them conclusively or require the Court to find the arrest unlawful. All eight reject restoration of employer summary judgment on the categorical grounds supplied. None equates resignation with an official employment decision or automatically attributes every act by a supervisor.

Duncan did not establish a universal independent intent-or-foreseeability condition. These commitments do not silently adopt its noncontrolling complete-rule proposal, treat Stevens or Ginsburg as having joined that proposal, or add a universal purpose-to-force-resignation element through an attribution label. The present categorical grounds can be decided without selecting among universal formulations of that additional issue. An actually preserved independent causation or mental-state contention must be adjudicated in its proper posture; no failure on such an unidentified ground is found here. This is a boundary of the question resolved, not a remand to supply missing evidence or a new requirement to plead unknown facts.

### S1 — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer: liability and proved avoidable loss remain distinct

These four would affirm the Third Circuit judgment reversing employer summary judgment and remand for ordinary proceedings under the actual-authority and Duncan rules, with the following answer to the prevention/nonuse question. Once an actionable, employer-attributable hostile environment is proved, reasonable prevention and employee nonuse do not erase that discrimination. Their proper additional operation is a loss-specific avoidability rule. The categorical assertion that resignation automatically establishes employer responsibility is rejected; it is unnecessary to characterize every constructive discharge as a tangible official act.

After the employee proves the claim and otherwise compensable injury, the employer must prove by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to afford redress without undue risk, effort, or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use them; and that the particular loss consequently could have been avoided. Recovery is reduced or barred only to that proved extent. A paper policy, nonreporting alone, an unsafe or futile route, correction after injury, or the mere possibility of a complaint does not satisfy the rule. The employee need not complain to the harasser. Injury incurred before a reasonable protective opportunity and injury the procedure could not have prevented remain compensable if otherwise established. Proof that all otherwise compensable loss was avoidable can eliminate that loss recovery, but does not itself eliminate discrimination, adjudicate other relief, or decide fees.

There is no special immunity for the absence of a formal personnel action and no categorical exemption from ordinary avoidability merely because an official act occurred. An official coercive act will often make a safe, effective preventive opportunity or causal avoidability impossible to prove; that is application, not an invented exception. The actual-authority burden stays with the plaintiff, and the avoidability burden stays with the employer. Duncan's consideration of realistic options at the compulsion stage remains separate from this later recovery inquiry.

The strongest contrary argument is that a complete defense better induces prompt internal prevention and avoids imposing liability on a diligent employer for a concealed supervisory wrong. S1 answers that the existing actual-aid requirement already confines vicarious attribution and that incentives do not justify extinguishing an otherwise proved violation or unavoidable injury. Its loss rule gives reasonable employee conduct causal work without a general reporting prerequisite. The three-Justice Ellerth concurrence expressly proposed this rule; it becomes a proposed holding here only to the extent the final coalition adopts it.

### S2 — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: a bounded complete defense is available

These four would vacate the Third Circuit judgment and remand with instructions to remove its categorical rule that constructive discharge always precludes a prevention/nonuse defense. They would retain reversal of the categorical employer-summary-judgment rationale but allow the defense to be adjudicated under the following new rule. This is a resolution of the previously reserved question, not a statement that Faragher or Ellerth already adopted it.

For a supervisor-created hostile environment, including one culminating in an objectively compelled resignation, an employer may establish a complete defense by proving by a preponderance both reasonable care to prevent and promptly correct the harassment and the employee's unreasonable failure to take advantage of the preventive or corrective opportunities provided or otherwise to avoid harm. The available measures must be practically accessible and reasonably effective; unsafe or futile channels and paper policies do not suffice. No particular written-policy form is invariably required, and nonreporting alone does not establish either element. The plaintiff's separate actual-authority/material-aid burden remains.

The defense is unavailable when an employer's official discriminatory employment act precipitates the compelled resignation: an authorized firing, demotion, or significant employer-imposed change in employment status or conditions is an example. A resignation is not itself such an act simply because the law treats it as a discharge. The employee must establish the asserted official employment act and its causal connection as part of the facts defeating the defense; the employer retains persuasion on the two affirmative-defense elements. A supervisor's title or police authority does not automatically make every act an official employment decision. The alleged arrest is important to authority, compulsion, and practical safety; this record does not conclusively classify it as the qualifying employment act or conclusively exclude that possibility.

The strongest contrary argument is S1's distinction between a proved violation and avoidable compensation. S2 regards agency responsibility under Title VII as permitting a defense that recognizes genuine employer diligence and unreasonable avoidable continuation when no official employment act consummates the discrimination. The actual-aid threshold and the complete defense perform separate functions. But S2 will not join a negligence-only attribution rule, a universal duty to exhaust complaints, or a defense that disappears solely upon proof of objective compulsion. It also will not restore summary judgment by finding effective procedures or unreasonable nonuse against the plaintiff-favorable record.

### Coalition and remedial limits

The eight Associates agree on the common objective-compulsion, actual-authority, causation, favorable-inference, and no-automatic-immunity propositions. Four support S1's formal affirmance and complete recovery rule; four support S2's vacatur and complete-defense framework. These are not eight votes for one framework, and S1 is not a logical subset of S2 merely because both can reduce money recovery. S1 preserves liability and unavoidable injury where S2 may defeat liability. No Marks conclusion or opinion assignment follows from this handoff.

All eight would continue the case in the lower courts rather than restore the supplied categorical employer summary judgment. Ordinary proceedings determine genuine facts and preserved independent grounds under the final adopted rules; no trial, new discovery, additional paper production, or liability finding is automatically commanded. Harassment injury, resignation injury, backpay, other compensatory loss, statutory remedy limits, and fees require their own lawful predicates. Failure to prove compelled resignation does not eliminate an independently sustainable hostile-environment claim, and discharge damages may not be recovered under a different label. No double recovery or punitive predicate is inferred.
