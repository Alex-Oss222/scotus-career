**Source-sensitive application:** All four robberies removed cash-register operating assets from retailers stocking goods originating outside Texas, and the owners tied those funds to replenishing inventory. The published account does not establish that every retailer directly bought goods across state lines or that an actual inventory purchase was cancelled or delayed. Quickway could replace the cash with bank funds; Gateway separately testified to delayed distributor payments. The common rationale uses actual depletion of working commercial capital and its supported business function, not a newly asserted cancelled order or a presumption arising from the word store. Maintaining purchases by replacing stolen funds does not eliminate the direct impairment of those assets. Robinson does not add a cancelled-order element.

**Alternative paths and preserved objections:** The constitutional objection requiring a substantial effect from each individual robbery conflicts with Robinson's rule for the connected economic class. The parallel degree-of-effect charge objections concern the words any/minimal and omission of substantially; the published charge still requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt and supplies only a permissive inference. The common ground rejects the individual-substantiality demand without adjudicating a separate automatic-business-label instruction defect not established in the grant or record. Statutory sufficiency is assessed from the actual operating-assets evidence under the retained offense element, not remanded to prove an invented nexus. The strongest contrary attenuation argument is answered by the business function of the stolen capital, which differs from a private victim's possible later consumption.

**Components and remedy:** The four Hobbs Act convictions and four corresponding section 924(c)(1) convictions stand against the granted commerce and wholly derivative objections; the sentence is undisturbed on those grounds. Counts 3 and 4 were acquittals and are not reopened. An attempt allegation was not submitted to the jury, and there was no conspiracy count; aggregation does not combine separate offenses or dispense with count-specific proof.
