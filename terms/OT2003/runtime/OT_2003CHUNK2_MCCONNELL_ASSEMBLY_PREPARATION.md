## 3. Enacted-text and qualification map

The complete exact text is already in `sources/mcconnell/BCRA-selected-exact.md`; use the section headings below to retrieve it. This map identifies indispensable limits without replacing that text.

| Component | Text and qualifications that must remain visible in analysis |
|---|---|
| §101 / FECA §323(a) | National committees, including congressional campaign committees, may not solicit, receive, direct covered value, or spend nonfederal funds. The extension to officers/agents acting on behalf of the committee and directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained, or controlled entities is part of the text. Receipt/solicitation and ultimate use are analytically distinct. |
| §323(b), §301(20) | Four affirmative federal-election-activity classes: registration in the 120-day period; identification/GOTV/generic activity in a federal-ballot election; candidate-referencing public communications promoting/supporting or attacking/opposing a federal candidate; and services of an employee whose compensated federal-election time exceeds 25 percent in a month. Preserve the four exclusions: qualifying state-only communications, state/local candidate contributions not designated for federal election activity, state/local conventions, and state-only grassroots materials. |
| Levin system, §323(b)(2) | Applies to the first two activity classes, subject to allocation. No federal-candidate reference; no broadcast/cable/satellite cost except a communication solely referring to a clearly identified state/local candidate; state-law-compliant donations capped at $10,000 per donor per committee per calendar year, including the controlled-person aggregation; the spending committee must raise its own funds; specified transfers, national/federal-candidate involvement and joint state/local fundraising are prohibited. The attendance/speaking exception in §323(e)(3) survives the stated cross-reference. These are cumulative conditions, not a general $10,000 exemption. |
| §323(c) | Fundraising costs for money used wholly or partly for federal election activity use federal funds. Statutory context; no independently established challenge. |
| §323(d) | Party committees, covered entities and agents acting on their behalf may not solicit for or make/direct donations to the specified §501(c) organizations engaged in federal-election spending, or the specified §527 organizations. Preserve the political-committee, state/local-party and authorized state/local-candidate-committee exceptions. Text reaches federal as well as nonfederal donations; a limiting construction must be justified, not silently inserted. Personal acts outside the representative capacity are distinct. |
| §323(e) | Separate federal-election and nonfederal-election rules. The latter imports source and amount restrictions, not every federal reporting requirement. Preserve the solely state/local candidacy exception with its referral limit; attendance, speaking and featured-guest permission; general §501(c) solicitations not specifying use, excluding principal-purpose voter-registration/mobilization entities; and specific solicitations for those activities/entities only to individuals and no more than $20,000 per individual per calendar year. |
| §323(f) | State/local candidates and officeholders: funding of §301(20)(A)(iii) public communications, with the own-state/local-election and state/local-candidates-only exception. It is not a ban on all state candidate issue speech or every mention of a federal officeholder. |
| §§102–103 | §102's state-committee limit and §307's other increases/indexing are different amendments. §103 supplies party reporting, the less-than-$5,000 state/local activity exception, over-$200 itemization, and the separate building-fund/nonpreemption treatment. Do not create an independent constitutional judgment on unpresented administrative details. |
| §201 definition | Broadcast, cable, or satellite; clearly identified federal candidate; 60-day general/special/runoff or 30-day primary/preference/convention/caucus window. The relevant-electorate requirement applies to nonpresidential candidates; reception by 50,000 persons in the specified district/state supplies it. News/commentary/editorial exception has the political-party/committee/candidate ownership-control limit; expenditures/independent expenditures and qualifying debates/forums are separate exceptions. Regulatory exemptions cannot include the statutorily excluded §301(20)(A)(iii) class. |
| §201 fallback | Activated only if the primary definition is held constitutionally insufficient by final judicial decision. It includes promotion/support/attack/opposition plus the additional no-plausible-meaning-other-than-an-exhortation-to-vote condition, and preserves §100.22(b)'s interpretation/application. The district severed the last substantive condition. That is a contested lower remedy, not enacted wording or current controlling law. |
| §201 reporting | More than $10,000 aggregate direct production/airing cost; report within 24 hours of each disclosure date; over-$200 disbursement detail. Donor disclosure is $1,000-or-more over the period from the first day of the preceding calendar year through disclosure. The qualifying individual-funded segregated account confines disclosure to that account's donors; absent it, the text reaches qualifying contributors to the disburser. The account may be used for other purposes. Section (5) separately deems executed contracts disbursements; (6) adds rather than replaces other reporting; (7) preserves tax-law definitions. |
| §§203–204 | Corporate/labor treasury restriction extends to applicable electioneering communications, including use of donated prohibited-entity funds. The §501(c)(4)/§527 statutory exception requires exclusively direct individual citizen/national/lawful-permanent-resident funds; prohibited-entity-origin funds do not qualify. A §501(c)(4) with business receipts or prohibited-entity funds must use the specified individual-only account to avoid the attribution rule. Contracts count; tax-law restrictions remain. Section 204 withdraws this statutory exception for its defined targeted communications. Day/MCFL's independent constitutional inquiry remains separate. |
| §§202, 211, 214(a),(d) | §202 treats actually coordinated electioneering disbursements/contracts as contributions and expenditures; it is not by itself a universal spending prohibition. §211 defines independent expenditure with express advocacy and absence of concert/cooperation/request/suggestion, including party connections. §214(a) separately addresses spending coordinated with party committees. §214(d) incorporates FECA contribution/expenditure definitions into §316; its pre-existing direct/indirect-payment language was not newly created. |
| §214(b),(c) | Repeal of the identified 2000 rule differs from the command for new regulations. No agreement or formal collaboration may be required; republication, common vendors, former employees, and substantial discussion must be addressed. That command does not itself declare every contact coordinated. The saved January rule requires payment, content, and conduct predicates and contains a legislative/policy inquiry safe harbor excluding campaign-plan discussions. |
| §212 | Separate $1,000/24-hour reporting after the 20th day but more than 24 hours before election; $10,000/48-hour reporting up to and including the 20th day; recurring aggregates; contract language; receipt-as-filing rule for the $1,000 report. The effective regulation measures the report deadline from public distribution/dissemination. Keep statute, administrative construction, and constitutional consequence distinct. |
| §213 | Postnomination election-cycle choice concerning the special coordinated-party allowance versus independent expenditures; specified committees treated as one; transfer/assignment/receipt restriction. The district explains that ordinary $5,000 multicandidate contribution/coordinated activity remains available after independent spending; the statute is not accurately described as eliminating every possible contribution. |
| §§304, 316, 319 | Separate Senate/House mechanisms and notice, opposition-funds, gross-receipts, timing, cessation and excess-return qualifications. The exact complete sections are supplied. The present threshold dispute does not authorize merits analysis from the label alone; if reached, apply the actual formula and claimant's category, not Daggett's different public-grant scheme. |
| §305 | Lowest-unit-charge certification concerns references to another candidate for the same office, with explicit television and radio compliance alternatives. Television requires the candidate image and specified readable statement for at least four seconds; radio requires the candidate's identifying/approval audio statement. Noncompliance affects subsequent covered broadcasts during the relevant 45-/60-day periods. Certification is made at purchase. This is not simply a prohibition on mentioning an opponent. |
| §307 | Distinguish individual candidate, national-party, biennial aggregate, senatorial committee and indexing changes; preserve the separately specified base years, rounding and odd-year periods. Contributions from January 1, 2003 are covered. Unchanged nonparty-PAC ceilings are not themselves an amendment. |
| §311 | Sponsor identification and candidate/other-speaker statements are distinct from §305's rate condition and §504's files. Preserve the challenged electioneering-communication extension and actual existing recipient class. |
| §318 | Complete text prohibits an individual aged seventeen or younger from contributing to a candidate or contributing/donating to a party committee. No earnings, consent or independent-decision exception appears in this section. Other voluntary political activity is not thereby prohibited. |
| §504 | Licensees must record purchase requests, accepted or rejected, by/on behalf of legally qualified candidates or about political matters of national importance, including the enumerated candidate, federal-election and national-legislative categories. Preserve the requested information, candidate/noncandidate distinctions, placement as soon as possible and retention for at least two years. It reaches beyond completed electioneering broadcasts. |
| §§401–403 | Severability extends to provisions and applications; it does not authorize rewriting. General November 6, 2002 operation, contribution-limit exceptions, runoff/recount/contest transitions, and the expired national-party transition are specified. Section 403's direct review and congressional-plaintiff permission do not supply Article III injury or automatically extend to an independent regulatory challenge. |
