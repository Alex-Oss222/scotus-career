## 1. Demore v. Kim — October 14, 2003

Source: supplement, Demore §§A–C, Issues 1–4. Reargument: October 6; first OT2003 merits event. The restoration order established no merits rule. The new two-gate and proof proposals remain Stone's positions until supported in assembly.

**Disposition and relief.** Affirm as modified and remand: preserve legal habeas review and protection against renewed categorical detention without the required process; do not facially invalidate §1226(c) or require an immediate hearing after every initial arrest. The district court retains its conditional-writ enforcement role. Kim remains released on the existing $5,000 bond unless lawfully modified under that writ. If removal proceedings remain pending and the Government seeks renewed §1226(c) custody, notice and a prompt custody determination ordinarily precede renewed detention. An independently lawful immediate-custody ground, such as a new criminal charge or proved release violation, remains possible but does not indefinitely excuse prompt immigration review. The judgment protects Kim; precedent may govern analogous cases, but the writ releases no class and supplies no nationwide injunction.

**Jurisdiction.** Section 1226(e) preserves §2241 review of statutory and constitutional detention authority, including legal inclusion, hearing, burden and duration objections. It does not permit judicial reweighing of a lawful discretionary custody decision or initial agency factfinding by the habeas court. Conditional release leaves the legal controversy live. Goncalves, Kolster, Calcano-Martinez, St. Cyr and Zadvydas Part I supply the effective legal-review channel; Ventura preserves agency factfinding. No fallback removes that channel; the Suspension Clause withdrawal question need not be decided.

**Inclusion gate.** A permanent resident must produce a substantial, good-faith, nondilatory objection capable of defeating every independently asserted mandatory-detention ground; this is not a burden to establish ultimate nonremovability. Government identifies each ground and supports convictions with reliable records. Stone prefers governmental preponderance for disputed historical predicates; legal classifications receive independent legal review. The express fallback permits a preliminary showing of some merit to each ground, while preserving enforceable category review and the interim hearing throughout a genuine material coverage dispute. That showing cannot become conclusive inclusion. If no category is lawfully established, ordinary §1226(a) custody rules apply; if inclusion is established, §1226(c) may operate subject independently to the duration gate. An objection to only one charge cannot defeat another established ground.

Corona-Sanchez supplies a substantial objection to the original 1997 aggravated-felony charge; the added burglary and moral-turpitude grounds require separate remand analysis. Neither conviction's ultimate classification nor removability is decided. Joseph category review may remain, but its substantially-unlikely-to-prevail standard cannot be the sole protection against continued categorical custody during a substantial dispute. National Center for Immigrants' Rights supports actual predicate determinations; Zadvydas supplies purpose-sensitive interpretation, without its six-month rule; Connecticut DPS v. Doe is distinguished because the constitutional gate makes necessity evidence legally material. Identical safeguards for differently situated nonpermanent residents are outside this as-applied claim.

**Independent duration gate.** Initial lawful custody does not authorize confinement after its nature, duration or circumstances lose a reasonable relationship to removal proceedings conducted with reasonable diligence. The resident identifies a concrete continued-custody concern. Government supplies chronology, basis, material-delay explanations and anticipated course, proving disputed diligence facts by preponderance. The whole proceeding matters: legitimate substantial defenses are not automatically misconduct; solely dilatory conduct may count against the resident; unjustified governmental or institutional delay counts against categorical continuation. Failure of continuing categorical authority triggers the individualized hearing even after valid inclusion. Material changes permit renewed review. There is no fixed day limit, automatic six-month release or transplanted §1231 presumption; Kim's historical duration requires findings, not a finding of official bad faith by this Court.

The approved duration fallback accepts an enforceable remedy for unreasonable or unjustified continuation even without Stone's full explanation; mere pendency cannot conclusively establish reasonableness. Flores, Gilbert, Krimstock, Fairley, Foucha and Jackson inform continuing authority; Zadvydas contributes the removal-purpose relationship without merging distinct statutory rules, schedules or burdens. Carlson remains distinct. Post-final-order custody remains governed by its own law.

**Hearing and proof.** Either gate yields meaningful neutral review of necessity, with relevant contrary evidence, examination of workable conditions separately and together, and reviewable reasons. Government must prove that reasonably available conditions cannot adequately address flight or danger. Stone prefers clear and convincing proof; the approved fallback is governmental preponderance with the same object of proof, hearing authority, release-conditions inquiry and enforceable relief. Stone never joins a transfer of the burden of persuasion to Kim; under Condition 6(4), he concurs in the stated substantial-question judgment and accepts its remedy as entered without joining the detainee allocation. Consider the identified personal, conviction, release-conduct and appearance evidence, including the removal case's reliability as relevant to incentives. If Government fails, conditional release must be available; if it succeeds and detention is otherwise lawful, custody may follow. Material changes permit renewed review; no fixed recurring schedule is imposed. The record establishes neither a prior hearing satisfying this rule nor ultimate danger or flight findings.

Mathews supplies the procedural inquiry; Salerno and Addington are regime-specific analogies for the preferred quantum, not universal rules. Foucha does not establish the burden Stone proposes; Krimstock's binding process and Stone's noncontrolling preponderance position remain distinct. Cooper addresses the risk of skewed uncertainty by analogy. Section 3143(b)'s criminal postconviction burden on the defendant is expressly rejected for Kim. The proposal changes neither ordinary §1226(a) bond rules generally, final removal proof, other detention statutes nor §1226(c)(2)'s witness-protection terms.

Under (4), Stone joins the section 1226(e) jurisdiction and statutory-threshold parts, concurs in the judgment and accepts the entered remedy, but joins no part allocating risk-assessment persuasion to Kim. The threshold is a category question of law or fact that is not insubstantial and not interposed solely for delay, addressed as to every asserted mandatory ground; Kim's existing $5,000 bond release remains effective pending that determination and any consequent bond decision. ## 5. Environmental Defense Center v. EPA — March 25, 2004

Source: supplement, Environmental Defense Center §§B–C, Issues 1–2. Affirm the federalism and speech rejections while preserving the separate administrative-law remands for general permitting, notice-of-intent/public review and forest roads.

**Federalism.** Own-discharge regulation and optional management are valid where a genuinely available lawful alternative regulates the municipality's discharges without compelling third-party administration. Government identifies the alternative; challengers may establish that it is illusory. Condon distinguishes own activity, New York bars compulsory legislative implementation, and Printz supplies neither immunity from ordinary compliance costs nor any compensation rule. The specified architecture satisfies this distinction; a paper alternative cannot validate an actual compulsory third-party program. Preserve the alternative and prior remands. Section 122.26(d) guarantees no numerical end-of-pipe permit and authorizes no otherwise unlawful third-party command. No universal approval of every permit, constitutional injunction or reimbursement award is authorized.

**Speech.** Assume the asserted municipal protection without deciding governmental speakers' entire constitutional status. Relevant factual pollution and disposal information serves an independent regulatory function; the agency must justify the connection. Attribution and independent criticism remain possible. Falsehood, political allegiance, personal ideological endorsement and suppression of contrary views are excluded. Glickman and United Foods supply the independent-function boundary; Barnette and Wooley remain operative beyond it. No specific false or ideological message is established here; any later such requirement or permit eliminating the alternative needs its actual terms and record. No special authorship direction or substantive fallback applies.
