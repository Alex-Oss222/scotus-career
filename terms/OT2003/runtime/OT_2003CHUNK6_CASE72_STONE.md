## 72. Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for application of the four-year period to claims made possible by the 1991 amendment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore claims wrongly dismissed under the shorter borrowed period; resolve accrual, tolling and merits only as actually disputed.
* **Remedial Reach:** The employees’ covered claims and any properly certified class; no finding of discrimination or damages.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify the amendment-dependent claims, apply the four-year statute and adjudicate surviving claims through ordinary procedure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Claims made possible by post-1990 enactments

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1658’s four-year limitation governs §1981 claims made possible by the Civil Rights Act of 1991’s amendment of an existing statute.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the recognized statutory expansion to an open limitations construction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutory trigger warrants a uniform rule for amendments. Every claim under an amended statute would be too broad. A §1981-only exception would ignore the general text.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A claim arises under a post-December 1, 1990 Act for §1658 when that enactment makes the claim possible, whether it creates a new statute or amends an existing one. A party invoking the four-year period must identify the new substantive basis; the asserted termination, hostile-environment and transfer claims qualify insofar as the 1991 amendment supplies that basis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Newly authorized claims, not unchanged causes merely pleaded after an amendment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rivers establishes that the amendment expanded the actionable contractual relationship. An amendment is itself an Act of Congress, so restricting §1658 to newly numbered statutory sections would disconnect the limitation from the liability Congress actually added.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc. is applied. Goodman v. Lukens Steel Co. remains for claims outside §1658.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
