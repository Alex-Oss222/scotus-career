## Event

Hamdi v. Rumsfeld, No. 03-6696; merits decision, June 28, 2004. Argued April 28, 2004. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 316 F.3d 450. The question is whether Congress authorized military detention of a citizen allegedly captured fighting with the Taliban in Afghanistan and what process permits him to contest that basis. Hamdi, a citizen born in Louisiana, was captured by Northern Alliance forces and transferred to United States custody. The Mobbs declaration alleges association with a Taliban unit and possession of a weapon; his next-friend petition disputes the asserted status. The court of appeals accepted sharply restricted review of that executive classification.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remands for a meaningful determination of the factual and lawful basis for Hamdi's continued custody.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated and remanded, 6–3.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Scalia would reverse and require release from this military custody unless ordinary lawful criminal process is promptly supplied or a valid suspension changes the governing law. Thomas would affirm.

The remand requires counsel, adequate notice of the asserted factual basis, a meaningful opportunity to respond before a neutral decisionmaker, and effective judicial review and relief if the custody is unlawful. The district court must proceed promptly to test the claimed authority and disputed status. The existing declaration cannot conclusively resolve that contest. The different proof and procedural proposals have the scope stated below.

## Holdings

### I. Congress authorized detention of the defined battlefield category

**Controlling proposition:** The AUMF authorizes military detention of an actual member of opposing Taliban forces captured in the authorized Afghanistan hostilities as an incident of the force Congress authorized, including when that person is a citizen. That Act supplies the congressional authority required by 18 U.S.C. §4001(a) for this category.

**Authority:** Lead Part I; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer directly adopt this proposition. Thomas joins this statutory proposition despite disagreeing with the remand and the additional limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress authorized necessary and appropriate force against the September 11-related forces identified by the AUMF. Capturing and incapacitating an opposing battlefield fighter is an established incident of that force. Citizenship does not categorically exempt an actual opposing fighter; Quirin supplies that limited law-of-war proposition. Section 4001(a) requires an Act of Congress, and this authorization supplies it for the defined category. Section 4001(b)'s military-institution exception concerns prison management and supplies no independent exception to subsection (a). The conclusion establishes authority for a category; the contested declaration still must be tested to determine whether Hamdi belongs to it.

### II. A disputed citizen classification requires an effective factual contest

**Controlling proposition:** A citizen held in prolonged domestic military custody on this disputed battlefield classification must receive counsel, notice of the asserted factual basis, a meaningful opportunity to answer before a neutral decisionmaker, and effective judicial review and relief if custody is unlawful. Judicial acceptance of the executive declaration as conclusive does not satisfy that requirement; initial battlefield capture does not require the same procedures at the point of capture.

**Authority:** Lead Part II; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt the stated minimum and its remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews requires attention to the citizen's loss of liberty, the risk of an erroneous classification, and the Government's demonstrated military interests. The authority to detain an actual fighter cannot establish that a particular prisoner is one. Counsel, adequate notice and neutral assessment allow the factual dispute to be answered while preserving room for supported security accommodations that leave the contest meaningful. St. Cyr supplies the importance of effective legal-authority review; Demore distinguishes classification from the consequences of a classification actually established. The declaration here records allegations disputed by the petition; treating it as conclusive would eliminate the inquiry. The Court therefore rejects the existing restricted review and requires prompt proceedings giving this common minimum practical effect.

**Unresolved fractured question:** No majority adopts a complete burden of persuasion or the additional presumption and military-procedure permissions. The differing positions are not logical subsets yielding one proof rule: three permit the stated rebuttal allocation, two expressly withhold that assent, and the Chief requires heightened government proof. The controlling minimum remains enforceable without converting any of those proposals into law.

### III. The authority remains tied to its combat purpose

**Controlling proposition:** Detention under this narrow battlefield authorization lasts only while the relevant active-hostilities justification for preventing return to combat continues. The authorization does not permit detention solely for interrogation; a changed purpose or end of the relevant justification requires renewed lawful authority.

**Authority:** Lead Part III; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer adopt the limitation directly; Souter and Ginsburg adopt it as a protection if the detention is sustained. These six support the same operative limit. Thomas does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Incapacitating an opposing fighter during the relevant conflict explains why detention falls within necessary and appropriate force. That explanation also limits its duration and purpose. Zadvydas supplies the method of relating detention to its statutory purpose, while its distinct immigration calendar and express security reservation remain intact. The present record establishes no cessation date; the lower court must examine the current hostilities-related basis as part of lawful-custody review. Interrogation alone cannot sustain detention under this construction after that basis fails. The statutory limit does not depend on treating the immigration regime as controlling wartime custody.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Quirin, 317 U.S. 1:** Supplies the limited proposition that citizenship does not itself remove an actual enemy belligerent from the law of war; it does not establish Hamdi's disputed status.
- **Ex parte Milligan, 71 U.S. 2:** Its civilian military-trial setting does not resolve detention of an actual opposing battlefield fighter under this authorization; the separate criminal-process position relies on its different account of the citizen's protection.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applied to the liberty, error and military interests supporting the common process minimum.
- **INS v. St. Cyr:** Its effective legal-authority review principle remains applicable with actual channel predicates.
- **Zadvydas v. Davis:** Its purpose-bound method informs the duration limit; its immigration-specific six-month framework and security reservation retain their original scope.
- **Demore v. Kim:** Its separation of actual classification from custody consequences informs review; its divided risk-burden rationale supplies no military-status proof rule.

## Law After Decision

The AUMF now supplies an express judicially recognized basis for detention of the defined actual opposing battlefield fighters, subject to the controlling purpose-duration limit and the common procedural protections for disputed prolonged domestic citizen custody. Whether Hamdi meets the factual category and whether the present justification persists are adjudication on remand under those rules. The detailed proof allocation remains fractured.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the statutory category, common process minimum and purpose-duration limit but would require the Government to establish the qualifying facts and continuing lawful basis by clear and convincing evidence. The severe, potentially prolonged loss of liberty and the danger of classification error justify that heightened rule under Mathews. Reliable summaries and other supported security accommodations may alter evidentiary form only while preserving a meaningful defense; they may not make the executive label conclusive or shift the ultimate burden to the prisoner. The district court should fix an expeditious hearing schedule and a reasonable implementation period. Failure of the governmental showing should end military custody within a court-fixed period unless an independently lawful basis is established. The immigration calendar in Zadvydas does not supply that period.

**Part IV, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer.** These three permit appropriately justified hearsay and a genuinely rebuttable evidentiary presumption: credible government evidence may require the detainee to rebut it with more persuasive evidence. The neutral decisionmaker must evaluate the evidence and permit a real response. A properly authorized military tribunal with adequate procedures may perform the initial status adjudication, with effective judicial review remaining. These are permissible accommodations, not compulsory steps or approval of an untested proceeding.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** They reject the Government's present statutory justification under §4001(a). An AUMF is not categorically incapable of authorizing detention; a demonstrated law-of-war detention within authorized force may present a sufficient basis, but the submitted justification is inadequate. Their statutory ground would require release from the challenged military custody unless a sufficient lawful basis were established. They nevertheless join the remand and the common protections because those give practical relief from the existing judgment. Their join does not accept a presumption casting the burden of rebuttal on Hamdi or a military procedure that obviates or truncates statutory habeas review.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Stevens, dissenting.** Where a citizen's enemy allegiance is contested, Milligan and the constitutional suspension structure require ordinary criminal process or a valid suspension. Quirin involved conceded belligerency and military prosecution and does not authorize replacing those protections with a judicially designed civil status hearing. They would require release from this military custody unless ordinary lawful criminal process is promptly supplied or a valid suspension changes the governing law. A new label or the majority's status proceeding cannot satisfy that alternative.

**Justice Thomas, dissenting.** Executive and military competence justify the deferential review sustained below. He accepts statutory authorization and also relies on broader executive war powers, but rejects the compelled new hearing. Intelligence gathering can serve a military purpose in his view, and political branches have substantially greater control over the necessity and duration of wartime detention than Part III permits. His position does not accept the full active-hostilities cutoff or interrogation-alone restriction.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts for prompt proceedings that provide the required counsel, notice, meaningful response, neutral determination and effective judicial review. The Government must identify the asserted authority, qualifying conduct and current custody justification. Effective relief must follow if that inquiry establishes unlawful custody.

## Event

Holland v. Jackson, No. 03-1200; merits decision, June 28, 2004. On review of the Sixth Circuit, 80 F. App'x 392, which reversed denial of habeas relief from Jackson's murder conviction and life sentence. The question concerns reliance on the later Gooch witness proffer and whether the state court applied a prejudice standard contrary to Strickland. Hughes, the principal eyewitness, placed Gooch at the scene; seven years after conviction Jackson proffered her denial. The state court rejected the later request on a procedural basis and also addressed limited impeachment value. The court of appeals relied on that proffer and isolated preponderance or probability language despite the state court's statement of the reasonable-probability standard. The State preserved its objection to reliance on the new material.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Sixth Circuit judgment granting habeas relief and remands for proper treatment of the evidentiary record and the statutory and constitutional requirements.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine support the stated correction and remand.

The appellate court must identify the state merits record, resolve the lawful basis for considering the late proffer and any properly presented procedural barrier, and then apply the proper statutory and Strickland inquiries if they remain available. An independently sufficient established ground may dispose of the claim without unnecessary further adjudication. Necessary factfinding returns to the district court. The conviction remains operative unless a lawful grant of relief follows.

## Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., STEVENS, J., O'CONNOR, J., SCALIA, J., KENNEDY, J., SOUTER, J., GINSBURG, J. and BREYER, J. joined.

## Holdings

### I. Evidence development and statutory review have distinct gates

**Controlling proposition:** A federal habeas court may not deem a state merits decision objectively unreasonable by relying on material not properly before that court without first identifying a lawful statutory and procedural basis for considering it. Section 2254(e)(2) applies when deficient diligence or greater fault is attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the state factual basis; an incomplete record alone does not trigger it.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Michael Wayne Williams makes attributable failure the trigger. If that trigger is established, the claim must rely either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or on a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence. It must also satisfy the cumulative requirement that the facts would establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Those alternatives and the cumulative requirement cannot be replaced by a seven-year rule. Terry Williams and Lockyer preserve the separate objective statutory inquiry into the state adjudication, using governing substantive holdings established at that adjudication; §§2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) retain their separate factual roles. The Sixth Circuit did not resolve the state rejection of the proffer or the necessary diligence predicates before relying on Gooch's account. Potential impeachment value does not cure that omission. An alternative state merits discussion does not itself eliminate an independent procedural basis for rejecting material, while a record-admission restriction does not automatically establish an adequate and independent default of the underlying claim. Any such default and applicable exception need their own predicates.

### II. The actual prejudice analysis controls

**Controlling proposition:** A state court acts contrary to Strickland when it actually requires a more-likely-than-not different outcome instead of a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the result. Isolated shorthand or a burden for proving underlying facts does not establish that substitution when the complete opinion applies the proper legal standard; a correct citation does not cure contrary actual reasoning.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Visciotti requires examination of the whole state opinion and supported record. References to preponderance, disbelief of an eyewitness or probability must be read in their function rather than treated as automatic legal error. Strickland separates proof of what occurred from the legal assessment of prejudice. Here the state court expressly stated reasonable probability, and its other language can be read consistently with that rule; detached phrases therefore cannot sustain the writ. Conversely, the opening citation alone does not establish a reasonable application. Lockyer requires the independent statutory threshold as well as an underlying constitutional violation, and Gentry's contextual performance analysis does not admit the new proffer or decide prejudice. If the claim remains available after the applicable gates, the appellate court must perform those inquiries on the evidence that may lawfully be considered.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420:** Applied to applicant-attributed failure, diligence and the separate statutory exceptions for factual development.
- **Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362:** Applied to the independent contrary-to and objectively-unreasonable-application clauses.
- **Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668:** Its reasonable-probability standard governs constitutional prejudice; a factual proof burden is a different question.
- **Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19:** Applied to the complete state reasoning and supported record, preserving both its rejection of isolated-word analysis and its substantive reasonableness inquiry.
- **Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63:** Preserves the state-adjudication substantive cutoff, separate statutory threshold and underlying constitutional requirement.
- **Yarborough v. Gentry:** Its contextual performance review remains distinct from admission of new material and prejudice.

## Law After Decision

The governing Williams, Strickland, Visciotti and Lockyer rules remain in force and require setting aside this inadequately grounded grant. Lawful consideration of the proffer, attributable diligence, any established default and its exception, and final constitutional prejudice are separate applications to be resolved on remand. Permission to develop evidence does not itself satisfy §2254(d) or establish a new claim.

## Event

Missouri v. Seibert, No. 02-1371; merits decision, June 28, 2004. Argued December 9, 2003. On certiorari to the Missouri Supreme Court, 93 S.W.3d 700, which suppressed Seibert's warned repetition and reversed her second-degree murder conviction. Police obtained an unwarned confession during thirty to forty minutes of questioning, paused about twenty minutes, then used the same officer, substantially overlapping questions and references to the first admissions after warnings and a signed form. The officer acknowledged deliberately using the question-first method. The question is whether those midstream warnings supplied an effective choice and waiver.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment suppressing the repeated confession and reversing the conviction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would vacate and remand for ordinary Elstad analysis.

The repeated confession must be excluded at any retrial, as must the initial statement already suppressed. Missouri may retry Seibert on independently admissible evidence within applicable procedure. The reversed conviction cannot itself support continued custody; custody must rest on valid charges and ordinary state authority.

## Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. Kennedy joins only the application to the deliberate, uncured sequence.

| Writing or portion | Author | Joined by | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead: objective effectiveness rule | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer | Five-Justice controlling rule independent of proof of deliberate evasion. |
| Lead: deliberate, uncured application and affirmance | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer | Six Justices agree that this sequence requires exclusion. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Kennedy | None | Joins the stated application; favors a deliberate-evasion trigger with effective cure, retaining ordinary Elstad for other omissions. |
| Concurrence | Breyer | None | Joins the lead and adds a fruits rule subject to good faith. |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia and Thomas | Would vacate for ordinary Elstad inquiry into the actual statements and circumstances. |

## Holdings

### I. Midstream warnings must communicate a genuine choice

**Controlling proposition:** When a confession follows unwarned custodial questioning, the prosecution must establish a knowing, voluntary and effective warning waiver by a preponderance of the evidence on the complete circumstances. In a coordinated question-first sequence, courts must assess the initial questioning's completeness, overlapping content, timing, setting, personnel, references to prior admissions and meaningful corrective measures; deliberate evasion is powerful evidence but is not necessary to find objectively ineffective warnings. A signed form cannot cure circumstances in which the warnings could not reasonably convey a real choice to stop.

**Authority:** The lead's rule portion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join at this generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda protects an informed choice in custodial interrogation, and Dickerson prevents replacing its constitutional safeguard with voluntariness alone. Elstad permits a genuinely effective later warning and waiver; it does not make every recitation effective simply because it follows an unwarned statement. The relevant circumstances determine whether the second interview offered an intelligible fresh choice or instead continued a confession already treated as irrevocable. The factors are considerations, not a numerical test. No fixed waiting period or additional sentence supplies a categorical safe harbor. Missouri's reliance on a voluntary answer and signed waiver does not answer whether the warnings communicated their required choice. A prior unwarned statement is also not an automatic permanent bar to a later confession: genuine separation and an effective waiver remain legally significant.

### II. This continuous, deliberate sequence requires exclusion

**Controlling proposition:** The warned repetition obtained through this detailed initial confession, short break, same officer, overlapping content and use of prior admissions must be suppressed. The established deliberate method lacked effective intervening measures that restored an informed choice.

**Authority:** The lead's application; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly support this sufficient ground. Kennedy's application join does not extend to Part I's intent-independent rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The officer first obtained the developed confession and then used it to elicit its repetition. The record supplies no meaningful change that conveyed a new choice to decline. Elstad's transition from a brief unwarned exchange to an effective warned interview is materially different. An intent requirement would not alter this result because the officer established deliberate coordination himself. The warning form consequently did not make the repeated statement admissible, and Missouri's requested reinstatement of the conviction fails. The state suppression and conviction-reversal judgment is affirmed.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436:** Applied to effective warnings and knowing, voluntary waiver in the whole custodial sequence.
- **Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Its constitutional safeguard governs; voluntariness alone cannot substitute for effective warnings and waiver.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Distinguished on the actual continuity and lack of effective separation; its rule for a genuinely effective subsequent warning and waiver remains.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157:** The ordinary prosecution burden to establish waiver remains distinct from proving actual coercion.

## Law After Decision

A direct five-Justice majority establishes objective effectiveness for later warnings following unwarned custodial questioning. Courts apply the complete-sequence rule without making subjective intent indispensable or treating any earlier omission as permanent taint. No Marks synthesis is needed. Suppression of this repeated confession follows under both the controlling objective rule and the six-Justice deliberate-uncured application.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He would retain ordinary Elstad analysis for initial warning omissions other than a deliberate two-stage method designed to defeat Miranda. For that deliberate method, the repeated statement is excluded unless effective curative measures restore an informed choice. A genuinely substantial break or an adequate additional explanation may suffice in an appropriate case, but neither automatically cures the defect and neither is established here. He joins the deliberate, uncured application but rejects making objective ineffectiveness alone the new general midstream trigger.

**Justice Breyer, concurring.** He joins the objective rule and would also exclude fruits of an unwarned interrogation unless the omission was in good faith. The proved deliberate method here fails that exception. His fruits formulation does not supply the other Justices' general rationale.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** Elstad calls for examining the actual statements and circumstances under the ordinary warning, waiver and voluntariness principles. They reject both the new objective midstream rule and a test turning on officer purpose. Actual coercion of an initial statement and its effect on a later statement remain relevant, as do the complete circumstances and a genuinely voluntary later answer. They would vacate and remand for that inquiry, rather than declare the repetition automatically admissible or treat the signed form as conclusive.

## Procedure After Action

The Missouri judgment remains effective. Any retrial must exclude both the initial statement and the challenged repetition and proceed on independently admissible evidence under ordinary state procedure.

## Source Notes

U.S. Const., Amends. V and XIV; Missouri Supreme Court, 93 S.W.3d 700. The officer's testimony establishes deliberate coordination. Warning effectiveness is distinct from a finding of actual coercion.

## Event

Rasul v. Bush and Al Odah v. United States, Nos. 03-334 and 03-343; merits decision, June 28, 2004. Argued April 20, 2004. On certiorari to the District of Columbia Circuit, 321 F.3d 1134, which affirmed categorical jurisdictional dismissal of the consolidated detainee actions. Foreign nationals captured abroad are held without military convictions at Guantánamo. Cuba retains ultimate sovereignty under the lease while the United States exercises complete and continuing jurisdiction and control. The questions concern statutory habeas and Al Odah's independently pleaded §§1331, 1350 and APA civil theories.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the habeas dismissal and the categorical dismissal of the civil branch, remanding for custody review and claim-specific civil adjudication.

## Holdings

### I. Section 2241 reaches these Guantánamo custody claims

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2241 permits a substantial claim that reachable United States custodial officials hold a person at Guantánamo in violation of federal law where the United States exercises the complete and continuing jurisdiction and control established here. Foreign citizenship and Cuba's reserved ultimate sovereignty do not alone defeat that statutory jurisdiction; the prerequisites and merits of relief remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The lead's habeas portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute concerns custody exercised by officials whom the court can reach. The United States controls administration, access and confinement at this enduring base outside an active battlefield. Reserved ultimate sovereignty does not make that governmental control disappear. Eisentrager involved a different territorial and posttrial setting, including military convictions; its continuing framework does not foreclose these unadjudicated, potentially indefinite restraints under the control arrangement here. Braden rejects an invariable requirement that the prisoner be physically present in the forum; it need not be read to destroy Eisentrager's statutory foundation to reach this result. St. Cyr and Calcano-Martinez preserve effective statutory review while retaining actual channel predicates. The Government's proposed categorical alien-abroad rule would deny the writ despite this control and reachable custodian. The narrower statutory answer requires no universal decision about extraterritorial constitutional rights. Disputed innocence remains for the custody adjudication.

### II. Alien status does not replace the civil claims' actual predicates

**Controlling proposition:** Nonresident aliens held at Guantánamo are not categorically disabled from invoking an otherwise available federal civil action and jurisdictional grant. Courts must examine each pleaded §§1331, 1350 and APA theory for its own jurisdiction, cause of action, reviewability, immunity and remedy requirements; neither alien status nor recognition of habeas jurisdiction establishes or defeats those conditions by itself.

**Authority:** All nine join rejection of universal alien incapacity and remand of genuinely independent statutory claims. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also require reversal of the categorical dismissal of the whole civil branch. Scalia and Thomas preserve their different disposition for claims necessarily dependent on the constitutional entitlement they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira and Kadic distinguish jurisdiction from a defined domestic action with actual norm, actor and remedy predicates. Those decisions preclude a universal incapacity to sue but establish neither an ATS detention action nor a waiver of United States immunity. The same source-specific method applies to Al Odah's pleaded civil theories. Eisentrager is not a general litigation disability for every independent statutory claim merely because its claimant is an alien held abroad. The court below disposed of the branch categorically instead of resolving those requirements. Remand permits the necessary individual legal inquiry, including the actual APA reviewability and waiver, the claimed ATS norm and action, and available relief. A statutory label alone cannot make an otherwise identical constitutional theory independent.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Johnson v. Eisentrager, 339 U.S. 763:** Distinguished on the enduring control, absence of military convictions and actual confinement setting; its statutory framework and unaddressed constitutional questions are not generally overruled.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Applied to reject an invariable physical-presence requirement when the proper custodian is reachable.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Their effective statutory-review principles inform the actual §2241 channel and preserve its predicates.
- **Abebe-Jira v. Negewo; Kadic v. Karadzic:** Their distinct jurisdiction and defined-action holdings require claim-specific civil analysis and do not themselves establish a detention action or governmental immunity waiver.

## Law After Decision

Section 2241 now expressly reaches the defined Guantánamo control arrangement when the responsible custodian is reachable. Courts must adjudicate the actual custody basis and effective relief; the asserted substantive extraterritorial constitutional entitlement remains undecided because the statutory ground resolves the habeas question. Al Odah's independent civil theories return for their actual statutory, immunity and remedial inquiries. Whether a claim satisfies those rules is ordinary claim adjudication, not a reservation of the rule rejecting universal alien incapacity.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting.** Eisentrager retains the statutory limit for foreign nationals captured and confined outside sovereign United States territory. Braden concerns a domestic detainer and does not remove that foreign-custody limit. They also reject the independently presented constitutional entitlement on these foreign-capture and foreign-confinement facts. They therefore would affirm habeas dismissal and any civil claim necessarily dependent on that rejected entitlement. Their agreement with Abebe-Jira and Kadic's jurisdiction distinctions nevertheless prevents treating aliens as universally incapable of suit. They accept remand of genuinely independent statutory claims for jurisdiction, recognized action, immunity, reviewability and remedy analysis. That limited remand recognizes no new detention action or waiver and does not allow the identical constitutional claim to proceed through a changed label.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must set a prompt return and briefing schedule, provide access necessary for the custody challenge with supported protective arrangements, and adjudicate preserved defenses. It must separately examine the civil theories under their actual legal predicates and grant effective relief within its jurisdiction if unlawful custody or another actionable violation is established.

## Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1350 and 2241–2243; Administrative Procedure Act; United States-Cuba lease arrangements. Allegations of innocence and unlawful treatment are disputed allegations. The petitioners have not received the military convictions present in Eisentrager.

## Event

Rumsfeld v. Padilla, No. 03-1027; merits decision, June 28, 2004. Argued April 28, 2004. On certiorari to the Second Circuit, 352 F.3d 695, which accepted New York jurisdiction but rejected authority for the military detention. Padilla was arrested in Chicago on a material-witness warrant and brought to New York. Before the later petition challenging military custody was filed, he was designated an enemy combatant and moved to the Charleston brig. That petition named the President, Secretary Rumsfeld and commander Melanie Marr. The questions are the proper custodian and forum and, only if reachable, detention authority.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the forum ruling, vacates the New York detention-merits adjudication and remands for prompt examination of a lawful transfer route or dismissal without prejudice.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Forum ruling reversed, merits adjudication vacated, and remanded, 5–4.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the exceptional forum and confined no-authority merits ruling and remand to implement relief from military custody.

The district court must promptly examine available statutory transfer authority and use it when its predicates and the interest of justice require transfer. If no lawful transfer is available, dismissal must be without prejudice to immediate proper refiling against the actual custodian. Officials must identify that custodian and location accurately. The forum disposition leaves detention legality for a proper action.

## Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., O'CONNOR, J., KENNEDY, J. and THOMAS, J. joined. Kennedy filed a concurring opinion, in which O'Connor joined. Stevens filed a dissenting opinion, in which Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined.

The Court's opinion addresses the domestic filing sequence, immediate custodian and conditional forum remedy. The concurrence's personal-jurisdiction or venue characterization and affirmative exceptions are its own additional ground; the four dissenters alone reach detention authority.

## Holdings

### I. A new domestic physical-custody petition ordinarily follows the immediate custodian

**Controlling proposition:** A new petition challenging present physical confinement at a known domestic facility ordinarily must name the immediate custodian and proceed in a district with authority over that custodian. Senior supervisory control alone does not replace those requirements; a properly supported continuing-jurisdiction or exceptional-forum claim requires its own predicates.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2242 and 2243 direct the petition and return to the person exercising the custody challenged. The known brig commander exercises Padilla's present physical custody in South Carolina. Braden concerns the restraint actually challenged and does not make every supervising official a domestic custodian; Endo preserves an already properly filed proceeding despite a later transfer. Padilla's earlier material-witness challenge was a different proceeding, and this military-custody petition followed the transfer. It therefore cannot borrow an earlier habeas filing that did not occur. St. Cyr protects effective review of authority, while Calcano-Martinez preserves the predicates of the actual channel. An effective South Carolina forum answers the claimed absence of review without treating the Secretary's supervisory authority as sufficient everywhere. The record establishes no bad-faith transfer exception. Detention authority remains outside the Court's reach in this forum posture.

### II. The forum remedy must preserve an available lawful route

**Controlling proposition:** On identifying this forum defect, the lower court must promptly consider an authorized transfer and apply the actual conditions of that authority; otherwise it must dismiss without prejudice to a properly filed challenge. Section 1631 requires a qualifying court's finding of want of jurisdiction, an eligible destination where the action could have been brought when filed, and the interest of justice; when those conditions are met, transfer is mandatory and the original filing date is preserved.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; the same five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The transfer statute supplies an alternative to requiring a new filing only when its text applies. The court must identify the character of the defect and the available destination rather than assume that every custodian or forum problem falls within §1631. Consideration does not establish eligibility, but an available mandatory transfer cannot be disregarded. If the conditions are absent and no other lawful route permits transfer, dismissal without prejudice allows immediate proper refiling. The receiving court must address properly presented requests for interim protection and custody review within its own lawful authority. That arrangement enforces the actual forum rules while preserving the effective review channel.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Endo, 323 U.S. 283:** Its continuation of already attached jurisdiction is distinguished because the military-custody petition here followed the transfer.
- **Braden v. 30th Judicial Circuit Court, 410 U.S. 484:** Its focus on the challenged custody and reachable custodian does not replace the ordinary domestic immediate-custodian rule with senior supervision.
- **INS v. St. Cyr; Calcano-Martinez v. INS:** Applied to preserve effective statutory authority review with actual custody, respondent and forum predicates.
- **Zadvydas v. Davis:** Its removal-purpose detention holding supplies no domestic military-detention merits answer in this forum disposition.

## Law After Decision

New domestic physical-custody petitions follow the ordinary immediate-custodian and territorial forum requirements, with actual continuing-jurisdiction or other exceptions considered on their established predicates. Conditional transfer consideration and dismissal without prejudice preserve the proper route. The AUMF's application to this domestic arrest and the constitutional detention question await an action in a forum able to decide them.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Kennedy, joined by Justice O'Connor, concurring.** The custodian and forum limitations concern personal jurisdiction or venue rather than the existence of federal subject-matter habeas power. They can be waived, and a supported exception may apply when officials obstruct access through concealment, evasive transfer or other interference with the appropriate forum. The actual filing sequence and known custodian establish no such exception here. They therefore join the ordinary-forum result and the conditional transfer consideration without treating it as a categorical subject-matter rule.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The official who personally directed this particular replacement of judicial custody by military restraint, controls it and can end it is a reachable custodian under a confined exceptional-control theory. That ground neither finds bad faith nor treats the later petition as filed before the transfer. On the merits, the supplied domestic arrest and designation fail to establish the congressional authorization and supported belligerent predicate required under §4001(a) and the AUMF. Section 4001(b)'s prison-management provision does not exempt this detention from subsection (a). They would implement relief ending the challenged military custody while permitting an identified independently lawful custody basis. Their position does not decide that every domestic arrestee is beyond the law of war or confer immunity from prosecution.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate returns the case for prompt examination of the actual transfer predicates. A qualifying transfer preserves the original filing date as the applicable statute directs; otherwise the petition is dismissed without prejudice to filing in the proper district. The receiving court considers properly presented counsel, interim-protection and detention-review requests within its lawful authority.

## Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §§2241–2243 and 1631; 18 U.S.C. §4001; AUMF, Pub. L. 107-40, 115 Stat. 224. The material-witness challenge preceded the military-custody petition; no adjudicated bad-faith transfer finding is present.

## Event

United States v. Patane, No. 02-1183; merits decision, June 28, 2004. Argued December 9, 2003. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 304 F.3d 1013, which rejected the district court's probable-cause rationale but affirmed suppression of a pistol as the fruit of incomplete Miranda warnings. Officers investigating a restraining-order violation arrested Patane and began warnings; he interrupted by saying he knew his rights. A subsequent answer disclosed the pistol's location. The reviewed question assumes the answer was voluntary and asks whether the incomplete warning alone requires suppression of its physical fruit.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses suppression insofar as it rests solely on the Miranda physical-fruits ground and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm suppression. Breyer would vacate and remand to determine whether the warning omission was in good faith, with exclusion absent that exception.

The automatic Miranda-fruits bar to the pistol is removed. The lower court must apply any genuinely preserved independent admission ground; the appellate rejection of the probable-cause rationale remains part of the posture. The pistol may be admitted if otherwise lawful, while case-in-chief use of the unwarned answer remains governed by the separate statement rule.

## Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, J., SCALIA, J., KENNEDY, J. and THOMAS, J. joined. Thomas filed a concurring opinion, in which Scalia joined. Souter filed a dissenting opinion, in which Stevens and Ginsburg joined. Breyer filed a separate dissenting opinion.

The Court's opinion adopts the narrow physical-evidence remedy. The Thomas concurrence's broader duty and deterrence theory has only its two named supporters. The separate dissents seek different judgments.

## Holdings

**Controlling proposition:** Failure to give complete Miranda warnings restricts case-in-chief use of the resulting statement but does not by itself require suppression of nontestimonial physical evidence derived from a voluntary answer. When voluntariness is properly contested, the prosecution must establish it under the applicable burden and satisfy other independent admission requirements; an unwarned answer is not thereby a valid waiver, and the rule does not extend to evidence produced by actual coercion.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas directly join the narrow remedial proposition and reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** Dickerson preserves Miranda's constitutional warning-and-waiver safeguard and prevents voluntariness alone from authorizing the answer's ordinary case-in-chief use. It left the physical-fruits question open. Tucker and Elstad show that constitutional status does not require identical exclusion for an unwarned answer and every derivative evidentiary product. Here the offered item is a pistol, the reviewed answer is assumed voluntary, and the challenge rests on the incomplete warning itself. The direct gain to investigators identified by the dissent does not eliminate those distinct remedial categories. Admitting a voluntary answer's physical product neither establishes a knowing waiver nor resolves an actual-coercion or independent acquisition claim. Chavez's fractured no-use damages disposition supplies no controlling theory of physical-fruit admissibility; its independent due-process inquiry remains distinct. The appellate court therefore erred in treating Dickerson's constitutional characterization alone as a sufficient fruits rule. The warning-only ground for suppressing this pistol must be reversed, with any genuinely preserved independent objection considered in its proper scope.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Miranda v. Arizona; Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428:** Their constitutional case-in-chief warning-and-waiver core is preserved; Dickerson's open physical-fruits question is resolved at the voluntary, nontestimonial scope here.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433; Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298:** Their differentiated consequences support this physical-remedy distinction without replacing the original statement's warning requirement.
- **Chavez v. Martinez, 538 U.S. 760:** Its no-use damages judgment had no controlling Fifth Amendment rationale and does not decide admissibility; its separate interrogation-abuse inquiry survives at its actual scope.

## Law After Decision

Incomplete warnings alone no longer justify excluding nontestimonial physical fruits of a voluntary answer. Statements, actual coercion and independently unlawful acquisition retain their own rules and properly preserved remedies. An incomplete warning does not establish waiver; the dispute decided concerns this evidentiary category.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, concurring.** They also rely on the Self-Incrimination Clause's criminal-case and testimonial focus. In their view, mere omission of warnings does not itself supply the constitutional violation that excluding this physical evidence would deter. Their broader characterization is unnecessary to the five-Justice narrow remedy and is not joined by O'Connor, Kennedy or Stone-Zsela. It provides no permission for actual coercion.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The gun is the direct investigative product of unwarned testimonial disclosure. Allowing that benefit weakens the constitutional safeguard in practice and encourages bypassing warnings. Tucker's setting and Elstad's effective intervening warning do not resolve this direct-fruit problem. They would affirm suppression without requiring a new finding of deliberate evasion or actual coercion.

**Justice Breyer, dissenting.** He would exclude fruits of unwarned questioning unless the warning omission was in good faith. Because this record establishes neither good faith nor deliberate evasion, he would vacate and remand for that inquiry. Incomplete warnings and the absence of a complete waiver do not themselves decide whether the exception applies; he does not vote for unconditional affirmance or unconditional admission.

## Procedure After Action

The prosecution returns to the lower court with the warning-only physical-fruits bar removed. The court must apply any genuinely preserved independent admission requirement, including properly contested voluntariness under its applicable burden, while respecting the existing appellate disposition of the probable-cause ground.

## Source Notes

U.S. Const., Amends. V and XIV; Tenth Circuit, 304 F.3d 1013. Voluntariness is the reviewed premise. Patane's interruption of warnings does not establish a complete waiver, and the record supplies no finding resolving good faith or deliberate evasion.
