## Judgment and common supported ground

All eight Associates vote to reverse the Ninth Circuit's antitrust-coverage ruling and remand for dismissal of the federal antitrust claims against the Postal Service, with the independent procurement and state-law claims retaining their separate posture. Each joins the substantive coverage holding and that remedy in full.

Under the then-operative statutes, the Postal Service is an independent establishment of the executive branch of the United States, not an antitrust defendant separate from the United States. Its broad sue-and-be-sued power removes sovereign immunity within its scope but does not supply the substantive antitrust coverage that the claim requires. The Sherman and Clayton Act person provisions, read with the received governmental-status rule in United States v. Cooper Corp., do not make this federal establishment a covered antitrust defendant merely because it contracts commercially or operates with administrative independence.

FDIC v. Meyer requires the coverage inquiry after waiver and supplies the decisive distinction. Loeffler v. Frank and Franchise Tax Board v. Postal Service support broad incidents of suit under the express postal waiver, rather than alteration of each underlying statute's defendant class. The commercial-operation argument therefore establishes neither an antitrust person nor the circuit's proposed immunity confined to congressionally commanded acts. Trinko concerns independently covered private-firm conduct, injury and remedies; its refusal to equate regulation with antitrust immunity does not answer this antecedent entity-status question.

## Alternative-path check and assembly boundary

The competing coverage path emphasizes the broad waiver and independent commercial operations. Its strongest point is the normal treatment of a sue-and-be-sued public enterprise as amenable to ordinary business litigation. Meyer answers that argument at its precise limit: amenability and coverage remain distinct. None of the eight commitments turns on immunity from an otherwise applicable antitrust duty. The allegation of a mail-sack scheme remains an allegation; the entity-status holding disposes of the presented claims without factfinding about that scheme.

No separate writing is needed to preserve a material Associate disagreement. Form and assignment belong to assembly. These commitments do not select an author, count the Chief, or decide other government entities or private contractors. Those absent questions are internal scope boundaries, not proposed public reservations.
