## Event

**Tennessee v. Lane, No. 02-1667.** Argued January 13, 2004. Decided May 17, 2004, on review of the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of the denial of Tennessee's immunity motion. The question is whether Congress validly authorized private Title II enforcement against a State for disability-based denial of access to its courts.

Lane alleges that inaccessible courtrooms required him to crawl to a criminal proceeding and that he was later arrested after refusing another inaccessible appearance. Jones, a court reporter, alleges exclusion from professional opportunities because of inaccessible courthouses. They seek damages and equitable relief. These allegations present distinct individual claims at an immunity stage.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the decision.

## Public Action

Affirmed and remanded, 7–2; pleaded court-access claims proceed under Title II.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The denial of Tennessee's categorical immunity motion stands for the pleaded court-access claims. The case returns for adjudication of statutory coverage, actual exclusion, accommodation, causation, defenses and lawful relief under the court-access rule.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela filed a concurring opinion. Thomas filed a dissenting opinion, joined by Scalia.

## Holdings

### Reasonable judicial-access duties are valid preventive enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may authorize private Title II actions against States for reasonable disability accommodations securing access to judicial proceedings and the services through which those proceedings operate. Those bounded duties congruently prevent and remedy constitutional denials of meaningful judicial participation, including statutory violations that do not require proof of a separate individual constitutional tort; §12202 unmistakably authorizes enforcement.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices directly adopt the court-access rule and ordinary statutory remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Popovich already sustains reasonable hearing accommodations and related protection enabling meaningful participation in custody adjudication. Physical exclusion from a courtroom threatens that same opportunity through a different barrier. Boerne permits prevention beyond conduct independently adjudged unconstitutional when the statutory means fit the constitutional injury. A criminal defendant's opportunity to attend and defend and the protected opportunity to participate meaningfully in adjudication supply concrete constitutional anchors. The present inquiry examines the functional judicial-access class rather than every Title II service.

Qualification, essential eligibility, reasonable modification, effective alternatives and the applicable fundamental-alteration and existing-facilities burden limits confine the statutory response. Ordinary disability rationality review remains the classification baseline; the protected opportunity for meaningful judicial participation supplies the constitutional interest here. Garrett's Section 5 conclusion concerned Title I's general employment requirements at their full presented scope; it preserved differently fitted prevention and left Title II to its own analysis. The immediate relationship between inaccessible judicial services and loss of meaningful participation supplies the fit here.

The contrary argument is that a courthouse houses persons and activities without identical individual constitutional entitlements. A courthouse address alone is insufficient. The rule concerns participation in judicial proceedings and their operative services. Reasonably accommodating persons who serve those proceedings can protect the judicial function without making every beneficiary's constitutional interest identical. This defined preventive class therefore permits adjudication of both pleaded courthouse-access claims. Jones must establish that her actual claim concerns judicial access or those services; her professional opportunity is not equated with Lane's criminal-defense right. A claim showing only an unrelated employment benefit falls outside this constitutional basis.

### Statutory conditions and the State's actual defense burdens remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish qualification under §12131, disability-based exclusion, denial or discrimination under §12132, and the conditions of the remedy supplied through §12133. A State invoking fundamental alteration must substantiate that defense under the applicable rule; the distinct existing-facilities defense and its procedural requirements govern only when their own conditions apply.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the claim requirements, defense allocation and qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 35.130(b)(7) requires necessary reasonable modifications unless the public entity demonstrates that they would fundamentally alter the service, program or activity. The claimant still establishes the applicable claim and necessary reasonable modification. The State must support its claimed alteration with evidence and reasons tied to that actual service and modification; a label, general inconvenience or cost assertion is insufficient.

Section 35.150(a) separately requires an existing-facilities service, program or activity, viewed in its entirety, to be readily accessible. It does not necessarily require accessibility of every existing building and does not require action threatening or destroying a historic property's historic significance. Under §35.150(a)(3), the entity bears the burden of proving fundamental alteration or undue financial and administrative burdens. The head or designee must consider all resources available to fund and operate the service and supply written reasons. Even when the proposed action creates those burdens or alteration, the entity must take another action avoiding them while ensuring receipt of its benefits or services.

Those provisions have distinct reach. Financial and administrative burden is not an added defense to every §35.130(b)(7) modification claim; the head-of-entity and written-reasons requirements likewise belong to §35.150(a)(3). Section 35.150(b)(1) permits effective alternative methods rather than structural change when those methods achieve compliance and prioritizes the most integrated appropriate setting. The court must apply those actual conditions to the claims and evidence. A barrier, ineffective judicial access and a compensable injury remain distinct inquiries. Other public-service applications lie outside the question presented.

### Prospective relief must effectively cure the proved violation within lawful remedial scope

**Holding and operative rule:** If statutory liability and the requirements for prospective relief are established, the court must provide effective relief within its lawful authority; a narrower measure suffices only if it fully cures the proved violation for the persons the court may lawfully protect. Any injunction must impose enforceable obligations, and policy changes or implementation deadlines may be required when the actual remedial findings establish their necessity for effective relief.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Popovich establishes an enforceable access duty while separating authority to sue from entitlement to the actual award. Frew requires effective enforcement of lawful prospective obligations and appropriate correction of established noncompliance; its existing decree does not predetermine a new decree's beneficiaries or terms. On remand the court must examine actual access barriers, reasonable accommodations and alternatives, supported defenses and relevant continuing risk. The actual standing, merits and remedial findings determine the form and reach of relief. Necessary policy correction may incidentally benefit others, but affected-person status alone supplies no standing or remedial entitlement. Lane's criminal-proceeding allegations and Jones's court-reporting allegations require separate application of these rules.

**Material precedent treatment:** Popovich's meaningful-participation holding is applied to physical judicial access, with its distinction between a valid statutory action and an established remedy. Boerne's fit framework governs; Garrett's Title I holding and Olmstead's integration question retain their own scope. Frew supplies the effective-enforcement principle at the actual stage and within lawful remedial authority.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas:** Applied and extended from reasonable assistance in parental-custody hearings to the defined physical judicial-access class; its prevention beyond individual constitutional torts and actual statutory conditions remain controlling.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Applied to require congruence and proportionality between the protected opportunity and the preventive duties, without a mandatory evidence quota or violation by each defendant State.
- **Board of Trustees v. Garrett:** Distinguished at its actual Title I scope; its Section 5 analysis leaves room for a differently supported and fitted Title II application.
- **Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs:** Preserves statutory-duty and constitutional-injury distinctions and statute-specific fit.
- **Olmstead v. L.C.:** Distinguished as a statutory integration decision, rather than a general immunity holding.
- **Frew v. Hawkins:** Applied for effective enforcement of lawful prospective obligations; its existing decree and defined beneficiaries remain distinct from the new remedies to be adjudicated here.

## Law After Decision

Expressly authorized Title II claims concerning access to judicial proceedings and their operative services may proceed against States under Section 5. The claimant need not establish an independent constitutional tort as an additional gateway within that valid preventive class. Actual statutory coverage, effective accommodation, defenses and remedial proof continue to govern each claim, including Jones's asserted role in judicial services. The State bears the applicable fundamental-alteration burden, with §35.150's distinct existing-facilities requirements preserved. Lawful prospective relief must effectively cure the proved violation; the necessity and extent of policy correction or deadlines depend on actual remedial findings and the persons the court may lawfully protect.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the court-access holding, the State's defense burden and the qualified effective-relief guidance. He would additionally prescribe a default for a proved unlawful access policy or practice: correct that policy for the persons it governs unless Tennessee shows that narrower relief completely cures the violation. He would direct the district court to identify each inaccessible service, effective accommodation and continuing risk, then set enforceable access measures and deadlines for the violation proved. Standing, statutory liability and the independent prerequisites for damages and prospective relief remain necessary.

His additional position gives the policy's proved reach operative remedial weight and places the showing for narrower correction on Tennessee. Popovich supplies the protected opportunity and an enforceable access duty. The Chief would specify this implementation requirement now so that recognition of meaningful participation produces effective correction, while preserving effective alternatives and actual statutory burden limits.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** The two would vacate the categorical immunity denial and remand for a supported statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful judicial participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it. They accept §12202's express authorization and the constitutional hearing right. Their objection concerns the fit of the broader preventive damages regime, particularly when the asserted interest is a professional opportunity rather than participation in one's own adjudication.

Following their Popovich position, they would reconsider that decision's preventive-damages reach to the extent necessary. They do not treat it as absent. Effective participation may be supplied by alternatives even when a further statutory accommodation duty is alleged. Boerne and Garrett require examination of that relationship rather than reliance on the courthouse setting alone. Their proposed remand concerns the actual preserved claims; it is not a universal rule that all Section 5 prevention requires an individual constitutional tort, a prescribed incident count or a violation by every regulated State.

## Procedure After Action

The lower court must determine each plaintiff's disability and qualification, the proceeding or service at issue, actual access, available reasonable accommodations and the State's supported defenses under the applicable regulation. It must resolve actionable exclusion, causation, compensable injury and lawful relief. If the predicates for prospective relief are established, it must provide an effective cure for those it may lawfully protect, with enforceable obligations and any policy changes or implementation deadlines shown necessary by the remedial findings. Lane's criminal-proceeding allegations and Jones's court-reporting allegations require separate application.
