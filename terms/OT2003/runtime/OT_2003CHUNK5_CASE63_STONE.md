## 63. National Archives and Records Administration v. Favish

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the disclosure judgment and remand with instructions to enter summary judgment permitting withholding of the four disputed photographs under Exemption 7(C).
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the request to compel release of those images. Order no wider secrecy decree and no finding that all surrounding investigative records are exempt.
* **Remedial Reach:** The four photographs and the present FOIA disclosure demand; other released or unchallenged records remain outside this judgment.
* **Practical Relief:** The custodian may withhold the four images from this FOIA demand. The judgment does not convert the exemption into an independent prohibition on every lawful disclosure route or reopen material already released.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Real family privacy and a supported public-interest claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The family’s own privacy evidence and the remaining images present a concrete balance. The request’s stated purpose requires evaluating its evidence of agency error without trying the underlying death investigation anew.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these relatives’ privacy may be protected and whether unsupported allegations justify compelled release of these photographs.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the actual privacy-and-transparency balance to living relatives’ death-image interests; reject both an automatic family veto and a universal prior-proof-of-misconduct rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: recognize a recurring protected interest and an evidence-based disclosure balance. A broader requirement to prove wrongdoing before any FOIA inquiry would contradict the limits of United States Department of State v. Ray. A narrower image-by-image aesthetic judgment would fail to identify the family’s own privacy and the actual governmental-transparency inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Exemption 7(C) protects living relatives against an unwarranted invasion of their own privacy through disclosure of a family member’s death-scene images. The agency must establish the law-enforcement and privacy predicates and justify withholding; when the asserted public benefit is exposing official misconduct, the requester must provide a reasonable evidentiary foundation for that inquiry and a plausible connection between the requested material and the governmental issue, without first proving misconduct itself. Speculation alone supplies no counterweight sufficient to compel these four photographs on this record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Law-enforcement death images implicating demonstrated surviving-family privacy; the rule does not create a perpetual proprietary right in every fact about a deceased person or a prerequisite for unrelated transparency requests.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A credible factual inconsistency tied to requested images can justify a different balance; bare suspicion cannot. Other demonstrated transparency purposes must be assessed on their own terms.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States Department of State v. Ray requires demonstrated incremental value, while preserving the possibility that derivative investigation matters. Bibles v. Oregon Natural Desert Association keeps the balance focused on government conduct and retains the agency’s ultimate burden. Those bounded rules can be restated as one inquiry into the actual privacy intrusion and supported incremental transparency, with Exemption 7(C)’s distinct statutory threshold. The family evidence establishes a concrete intrusion; unsupported doubts about the official conclusion do not establish how these images would materially illuminate an agency failure. Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority further prevents permission to withhold from being mislabeled a universal legal ban.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States Department of State v. Ray and Bibles v. Oregon Natural Desert Association applied and extended to the demonstrated family interest; Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority preserved at the permission-versus-prohibition distinction. No presumption that official conduct is infallible is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unrequested records, different supported investigative evidence, and independent disclosure prohibitions are outside this record. Until such a claim is decided, courts apply the statutory exemption and actual evidence-based balance, with the agency retaining its withholding burden.
