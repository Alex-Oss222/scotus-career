## Event

United States v. Dominguez Benitez, No. 03-167; 542 U.S. 74. Merits decision, June 14, 2004. Argued April 21, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 310 F.3d 1221, which set aside the plea because of the omitted personal warning. The question is what substantial-rights showing governs an unpreserved failure to warn that rejection of a nonbinding sentencing recommendation does not permit plea withdrawal. The written agreement contained the warning. The judge omitted it. The expected safety-valve reduction failed because of prior convictions, and the court imposed the ten-year minimum. The omission was first challenged on appeal.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The appellate judgment is reversed and the case remanded for application of the reasonable-probability standard to the defendant's plea choice and the remaining plain-error conditions.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 8–1.

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would vacate and remand under the same substantive review rule.

All nine agree that the court of appeals must apply the reasonable-probability standard on the relevant existing record, together with every other Rule 52(b) condition. The appellate award of plea relief is set aside. Whether that relief is warranted requires the specified application below.

## Holdings

##### I. Prejudice concerns the decision to plead guilty

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant seeking plain-error relief for the unpreserved omission of the required warning that rejection of a nonbinding sentencing recommendation does not permit withdrawal must show a reasonable probability that, with the warning, he would not have pleaded guilty. That probability must undermine confidence in the plea decision, is less than a more-likely-than-not threshold, and concerns the choice to plead rather than probable acquittal at trial; error, plainness and the separate discretionary-correction condition remain independently necessary.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of the Court's opinion, including this rule and the whole-record inquiry below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Vonn's defendant-borne substantial-rights inquiry must address the decision affected by this warning. Strickland and Bagley supply the familiar reasonable-probability measure of outcome uncertainty. Harris applies that measure to an actual counterfactual outcome within Rule 52(b); this case extends that method to the plea decision. A merely possible different choice is insufficient, but the defendant need not prove that a trial would have ended in acquittal.

The judge's personal warning remains mandatory. The contrary argument emphasizes that duty and the loss of the expected sentencing benefit. Those circumstances do not make every omission automatically prejudicial. Vonn applies ordinary forfeiture review, and Rule 11(h) creates no exception for this omission. The defendant must establish error, its plainness and substantial-rights effect; even then Olano requires an independent determination that correction is warranted because the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings.

The reviewing court considers the relevant existing record, including the agreement, actual advisements, demonstrated comprehension, sentencing expectations, plea alternatives and the consequences of trial. A signed agreement is relevant but neither conclusively proves comprehension nor performs the judge's omitted personal duty. Disappointment over the safety-valve reduction does not alone show that the missing withdrawal warning caused acceptance. Representation and evidence bearing on the consequences of trial may matter without becoming presumptions or an innocence requirement.

**Precedent treatment:** Vonn's ordinary forfeiture burden and whole-record rule are applied; Harris's actual-record probability method is extended to plea choice; Olano's independent correction condition is retained; Strickland and Bagley supply the probability formulation without importing their separate substantive claims.

##### II. The correct standard must be applied on remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The court of appeals' incorrect prejudice rule requires reversal and remand for application of the reasonable-probability standard to the whole relevant existing record. The appellate court must decide each distinct Rule 52(b) condition before determining whether plea relief is warranted.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part II. Stone-Zsela agrees with the prescribed inquiry but would vacate rather than reverse.

**Controlling explanation:** The legal rule used below does not govern the effect of this forfeited omission. Correcting that error warrants reversal while leaving the record-specific application to the court of appeals. Vonn requires consideration of the relevant record and Olano preserves the independent correction inquiry. The need for those proceedings does not require the Court to use vacatur rather than reversal. The agreement and failed sentencing expectation must receive their actual evidentiary weight in deciding the plea choice; neither is conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:** Vonn and Olano govern the remand inquiry; Vonn's formal vacatur in its own procedural setting does not require that disposition for every corrected appellate review rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Vonn, 535 U.S. 55: applied; forfeited Rule 11 omissions receive Rule 52(b) review with the defendant's ordinary prejudice burden and relevant whole-record consideration, while the personal judicial duty remains.
- Harris v. United States: extended from its actual-record sentencing inquiry to the counterfactual plea choice; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725: applied for the four distinct conditions and independent discretionary correction.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668, and United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: their reasonable-probability formulation informs the outcome measure without converting the claim into ineffective assistance or suppressed-evidence litigation.
- McCarthy v. United States, 394 U.S. 459: its personal-colloquy duty remains; later Rule 11(h) and Vonn govern the remedy for this forfeited omission.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applied through Vonn for relevant whole-record assessment.

## Law After Decision

For this class of forfeited Rule 11 warning omission, the defendant must establish a reasonable probability of a different plea choice on the relevant existing record. The reviewing court must separately apply all remaining Rule 52(b) conditions, including discretionary correction. Written advice can bear on effect while the judge's personal-warning duty remains mandatory. The lower court's actual assessment is ordinary application of the settled rule.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief joins Part I's reasonable-probability standard and relevant-record inquiry. He would vacate the appellate judgment and remand for that inquiry and all four plain-error conditions. The written nonwithdrawal term, expected reduction and known mandatory exposure bear on the actual choice but require evaluation rather than conclusiveness. The court of appeals must decide substantial-rights effect and the independent basis for discretionary correction before permitting withdrawal. Any authorized withdrawal returns the charges to the posture supplied by ordinary criminal procedure.

## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must apply the reasonable-probability inquiry on the whole relevant existing record, then separately resolve the remaining plain-error conditions and the resulting appellate relief. The prior award of plea relief cannot stand on the rejected prejudice rule.
