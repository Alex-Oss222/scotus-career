## Proposed rule, application and remedy

P1. Partnership recognition under §§761 and 7701(a)(2) requires the genuine intent to join in conducting an enterprise described in Culbertson and Tower, assessed from the parties' agreement, actual conduct, contributions, control and sharing of economic benefits and burdens in the whole supported record. Formal membership and actual investment trades are relevant but not conclusive. Gregory permits examination of whether the statutory arrangement actually exists; tax motive cannot establish statutory qualification or, by itself, defeat an arrangement that does qualify.

P2. The inquiry does not impose an additional requirement that a taxpayer prove the partnership was necessary to make investments it could have made directly. Nor may an appellate court replace the totality inquiry with a categorical rule denying recognition whenever specially created partners and elaborate tax planning are present without a separate nontax necessity. The absence of any genuine shared business object is probative and may be dispositive on supported findings; the absence of a need to use the partnership form is a different proposition. Gitlitz enforces statutory qualifications and consequences and does not confer immunity on a sham entity. Its statutory-account discipline also precludes a free-standing policy veto based on the size of the tax benefit.

P3. The D.C. Circuit expressly sustained the trial court's factual findings and rejected using other litigation's contrary findings as clear-error proof. It nevertheless required a finding of nontax necessity and treated failure to make it as determinative. That legal requirement cannot sustain the judgment. The district court's finding that the participants actually intended to share income, expenses, gains and losses must receive its appropriate factual weight under the correct inquiry; it is not insulated from review merely because phrased in partnership terms. The created foreign entities, their financing and planned withdrawal, premium payments, engineered allocation sequence, costs and actual investment exposure must all be evaluated for the genuine joint-enterprise question.

P4. Vacate the D.C. Circuit judgment and remand for application of Culbertson's actual inquiry to the supported record and disposition of the remaining properly preserved adjustment grounds. The court must preserve Rule 52 deference to factual findings, independently review the governing legal standard, and distinguish the entity theory allocating recognized transaction gains and losses to AHP from the transaction-sham theory eliminating those gains and losses. If recognition fails, adjustments must follow that legally supported ground; if recognition survives, any independently preserved transaction theory must be resolved before the final tax consequences are fixed. This remand does not promise recognition or a tax amount. It corrects the ground actually used below and leaves factual or alternative-ground work within the proper appellate/district allocation.

## Adversarial alternatives

Affirmance's strongest account is that the specially formed entities, costly arranged sequence and foreign gain/domestic loss design demonstrate that the participants never joined a genuine business at all; real trades cannot rescue a shell. The objection to vacatur is that the lower court's necessity language merely expressed this substantive conclusion. That objection is rejected because the reviewed opinion repeatedly makes necessity indispensable while accepting the trial findings; the operative test would reject even a genuine joint enterprise because direct purchase was cheaper. The decisive distinction is statutory substance versus mandatory necessity, not approval of the transactions.

Immediate restoration of the taxpayer's favorable district judgment has the competing strength of the sustained findings about actual sharing and real investments. Its defect is that those facts alone do not dispose of all Culbertson considerations or the government's separate preserved adjustment theory. Remand supplies a complete rule and the required remaining work instead of appellate replacement findings. Preservation does not bar consideration of the government's nontax-purpose argument: its trial memorandum raised it.
