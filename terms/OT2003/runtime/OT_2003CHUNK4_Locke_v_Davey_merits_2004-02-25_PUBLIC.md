## Event

Locke v. Davey, No. 02-1315; merits. Argued December 2, 2003; decided February 25, 2004. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which reversed summary judgment for Washington. The question is whether excluding pursuit of a devotional theology degree from the Promise Scholarship violates the Free Exercise Clause. Davey attended an eligible religious college and pursued pastoral ministries and business; the devotional character of the pastoral degree is undisputed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed within the granted Free Exercise claim.

## Holdings

### A State may decline support for the devotional ministry degree presented here

**Controlling proposition:** The Free Exercise Clause permits a State to exclude pursuit of a devotional degree preparing the student for ministry from this individual educational scholarship while allowing religious students, religious institutions, individual theology courses and secular courses of study to remain eligible. The permission concerns the devotional-degree condition as applied to Davey's undisputed pastoral training; it does not authorize disqualification merely because of religious belief, institutional affiliation or an isolated theology course.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct joins to the rule, application and remedy. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supports reversal concerning devotional formation on a distinct ground and does not join this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Washington may make this confined choice against supporting preparation for ministry even though the Establishment Clause does not require exclusion of the student benefit. Walz recognizes room between what the Religion Clauses require and prohibit. Witters preserves individual vocational assistance as a permissible relationship; permission to include religious training does not establish an obligation to support every devotional degree.

The actual award is student assistance. Funds pass through the institution for distribution to the student and may meet tuition, room, board and other educational expenses. It is not the restricted provider purchase adjudicated in Zelman or the ordinary integrated tuition in Strout. Those decisions establish the importance of actual function but do not eliminate the separate Free Exercise question here. Washington permits religious-school attendance, religious students and theology courses. It also allows a scholarship for a secular degree at another institution while devotional study occurs elsewhere. The exclusion targets the course of devotional preparation for ministry rather than a religious identity.

The contrary equality argument correctly insists that K.D.M. and Columbia Union protect eligible secular services and work from exclusion based on religion. But this benefit is conditioned on the course of study, and the undisputed pastoral-ministry major falls within the confined condition. Davey's business double major does not convert the scholarship into the restricted secular-department grant adjudicated in Columbia Union. The State need not establish that every dollar purchases prohibited devotion before declining this particular ministry support. The Ninth Circuit's contrary Free Exercise judgment is reversed, and the State's favorable judgment on the granted claim is restored.

**Operative qualification:** The parties agree that the statute's theology-degree term covers degrees devotional in nature or designed to induce religious faith. That agreed application supplies this decision's scope.

## Precedent Treatment

Walz v. Tax Commission is applied for the distinction between constitutional prohibition and permissible separation. Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind remains effective for the individual-benefit relationship; its permission does not compel support for the devotional degree here. Strout v. Albanese retains its refusal rule for proved integrated tuition, and Zelman v. Simmons-Harris retains its rule for restricted purchases; their payment relationships differ from this scholarship. Agostini v. Felton retains the actual-function inquiry rather than an identity presumption. K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District and Columbia Union College v. Oliver retain equal eligibility for independently identifiable secular services and work. Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah retains its religious-targeting rule; the Court distinguishes this confined ministry-support choice from the disability there.

## Law After Decision

States may retain the confined devotional-ministry-degree condition described here within an otherwise available individual education scholarship. Existing protection for religious students, eligible religious institutions, individual theology courses and separately eligible secular work continues. The permissive Free Exercise holding does not rest on a constitutional command to exclude these payments.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal insofar as the judgment compels payment for the established pastoral-formation function, and dissents from ending any preserved genuinely separated secular claim. He applies Strout's actual-function refusal rule and Columbia Union's requirement that proof concern the work the public funds purchase. The claimant must identify the denied benefit and unequal treatment; the State's funded-function justification must match the actual course and payment design. Religious governance, faith and speculative freeing of resources do not establish a devotional purchase. The scholarship's actual terms must determine whether independently eligible secular study exists; the business major alone proves neither separation nor inseparability. He would remand that preserved question for equal consideration, while rejecting unconditional payment and damages on the devotional-subsidy theory.

Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. The actual award is independently directed educational assistance, including living costs, within Witters's surviving relationship. Under Lukumi and the equality principles of K.D.M. and Columbia Union, excluding an otherwise eligible student for devotional study requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring. A historical preference against ministry support does not supply the necessary justification for withholding this student benefit. Allowing a business degree only at another institution does not answer the discrimination at the eligible college. The dissent would require equal consideration under the remaining lawful academic, income and enrollment conditions and separate adjudication of any properly presented monetary claim.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the ordinary mandate for effectuation of reversal and restoration of Washington’s favorable judgment within the granted Free Exercise claim.
