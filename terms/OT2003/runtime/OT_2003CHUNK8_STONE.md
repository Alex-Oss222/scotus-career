## 104. Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of categorical statutory barriers and remand for a reasoned, application-specific exercise of discretion.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit only properly supported, proportionate assistance consistent with privileges and the actual foreign proceeding. Require consideration of the Commission's position and reject any assumption that eligibility entitles AMD to every requested document.
* **Remedial Reach:** AMD's application for identified evidence in this proceeding. No general entitlement to worldwide competition discovery or access by all foreign complainants follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court should identify requested materials, relevance to the contemplated proceeding, applicable privilege and confidentiality protection, foreign receptivity, circumvention concerns and actual burden. It may narrow, condition or deny production with reasons; no immediate production order is entered here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Foreign adjudicative assistance and discretion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Statutory eligibility is distinct from discretion to grant discovery. AMD's role, a proceeding within reasonable contemplation, the adjudicative body's function, privilege and burden all matter. The proceeding is not an identified private arbitration, so no universal ruling on all arbitral panels is necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1782 authorizes discovery for a complainant participating in European Commission competition proceedings before adjudication is pending, without a foreign-discoverability prerequisite, and how discretion must be exercised.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose a new construction of the open §1782 questions, grounded in text, amendment history and the body's actual function.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: resolve the actual eligibility obstacles and administer the express discretion. A broader rule for every foreign investigation or private arbitration lacks this record. A narrower pending-proceeding condition would restore wording Congress deleted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1782 permits, but does not require, assistance to an interested person for use in a reasonably contemplated proceeding before a foreign governmental body exercising adjudicative authority, without a categorical foreign-discoverability requirement. The applicant must establish the statutory predicates; the court then considers the foreign proceeding's nature and receptivity, access to evidence there, evasion of lawful restrictions, privilege and undue burden before ordering tailored assistance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** An interested complainant seeking evidence from a person found in the district for reasonably contemplated adjudication by a foreign governmental body acting as first-instance decisionmaker.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those categories are decided, courts require the statute's actual tribunal, proceeding and interested-person predicates rather than treating this governmental competition proceeding as a universal analogy.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress deliberately removed the pending-court formulation. AMD's participation and evidentiary opportunity give it a practical legal interest, while the Commission's adjudicative function supplies the relevant tribunal characteristic. The directorate's refusal to request evidence may reflect its procedure or priorities; it must be considered without being converted into a statutory veto. The word “may” preserves a meaningful decision about this production.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No contrary §1782 holding is displaced; this is a new statutory construction at the presented scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Private commercial arbitration and speculative future litigation are outside this record.

## 105. Duckworth v. State Administration Board of Election Laws

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for the §2284 tribunal. Vieth v. Jubelirer recognizes the preserved partisan equal-protection claim, so it is not wholly insubstantial. Ordinary failure to state a claim is not constitutional insubstantiality.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the proper statutory tribunal to adjudicate the preserved substantial claim. Do not redraw districts, reinstate abandoned theories or presume a merits violation.
* **Remedial Reach:** Duckworth's preserved challenge to the 2002 map. The judgment confers a lawful adjudicative forum and no policy-wide substantive relief.
* **Practical Relief:** The district judge must request the three-judge court. That court then permits conforming amendment and resolves pleading, proof and remedy under Vieth's rule; the State continues lawful election administration unless a separately justified order requires change.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory tribunal and insubstantiality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved appeal is the Fourteenth Amendment claim. Statutory tribunal assignment precedes an ordinary merits sufficiency decision unless the claim is wholly insubstantial or jurisdiction is absent. Vieth's holding that the partisan equal-protection claim is justiciable, with its defined elements, confirms that the preserved claim is not wholly insubstantial.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a single district judge may dismiss a constitutional congressional-apportionment claim for failure to state a claim without convening the three-judge court required by §2284, and whether alleged district shapes adequately support the preserved partisan claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering rules and resolve the identified open application; no undisclosed change is proposed.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: state the allocation rule for covered apportionment claims. Broader protection for frivolous cases would waste the statutory tribunal. Narrower correction confined to this map would leave the erroneous equivalence intact.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A single district judge may not bypass §2284 by equating ordinary failure to state a constitutional apportionment claim with jurisdictional insubstantiality. Unless the claim is wholly frivolous, clearly foreclosed or outside federal jurisdiction, the required three-judge court decides its merits. The claimant still bears the ordinary burden of establishing the substantive constitutional injury; the tribunal rule creates none.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Substantial constitutional challenges to congressional apportionment; jurisdictionally defective, wholly frivolous or clearly foreclosed claims remain outside compulsory three-judge merits adjudication.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Vieth v. Jubelirer governs the preserved Fourteenth Amendment claim. Because Vieth recognizes that claim, the required three-judge court decides its merits and any permissible amendment under ordinary procedure, applying Vieth's elements, lawful comparison and same-decision defense. The tribunal ruling does not revive abandoned Article I or independent First Amendment claims.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Goosby v. Osser distinguishes a wholly insubstantial constitutional claim from one that can fail on ordinary merits review. The district court treated an earlier map's evidentiary outcome as a pleading bar, although the map changed, and the appellate court used Rule 12(b)(6) as the substantiality threshold itself. That removes the statutory distinction. Under Vieth, irregular boundaries alone establish no material impairment of an identified political capacity, but an allegation that a county was divided among four districts to impair Republican participation asserts the claim Vieth recognizes rather than one plainly foreclosed. The proper panel must apply Vieth's rule and ordinary amendment procedure; the remand establishes neither liability nor a right to redraw the map.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Goosby v. Osser's substantiality distinction and Vieth v. Jubelirer's partisan equal-treatment rule; preserve Shaw v. Reno's actual injury requirements. No further substantive partisan-districting test is proposed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The substantive merits belong to the statutorily required tribunal after the proper substantiality determination; claims abandoned on appeal remain outside this remand.

## 106. Tennard v. Dretke

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the certificate denial and remand for an issue-specific appeal under the correct relevance rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require consideration of the preserved mitigation claim without the permanent-handicap or crime-nexus gates. Apply the statutory and State-borne effect rules if their predicates are established; grant no immediate writ merely because a certificate should issue.
* **Remedial Reach:** Tennard's penalty-phase claim. The conviction remains intact, and no categorical disability finding or class-wide sentence relief follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The court of appeals must issue the appropriate certificate and adjudicate the full claim. If an established preserved original-charge mitigation defect is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt under the applicable rule, require a lawful sentencing correction within a reasonable court-fixed period or a noncapital sentence.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Mitigation relevance and appellate access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This is certificate review, not a completed federal merits appeal. Section 2254 eligibility, actual mitigation error and effect must remain distinct. The previous historical remand is procedural history, not a adoption of an outcome. Atkins does not automatically convert a low score into a categorical exemption.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a capital habeas petitioner must prove a uniquely severe permanent impairment and a causal nexus to the offense before low-intellectual-functioning evidence receives constitutional mitigating significance, and whether a certificate of appealability should issue.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering rules and resolve the identified open application; no undisclosed change is proposed.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: apply the rule to the challenged relevance screen and certificate. Broader automatic sentence relief would erase remaining merits and effect requirements. Narrower insistence on a diagnosis would recreate the forbidden threshold.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A capital defendant's supported culpability mitigation cannot be excluded from constitutional consideration merely because it lacks a uniquely severe permanent impairment or a demonstrated causal nexus to the crime. A certificate issues when reasonable jurists could debate whether the sentencing mechanism denied meaningful effect to that evidence. The applicant must make that substantial showing; ultimate statutory eligibility, violation and effect remain separate. Miller-El requires that the debate concern statutory eligibility as well as constitutional error; it does not require a conclusive showing of either before the appeal. This certificate leaves the ultimate merits and relief for their proper adjudication.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Supported intellectual-functioning and other culpability mitigation beyond the literal special issues; no mandatory weight, diagnosis or automatic death exclusion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those predicates are resolved, courts apply Penry v. Johnson's supplied mitigation rule and Brecht's general State-borne effect rule as Penry applied it, not the historical Brecht standard.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Graham v. Collins expressly rejects the lower court's restrictive gate and protects mitigation that bears on culpability independently of future danger. The low score provides a supported basis for that argument even without a complete disability diagnosis. Penry v. Johnson requires a lawful vehicle for the jury's judgment, while Atkins v. Virginia supplies a different categorical inquiry that the score alone does not resolve.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Graham, Penry, Slack and Miller-El at their separate mitigation, certification and relief scopes; distinguish Atkins's categorical disability inquiry.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Complete-charge merits, any preserved procedural bar and actual effect require appellate adjudication on the full record.

## 107. Blakely v. Washington

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacate the 90-month enhanced sentence and remand for lawful resentencing while preserving the guilty plea and conviction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** On the existing admission, sentence within the lawful standard range. Any further aggravated proceeding requires independent state-law authorization and compliance with notice, plea, waiver and double-jeopardy limits; the remand does not create that authority.
* **Remedial Reach:** Blakely's sentence. The controlling rule applies to covered binding ranges, but this judgment does not vacate sentences of persons absent from the case or decide collateral reopening.
* **Practical Relief:** The sentencing court must identify the range authorized by the actual admission and impose a sentence on a lawful basis. It cannot reimpose 90 months merely by repeating deliberate-cruelty findings. Any requested new adjudication or waiver must be valid and separately justified; no immediate release follows automatically.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Binding range and aggravating elements

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The presented claim is on direct review. A constitutional range rule does not itself authorize a new aggravated prosecution, reopen the plea, or decide Washington severability. Harmlessness cannot be presumed from a judge's finding or evidence strength.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a judge may exceed Washington's binding standard sentencing range by finding deliberate cruelty that the defendant did not admit and a jury did not find.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing law. Three or more supplied range rules govern; restate Apprendi v. New Jersey, United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States as one authorization test without enlarging their reserved questions.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad in coverage but an application of settled law: the operative range rule covers all materially equivalent binding schemes. Broader resolution of recidivism, state charging or every severability issue would exceed the question. Narrower treatment limited to deliberate cruelty would obscure the existing rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A judge may not exceed a binding sentencing range on a present-offense aggravating fact that was neither validly admitted as an element nor proved to the proper guilt factfinder beyond a reasonable doubt. The binding range supported by the plea or verdict supplies the constitutional boundary even when a broader statutory classification has a higher ceiling. Washington's contested deliberate-cruelty finding cannot authorize this 90-month sentence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Binding sentencing ceilings and floors changed by present-offense facts, including this exceptional-range cruelty finding. Advisory considerations within lawful authority remain distinct.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those reserved matters are decided, courts apply the existing source-specific rules and do not infer a recidivism exception or collateral reopening from this judgment.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Angle already rejects the outer-statutory-maximum distinction for binding ranges. Apprendi v. New Jersey establishes that awareness of exposure does not itself admit the additional fact. Harris v. United States confirms that sentencing labels cannot remove element protection, and Ring v. Arizona extends that principle to a different penalty setting. Those bounded applications can be restated as one range-authorization rule without overruling any of them.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply and restate together Apprendi v. New Jersey, United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States; distinguish Ring v. Arizona's additional capital-remedy question. No new overruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Recidivism, state grand-jury requirements, unrelated guideline provisions and collateral retroactivity are outside this question.

## 108. Schriro v. Summerlin

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand. Sustain Ring's retroactive availability under the watershed exception, reject its classification as a substantive rule, and require adjudication of the actual eligibility error and remedial prerequisites before an operative sentencing writ. If five votes do not support a judgment sustaining Ring's retroactive availability, reverse the Ring-based grant and preserve genuinely remaining independent claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the confined Ring claim to proceed under the former-law habeas regime applicable to this petition. Retroactivity does not itself vacate the death sentence. Require an actual-record inquiry for any established eligibility omission, preserve the unreviewed prior-conviction issue, and leave application of the governing collateral prejudice standard for lawful determination below. Order no execution, automatic new aggravator trial or automatic rejection of independent claims.
* **Remedial Reach:** Summerlin's collateral claim concerning an indispensable present-offense death-eligibility finding. The murder conviction, ultimate discretionary sentence selection and independently reviewable sentencing claims remain distinct.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must determine whether a necessary eligibility finding was withheld from the jury, subject to any properly available independent eligibility ground and the limits of the certiorari grant. For an established Ring omission, apply the collateral error standard supplied by Brecht's general rule to the actual instructions, findings and record. Retroactivity alone supplies neither a presumption of harm nor a harmlessness finding. If all requirements for relief are met, enter conditional sentence relief under governing law; further sentencing needs independent state-law authority and compliance with constitutional limits. Under the fallback, remove Ring retroactivity as the basis for relief and decide preserved independent grounds.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retroactivity of capital factfinder allocation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Ring was not dictated when Summerlin's judgment became final. Classify the new rule before applying the preserved watershed exception, and keep retroactivity separate from violation and relief. This pre-AEDPA petition does not receive §2254(d)'s amended merits restrictions merely because its appeal occurred later.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the jury determination of a present-offense aggravating fact indispensable to death eligibility qualifies for watershed application on collateral review.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the accuracy-and-bedrock reasoning of O'Dell and Tyler to the indispensable jury determination at the boundary between imprisonment and death. This is a new application of their principles, not a conclusion already compelled by those cases. If five votes do not support a judgment sustaining Ring's retroactive availability, retain Ring's procedural classification and deny retroactivity without narrowing either existing watershed holding.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confine the extension to a fact necessary to authorize death in a jury-tried prosecution, subject to valid admission and lawful waiver rules. The severity of death reinforces the required accuracy analysis but does not replace either Teague requirement. The position does not make all important jury procedures retroactive or require juries to perform the ultimate discretionary sentencing choice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ring announces a new procedural rule because it assigns an otherwise required aggravating-fact determination to the jury without changing the substantive aggravators or the class legally eligible for punishment. Nevertheless, its protection for an indispensable present-offense death-eligibility finding satisfies both parts of the watershed exception.

  **Fundamental accuracy.** Authorizing death requires a reliable determination of the fact that crosses the statutory eligibility boundary. Where that decisive finding is withheld from the jury, independent collective scrutiny of the prosecution's proof is absent at the very point where an irreversible penalty becomes lawful. The deficiency is especially acute for an evaluative aggravator such as exceptional cruelty: the reasonable-doubt standard constrains proof, but does not itself supply the constitutionally required collective assessment. The risk is impermissibly large in this confined capital category. This conclusion does not assume that every judge is unreliable, that juries are infallible or that Ring introduced a new burden of proof.

  **Bedrock procedure.** The jury's determination of a fact necessary to authorize punishment belongs to the basic structure of a fair criminal adjudication. Ring makes that protection effective for the factual element separating imprisonment from death. Restoring it at that boundary alters the understanding of an indispensable adjudicative safeguard, rather than merely improving a discretionary sentencing procedure.

  **Distinct remedial inquiry.** Retroactive availability does not establish that a necessary fact was omitted in this case or that the omission affected the sentence. Ring requires attention to the actual instructions, necessary findings and reliably connected record inferences rather than new credibility findings or a hypothetical replacement verdict. Its direct-review burden does not itself decide the separate collateral-error question. Brecht's general rule supplies that standard for a properly reached claim unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural; under it, the State must show beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that an established omission did not contribute to the death sentence. This retroactivity holding does not decide the ultimate harm result.

  **Fallback.** If five votes do not support a judgment sustaining Ring's retroactive availability, hold Ring new, procedural and nonretroactive. Its importance alone cannot satisfy the exception. O'Dell's parole-information holding and Tyler's verdict-wide reasonable-doubt holding remain fully effective.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Present-offense factfinding necessary for death eligibility in judgments already final. No categorical substantive restriction on punishment, general jury-sentencing right or decision about every prior-conviction fact follows.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the indispensable eligibility fact, the actual jury findings, any valid admission or waiver and every genuinely applicable prerequisite to relief. Do not infer entitlement to resentencing from retroactivity alone. A previous conviction that may independently authorize death must receive its own lawful analysis; this case does not answer the question excluded from review.
* **Transition:** Apply the confined watershed rule to qualifying collateral claims, including this pending petition, under the legal regime otherwise governing them. Preserve ordinary finality rules for claims outside the defined category and independent statutory restrictions where actually applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** O'Dell treats the ability to answer a materially misleading capital-sentencing premise as fundamental to reliable adjudication; Tyler recognizes the exceptional significance of an adjudication lacking the constitutionally necessary reasonable-doubt verdict. Neither is identical to Ring. Together they support examining the operation of the missing safeguard at the decisive factual boundary, rather than assuming that a procedural label ends the inquiry. Arizona already required aggravators to be proved beyond reasonable doubt; the defect concerns the constitutionally required factfinder, not a lowered burden. Independent collective evaluation has particular importance when the aggravator asks whether the offense was exceptionally cruel. DeStefano's refusal to reopen ordinary noncapital bench trials is substantial contrary authority on the claimed accuracy benefit, but does not resolve this confined capital question under Teague's later two-part exception. Death's finality alone is insufficient; the decisive features are a withheld eligibility element and the jury's independent assessment of that element. An inquiry directed to the actual adjudication permits the courts to assess any established omission under the proper collateral standard without inventing a missing jury finding. The judge also found a prior violent felony; whether that fact independently established eligibility was excluded from certiorari and cannot be assumed away or decided here.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Caspari and Horn to reach the preserved retroactivity defense. Extend, rather than merely restate, O'Dell and Tyler to Ring's expressly reserved collateral question. Distinguish DeStefano at the capital eligibility boundary without declaring all bench-trial judgments unreliable. Preserve Ring's distinction between eligibility and selection, Sullivan's separate verdict-wide structural rule and the requirement to examine the actual adjudication. Brecht's general rule, which O'Neal applied, supplies the separate collateral standard; whether an established omission was harmless is not resolved by the retroactivity classification. Neither the principal holding nor the fallback abolishes or contracts the watershed exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The independently sufficient effect of the prior-conviction aggravator is outside the certiorari grant. Bias, counsel, factual innocence, other preserved claims and any ultimate resentencing authority require their own lawful adjudication. The prior-conviction exception is not decided.

## 109. Cheney v. United States District Court for D.C.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE AND REMAND. Vacate the Court of Appeals' judgment and remand for reconsideration under the full mandamus standard, with binding limits on further discovery. A formal invocation of executive privilege is not a prerequisite to judicial consideration of unnecessary intrusion into presidential decisionmaking. The Vice President has no categorical immunity from the focused judicial inquiry authorized below. If the Court adopts Stone's reasoning, Stone writes the opinion.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Court of Appeals must evaluate the scope, necessity and alternatives to the challenged orders and give effect to a staged District Court inquiry into statutory coverage. The existing broad demands may not simply proceed unchanged on the premise that every constitutional concern must await a document-by-document privilege confrontation. Any further discovery must first address whether nongovernmental participants actually functioned as members of the National Energy Policy Development Group. No wholesale disclosure, final finding of FACA coverage or dismissal of the action is ordered. Issuance of appellate mandamus remains subject to its independent requirements.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged discovery orders, the plaintiffs' preserved statutory claims and the supported institutional interests of the officials subject to those orders. The judgment supplies neither a general exemption for presidential advisers nor an entitlement to every document requested. Any eventual statutory relief must identify the responsible defendant, established duty, authorized enforcement route and materials or practices within the proved violation.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the Court of Appeals must ensure that the District Court states the statutory and procedural basis for the proposed inquiry, identifies the concrete membership dispute, and replaces unrestricted demands with defined requests and protective procedures before sensitive material is compelled. The District Court must begin with membership, attendance, assigned responsibilities and actual participation in collective advisory work, ordinarily using official records, sworn declarations and other nondeliberative evidence. It must explain why any additional sensitive material is necessary and why an adequate less intrusive source is unavailable, then resolve properly asserted privileges and specific objections before compelled disclosure. Protective orders, redaction or justified in-camera review may be used when they address the actual concern. Failure to establish coverage or an available enforcement route results in the appropriate statutory disposition; a supported claim proceeds within those limits. Neither party receives final merits relief merely from this remand.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Mandamus and concrete executive burdens

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Appellate mandamus under 28 U.S.C. §1651 requires no adequate alternative remedy, a clear and indisputable entitlement to relief, and appropriateness in the issuing court's discretion. An appeal deadline does not mechanically govern the petition, although delay, prejudice and available narrower remedies remain relevant. The Court reaches the discovery and functional-membership rules needed to supervise this coverage inquiry; it does not assume disputed membership or adjudicate unasserted privileges. The plaintiffs' district-level §1361 enforcement theory and any APA route are separate from appellate mandamus and must satisfy their own requirements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a court must consider concrete separation-of-powers burdens before requiring a formal privilege confrontation, and how a court may investigate disputed private membership in a presidential advisory body without granting executive immunity or permitting unrestricted discovery into confidential deliberations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established extraordinary-writ requirements and extend the concrete-accommodation principle to this official advisory setting. Announce a staged coverage inquiry and an express functional-membership standard: sustained integration into the body's collective advisory work can constitute membership without a formal vote or veto, while consultation and influence alone cannot. These statutory and procedural applications require an affirmative holding; they are not already resolved by Clinton v. Jones or United States v. Nixon.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule governs the actual recurring conflict between statutory coverage and protected presidential advice. Categorical immunity would surrender independent adjudication of federal duties, while a privilege-only response would leave avoidable disclosure burdens unexamined. The record supports defined membership inquiry and mandatory discovery safeguards, not a final conclusion about coverage or the disclosure of particular communications.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Executive confidentiality requires concrete protection against unnecessary intrusion, but does not give executive officials exclusive authority to decide whether their advisory activities are governed by federal law. Courts may authorize focused fact-finding necessary to resolve a genuine statutory-coverage dispute after identifying an available judicial enforcement route, limiting the inquiry to material coverage facts and protecting confidential deliberations.

  **Functional membership.** Under FACA's definition and its exclusion for bodies composed wholly of federal officers or employees, a private participant may be a de facto member when the person's actual role shows sustained integration into the organized body's internal, collective deliberative and advisory functions. A de facto member is a person functioning as a member despite a different formal title. Relevant facts include regular participation in internal meetings, continuing assigned responsibilities, involvement in developing the body's collective advice, and performance of work materially indistinguishable from that of recognized members. No single fact automatically decides the question. Formal voting, veto or supervisory authority is not indispensable. Attendance alone, episodic consultation, lobbying, advocacy, submission of outside recommendations or substantial influence without integration into the collective process is insufficient. Courts must examine an actual advisory body and the participant's functions rather than converting every outside adviser into a committee member.

  **Stage 1: statutory basis and concrete coverage dispute.** Identify the relevant FACA provision, defendants, alleged private participants and factual allegations that would establish their integration if proved. Separately determine personal injury and an available enforcement channel. Ordinary pleading rules govern; a generalized allegation of private influence does not itself authorize a search through presidential deliberations. Neither an executive label nor a plaintiff's allegation conclusively establishes membership.

  **Stage 2: focused operational facts.** Confine the initial inquiry to membership, attendance, assigned responsibilities and actual participation in the committee's collective work. Prefer official rosters, calendars or comparable records, sworn declarations and other adequate nondeliberative evidence before demanding the substance of presidential advice, recommendations or internal policy communications. The purpose is to determine what role participants performed, not to obtain the policy debate before coverage is established. A title or conclusory declaration does not preclude appropriate testing of a genuinely disputed material fact.

  **Stage 3: necessity and protection.** Before enlarging the inquiry, identify the remaining material factual dispute, explain why the requested information is necessary and consider an adequate less intrusive alternative. Membership and operational facts are analytically different from confidential advice, but no category is automatically unprivileged. Narrow requests, segregation, redaction and protective orders must respond to the actual sensitivity and burden. In-camera review means inspection by the judge outside public disclosure; it is available when justified and is not an automatic substitute for deciding relevance, necessity or privilege.

  **Stage 4: particular objections and decision.** Resolve properly asserted privileges and concrete objections before ordering disclosure of protected material. The party asserting protection must support the applicable privilege or burden with the particularity reasonably possible without defeating the protection itself. A formal claim of privilege may be necessary to decide the privilege, but is not an invariant prerequisite to correcting an unnecessarily broad discovery order. Plaintiffs must establish coverage and the prerequisites for final statutory relief; factual membership remains to be proved rather than presumed from access, influence or the Government's resistance to disclosure.

  **Appellate supervision.** Confidential advice cannot be made confidential again after disclosure. That irreversibility can make ordinary later review inadequate and warrants consideration of protective relief before a final privilege ruling. It does not establish every mandamus element automatically. The Court of Appeals must assess the actual alternatives, clear entitlement and appropriateness of intervention, including whether narrower discovery and District Court supervision adequately protect the asserted interest. The response must address the intrusion rather than extinguish a potentially valid statutory action by categorical immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Presidential advisory bodies alleged to include private functional members and civil discovery directed to the officials and records needed to decide that issue. The holding neither exposes ordinary consultation to automatic FACA coverage nor immunizes integrated private membership through nominal titles. It distinguishes membership and operational facts from the content of presidential deliberations, while preserving particular privileges and the limits of the asserted judicial enforcement route.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the same functional inquiry whether the private participant is an industry representative, outside expert, advocate or informal adviser: sustained participation in the body's collective work, rather than a favored or disfavored viewpoint, controls. A federal-only body receiving outside submissions remains within the statutory exclusion absent actual nongovernmental membership. A private participant performing continuing internal member functions is not excluded merely for lacking a formal vote. Until coverage and particular privilege disputes are resolved, courts apply the staged inquiry and the governing qualified-privilege rules. For final enforcement under §1361, courts require a legally enforceable, sufficiently clear nondiscretionary duty and the other prerequisites; any APA theory requires an eligible agency defendant and its separate statutory conditions. Appellate §1651 review does not supply those missing predicates.
* **Transition:** Apply the stated discovery sequence and functional-membership standard to this live action on remand. No completed judgment is reopened and no later decision supplies an earlier governing rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government has a substantial constitutional interest in protecting candid presidential advice. That interest does not permit executive officials to settle the legal reach of FACA by calling integrated participants consultants. Conversely, private influence is a routine feature of policy advice; treating influence itself as membership would make ordinary consultation the basis for extensive civil discovery. Functional integration and staged inquiry preserve an adjudicable statutory question without exposing deliberations unnecessarily.

  Clinton v. Jones rejects a temporary civil exemption for unofficial pre-presidential conduct and requires attention to concrete interference with official responsibilities. Its accommodation principle supports the method here, while its different factual setting does not itself decide official advisory discovery. United States v. Nixon rejects absolute insulation of presidential communications but involved a demonstrated need for identified criminal evidence; it does not authorize sweeping civil demands into policy formation. Nixon v. Fitzgerald protects the President from personal civil damages for official conduct, a different question from focused judicial scrutiny of statutory compliance.

  Public Citizen v. Department of Justice supports a construction of FACA attentive to ordinary executive consultation and serious separation-of-powers concerns. Association of American Physicians & Surgeons v. Clinton examines actual, regular participation functionally indistinguishable from committee membership and distinguishes episodic advice. That functional approach supports the express rejection of a mandatory voting-or-veto requirement while leaving mere influence outside the rule.

  Akins v. FEC recognizes concrete injury from continuing denial of particular information allegedly protected by statute; it does not establish standing from a generalized wish for compliance or entitle plaintiffs to every requested item before coverage is decided. Chamber of Commerce v. Reich permits qualified prospective review of responsible implementing officers acting outside statutory authority; it does not itself create a private action for every FACA error, settle presidential APA status or remove statutory remedial limits. Kerr v. United States District Court reinforces mandamus's exceptional character and the relevance of particular objections and adequate protective alternatives.

  Here the allegations concern private participation in the National Energy Policy Development Group, but actual membership and coverage remain disputed. The broad discovery reaches beyond the immediate membership question and cannot be justified simply by postponing protection until all requested documents have been identified for a privilege contest. Initial records and declarations concerning who attended, what responsibilities were assigned and how collective work occurred may resolve or define the dispute. Any necessary further inquiry must follow the stated safeguards.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal court may not deem a state merits decision objectively unreasonable by relying on material not properly before that court without first resolving the statutory and procedural basis for considering the additional evidence. Applicant-attributable deficient diligence triggers §2254(e)(2) under its actual conditions; an incomplete record alone does not. A state court acts contrary to Strickland if it actually requires a more-likely-than-not different outcome, but isolated shorthand or a factual proof burden does not establish that substitution when the complete reasoning applies reasonable probability.
* **Reach Within the Question:** A habeas grant resting on an undeveloped witness statement and an alleged substitution of a preponderance prejudice standard. The two Williams decisions retain their separate subjects and conditions.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Sixth Circuit did not resolve the state rejection of the late Gooch proffer or the necessary diligence predicates. Those omissions matter independently of whether her account could impeach Hughes. The state opinion expressly recited reasonable probability, and its factual-burden language can be read consistently with that standard. Neither an automatic assumption of error nor immunity based on a correct opening citation is justified; the actual analysis controls. Lockyer confirms that independent disagreement cannot replace objective statutory unreasonableness. * **Precedent Treatment:** Apply both Williams holdings at their distinct scopes, Strickland's reasonable-probability standard, Visciotti's whole-opinion and whole-record inquiry, and Lockyer's separate statutory and constitutional requirements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate diligence finding, any properly excused default, the lawful scope of added evidence and final prejudice determination remain for adjudication under their separate predicates.

## 119. Roeder v. Islamic Republic of Iran

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to controlling decisions actually effective before this case's June 30, 2004 event.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM dismissal. The case-specific immunity amendments do not abrogate the Algiers Accords' separate bar or require preservation of the liability default. Apply Plaut at its actual scope: this action remained pending when Congress acted, so the amendments operate as a change in the law governing pending litigation that courts apply independently, and no legislative command to reopen a completed final judgment is presented. If the Court adopts Stone's reasoning, Stone writes the opinion.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the dismissal and vacatur of the liability default in place. No reinstatement, damages award, compelled congressional payment or further damages proceeding follows. The statutory bar resolves this action without a remand to determine a hypothetical constitutional reopening claim.
* **Remedial Reach:** The claims of the former hostages and family members in this action against Iran and its Ministry arising from the covered seizure and detention. No prior securities judgment is reopened or altered, and no new liability is imposed on the United States.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower-court dismissal remains effective, the liability default supplies no enforceable damages entitlement, and no merits or damages proceedings resume on the rejected statutory theory. Any distinct legislative compensation program operates according to its own enacted terms rather than an order awarding compensation in this suit.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The immunity amendments leave the independent claim bar intact

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the enacted provisions' effect on the Accords because that question determines whether the suit may proceed. The United States has a concrete interest in compliance with its own agreement and may invoke the ordinary intervention and reconsideration procedures on that basis. Iran's default does not dispense with the court's §1608(e) duty to examine the legal right to relief.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the 2001 immunity amendment and 2002 technical correction supersede the Algiers Accords' bar on the particular hostage-related claims.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the distinction between immunity, a substantive claim and an independent bar to recovery. Require an actual enacted displacement of the agreement; legislative explanations may inform interpretation but cannot enact a missing abrogation or a command to preserve the default.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Resolve the text's operation and the recurring distinction between jurisdictional access and entitlement to judgment. A broader ruling on every terrorism cause of action is unnecessary because the surviving bar decides this suit. A narrower conclusion that the amendments accomplished nothing would deny their actual effect on immunity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A statute removing a specified sovereign-immunity obstacle does not, without enacted language accomplishing that further result, extinguish an independent legal bar to the plaintiff's claim or direct entry of judgment. Section 626(c) changes the state-sponsor-designation condition for this identified litigation, and §208 corrects its case designation. Neither provision abrogates the Accords' covered-claim prohibition. The explanatory statements cannot supply the missing operative rule. Plaintiffs therefore cannot proceed against Iran or its Ministry on the present statutory theory, and dismissal is affirmed.

  **Separate legal predicates.** Sovereign immunity concerns whether the foreign state may be subjected to the court's jurisdiction; a cause of action identifies a legally enforceable claim; the Accords separately preclude the covered hostage-related suit; and §1608(e) requires satisfactory support for relief despite nonappearance. Removing one obstacle establishes none of the others. The claimant must establish the invoked exception and right to relief under the applicable statute; there is no presumption of liability from default.

  **Enacted displacement.** Congress may change domestic law concerning an executive agreement within its constitutional authority. Courts examine the legislation's operative text and necessary legal effect for a clear displacement. No talismanic phrase is required, but the change must be accomplished by the enactment itself. A correction to a judge's initials does not carry the substantive effect attributed to an explanatory statement, and a desire to secure recovery cannot replace the law needed to permit it.

  **Intervention and default.** The United States' interest concerns its own obligation to bar covered claims. Its timely intervention after learning of the conflict, the risk to that obligation and Iran's nonappearance support its participation. Its motion invokes judicial reconsideration under the procedural rules, not executive authority to vacate a judgment by decree. The court independently decides the motion and the legal bar.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The designated immunity amendments and hostage-related claims against Iran and its Ministry. The holding does not declare every executive agreement immune from later legislation, create an immunity exception for unrelated conduct or establish that every foreign-state default may be disregarded without an authorized judicial basis.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify each independent jurisdictional and substantive predicate before treating a limited amendment as dispositive. Where Congress actually enacts a new rule governing an unresolved claim, courts apply its temporal reach and constitutional limits independently. Where an agreement-based bar remains, a payment provision requiring an otherwise valid judgment does not create the missing entitlement. No additional interim rule is needed for an alternative cause of action whose resolution cannot overcome the dispositive bar.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The enacted amendments apply to the designated pending case according to their terms; no completed final judgment is reopened by this holding.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The hostages' injuries are grave, but the court must identify the legal entitlement Congress enacted. The amendment is not futile: it removes the designation-timing immunity obstacle and permits the court to consider the remaining legal questions. Congress could have addressed the Accords directly or through operative language necessarily inconsistent with their bar. It did not do so in these provisions. Treating explanatory language as a binding disposition would both enlarge the enactment and displace the court's responsibility to decide the Government's independent argument.

  Dames & Moore sustains the pertinent claims-settlement authority at its actual scope; it does not prevent Congress from legislating. Trans World Airlines, Cook and Weinberger require a genuine legislative basis for displacing an international commitment rather than reliance on legislative history alone. Landgraf reinforces the need to identify the legal change and its temporal operation separately from constitutional permission. None turns removal of immunity into an award of damages. The Ministry's status as Iran itself supplies no route around the same claim bar, and the payment statute presupposes a legally supportable judgment rather than creating one.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply the agreement-displacement principles and preserve the separate immunity, cause-of-action and relief inquiries. Do not resolve the broad Flatow cause-of-action question merely because the Government offers it as an additional ground. No constitutional authority to override a completed judgment is necessary to this statutory disposition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The scope of a cause of action that could survive an enacted removal of the Accords' bar is unnecessary to this judgment and is not decided. No new compensation claim against the United States is presented. The constitutional validity of a different statute actually compelling a particular adjudicative result would require its enacted terms and proper presentation.

### Issue 2: Independent adjudication and Plaut's line between pending and completed cases

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Explain the changed-law and independent-adjudication principles relevant to the asserted legislative commands. Apply Plaut's finality rule and its qualifications to the question actually presented: this action remained pending, damages were unresolved and no legislative command to reopen a completed final judgment determines the outcome.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether legislative statements favoring the plaintiffs' judgment may substitute for enacted law and judicial determination, and how Plaut's finality rule and its pending-case and legislative-adjudication qualifications apply to case-specific legislation enacted while this action remained pending.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Preserve Congress's authority to change governing law for pending proceedings and the courts' responsibility to apply it. Apply Plaut's finality rule at its actual scope: Article III protects a private judgment that has become final through the ordinary judicial process against a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed, while Congress remains free to change the law governing a pending case and remains barred from dictating findings or imposing predetermined liability. This action was pending when Congress acted and no judgment finally disposing of it is reopened, so the amendments fall on the pending-case side of Plaut's line. The enacted provisions change immunity law without dictating findings or liability; the unenacted explanatory statements cannot supply a command to preserve the default, and the validity of a statute enacting such a command is not decided.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad in protecting the judicial function, confined to the actual posture in its judgment. A rule treating case-specific legislation as automatically invalid would exceed what independent adjudication requires. Extending Plaut's finality rule to a pending action and a nonfinal liability default would exceed what that holding protects, and reading its pending-case qualification as approval of every targeted statute would disregard its bar on dictated findings and liability.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may enact a new legal rule for a pending controversy, including an identifiable controversy, but may not turn unenacted legislative explanations into binding judicial commands or substitute a legislative determination for a court's independent application of law and fact. The immunity provisions are applied for the legal change their text makes; they do not command preservation of the default or defeat the independent Accords bar. Their reference to this case does not itself establish an unconstitutional adjudication, and affirmance does not require deciding every possible constitutional objection to targeted legislation.

  **Plaut's finality rule.** Plaut holds that finality is itself an Article III protection: once a private judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process, Congress may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable, require a court to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation, and later independent adjudication of eligibility and the merits does not restore the completed disposition. The rule protects completed adjudication; it leaves Congress free to change the law while a case remains pending. Public Law 107-77 and Public Law 107-117 were enacted while this action was pending: the August 2001 default addressed liability only, damages remained unresolved and no judgment finally disposed of the action. Plaut therefore does not bar the amendments' application here. Nor does it protect the default, which was not a judgment final through the ordinary judicial process and was vacated through judicial reconsideration on the Government's motion rather than by a legislative command.

  **The governing distinctions.** A pending proceeding remains subject to valid enacted changes with the temporal reach the law gives them. A private judgment final through completion or expiration of direct review is protected by Plaut against a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed. Continuing prospective relief remains subject to the law governing future conduct, as Plaut's Wheeling Bridge qualification and Miller v. French recognize within their own limits. Ordinary judicial reconsideration under an authorized rule is a judicial act requiring that rule's predicates, not a congressional appellate decree. These distinctions do not permit legislative fact-finding or dictated liability under the label of procedure.

  **Effect here.** Because the action was pending when Congress acted, the amendments fall within the authority Plaut preserves to change the law governing pending litigation, and courts apply that change independently. The enacted provisions remove a designated immunity obstacle and correct the case designation without prescribing findings, liability or damages, so what Congress enacted does not transgress Plaut's legislative-adjudication limit. The broader command attributed to the explanatory statements was not enacted. The court can reject that asserted command, apply the surviving claim bar and affirm without reopening a completed judgment or deciding the validity of a statute dictating a particular result.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Legislative effect on this unresolved controversy and the courts' independent responsibility to determine the text's consequences. Plaut's finality rule is confined to completed private judgments; this holding does not extend it to nonfinal orders, treat its pending-case qualification as validating every case-specific statute or decide the questions Plaut left open.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts distinguish genuine law changes from adjudicative commands and apply valid new rules to pending proceedings within their lawful temporal reach. Where a private judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process, Plaut bars a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed, while judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures remains available; Morgan Stanley, affirmed by an equally divided Court, supplies no rule. Prospective-relief disputes follow Miller v. French's changed-law and meaningful-adjudication requirements, and judicial reconsideration requires its independent procedural basis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The present judgment reopens no completed case and alters no judgment in the §27A(b) litigation; the amendments apply to this pending action according to their terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Robertson permits Congress to substitute operative legal standards while leaving application to courts. Klein protects the adjudicative responsibility that a legislature cannot assume merely by expressing the result it wants. The distinction turns on what the statute does, not simply on whether its beneficiaries or lawsuit can be identified. Here the enacted immunity change has legal work to do, while the broader command attributed to explanatory statements was not enacted.

  Plaut answered for completed private judgments the final-judgment question Robertson left open; Morgan Stanley's equal division supplied no precedent on it. Plaut's rule turns on the completed judicial disposition itself, which later independent adjudication cannot restore, so it reaches judgments that have become final through the ordinary judicial process, not pending proceedings. The same opinion preserved Congress's power to change the law governing pending cases and applied Klein and McCullough to bar legislatively prescribed findings or liability. This case lies on the pending side of Plaut's line, and the enacted provisions prescribe no finding or result; that leaves the actual disposition resting on the surviving claim bar.

  Miller v. French concerns future enforcement of prospective relief and preserves a meaningful judicial opportunity to decide statutory and constitutional predicates. It does not cancel accrued damages or supply a legislative appellate power over completed private judgments. Landgraf separately addresses temporal operation; express retrospectivity does not itself establish constitutional validity. The present default and unresolved damages remain distinct from the completed dismissals in Morgan Stanley and Plaut.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Robertson's genuine-change-of-law and independent-application distinction and Miller v. French's bounded prospective-relief rule. Apply Plaut's finality rule, together with its preservation of Congress's power to change the law governing pending cases and its bar on legislatively dictated findings or liability. Apply Klein's protection of independent adjudication without claiming its full reach is resolved. Morgan Stanley, affirmed by an equally divided Court, establishes no precedent. No precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The questions Plaut left undecided, including §27A(a) and the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, and any legislative command to vacate a completed judgment lie outside this pending-action record. Other forms of finality, criminal or innocence remedies, executive claims-settlement measures outside the governing authorities and a statute actually dictating disputed facts or liability are not adjudicated here.
