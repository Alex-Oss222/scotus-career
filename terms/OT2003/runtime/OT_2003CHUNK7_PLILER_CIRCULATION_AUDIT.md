## Exact propositions tested

### N. Minimum notice and opportunity

The affirmative duty applies when a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to elect between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing to exhaust other claims, and the materials already before the court disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat a timely return. The notice must state that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the first filing date and that a later petition may be barred. The prisoner must have a meaningful opportunity to delete the unexhausted claims and proceed on the exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements.

No missing-record investigation, exact deadline computation, claim-strength evaluation, strategic recommendation or promised stay is required by this minimum rule. Mere complexity or an unsuccessful filing is insufficient. The rule applies to Ford's pending review and later qualifying elections; it neither reopens unrelated final cases nor creates a new full year.

**Necessary scope:** A sufficient trigger is not an exclusive ceiling. For the four joining Associates, declining to require a strategic recommendation or a promised stay does not license omission of an actually available requested protective procedure from a court's presentation of the options. They may join this minimum rule and retain that fuller position in a separate explanation. They do not require the lead proposition itself to validate the entire stay procedure, but cannot join a declaration that the minimum warning exhausts all duties in Ford's circumstances.

### E. Defined equitable availability

Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling to remedy a proved court-created impediment of the specified kind: omission of the required notice or another materially misleading direction that caused the loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevented timely return. A notice violation does not by itself prove reliance, causation or diligence. The inquiry requires a lawful remedial basis capable of curing the actual timing defect; causal loss alone does not establish that tolling, as opposed to some other lawful correction, is the available remedy.

**Necessary scope:** The relevant obstacle is a court-created loss of the timely opportunity or court-caused prevention of return. Routine processing delay, pro se status or an unfavorable calculation does not automatically become such an obstacle. This resolves equitable availability for the defined problem, not a general code for every equitable-tolling claim.

### P. Proof, diligence and the causal interval

The petitioner bears the burden of establishing the factual predicates by a preponderance of the evidence. Reasonable diligence concerns the relevant period and conduct after the obstacle ended. It is assessed in the actual circumstances, including the court's communication and the petitioner's supported understanding; it is not perfect diligence or a requirement to anticipate and independently correct the very misleading direction at issue.

The remand court must identify the actual interval needing protection and a legally sufficient causal account for it, exclude independently avoidable delay, and explain how the chosen lawful remedy cures the full timing defect. It must confront time already expired before the election; it cannot silently toll only later days and call the petition timely, or automatically subtract all federal pendency.

**Necessary scope:** The pre-election inquiry does not require a later omission to have physically caused earlier elapsed days. It distinguishes the loss of a still-pending, timely original proceeding from prevention of a timely later filing. A lawful reopening theory must satisfy its own prerequisites; a tolling theory must justify the interval it protects. The court must determine whether the supported causal chain and permissible remedy reach the earlier expiration. The audit approves neither automatic retroactive tolling nor a categorical rule that pre-election expiration defeats every possible remedy. Independent delay remains excluded to the extent it is actually avoidable and causally material.

### R. Scoped relation back and surviving branches

A separate later action does not automatically become an amendment to a closed earlier action merely because the specified warnings were omitted. If relief depends on reopening, an independent lawful reopening predicate is necessary. Whether relation back follows an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remains open because that procedural predicate has not been established. A genuinely retained and stayed original petition is a different procedural situation. Agreement on R does not decide general stay authority.

Every Associate preserves the separate dismissal of two new Loguercio assistance claims and the factual-development direction concerning access to legal papers for five new Weed claims, together with the existing denial of automatic new-claim relation back. Preservation of the repeated-claim equitable argument remains for proper determination; the lower dissent's objection is not a found waiver.

## Proof-standard basis and competing route

Grogan, 498 U.S. at 286–291, expressly treats preponderance as the ordinary civil allocation and asks whether a particular interest or statutory structure warrants departure. Stevens authored the unanimous opinion; Souter and O'Connor were among its joining members. Herman & MacLean, 459 U.S. at 387–390, explains the ordinary allocation, the role of judicial selection when no governing standard is prescribed, and the reasons for heightened proof in proceedings threatening certain individual interests. Its note 27 also identifies the distinct historical concern over equity setting aside written instruments for fraud. Steadman, 450 U.S. at 95–104, concerns an APA-prescribed standard and cannot itself transplant the APA into habeas.

The strongest contrary route would decline a numerical standard because habeas implicates finality and liberty, or demand heightened proof before equitable interference with an otherwise operative deadline. The proposed inquiry concerns what happened in federal procedure, its causal effect and reasonable diligence. Success restores an opportunity for ordinary adjudication; it establishes no constitutional merits, entitlement to release, or automatic reopening. The prisoner's liberty interest supplies no reason to heighten the prisoner's burden to prove these procedural facts. The State's finality interest warrants the substantive causal and remedial limits already stated, rather than an unsupported higher evidentiary quantum. This is not an equity suit to invalidate a written instrument for fraud. Existing proof requirements attached to a different statutory or remedial predicate remain intact.

For those reasons, the ordinary burden is a supported extension for the four Associates who accept the defined equitable claim. It does real work when the actual communication, reliance, diligence or causal period is disputed. It is not inferred merely from statutory silence or from a general preference for access. The civil authorities do not establish notice liability, create equitable jurisdiction, settle preservation, or justify changing any formal judgment.
