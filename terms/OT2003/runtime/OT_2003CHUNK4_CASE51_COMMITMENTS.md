## Judgment and complete common ground

All eight Associates vote to vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment sustaining the challenged Department of Corrections funding and remand for the payment-specific actual-function inquiry and effective prospective relief described below. Each joins the rule, correction of the appellate premise and complete remand direction in full. The separate Workforce Development judgment retains its existing status outside this review.

A State's contract payments for rehabilitation placements may not finance devotional formation integrated into the publicly purchased treatment. Voluntary selection, a secular alternative and a secular rehabilitative objective do not cure an established purchase of that religious function. A religious provider may receive payment for independently identifiable secular services when actual operational separation and reliable accountability preserve that financed work. The court must determine what the contract payments actually buy; affiliation, a religious theme somewhere in the institution or speculative freeing of other resources cannot replace that determination.

Agostini supplies the unified governmental-function inquiry. Mitchell requires an actual funded-use showing and relief fitted to the proved scope, while Columbia Union preserves equal eligibility for independently identifiable secular work under effective restrictions and accountability. Strout and Zelman reject the proposition that private choice alone validates a public purchase of integrated devotion. These rules apply to contracted correctional treatment as they do to the identified educational expenditures; a rehabilitative objective cannot change the constitutional character of religious means that government actually purchases.

The Seventh Circuit treated the State's contractual reimbursement as a voucher arrangement insulated by offender choice. Its account establishes that Wisconsin reimburses part of the cost of placement under a contract with Faith Works. This is not an independent grant distributed to the offender to meet broadly selected personal expenses. Faith Works encourages a relationship with God through Jesus Christ as part of its treatment, and the reviewed funding supports placements in that program. Those facts require the financed-function inquiry; they cannot be resolved by the label voucher or by the fact that most other contracted providers are secular.

The supplied appellate account does not identify the contract's reimbursable cost categories, any operative secular-use restriction, or findings establishing the extent of reliable operational separation. The Court therefore cannot decide the permissible payment scope by assuming either that every provider activity is financed or that public money pays only secular care. The lower courts must apply the rule to the trial record, use its established findings, and determine the actual contracted work, payment allocation and operation of any controls. They must correct every proved devotional purchase through effective prospective relief while preserving independently lawful secular applications. Findings already establishing a particular funded use need not be reproved. Additional proceedings are required only to resolve a material gap in applying that standard, rather than because a new hearing is categorically mandatory.

## The completed Locke holding and this payment relationship

Locke permits Washington's confined refusal to support the devotional ministry degree within the particular individual scholarship there. Its controlling opinion expressly classifies that scholarship as student assistance, including room, board and other educational expenses, and distinguishes the restricted purchases in Strout and Zelman. It neither authorizes purchase of devotional rehabilitation nor requires a State to exclude every religious provider. Its distinction between individual assistance and the work actually purchased reinforces the need to identify this contract's function.

The public dissent in Locke relied on the student's independent benefit and disputed the devotional-degree exclusion on equality grounds. That ground does not establish an individual-benefit relationship here: the offender selects among contracted placements, but the State reimburses its provider for contracted services. The ability to choose the provider is not control of an independently distributed personal grant. Columbia Union's unanimous financing and secular-eligibility rules govern each Justice's commitment to this actual relationship.

## Voluntariness, recommendations and the funding question

The accepted findings establish that officers offered a secular alternative, explained that their recommendation was nonbinding, identified Faith Works's Christian character and did not advise conversion. The nine-month program's attractiveness compared with the three-month alternatives does not by itself contradict those supported findings. The bidding waiver is attributed to the longer program's rehabilitative features, not a finding of religious favoritism. Those findings remain operative on remand.

The plaintiffs' recommendation argument does not supply a reason to deem every offered choice compulsory, and the State's voluntariness response does not settle the distinct expenditure objection. The challenged public action is funding under the Department's contracts. The court must examine that action under the actual-function rule even though the lower no-coercion findings stand. Neither the lower opinion's general efficacy assertions nor an unverified reported oral-argument acknowledgment supplies an additional premise for constitutional approval.

## Alternative-path check and implementation

The strongest affirmance path treats an offender's free selection and the secular alternative as enough to make the expenditure private. That path fails under the controlling restricted-purchase rule and does not obtain support from Locke's materially different individual-assistance relationship. The strongest immediate categorical-injunction path treats the program's integrated Christianity as proof that every reimbursed cost finances devotion. The supplied source does not establish that complete payment scope, and Columbia Union and Mitchell require the actual operational connection and fitted relief. The common vacatur/remand resolves the erroneous legal premise and supplies the full governing standard without filling either factual gap by assumption.

On remand, the court determines the contract's purchased work and the supported scope of any separation on the existing trial record, makes the necessary findings, and enters effective prospective relief against prohibited payments at that scope. Independent lawful secular work remains eligible on equal terms under valid program conditions. This is application and remedial work under the adopted rule, not an unresolved choice between a private-choice immunity and a categorical ban on religious providers.
