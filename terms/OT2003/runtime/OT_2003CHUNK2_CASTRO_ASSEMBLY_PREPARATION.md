## Controlling-law draft

### I. The antecedent classification is reviewable

**Controlling proposition:** The authorization-review bar incorporated through section 2255 applies to actual decisions granting or denying circuit authorization to file a successive motion. It does not bar review of this antecedent classification judgment, where no authorization application was made and the appealed ruling treated a prior filing as the first motion.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker enforces the statutory bar against review of the specified authorization act. Stewart separately permits review of whether a proceeding requires authorization at all. Here the court of appeals affirmed a district-court dismissal on the premise that the 1994 conversion consumed Castro’s first motion. Its additional observation that he could not satisfy the successive criteria does not transform that ordinary appellate judgment into adjudication of a nonexistent authorization request. The Government’s characterization therefore does not establish the barred act. The inquiry remains action-specific; changing a label cannot reopen an actual authorization denial.

### II. Unwarned conversion cannot consume the first-motion opportunity

**Controlling proposition:** Before a federal court makes a pro se filing bearing another label count as the litigant’s first section 2255 motion for later second-or-successive restrictions, it must give notice of the intended conversion, warn that later section 2255 motions will face those restrictions, and provide a meaningful opportunity to withdraw or amend the filing to include the claims the litigant wishes to present. Without those protections, the converted filing cannot supply that later counting predicate.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins establish the rule independently of the judgment concurrences.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal courts sometimes construe pro se pleadings to reach their substance rather than lose a claim through an imperfect label. Haines supports that practice; it does not justify secretly consuming a separate statutory opportunity. The Court supervises its federal courts’ own recharacterization practice in light of section 2255’s first-motion and successive-motion structure. Notice identifies the changed vehicle; warning explains the future consequence; withdrawal preserves the choice to defer that vehicle; amendment permits an informed, complete initial submission.

The Government argues that Congress enacted no warning exception and that an actual prior adjudication should count. But the question is whether the court’s unsolicited label establishes the statutory first-motion predicate, not whether fairness excuses a concededly successive motion. Lonchar requires an actual lawful dismissal ground, and Stewart recognizes the distinct classification inquiry. Neither already prescribed these warnings; the Court adopts them now. The broader objection to all involuntary conversion identifies genuine risks, but does not require eliminating useful liberal construction to resolve this adverse counting consequence. The rule regulates that consequence and does not make every warned conversion lawful in every respect. Otherwise applicable amendment and limitation rules retain their force.

### III. The later counting consequence must be corrected

**Controlling proposition:** The unwarned 1994 conversion cannot make Castro’s express 1997 motion second or successive, and failure to appeal the earlier label does not supply the missing informed election. The later motion must return for ordinary first-motion adjudication, while the earlier merits judgment remains intact.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices; Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on their independent ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Castro selected Rule 33 and reiterated that choice. The court supplied the alternative section 2255 label without explaining its consequences or permitting withdrawal or amendment. Notice serves an informed decision whether to amend, withdraw or challenge conversion, including on appeal. The absence of an appeal therefore cannot cure the very lack of information that prevented informed choice. Even assuming law of the case applies, it is an adjudicative practice rather than an absolute limit on judicial power and does not bar correcting this later counting consequence.

The supervisory rule applies at least in the case announcing it, including the later use of the 1994 conversion to bar the post-enactment motion. This is not a general exemption for filings predating AEDPA. The district court must identify any valid independent procedural or timeliness bar and decide the ineffective-assistance claims that properly survive. Those ordinary adjudications determine entitlement to collateral relief; first-motion classification itself does not.

## Merits alternatives and consequences

**P2 — minimum protection before adverse counting.** A federal court may not make a pro se filing styled under a different rule count as the prisoner's first section 2255 motion for later second-or-successive restrictions unless the prisoner has been informed of the intended recharacterization, warned that later section 2255 motions will be subject to those restrictions, and given a meaningful opportunity to withdraw the filing or amend it to include the section 2255 claims he wishes to present. Without those protections, the converted filing cannot supply that later counting predicate.

For Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, this is a rule governing federal courts' own administration of liberal pro se pleading construction in light of section 2255's structure. Haines supports reading a pro se pleading for its substance, not imposing an unannounced permanent procedural loss through an accommodation. The enacted successive gates remain fully effective when their first-motion predicate exists. Lonchar requires legally supported dismissal predicates; Stewart shows that antecedent classification is a real legal inquiry, although its preserved-premature-claim rule does not itself decide conversion. The persuasive lower decisions collected in Roney's dissent—particularly Raineri on pre-enactment conversion and Adams, Miller, Seesing, Kelly and Henderson on warning and election—demonstrate workable approaches to this judicially created problem. They are not controlling Supreme Court law or a substitute for statutory reasoning.

Each required protection does work. Notice identifies the different vehicle; the warning identifies the consequence for later claims; withdrawal preserves the choice not to invoke the vehicle now; amendment permits a complete first submission if the prisoner chooses to proceed. This neither guarantees amendment beyond otherwise lawful limitations nor requires advice about the merits of every possible claim. It is a condition on judicial conversion's adverse counting effect, not a general equitable suspension of AEDPA or a universal duty to counsel pro se litigants.

**P3 — application and remedy.** The unwarned 1994 conversion cannot make the 1997 filing second or successive. Failure to appeal the earlier label does not supply informed acceptance of a consequence the court never explained, especially where Castro had insisted on Rule 33. Vacate the judgment affirming dismissal and remand for ordinary section 2255 proceedings without this successive-motion bar. The 1994 Rule 33 denial is not reopened. The lower court must examine the later motion under applicable first-motion requirements, including any properly raised independent timeliness or procedural defense and the ineffective-assistance claim if reached. First-motion classification determines neither those gates nor entitlement to relief.

**Contrary path — count the actual prior adjudication.** The lower majority's strongest position is that the judge actually adjudicated constitutional claims under section 2255, and Congress made no warning exception to the second-or-successive restrictions. Finality and the unchallenged earlier label reinforce that position; the later ineffective-assistance claim was available earlier. The response is that the statutory gates presuppose a first section 2255 motion. Congress's restrictions do not require a court's unsolicited, unexplained alternative label to consume the prisoner's opportunity to choose and prepare that motion. Limiting the adverse effect of the court's own recharacterization is not excusing a concededly successive motion for unfairness. The previous judgment retains its merits finality while its newly asserted collateral counting effect is corrected.

**Broader alternative — forbid involuntary conversion altogether.** Relabeling can create risks beyond the successive-motion gate; courts may assist comprehension without changing a litigant's selected procedural vehicle. That broader objection has particular force for Scalia and Thomas, but a blanket prohibition on every beneficial recharacterization is not required to decide the present adverse counting issue. No proposed opinion may describe their agreement with the remedy as an affirmative endorsement of conversion whenever three warnings are supplied.

## Merits: six full rule joins and two independent grounds

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain P2–P3 in full.** Before a court makes a pro se filing bearing another label count as the prisoner's first §2255 motion for later successive-motion restrictions, it must give notice of the intended conversion, warn about the restrictions on subsequent motions, and provide an opportunity to withdraw or amend to include the §2255 claims the prisoner wishes to raise. Without those protections the converted filing does not count for that later restriction. This is an exercise of federal judicial supervision of courts' own pleading administration in the statutory setting, not a new exception allowing a concededly successive motion through AEDPA's gates.

The rule operates here despite the earlier conversion's 1994 date: it governs use of that unwarned conversion as the predicate for barring the 1997 motion, and the supervisory rule applies at least to the case in which it is announced. It neither reopens the old Rule 33 denial nor declares all pre-enactment conversions exempt from statutory consequences. Haines supports liberal reading, not secret exhaustion of a statutory first opportunity. Lonchar and Stewart support attention to the actual dismissal and classification predicates without already having established these exact warnings.

Failure to appeal the label does not cure the omitted warning. Notice serves informed amendment or withdrawal and informed challenge to conversion, including appeal. The absence of an appeal therefore cannot establish the informed choice that the missing warning was designed to permit. Even assuming law of the case applies, it describes a judicial practice rather than an absolute restriction on power; it does not bar correction of this later counting consequence. The Court need not decide that every earlier recharacterization judgment is void.

**Scalia and Thomas support noncounting and the corrective remedy on an independent ground, not P2's affirmative permission-and-warning framework.** Liberal construction of allegations does not justify overriding a prisoner's chosen procedural vehicle and silently imposing the costs of a first §2255 motion. Their party-control objection survives warnings: a court cannot reliably predict every adverse consequence, and even an apparent immediate benefit may disappear on appeal. They would avoid involuntary conversion into a first §2255 motion where that risk cannot be removed, rather than treat three warnings as a sufficient license to convert. Their ground is the impropriety of imposing the unchosen procedural vehicle, not an equitable exception to a properly triggered statutory gate.

Their historical concurrence also found a narrower sufficient reason: conversion offered Castro no benefit because Rule 33 validly presented a claim no weaker on its merits under that vehicle. That historical application confirms the strength of their objection but is not needed to add a new factual premise to this frozen packet. The reconciled commitment rests on the broader party-control objection already tested in independent modeling, applied to the supported fact that Castro selected and reiterated Rule 33 and the judge imposed the additional label. No exact filing-date finding or new assessment of the Rule 33 claim's substantive strength is adopted. If assembly instead wishes to make the historical no-benefit application a separate operative ground, the exact procedural and merits equivalence premise requires bounded neutral source validation before its adoption; absence of that additional finding does not block the present independent ground.

Both join the jurisdiction holding and agree that law of the case neither bars nor automatically resolves the question. They refuse full merits-opinion joinder even if its practical remedy is identical. Their noncounting result is not an endorsement of every warned conversion, and their broader proposed restriction is not controlling merely because they agree with the other six on this disposition.
