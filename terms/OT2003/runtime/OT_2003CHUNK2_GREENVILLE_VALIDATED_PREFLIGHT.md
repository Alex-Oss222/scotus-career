## Competing paths that independent modeling must test

### 1. Delegation and effective control

The strongest sustaining path distinguishes an objective medical qualification from an actual delegation of licensing judgment. It uses the enacted §305 transfer alternative, §205 staff exception, existing licensed operations, professional emergency-care standards and lawful constraints on public hospitals as evidence of operation. The majority found no actual licensing refusal on this record. A complete rationale must explain why the relevant provider arrangements leave the State responsible for licensing and do not place protected care at an outsider's uncontrolled discretion.

The strongest contrary path asks whether continuing compliance depends on a private hospital or consultant's unbounded consent despite the absence of a past refusal. Public-hospital protections do not automatically govern private hospitals. Existing success does not decide prospective effective control, and an unassured discretionary waiver may not cure it. Each of §§205, 305 and 309 must be tested separately; an alternative in one section does not resolve all three. Tailored relief can address an identified veto or require a legally available effective alternative without invalidating every safety provision. The clergy delegation theory remains distinct from the Establishment Clause theory even if one ground supplies complete relief.

Casey's actual six-step standard forbids effective third-party control and recognizes supported prospective injury. It does not presume that every medical arrangement is a veto or that every additional licensing condition triggers heightened access scrutiny. Challengers must identify the actual obligation, causal interference and affected population. Do not import the lower majority's categorical no-valid-application formula as a substitute for the governing framework.

### 2. Religion and legally supportable construction

The strongest sustaining path treats clergy referral as help available when the patient requests it, without mandated religious counseling, compelled ideological assent, denominational screening or transfer of civil power to clergy. It must identify why that reading is lawful and adequate to constrain enforcement. The majority described a list or directory and contacting a requested provider, not obligatory institutional religious affiliation.

The contrary path has concrete published support. Dissent paragraph 111 recounts drafter/licensing official Moore's testimony favoring advance arrangements with several denominations and expressing denominational preferences; note 14 discusses its interpretive significance. This is a judicial recitation of testimony, not a transcript-based party concession or an agreed authoritative interpretation. It prevents a handoff from describing every enforcement concern as pure conjecture. Required denominational choices, official evaluation of religious adequacy or clergy control of licensure would raise a materially different problem from requested assistance.

Commack's unanimous actual rule forbids governmental theological judgment and compulsory religious civil authority while permitting factual verification and private religious choice. It expressly applies Agostini without reviving independent Lemon prongs. The lower dissent's historical Lemon analysis therefore cannot be copied as the current test. Modeling may sustain a legally established limiting construction, reject the offending religious obligation, or identify a genuinely unresolved construction/factual question requiring bounded further proceedings. It may not silently rewrite the text or assume that a federal panel's construction is an authoritative state-high-court ruling.

### 3. Fair notice and discretionary enforcement

The sustaining path draws meaning from medical context, professional usage and the specific duties rather than isolated words. Both lower writings accept the clarity of many challenged provisions. The majority also relies on graduated civil enforcement and good-faith considerations. That path can preserve ordinary facility, staffing and safety standards without endorsing every open-ended clause.

The contrary path focuses on the two disputed clusters: the actual staff/consulting language of §305(A), and the Department-interpreted best-practices language in Chapter 10 and §103(C). Existing medical context may clarify the first; an agency's power to punish conduct under an unspecified later understanding raises a different notice problem. The first Class III warning does not by itself settle repeated penalties or criminal enforcement. Protected-care implications also remain independent of criminal classification. A narrowing construction must state its legal source and actual limits, and relief must identify the defective command and dependent enforcement rather than nullify the full regulation.

**Specific source limit:** If an independent model makes the existence or absence of criminal liability decisive, it must first verify the date-eligible full text and applicable incorporation of §44-7-340 and any relevant penalty provisions. The lower majority/dissent conflict alone cannot resolve that linkage. This is a conditional targeted source need, not an absence-of-record conclusion or a blocker to all four components.

### 4. Identified inspection and confidentiality

The strongest sustaining path treats inspections as confidential health administration tied to assessing patient care, with relevant identifiers sometimes useful, statutory disclosure limits, regulated retention and staff confidentiality. Whalen's safeguarded administration and Doe's actual assumed-right, confined benefits holding support evaluating function and safeguards without demanding that every datum be indispensable. Neither case gives government a general entitlement to intimate information.

The strongest contrary path distinguishes routine inspection of premises, staffing and general compliance from identified clinical investigation. It requires an adequate connection between the information, authorized recipients and actual inspection need, accounting for usable redaction, consent and targeted follow-up. The lower dissent acknowledges that anonymous complaints or corroboration may justify identified access in particular investigations; its position is not a categorical ban. The access clause and copying qualifier must be analyzed on their own terms.

The record dispute is not confined to a hypothetical leak. Dissent paragraph 104 recounts a protest flyer containing a copied medical record concerning a fifteen-year-old patient reportedly obtained from DHEC, plus physician testimony about similar releases. The original flyer and testimony have not been independently inspected, and the recitation does not establish every identifying detail or the basis of every release. Preserve the evidence and its disputed significance; do not elevate it into an agreed finding or erase it through the majority's description of remote risk.

Current law keeps three questions distinct: lawful confidential administrative access; unnecessary or unjustified access/dissemination of intimate information; and any separately proved additional interference with protected choice. Doe leaves broader internal uses open. Herring's recipient-and-function rule concerns outside disclosure, not a categorical rule against health inspectors. The entering-law intimate-information register also recognizes Lawall's sufficient-connection requirement for authorized outside access without requiring a completed leak; necessary confidential professional handling remains permissible. Casey sustains specified nonidentifying clinical reports with actual safeguards and supplies the separate access inquiry. No precedent here automatically requires universal strict scrutiny or the least intrusive imaginable inspection system.
