# Hudson: neutral clarification of judicial scope and final-rule chronology

## Finding from the supplied record

**The supplied judicial record establishes a challenge to the April 2002 interim rule's delegated authority, emergency procedure and preemptive effect. It does not establish a separately presented judicial challenge to the January 2003 final notice's compliance with APA section 553(c) or its own section 553(d) effective date.** The later finalization is established as an objective regulatory event. This finding is about the scope supported by the supplied materials, not a determination that no later paper exists, that a litigant waived an objection, or that a final-rule objection would fail.

The reviewed New Jersey judgment is dated June 12, 2002. It therefore concerns the April 2002 interim rule, not a notice first published January 29, 2003. The approved neutral packet identifies delegated authority, emergency rulemaking and displacement of local disclosure law as the ACLU's judicial objections, and expressly confines review to the preserved issues and identified judgment. The packet supplies no supplemental judicial filing, amended pleading, later judgment, argument transcript or granted-question expansion presenting a final-notice procedure challenge.

The final notice reports four administrative comments and gives the agency's responses. Its procedural discussion describes objections to the interim rule's promulgation. Those are rulemaking comments, not evidence that a particular final-notice claim was pleaded, preserved or argued in this judicial proceeding. Nor does the notice identify every commenter or establish the commenters' litigation role. The agency's prospective-efficacy assertion is its published position; it is not a verified judicial concession, supplemental brief or ruling on the scope of this appeal.

## What an affirmance on the established interim grounds requires

On the supplied scope, an affirmance sustaining the interim rule's actual delegation, separate notice-and-comment and delayed-effectiveness exceptions, and the reviewed structural/preemption ground does **not automatically require** an additional adjudication that the final notice independently satisfies section 553(c), that section 553(d) is inapplicable to unchanged continuation, or that a defect in the final notice has been cured. That would be an additional legal ground, not a necessary consequence of recognizing final publication.

The Court can resolve the federal basis on which the June 2002 judgment rests while accurately acknowledging that the identical regulatory text was subsequently adopted as final. The January 2003 event may be current regulatory background without turning every possible objection to that event into an issue presented for decision. The entry must distinguish that objective continuity from a new affirmative holding validating all final-rule procedures. It must not claim to sustain an independently contested final procedure when such a contest is not established.

The validity of federal law remains a predicate to preemption. But that principle does not require inventing a new challenge, presuming a final-notice defect, or treating a missing research paper as an actual failure to preserve. If the reasoning sustains the reviewed interim procedure and does not need the final notice to repair it, there is no automatic-curative premise to decide. This is a scope assessment, not a recommendation to sustain that procedure.

## Supported lawful paths and their different premises

1. **Resolve the established interim-rule challenge.** Decide the actual delegated-authority, interim APA and structural/preemption questions supporting the reviewed judgment. If those grounds sustain it, finalization may be identified as unchanged current background. Do not add an independent section 553(c)/(d) holding, a waiver finding or a general approval of every final procedure merely to describe that chronology.

2. **Find a defect in the reviewed interim basis and assess the surviving law's effect on relief.** An interim defect would not itself establish that current disclosure must be ordered, because the final notice exists. Conversely, the agency's claim of final efficacy does not itself save the earlier judgment, establish retroactive cure or make the case moot. The Court must connect the defect, the actual temporal relief and the surviving rule to a lawful disposition. A bounded return for the required surviving-law inquiry remains a path if the matter cannot properly be resolved from the established record and scope. This is an analytical alternative, not an assertion that a litigant requested that particular return.

3. **Reach an independently material final-operation ground if the case actually requires it.** If the proposed disposition instead rests on final operation despite an interim defect, or on an alleged final-procedure defect, the final rule's actual procedural basis must be addressed at the necessary scope. The complete final notice supplies the published comments, responses, effective date and adopted interim rationale. It does not establish preservation of a judicial objection or permission to invent a separate final good-cause finding. An unchanged-continuation section 553(d) argument may be analyzed as a legal possibility arising from the source, but it is not an already established Court holding, party concession or necessary issue in every affirmance.

No path depends merely on choosing a preferred result. Each must remain within the actual questions and connect its legal basis to the relief under review. The complete-resolution requirement concerns material questions actually presented or necessary to the decision; it does not convert an operator's conceivable procedural objection into a litigated claim.
