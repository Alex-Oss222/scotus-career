## 62. United States v. Flores-Montano

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Flores-Montano
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1794; 541 U.S. 149.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-30
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourth Amendment requires reasonable suspicion before customs officials at the international border remove, disassemble and reassemble a vehicle’s fuel tank to inspect for contraband.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit summarily affirmed suppression under its rule requiring reasonable suspicion for fuel-tank removal.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of suppression; Flores-Montano seeks preservation of the reasonable-suspicion requirement.
* **Material Record:** At the Otay Mesa border crossing, officials had a mechanic remove a vehicle’s fuel tank and found marijuana inside. The inspection involved reversible disassembly and roughly an hour including the wait. The record did not establish serious damage or destruction. The Government declined to rely on reasonable suspicion as its ground in the district court.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The actual international-border location and vehicle inspection are essential. This is not a bodily search, an interior investigative stop, or a record establishing destructive drilling. The Government’s preserved theory does not require a new factual finding of suspicion.
* **Relevant Dates:** The circuit suppression judgment issued March 14, 2003; the scheduled event is March 30, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Border inspection and the manner of a property search

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted holding decides this fuel-tank procedure. United States v. Ramirez keeps the means and extent of property destruction independently subject to Fourth Amendment reasonableness; authority to search does not authorize gratuitous damage. Soldal v. Cook County recognizes independent possession protection for a home, without deciding every property category.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual border predicate and inspection function, then assess any distinct intrusion in the method. Personal bodily dignity, property damage, and ordinary vehicle disassembly are not interchangeable facts.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; 19 U.S.C. §1581(a) as applicable to the border inspection.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Ramsey and United States v. Montoya de Hernandez supply the received border-search framework. The latter’s treatment of detained persons does not itself create a technical-complexity rule for vehicle inspection.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Border authority permits inspection of entering effects. It does not automatically approve excessive destruction, arbitrary detention, or bodily intrusion, each of which has its own factual and doctrinal predicates.
* **Open Point:** Whether reversible fuel-tank removal itself creates an additional suspicion requirement despite the established border-inspection setting.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Flores-Montano invokes interference with property, possible mechanical damage, specialized labor and delay to classify the search as requiring suspicion. The United States argues that vehicle compartments remain inspectable at entry and that reversible mechanical work does not resemble bodily intrusion. The circuit’s categorical rule treated this disassembly as nonroutine. Potential harm must be distinguished from actual destructive execution; neither an unproved accident nor a new suspicion finding can decide this record.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the border exception includes this nondestructive inspection without individualized suspicion. Affirm if the particular property intrusion requires suspicion even absent demonstrated destruction. A distinct manner challenge would require facts establishing unreasonable damage or delay, which cannot be invented from the equipment used.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule may cover reversible inspection of vehicle fuel tanks at the border while preserving different rules for bodies, destructive methods and materially prolonged detention. It need not classify every conceivable vehicle search.
