## Proposed notice proposition

When a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to elect between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing the petition to exhaust other claims, and the materials before the court disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat timely return, the court must give neutral notice that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the original filing date and that a later petition may be time barred. It must provide a meaningful opportunity to delete unexhausted claims and proceed with the exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements. It need not calculate a precise deadline, recommend a strategy, evaluate claim strength or promise a stay.

The rule regulates the accuracy of the procedural election the court itself presents. Mere statutory complexity or an unsuccessful filing is not its trigger. The court need not investigate missing records. Duncan's exclusion of federal pendency under §2244(d)(2) remains unchanged. Castro supplies an analogy concerning judicial management that can silently consume a statutory opportunity, but the mixed-petition protection is an express supervisory extension rather than an earlier Castro holding.

The protection applies to review of Ford's still-pending dismissal consequences and later qualifying elections. It does not reopen unrelated final cases or supply a new full limitation period.

## Proposed court-created-impediment equitable rule

Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling for a court-created impediment when the petitioner proves by a preponderance the required causal connection and reasonable diligence during the relevant period and after the obstacle ended. The court must determine whether omission of the required notice or another materially misleading direction caused loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevented timely return. A notice violation alone does not prove reliance, causation or diligence, and causal loss alone does not establish that tolling is the available remedy.

The court must identify the precise interval requiring protection and a legally sufficient causal basis for tolling it, including any time that expired before the election, and exclude independently avoidable delay rather than automatically subtract all federal pendency. A later action does not automatically relate back to a closed earlier action. If relief requires reopening that action, an independently lawful basis is necessary. Without a lawful basis that actually cures the timing defect, the later petition remains untimely.

The rule resolves equity only for the defined court-created impediment; it does not announce a complete general equitable-tolling code. It does not require a finding of a misleading communication, reliance, diligence, causal prevention or ultimate timeliness on this appellate record.

## Relation back, stayed petitions and actual remand

The proposal rejects automatic treatment of Ford's later actions as amendments to pending originals. It leaves open the distinct question whether relation back could follow an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening; that question depends on a procedural predicate not yet established. It preserves the separate theory that deleting unexhausted claims, retaining and staying the exhausted claims, and later lawfully amending is an available protective procedure. Whether that broader stay theory receives controlling agreement must be assessed separately, not inferred from agreement on the minimum notice rule. A Justice may retain that broader position while adopting the minimum notice proposition if the frozen reasoning supports that relationship.

For the repeated claims, Loguercio's affirmative election and Weed's nonresponse require separate inquiries. Weed dismissal occurred October 14 after nonresponse, state filing October 24. State review ended March 25, 1998 and the federal returns were filed April 7. The period expired during initial federal pendency; protecting only time after dismissal may not cure the timing defect. Preservation remains an issue to decide, not a found waiver.
