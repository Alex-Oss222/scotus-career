P2. A closing argument must be assessed in the context of the supported defense and the argument as a whole. Counsel may concentrate on the disputed intent, concede damaging background facts and use candor to resist improper character reasoning. The identified intent argument and impeachment make the state performance determination reasonable. No missing jury instruction is reconstructed to cure the phrasing. ## Component D — medical qualification and secular referral delegation

**D1: Common sufficient ground.** The State may require objective medical qualifications and arrangements serving emergency care without thereby transferring its licensing judgment to private persons. It may not make access to protected care depend on an outsider's arbitrary, legally uncontrolled consent. Whether an arrangement is such a veto depends on its operative conditions and effect, not the label private or medical. Yick Wo supplies the prohibition against arbitrary licensing administration; Danforth and the actual Casey decision supply the effective-third-party-control limitation. Professional qualification is not categorically private governmental power.

**D2: Application separately to each medical provision.** All eight would affirm rejection of the medical and secular referral delegation theory on this record. Section 205's availability and on-staff terms identify a clinical service and avoid requiring an outside agreement where the specified physician is on staff. Section 305 additionally permits Department-approved emergency-transfer arrangements. Those provisions have concrete medical functions and alternatives relevant to whether a private actor controls licensing. Section 309 must stand on its own: a privilege qualification relates to the physician's ability to secure appropriate hospital care, but it has no supplied transfer exception. The present record does not establish that this separate command places these providers' continued operation at an identified hospital's arbitrary discretion, or that an impending refusal creates such effective control. The secular section 307 specialties likewise identify needed care and referrals rather than civil authority for consultants to decide whether a clinic may exist.

Existing licensed operations and the absence of a demonstrated refusal support the confined application; they are not a rule that plaintiffs must suffer closure before suing. Reasonably supported impending interference is cognizable. Public-hospital legal constraints do not automatically bind private hospitals. The waiver is not treated as an assured cure. Medical relevance alone also would not justify a proved private veto. The sufficient conclusion is that the challenged requirements, considered distinctly and in their established operation, have not been shown to confer that veto here.

**Strongest contrary path and answer.** A private hospital or consultant can withhold cooperation for reasons unrelated to care, and an unassured waiver may leave the provider powerless. Section 309 is the strongest example because section 305's alternative cannot answer it. This objection would warrant fitted relief upon proof of effective arbitrary control or supported impending denial; it does not establish that every professional cooperation requirement presently has that operation. The disposition rejects neither prospective proof nor a later challenge to such a refusal. It does not adopt the lower court's categorical no-valid-application formula in place of Casey.

**D3: Scope and remedy.** Affirm on this delegation ground only; preserve the separate clergy issue and independent notice and privacy claims. Do not validate every possible enforcement action or decide the previously adjudicated general access claim. If a future or otherwise preserved application proves an effective veto, relief must address that condition and available effective alternatives under the actual Casey rules and severability law, rather than automatically abolishing medical qualifications.

All eight accept D1's ordinary arbitrary-licensing limitation and D2–D3's confined result. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept the complete governing Casey framework; Kennedy accepts its core-choice and effective-control reasoning without affirmatively joining the residual lesser-burden tier. Scalia and Thomas join the ordinary qualification/arbitrary-control ground and result while preserving their prior objection to Casey's broader constitutional framework. Their agreement does not supply eight affirmative joins to that framework.

## Component R — clergy arrangements and religious authority

**R1: Common governing boundary.** All eight accept Commack's actual rule: government may facilitate private religious choice and verify objective facts, but may not decide theological correctness, require denominational conformity or grant clergy compulsory civil control. Agostini supplies the applicable religion framework as used in Commack; no independent historical Lemon-prong test is revived. Helping a patient contact clergy she requests can satisfy this boundary. Requiring institutional affiliations that make licensing turn on religious approval or official denominational adequacy cannot.

**R2: Six commitments to vacatur and bounded remand.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate rejection of the clergy claims and remand to determine the legally operative obligation. The majority's patient-requested list/contact construction is a plausible permissible mechanism, but it is not an authoritative state-high-court construction established by this record. Moore's published deposition recitation describes advance arrangements with several denominations and expresses denominational preferences. That is concrete contrary enforcement evidence. It is neither a transcript-verified concession binding all parties nor proof that every inspector necessarily follows it. The conflict cannot be removed by silently adding patient-request language to the regulation or calling the concern wholly hypothetical.

The remand must resolve whether state law and actual administration confine the duty to requested neutral assistance, or require the provider to secure clergy affiliations and satisfy religious criteria as a licensing condition. Existing material may suffice; no new hearing is mandated. A lawful, enforceably bounded requested-assistance construction permits the provision to stand at that scope. If compulsory religious arrangements, denominational evaluation or clergy control are established, enjoin that operation and dependent licensing sanctions, applying state severability law while preserving secular referral duties and voluntary religious assistance. The same inquiry answers the distinct clergy-delegation theory insofar as licensing actually depends on clerical consent. Ordinary equitable rules govern surviving interim requests.

**Strongest sustaining objection.** As needed naturally confines referrals to patient demand, a directory can implement it, and federal courts ordinarily may adopt a fair saving construction. The six recognize that path but withhold an unconditional affirmance because the operative command is arrangements, the published administering official describes more, and an enforceable state-law limitation has not been established. Their judgment is not immediate facial invalidation or a ruling that mentioning clergy is itself establishment.

**R3: Scalia and Thomas would affirm on a bounded construction.** They treat the lower majority's practical reading of section 307 as the best lawful reading of availability as needed: reasonable assistance to reach requested clergy, without obligatory religious counseling, ideological assent, denominational screening or a clergy veto. Their Commack joins forbid those latter mechanisms. The drafter's testimony deserves consideration but does not amend the enacted rule; on this reading it does not require invalidating the provision or reopening the case. They would expressly limit the affirmance to the requested-assistance construction and leave a proved contrary enforcement practice challenge available. Their barrier to R2 is its premise that the published interpretive dispute presently requires further proceedings; their barrier to any broader affirmance is compulsory religious civil power. Neither would sustain the denominational arrangement described in the testimony as a governmental religious standard.

## Component V — fair notice and enforcement discretion

**V1: Ordinary regulatory terms.** All eight would affirm rejection of the ordinary vagueness challenges. Read in their facility and professional setting, the challenged rules concerning similar names, waiver administration, penalty factors, written policies, trained personnel, health/background verification, contagious conditions, qualifications, safe professional care, appropriate equipment, indicated referrals, quality review, safety, sterile storage, cleanliness and odors, grass/pest control and room requirements have intelligible functions and contextual standards. They do not become void merely because professional judgment and factual evaluation are necessary. Hoffman Estates supplies contextual notice and enforcement analysis; Grayned distinguishes practical standards from standardlessness. This agreement does not rest on a presumption that the regulations carry only civil consequences.

**V2: Section 305(A).** All eight would reject the separate staff/consulting-language vagueness claim. Physicians is the relevant professional class, whether staff, consulting or both. Reading the full sentence does not plausibly require receptionists or every nonphysician employee to obtain hospital privileges. The enacted alternative is documented Department-approved emergency transfer when hospitalization is necessary. That resolves the claimed uncertainty in this sentence without erasing a duty or importing its alternative into section 309. Neither the ordinary terms nor section 305 fails under the stricter clarity concern asserted by the challengers; no criminal-linkage finding is needed for these conclusions.

**V3: Six commitments against the independent best-practices sanction mechanism.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the rejection of this discrete challenge and remand for a fitted decree. Chapter 10 and the independent best-practices branch of section 103(C) permit the Department to identify an otherwise unannounced obligation and classify its violation. No objective source or substantive criterion identifying which additional practice is compulsory has been established. The professional context gives many specific provisions meaning but does not itself define this residual Department-selected obligation. Section 103(C)'s operative classification prevents treating Chapter 10 simply as authority to adopt future regulations.

The actual Morales holding supplies the decisive distinction: meaningful enforcement guidance must govern the condition authorizing a command; an individual warning does not substitute for that legal criterion. Kolender and Grayned support that independent rule. This branch's common defect is the absence of a definite additional obligation, not uncertainty at the margins of an otherwise intelligible duty. The holding reaches sanctions based solely on that unbounded best-practices mechanism, not the other residual classification of breaches of definite regulatory duties. The State remains able to adopt prospectively adequate standards through lawful means.

**Strongest contrary path and answer.** Medical best practice has professional content; the first Class III response is a warning and section 103(E) considers good faith, compliance history and circumstances. Those protections can moderate application of an intelligible rule. They do not select the compulsory standard or constrain whose practices the Department may reject. The majority's first-offense account also does not prove immunity from later fines or criminal exposure. The six find the mechanism defective even assuming only civil licensing consequences, given the undefined operative duty and protected-care setting. They therefore neither resolve nor rely on the disputed incorporation of section 44-7-340.

**V3 remedy.** Restrain independent sanctions and adverse licensing consequences based solely on the undefined Department-interpreted best-practices branch until an adequate legally operative standard exists. Preserve Chapter 10 to the extent it supports lawful prospective standard setting, definite duties elsewhere, and section 103(C)'s classification of definite violations not classified I or II. The lower court must conform the decree to state severability law. This is no universal injunction against professional standards, no rewriting of section 305 or 309, and no reopening of all access regulation.

**V4: Scalia and Thomas would affirm the best-practices disposition.** Their Morales separate position distinguishes missing legal content from a rule with an intelligible contextual core. Here professional clinical practices and the regulation's health-care functions supply such a core; a later contested choice between defensible practices may require an as-applied notice or arbitrary-enforcement challenge. They would read Department interpretation as application of professionally recognizable practices, not unlimited authority to invent new crimes or retroactive obligations. Hoffman Estates supports that bounded approach. Practical clarification, warnings and mitigating factors supplement the core; they do not cure every undefined law.

Their strongest objection to V3 is that it converts disagreement about how professional standards apply into absence of any standard. Their limiting barrier is clear: affirmance does not authorize an unforeseeable sanction simply because an inspector preferred a different practice. This conclusion holds even if the challengers' asserted criminal-enforcement account is assumed for this analysis; no finding that criminal sanctions are absent is necessary. The precise section 44-7-340 linkage remains undecided, not silently resolved.

## Component P — identified records, authorized access and safeguards

**P1: Six accept a sufficient-connection rule, not universal indispensability.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply Lawall's requirement to determine the actual information, recipients, official function and safeguards when government authorizes an additional transfer of intimate clinical information. Private-clinic records becoming available to inspectors require that inquiry even though the recipient works in health administration. The rule protects against unjustified access and redistribution; it does not forbid routine lawful inspection or presume that all identified review is improper.

Whalen permits genuine safeguarded health administration. Doe's actual assumed-right holding permits the confined benefits use there without requiring every datum to be indispensable; it does not establish unrestricted inspection authority or make public disclosure essential to every claim. Lawall expressly distinguishes necessary handling from additional legally permitted access. Herring adds recipient-specific limits on outside dissemination, not a categorical bar on confidential health inspectors. These authorities leave legitimate identified clinical audits, investigation of complaints, corroboration, continuity and other sufficiently connected functions available.

**P2: Application and strongest sustaining alternative.** South Carolina has a substantial, concrete interest in verifying patient-care compliance; complete records and confidential inspector access can serve it. Section 102(F)(2)'s copying qualifier and the retention, training and privacy rules supply real limits. Those facts defeat a rule that every identifier requires a compelling-interest finding or that name redaction must always substitute for inspection. They do not establish that the full authorized audience, inspection uses, copying and later disclosures are adequately confined in every material respect.

The district court's general compelling-need formulation is too categorical; the Fourth Circuit's reliance on confidentiality and administrative purpose is insufficiently attentive to the actual permissions. Sections 44-7-310 and 315 must be read with their distinct exceptions and competing constructions. Section 44-41-60 governs registrar reports, and section 44-41-360 supplies a constitutional anonymity decision, not absolute confidentiality. A court cannot combine these different rules into an absolute bar that none enacts. Name-only redaction is likewise not necessarily effective deidentification given section 401's other fields.

The reported flyer reproducing a record of a fifteen-year-old patient's termination, reportedly obtained from DHEC, and physicians' accounts of similar releases are material evidence. They defeat a characterization of the entire concern as a purely speculative leak. The recitation does not establish every identifying detail, every release's legal authorization, the State's actual safeguards or liability for all copies. The six would require its actual significance to be assessed rather than treating it as an agreed constitutional violation or erasing it.

**P3: Judgment and remedy.** The six would vacate the reversal of the district court on this component and remand for determination of the actual access, copying, use and redistribution authority, its connection to patient-care functions, and effective safeguards. Neither blanket approval of identified access nor the district court's categorical compelling-need restriction survives as the final merits resolution. Existing material may answer the questions without a new hearing. The lower court must prevent established improper access or dissemination while preserving lawful confidential inspection and necessary clinical records, and decide surviving interim requests under ordinary equity. Any restriction must identify the defective permission or practice and follow applicable severability law.

Redacted review, consent and targeted identified follow-up are concrete alternatives whose adequacy must be assessed where they can serve the actual inspection objective. They are not rigid prerequisites to every inspection, a warrant system, universal judicial preclearance or a least-intrusive-imaginable-method rule. Sufficiently connected identified review remains permissible even where one datum is not independently indispensable. Confidentiality need not promise perfect containment, but penalties for unauthorized leaks do not justify unnecessary lawful access.

**P4: Separate access inquiry and join limit.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also accept the complete actual Casey framework if the proved inspection obligation causes additional interference with protected choice: identify the actual duty, protected baseline, affected population, causation and interference; apply the corresponding ordered protection and fit inquiry; preserve necessary timely care, clinical judgment, communication and fitted relief. Effective veto, prohibition, obstruction and other severe or meaningful interference retain their actual distinct consequences. No burden is inferred merely from the word abortion, asserted fear or the presence of identifiers, and no finding that every physician or patient is deterred is made. Kennedy joins P1–P3 on independent privacy and actual-permission grounds, not P4's residual lesser-burden rationale. None of these commitments reopens the earlier overall access or equal-protection adjudication.

**P5: Scalia and Thomas would affirm rejection of the identified-access claim.** They preserve their Doe concurrence's source objection and Lawall separate position against the broader substantive informational-privacy extension. They accept legitimate confidential inspection and reject general government immunity for unrelated releases. Independently assuming the asserted right, the direct clinical-regulation function, copying limitation and stated confidentiality controls provide a sufficient facial connection on this record under Doe's bounded method. Unnecessary dissemination, the reported releases and an actual overbroad request can support an appropriately grounded particular challenge; they do not establish the invalidity of general inspection authority here.

Their strongest opposing consideration is Lawall's controlling treatment of an authorized outside transfer, reinforced here by concrete release evidence. Their distinction is that this inspection clause itself identifies a direct health-care regulatory task, whereas the independent outside authorizations in Lawall had not been identified; they do not claim that confidentiality alone answers every legal permission. They refuse P1's extension as their affirmative rationale and P3's further proceedings because the facial challenge does not establish improper operation of the whole inspection permission. They would not restore the district court's universal compelling-interest requirement. No general waiver, absolute statutory secrecy, absence of leak evidence or insistence on completed public disclosure is part of their position.

## Rules and adversarial alternatives

**P1 — constitutional timing and force.** After meaningful announcement, the reasonableness of the delay and entry depends on the circumstances known when force begins. Wilson protects the opportunity for peaceful entry; Richards requires particular facts and rational inferences for threatened destruction and rejects a drug-case categorical excuse. Ramirez rejects a higher announcement threshold because the door must be damaged, while independently restricting the means and amount of force. The time needed to preserve the evidence, as well as the opportunity to hear and respond, matters. A clock interval by itself establishes neither compliance nor violation.

**P2 — this entry.** This entry was reasonable. Loud daytime notice at this small apartment afforded a practical opportunity to acknowledge the officers. The unsupported alternative that Banks might not have heard them is a possible innocent explanation, not knowledge that nullifies the objectively reasonable inference. Neither the shower nor evidence found afterward supplies that inference. The door breach used to obtain the authorized entry is not shown to have exceeded that purpose.

**P3 — independent statutory route.** Section 3109's ordinary notice-and-refused-admission route is satisfied. Refusal can be reasonably inferred from failure to respond after a meaningful opportunity under the actual circumstances; an express verbal refusal is not indispensable. Here the loud announced demand, small dwelling, daytime execution and elapsed interval permit that inference. The disposable evidence is contextual support for assessing the delay, not a substitute for notice or a declaration that every silence is refusal. The statute is satisfied through notice and implied refusal; no liberation predicate is asserted, and an independent statutory destruction exception is unnecessary to the disposition.

**P4 — remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's suppression ruling on entry and remand for proceedings consistent with the conclusion that this entry violated neither the Fourth Amendment nor section 3109. The entry ground does not defeat the conditional conviction. The mandate preserves the independent interrogation rulings outside the grant and permits only any otherwise live proceedings; it does not itself determine an unpresented sentencing question.

Loud afternoon notice, the small premises and the entire elapsed interval materially differentiate this case from an unannounced drug entry, a barely completed announcement or execution when occupants are likely asleep. The conclusion cannot be transferred by the interval alone to a materially different dwelling, time or response. ## Common legal propositions and supported rival paths

**P1 — association.** Apply Anderson–Burdick to the actual burden, precise state interests and fit in the whole nomination system. The one-year disaffiliation requirement, as applied to this party-primary candidacy, is a reasonable, nondiscriminatory selection rule rather than a severe burden. It applies to changes from any other qualified party, requires a period of genuine separation before access to the new party's selection, and leaves association, advocacy and eligibility after that period. It need not allow every late change of electoral allegiance. Its burden is real but proportionate to preserving distinct nomination channels and discouraging temporary party switching to exploit a primary.

Storer supplies substantial support because it sustained a one-year disaffiliation period protecting California's distinct candidate-selection routes. Its independent-candidate application does not mechanically answer this primary case; the present regulation must be examined on its own terms. Timmons confirms that official nomination access may bear reasonable preventive conditions, without making stability an unlimited justification. Tashjian and Eu protect actual party choices, while Jones prohibits compelled outsider control of an objecting party's nomination. This rule does not replace a party's electorate with outsiders or dictate its platform or leadership; it sets a neutral prefiling separation condition for someone seeking its public primary. Those distinctions sustain the rule even when candidate and party members wish to associate now.

**P2 — equal protection.** The earlier calendar date confronting partisan candidates does not, by itself, establish unconstitutional discrimination. Party candidates enter a primary which makes the legally operative selection; independents use a different route to the general-election ballot. Requiring disaffiliation in advance of each route's relevant selection process is reasonably related to keeping the routes distinct and preventing strategic crossover. Van Susteren's comparison of approximately twenty-three months versus thirteen months before the general election identifies an actual calendar difference, but omits the intervening primary and its necessary prefiling stage. The relevant analysis considers both that difference and each route's function; it does not end with the categorical assertion that party and independent candidates can never be compared. On this record the difference is justified and supplies no separate equal-protection violation.

**P3 — disposition.** Affirm the defense summary judgment on the granted First and Fourteenth Amendment grounds. Deny the requested prospective declaratory and ballot-access relief against this application of the twelve-month rule. The limited holding concerns this requirement and this system; it creates no unlimited power to impose longer periods or every other affiliation restriction.

**Rival association path.** Reverse because a year before filing excludes a willing candidate from a willing party long before the general election and burdens the party's central choice more than the State's generalized stability interest warrants. Tashjian, Eu and Jones protect nomination choices; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's Timmons positions require more than pointing to endorsement or other possible candidates. The strongest objection to affirmance is that the State calls the rule protection against outsiders even when the new association welcomes the candidate.

The response sustaining P1 is that the actual temporal rule structures admission to a nomination process before its competitive selection; it does not bar a consenting already-selected nominee solely because another party also chose him, as in Timmons, or force an electorate on an objecting party, as in Jones. Disaffiliation periods can address opportunistic allegiance before the particular manipulation occurs. Storer and the preventive-rule principle retained by Burdick and Timmons permit that direct connection without proof that this candidate personally planned a raid. This record supplies no burden from additional requirements that makes this one-year condition a severe obstacle beyond the identified inability to seek the chosen nomination immediately. That is a bounded merits assessment, not a burden of producing unavailable research documents.

**Rival equal-protection path.** Reverse because the partisan route practically requires a substantially earlier commitment than the independent route. Its strength is the longer total separation before the eventual office-selecting election. Its weakness is treating that election as if the party primary did no separate selection work. The rule's link to the primary and its filing process explains the differential; no finding that independents and party candidates enjoy identical chances is needed. A remand merely to invent a future candidacy, prove the obvious calendar difference again, or seek a more elaborate record is not warranted on the supplied live dispute and published facts.

## Competing legal constructions

**V1 — reserved use free of unilateral licensing.** Maryland's riverbed title and Virginia's protected use beyond the boundary coexist. The Award names Virginia and reserves use essential to full enjoyment of its riparian ownership as part of an interstate settlement; the protected shore-owner rights extend to Virginia, its subdivisions and citizens exercising appurtenant rights. Conditioning those uses on Maryland's discretionary prior permission would make the express reservation subject to the other party's unilateral veto. The navigation and proper-use conditions specify real substantive limits, but do not allocate Maryland sole authority to determine Virginia's needs or make its permission the source of the right. The historically mutual regulation provisions reinforce the distinction between title and all-purpose unilateral control. The 1958 savings clause preserves, rather than recreates or enlarges, that allocation.

**M1 — protected use subject to compatible regulation.** The strongest contrary construction treats the rights as enforceable riparian entitlements on Maryland's side of a sovereign boundary, rather than a transfer of regulatory jurisdiction. The instruments speak of ownership, privileges and use, and expressly condition the use on navigation and Maryland's proper enjoyment; they do not expressly exempt it from Maryland's otherwise reasonable police-power administration. Protecting the entitlement against a prohibitory or incompatible permit decision can give the reservation effect without eliminating advance review of whether a proposed use satisfies its limits. Article Eighth's specific mutual-consent categories demonstrate that the parties knew how to allocate regulatory authority expressly. The 1958 savings clause preserves existing rights, so it does not resolve that original meaning by creating an exemption.

**Strongest objection to V1.** Private property rights ordinarily coexist with public regulation, and a licensing procedure need not be a veto if constrained by the superior compact right. The answer supporting V1 is the interstate character of the Award's express reservation to Virginia: its purpose is not just to create a revocable benefit within Maryland's unilateral licensing system. Maryland can enforce the retained limits through lawful interstate and federal channels, so immunity from its prior permit requirement does not erase those limits.

**Strongest objection to M1.** A permit regime that lets Maryland decide whether Virginia needs an improvement subordinates a sovereign reservation to the other State and can defeat the bargain in practice. The answer supporting M1 is that the entitlement is superior law constraining every permit decision: Maryland cannot withhold a protected reasonable use merely to suppress Virginia's growth or obtain bargaining power. Judicial protection against such interference remains available. The alternative supports compatible regulation, not the unrestricted discretionary regime Virginia describes.

**A1 — acquiescence not established.** Maryland has not shown that Virginia surrendered an instrument-secured exemption, if V1 correctly identifies one. New Jersey requires supported adverse exercise and knowledge or sufficiently open conduct supporting notice, with acquiescence; it does not turn routine cooperation into automatic displacement of a sovereign entitlement. Earlier permit applications are relevant, but their significance must be considered with official knowledge, protest and the first actual refusal. The supported course contains protests and contested control rather than a sufficiently established accepted surrender. No fixed prescriptive period, mandatory formal-protest rule or categorical bar to acquiring rights by prescription is created. New Jersey's precise territorial-acquisition burden is not mechanically converted into a new universal burden for every regulatory-right dispute.

The stronger opposing acquiescence inference combines repeated applications, receipt of permits, successive amendments and official knowledge into practical acceptance of Maryland's authority. Its weakness is that cooperating to obtain a project while contesting sovereign authority is not the same as conceding the claimed entitlement, and the supported protests prevent treating this record as an unbroken settled construction. A disposition based on some additional episode of accepted adverse control would require that actual episode's source; its absence from the extract is not evidence that no such document exists.

The important preserved statutory distinctions are these:

- Section 323(a) covers national committees, their covered controlled entities and agents acting for them, and solicitation/receipt/direction as well as spending. A person's private act outside that representative capacity is different. Section 323(b) regulates the specified federal-election activities of state/local committees and the other stated groups, not all their state activity.
- The first two federal-election-activity classes are registration within 120 days and identification, turnout or generic activity in a federal-ballot election. The third is a candidate-referencing public communication promoting/supporting or attacking/opposing a federal candidate for office. The fourth is services of an employee who spends more than 25 percent of compensated time in a month on federal-election activity. The state-only communication, undesignated state-candidate contribution, state/local convention and state-only grassroots-material exclusions remain distinct.
- Levin allocation applies only to the first two classes, with cumulative no-federal-candidate-reference, broadcast-cost, state-law, $10,000-per-donor/committee/year, controlled-source aggregation, own-fundraising, transfer and joint-fundraising conditions. The statutory state-candidate-only broadcast exception and §323(e)(3) attendance/speaking exception remain operative. This is no general $10,000 soft-money exemption.
- Section 323(c)'s federal-fund fundraising-cost rule is statutory context. The clean record establishes no independent preserved challenge to it. Sections 102 and 103 have their distinct state-committee amount, reporting thresholds, itemization and building-fund/nonpreemption provisions; no independent challenge is created merely by describing them.
- Section 323(d) reaches specified §501(c) organizations that engage in the defined federal-election spending, and specified §527 organizations, with the political-committee, state/local-party and authorized state/local-candidate exceptions. Its words cover **any** funds. Federal-compliant donations cannot silently be read out of the statute.
- Section 323(e)(1)(A) addresses federal elections, whereas (B) imports source and amount restrictions for other elections without importing every federal reporting requirement. Its own-state/local-candidacy exception, attendance/speaking/featured-guest permission, general charitable solicitation exception, and individual-only $20,000 annual specific-solicitation exception retain their separate terms. Section 323(f) has the own-state/local-election, state/local-candidates-only exception.
- The §201 primary category has broadcast/cable/satellite, identified-candidate and 60-/30-day conditions, plus the relevant-electorate/50,000-reception condition for nonpresidential candidates. News/commentary/editorial, qualifying debate/forum, already-covered expenditure/independent-expenditure and regulatory exceptions retain their different predicates, including the media ownership/control limit and the restriction on the regulatory exemption. The fallback is conditional and cumulative; its no-plausible-meaning-other-than-an-exhortation-to-vote condition is enacted law, not disposable surplusage.
- Sections 203 and 204 concern treasury sources and a separate statutory nonprofit exception; §204 withdraws that exception for its defined targeted communications. Direct individual-origin requirements, prohibited-source attribution, the segregated individual-only account, contracts, and tax-law limits remain distinct. Constitutional Day/MCFL protection is not repealed by the withdrawal of a broader statutory exception.
- Section 202 classifies actually coordinated electioneering disbursements or contracts as contributions/expenditures; it is not itself a universal ban. Section 211 defines independence. Section 214(a) separately concerns coordination with parties; (b) repeals the identified rule; (c) commands replacement regulations without an agreement/formal-collaboration prerequisite; (d) incorporates contribution/expenditure definitions without newly enacting the preexisting direct/indirect-payment words.
- Section 213 concerns the postnomination cycle choice between independent expenditure and the special coordinated-party allowance. Ordinary $5,000 multicandidate contributions/coordinated spending remain available after independent spending. The single-committee and transfer/assignment/receipt rules implement that choice. Section 318 has no own-earnings, consent or independent-choice exception for persons seventeen or younger.

For clarity, the Levin own-fund rule excludes funds supplied by another state/district/local committee of any state party, a national or national congressional committee, their representative officers/agents, and their directly or indirectly established, financed, maintained or controlled entities. The specially permitted nonfederal amounts may not be solicited, received, directed, transferred or spent by or in the name of §323(a) or (e) actors, apart from the expressly retained §323(e)(3) attendance/speaking permission. The four state-activity exclusions do not convert registration or turnout/generic activity into excluded activity merely by naming only a state candidate: the state-only public-communication exclusion itself excludes activity in classes (i) and (ii). Section 323(b)'s covered actors also include associations or similar groups of state/local candidates or officeholders. The party tax-exempt recipient rule includes a §501(c) organization exempt under §501(a), or an applicant for that determination, that makes federal-election expenditures/disbursements; the separate §527 branch carries its own listed exceptions.

A qualifying candidate debate/forum must comply with Commission regulations; the separate promotional exception covers a communication solely promoting it and made by or for its sponsor. Section 203's statutory nonprofit exception concerns §501(c)(4) entities, including applicants for that status, and §527(e)(1) organizations using exclusively funds provided directly by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents. ## 3. Title I — selected grounds and rival lawful paths

### A. National-party money, §323(a)

**Selected sustaining ground (Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer):** Reverse the decree's invalidation and sustain the national-party restriction on this facial record. The regulated transaction is receipt or solicitation of a donation into the national party's federal-candidate financing institution, including the circumvention paths documented through federal-officeholder fundraising, donor understandings, fundraising credits, allocation and state-party transfers. That record supplies more than party identity, public dislike of wealth, or ordinary access. The danger can arise when funds are raised; their ultimate use for a state project does not erase the donor's demonstrated relation to that fundraising channel. Source/amount/reporting compliance at receipt closes the demonstrated route without a ceiling on how much compliant money the party may spend.

The government's fit burden is met here by that functional record, the meaningful retained individual/PAC contribution channels and increased national-party allowance, and the documented operation of substantial federal fundraising. The finding is not that losing soft money costs nothing, that all fundraising losses can necessarily be replaced, or that electoral success proves adequate association. A second unlimited account would preserve the same covered solicitation and receipt mechanism; nominal allocation of its later spending is not an adequate answer to it. The $60,000 alternative illustrates a different legislative line, not proof that this line destroys meaningful association. No particular donor is adjudged corrupt.

**Selected contrary ground (Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas):** Affirm the invalidation of the nonfederal/mixed-use receipt and spending sweep and reverse the residual approval of the national party's independently controlled candidate-advocacy spending. Protect actual candidate transfers, candidate-controlled financial substitutes and genuine conduits under existing law; reject treating every donation and every use by the national institution as such a transfer. The record supports regulation of officeholder solicitation and tacit conduits but does not justify the complete new channel/source restriction for the party's own genuinely independent and nonfederal expression. Mariani's accepted distinction between direct control and materially different party arrangements, Lamb's separate fit requirement and these Justices' Colorado opinions supply the ground. Their relief does not invalidate ordinary FECA contribution limits or immunize sham accounts.

**Adversarial test and join limits:** The best argument against the sustaining path is the real loss of the chosen party channel for state activity and independent issue speech. The sustaining Justices answer at the receipt transaction and actual institutional fundraising record, not by calling those expenditures coordinated. The best argument against the contrary path is that a use-based limitation leaves the proven solicitation channel intact. The three answer with the narrower direct-officeholder and genuine-conduit controls and insist that the additional institutional presumption needs its own operational justification. Neither path may claim that ordinary access itself is corruption. The three will not join a rule validating all party money on a label; the five will not join a rule requiring a completed bribe or a formal earmark.

### B. State/local federal-election activity, §323(b)

**Registration and turnout/generic activity; Levin conditions.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse invalidation of the first two classes and sustain the challenged Levin conditions. The documented national-to-state transfers and donor directions exploit shared federal-election machinery, not merely the statistical fact that a state voter may also vote federally. The limited activity categories, express state-only exclusions and modified allocation route preserve substantial state activity. Requiring each committee to raise its own Levin funds, and restricting transfers and joint fundraising, prevents multiplication of the per-committee allowance into the very federal channel Congress closes. The restrictions do not prohibit the separate §323(e)(3) attendance/speaking permission. The facial holding does not decide a materially different demonstrated application or treat every local expenditure as federally connected.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm invalidation of these broad categories and their operative restrictions. A federal candidate on the same ballot, a 120-day period, and generic party identification can include independently chosen local activity without actual candidate resources or a demonstrated conduit. Levin preserves some funding but burdens chosen-party pooling and joint fundraising; the government cannot cure an inadequately fitted underlying category merely by offering a constrained alternative. Their ground accepts the actual transfer/donor-understanding evidence and permits tailored regulation of those transactions; it rejects the categorical reach, not all allocation or contribution law.

**Candidate-promoting public communications, class (iii).** The five sustaining Justices would affirm the district judgment on this class. The words promoting/supporting or attacking/opposing a candidate **for that office**, in this party-financing setting, provide a determinate candidate-election connection. Mere mention, issue inquiry or a statement that has only an incidental electoral effect does not by itself satisfy that formulation. It is a source-and-receipt safeguard on the documented party financing route, not a declaration that independent party expression is coordinated or that candidate gratitude alone justifies an expenditure ceiling. They reject both a mandatory list of electoral slogans and a subjective-intent prosecution.

The three contrary Justices would reverse this affirmance to the extent it subjects the party's independently controlled or jointly expressed message to the new source restriction without an actual resource/conduit connection. Their Colorado I/II and Bartlett separate positions require speaker-supplied electoral direction for independent expression and distinguish expression from candidate-controlled resources. A message's helping a candidate and a party's sophistication do not supply the missing connection. Genuine contributions remain regulable.

**Employee services, class (iv).** Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse invalidation and sustain this facial class: workers materially engaged in the shared federal campaign infrastructure are an identifiable resource channel, and a monthly more-than-quarter threshold leaves staff with only limited federal work outside this particular rule. They accept a practical payroll boundary as part of the supported integrated-operation safeguard; they do not rely on absence of a separately briefed objection.

O'Connor and Souter would vacate the class-(iv) judgment and remand for a distinct fit determination under Lamb. The rule subjects the whole covered salary to federal funding when federal work crosses 25 percent; the existing competing legal conclusions do not connect that full-payroll consequence to the particular circumvention mechanism and meaningful state-party operation under the governing burden. Neither Leon's indirect-effect-only rationale nor automatic validation of the other classes decides this question. Adequate existing evidence may suffice; no new study or evidentiary hearing is commanded. If the government cannot justify the actual operation, the court must provide corresponding relief, with applications severed only where statutory independence permits.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas prefer affirmance of class-(iv) invalidation: charging the entire salary to the federal account reaches substantial nonfederal work without a sufficiently fitted connection. They also **join the preceding vacatur/remand ground and remedy as a narrower disposition** if broader invalidation is not adopted. This express remedial agreement does not concede constitutionality or allow the district court to put the proof burden on parties. The alternatives are adjudicative choices, not unresolved source possession. No finding is made that the whole historical record contains no payroll evidence.

**Competing considerations across the classes:** The sustaining path must answer local burdens and changed allocation, not simply cite federal-election influence. The contrary path must answer the demonstrated shared fundraising/transfers and the impossibility of closing the national account while leaving that same channel untouched. Each class receives its own answer above. Class (iv)'s distinct remedial convergence is not extended to classes (i)–(iii).

### C. Party dealings with tax-exempt groups, §323(d)

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the blanket invalidation **only for nonfederal-fund applications** and affirm relief for solicitation, donation or direction of federally compliant funds. Covered election-spending tax-exempt groups can become substitute party conduits; the documented use of a nominal ballot organization to finance partisan registration and the broader satellite-organization record supply the preventive mechanism. The specified recipient classes, representative-capacity limitation and express exceptions matter. A restriction on noncompliant funds can operate independently and is materially directed to avoidance of Title I's lawful source/amount/reporting rules.

The statute nevertheless says any funds. The government has not supplied a sufficient distinct justification for barring donations already satisfying the federal safeguards merely because the recipient also engages in federal-election activity. A different PAC operated by that recipient does not automatically answer the lost chosen channel under Day and Mariani. Section 401 permits severance of the federal-compliant applications; it does not permit pretending that Congress wrote a soft-money-only definition. The proposed mandate would expressly identify the protected applications and the surviving nonfederal applications, retaining the existing recipient and actor exceptions.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm invalidation of §323(d) in full. They agree that hard-money applications fail, but also find the nonfederal sweep insufficiently fitted: covered recipients may conduct independent issue advocacy or genuine state activity, and the provision disallows the party's assistance without requiring that particular transaction to finance a federal candidate or function as a conduit. They accept the ballot-organization example as a real circumvention case and would preserve ordinary false-source, earmark and actual-resource enforcement. They will join the narrower protection for federal-compliant funds without joining approval of the balance.

The strongest sustaining argument is fungibility and predictable substitution of tax-exempt entities for party accounts. The strongest contrary argument is the complete bar on lawful assistance to an organization conducting mixed activities. The five's application severance answers the additional hard-money burden and leaves a source-specific anti-evasion rule; the three regard purpose-independent nonfederal coverage as a remaining substantial defect. Neither side invents a statutory earmarking exception or a finding that every tax-exempt group is a party affiliate.

### D. Federal officeholders and candidates, §323(e)

All eight Associates would affirm the district's facial sustaining judgment, on the ground stated here, while preserving the enacted exceptions. The relevant connection is the federal candidate's or officeholder's own solicitation, direction, receipt, transfer or spending, including action through the specified agents and controlled entities. The record of large donations obtained with the official's personal intervention and credited to that official supplies a direct obligation/circumvention risk even where the ultimate recipient spends in a state election. This does not depend on finding an express bargain or treating ordinary political access as a sufficient interest.

The federal-election branch retains federal source, amount and reporting rules. The other-election branch retains source and amount restrictions, not all federal reporting rules. The own-state/local-candidacy exception applies to an individual who is or was also such a candidate; the solicitation, receipt or spending must be solely in connection with that election, lawful under state law, and refer only to that candidate, another candidate for the same state/local office, or both. Attendance, speaking and featured-guest status remain permissible. General solicitations for eligible §501(c) organizations may not specify use and exclude entities principally devoted to the registration, voter-identification, get-out-the-vote or generic campaign activities described in §301(20)(A)(i)–(ii). The separate specific-solicitation route concerns funds for those activities or entities principally devoted to them, reaches only individuals, and permits no more than $20,000 solicited from each in a calendar year. These accommodations preserve ordinary participation and charitable association while preventing the personal federal fundraising route from becoming an unlimited channel.

For Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, this is a **distinct supported ground**, not a withdrawal of their objections to §§323(a), (b), (d) or (f). Their Lamb joins accept actual resources and tacit conduits; the official's own fundraising intervention supplies the materially closer donor-official connection missing from a party label alone. Their Mariani/Beaumont dissents do not immunize every business-source channel. They accept this facial source-and-amount safeguard with its unusually explicit solicitation exceptions on the present record, not a universal power to restrict an official's issue speech.

The strongest contrary path would protect solicitations for projects unrelated to the official's own campaign as pure association. The adopted answer is that the supported fundraising obligation attaches to the official's request, not only to the spending destination. A law barring attendance, endorsements, or ordinary issue cooperation would raise a different burden; the enacted permissions prevent that from being this case. No unpresented solicitations are adjudged unlawful.

### E. State/local officeholders' federal-candidate communications, §323(f)

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm, applying the same qualified candidate-for-office meaning used for class (iii). This rule closes the state-candidate account route to the federal candidate-promotion financing that the party safeguards address. Its actor and communication predicates, plus the state/local-only own-election exception, limit the reach. It does not prohibit all state-candidate issue speech or all references to federal officials. The government must prove the statutory predicates in enforcement; a state title alone supplies no coordination finding.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the sustaining judgment for independently controlled and joint expression lacking an actual candidate-resource/conduit connection. Their narrower lawful alternative is control of actual transfer or candidate-directed financial substitutes, leaving the state candidate's own message protected. They agree that the statutory own-election exception must be retained but do not regard it as curing the independent-expression sweep. The government's best counterargument is substitution of a state candidate's unrestricted account for a prohibited federal channel; the three require the real resource connection rather than the fact of praise or criticism alone.

## 4. Electioneering category and treasury sources

### A. Primary definition and the distinct facial treasury challenge

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse invalidation of the primary definition and sustain the challenged §§203–204 scheme against the presented categorical/facial attack **with the constitutional limits below expressly preserved**. The enacted medium, calendar, identified-candidate, and relevant-electorate elements are objectively ascertainable. Maine does not establish a constitutional magic-words prerequisite, and Bartlett's invalid general influence formula does not itself invalidate every more definite legislative category. The record independently supports the concentration of candidate-centered broadcasts in the chosen periods and electorate. The rationale is not that every ad influencing an election is suppressible.

The treasury rule requires its own source justification under Austin's surviving independent-election-spending setting and MCFL/Day, not Beaumont's direct-contribution holding. Austin's concern with a treasury accumulated through the business structure rather than voluntary political support retains force in that distinct setting; it is not an equality-of-influence interest or a finding that every independent supporter corrupts a candidate. The evidence of commercial/union financing of candidate-centered advertisements through nominally issue-oriented sponsors, protection of economic payers whose funds were accumulated for other purposes, and the lawful voluntary political funding route support rejecting a facial exemption for all covered entities. Corporate form or a nominal alternative fund alone would not do so. Genuine voluntary nonprofits retain the actual-characteristics protection in part B below, and Bartlett continues to protect genuine independent issue advocacy against an inadequately fitted prohibition. A particular communication or materially different organization may establish that constitutional application without first showing a right to invalidate the whole statute.

These five will not join a declaration that every communication satisfying the calendar/candidate criteria may constitutionally be barred from every treasury. Their facial judgment is not an automatic merits rejection of a future concrete genuine-issue or Day application. Nor do they add a textual pending-legislation exemption, reclassify all issue advertising as contributions, require speakers to obtain advisory permission, or state that choosing a different date/name/funding pool entails no burden. The new category's facial adequacy is a distinct proposition from the validity of every source-restricted application.

**Adversarial comparison:** Leon's account of genuine legislative advertisements, including the Fast Track call-to-action example, shows a real protected-speech concern. The competing statistical denominators and coding show why neither the unqualified 1998 estimates, the rejected 2000 0.6-percent estimate, nor the disputed treatment of 17 percent can be treated as an uncontested constitutional measurement. The five accept the objective concentration evidence and the statute's substantial legitimate operation while leaving constitutional application protection available. The stronger facial-remedy alternative would find the demonstrated issue-speech category substantially overbroad; these five decline to invalidate the whole primary category because the category also performs lawful informational and election-finance work and the record does not justify collapsing the distinct restrictions into one universal prohibition. They give no dispositive weight to public willingness to silence a speaker or to a mere capacity to edit out a candidate.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would retain relief from the expanded treasury prohibition through §401 application severance of §203, and reverse the corresponding sustaining portions of §§203–204; they would not invalidate the primary category's independently valid reporting operation. Candidate mention, timing, geographic reception and political impact do not establish speaker-supplied electoral direction or actual candidate resources. Their Maine and Bartlett separate positions, Colorado protection of independent expression, and the chosen-channel objections in Mariani and Beaumont support treating forced use of another pool as a real burden. Congress's objective line solves notice more readily than constitutional breadth. The documented genuine legislative advocacy is not made suppressible by an election window.

Their remedy leaves the preexisting lawful express-advocacy and actual-contribution scheme intact, leaves Austin within its existing scope, preserves applicable disclosure on its separately justified ground, and does not grant corporations immunity for genuine candidate financing. They do not rest on a constitutional list of canonical words. The strongest objection is that electoral advocacy can intentionally avoid such words; their answer permits functional explicit electoral direction supplied by the message but refuses to have election timing or candidate gratitude generate that direction. Their position on reporting is recorded separately rather than inferred from this source-restriction result.

### B. Nonprofit protection and §204

All eight Associates adopt the following proposition: withdrawing §203's specified **statutory** nonprofit exception does not withdraw constitutional protection for an actual voluntary political association under MCFL and Day. The inquiry concerns economic claimants, material commercial accumulation, material corporate/union funding or control, and whether the advocacy resources reflect voluntary political support. Incidental revenue and the lack of a formal refusal policy alone do not disqualify an association. Conversely, nonprofit status, ideological mission or funding from some individuals does not by itself immunize a materially commercial conduit.

The five sustaining the facial scheme would modify the lower decree so that it does not suggest §204 is facially void or that constitutional protection depends exclusively on a literal historical MCFL checklist. The retained declaration protects the Day/MCFL class; application to a named organization requires its actual characteristics and the actual communication. The statutory exception can otherwise be withdrawn in its stated targeted-communication category. There is no finding that every named plaintiff qualifies or that its direct candidate contributions share that protection.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join that nonprofit proposition and its protection but do not join approval of the expanded treasury reach in part A. As an independently sufficient narrower position, they agree that §204 cannot be enforced against the protected Day/MCFL class. They do not assert a categorical ideological-nonprofit contribution exemption rejected in Stengel. The government's strongest response—protecting against use of a nonprofit to pass commercial funds into election advertising—answers material conduit financing, not a mere checklist omission. The contrary claim that §204 itself eliminates all constitutional exemptions is rejected by every Associate.

### C. Fallback and the district's deletion

For the five sustaining the primary definition, the statutory activation condition for the fallback is not met by their own disposition. They would vacate the district's fallback declaration and its deletion of the final substantive limiter as unnecessary to the surviving primary scheme. They support no operative fallback rule in that event.

Their alternative position, **only if the final Court judgment activates the fallback**, is to sustain the enacted cumulative definition under a construction requiring candidate promotion/opposition plus a communication whose message, considered as a whole with only properly limited context, admits no plausible meaning other than an exhortation to vote for or against a specific candidate. The enacted final condition cannot be deleted to broaden the prohibition. The fallback is a broadcast/cable/satellite definition; it does not import the primary definition's 30-/60-day windows or its geographic threshold. Maine's actual cumulative construction and Bartlett's definite-coverage/issue-speech protection supply the comparison; the saved §100.22(b) sentence remains effective. This is conditional compatibility information, not an additional entered holding.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas select **application relief against the expanded §203 treasury prohibition while preserving the primary definition's independently valid §201 reporting use**. Section 401 expressly preserves valid applications; their disposition does not declare the primary definition itself constitutionally insufficient for its reporting regulation. They therefore also vacate the lower fallback declaration and deletion as unnecessary on their selected remedy. A ruling against a treasury application is not silently converted into a judgment activating a replacement definition for all other statutory uses. If a different final disposition instead holds the primary definition constitutionally insufficient in the sense specified by the enacted activation clause, the clause must be honored; §401 does not authorize disregarding an actually satisfied activation condition.

Their separately recorded **conditional** fallback position rejects the district's deletion and requires the complete enacted text, with explicit or functionally explicit electoral direction supplied by the communication itself. Ordinary context may identify the candidate or explain the words, but may not generate electoral direction missing from the message. The enacted no-plausible-meaning-other-than-an-exhortation-to-vote limiter permits that construction. If that construction is not adopted, they oppose the expanded nonexpress treasury application. They do not support a fallback triggered solely by finding a protected particular treasury application, do not import primary windows or geography into fallback text, and do not permit bare promotion/support/attack/opposition to replace its cumulative limiter. The five's conditional construction above permits properly limited contextual interpretation; the three withhold assent where context supplies rather than explains the electoral direction. That is a real join barrier, not a claim that one formulation automatically controls the other.

Thus no Associate's selected disposition enters a new operative fallback holding. The conditional positions are available only if reconciliation and final compatibility establish an activating judgment. Primary reporting remains independently supported on the finite informational ground in section 7; it is not enlarged to fallback's temporally broader category by an invalidation of a different burden.

## 5. Coordination, independence, party choice and minors

### A. Sections 202, 211 and 214(a), (d)

All eight agree that an actual candidate/party request for a financial resource or genuine spending collaboration can be shown without a signed agreement, a formal contract between the political actors, or proof of a completed bribe. Ordinary affiliation, endorsement, policy discussion, vendor identity or a former employment relationship alone does not establish that spending connection. Clifton's policy-inquiry holding and Colorado I's actual independence rule remain effective. Nothing in §214(c) requires treating every contact as coordination.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the facial sustaining judgment on §202 and §214(a), applying the actual expenditure connection. A third party's purchase responding to a candidate's or party's actual campaign spending request may supply a contribution even without express words urging a vote; the contribution classification is functional, not merely semantic. The objection that all coordinated communications require express advocacy fails under Akins's actual candidate-financing rule, Colorado II's supported coordinated-spending holding and Lamb. This does not decide that every jointly developed message is constitutionally identical or that every regulatory content/conduct category is valid. Policy inquiries and independently edited issue publications remain outside coordination absent the necessary spending relation.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the blanket §202/§214(a) approvals and remand for declarations protecting the association's own independently controlled or jointly expressed message while leaving actual candidate/party-controlled financial substitutes and genuine conduits regulable. Kennedy's Colorado I/II position, joined by both, is the narrower-party-expression and chosen-channel ground; Bartlett and Clifton reinforce that mere issue collaboration is insufficient. Their Lamb joins permit real resources and tacit conduits and preclude a formal-agreement prerequisite. They do not invalidate the entire coordination mechanism or impose expenditure scrutiny on all cash and in-kind candidate contributions. As narrower remedial agreement, they join a declaration of the actual-resource rule and the inquiry/identity exclusions, but not a categorical approval of all joint messages.

All eight would reject a freestanding facial attack on §211's definition merely because it includes party cooperation/request/suggestion alongside candidate connections. The definition does not by itself make every communication outside it constitutionally unprotected. The stated express-advocacy element and absence-of-actual-coordination conditions remain; the preceding constitutional limits on particular burdens remain independent. The three's broader joint-expression view is not surrendered by accepting the statutory definition.

All eight would affirm rejection of the asserted §214(d) amendment challenge on its actual statutory ground: incorporation of FECA's contribution/expenditure definitions does not newly enact the preexisting direct/indirect-payment language or turn every policy communication into a contribution. An attack on those old words is not converted into a new BCRA amendment challenge. This ground neither validates every old application nor negates the constitutional remedies stated above.

**Alternatives and limits:** The government's strongest case concerns financed campaign work arranged in fact but without a formal agreement; the challengers' strongest case concerns ordinary lobbying or a publisher's editorial collaboration. The five sustain actual-connection applications and the facial statutory mechanism while reserving a concrete constitutional characterization of materially different joint speech. The three require protection of that joint-expression category in the present declaration. No Associate adopts a rule that common vendors, former employees or policy questions automatically establish coordination. The January rule's conjunctive payment/content/conduct structure and its inquiry safe harbor defeat the premise that no clarifying rule exists, without deciding that regulation in this statutory direct appeal.

### B. Section 214(b) repeal and (c) rulemaking command

All eight would affirm the threshold disposition of the regulatory-vacuum claim and the unripe attack on unspecified future applications of the rulemaking command. The promised replacement rules became effective February 3, 2003; the claimed unregulated interval no longer supplies prospective relief against the completed repeal. The command does not itself designate every contact as coordinated, and the actual rules preserve an express policy-inquiry safe harbor. Their validity and concrete application require the appropriate regulatory challenge, not a presumption in this statutory appeal that all future contact investigations will be unconstitutional.

This conclusion does not rest on compulsory use of an advisory opinion, on the absence of criminal sanctions, on the proposition that a losing legal theory defeats injury, or on treating an operative present duty as nonbinding. Maine and Lee forbid those shortcuts. The statutory no-formal-agreement premise is addressed at its actual-resource level in part A; no additional general merits holding about the replacement rule follows from dismissal here. The strongest contrary argument is present chilling from an unavoidable statutory instruction. The operative clarification, separate actual-connection predicates and absence of a specified protected contact forced into coordination by the instruction itself distinguish that allegation from Maine's ceased recurring communications. A later supported present application remains reviewable.

### C. Section 213 choice

All eight would affirm invalidation of §213 as a unit. The provision conditions exercise of the special coordinated-party allowance on forgoing constitutionally protected independent expenditure, and conversely removes that allowance after independent expenditure. It is incorrect to say every ordinary contribution is forbidden: the $5,000 multicandidate route remains. That residual route does not justify the prohibition on independent spending after use of the otherwise lawful allowance or the burden on meaningful chosen-party association.

The government's strongest alternative is that a party's prior coordination makes later independence unreliable and that the allowance is an optional statutory benefit. Colorado I requires actual independence/connection rather than a status presumption; Colorado II does not authorize eliminating independent speech as the price of permitted coordinated support. The record supplies no fitted basis for conclusively converting every later transaction into coordination. Genuine coordination can be regulated on its facts. For Stevens and Ginsburg, their older institutional-channel dissent does not override the current Colorado I holding; their Colorado II and Lamb joins support real preventive controls without this conclusive choice. For O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, Day/Daggett additionally identify the expressive cost of an adverse legal consequence attached to protected activity, without converting every funding condition into an unconstitutional burden. Souter and Breyer's Colorado I/II positions preserve both independent expression and supported actual-coordination limits.

Section 401 does not authorize creating a different one-sided option. The single-committee and transfer/assignment/receipt restrictions implement the reciprocal choice and have no independently adjudicated survival function on this record once that choice fails. The proposed relief removes §315(d)(4) as added by §213, preserving the preexisting ordinary contribution and special coordinated-allowance rules with their own lawful limits. No new allowance, donor refund, past-election remedy or automatic finding of independence is ordered.

### D. Section 318 minors

All eight would affirm invalidation of the complete ban. Persons seventeen or younger retain political speech and associational interests; a rule forbidding even a voluntary gift from a minor's own resources is not closely drawn to the supported interest in preventing parental use of children as false donors. Mariani's independent true-source rule, ordinary attribution/conduit controls and the requirement of actual voluntary contribution address the identified evasion directly. The ability to volunteer or make independent expenditures does not replace the chosen contribution channel under Day, Russell and Lamb.

The strongest sustaining argument is the difficulty of distinguishing parental money and influence from a child's genuine gift, together with an administrable age line. A complete ban reaching independent decisions and every lawful amount does not fit that risk merely because enforcement of narrower controls takes work. This is a legal fit conclusion, not a finding that no parental circumvention has ever occurred or that all age distinctions are unconstitutional. The common ground fails the governing contribution standard; no Associate needs to adopt Leon's separate theory that every total contribution ban automatically receives expenditure-level strict scrutiny. Tinker supports the existence of minors' rights; it does not replace the contribution inquiry.

The entire new age prohibition falls; severability does not authorize choosing a younger age, an earnings threshold or a parental-consent exception that Congress did not enact. Other valid source, amount, voluntariness and false-name restrictions remain. There is no repayment or retrospective-liability order.

### 7.2. Statutory conditions and actual lower judgment

#### Reporting, identification and broadcast records

**Section 201 ordinary reporting.** The primary category is a broadcast, cable or satellite communication identifying a federal candidate within 60 days of a general, special or runoff election, or 30 days of a primary, preference election, nominating convention or caucus. Nonpresidential coverage additionally requires the relevant-electorate condition: reception by at least 50,000 persons in the specified House district or Senate State. News/commentary/editorial communications have the party/committee/candidate ownership-control qualification; expenditures and independent expenditures, qualifying debates/forums and their solely promotional communications, and lawful regulatory exemptions are distinct exclusions. The statute forbids a regulatory exemption for an otherwise covered section 301(20)(A)(iii) communication. The conditional fallback requires both promotion/support/attack/opposition and no plausible meaning other than an exhortation to vote; the district's deletion of the latter condition is a disputed remedy, not enacted text.

For direct production/airing costs aggregating **more than $10,000** in a calendar year, a statement is due within 24 hours of each disclosure date, including each later additional over-$10,000 aggregate. It identifies the disburser, persons directing or controlling its activity, records custodian and nonindividual principal place of business; each disbursement of **more than $200** and its recipient; and pertinent elections and candidates known or to be identified. Section 304(f)(2)(E) confines donor names and addresses to donors of **$1,000 or more** to a qualifying segregated account, measured from the first day of the preceding calendar year through disclosure. That account must consist solely of direct contributions for electioneering communications by individual citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents; it may also be used for other purposes. Without that account, (F) reaches $1,000-or-more contributors to the disburser over the same period. It is inaccurate to say every entity need disclose only earmarked advertising donors or only donors to a special account. Reporting is additional to other reporting duties; the tax-law definitions remain unaffected.

**Section 304(f)(5).** Executed contracts are deemed disbursements. Section 104.20 starts reporting upon public distribution when the aggregate exceeds the threshold, but its report of obligations can include other contracted communications that have not aired. Thus the existence of that rule does not itself dispose of this challenge. The district sustained ordinary disclosure but severed the contracts clause in full.

**Section 212.** The statutory independent-expenditure report is due within 24 hours for $1,000-or-more aggregates after the twentieth day but more than 24 hours before an election; within 48 hours for $10,000-or-more aggregates up to and including the twentieth day; and again upon each corresponding additional aggregate for the same election. Contracts are included in the statutory wording; receipt is filing for the $1,000 report. The effective section 109.10(c)–(d) rule measures those report deadlines from public distribution/dissemination. The live challenge attacks compelled advance reporting, and the district dismissed it as unripe. That is different from a merits approval of every section 212 duty.

**Section 311.** The challenged change extends source identification to electioneering communications and other covered non-express advocacy. The additional printed-box/type/contrast, candidate approval, image, voice and four-second specifications are enacted context; the district identifies no independent preserved attack on each such detail. Candidate/authorized-speaker identification differs from the other-speaker responsible-for-content statement and connected-organization identification. Neither is section 305's price condition or section 504's public file. The district rejected the extension challenge.

**Section 504.** A licensee must keep a public record of a request to buy broadcast time by or for a legally qualified candidate for public office, or communicating a message relating to a political matter of national importance, including a legally qualified candidate, a federal election, or a national legislative issue of public importance. Accepted **and rejected** requests are covered. The record states acceptance/rejection, charged rate, airing date/time, purchased class, and pertinent candidate/office, election or issue. Candidate requests identify candidate, authorized committee and treasurer; other requests identify purchaser, a contact person's name/address/telephone, and chief executive officers or executive-committee/board members. Information is placed in the file as soon as possible and retained at least two years. The section is not restricted to completed broadcasts, electioneering communications, federal-candidate purchases or a $10,000 threshold. The district invalidated it entirely.

#### Threshold subjects

**Section 305.** The lowest-unit-rate certification is made at purchase. A candidate may refer to another candidate for the same office while keeping eligibility by satisfying the television or radio alternative. Television requires the candidate image and readable identification/approval/authorized-committee-payment statement simultaneously for at least four seconds at the end; radio requires the candidate's personal identifying, office and approval statement. A noncompliant reference loses the rate for that and subsequent covered broadcasts in the applicable 45-/60-day periods. The district dismissed for lack of standing. It described Pence's actual testimony about his advertisements and future campaign practice, Barr's showing, and McConnell's future opponent-critical plans and 2008 election. These reported features support a bounded threshold decision; they do not authorize saying civil price consequences can never injure a speaker.

**Section 307.** Beginning with contributions made January 1, 2003, the individual candidate ceiling rises from $1,000 to $2,000, the national-party ceiling from $20,000 to $25,000, the aggregate becomes a two-calendar-year allowance of $37,500 for candidates plus $57,500 for other contributions with the $37,500 nonnational-party sublimit, and the senatorial committee allowance becomes $35,000. The specified ceilings receive the enacted inflation adjustments, $100 rounding and odd-year/two-year operation where applicable; the text retains different 1974 and 2001 base years. Unchanged nonparty-PAC ceilings do not become amended provisions merely because other ceilings were changed. The lower court dismissed the increased-limit/indexing challenges, without separately resolving Paul's operative candidate-ceiling injury on its actual theory.

**Sections 304/316/319.** These are distinct candidate-response mechanisms, not Daggett's public-grant program. The Senate threshold formula is $150,000 plus four cents per voting-age resident, with the enacted twice/four-times/ten-times bands, threefold or sixfold contribution allowances, aggregate-limit exceptions and the highest band's coordinated-party relaxation. Opposition personal funds compare the opposing and recipient candidates' personal expenditures, with section 316's half-gross-receipts-advantage adjustment and specified June 30/December 31 measurements. The House mechanism uses opposition personal funds over $350,000, tripled individual limits, aggregate exception and coordinated-party relaxation, its own gross-receipts rule, and a 100-percent cap; the Senate acceptance cap is 110 percent. Receipt of notification precedes increased-limit acceptance, attributable permission ceases when the relevant opponent withdraws, and unspent excess contributions must be returned within 50 days. The distinct declarations, initial/additional notices, recipient rules and excess reports remain part of the statute. Section 304 also contains the personal-loan repayment limitation above $250,000 from post-election contributions. The presented self-financing challenge and lower threshold disposition do not establish a claimant subject to that separate loan limit. No merits ruling on it is manufactured. The district dismissed the actual self-financing claims for lack of standing.

### 7.4. Ordinary section 201 reporting and section 311 extension

#### Common operative commitment

Each Associate would **reject the facial categorical challenge to ordinary section 201 reporting** and **affirm rejection of the preserved section 311 extension challenge**. The primary category can constitutionally define these disclosure duties even if it cannot define the whole permissible reach of a treasury prohibition. Large, objectively identified candidate-referencing broadcasts close to an election have a direct electoral-information connection. Identifying their payer and significant financing sources enables voters to assess organized election-period advocacy and trace financing through intermediary names. It neither makes the expenditure a candidate contribution nor requires a showing of corruption by every independent speaker.

The actual section 201 thresholds, finite reporting event, segregated-account alternative and donor amount/period limitations do constitutional work. The account option confines disclosure to that pool if its actual source conditions are met; the alternative all-qualifying-contributor rule cannot be silently rewritten as earmarked-donor-only reporting. On this facial attack the substantial relation of the financial pool to the purchased candidate-focused communication, together with the lawful segregation option, is enough. The rationale is not permission to publish every member or unrelated person associated with an issue organization. A supported reasonable probability of threats, harassment or reprisals remains governed by Buckley/Brown and Mariani; a general desire for privacy does not establish the exception. These commitments rely on the district's adjudicated facial posture and the legal rule, not a new categorical assertion that every cited reprisal account is false or absent.

For section 311 the factual source designation remains distinct from an ideological pledge. Identifying the sponsor of the objectively covered communication does not force the sponsor to endorse a governmental position. Stengel's organizational-finance rationale and McIntyre's reserved distinctions permit rejecting the specific assertion that identification necessarily fails whenever the message lacks express advocacy. The additional typography, image and timing details are not independently adjudicated beyond the presented extension claim.

The strongest alternative would import the expressive-content boundary governing prohibitions or full political-committee status into every report, or apply McIntyre's ordinary individual leaflet rule to all paid broadcast finance. Bartlett itself preserves distinct sponsor-disclosure predicates, Stengel distinguishes financed organizational candidate advocacy, and Mariani establishes an independent source-attribution justification. Conversely, these authorities cannot justify an unrestricted donor registry: the actual covered communication, reporting scale, funding-pool relationship, statutory account choice and supported reprisal protection are necessary limits of this conclusion.

### 7.6. Independent Press Clause and media-exemption theory

Each Associate would **affirm rejection of the categorical claim that describing candidate campaigns, political organizations and all communicators as the general press immunizes them from otherwise valid contribution, source-reporting or sponsorship rules**. Press freedom is not confined to a privileged institutional caste. That does not mean actual candidate receipts become the publisher's own independent expenditure, or that every regulation of a communicative transaction is an impermissible editorial license. Buckley, McIntyre, Stengel, Akins, Clifton and Mariani require examination of the actual regulated activity, not a choice between absolute general-press immunity and no press protection.

Each would also **reject the asserted automatic invalidity of BCRA because it preserves a news/commentary/editorial exemption**. Bellotti protects noninstitutional speakers against exclusion from First Amendment protection. It does not require treating a candidate's campaign receipt or paid advocacy purchase as identical to a broadcaster's independent news function. The statutory ownership/control limit and the actual news function must be acknowledged; the exemption does not itself confer a universal license to favor media owners or define away protected independent advocacy. A concrete discriminatory application or unjustified expenditure prohibition must be decided on its own proper component, and the analysis of treasury and issue-speech applications in section 4 remains independent.

### 7.7. Integration and remaining limits

1. These are supported decisional conclusions and alternatives, not private deliberation or fictional circulation. The final reconciliation context must independently assess the commitments against the comparator; the independent component context neither received nor applied it.
2. The complete judicial remedy must preserve the mixed lower decree: affirm the identified nonmerits dismissals; reverse Paul's amended-ceiling no-standing determination while rejecting the preserved categorical theory; keep unchanged FECA limits outside the special BCRA review judgment; preserve ordinary section 201 and section 311 results; and select the actual contracts/broadcaster application remedies supported by the eventual participating coalition.
3. No current source gap stops the concrete bounded results above. The factual premise for Paul's actual candidate injury is supported by the original appendix; a categorical evidentiary-default account would conflict with it. Adams's evidence is acknowledged without turning the requested equality of influence into a personal statutory receipt restraint.
4. **Preservation limit:** The current clean sources establish Paul's categorical Press Clause claim, its operative ceiling injury and the separate old-PAC claim. They do not by themselves establish the full scope of any independently preserved close-fit challenge to the amended dollar amount. A final judgment purporting to resolve such an additional application requires the actual amended complaint and relevant preserved lower filings identified in Paul's brief, rather than inference from his standing evidence. Rejecting the presented categorical theory and leaving any genuinely distinct claim to its proper channel avoids that unsupported expansion.
5. **Threshold record limit:** The proposed Adams and self-financing dispositions use the theory and claimant posture actually reported and argued. They do not certify the absence of every future-race declaration or other document in the complete appendix. If assembly proposes a new negative factual finding about a particular candidate, it must first obtain scoped verification of that specific predicate. These conclusions do not require such a finding.
6. **Reprisal limit:** No new claim-specific determination that a donor experienced, or did not experience, reprisals is made. ### A. Presented claims and the actual lower disposition

## Selected propositions and competing paths

### D1. Statutory coverage and the two distinct interpretive inquiries

Sections 1311, 1344 and 1362 operate together: the discharge prohibition and permit program apply to navigable waters, defined in section 1362(7) as waters of the United States. Section 1344(a) entrusts dredged-or-fill permits to the Secretary; subsection (d) identifies the Secretary of the Army acting through the Chief of Engineers. The program's structured permit, guideline, general-permit and state-administration provisions supply actual administrative authority. This is a congressional delegation implemented through binding Corps regulations, not authority inferred solely from statutory ambiguity, expertise, the regulation's label or longstanding practice. Under Haggar and Mead, the Court first enforces the statute and the limits of that delegation, then reviews a reasonable agency choice within genuine unresolved scope. Barnhart's completed holding reinforces that sequence; its separate section 405(a) delegation does not supply the Army's authority.

Congress did not resolve the exact reach of every remote, nonnavigable branch of a navigable water's tributary system. The enacted definition is broader than navigability in fact; Riverside Bayview's surviving adjacent-wetland rule confirms that point without deciding every connection. The Corps reasonably includes this physically connected tributary system and its conceded adjacent wetlands to protect the receiving waters against upstream discharges. This is an application of sections 328.3(a)(1), (5) and (7), including the latter provision's exclusion of wetlands themselves as the predicate waters. It is not approval of every other-water criterion in subsection (a)(3), every isolated pond, or every possible use of the term tributary.

There is a separate regulatory question: whether tributaries in subsection (a)(5) include feeder branches that reach the navigable water through intermediate streams. The text permits that ordinary whole-system meaning; the alternative direct-feeder meaning is linguistically plausible but not compelled. The Corps' responsible, considered construction consistently uses the term for the feeder system. The 1975 preamble's primary, secondary and tertiary tributaries support that usage; the former headwaters restriction limited coverage then rather than establishing that more remote streams were never tributaries. Under the current Auer rule, this fair construction controls because it is neither plainly erroneous nor inconsistent with the regulation. It does not receive force simply because it appears in a brief or preamble, and it cannot displace contrary text or prove that a drainage route exists.

**Strong contrary path:** SWANCC's statutory restraint, the word navigable, state responsibility and the potential breadth of all tributaries could favor a direct-feeder limit or rejection of this regulation as beyond delegation. The answer is the actual statutory program plus a supported water connection. Current SWANCC excludes bird-use-only authority over the isolated ponds there and expressly preserves materially different supported water connections and Riverside Bayview. It supplies neither a universal present-navigability rule nor a direct-adjacency requirement. Nor does it authorize jurisdiction whenever environmental benefits cross state lines. Here the physical drainage relationship, supported receiving-water effects and lawful regulatory chain supply what bird use alone did not.

The lower opinion's proposition that ambiguity alone necessarily delegates interpretive authority is not adopted. Mead requires the preceding inquiry into statutory conferral and its actual exercise. Likewise, incompatible historical descriptions of SWANCC or Commerce doctrine in the lower opinion are not carried into this commitment. If statutory coverage failed, relief from the application would precede the constitutional question; that rival disposition is rejected on this record for the reasons above.

### D2. Congress may protect the navigable receiving channel against this upstream discharge class

The enacted conduct at issue is discharge of dredged or fill material into the covered connected waters, not the owners' development business in general. The interstate mechanism is physical protection of a navigable waterway from pollutant effects carried through its actual tributary system, including the effects of discharge into adjacent wetlands. Congress's channel power permits protection against injurious uses; it is not confined to keeping a boat passage free of a physical obstruction. Rio Grande Dam, 174 U.S. 690, 708–709, supplies protection of the navigable water against upstream interference; Appalachian Electric Power, 311 U.S. 377, Oklahoma ex rel. Phillips, 313 U.S. 508, 525–526, and Grand River Dam Authority, 363 U.S. 229, 232, support protection through relevant nonnavigable reaches. Caminetti, 242 U.S. 470, 491, supports protection of channels against harmful uses. None creates a general federal power over local land.

Lopez preserves direct channel protection while requiring identification of the regulated class and a real interstate mechanism before aggregation can do work. The agreed route to the Wicomico establishes the particular water connection. The lower opinion reports the Corps' supporting evidence that discharges into nonnavigable tributaries and adjacent wetlands substantially affect water quality in navigable receiving waters; the owners did not contend that the reported class effect was overstated. That is affirmative supported evidence, not a missing-file inference against them. Together the actual drainage and the supported pollution mechanism justify preventative permit regulation of the connected discharge class. Neither an independently substantial effect from this single fill nor proof that an identified particle arrived in the river is required. The dispute over amount and consistency of flow does not itself disprove the agreed drainage route or the supported mechanism. No unstated flow-volume threshold or permanent-water requirement is invented.

**Strong contrary path:** distant flow through intervening streams, ponds and dams may attenuate the relationship so far that channel language becomes a label for ordinary local land regulation. This objection would matter if the Government relied on eventual economic consequences, a geographical assertion alone, or unsupported speculation that every ditch connects. Here the record identifies the route and supplies the class's receiving-water effect. Regulating discharge into that connected system fits protection of the identified navigable channel. The holding therefore does not rest on an unlimited claim that the CWA reaches every constitutional power Congress might possess.

Home Builders' independent substantial-interstate-externality route would require its own concrete cross-border effect, substantial class importance, territorial-control limit and fit. The Bay's name, multiple states' environmental interest or desirability of national uniformity alone would not meet that test. Because direct channel protection fully resolves this application, these commitments neither add an independent externality holding nor silently erase Scalia and Thomas's opposition to that route. Nor is the mere breadth of the CWA a demonstrated necessary-inclusion mechanism under Holston and the Hale line.

Ho's completed November 20 holding supplies a distinct comparison. It sustained regulation of an actual asbestos-abatement service operation in an interstate service market; prevention and notice did not require proof of an actual asbestos release once that market connection was established. A contractor was hired here, but no new market findings, bid comparison or commercial element may be invented from Ho. The hydrological and channel ground is sufficient on this record. Ho confirms that prevention may follow a demonstrated eligible connection; it does not establish the water connection in this case or make every development project a service-market case. The Associates' earlier differences about economic habitat conversion remain intact without requiring an unnecessary alternative holding here.

### D3. The twelve-inch root-zone indicator supports this delineation within the complete definition

Section 328.3(b) requires inundation or saturation by surface or ground water at a frequency and duration sufficient to support, and under normal circumstances actually supporting, a prevalence of vegetation typically adapted to saturated soils. Water within twelve inches on one occasion is not a replacement for those conditions. The 1987 Manual applies the regulatory definition through vegetation, hydric soil and hydrology. It ranks recorded water data above field indicators; within field methods it recognizes visual observation of saturation, including saturation within a major portion of the root zone, usually within twelve inches of the surface. That saturation can have the overriding effect on vegetation relevant to wetlands hydrology.

The Corps used the Manual's express root-zone indicator and visual methodology. The owners monitored twenty-two wells for several months and argued that the sites were not saturated to the surface for sufficient time. They relied on the Manual's description of saturation to the surface at some time during the growing season. Their preserved contention is that the Corps misapplied the Manual; they did not argue that the Manual itself is a plainly erroneous or inconsistent interpretation of the regulation. Reading its general surface description together with its specific root-zone indicator defeats the claimed contradiction. Their reported monitoring assertion addresses a surface threshold and does not itself refute application of the express root-zone indicator.

**Strong contrary path:** a lower-ranked field observation cannot automatically overcome more reliable measurements, and the regulation retains frequency and duration. That would require a return for findings if the Court had to resolve genuinely contrary root-zone measurements or supply an absent regulatory condition. But the described challenge does not establish that factual conflict: it uses the asserted surface-only legal requirement to attack an express indicator. Auer supports the responsible, scientifically grounded regulatory interpretation; it does not elevate an observation above all recorded data or transform the Manual into authority to disregard the regulation. Affirmance rejects this preserved interpretation objection, retains the undisputed soil and vegetation findings, and leaves the full regulatory combination intact. No fourteen-day rule, growing-season percentage, universal twelve-inch classification or new factual presumption is adopted.

### D4. The restoration order survives independent review of the owners' alternative

The district court required refilling the excavated ditch and restoration of previolation elevation. The owners proposed hauling sidecast soil to a nonwetland part of their land, arguing that digging alone is not the regulated discharge and that this removal would have avoided the violation if used initially. The court found that their proposal would retain the drainage benefit of the unlawful project and doubted that removal could occur without further ecological harm. Those are actual reasons for the selected restoration, not consequences presumed from jurisdiction.

A restoration order must bear an equitable relationship to the actual violation, be practically achievable and serve restoration of the affected environment. Cumberland Farms, 826 F.2d 1151, 1164, supplies persuasive factors concerning environmental benefit, feasibility and equitable relation; this commitment does not turn those factors into a new mandatory checklist or require a formulaic finding under each heading. The supported findings adequately connect refilling to restoration of the previolation condition and explain why removal alone would be deficient or harmful. Section 1251(a)'s restoration objective supports that remedial choice; it is not an independent source of unlimited jurisdiction over the property.

**Strong contrary path:** because digging itself was not the established discharge violation, refilling may appear to punish an otherwise lawful excavation rather than correct the deposited material. The answer is the relationship of the chosen remedy to this completed unlawful project and the supported harm, not a newly announced prohibition on digging. An option that might have avoided a violation before the work does not necessarily restore the affected wetlands afterward. Removing the piles while preserving the drainage benefit and risking further damage need not be accepted as an adequate remedy. On this record the district court did not abuse its discretion. The affirmance authorizes neither general redesign of the parcel nor unrelated enhancement or punishment.

The officer stopped a private automobile at approximately 3:16 a.m. for speeding. Partlow, the driver and owner, consented to the search. Pringle was the front passenger and Smith the rear passenger. The officer saw rolled cash when the driver opened the glove compartment for registration; the search recovered $763 there and five cocaine baggies behind the movable rear-seat armrest. Before arrest the officer had no established confession identifying the sole owner and no other supported information identifying a single exclusive possessor. Pringle's later confession and description of the other occupants cannot supply earlier knowledge or justification.

The validity of the stop and consent search is outside the grant. Maryland possession law permits actual or constructive dominion or control, individually or jointly, and requires knowledge. Those elements identify the offense under investigation, while the Fourth Amendment asks for a practical probability rather than trial proof. The disputed alleged promise to release the others if someone confessed is not an established fact and is unnecessary to the antecedent probable-cause issue.

Ornelas governs independent review of ultimate probable cause, with protection for historical findings and real weight for grounded contextual inferences. Wardlow and Valentine forbid location, association or a bare presumption from supplying an absent factual connection to wrongdoing. The probability inquiry remains distinct from trial sufficiency and from the lower reasonable-suspicion threshold. Ybarra protects individuals against searches based merely on their presence among public-place strangers; Di Re requires attention to actual information pointing to a different identified participant. Neither establishes universal innocence or guilt for automobile passengers.

The completed Banks public holding of December 2 requires particular circumstances and contemporaneous justification for its home-entry destruction exception, rejects mechanical waiting-time categories, and preserves the existing review allocation. Its independent section 3109 destruction exception concerns announced warrant execution, not this passenger's probable cause. Each Associate's full Banks join is consistent with the individualized inquiry below, but does not itself establish this arrest's validity or lower the probable-cause threshold to reasonable suspicion. Raytheon's production and whole-record discrimination rules are distinct. ## Supported alternatives and operative propositions

**P1 — individualized common-possession probability.** Police may arrest a passenger when the facts known at arrest, considered together, establish a fair probability that he knowingly exercises sole or joint dominion or control over the contraband. A supported common-enterprise inference can particularize the probability to more than one occupant; officers need not prove exclusive ownership. Presence in a vehicle, the presence of drugs somewhere in it, or refusal to volunteer ownership does not establish that conclusion categorically.

**P2 — this complete record supplies probable cause.** The small private-car setting, actual shared travel in the early morning, five separately packaged cocaine baggies accessible in the passenger compartment and rolled cash in the front glove compartment together support a practical inference of a common possession enterprise including Pringle. The inference is grounded in the relationship of the people and evidence within the occupied vehicle, not in an assumption that every companion of a criminal is guilty. Concealment defeats a claim that police observed Pringle seeing the cocaine, but it does not eliminate the probability that occupants transporting these accessible packages and cash were jointly involved. The cash is ordinary property standing alone; with the packaged drugs and shared transport it may contribute to the inference. The late hour is context, not an offense or substitute for connection.

The absence of an ownership admission supplies no independent badge of guilt and does not create a duty to speak. It means only that the supported joint-possession inference was not displaced by additional reliable information singling out another person. Probable cause can extend to each participant in a reasonably inferred common enterprise without being divided into a one-in-three arithmetic contest for sole ownership.

**P3 — disposition and derivative remedy.** Reverse the Maryland Court of Appeals' arrest-based suppression ruling and remand for proceedings consistent with the lawful arrest. This arrest supplies no basis for excluding the subsequent confession as fruit of an unlawful seizure. The lower court must implement that correction and address any independently preserved admissibility or other issue remaining within its lawful scope. The Court does not use Miranda warnings to cure an arrest defect; it finds no defect in this arrest.

**Strong rival path: affirm for want of an individual link.** The drugs were hidden in the rear, Pringle had no possessory interest in the car, cash is innocent and no incriminating movement, odor or admission linked him to the drugs. A generalized common-enterprise inference could permit detention of innocent passengers until one confesses. The state majority and Raker concurrence expressly recognized that probable cause is less than trial proof; their argument should be answered substantively, not caricatured as requiring proof beyond reasonable doubt.

The answer supporting P2 is the cumulative shared-transport evidence. The known crime is actual packaged cocaine in a small occupied vehicle, with associated cash and accessibility across the common passenger space. That furnishes a connection absent from Wardlow's ambiguous departure in a narcotics area and Valentine's unresolved relation between reliable gun possession and illegality. It is also different from Ybarra's incidental presence in a public establishment and Di Re's affirmative information identifying another transaction participant. The supported inference is reasonable although an innocent-passenger explanation remains possible. The probable-cause threshold is higher than reasonable suspicion, but does not require elimination of all innocent possibilities or proof of knowing control by direct observation.

The physical location behind the rear armrest must remain in the reasoning: the drugs were not found in Pringle's pocket or in plain view. Conversely, treating that hidden location as conclusive against his knowledge would give it more force than the practical inquiry permits. No rule about every private car, bus, taxi, public establishment or personal container is decided by this application.

**If the rival path prevailed.** The actual state judgment found attenuation unpreserved and also rejected it on the supported continuing-custody record. Independent source and inevitable discovery were not raised. No model may repair an unlawful arrest by inventing one of those grounds or by declaring Miranda warnings sufficient. The derivative suppression outcome would then follow the preserved state posture; the disputed promise would not become an established independent coercion holding. None of the eight commitments selects that path on this record.

## Merits alternatives and consequences

**P2 — minimum protection before adverse counting.** A federal court may not make a pro se filing styled under a different rule count as the prisoner's first section 2255 motion for later second-or-successive restrictions unless the prisoner has been informed of the intended recharacterization, warned that later section 2255 motions will be subject to those restrictions, and given a meaningful opportunity to withdraw the filing or amend it to include the section 2255 claims he wishes to present. Without those protections, the converted filing cannot supply that later counting predicate.

For Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, this is a rule governing federal courts' own administration of liberal pro se pleading construction in light of section 2255's structure. Haines supports reading a pro se pleading for its substance, not imposing an unannounced permanent procedural loss through an accommodation. The enacted successive gates remain fully effective when their first-motion predicate exists. Lonchar requires legally supported dismissal predicates; Stewart shows that antecedent classification is a real legal inquiry, although its preserved-premature-claim rule does not itself decide conversion. The persuasive lower decisions collected in Roney's dissent—particularly Raineri on pre-enactment conversion and Adams, Miller, Seesing, Kelly and Henderson on warning and election—demonstrate workable approaches to this judicially created problem. They are not controlling Supreme Court law or a substitute for statutory reasoning.

Each required protection does work. Notice identifies the different vehicle; the warning identifies the consequence for later claims; withdrawal preserves the choice not to invoke the vehicle now; amendment permits a complete first submission if the prisoner chooses to proceed. This neither guarantees amendment beyond otherwise lawful limitations nor requires advice about the merits of every possible claim. It is a condition on judicial conversion's adverse counting effect, not a general equitable suspension of AEDPA or a universal duty to counsel pro se litigants.

**P3 — application and remedy.** The unwarned 1994 conversion cannot make the 1997 filing second or successive. Failure to appeal the earlier label does not supply informed acceptance of a consequence the court never explained, especially where Castro had insisted on Rule 33. Vacate the judgment affirming dismissal and remand for ordinary section 2255 proceedings without this successive-motion bar. The 1994 Rule 33 denial is not reopened. The lower court must examine the later motion under applicable first-motion requirements, including any properly raised independent timeliness or procedural defense and the ineffective-assistance claim if reached. First-motion classification determines neither those gates nor entitlement to relief.

**Contrary path — count the actual prior adjudication.** The lower majority's strongest position is that the judge actually adjudicated constitutional claims under section 2255, and Congress made no warning exception to the second-or-successive restrictions. Finality and the unchallenged earlier label reinforce that position; the later ineffective-assistance claim was available earlier. The response is that the statutory gates presuppose a first section 2255 motion. Congress's restrictions do not require a court's unsolicited, unexplained alternative label to consume the prisoner's opportunity to choose and prepare that motion. Limiting the adverse effect of the court's own recharacterization is not excusing a concededly successive motion for unfairness. The previous judgment retains its merits finality while its newly asserted collateral counting effect is corrected.

**Broader alternative — forbid involuntary conversion altogether.** Relabeling can create risks beyond the successive-motion gate; courts may assist comprehension without changing a litigant's selected procedural vehicle. That broader objection has particular force for Scalia and Thomas, but a blanket prohibition on every beneficial recharacterization is not required to decide the present adverse counting issue. No proposed opinion may describe their agreement with the remedy as an affirmative endorsement of conversion whenever three warnings are supplied.
