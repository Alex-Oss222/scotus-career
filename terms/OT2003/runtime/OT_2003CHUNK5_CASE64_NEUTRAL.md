### Issue 1: Meaning of the mineral reservation

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling holding resolves the Pittman Act reservation. The governing statutory decisions require adherence to enacted language and do not authorize expanding a reservation simply because the material later becomes profitable.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Interpret the particular enactment and its legal context; distinguish a governing construction of another statute from a binding construction of this one.
* **Other Governing Law:** Pittman Underground Water Act of 1919, §8, reserving valuable minerals, and the patent issued under that Act.
* **Material Other Authority:** Watt v. Western Nuclear addresses minerals under the differently worded Stock-Raising Homestead Act and is relevant surviving pre-simulation authority, not a decision of this dispute.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law protects federal mineral reservations but leaves the effect of this Act’s valuable-mineral wording unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether the historical mining-law context excludes commonplace sand and gravel despite present commercial utility.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit treated the reservation as reaching these economically useful substances in light of Western Nuclear.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The enacted valuable-mineral limitation and its 1919 setting exclude ubiquitous surface materials; a later market cannot enlarge reserved title.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The Government invokes the protective mineral-reservation scheme and continuity with Western Nuclear; commercial value supports inclusion.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** A court could distinguish the two statutes without overruling Western Nuclear; the validity of that earlier construction is not a necessary antecedent.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States is the respondent and advances the reservation interpretation described above.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Reading the reservation to exclude ordinary sand and gravel reverses the federal-title judgment and defeats the asserted mineral-trespass basis. Reading it to include commercially useful mineral substances affirms. Either rule can be confined to Pittman patents; neither requires adjudication of all federal mineral reservations or damages not established below.
