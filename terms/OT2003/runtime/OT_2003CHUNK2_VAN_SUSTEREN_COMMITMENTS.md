Van Susteren sought the Libertarian nomination while having been registered Republican in that interval. ## Common legal propositions and supported rival paths

**P1 — association.** Apply Anderson–Burdick to the actual burden, precise state interests and fit in the whole nomination system. The one-year disaffiliation requirement, as applied to this party-primary candidacy, is a reasonable, nondiscriminatory selection rule rather than a severe burden. It applies to changes from any other qualified party, requires a period of genuine separation before access to the new party's selection, and leaves association, advocacy and eligibility after that period. It need not allow every late change of electoral allegiance. Its burden is real but proportionate to preserving distinct nomination channels and discouraging temporary party switching to exploit a primary.

Storer supplies substantial support because it sustained a one-year disaffiliation period protecting California's distinct candidate-selection routes. Its independent-candidate application does not mechanically answer this primary case; the present regulation must be examined on its own terms. Timmons protects official recognition of a qualified party's consenting nomination and does not make stability an unlimited justification, but it leaves sore-loser rules undecided and does not address a neutral condition on entering a primary before selection. Tashjian and Eu protect actual party choices, while Jones prohibits compelled outsider control of an objecting party's nomination. This rule does not replace a party's electorate with outsiders or dictate its platform or leadership; it sets a neutral prefiling separation condition for someone seeking its public primary. Those distinctions sustain the rule even when candidate and party members wish to associate now.

**P2 — equal protection.** The earlier calendar date confronting partisan candidates does not, by itself, establish unconstitutional discrimination. Party candidates enter a primary which makes the legally operative selection; independents use a different route to the general-election ballot. Requiring disaffiliation in advance of each route's relevant selection process is reasonably related to keeping the routes distinct and preventing strategic crossover. Van Susteren's comparison of approximately twenty-three months versus thirteen months before the general election identifies an actual calendar difference, but omits the intervening primary and its necessary prefiling stage. The relevant analysis considers both that difference and each route's function; it does not end with the categorical assertion that party and independent candidates can never be compared. On this record the difference is justified and supplies no separate equal-protection violation.

**P3 — disposition.** Affirm the defense summary judgment on the granted First and Fourteenth Amendment grounds. Deny the requested prospective declaratory and ballot-access relief against this application of the twelve-month rule. The limited holding concerns this requirement and this system; it creates no unlimited power to impose longer periods or every other affiliation restriction.

**Rival association path.** Reverse because a year before filing excludes a willing candidate from a willing party long before the general election and burdens the party's central choice more than the State's generalized stability interest warrants. Tashjian, Eu and Jones protect nomination choices; Timmons, which Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg joined, requires more than pointing to endorsement or other possible candidates. The strongest objection to affirmance is that the State calls the rule protection against outsiders even when the new association welcomes the candidate.

The response sustaining P1 is that the actual temporal rule structures admission to a nomination process before its competitive selection; it does not bar a consenting already-selected nominee solely because another party also chose him, as in Timmons, or force an electorate on an objecting party, as in Jones. Disaffiliation periods can address opportunistic allegiance before the particular manipulation occurs. Storer, and the preventive-regulation principle of Munro that Burdick and Timmons retain subject to fit, permit that direct connection without proof that this candidate personally planned a raid. This record supplies no burden from additional requirements that makes this one-year condition a severe obstacle beyond the identified inability to seek the chosen nomination immediately. That is a bounded merits assessment, not a burden of producing unavailable research documents.

**Rival equal-protection path.** Reverse because the partisan route practically requires a substantially earlier commitment than the independent route. Its strength is the longer total separation before the eventual office-selecting election. Its weakness is treating that election as if the party primary did no separate selection work. The rule's link to the primary and its filing process explains the differential; no finding that independents and party candidates enjoy identical chances is needed. A remand merely to invent a future candidacy, prove the obvious calendar difference again, or seek a more elaborate record is not warranted on the supplied live dispute and published facts.
