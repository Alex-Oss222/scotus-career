The historical seven-Justice opinion expressly acknowledged that this statement could be excluded under the received reliability rules. It nevertheless concluded that judicial reliability assessment could not substitute for the confrontation procedure for testimony of this kind. Thus a sufficient Wright route, an already developed record and reluctance to decide more than necessary were all available historically. They are not changed premises supporting five new refusals to join the categorical ground.

Current Lilly gives its intrinsic-guarantee rule and application five direct votes rather than the historical plurality. ## Supported categorical proposition and precise precedent treatment

When investigating officers conduct a formal, recorded custodial interrogation to obtain a witness's account of past criminal events for evidentiary use against the accused, that accusation, offered for its truth, is within the constitutional testimony protection. If the witness is unavailable at trial, its admission requires a prior opportunity for cross-examination; judicial findings of reliability, a penal-interest label or correspondence with other evidence cannot supply that missing opportunity. Sylvia's structured recorded questioning falls within this defined class, and Crawford never had that opportunity.

The rule adopts the existing modeled categorical path at its actual scope. Mattox supplies unavailability and prior examination for absent testimony; the historical ex parte examination authorities explain why government-obtained trial accusations require that procedural protection even without an oath. The principal objection is that courts can exclude this statement through ordinary Wright analysis without revising doctrine. The seven answer that the protected method cannot depend on whether a court considers an accusation trustworthy, and repeated application of that substitute does not cure its constitutional defect for this class.

Roberts is displaced insofar as it authorizes this defined formal investigative accusation without prior cross-examination upon a reliability finding. Lilly's exclusion of the challenged accusation is consistent with the result, but its reliability method is no longer sufficient to admit an accusation within this defined class. White's accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment holdings remain effective on their stated foundations; rejection of an exclusively formal boundary is not silently converted into a holding that all other statements lack constitutional protection. Hale's accepted independent-public-record search/nonentry holding remains effective on its bounded function. The current event does not supply the disputed facts needed to extend or overturn those accepted applications.

Existing forfeiture by wrongdoing remains an independent equitable ground for losing a confrontation claim, rather than a reliability substitute. Prior statements may be tested when the declarant appears for cross-examination at trial, and a genuine nontruth use remains outside this substantive truth-use rule. No such route establishes admission here. The historical dying-declaration question and a complete definition covering every kind of testimony are outside this record; the opinion need not recite hypothetical applications as open work in the mandate.
