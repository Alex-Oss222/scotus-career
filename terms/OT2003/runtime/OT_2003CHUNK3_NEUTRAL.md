## 24. In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.

* **Citation or Docket:** 322 F.3d 283

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-12-15

* **Question Presented:** Whether §363(f) authorizes an asset sale free of successor liability for the identified employment-discrimination claims and travel-voucher settlement obligations, although those claims are not liens or other strictly in rem interests.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit affirmed the district court’s affirmance of the bankruptcy sale order barring the presented successor claims against American Airlines.

* **Relief Requested:** The EEOC and the voucher class seek preservation of successor liability; the airlines seek enforcement of the free-and-clear sale order.

* **Material Record:** American agreed to buy substantially all TWA assets for $742 million in a bankruptcy sale; no other qualifying bid emerged. The EEOC had pending discrimination charges, and a settled maternity-discrimination class held travel vouchers. Both objected and participated in the sale hearing. The sale order extinguished specified successor liability and enjoined its enforcement against American. The claims’ connection to the transferred enterprise and capacity for monetary satisfaction are disputed legal predicates. Record and order.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review assumes, without deciding, otherwise viable nonbankruptcy successor claims. The actual litigants had an opportunity to object; notice to unidentified future claimants is not established. The sale order’s broad language cannot be treated as an adjudication of every unknown claim or the purchaser’s own later misconduct. The limited-stay request is ancillary to the merits.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Property interests and monetary satisfaction

* **Controlling Law:** In re Dow Corning Corp. holds that §§105(a) and 1123(b)(6) do not authorize forced release of independent claims against nondebtors; equitable usefulness cannot replace a specific statutory source. Section 363(f)'s distinct authority over interests connected to transferred property remains to be construed here. Patterson v. Shumate requires application of the Code's operative text.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The proponent of free-and-clear treatment must establish an interest in the transferred property and at least one enacted §363(f) condition; economic desirability alone is insufficient.

* **Other Governing Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§101(5), 363(f), especially (f)(5), and 363(e); applicable employment statutes and the settlement establish the underlying obligations.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A lien is one statutory type of interest, not necessarily the only one; a general right against the debtor does not automatically become an interest in every asset.

* **Open Point:** Whether successor claims arising from the transferred operating enterprise and reducible to money fit the statute, and what remains outside the order.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit treated the claims as interests connected to the assets and capable of monetary satisfaction. The EEOC, representing the United States, and the class argue that employment rights are not interests in property and that extinguishing successor remedies sacrifices civil-rights enforcement; the class also stresses the noncash voucher promise. The airlines invoke the transfer connection, §363(f)(5), the priority system and the value preserved by a viable sale. The alternative contention that nonbankruptcy successor liability never existed was not necessary below and cannot be silently converted into a finding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance may encompass the identified transferable-enterprise liabilities without extinguishing all unsecured claims or all future regulatory duties. A strict in-rem construction would preserve the successor claims and require a remedy consistent with the consummated transaction and applicable sale-finality law. A predicate-specific remand is possible if monetary satisfaction or the property connection is unproved; sale value cannot replace those statutory elements. No path authorizes a bankruptcy court to license future discrimination.

* **Record and Legal Uncertainty:** The broader notice record must be established before the sale order can bind persons whose notice is disputed.

## 25. Concrete Works of Colorado v. City and County of Denver

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Concrete Works of Colorado v. City and County of Denver

* **Citation or Docket:** 321 F.3d 950

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-12-18

* **Question Presented:** Whether Denver’s evidence supports its minority- and women-owned-business contracting program and whether the lower court properly treated the previously unchallenged tailoring determination as law of the case.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit reversed declarations and an injunction against Denver’s contracting program and directed judgment for Denver. It vacated moot relief concerning superseded ordinances.

* **Relief Requested:** Concrete Works seeks restoration of relief against the program and preservation of its damages claim; Denver seeks affirmance.

* **Material Record:** Denver’s successive ordinances set participation goals for minority- and women-owned firms on city construction and design contracts. The record includes marketplace disparity studies, testimony about exclusion, and evidence linking city spending to private discrimination. After an earlier appeal, the district court held a bench trial and ruled against Denver. The earlier tailoring ruling had not been challenged in that appeal. The 1998 ordinance continues the contested program; damages concern the 1990 ordinance. Trial and appellate history.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The current ordinance sustains a live prospective dispute; the old 1990 ordinance remains relevant to damages. The superseded 1996 ordinance has no identified live damages claim. CWC’s earlier waiver and the absence of a demonstrated material amendment constrain reopening fit; law of the case is not jurisdictional. Racial and sex classifications must be analyzed under their respective entering standards, not merged because the ordinance combines them.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any effective Valeria v. Davis holding applies at its actual scope; political-process restructuring differs from Denver's evidence supporting procurement remediation.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Evidence and remedial classification

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors v. Peña establishes the common substantial-justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications. An important lawful objective and an evidence-supported substantial relation, including burdens, coverage and concrete comparably effective less racially disadvantageous alternatives, are required. It displaces the historical categorical strict-scrutiny formulation. City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters preserves the distinct sex-classification inquiry. Grutter v. Bollinger confirms the government's burden to justify an important lawful objective, an evidence-supported substantial relationship, actual competitive burdens and concrete practicable alternatives comparably effective with materially less racial disadvantage. Gratz rejects a corrective defense lacking that connection, without imposing prior adjudication or individual beneficiary injury as universal prerequisites. Those admissions applications reinforce, but do not decide, this procurement record.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government bears constitutional justification. Correcting demonstrated discrimination or barriers is a lawful objective; neither general societal disparity alone nor a prior adjudication of each beneficiary’s injury is necessary or sufficient. Engineering Contractors Association v. Metropolitan Dade County requires serious assessment of supported practical alternatives.

* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Denver’s 1990, 1996 and 1998 ordinances; 42 U.S.C. §1983 supplies the asserted civil remedy subject to its own predicates.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Reliable market evidence and linked private discrimination may establish the city’s actual remedial problem; no historical burden-shifting rule relieves Denver of ultimate justification.

* **Open Point:** Whether the established evidence supports the actual categories and relationship, and whether remaining fit objections were preserved.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court demanded forms of direct proof and rejected parts of the city’s statistical and anecdotal showing. Denver argues that those legal filters excluded evidence of barriers affecting its procurement market and that CWC waived the separate tailoring attack. CWC contests reliability, firm availability, causal inferences and use of private-market data. The United States appeared as amicus supporting the remedial program; no distinct verified remedial proposal is attributed. The Tenth Circuit applied historical heightened-scrutiny terminology and a challenger rebuttal framework; those formulations are not the governing burden.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance can rest on the supported remedial problem, substantial relationship and preserved law-of-the-case ruling under the Court's standards, without adopting historical strict scrutiny. Reversal requires an actual evidentiary or fit failure, not the absence of individual discrimination adjudications. A focused remand would be warranted for a genuinely unresolved material category or alternative, but a retrial merely to relabel an adequate record would serve no legal need. Invalidity would require relief against the program’s proved defect and separate proof for damages; validity defeats the presented liability theory.

## 26. Center for National Security Studies v. U.S. Department of Justice

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Center for National Security Studies v. U.S. Department of Justice

* **Citation or Docket:** 331 F.3d 918

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-01-06

* **Question Presented:** Whether FOIA permits categorical withholding of information identifying persons detained in the post-September 11 investigation and their lawyers, and whether the First Amendment or common law independently requires access.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit reversed an order requiring release of detainee and attorney names and otherwise sustained withholding; Judge Tatel dissented.

* **Relief Requested:** The requesting organizations seek disclosure and reversal of categorical withholding; DOJ seeks affirmance under FOIA exemptions and rejection of independent access claims.

* **Material Record:** The requests cover identities, lawyers, arrest and release dates, locations and reasons for detention. Records concern immigration detainees, persons charged criminally and material witnesses subject to court orders. DOJ invoked interference with enforcement, privacy, safety and other protections, relying on declarations about what an adversary could infer from combined information. The district court ordered names released while allowing other information to be withheld. Release or deportation of many detainees matters to whether an ongoing enforcement connection remains.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires an adequate exemption showing and review of segregability. Sealed judicial records, grand-jury material, agency records and publicly filed criminal charges are not interchangeable. An assertion that detainees could contact lawyers or disclose their own names does not establish actual access for every person or eliminate the statutory request.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Exemptions and judicial assessment

* **Controlling Law:** United States Department of Justice v. Landano rejects an agency-wide presumption and permits supported, rebuttable categorical inferences under Exemption 7(D); it preserves the government’s burden, protective review and reasonably segregable disclosure. Its exact confidentiality holding does not itself decide Exemption 7(A).

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under FOIA the agency bears the burden of establishing a statutory exemption. Defined categories may suffice when their actual characteristics support the predicted harm; classification as national security is not itself an exemption.

* **Other Governing Law:** 5 U.S.C. §552, especially (a)(4)(B), (b)(3), (b)(7)(A), (C), and (F), and segregability; applicable court sealing orders and Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e).

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Predictive intelligence judgments deserve informed weight but must connect disclosure to the exemption’s statutory harm.

* **Open Point:** Whether the submitted blanket categories and declarations adequately establish interference with ongoing enforcement and protect only exempt information.

### Issue 2: Independent access

* **Controlling Law:** North Jersey Media Group v. Ashcroft recognizes qualified public access to adversarial removal hearings, subject to supported closure findings and consideration of narrower alternatives. United Reporting preserves the absence of a universal right to executive records while requiring scrutiny of speech-selected access. A hearing-access right and access to an executive investigative compilation are distinct.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the requested institution and record; judicial openness does not automatically establish a right to an executive investigative list.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment and the asserted common-law access doctrine, distinct from FOIA’s enacted disclosure rights.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Government cannot evade applicable statutory disclosure by invoking absence of a general constitutional entitlement.

* **Open Point:** Whether any preserved access claim extends to these executive compilation records rather than particular judicial proceedings.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

DOJ argues that identities reveal investigative direction, permit coordination and intimidation, and create a mosaic useful to terrorists; privacy and safety independently support withholding. Requesters answer that largely immigration-based detentions and completed releases cannot all be presumed part of an ongoing terrorism proceeding, and seek meaningful segregability review. The majority below gave substantial weight to predictive declarations; the dissent disputed the sufficiency and breadth of the showing. A supported category can avoid person-by-person public explanations without making judicial review ceremonial. The common-law and First Amendment arguments must be addressed separately from statutory exemptions. Competing opinions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance could accept defined categories as adequate under Exemption 7(A), preserving other exemptions. Vacatur could require a concrete interference nexus and segregability, with protected submissions where needed. Immediate wholesale release would be unwarranted without resolving independent privacy, safety and sealing grounds. Rejecting a universal constitutional list-access right would not decide whether a particular removal or criminal hearing may be closed, and would not defeat FOIA relief.

* **Record and Legal Uncertainty:** Each withholding category requires the actual declarations and record; no detainee's investigative role, innocence or access to counsel is presumed.

## 27. Nebraska v. Environmental Protection Agency

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nebraska v. Environmental Protection Agency

* **Citation or Docket:** 331 F.3d 995; D.C. Circuit No. 01-1101

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-01-08

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Safe Drinking Water Act’s regulation of noncommunicable contaminants is facially invalid under the Commerce Clause when covered systems include interstate sellers and wholly intrastate operations; and whether regulating States and municipalities as water-system owners impermissibly commandeers them.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit denied Nebraska and the City of Alliance’s petition for review, rejecting the facial statutory constitutional challenges and finding challenges to the regulation unpreserved.

* **Relief Requested:** Petitioners seek invalidation of the federal regulation or its enabling authority; EPA seeks denial of review.

* **Material Record:** The Act directs EPA to set maximum contaminant levels for public water systems. A January 2001 rule set arsenic at .01 mg/L, with compliance in 2006. Covered systems include private and public providers and systems supplying water across state boundaries. Petitioners presented none of their current objections during rulemaking. The agency could not invalidate the statute, but could have considered regulatory implementation and the evidentiary record.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The facial challenge to Congress’s authority survives the agency-presentation objection; the specific rule challenge does not automatically do so. Petitioners conceded some waiver. The question does not establish an as-applied record for a particular wholly intrastate system. The scientific choice of the arsenic level is not reopened through a facial federal-power claim.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Reconcile any effective United States v. Holston, United States v. Ho or United States v. Deaton decisions. Their distinct production, renovation and waterway mechanisms cannot be presumed to decide this commodity-sale question.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Commercial commodity regulation

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lopez and National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt require an identified constitutional connection before the magnitude of effects. Direct regulation of interstate commercial transactions does not depend on speculative downstream costs.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the regulated activity and supported federal mechanism; a facial claim cannot prevail by ignoring indisputably valid applications. The government must support the mechanism it invokes.

* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses; Safe Drinking Water Act, 42 U.S.C. §§300f and 300g-1, including the 1996 arsenic direction.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Water sold by utilities is a commercial commodity, and interstate supply provides a direct connection. Noncommunicable contamination does not remove the transaction from commerce.

* **Open Point:** Whether the asserted facial invalidity can survive the Act’s valid interstate utility applications; a specific wholly local application is not presented.

### Issue 2: States operating utilities

* **Controlling Law:** Reno v. Condon permits regulation of States’ own covered activity without requiring them to legislate or enforce federal rules against unrelated parties. New York v. United States and Printz v. United States preserve the anti-commandeering limit at their adopted scope.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish compliance as a regulated operator from compelled government of third parties. Compliance expense is not alone commandeering; Printz locates the defect in the compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not in its cost or reimbursement.

* **Other Governing Law:** Tenth Amendment and the Act’s application to public and private water suppliers.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A municipality does not acquire an exemption from valid commodity-safety regulation by operating the utility publicly.

* **Open Point:** Whether this direct operational duty can be treated as a command to exercise sovereign regulatory authority.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Petitioners argue that local water and noncommunicable contaminants lack a sufficient interstate connection, and that public-system compliance invades state autonomy. EPA answers that commercial utility sales include interstate transactions and that the Act regulates operators themselves. The lower court’s facial reasoning did not resolve every wholly intrastate application. On preservation, petitioners claimed agency presentation would be futile; EPA responds that administrative consideration could have shaped the rule or supporting record even though the statute’s constitutional validity was beyond the agency’s power.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Denial of facial relief can rest on valid commercial applications and direct regulation of public operators. That does not decide an unpreserved rule objection or a factually undeveloped local application. A broader categorical invalidation of noncommunicable-contaminant regulation would reach actual interstate sales without a basis in Lopez. A successful challenge to a separately proved commandeering provision would require severance analysis rather than automatically invalidate all water standards.

## E. SOURCES

* Nebraska v. Environmental Protection Agency, 331 F.3d 995; D.C. Circuit No. 01-1101.

## 28. SEC v. Edwards

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** SEC v. Edwards

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1196; 540 U.S. 389

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-01-13

* **Question Presented:** Whether an investment arrangement promising a fixed contractual return can be an investment contract under the federal securities laws.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit reversed the district court’s preliminary relief for the SEC, reasoning that the fixed return and contractual entitlement were outside the investment-contract definition.

* **Relief Requested:** The SEC seeks reversal and further enforcement proceedings; Edwards seeks exclusion of the payphone sale-and-leaseback arrangements from the securities definition.

* **Material Record:** Investors bought payphones and could lease them back to an affiliated enterprise that selected sites, operated equipment and promised a fixed annual return. The marketed arrangement placed operational responsibility with the promoter. The SEC alleged that income was insufficient and new investor funds supported payments. The preliminary posture does not establish ultimate fraud liability.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The granted definitional question is whether a fixed return is categorically excluded, not whether every Howey element or every enforcement allegation is conclusively proved. The statute applicable to the transaction controls, rather than the label assigned to the physical equipment.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Investment-contract returns

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted holding replaces the surviving investment-contract framework of SEC v. W. J. Howey Co. The securities cases, including SEC v. Zandford, require application of the enacted security and fraud elements rather than invention of an independent fairness cause.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Howey asks about an investment of money in a common enterprise with expected profits from the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts of others, considering the economic arrangement as a whole.

* **Other Governing Law:** Securities Act §2(a)(1), 15 U.S.C. §77b(a)(1), and Exchange Act §3(a)(10), 15 U.S.C. §78c(a)(10).

* **Material Other Authority:** United Housing Foundation v. Forman distinguishes investment income or appreciation from consumptive benefits; a sentence concerning fixed returns in Reves v. Ernst & Young cannot replace Howey’s actual test.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The form of the investment and the existence of a contract do not by themselves settle its economic character.

* **Open Point:** Whether expected profits include a fixed return, and whether contractual entitlement eliminates reliance on others’ efforts.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eleventh Circuit treated fixed payments as outside profits and reasoned that a contractual right to payment does not depend solely on others’ efforts. The SEC argues that fixed income is still an investment return and that the enterprise’s ability to pay depends on its managerial activity. Edwards invokes the tangible phone purchase and promised contractual payment. The relevant distinction is investment dependence versus a consumptive purchase, not variable versus fixed yield. Positions and cited authorities.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Reversal may remove the two categorical exclusions and remand the remaining elements. Affirmance would require explaining why the statutes protect a speculative variable return but exclude an equally enterprise-dependent fixed return. A holding that all contractual interest or every equipment lease is a security would exceed both the question and Howey; ordinary instruments retain their separate statutory categories and exceptions.

## 29. Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-682; 540 U.S. 398

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-01-13

* **Question Presented:** Whether a complaint alleging an incumbent telephone carrier’s discriminatory failure to furnish network access required by the Telecommunications Act states a Sherman Act §2 claim, and what independent antitrust injury and conduct requirements apply.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reinstated Trinko’s antitrust claim after the district court dismissed it.

* **Relief Requested:** Verizon seeks dismissal; Trinko seeks continued class litigation for alleged monopolistic impairment of rival service.

* **Material Record:** Trinko is a customer of AT&T, a competitive local carrier purchasing access from Verizon. Verizon’s interconnection obligations included operations-support systems. Competitors complained of deficient access; the FCC and New York commission investigated and imposed remedial measures and financial consequences. Trinko alleges discriminatory order handling designed to discourage use of rival carriers. Its own injury and the causal relationship to injury suffered directly by AT&T are distinct from the existence of Verizon’s regulatory duty.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a pleading case: supported allegations receive the governing pleading treatment, not findings of deliberate exclusion. Antitrust standing under Associated General Contractors is distinct from Article III and from §2 conduct. Agency remediation does not establish complete compensation or resolve all antitrust elements. The statutory saving clause prevents automatic displacement but does not itself create a cause of action.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Same-day decisions supply no precedent unless effective before this case.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Regulated access and independent monopolization

* **Controlling Law:** CSU v. Xerox rejects conclusive statutory-exclusivity defenses while requiring market power, unlawful maintenance, competitive injury and causation; no general duty to assist rivals follows. Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services rejects categorical economic assumptions inconsistent with supported actual market conditions. Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp. remains an example of unjustified exclusion, not a verified exclusive checklist.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Plaintiff must establish each monopolization element and a competitive mechanism beyond injury to a rival. Genuine statutory entitlements, efficiency, investment and operational explanations have substantive force. Neither refusal nor regulation alone decides legality.

* **Other Governing Law:** Sherman Act §2; Telecommunications Act §§251 and 252; §601(b)(1)’s antitrust saving clause.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The regulatory obligation is broader in some respects than an antitrust duty; its breach neither automatically establishes nor categorically excludes a §2 violation.

* **Open Point:** Whether the pleaded deliberate degradation identifies an independently actionable exclusionary mechanism, and how regulatory structure affects that inquiry.

### Issue 2: Plaintiff and remedy

* **Controlling Law:** the Steamfitters decision applies Associated General Contractors to reject insufficiently direct derivative expenditures while preserving the distinction between consumer status and actual causal directness.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess nature and directness of injury, causal intermediaries, alternative plaintiffs, duplication and workable allocation; consumer identity alone is neither sufficient nor disqualifying.

* **Other Governing Law:** Clayton Act §4 for damages and §16 for prospective relief have distinct predicates; requested relief must respond to the plaintiff’s proved antitrust injury.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** AT&T’s direct operational injury and Trinko’s claimed customer injury cannot simply be treated as identical.

* **Open Point:** Whether this complaint supplies a sufficiently direct personal antitrust injury and a viable remedy rather than a private action to supervise all interconnection compliance.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Verizon argues that compelled access is not a voluntarily supplied product, that supervision belongs to specialized regulators, and that ordinary monopoly law imposes no general obligation to support rivals. The United States urges reversal under established antitrust limits. Trinko argues the saving clause preserves a claim for purposeful exclusion through discriminatory access and that regulation does not immunize the conduct. The Second Circuit permitted broader refusal and leveraging theories. Any theory of monopolization must still establish the required monopoly-maintenance or attempt elements; using power in one market is not a free-standing offense without the legally required exclusion and market consequences.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Dismissal could rest on a properly established directness defect or failure to plead an independent §2 mechanism. A bounded remand can distinguish regulatory breach from antitrust exclusion and assess the actual allegations without a categorical immunity or compulsory-dealing entitlement. A rule making every access breach an antitrust offense would displace the separate statutory structures; a rule immunizing every regulated input refusal would conflict with the entering rejection of conclusive statutory defenses. Relief, if ultimately earned, must cure proved exclusion without turning the district court into the ordinary tariff administrator.

Opinions and record.

### Issue 1: Protection of a communications instrumentality

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lopez preserves federal authority over interstate instrumentalities. The instrumentality cases distinguish a direct network or instrumentality relationship from an object’s remote manufacturing history.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the instrumentality actually used and the statutory conduct directed at it. A local instance does not defeat protection of an interstate system, but a historical interstate component does not federalize unrelated conduct.

Carnes argues that wholly intrastate calls and conduct lack a substantial interstate effect. The Government responds that the telephone network is an interstate instrumentality and unauthorized interception directly compromises it. ## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the statutory offense reaches materially different conduct without the asserted network relationship, analyze that actual provision rather than add an element. No wholesale affirmance of unrelated sentencing components follows from rejection of the commerce ground.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The lawful rule covers covered intrastate calls carried by an interstate telephone system. It does not establish federal power over all face-to-face conversations or possession of an imported recorder.
