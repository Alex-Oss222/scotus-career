## 8. Yarborough v. Gentry

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Yarborough v. Gentry

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1597; 540 U.S. 1

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-10-20

* **Question Presented:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied the Sixth Amendment standard by rejecting an ineffective-assistance challenge to defense counsel's closing argument.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the federal district court's denial of habeas relief after California courts rejected the assistance claim.

* **Relief Requested:** The warden seeks reversal of the federal habeas judgment; Gentry seeks a new trial through a conditional writ.

* **Material Record:** Gentry admitted stabbing his girlfriend but claimed accident. Counsel challenged an eyewitness's inconsistent description, opposed reliance on Gentry's criminal history, and identified intent as the dispute. Counsel also used disparaging descriptions of his client and omitted some potentially favorable points. The claim concerns the actual summation, not an established absence of investigation.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The state merits rejection triggers §2254(d)(1). The constitutional rule must have been clearly established at the state adjudication, not supplied by a later case. An inadequate argument is not automatically complete abandonment.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** Mitchell v. Esparza concerns a capital charging and eligibility omission rather than counsel's summation; the two records share a statutory habeas gateway, not the underlying constitutional claim.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Closing argument and federal habeas limits

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor and Bell v. Cone require objective unreasonableness at the statutory gateway, with ordinary Strickland performance and prejudice remaining distinct. Bell v. Cone rejects presumed prejudice where substantive advocacy occurred. Lockyer v. Andrade confirms that §2254(d)(1) requires a contrary governing rule or an objectively unreasonable application, rather than independent error alone; clearly established substantive law is measured at the state merits adjudication without demanding a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record rather than isolated phrases or a correct formula alone.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Evaluate the supported defense as a whole, including informed tactical choices; a strategy label is not dispositive. Petitioner must establish deficient performance and reasonable-probability prejudice unless an actual Cronic category applies.

* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1).

* **Material Other Authority:** Strickland v. Washington and Herring v. New York supply the underlying performance and closing-argument principles.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Closing is protected advocacy. Neither perfect presentation nor every arguably useful point is required.

* **Open Point:** Whether this particular summation falls outside the range of reasonable representation and whether the state rejection exceeded the lawful range.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit treated the omitted arguments, derogatory language and counsel's treatment of credibility as deficient advocacy. The warden argues that counsel concentrated on intent and reasonable doubt, confronted damaging evidence, and made choices a reasonable advocate could make. Gentry argues that counsel abandoned affirmative reasons to acquit and improperly invited conviction if the jury disbelieved him. The complete argument and jury instructions matter; no particular phrase is conclusive. No independently different Solicitor General rule is verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A reversal can establish only that the state court permissibly rejected this supported tactical-performance claim, leaving genuinely distinct claims untouched. Affirmance would require a supported statutory gateway and both assistance elements, followed by a conditional new-trial writ. A remand may be necessary if the appellate court applied ordinary disagreement instead of §2254(d), but it cannot invent favorable strategy or prejudice facts.

## 22. Maryland v. Pringle

The state high court regarded the rear-seat drugs and location of the cash as insufficiently connected to Pringle. ## 23. Castro v. United States

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Castro v. United States

* **Question Presented:** Whether an unwarned judicial recharacterization of a pro se Rule 33 motion as a first §2255 motion may make a later expressly filed §2255 motion second or successive; and whether the authorization-review bar prevents review of that classification.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed dismissal of Castro’s 1997 motion as an unauthorized second or successive §2255 motion.

* **Relief Requested:** Castro seeks first-motion treatment of the 1997 filing and adjudication under the ordinary applicable rules; the United States seeks affirmance and raises a jurisdictional objection.

* **Material Record:** Castro filed a pro se Rule 33 motion in 1994. The district court denied it, referring to both Rule 33 and §2255, without notifying him of recharacterization, warning of successive-filing consequences, or offering withdrawal or amendment. His pro se appeal did not contest that label. In 1997 he expressly invoked §2255 and raised additional ineffective-assistance claims; the later dismissal relied on the earlier conversion.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Castro did not request circuit authorization to file a successive application. Classification and an actual authorization denial are different actions. The merits of the ineffective-assistance claim, limitations and other independent gates are not established by correcting the filing classification.

* **Relevant Dates:** The first filing was July 5, 1994; the express §2255 motion was April 18, 1997. The distinction matters because the later motion followed AEDPA, while the unannounced conversion preceded it.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

### Issue 1: Review channel

* **Controlling Law:** Felker v. Turpin bars ordinary certiorari review of an actual circuit authorization grant or denial. Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal permits review of the distinct determination whether authorization is required; a court’s label is not dispositive.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the action reviewed and the precise statutory restriction before reaching the merits.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§2244(b)(3)(E) and 2255, as amended by AEDPA.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Court cannot review an actual authorization denial indirectly, but the statute does not turn every first-motion classification into one.

* **Open Point:** Whether the present classification fits the reviewable category.

### Issue 2: Recharacterization and meaningful first review

* **Controlling Law:** Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal and Lonchar v. Thomas require the stated predicates for successive treatment and lawful dismissal; they do not create an equitable power to disregard enacted gates.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A procedural label with permanent consequences must be distinguished from a litigant’s informed election to invoke the remedy. No existing holding requires these specific warnings.

* **Other Governing Law:** Section 2255’s restrictions on second or successive motions; Rule 33’s distinct new-trial procedure; ordinary federal authority to construe pro se filings.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Courts may read a pro se submission for the relief it seeks, but existing law does not resolve when involuntary conversion consumes the first §2255 opportunity.

* **Open Point:** Whether notice, warning and an opportunity to withdraw or amend must precede a conversion carrying that consequence.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The lower court treated the 1994 merits ruling and unchallenged recharacterization as sufficient. Castro argues that conversion cannot silently forfeit his opportunity to present all collateral claims. The United States argues that the prior characterization is law of the case and that the circuit’s observation about successive requirements implicates the review bar. A warning must permit an informed decision both about amendment and about challenging the conversion; absence of an appeal does not by itself demonstrate that understanding. Competing positions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may distinguish classification from authorization and require warnings before a converted motion counts for future successive restrictions. That would vacate the dismissal without deciding entitlement to collateral relief. Alternatively, treating any prior substantive §2255 adjudication as sufficient would preserve the dismissal but require an explanation of why the litigant’s chosen procedural vehicle is immaterial. A prohibition on all beneficial recharacterization is broader than necessary to resolve the adverse successive-filing consequence.
