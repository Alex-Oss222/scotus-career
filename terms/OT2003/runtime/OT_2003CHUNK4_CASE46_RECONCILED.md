## Issue-specific historical comparison

History supplies the same five participating Associates for affirmance and O'Connor and Scalia for remand. Its material disagreement is whether the repeated refusal itself is an unusual external event, not whether this was a tragic injury or a negligent failure to give aid. The original independent all-affirm model did not survive correction of participation and the complete response sequence. The corrected model squarely confronts the historical distinction between acknowledgment of inaction and an affirmative event with an independent causal role. No remaining judgment departure requires a changed-premise justification.

The trial-supported finding treats the repeated refusals as unusual in light of policy and practice, and the carrier did not challenge that characterization in the appellate court. The Court therefore need not establish a general industry duty or make an original finding about every request. Nor does the accepted causal significance of the refusals as a whole establish that the distinct false statement independently caused a lost chance of successful self-help.

Saks supplies the unexpected or unusual external event requirement, its application to all the circumstances and its causal-chain rule. An ordinary physiological response to normal carriage alone is insufficient; an unusual external link need not be the sole or final physiological cause. Current Zicherman keeps domestic loss categories subject to treaty liability conditions. Current Tseng makes those conditions exclusive within Article 17's field and preserves Saks; its Article 25 ruling distinguishes intentional touching from established treaty willful misconduct. None changes the premise underlying these historical positions. Chan's refusal to add consequences absent from the treaty supplies an additional date-eligible methodological test, not a new command deciding this case.

## Substantive reasons and reservations for assembly

The five affirming Associates accept that rejection of an explicit request can be an event or happening. Here the repeated responses after notice of the medical problem, considered in their actual setting, were the accepted unusual external causal link. Merely identifying ordinary smoke as the final physical stimulus does not exclude another event in the chain. The carrier's action/inaction terminology cannot replace that inquiry. This does not make every failure to prevent injury, deviation from airline practice, medical crisis or intentional act a treaty accident. No revised damages award or new Article 25 finding is supplied.

O'Connor and Scalia answer that the event requirement and unusualness requirement are cumulative. Speech acknowledging nonperformance does not become the accident merely because the withheld service would have prevented injury. They distinguish the pre-takeoff directions from the in-flight permission to seek a willing passenger; no finding establishes the relevant pre-takeoff policy or an independently sufficient causal role after permission to move. The false assertion that the aircraft was full differs because it conveyed affirmative information about available seats. The lower court may consider, under the existing record and ordinary preservation rules, whether that representation was unusual and whether it actually prevented an effective response. Eleven empty seats prove neither reliance nor causation. The proposed remand does not invent a claim, authorize automatic additional evidence, or decide that respondents actually preserved a distinct theory.

Historically O'Connor joined only Parts I and II of Scalia's dissent, not its introductory comparison between foreign treaty interpretation and constitutional uses of foreign law. That limited historical join cannot be flattened into an all-opinion join. In the present model both dissenting Associates accept the stated domestic core; either may join a writing containing that core without importing historical authorship or an unmodeled introduction. A proposed introduction with additional claims must undergo its own join test.

The full foreign judgments in Deep Vein Thrombosis, [2003] EWCA Civ. 1005, and Qantas v. Povey, [2003] VSCA 227, remain unavailable under the neutral research receipt. The historical dissent was fully read and establishes what Scalia and O'Connor historically accepted; it is not represented as a full reading of either foreign judgment. This reconciliation neither invents foreign rules nor treats research unavailability as a changed litigant record or as a reason to reverse a historical vote. No asserted foreign consensus or attack on the Court's treatment of those unread opinions is required by the supported current writing.
