## 24. In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment as to the identified EEOC and voucher-class successor claims.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the free-and-clear transfer and the injunction against these successor claims in place; preserve claims against the TWA estate and adjudication under the statutory distribution process. Dispose of the ancillary stay appeal consistently with the merits.

* **Remedial Reach:** The litigating EEOC claimants and the represented voucher class as bound by lawful process; this holding does not enlarge the order against unknown or unnotified persons.

* **Practical Relief:** American need not honor the specified obligations as successor solely because of this asset purchase. The bankruptcy court must continue claims administration and distribution under the Code; it may enforce the sale order only within the statutory and notice boundaries, without inventing a guaranteed recovery.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Defined successor claims in a free-and-clear sale

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the presented claims satisfy the Code’s property and monetary-satisfaction predicates.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose a text-based construction of an unresolved application, preserving separate statutory safeguards.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must explain these enterprise-connected claims rather than rest on airline rescue. A broader all-liabilities discharge would erase the property limitation and notice protections. A liens-only rule would read the statute’s separate treatment of liens as exhaustive without textual support.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 363(f) permits sale free of a successor liability that arises from the debtor’s use of the transferred operating assets and can be compelled into monetary satisfaction under applicable legal process, when the affected claimant receives the process required by law. The sale proponent bears the ordinary civil burden of establishing those statutory predicates. The presented discrimination claims and monetizable voucher obligations meet them; their estate claims remain governed by bankruptcy priority and allowance rules.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The identified pre-sale successor claims against American, not the purchaser’s independent post-sale violations, unrelated claims without a property connection, or unnotified persons whose rights were not lawfully adjudicated.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those distinct claims are decided, courts enforce ordinary notice, statutory sale predicates and independent liability rules; this order cannot be cited as a blanket immunity for the purchaser.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** In re Dow Corning Corp. rejects general equitable authority to extinguish independent nondebtor claims. This sale therefore requires §363(f)'s own predicates, not simply reorganization necessity. Section 363(f)(3) treats liens as a subset of interests, while subsection (5) supplies the monetary-satisfaction condition applied to these property-connected successor obligations. Preserving estate claims and priority supports the statutory design but does not itself authorize extinguishment. Adequate notice and any properly invoked §363(e) protection remain necessary; American's independent misconduct after the sale is outside the sale's release.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish In re Dow Corning Corp.'s independent nondebtor releases from the identified §363(f) property-connected successor claims; apply Patterson v. Shumate's textual method and preserve notice, priority and adequate-protection requirements.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Claims of persons without adequate notice, future injuries, and independent purchaser wrongdoing require facts or claims not before the Court.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Supported public-procurement remediation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the evidentiary and procedural grounds support judgment for Denver under current law.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Adarand’s common substantial-justification rule, not the lower court's constitutional vocabulary.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The complete procurement program and evidentiary method are presented. A general license for racial balancing would lack the necessary lawful objective and fit. A requirement of adjudicated discrimination against each beneficiary would disable supported remediation beyond what entering law requires.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public contracting classification survives when the government establishes an important lawful remedial objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, accounting for actual burdens, covered groups and supported practicable alternatives. Linked statistical and anecdotal evidence may establish barriers in the procurement market without prior judgments against each contractor. Denver’s supported record, together with the binding unchallenged fit determination and absence of a material amendment reopening it, satisfies that burden here; the women-owned-business provisions also satisfy the distinct important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged ordinances and litigated record, including live 1990 damages and 1998 prospective claims. The rule does not presume that every disparity is discrimination or that every listed group is always properly included.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until such a later challenge is decided, courts apply Adarand’s government-borne justification rule and ordinary preclusion and preservation principles; prior validity is not permanent immunity from changed facts.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand rejects categorical levels keyed to which government acts and requires substantive justification. Its rule permits evidence of the market in which public dollars operate, while Engineering Contractors requires attention to genuinely supported alternatives. The district court’s demand for narrower direct proof disregarded probative combined evidence. The waiver ruling leaves an established fit determination rather than an evidentiary void; CWC identified no substantial amendment requiring fresh adjudication. The constitutional burden remains Denver’s throughout. Grutter confirms that concrete practicable alternatives require a supported answer, not exhaustion of every conceivable method. Gratz shows that general historical exclusion cannot replace the connection between a remedial mechanism, its coverage and its burdens. Denver's procurement evidence and the preserved fit determination must satisfy that same substantive discipline; neither admissions outcome determines this distinct record.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Adarand Constructors v. Peña, Engineering Contractors Association v. Metropolitan Dade County and City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters. Do not adopt the appellate formulation placing ultimate constitutional justification on CWC.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially changed ordinance or demonstrated new unsupported classification is outside this preserved record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reviewable statutory harm

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether generalized mosaic assertions justify this categorical withholding.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply FOIA’s government-borne burden and extend Landano’s supported-category method to the distinct statutory harm under Exemption 7(A).

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A usable category rule is needed for sensitive investigations. A universal public-name requirement would ignore established exemptions. A blanket national-security presumption would eliminate Congress’s required judicial determination.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An agency withholding law-enforcement information under Exemption 7(A) must show, through sufficiently specific facts or supported categories, how disclosure could reasonably be expected to interfere with an identified pending or reasonably anticipated enforcement proceeding. Courts may protect sensitive supporting material, but must independently assess the nexus and release reasonably segregable nonexempt information. The undifferentiated treatment here requires renewed review separating active investigative connections, concluded detentions and independently protected records.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Requested agency records and properly defined categories, including identities and lawyer information where the necessary harm is actually established; not judicial authority to disregard a lawful sealing order.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those grounds are decided, courts apply each exemption’s own predicates, use justified protective review and segregate releasable material; no blanket release or blanket secrecy follows.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Landano permits category inferences supported by their circumstances rather than an agency label. The same discipline gives effect to 7(A)’s different text without demanding disclosure of the very intelligence being protected. The seriousness of the investigation explains the potential harm but not why each category, including concluded detention records, necessarily reveals an active operation. Other exemptions may still justify particular withholding.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend United States Department of Justice v. Landano’s burden-and-category reasoning without importing its confidentiality element into 7(A).

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The existing record does not resolve all independent privacy, safety, grand-jury and sealing grounds.

### Issue 2: Access theories

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Constitution independently requires this executive list.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering distinction between general access and discriminatory access conditions.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The requested executive compilation is the vehicle. A ruling on courtroom closure would reach different institutions and records. Avoiding the access question entirely would leave an independently asserted route unresolved.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The First Amendment does not create a general right to an executive investigative compilation merely because it identifies detained persons, and the common-law access claim presented here does not displace FOIA’s specific regime for these agency records. A speech-selected access restriction or a particular judicial proceeding remains governed by its own law.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The asserted general right to the requested DOJ compilation; no adjudication of a detainee’s access to counsel, judicial process or freedom to disclose personal information.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those distinct claims are decided, courts apply the existing access and due-process rules for the institution and person involved.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** North Jersey Media Group requires a justified, appropriately limited closure of adversarial removal proceedings; a categorical national-security label is insufficient. That holding does not entitle a requester to an executive compilation of every detainee's identity. United Reporting separately addresses speech-selected access conditions, which are not established here. The hearing-access rule, individual detention rights and FOIA's record-specific duties each remain enforceable through their own legal channels.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply United Reporting to access classifications and distinguish North Jersey Media Group v. Ashcroft's qualified removal-hearing access without weakening its closure safeguards.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific judicial or removal-hearing closure and individual detention or counsel claims are outside this record.

## 29. Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court must assess the pleaded claim and any properly permitted amendment, dismissing a deficient theory and allowing a sufficient ordinary §2 claim to proceed. No categorical regulatory immunity and no automatic restoration of every claim.

* **Remedial Reach:** Trinko’s named and proposed class claims at the pleading stage; no proved exclusionary policy has yet earned a broader injunction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The independent antitrust claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** What the saving clause preserves and what a plaintiff must establish beyond a regulatory breach.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply CSU v. Xerox and the ordinary monopolization requirements to regulated network inputs; reject both automatic liability and categorical immunity.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring interaction of regulatory duties and antitrust law is squarely presented. A general duty to share all monopoly assets lacks legal support. An exclusive prior-voluntary-dealing gateway would preclude supported exclusion claims without analyzing their mechanism.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A regulated firm’s breach of a statutory access duty violates §2 only when the plaintiff independently establishes monopoly power, exclusionary acquisition or maintenance, and causally connected antitrust injury; regulation neither supplies those elements nor immunizes their violation. The plaintiff bears the ordinary civil burden. Deliberate discriminatory degradation of access may qualify when it is proved to maintain monopoly power through an exclusionary mechanism and lacks an adequate business justification, but a service lapse, rival injury or regulatory finding alone is insufficient.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Regulated-input conduct meeting the ordinary antitrust elements, including supported discriminatory degradation. The holding creates no universal essential-facilities entitlement and no free-standing monopoly-leveraging offense.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until liability is adjudicated, courts require a concrete pleaded and proved monopoly-maintenance mechanism, give actual regulatory remedies and legitimate operational explanations their proper weight, and avoid treating regulatory noncompliance as conclusive antitrust proof.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** CSU v. Xerox preserves substantive statutory justifications but rejects their use as conclusive commercial-refusal immunity. The saving clause points to the same separation here. Aspen Skiing demonstrates how actual conduct and justification can identify exclusion; prior voluntary cooperation is probative but is not an exclusive element adopted in the law. Eastman Kodak requires evidence about real competitive constraints rather than abstract assumptions. The regulatory system bears on incentives, actual access, available remedies and business explanations, but its existence alone cannot erase an express saving clause.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend CSU v. Xerox’s separation of statutory rights, conduct and competitive injury to this regulatory setting; preserve Eastman Kodak and Aspen Skiing. No general duty to aid competitors is adopted.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The pleading record does not establish the ultimate exclusion, justification, market facts or damages.

### Issue 2: Trinko’s causal position and the judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this reinstatement can stand without separate attention to the plaintiff and the independent theory.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering directness rule and require a correctly framed claim before merits discovery proceeds.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow as to the present procedural disposition. A final liability judgment would require facts not found. A simple affirmance of an undifferentiated regulatory-breach theory would omit necessary elements.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A retail customer seeking antitrust damages for interference with its carrier must allege an injury sufficiently direct under Associated General Contractors and identify the independent exclusionary mechanism that caused it; customer status alone does not establish entitlement. Vacate the reinstatement and remand for that assessment, permitting amendment only under ordinary pleading rules. If the claim alleges merely derivative carrier losses or regulatory noncompliance without an independent §2 mechanism, dismiss it; if it adequately alleges both direct antitrust injury and that mechanism, allow the properly bounded claim to proceed.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The named plaintiff and proposed class claims, with class treatment and individual injury still unadjudicated.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those matters are adjudicated, courts apply ordinary pleading and class rules and the directness factors; no antitrust damages or ongoing access decree is authorized by this remand.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** the Steamfitters decision rejects shifting derivative expenses into an antitrust action merely because they follow unlawful conduct. It does not hold that all customers of an injured competitor are categorically remote. The lower court must identify what Trinko itself lost, the intervening causal steps and risk of duplicate recovery. The governing pleading standard, not a newly invented evidence requirement, controls at this stage.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Associated General Contractors and the Steamfitters directness holding without making noncompetitor status dispositive.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual directness findings, an amended complaint, certification and final relief are not in the record.

## 32. Kontrick v. Ryan

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Seventh Circuit judgment.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the adjudicated discharge denial intact; reject the first post-merits timing objection.

* **Remedial Reach:** Kontrick’s discharge proceeding and the litigated family-account claim; no broader creditor class relief is ordered.

* **Practical Relief:** The bankruptcy court may administer the estate with the discharge denial in force. No new hearing or reconsideration of the conceded timing point is required, and this judgment does not adjudicate unrelated creditor claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A mandatory defense raised too late

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this debtor may undo the merits adjudication through the missed filing defense.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering jurisdiction/procedure distinction and resolve its reserved forfeiture consequence here.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A clear rule is needed for objections first raised after merits adjudication. Broader permission to disregard timely objections would contradict the rule’s mandatory terms. A debtor-specific exception would leave similarly situated litigants free to gamble on the merits.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The filing periods in Bankruptcy Rules 4004 and 9006 do not limit subject-matter jurisdiction, and a debtor forfeits their protection by litigating an objection to discharge without raising untimeliness until after the court adjudicates its merits. The creditor retains the burden of proving the discharge objection; the debtor must timely invoke the procedural defense. This conclusion does not authorize an extension forbidden by the rules when a timely objection is made.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The specified bankruptcy deadlines and this post-merits forfeiture; not every statutory time limit or every earlier stage at which a defense might be lost.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those distinct questions are decided, courts enforce the rule’s text and existing extension limits when timely invoked; this holding supplies no equitable enlargement power.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Carlisle enforced a timely invoked deadline and expressly left forfeiture open. Its distinction between remedial authority and subject-matter jurisdiction is decisive, not an obstacle. Henderson confirms that procedural timing must be classified from its source and operation. Congress granted jurisdiction over discharge objections, while the rules regulate their processing and disavow jurisdictional enlargement or contraction.
