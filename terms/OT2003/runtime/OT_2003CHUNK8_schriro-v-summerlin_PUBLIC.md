## Event

Schriro v. Summerlin, No. 03-526, 542 U.S. 348. Argued April 19, 2004; decided June 24, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, sitting en banc, 341 F.3d 1082.

Summerlin’s murder judgment became final in 1984. The trial judge found a prior violent felony and exceptional heinousness, cruelty or depravity and imposed death under Arizona’s former scheme. His federal petition preceded AEDPA and remained pending when Ring v. Arizona was decided. The Ninth Circuit granted Ring-based sentencing relief. The question is whether the jury determination of an indispensable death-eligibility fact applies retroactively. Review excludes whether the prior-conviction aggravator independently established eligibility.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded. Ring is available under the confined watershed exception, but the actual error and requirements for sentence relief must be adjudicated.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded with the Ring claim available, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the Ring-based grant and require rejection of that claim under Teague.

The rejection of the retroactivity bar is sustained on the watershed ground. The lower court must determine the actual eligibility omission and apply the governing collateral effect standard before entering any conditional sentence relief. The murder conviction and independently reviewable claims remain distinct. The prior-conviction sufficiency question remains outside this Court’s grant.

## Holdings

### I. Ring changes procedure rather than the substantive reach of punishment

**Holding and operative rule.** Ring’s allocation to the jury of an otherwise required statutory aggravating-fact determination is a new procedural rule for a judgment final in 1984. It does not change the substantive aggravators, conduct or classes of persons legally subject to death, so the substantive-rule exception does not apply.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join Part I.

**Controlling explanation.** The aggravating circumstance was already necessary under Arizona law, and the State already required proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Ring changes who must determine that fact. Calling the fact an element explains its constitutional protection but does not itself remove conduct or a class of offenders from the State’s power to punish. Caspari and Horn require consideration of a preserved retroactivity defense before granting relief; classification is one step in that inquiry. Because the rule was not dictated at Summerlin’s 1984 finality, its availability depends on the separately preserved exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

### II. The indispensable capital-eligibility determination satisfies the watershed exception

**Holding and operative rule.** In a jury-tried prosecution, Ring’s protection for the determination of a present-offense fact indispensable to authorizing death, subject to valid admission and lawful waiver requirements, satisfies both the exceptional accuracy and bedrock-procedure requirements for watershed application on collateral review. Qualifying claims may proceed under the legal regime otherwise governing the petition.

**Authority.** Part II is joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Controlling explanation.** The missing safeguard operates at the factual boundary between imprisonment and death. Independent collective scrutiny of the prosecution’s case is absent at the point where the irreversible punishment first becomes lawful. That deficiency creates an impermissibly large risk in this confined category, especially when an aggravator calls for evaluation of exceptional cruelty rather than a merely mechanical fact. A correct burden of proof does not itself supply that collective assessment. Jury determination of a punishment-authorizing fact also belongs to the basic structure of a fair criminal adjudication; making it effective at this boundary changes understanding of an indispensable safeguard.

O’Dell recognizes a confined watershed safeguard against a materially misleading capital-information premise, and Tyler recognizes the exceptional significance of an adjudication lacking the required verdict-wide reasonable-doubt safeguard. Neither already decides Ring, but both require examination of how a missing procedure operates. DeStefano’s refusal to reopen ordinary noncapital bench trials under an earlier retroactivity framework supplies substantial caution: judges can find facts reliably. It does not resolve this confined capital question under Teague’s two requirements. The Court’s conclusion rests on the withheld eligibility determination and its evaluative function, not death’s severity alone, presumed judicial incompetence or a general preference for greater mercy. Ultimate discretionary selection among already authorized punishments is distinct from the eligibility rule applied here.

### III. Availability does not establish an omission’s effect or authorize an immediate writ

**Holding and operative rule.** The lower court must determine whether a necessary death-eligibility finding was withheld from the jury and address the remaining prerequisites to relief on the actual record. For a properly reached, nonstructural eligibility omission, Brecht’s governing collateral rule places on the State the burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the death sentence.

**Authority.** Part III is joined by the same five Justices. It directs the remaining adjudication under existing law; the separate writings preserve the differing views about the detailed actual-record method.

**Controlling explanation.** Ring separates the jury entitlement from an actual error’s remedial consequence, and Brecht supplies the collateral effect standard. Retroactivity neither establishes that an indispensable fact was omitted nor resolves contribution to the sentence. Lindh and Carriger preserve the former merits and factual-presumption regime for this pre-AEDPA petition; Slack separately governs the later appeal’s certificate. If the remaining requirements are met and the State fails its effect burden, conditional sentence relief must permit lawful correction under governing law. Further sentencing requires independent state authority and compliance with applicable constitutional limits. Whether the prior violent conviction independently supplied eligibility was excluded from review and must receive its own lawful treatment within the grant’s limits.

## Precedent Treatment

Ring v. Arizona is classified as procedural and its reserved collateral question is answered for indispensable present-offense death-eligibility findings. Its distinction between eligibility and ultimate selection, and its qualified actual-adjudication method, retain their existing force. O’Dell v. Netherland and Tyler v. Cain are extended through their confined accuracy-and-bedrock principles; neither exception holding is narrowed. DeStefano v. Woods is distinguished at the capital-eligibility boundary and under the later Teague framework.

Caspari v. Bohlen and Horn v. Banks supply the obligation to resolve the preserved retroactivity defense. Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart preserve the petition’s former-law merits regime, while Slack v. McDaniel separately governs appellate certification. Brecht v. Abrahamson, as applied in O’Neal v. McAninch, supplies the State-borne collateral effect standard. Sullivan v. Louisiana retains its distinct rule for the absence of a valid verdict-wide reasonable-doubt determination.

## Law After Decision

Ring’s indispensable present-offense death-eligibility factfinding protection is available on collateral review through a confined watershed application. The rule remains procedural, and ordinary finality rules continue outside that category. Each qualifying petitioner must establish the actual violation and satisfy the otherwise applicable conditions for relief; the State bears the governing beyond-reasonable-doubt effect burden for a properly reached nonstructural omission.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs, joined by Justices Stevens and Souter. Ring’s qualified actual-adjudication method requires attention to the actual instructions, the evidence they directed to the jury, necessary findings and material conflicts. Genuine equivalent jury findings can establish noncontribution but are not the exclusive route: reliable whole-record inferences must remain connected to the actual adjudication. New credibility findings, sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict and appellate reweighing that supplies a missing eligibility adjudication cannot substitute for that inquiry. The State’s burden under Brecht remains separate from the finding of retroactive availability.

Justice Ginsburg concurs. She joins the procedural classification, the confined watershed holding and the bounded remand, but retains her view that harmlessness for the omitted eligibility determination requires genuinely equivalent actual findings. A hypothetical jury verdict or the court’s assessment that the evidence was strong cannot supply them. Her narrower method does not turn retroactivity alone into an entitlement to sentence relief.

Justice Breyer concurs. His support rests on the jury’s independent collective role in reliable capital adjudication, including the community evaluation required by an aggravator such as exceptional cruelty. He joins the confined eligibility holding without adopting the general Apprendi theory for noncapital sentencing, and preserves his broader Eighth Amendment view of the jury’s role in choosing death without making that additional issue part of this decision.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in procedural classification and dissents from retroactivity. Constitutional allocation of authority on direct review does not establish the extraordinary accuracy showing needed to reopen final judgments. Arizona already required aggravating facts to be proved beyond reasonable doubt, and a competent judge’s findings are not shown to create the necessary impermissible accuracy risk merely because a jury is constitutionally required. The evaluative nature and capital importance of the finding do not, in their view, bridge that gap. O’Dell’s misleading-parole-premise protection and Tyler’s invalid verdict-wide burden remain distinct. They would reverse the Ring-based grant under Teague and preserve genuinely remaining independent claims.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for determination of the actual eligibility error and the remaining collateral requirements under the petition’s applicable former-law regime. Conditional sentence relief depends on those determinations and the State’s failure to establish the required noncontribution; further sentencing must have its own lawful authority.
