## Historical comparison and changed-premise test

Historically the Court unanimously reversed the reviewed ground for sentence relief, rejected categorical structural treatment, found the state application within the statutory range, and remanded while leaving other grounds open. This was a definitive rejection of the reviewed omission theory, not merely a direction to redo its gateway analysis.

The historical opinion already considered the predicates on which provisional Path B relies. Its footnote 2 addressed Presnell and distinguished an unnotified, unsubmitted different charge from this incomplete allegation/instruction. Footnote 3 expressly acknowledged the later federal evidence suggesting Jasso's participation and distinguished it from the trial record used for the state adjudication. Footnote 4 preserved other habeas grounds. The body recognized the two state decisions and relied on the actual purposeful-killing and aggravated-robbery instructions. Accordingly, the later evidence, availability of independent claims, and distinction between notice and narrowing are not changed historical premises.

The actual simulated Neder and Ring holdings do differ from historical formulations: they require a qualified inquiry into noncontribution to the actual verdict and reject strong evidence or a predicted hypothetical verdict as an automatic substitute. Their present force must be respected. But they do not create clearly established 1992/1994 constitutional duties, move the state-adjudication cutoff, make a later rule retroactive at 1989 finality, or remove the independent statutory gateway. The common Williams, Visciotti, Andrade and completed Gentry law expressly preserves those distinctions. Simulated Neder and Ring can govern the method for a legally available error; they cannot themselves establish that the earlier state disposition was objectively unreasonable.

The historical result also does not require adoption here of a general hypothetical-verdict rule. The supported state-record inference is narrower: the jury was asked whether Esparza purposefully caused the death while committing aggravated robbery, under a trial theory and evidence identifying one robber/shooter, and separately considered the robbery specification beyond a reasonable doubt. The indictment's death-specification cover and sole-defendant construction support notice, but are not alone treated as a jury finding. The state could reasonably regard the actual adjudication as supplying the principal-offender culpability rather than creating a different aggravator after trial. The later evidence may matter to a separately available claim under its own requirements; it does not retrospectively become trial evidence or automatically displace that statutory conclusion.

Provisional Path B correctly insists that sufficiency alone is not harmlessness and that no categorical capital exception disposes of every component. Its proposed continued inquiry, however, identifies no additional changed fact, earlier governing rule or material prior individual position that makes historical definitive rejection inapplicable at this statutory level. The contemporaneous charge and state adjudication cannot be disregarded because the operator did not recover the entire original charge. Nor does a newer review-method preference itself justify keeping a statutorily defeated theory open. The historical match is therefore strong evidence supporting correction of these four provisional determinations after the full source review, not a command to reproduce all historical reasoning.

## Reconciled propositions and complete remedy

All eight retain C1–C4 from the provisional handoff. All eight now also support A1–A3 at their stated statutory scope:

1. Reject the Sixth Circuit's categorical rule that this capital allegation/eligibility omission cannot receive harmless-error review. The invalid reasonable-doubt standard in Sullivan is distinct from omission of this issue, and contemporaneous capital-error precedents permit meaningful error review.
2. Reject the reviewed omission ground under §2254(d)(1). On the actual state record and law available at the appropriate adjudication, the state disposition is neither contrary to the governing holdings nor an objectively unreasonable application of them. Read the actual instructions, theory and adjudication together; sole indictment, sufficient evidence and a state-law label are not individually dispositive.
3. Reverse the sentence-writ affirmance insofar as it rests on that reviewed ground and remand for the independently remaining sentence grounds. The district court's other grounds and the preserved sentencing-assistance, continuance and cumulative-error alternatives require their own adjudication. Any properly available distinct later-evidence claim retains its own legal conditions. The convictions and already rejected guilt-phase claims remain outside this grant.

These propositions do not find a fresh constitutional violation or make an independent de novo harmlessness determination under the later Neder/Ring framework. The sufficient statutory rejection makes that further inquiry unnecessary for this reviewed ground. The remand does not erase every basis for the existing sentence writ or authorize implementation of death while independent relief remains operative.
