## 64. BedRoc Limited, LLC v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand for judgment implementing the owners’ title to the ordinary sand and gravel.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the Government’s reservation-based claim to these deposits and adjudicate any remaining accounting consistently with that construction.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parties and deposits covered by the disputed patents; this title judgment does not distribute rights under other statutes.
* **Practical Relief:** On mandate, the lower court must reject mineral-trespass liability resting solely on ownership reserved by this provision and resolve any remaining lawful claim separately.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Meaning of the mineral reservation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether ordinary sand and gravel are valuable minerals reserved to the United States by the Pittman Underground Water Act of 1919.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve an open statutory question by applying contextual interpretation, without overruling a construction of a different enactment.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The enacted reservation and reliance in existing patents justify a statute-specific rule. Broader reconsideration of every mineral reservation is unnecessary. A single-parcel exception would fail to interpret the recurring statutory term.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Pittman Act’s reservation of valuable minerals does not reserve ordinary sand and gravel merely because a later market makes their extraction profitable. The Government must establish reserved title under the enactment; present profitability alone is insufficient. The commonplace deposits in this record were conveyed with the surface estate.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Ordinary sand and gravel under Pittman Act patents; distinct deposits or differently worded land grants require their own construction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Western Nuclear construed a different statutory reservation and does not erase a material textual qualifier here. Reading the enacted phrase against its mining-law setting gives the reservation a stable legal meaning and avoids changing the patent’s estate whenever nearby development raises prices.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Watt v. Western Nuclear is distinguished by statute, not overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
