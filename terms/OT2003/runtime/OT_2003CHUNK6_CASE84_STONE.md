## 84. Benjamin v. Fraser

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2003, subject to any controlling decision effective before this case's event date.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the two presented legal distinctions and preserve remand for provisions lacking the required current, tailored findings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the district court to match each continued obligation to a proved current violation and lawful correction, retaining nonadjudicative monitoring needed to verify compliance.
* **Remedial Reach:** The affected detainee classes and facilities where an unlawful practice is proved. Under §3626, relief reaches the practice and those subject to it to the extent necessary to cure it; the City bears the showing that a narrower cure fully works.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify current deficiencies, assign responsible officials and compliance dates, require verifiable condition reports, and terminate unsupported or completed obligations. OCC reports remain evidence to assess, not binding findings. Failure to meet a valid order permits ordinary judicial enforcement after notice.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Pretrial conditions and continued prospective relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether pretrial detainees challenging longstanding, systemic jail conditions must prove officials’ actual knowledge and disregard of health risks, and whether a nonadjudicative compliance monitor is a special master under 18 U.S.C. §3626(f).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Bell and the existing PLRA rule; resolve the distinct prospective institutional question without extending Farmer’s individual-awareness requirement wholesale.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Systemic pretrial confinement requires a usable institutional rule. Eliminating fault requirements for every damages case would exceed the question. Requiring proof of each official’s private awareness would frustrate correction of demonstrable continuing institutional conditions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For prospective relief from longstanding systemic pretrial confinement conditions, detainees must prove by a preponderance that current conditions expose them to a substantial risk of serious harm and are not reasonably related to, or are excessive in relation to, legitimate nonpunitive objectives. They need not separately prove each responsible official’s subjective awareness. Every retained order must also satisfy §3626’s current-violation and need-narrowness-intrusiveness requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Prospective institutional relief for the pretrial classes, not ordinary negligence, convicted-prisoner Eighth Amendment claims or individual damages liability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a distinct individual-damages claim, courts apply existing due-process and personal-liability law; Farmer continues to govern its Eighth Amendment setting.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bell asks whether pretrial detention conditions amount to punishment without adjudication. Farmer concerns a different constitutional and liability setting. Benjamin v. Jacobson prevents this distinction from turning historic decrees into permanent supervision: present risk and each remedial provision still require supported findings.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Bell v. Wolfish is applied to systemic pretrial conditions; Farmer v. Brennan is distinguished. Benjamin v. Jacobson remains controlling on continued relief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual damages liability and convicted-prisoner claims are outside the presented prospective pretrial dispute.

### Issue 2: Monitoring versus adjudicative mastery

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved dispute permits this legal determination; no missing merits finding is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether pretrial detainees challenging longstanding, systemic jail conditions must prove officials’ actual knowledge and disregard of health risks, and whether a nonadjudicative compliance monitor is a special master under 18 U.S.C. §3626(f).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the functional statutory category, preserving supervision without evasion.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The OCC’s specified powers supply the boundary. Exempting every entity called a monitor would invite circumvention. Treating every information-gathering agent as a master would erase the functional limitation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court-appointed body limited to observing and reporting compliance, without adjudicative powers or findings entitled to judicial deference, is not a special master under §3626(f). The party defending the appointment must establish its actual functions; a body exercising a master’s powers remains subject to the statute regardless of title. The OCC’s identified functions fall outside that category.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Nonadjudicative monitoring only; judicial adoption of binding or presumptively controlling findings requires a separate functional assessment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides a materially expanded appointment, courts assess its actual duties under §3626(f) and may not exempt quasi-judicial authority merely by using the monitor label.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute refers to the work of a master, not every person who assists a judge. The OCC supplies operational information while the court independently determines violations and relief. That distinction preserves both useful monitoring and the statutory safeguards against delegated adjudication.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No governing precedent is overruled; the appointment is classified under the statutory definition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A future expansion of the OCC’s actual powers would require facts absent from this record.
