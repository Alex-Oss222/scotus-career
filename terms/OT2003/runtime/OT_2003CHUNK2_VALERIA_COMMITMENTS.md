Proposition 227 establishes structured English immersion, normally a transition not exceeding one year, with transfer to mainstream English instruction on proficiency. Parental consent is required for each waiver. Waivers are available when the child already knows English; when the child is at least ten and the school finds an alternative better serves English education; or after thirty days in immersion when the school finds special physical, emotional, psychological or educational needs and an alternative better serves educational development. Twenty waivers in a grade at a school require an alternative class. Amendment requires voter approval or a statute furthering the act's purpose passed by two-thirds of each legislative house and signed by the Governor. These are distinct conditions; neither the waiver opportunity nor the amendment route is unlimited local policy discretion.

The trial finding of no discriminatory pedagogical purpose is retained subject to ordinary clear-error review; no supplied basis warrants replacing it. The grant concerns racial political restructuring, not the merits of competing pedagogies or independent statutory language-access duties. Those statutory causes were omitted from the second amended complaint; this grant does not restore them.

## Shared operative propositions

C1. Hunter and Seattle address unequal political decisionmaking based on the racial nature of the issue. Coalition for Economic Equity requires examination of a supported selective relocation of authority over otherwise lawful racial measures; describing the policy as voluntary or a preference does not end the inquiry. No antecedent judgment of discrimination or compulsory remedial duty is a universal prerequisite. Crawford preserves neutral policy withdrawal and ordinary allocation of statewide authority. The constitutional question is the racial decisionmaking mechanism, not an entitlement to a favored program or to local victory.

C2. A State may choose English instruction and centralize educational authority on a neutral subject-matter basis. Demographic concentration among a program's beneficiaries and campaign references to those beneficiaries do not alone establish racial allocation of political authority. Conversely, absence of racial hostility does not conclusively defeat a structurally racial barrier. The no-animus finding and the distinct question whether race actually defines the special political burden must not be merged. Language proficiency is not automatically race, and a deliberate racial proxy must be supported rather than assumed.

C3. If an actual racial mechanism is established, Adarand's current common substantial-justification standard governs: an important lawful objective, substantial evidence-supported relationship to that objective, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Mere educational importance is not a complete fit analysis. The historical strict-scrutiny terminology in the lower opinion supplies no governing standard here. Romer's class-wide foreclosure of ordinary nondiscrimination protection remains distinct and does not independently invalidate every statewide curricular choice.

All eight accept C1–C3 as the governing rules for this record's assessment, without requiring the prior Coalition dissenters to renounce their published disagreement with that decision. They disagree on whether the completed trial and appellate record permits a final rejection under those rules.

## Path R: vacate and remand for the distinct structural inquiry

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur of the judgment rejecting the political-process claim and a bounded remand. The appellate rationale repeatedly makes the absence of a prior discrimination remedy and racial animus decisive. It therefore does not independently answer Coalition's distinct structural inquiry. The disputed mechanism is concrete: California ordinarily leaves a range of educational methods to local officials, while this initiative specially relocates the bilingual-instruction choice to statewide amendment subject to the stated restraints; the affected program overwhelmingly serves Latino and Asian/Pacific Islander pupils, and official campaign materials specifically identified Latino children. These facts support an inquiry into whether racial considerations actually defined the special authority allocation. They do not conclusively establish the violation.

On remand, the lower court must examine the initiative's text and practical allocation, the treatment of comparable educational decisions, the supported campaign/context evidence, the waiver routes and the actual amendment burdens to determine whether the structure selects racial matters or a deliberate racial proxy rather than a neutral curricular subject. This is application of the existing structural rule, not a new mandatory checklist, presumption from demographics or demand to poll voters. Existing findings receive their proper factual review; a finding about hostility or the merits of pedagogy cannot be treated as an already completed finding about the distinct mechanism. The court should use the developed record and undertake only proceedings legally necessary to decide that application.

If no racial mechanism is established, judgment for the State follows. If one is established, the State must satisfy C3 and any relief must address the unequal political barrier, with state-law severability and the rights actually proved; the court may not simply order a preferred curriculum. Vacatur itself grants no permanent injunction or entitlement to bilingual instruction. The law remains operative absent independently justified interim relief during the remand. This route resolves the legal error and gives the lower court the complete standard for the remaining factual/mixed inquiry.

The strongest objection is that the appellate opinion also expressly finds that racial makeup did not shape the allocation, and the underlying trial found neutral pedagogy. These four conclude that the asserted additional conclusion is intertwined with the impermissible remedial-program/animus prerequisites, rather than a demonstrated separate application of the structural rule. The shared legal correction is material, not harmless solely because the State may ultimately prevail. The strongest broader reversal path would declare the selective burden racial now; these four reject that leap because a language-acquisition policy can be neutral and the existing findings do not resolve the necessary distinction under the correct rule.

## Path A: affirm on a completed neutral-subject determination

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance on C1–C3 and the following application. The structure draws its line at the method of teaching English to children with limited proficiency, not at permission to seek protection from racial unequal treatment or to use lawful racial classifications. It applies to every family seeking that pedagogical method, regardless of ethnicity, and the waiver routes turn on language ability, age and educational needs with parental consent. The policy and amendment limits remain general within the defined instructional subject. The demographic and campaign facts identify the children most affected but do not show that their race, rather than a contest over language-instruction methods, generated the decisionmaking rule. On this tried record the supported no-discriminatory-purpose finding and the text's actual operation permit final rejection of the asserted racial mechanism.

These four do not affirm a universal proposition that a program must remedy proved racial discrimination before Hunter or Seattle can apply. They distinguish Coalition's expressly race-selected removal of classification authority from this proficiency-selected instructional rule. Nor do they hold animus essential: an overt or demonstrated proxy-based racial structure would trigger review without hostility. The strongest remand objection is selective statewide treatment of the policy particularly useful to minorities; they answer that subject-specific centralization and disparate beneficiaries cannot themselves establish a racial structure, and the present record identifies no additional supported mechanism requiring a new trial. The remedial consequence is affirmance of judgment for the State on the facial equal-protection claim, leaving the initiative effective.
