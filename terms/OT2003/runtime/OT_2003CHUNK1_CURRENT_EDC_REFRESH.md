## Lomont's material addition to EDC

Lomont's unanimous opinion by Scalia adds two operative rules. An official's concrete, traceable and redressable own-work injury does not require separate authorization to represent the State, and an adverse merits answer does not retroactively eliminate that concrete controversy. Optional applicant certification does not commandeer the officer when refusal is unconditional, without federal penalty, compulsory investigation or substitute work. Constituent pressure alone is insufficient; the actual legal consequences and operation control. The opinion expressly preserves Condon's distinct own-activity rule, Printz's rule against compelled state executive administration, which does not turn on reimbursement, and the need for actual statutory delegation.

For EDC's standing, the relevant asserted injury concerns obligations imposed on the municipal operator's own operations. Lomont supports distinguishing that concrete burden from a claim to represent the State and from the eventual merits determination. It does not itself decide municipal First Amendment personhood; the neutral packet's assumption of the asserted protection remains a distinct possible means of resolving the speech question.

For commandeering, Lomont makes the distinction between a real option and an optional label directly relevant. But its unconditional right to refuse applicant-assistance duties is not a requirement that a municipality may refuse valid direct regulation of its own discharges. EDC's alternative may impose lawful discharge controls and compliance expense. The actual inquiry is whether a legally available, operative route regulates the municipal system's own discharges without requiring the municipality to administer a third-party program. If both alternatives actually compel that third-party administration, labeling one an individual permit does not supply a genuine choice. Conversely, an alternative does not become illusory merely because valid own-discharge compliance is costly or inconvenient. Those propositions require application of Condon and New York to the actual permitting arrangement, not transposition of Lomont's different factual result.

The exact operative rule remains important. Section 122.33(b)(2)(ii) permits a different individual program under the section 122.26(d) application requirements and does not require the specified legal-authority information unless the applicant wants the permit writer to consider it. Section 122.34(a)'s minimum-control requirement expressly excepts an application under section 122.26(d). Those application provisions do not predetermine every final permit condition. The minimum measures' state-law qualifications, the distinction between commands and labeled Guidance, the construction-site waiver, and the non-stormwater qualifications remain in the script-copied official text. Neither permission to omit information nor a recommendation may be rewritten as an obligation. The separate general-permit administrative remands are not erased by the constitutional questions.

Lomont supplies no payment entitlement for municipal operations and no permission for an agency to enlarge its statutory authority merely by offering an alternative. Printz itself creates no compensation rule. The direct-regulation question therefore must not be recast as a compulsory-assistance reimbursement dispute.

## Event

Lomont v. O’Neill; judgment below reported at 285 F.3d 9 (D.C. Circuit). Merits decision, March 11, 2004, October Term 2003.

On merits submission for review of the April 2, 2002 circuit judgment recognizing the officers' own official-injury challenge and rejecting their constitutional attack on voluntary certification. Two local chief law-enforcement officers and private firearm applicants challenge the historical National Firearms Act certification arrangement under 27 C.F.R. §§179.63 and 179.85. Applicants obtain local certifications concerning identity materials and the absence of information indicating unlawful possession or purpose. Federal law leaves the officer free to refuse without penalty; the officers describe constituent pressure to participate.

The questions are whether separate state authorization is necessary for the officers' own concrete official-injury challenge and whether the optional arrangement constitutes commandeering. The challengers seek invalidation of the rules; Treasury and ATF seek affirmance.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment, recognizing the officers' ability to present their own concrete official-injury claim and rejecting the commandeering challenge to the voluntary certification arrangement.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirmed, 9–0, on the granted official-injury and Tenth Amendment questions. The officers may present their own concrete official-injury challenge without separate state authorization, but their challenge to the voluntary certification arrangement fails. The requested invalidation of that arrangement is denied.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The certification rules remain effective against this constitutional attack, and the officers retain the unconditional freedom to decline on which the judgment rests.

## Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

## Holdings

### I. A concrete burden on the plaintiff officer's own work does not require authorization to represent the State

**Controlling proposition:** A local officer challenging a concrete federal burden on the officer's own official work need not obtain separate state authorization merely to present that claim. The officer must establish injury, traceability and redressability; a concrete official controversy does not disappear merely because the Court ultimately concludes that the challenged arrangement imposes no unlawful command.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the common nine-Justice coalition in Opinion Topology. That coalition directly supports both holdings and their explanations.

**Controlling explanation:** Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States recognized the plaintiff officers' own redressable injury from work directed at their offices. Raines v. Byrd rejected an abstract claim of institutional dilution after fully effective legislative votes. Those decisions distinguish a concrete official injury from a claim to represent a State or institution without authority.

The officers here identify certification work and demands directed at their offices through the challenged federal arrangement. Relief removing that arrangement would remove the disputed official role. That is their own asserted burden, connected to the challenged rules and the requested relief. It is not an injury borrowed from the applicants' difficulty securing a favorable signature. Official title and a nonfrivolous legal label alone would not supply Article III injury; the concrete office-specific controversy supplies it here.

The contrary argument combines two different questions. Whether the officers may speak for the State does not decide whether they may contest an asserted burden on their own offices. Whether federal law actually compels the certification is then the merits question. Resolving that question against the officers does not retroactively erase their concrete stake. Because the officer plaintiffs support the same requested relief, independent private-applicant standing is unnecessary to the disposition and is not decided. Their standing supplies no authority to litigate independent obligations of absent applicants.

### II. Certification remains voluntary when refusal is unconditional

**Controlling proposition:** A federal requirement that an applicant obtain local certification does not commandeer the certifying officer when the officer may unconditionally refuse without a federal penalty, compulsory investigation or substitute work obligation. The challenger must identify an actual federal command or adverse consequence; constituent pressure alone does not convert an applicant's obligation into the officer's legal duty.

**Authority:** The same opinion and common nine-Justice coalition directly support this rule, its application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** New York v. United States distinguishes a genuine choice, despite consequences for private parties, from alternatives that both require an unwilling State to assume federal responsibilities. A federal program's reliance on local cooperation therefore requires examination of the officer's actual legal choice, rather than acceptance of an optional label alone.

The historical certification arrangement preserves that choice. Federal law imposes no duty to sign and no adverse federal consequence or mandatory substitute task when an officer declines. Applicants may press their requests, and refusal may be politically unpopular. Those pressures leave the officer free to refuse; they do not transfer the applicant's federal obligation to the local office. The officers' functional-conscription argument accordingly fails on this arrangement's actual operation.

Printz and Mack forbid commanding unwilling state executive officers to perform investigative, screening or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons. That rule preserves voluntary cooperation; an officer free to refuse is assigned nothing. The optional certification upheld here supplies no authority to compel a new investigation or broader program administration. Nor can voluntariness supply an agency delegation that is otherwise absent. Reno v. Condon's direct regulation of a State's own activity is likewise a separate ground; the applicants' regulated activity does not become the officers' activity because they are asked to certify.

Printz located the defect in compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not hours or reimbursement, and created no compensation rule. Courts apply it to properly presented compulsory programs.

**Application and remedy:** The challenged certifications under 27 C.F.R. §§179.63 and 179.85 remain voluntary because the officers retain unconditional refusal without federal sanction. The judgment rejecting the commandeering claim is affirmed, and the requested invalidation of this arrangement is denied.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Printz v. United States and Mack v. United States, June 27, 1997:** Applied to a plaintiff officer's own concrete official injury without a categorical state-authorization barrier. Its rule against commanding unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons is distinguished, because an officer free to refuse is not commanded; the rule is neither narrowed nor extended. It created no compensation rule.
- **Raines v. Byrd, June 26, 1997:** Distinguished. Its abstract institutional-dilution claim differs from a concrete burden directed at the plaintiff's office. Its Article III injury requirement and unauthorized-representation boundary retain their force.
- **New York v. United States, June 19, 1992:** Applied to distinguish genuine choice despite consequences for private participants from compulsory state responsibility. Its prohibition on two compulsory alternatives and its separate lawful-incentive holdings remain effective.
- **Reno v. Condon, January 12, 2000:** Distinguished as direct regulation of a State's own handling of information. Its separate state-activity ground is preserved; it does not convert certification for private applicants into the officer's own regulated activity.

## Law After Decision

Effective March 11, 2004, officers presenting a concrete, traceable and redressable challenge to federal burdens on their own work may litigate without separate authorization to represent the State. The official-injury inquiry remains distinct from whether the challenged measure imposes an unconstitutional command.

Federal certification arrangements of this kind remain voluntary when officers can refuse unconditionally without federal penalty, compelled investigation or substitute work. Courts examine legal consequences and actual enforcement, with the challenger required to identify an actual command or adverse federal consequence; constituent pressure alone does not supply one. This decision adds a controlling application of official-injury and genuine-choice principles. Printz's rule against compelled state executive administration, which does not turn on reimbursement, and Condon's direct state-activity rule retain their distinct scope.

# Exact current Rancho Viejo Public Projection

## Event

Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton; D.C. Circuit No. 01-5373; judgment below reported at 323 F.3d 1062. Merits decision, February 5, 2004, October Term 2003.

On merits submission for review of the D.C. Circuit's April 1, 2003 judgment, rehearing denied July 22, 2003. The question is whether the Commerce Clause authorizes application of the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition to a commercial housing development threatening a species found entirely within one State. The proposed 202-acre San Diego County development threatens the arroyo toad. The circuit sustained the application by treating the commercial development as the regulated activity. Rancho Viejo seeks reversal and relief against the application; the federal officials seek affirmance.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment sustaining the challenged application and remands for the existing-record inquiry required by National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Vacated and remanded, 7–2. The vacatur acts on the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application of the taking prohibition, not only its explanation.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Souter would affirm on the economic-application ground stated in their dissent.

The case returns through the D.C. Circuit for examination of the relevant existing legislative and adjudicative record under the two Home Builders mechanisms. A materially supported eligible mechanism with the required fit permits sustainment on that identified ground; failure to support such a connection requires relief confined to the challenged application. Rancho Viejo's request for immediate final relief against that application is not granted at this stage. The statutory prohibition remains in force; interim protection requires the ordinary equitable showing.

## Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacates and remands | All three holdings, both existing mechanisms and their limits, support and fit inquiries, and full remand |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter | Would affirm | Economic-application ground, proposed reconsideration of Home Builders's contrary class ruling at that scope, and affirmance of the challenged application |

## Holdings

### I. A commercial project does not replace the statutory taking class

**Controlling proposition:** The commercial character of a housing project does not by itself establish Congress's authority to apply the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition. The Government must identify and materially support an eligible interstate mechanism fitted to the taking class Congress enacted, including the habitat-caused injury that class reaches.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices directly join all three holdings, their explanations and the remand; Stevens and Souter dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt governs this question. Section 9 prohibits taking protected wildlife without making a project's commercial character the defining condition of the prohibition. United States v. Lopez requires a constitutionally sufficient connection for that enacted class before aggregation can measure its effects.

The D.C. Circuit relied on Rancho Viejo's commercial housing development as the regulated activity. The development is commercial, and the contrary view correctly identifies habitat conversion itself, rather than an incidental purchase, as the injury-producing conduct. But Home Builders already considered and rejected that feature as an independently sufficient connection for the enacted taking class. Describing the challenge as applied to this project does not remove the need to identify the mechanism that connects the prohibition to interstate commerce. The Court applies that existing rule.

Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon supplies the statutory treatment of habitat-caused injury. It does not supply the separate constitutional connection. Wickard v. Filburn and Perez v. United States permit class-wide assessment within properly connected economic classes; under Home Builders they do not make this project's commercial character a substitute for the required connection.

### II. The existing record must be examined under the two supported interstate mechanisms

**Controlling proposition:** A challenged intrastate-taking application may be sustained when its inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of an identified valid interstate regulatory regime, or when the regulated class generates a materially supported substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize through ordinary territorial authority and the prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. The Government must identify and support the invoked mechanism with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis; courts independently determine constitutional eligibility and reasonable fit, while Congress receives substantial latitude on supported empirical predictions, magnitude and practical regulatory design after the eligible connection is identified.

**Authority:** The common seven-Justice Court opinion directly applies both Home Builders routes and their complete governing limits. Each route remains available on remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The necessary-inclusion route requires an actual relationship between the local taking class and the operation of the identified interstate regime. United States v. Hale and United States v. Kirk supply that method. United States v. Holston applies it where local production creates the reproducible, concealable and substitutable supply of the prohibited interstate image market. That demonstrated relationship does not establish a wildlife-market relationship here. The relevant evasion or impairment must be supported for the regime actually invoked.

The externality route requires a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable effect, substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; a supported explanation why affected States cannot reasonably contain or internalize that effect through ordinary territorial authority; and reasonable fit between the federal prohibition and that spillover. An effect's occurrence in many States or the desirability of uniform national policy does not establish those conditions.

Home Builders requires material support for either route. The conservation law's comprehensiveness, biodiversity's general importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism and development pressure are insufficient by themselves. Aggregation measures magnitude only after a constitutionally eligible connection exists. Judicial review must retain an enforceable distinction between the commerce power and a general police power, without turning that inquiry into a free-form balance of federal and state interests.

The material-support obligation uses Home Builders's legislative-or-adjudicative inquiry, with its predictive latitude; it imposes no universal formal or defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, fixed numerical threshold or clear-and-convincing burden. The court must examine the actual support and fit rather than invent an absent market, spillover or causal pathway. The toad's intrastate range does not categorically defeat either supported route.

### III. Vacatur and an existing-record remand are required

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment sustaining a challenged taking rests on the insufficient commercial-project ground and has not adequately determined whether the existing legislative and adjudicative record supports either eligible mechanism, the judgment must be vacated and the case remanded for that inquiry. If the existing record materially supports a permitted mechanism and the required fit, the lower court may sustain the application on that identified ground; if it cannot, the court must grant relief confined to the unsupported application actually challenged.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion controls the disposition and both remand conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** Home Builders requires a decision under the actual class-and-mechanism rule. The commercial ground below leaves that inquiry unperformed. An alternative mechanism sufficiently established in the record could support affirmance, but the present determination supplies no such ground. Conversely, the inadequacy of the lower court's inquiry does not establish that the complete relevant record lacks every eligible connection. Rancho Viejo therefore does not obtain immediate final relief against the application merely by showing that the lower court used the wrong ground.

The lower courts must identify the mechanism asserted, examine its existing legislative or adjudicative support, determine constitutional eligibility and fit, and enter an enforceable judgment under the two conditions stated above. They may not repeat the commercial-project rationale or substitute national importance for the required connection. This is factual and adjudicative work under settled law, rather than an unresolved legal standard or permission to manufacture missing facts.

**Application and remedy:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application is vacated, and the case is remanded through that court for the specified existing-record inquiry. The statutory prohibition remains in force. Any interim protection requires the ordinary equitable showing.

## Precedent Treatment

- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998:** Reaffirmed and applied. Its enacted taking class, rejection of project commerce as independently sufficient, two supported mechanisms, Government support responsibility, independent judicial inquiry, legislative latitude and existing-record remedy govern this application.
- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied. The constitutional connection precedes magnitude, must fit the enacted class and must leave an enforceable boundary; neither project commerce nor insertion of an unexpressed commerce element supplies the required inquiry.
- **United States v. Holston, October 30, 2003:** Applied for the supported necessary-inclusion method and distinguished on its actual image-supply relationship. Its demonstrated mechanism supplies no presumed wildlife market or ecological spillover.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Preserved and applied for necessary inclusion within an identified valid interstate regime; the actual evasion or impairment relationship remains essential.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for a fitted prohibited-stock relationship without a universal individual-sale requirement. Its specific stock relationship must be supported before an analogous necessary-inclusion ground can sustain a different class.
- **Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111:** Preserved for aggregation within a properly connected economic class; it does not displace Home Builders's enacted-class rule.
- **Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146:** Preserved for class-wide assessment of connected economic conduct without a separately substantial effect from each act; it does not create a missing connection through this project's commerce.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Preserved as the separate statutory rule. Significant habitat modification must actually kill or injure protected wildlife through significant impairment of essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering, with proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability. Speculative harm and bare but-for linkage are insufficient; an omission requires an independent legal duty, and otherwise lawful development alone is no exemption. Those statutory conditions remain distinct from constitutional authority and from any section 10 permission.

## Law After Decision

Effective February 5, 2004, the Home Builders class-and-mechanism rule continues to govern equivalent intrastate-taking applications involving commercial projects. Courts must identify and materially evaluate either supported necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime or a substantial interstate externality satisfying the cross-border, territorial-control and fit requirements. The Government's support responsibility, independent judicial inquiry and bounded legislative latitude remain unchanged.

The commercial-project rationale cannot alone sustain this application. The lower courts must resolve its constitutional validity through the existing-record inquiry and enter relief according to the supported mechanism or its absence. This decision applies the existing doctrine without creating a new Commerce category; the unresolved work concerns the record's support and the resulting application-specific judgment.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter.** They would affirm because the activity directly producing the alleged habitat injury is commercial housing construction and land conversion. Sweet Home identifies that physical habitat alteration as the conduct reached by the taking prohibition. The dissent therefore looks to the actual economic application, rather than to the developer's collateral equipment purchases or a generalized claim that species preservation benefits the national economy.

Wickard and Perez permit class-wide regulation of connected economic activity, and Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association permits regulation of economic land use for environmental ends. On that reasoning, the actual construction supplies a sufficient economic connection without a market in the toad or a newly found ecological spillover. The dissent accepts the continuing statutory requirements of actual habitat-caused injury and ordinary causation; its proposed affirmance concerns the Commerce Clause challenge.

Home Builders's contrary enacted-class holding directly obstructs this ground. Stevens and Souter would reconsider it to the extent it excludes reliance on the actual injury-producing economic activity in this application. They regard treating the prohibition's broader coverage as a reason to disregard this commercial application as a mistaken class inquiry. They neither claim that their earlier dissent already controls nor distinguish Home Builders through a new factual premise. Their ground would leave this judgment effective and make the mechanism remand unnecessary; it would not make every noneconomic taking an economic activity.
