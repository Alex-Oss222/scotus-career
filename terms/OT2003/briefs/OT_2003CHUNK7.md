## 88. Williams v. Morton

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Williams v. Morton
* **Citation or Docket:** 343 F.3d 212 (3d Cir. 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Supreme Court merits review, designated MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-06-02
* **Question Presented:** Whether a prison violates Free Exercise or Equal Protection by providing kosher meat meals to Jewish prisoners while offering Muslim prisoners only vegetarian meals despite their asserted religious need for halal meat. The question’s kosher-meat premise is contested and, on the verified appellate record, unsupported.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit, affirming summary judgment for prison officials on the federal constitutional diet claims.
* **Relief Requested:** The prisoners seek inclusion of halal meat in at least some meals and damages; officials seek judgment sustaining the policy.
* **Material Record:** The prison supplied religious vegetarian meals to about 225 prisoners and kosher meals to four. A witness corrected his earlier statement that kosher meals included meat; corroborated testimony described them as meatless. Plaintiffs disputed the administrative and security necessity of refusing halal meat, relying on food-service testimony about outside vendors. Added cost was acknowledged. Their sincere asserted religious need was not rejected. Record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires genuine factual disputes to be assessed for materiality under the governing test. Contamination claims were voluntarily dismissed to expedite appeal and are not presented. No preserved RLUIPA claim is established.

### Issue 1: Religious diet and the Turner inquiry

* **Controlling Law:** Overton v. Bazzetta recognizes compatible family association in prison and applies Turner's four factors, with the prisoner retaining the ultimate burden to show unreasonableness. O'Lone applies the governing prison standard to religious exercise; Lukumi forbids religious targeting. Neither deference nor sincerity permits a court to invent operational facts.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess a rational connection to legitimate penological objectives, alternative exercise, consequences of accommodation and ready alternatives. Reasonable deference does not authorize resolving a genuinely material evidentiary conflict against the nonmovant.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; 42 U.S.C. §1983; Rule 56.
* **Material Other Authority:** Turner does not require a least-restrictive-means showing. The asserted religious obligation need not be accepted as universal doctrine to be sincere.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The constitutional framework is established; the disagreement concerns its application to cost, supply and security evidence.
* **Open Point:** Whether the evidence permits judgment as a matter of law or requires findings about the proposed accommodation.

### Issue 2: The alleged meat-based religious comparator

* **Controlling Law:** Equal protection requires an actual differential treatment attributable to government. The religious-neutrality rules do not permit a court to invent that predicate from a mistaken factual premise.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify similarly situated persons and the challenged difference before testing its justification. Different aggregate costs are not themselves proof of denominational hostility.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause; 42 U.S.C. §1983.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Rule 56 record must be assessed as corrected and corroborated, without treating a superseded assertion as an established benefit.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An actual religious preference can be challenged; the specific alleged meat preference must first exist.
* **Open Point:** No new equal-protection test is necessary for the unsupported meat comparator.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Third Circuit found the diet reasonably connected to cost, simplified service and security under Turner and rejected the meat-based equal-protection claim because the kosher meals were also meatless.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The prisoners contend that conflicting operational evidence prevents summary judgment and that religious accommodation should be evenhanded.
* **Respondent or appellee:** Officials invoke the much larger scale of the proposed program, additional screening and expense, alternative religious opportunities and the corrected comparator evidence.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** The separate evidentiary ruling allowing a late-disclosed witness followed additional deposition, briefing and expense reimbursement; it is not independently included in the question.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** None separately verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Affirmance may rest on Turner’s reasonableness and the absent meat comparator. A partial vacatur can require factual adjudication of operational fit while affirming rejection of the unsupported comparative claim. Neither route permits declaring all Muslims must receive meat on this record or denying sincerity by theological judgment.

* **Relevant Dates:** Accrual and retirement preceded the 1998 amendment. Only benefits already accrued are the presented cutback issue.

### Issue 1: New conditions on accrued benefits

* **Controlling Law:** Lockheed Corp. v. Spink treats plan design as a sponsor function but retains statutory limits. Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson protects defined accrued entitlements without giving participants ownership of every plan surplus.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the accrued entitlement and its existing conditions, then ask whether the amendment reduces it. Sponsor authority to amend does not override the anti-cutback command.
* **Other Governing Law:** ERISA §204(g), 29 U.S.C. §1054(g), including early-retirement benefits; §203(a)(3)(B), 29 U.S.C. §1053(a)(3)(B); corresponding Internal Revenue Code provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Applicable Treasury regulations support protection of accrued early-retirement benefits. Informal agency guidance cannot amend the statute.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Accrued benefits are protected; the open application concerns an added suspension condition rather than a lower nominal dollar amount.
* **Open Point:** Whether the plan may reduce access to accrued benefits while claiming their nominal amount is unchanged.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning below:** The Seventh Circuit treated new employment restrictions as an impermissible reduction of already-accrued pension rights.
* **Petitioner or appellant:** The plan argues that suspension is not reduction and that ERISA expressly permits suspension during specified employment.
* **Respondent or appellee:** The retirees argue that the new condition removes a promised right to payment and that permissible original terms cannot be added retroactively.
* **Alternative or unreached grounds:** Prospective terms for future accruals may be altered within statutory limits; neither side’s position requires freezing every future plan term.
* **Concessions and limiting principles:** No unverified oral-argument concession is used.
* **Solicitor General or material amicus position:** The United States supports enforcement of the anti-cutback rule; tax qualification and any authorized administrative reliance relief are distinct from the participants’ payment rights.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible holding scope and consequences:** Applying the anti-cutback rule affirms and requires restoration of benefits withheld solely under the new condition, subject to calculation below. Excluding suspension conditions would permit the challenged withholding. Neither approach decides plan-wide tax qualification or authorizes recovery beyond the statutory benefit claim.
