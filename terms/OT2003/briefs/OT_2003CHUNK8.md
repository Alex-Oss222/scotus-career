* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established extraordinary-writ requirements and extend the concrete-accommodation principle to this official advisory setting. Courts must examine an actual advisory body and the participant's functions rather than converting every outside adviser into a committee member.

It does not establish every mandamus element automatically. The Court of Appeals must assess the actual alternatives, clear entitlement and appropriateness of intervention, including whether narrower discovery and District Court supervision adequately protect the asserted interest. The response must address the intrusion rather than extinguish a potentially valid statutory action by categorical immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Presidential advisory bodies alleged to include private functional members and civil discovery directed to the officials and records needed to decide that issue. The holding neither exposes ordinary consultation to automatic FACA coverage nor immunizes integrated private membership through nominal titles. It distinguishes membership and operational facts from the content of presidential deliberations, while preserving particular privileges and the limits of the asserted judicial enforcement route.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the same functional inquiry whether the private participant is an industry representative, outside expert, advocate or informal adviser: sustained participation in the body's collective work, rather than a favored or disfavored viewpoint, controls. A federal-only body receiving outside submissions remains within the statutory exclusion absent actual nongovernmental membership. A private participant performing continuing internal member functions is not excluded merely for lacking a formal vote. Until coverage and particular privilege disputes are resolved, courts apply the staged inquiry and the governing qualified-privilege rules. For final enforcement under §1361, courts require a legally enforceable, sufficiently clear nondiscretionary duty and the other prerequisites; any APA theory requires an eligible agency defendant and its separate statutory conditions. Appellate §1651 review does not supply those missing predicates.
* **Transition:** Apply the stated discovery sequence and functional-membership standard to this live action on remand. No completed judgment is reopened and no later decision supplies an earlier governing rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government has a substantial constitutional interest in protecting candid presidential advice. That interest does not permit executive officials to settle the legal reach of FACA by calling integrated participants consultants. Conversely, private influence is a routine feature of policy advice; treating influence itself as membership would make ordinary consultation the basis for extensive civil discovery. Functional integration and staged inquiry preserve an adjudicable statutory question without exposing deliberations unnecessarily.

  Clinton v. Jones rejects a temporary civil exemption for unofficial pre-presidential conduct and requires attention to concrete interference with official responsibilities. Its accommodation principle supports the method here, while its different factual setting does not itself decide official advisory discovery. United States v. Nixon rejects absolute insulation of presidential communications but involved a demonstrated need for identified criminal evidence; it does not authorize sweeping civil demands into policy formation. Nixon v. Fitzgerald protects the President from personal civil damages for official conduct, a different question from focused judicial scrutiny of statutory compliance.

  Public Citizen v. Department of Justice supports a construction of FACA attentive to ordinary executive consultation and serious separation-of-powers concerns. Association of American Physicians & Surgeons v. Clinton examines actual, regular participation functionally indistinguishable from committee membership and distinguishes episodic advice. That functional approach supports the express rejection of a mandatory voting-or-veto requirement while leaving mere influence outside the rule.

  Akins v. FEC recognizes concrete injury from continuing denial of particular information allegedly protected by statute; it does not establish standing from a generalized wish for compliance or entitle plaintiffs to every requested item before coverage is decided. Chamber of Commerce v. Reich permits qualified prospective review of responsible implementing officers acting outside statutory authority; it does not itself create a private action for every FACA error, settle presidential APA status or remove statutory remedial limits. Kerr v. United States District Court reinforces mandamus's exceptional character and the relevance of particular objections and adequate protective alternatives.

  Here the allegations concern private participation in the National Energy Policy Development Group, but actual membership and coverage remain disputed. The broad discovery reaches beyond the immediate membership question and cannot be justified simply by postponing protection until all requested documents have been identified for a privilege contest. Initial records and declarations concerning who attended, what responsibilities were assigned and how collective work occurred may resolve or define the dispute. Any necessary further inquiry must follow the stated safeguards.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Clinton v. Jones's concrete-accommodation principle without altering its limited unofficial-conduct holding or Fitzgerald's official-act damages immunity. Preserve Nixon's qualified protection and distinguish its criminal-evidence setting. Apply Public Citizen's statutory caution and adopt the functional reasoning of Association of American Physicians & Surgeons with the stated no-voting-or-veto prerequisite. Apply Kerr's extraordinary-writ requirements and preserve Akins and Reich at their informational-injury and authorized-review scopes. No cited precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual private membership, ultimate FACA coverage, properly asserted privileges concerning particular evidence, the sufficiency of each enforcement route and final disclosure or other relief require factual development and claim-specific adjudication. The constitutionality of a particular final disclosure demand cannot be decided without that record.

The amendment is not futile: it removes the designation-timing immunity obstacle and permits the court to consider the remaining legal questions. Congress could have addressed the Accords directly or through operative language necessarily inconsistent with their bar. It did not do so in these provisions. Treating explanatory language as a binding disposition would both enlarge the enactment and displace the court's responsibility to decide the Government's independent argument.

  Dames & Moore sustains the pertinent claims-settlement authority at its actual scope; it does not prevent Congress from legislating. Trans World Airlines, Cook and Weinberger require a genuine legislative basis for displacing an international commitment rather than reliance on legislative history alone. Landgraf reinforces the need to identify the legal change and its temporal operation separately from constitutional permission. None turns removal of immunity into an award of damages. The Ministry's status as Iran itself supplies no route around the same claim bar, and the payment statute presupposes a legally supportable judgment rather than creating one.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply the agreement-displacement principles and preserve the separate immunity, cause-of-action and relief inquiries. Do not resolve the broad Flatow cause-of-action question merely because the Government offers it as an additional ground. No constitutional authority to override a completed judgment is necessary to this statutory disposition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The scope of a cause of action that could survive an enacted removal of the Accords' bar is unnecessary to this judgment and is not decided. No new compensation claim against the United States is presented. The constitutional validity of a different statute actually compelling a particular adjudicative result would require its enacted terms and proper presentation.

### Issue 2: Independent adjudication and Plaut's line between pending and completed cases

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Explain the changed-law and independent-adjudication principles relevant to the asserted legislative commands. Apply Plaut's finality rule and its qualifications to the question actually presented: this action remained pending, damages were unresolved and no legislative command to reopen a completed final judgment determines the outcome.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether legislative statements favoring the plaintiffs' judgment may substitute for enacted law and judicial determination, and how Plaut's finality rule and its pending-case and legislative-adjudication qualifications apply to case-specific legislation enacted while this action remained pending.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Preserve Congress's authority to change governing law for pending proceedings and the courts' responsibility to apply it. Apply Plaut's finality rule at its actual scope: Article III protects a private judgment that has become final through the ordinary judicial process against a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed, while Congress remains free to change the law governing a pending case and remains barred from dictating findings or imposing predetermined liability. This action was pending when Congress acted and no judgment finally disposing of it is reopened, so the amendments fall on the pending-case side of Plaut's line. The enacted provisions change immunity law without dictating findings or liability; the unenacted explanatory statements cannot supply a command to preserve the default, and the validity of a statute enacting such a command is not decided.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad in protecting the judicial function, confined to the actual posture in its judgment. A rule treating case-specific legislation as automatically invalid would exceed what independent adjudication requires. Extending Plaut's finality rule to a pending action and a nonfinal liability default would exceed what that holding protects, and reading its pending-case qualification as approval of every targeted statute would disregard its bar on dictated findings and liability.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may enact a new legal rule for a pending controversy, including an identifiable controversy, but may not turn unenacted legislative explanations into binding judicial commands or substitute a legislative determination for a court's independent application of law and fact. The immunity provisions are applied for the legal change their text makes; they do not command preservation of the default or defeat the independent Accords bar. Their reference to this case does not itself establish an unconstitutional adjudication, and affirmance does not require deciding every possible constitutional objection to targeted legislation.

  **Plaut's finality rule.** Plaut holds that finality is itself an Article III protection: once a private judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process, Congress may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable, require a court to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation, and later independent adjudication of eligibility and the merits does not restore the completed disposition. The rule protects completed adjudication; it leaves Congress free to change the law while a case remains pending. Public Law 107-77 and Public Law 107-117 were enacted while this action was pending: the August 2001 default addressed liability only, damages remained unresolved and no judgment finally disposed of the action. Plaut therefore does not bar the amendments' application here. Nor does it protect the default, which was not a judgment final through the ordinary judicial process and was vacated through judicial reconsideration on the Government's motion rather than by a legislative command.

  **The governing distinctions.** A pending proceeding remains subject to valid enacted changes with the temporal reach the law gives them. A private judgment final through completion or expiration of direct review is protected by Plaut against a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed. Continuing prospective relief remains subject to the law governing future conduct, as Plaut's Wheeling Bridge qualification and Miller v. French recognize within their own limits. Ordinary judicial reconsideration under an authorized rule is a judicial act requiring that rule's predicates, not a congressional appellate decree. These distinctions do not permit legislative fact-finding or dictated liability under the label of procedure.

  **Effect here.** Because the action was pending when Congress acted, the amendments fall within the authority Plaut preserves to change the law governing pending litigation, and courts apply that change independently. The enacted provisions remove a designated immunity obstacle and correct the case designation without prescribing findings, liability or damages, so what Congress enacted does not transgress Plaut's legislative-adjudication limit. The broader command attributed to the explanatory statements was not enacted. The court can reject that asserted command, apply the surviving claim bar and affirm without reopening a completed judgment or deciding the validity of a statute dictating a particular result.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Legislative effect on this unresolved controversy and the courts' independent responsibility to determine the text's consequences. Plaut's finality rule is confined to completed private judgments; this holding does not extend it to nonfinal orders, treat its pending-case qualification as validating every case-specific statute or decide the questions Plaut left open.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts distinguish genuine law changes from adjudicative commands and apply valid new rules to pending proceedings within their lawful temporal reach. Where a private judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process, Plaut bars a legislative command to vacate it and reinstate the same litigation solely because the applicable law has changed, while judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures remains available; Morgan Stanley, affirmed by an equally divided Court, supplies no rule. Prospective-relief disputes follow Miller v. French's changed-law and meaningful-adjudication requirements, and judicial reconsideration requires its independent procedural basis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The present judgment reopens no completed case and alters no judgment in the §27A(b) litigation; the amendments apply to this pending action according to their terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Robertson permits Congress to substitute operative legal standards while leaving application to courts. Klein protects the adjudicative responsibility that a legislature cannot assume merely by expressing the result it wants. The distinction turns on what the statute does, not simply on whether its beneficiaries or lawsuit can be identified. Here the enacted immunity change has legal work to do, while the broader command attributed to explanatory statements was not enacted.

  Plaut answered for completed private judgments the final-judgment question Robertson left open; Morgan Stanley's equal division supplied no precedent on it. Plaut's rule turns on the completed judicial disposition itself, which later independent adjudication cannot restore, so it reaches judgments that have become final through the ordinary judicial process, not pending proceedings. The same opinion preserved Congress's power to change the law governing pending cases and applied Klein and McCullough to bar legislatively prescribed findings or liability. This case lies on the pending side of Plaut's line, and the enacted provisions prescribe no finding or result; that leaves the actual disposition resting on the surviving claim bar.

  Miller v. French concerns future enforcement of prospective relief and preserves a meaningful judicial opportunity to decide statutory and constitutional predicates. It does not cancel accrued damages or supply a legislative appellate power over completed private judgments. Landgraf separately addresses temporal operation; express retrospectivity does not itself establish constitutional validity. The present default and unresolved damages remain distinct from the completed dismissals in Morgan Stanley and Plaut.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Robertson's genuine-change-of-law and independent-application distinction and Miller v. French's bounded prospective-relief rule. Apply Plaut's finality rule, together with its preservation of Congress's power to change the law governing pending cases and its bar on legislatively dictated findings or liability. Apply Klein's protection of independent adjudication without claiming its full reach is resolved. Morgan Stanley, affirmed by an equally divided Court, establishes no precedent. No precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The questions Plaut left undecided, including §27A(a) and the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, and any legislative command to vacate a completed judgment lie outside this pending-action record. Other forms of finality, criminal or innocence remedies, executive claims-settlement measures outside the governing authorities and a statute actually dictating disputed facts or liability are not adjudicated here.
