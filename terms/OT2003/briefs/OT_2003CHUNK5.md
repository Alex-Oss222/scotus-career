## 56. Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara
* **Citation or Docket:** 335 F.3d 357 (Fifth Circuit, June 18, 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-09
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal court enforcing a Swiss arbitral award under the New York Convention may enjoin the losing party from pursuing Indonesian proceedings to annul the award and restrain enforcement.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit vacated the preliminary foreign anti-suit injunction and the surviving civil-contempt consequence. The district court’s separate judgment recognizing the award was the subject of a different appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** The award creditor seeks restoration of the injunction; Pertamina seeks preservation of vacatur and freedom to pursue the foreign proceedings.
* **Material Record:** Contracts concerning an Indonesian geothermal project required Swiss arbitration. After a substantial award and a United States enforcement judgment, Pertamina sought annulment and anti-enforcement relief in Indonesia. The district court restrained substantive participation and imposed civil contempt for violation of its temporary order.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged relief is an in-personam foreign anti-suit injunction, not an appeal deciding recognition of the award itself. The continuing civil-contempt issue follows the injunction’s validity; criminal contempt and enforcement merits cannot be assumed included.
* **Relevant Dates:** Arbitral award December 2000; United States recognition judgment December 2001; foreign restraint proceedings 2002; circuit judgment June 18, 2003; assigned simulation date March 9, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1) supplies review of an injunction. Equitable discretion must operate within the treaty and statutory framework, respect foreign proceedings, and identify necessity rather than protect a litigant from ordinary parallel costs.

### Issue 1: Foreign proceedings during Convention enforcement

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted decision establishes a controlling New York Convention anti-suit test for this setting. The Fifth Circuit's competing approach is subject to review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate adjudicative authority to recognize the award, the effect of a foreign annulment or restraint, and the necessity of an injunction against a litigant. Relief must address an established threat within the court’s authority and remain proportionate.
* **Other Governing Law:** New York Convention Articles III, V and VI; Federal Arbitration Act Chapter 2. The Convention allocates recognition and annulment questions among courts and supplies grounds for withholding or postponing enforcement.
* **Material Other Authority:** The district and appellate reasoning identify competing anti-suit approaches. Their tests are arguments within this review, not independently adopted rules. The treaty text controls over a free-floating preference for either enforcement or international noninterference.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Convention permits proceedings in more than one national court and gives the enforcing court its own recognition task. A foreign proceeding’s existence does not itself remove that authority.
* **Open Point:** Whether the shown Indonesian proceedings threaten the United States court’s ability to decide or effectuate its own lawful judgment sufficiently to justify this injunction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The creditor argues that the Indonesian action attacks a final recognition judgment and frustrates the Convention’s enforcement policy. Pertamina argues that the Convention anticipates multinational proceedings and that the United States court can decide the effect of foreign orders without suppressing their pursuit. Indonesia’s amicus participation makes the sovereign interests concrete. The district court relied on protecting its judgment; the circuit found that justification insufficient in this treaty structure. Neither characterization settles the separate recognition appeal.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm vacatur if the domestic court can preserve its independent jurisdiction and judgment without this restraint. Reverse if a demonstrated foreign interference makes a tailored injunction necessary and the equities justify it. A narrower remand would require identification of a specific, otherwise unremediable threat. The separate enforcement judgment remains outside the judgment under review.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule can reject ordinary parallel proceedings as sufficient necessity without denying every possible foreign anti-suit power. It need not decide conclusively which country has primary annulment jurisdiction or whether a later annulment defeats recognition.

## 57. Batzel v. Smith

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Batzel v. Smith
* **Citation or Docket:** 333 F.3d 1018 (Ninth Circuit, June 24, 2003).
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-18
* **Question Presented:** Whether denial of a California anti-SLAPP motion is immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine; if review is authorized, whether §230 protection for republication of a third-party email depends on the sender’s actual intent or the moderator’s reasonable perception of intended publication.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit accepted interlocutory jurisdiction, construed §230 to require a reasonable perception that the email was supplied for Internet publication, and remanded the immunity issue. A separate appeal addressed a sponsor’s final summary judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** The moderator seeks protection from the defamation suit under §230 and California procedure; Batzel disputes immediate jurisdiction and the claimed immunity.
* **Material Record:** A handyman emailed allegations connecting Batzel’s artwork to Nazi theft to a museum-security website operator, who circulated the message. Batzel disputes the allegations. The sender later said he did not intend publication. Whether the recipient reasonably perceived permission to publish is disputed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal from denial of the special motion was interlocutory. A state statute’s permission to appeal does not itself establish federal appellate jurisdiction. The separate final judgment concerning Mosler cannot bootstrap independent claims into review. Falsity, intent, and reasonable belief must not be treated as found merely because the case describes the allegations.
* **Relevant Dates:** Email and publication in 1999; circuit judgment June 24, 2003; assigned event March 18, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion is consolidated here. Cited authorities receive no separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1291 and the narrow collateral-order doctrine govern this federal appeal; California Code of Civil Procedure §425.16 supplies the special motion. Section 1292(b) certification is a distinct route, not presumed here.

### Issue 1: Immediate appellate jurisdiction

* **Controlling Law:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. applies cumulative conclusiveness, separateness, importance, and effective-unreviewability requirements; litigation expense or a claimed right to avoid trial is not enough. Swint v. Chambers County Commission rejects immediate review of an ordinary liability defense and independent pendent review merely for convenience.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess the category of order under federal finality law. Every collateral-order condition must be met; the existence of a substantive defense and state appeal permission are separate from federal jurisdiction.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1291 and 1292; the California special motion and its probability-of-success inquiry.
* **Material Other Authority:** Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. supplies the narrow exception. Recognized immunity appeals do not automatically extend to every anti-litigation protection.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law disallows appeals justified only by irretrievable trial burdens. It has not categorically adjudicated this particular state special motion.
* **Open Point:** Whether this anti-SLAPP denial satisfies every federal collateral-order requirement.

### Issue 2: Material supplied by another information-content provider

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted holding conclusively resolves the meaning of information provided by another under §230 for an email not actually intended for publication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** If jurisdiction exists, separate service-provider or user status, another person’s creation or development of the challenged content, treatment as publisher or speaker, and whether that content was provided within the statute’s meaning.
* **Other Governing Law:** 47 U.S.C. §230(c)(1), (e) and (f), as in force at the publication; applicable defamation law remains subject to the statute’s actual reach.
* **Material Other Authority:** The circuit’s competing constructions are persuasive arguments within the matter, not adopted governing law. Editorial selection and independent creation of defamatory content are materially different activities.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The enacted protection does not make every Internet speaker immune from the speaker’s own content. The intent or reasonable-perception question remains unresolved in the adopted law.
* **Open Point:** Whether actual intended publication, reasonable perceived publication, or a broader supplied-content construction governs this email, if the Court may reach it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The moderator invokes the anti-SLAPP protection from litigation and argues that a reasonable belief in intended publication suffices under §230. Batzel argues that the appeal is premature and that an unintended private communication was not provided for public dissemination. The circuit treated the special motion as an immediately reviewable immunity and adopted the reasonable-perception construction. A broader reading would eliminate any publication-intent condition; an actual-intent reading would allocate the sender’s misunderstanding differently. Those merits alternatives cannot supply missing appellate jurisdiction.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacate the interlocutory merits disposition and require dismissal if federal finality is absent. If jurisdiction exists, affirm or revise the §230 construction and remand for any disputed predicate facts; none of those paths permits fact-finding about the alleged theft in this Court. Preserve any genuinely independent final sponsor judgment outside the granted issues.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The jurisdiction rule can govern this category of anti-SLAPP denial without resolving all specialized appeal statutes. Any merits rule must distinguish ordinary editing from creating content and state who bears the actual protection predicates.

## 58. South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe of Indians

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** South Florida Water Management District v. Miccosukee Tribe of Indians
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-626; 541 U.S. 95.
* **October Term:** 2003
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-03-23
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Clean Water Act requires a discharge permit for a pump conveying pollutant-containing water when the pollutants did not originate in the pump, including the material distinction between transfer within one water body and addition to another.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment requiring a permit for the S-9 pumping station, treating the C-11 canal and WCA-3 impoundment as distinct waters.
* **Relief Requested:** The water district seeks reversal of the permit requirement; the Tribe and environmental plaintiff seek its preservation.
* **Material Record:** S-9 pumps canal water containing phosphorus into the Everglades conservation area. The parties dispute whether the canal and conservation area are meaningfully separate despite hydrological connections. The pump conveys, rather than generates, the pollutants.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant centers on whether pollutants must originate in the point source. The hydrological premise was litigated below, but the permissible scope of further review is disputed. The Government’s broader all-waters-as-one theory was not developed below in its present form and must not be adopted as an uncontested agency position.
* **Relevant Dates:** The district and circuit permit rulings precede the March 23, 2004 event. Later water-transfer rules or cases are not governing law here.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Deaton addresses the geographic reach of covered waters; Environmental Defense Center, Inc. v. United States Environmental Protection Agency addresses municipal stormwater permit conditions. This case concerns an addition through a pump and whether its source and receiving waters are distinct.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** no earlier Deaton holding is relied on. Any effective jurisdictional holding must be checked if provided; its existence is not inferred from the earlier calendar position.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment requires absence of a genuine material factual dispute. A remand must identify that dispute rather than assume facts to avoid deciding the statutory point-source issue.

### Issue 1: Conveyance, addition and distinct waters

* **Controlling Law:** Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers holds that equipment actively conveying and depositing pollutants can make a statutory discharge even if the material was not imported from offsite; mere movement or hydrological harm is insufficient. Arkansas v. Oklahoma recognizes interconnected water-quality obligations. Neither determines whether this pump transfers pollutants between distinct receiving and source waters.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a pollutant, an addition to covered waters, and a point-source conveyance. The components are distinct; proving a conveyance does not automatically prove an addition between distinct receiving and source waters.
* **Other Governing Law:** 33 U.S.C. §§1311(a), 1342, and 1362(6), (7), (12), (14); relevant state water-allocation preservation in §1251(g), and the statutory distinction between point and nonpoint controls.
* **Material Other Authority:** The definition includes pipes, ditches and conduits, supporting transport as a point-source function. Existing intake-credit rules and waterbody-specific standards bear on the Government’s unitary construction but do not substitute for its proper presentation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory definitions govern the source question. Whether two hydrologically connected areas are meaningfully distinct requires facts; the Act does not make map labels decisive.
* **Open Point:** Whether conveyance alone can be the point-source function, whether the undisputed record establishes distinct waters, and whether the broader unitary theory is properly ripe for decision.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district argues that a point source must add pollutants of its own and that the water areas are one connected system. The plaintiffs argue that pumping introduces canal pollution into a distinct receiving area. The United States proposes that all navigable waters be treated as one for the addition inquiry, a broader construction that could eliminate the permit requirement even for transfers between otherwise distinct waters. It also supports the district’s factual connection argument. The circuit’s judgment rests on a contrary distinct-water premise.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the origin requirement and affirm if distinct waters and addition are conclusively established within the proper review scope. Reject that requirement but vacate summary judgment if a material hydrological dispute remains. Accepting the Government’s broader theory could dispose of the permit claim, but would require resolving its preservation, textual and regulatory difficulties. A source-only disposition would leave other issues to ordinary proceedings without deciding them.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A conveyance rule reaches pumps moving existing pollutants without extending permitting to movement entirely within the same water body. A distinct-waters remand need not decide whether all transfers between covered waters are exempt as a matter of law.

## A. STATUS

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject the claim that the pump must originate pollutants, vacate the premature permit summary judgment, and remand the genuinely disputed distinct-water issue.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require application of the conveyance rule and factual determination of whether the pump adds pollutants to a meaningfully distinct water body. Permit the properly presented broader statutory theory to be addressed on remand without adopting it here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Conveying pollution can constitute the point-source act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The pump’s operation presents the granted source question; the litigated relationship between source and receiving areas determines whether summary judgment can stand. Resolving that factual uncertainty does not require accepting the newly elaborated unitary theory.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether existing pollutants conveyed by a pump are excluded solely because the pump did not generate them, and whether this record conclusively establishes the necessary addition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted definitions and remand the material factual premise; propose no general transfer exemption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: settle the recurring conveyance issue and preserve the distinct addition element. A broader all-transfer rule would decide a materially underdeveloped theory and erase the factual distinction. A narrower source-only affirmance would sustain an operative permit judgment despite its disputed necessary premise.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A point source may discharge pollutants by conveying them from elsewhere; the Clean Water Act does not require the source to generate the pollutants. A claimant must still establish an addition to covered receiving waters, so movement within a single water body does not become an addition merely because a pump is used. The party seeking summary judgment must show the absence of a genuine material dispute about those predicates, which this hydrological record does not establish.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Conveyances such as pumps, pipes, and ditches that transport pollutants; this holding retains the separate covered-water and addition requirements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish point-source conveyance from proof of an addition. Hydrological connection is relevant evidence, not an automatic exemption or automatic showing of one water body.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory list describes transport mechanisms, and treating each as a pollutant generator would exclude the ordinary function Congress named. Arkansas v. Oklahoma confirms that permit consequences turn on actual statutory water-quality relationships, not an invented universal rule. The canal and impoundment evidence nevertheless requires assessment of their actual connection and distinction. Neither separate names nor some shared groundwater conclusively answers that question. Borden Ranch confirms that a point-source instrument need not manufacture the material it conveys, while requiring an actual addition rather than environmental harm alone. Its §404 soil-deposit and farming-recapture holding does not settle this §402 water-transfer application. The distinction reinforces the need to resolve the disputed relationship between the canal and receiving conservation area.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Arkansas v. Oklahoma preserved at its permit and supported-record scope; no adopted water-transfer precedent is displaced.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Government’s broader unitary-waters construction requires proper presentation and development; the factual distinction requires findings. Until this Court decides that broader theory, courts apply the enacted discharge elements and the conveyance rule stated here, considering preserved arguments without treating all covered waters as one by fiat.

## 59. United States v. Galletti

* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal and consideration of its claims against the partners; the debtors seek their disallowance as untimely.
* **Material Record:** A partnership failed to pay employment taxes for periods from 1992 to 1995. The IRS timely assessed the partnership. General partners later entered Chapter 13, and the IRS asserted the unpaid partnership debt in their proceedings based on California partnership liability.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The assessment of the tax, the existence of state-law partner liability, and the lawful collection mechanism are different questions. This appeal does not establish that every partner owes every partnership obligation or that every administrative collection prerequisite is satisfied.
* **Relevant Dates:** The underlying employment-tax periods and assessment dates control limitations; the scheduled event is March 23, 2004. The later bankruptcy filing does not require a second assessment merely by changing the enforcement forum.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Government’s proofs of claim must satisfy applicable bankruptcy procedure and substantive liability requirements. The granted limitations issue is a legal question, distinct from unresolved claim defenses.

### Issue 1: Assessment of a tax and collection of the same debt

* **Controlling Law:** No adopted holding resolves this partnership-assessment issue. The enacted assessment provisions control.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the taxpayer whose obligation was assessed, the amount and timeliness of the assessment, and the independent law making the collection target liable for that debt. Do not confuse a new tax liability with another person’s responsibility for an existing debt.
* **Other Governing Law:** 26 U.S.C. §§6201, 6501(a) and 6502(a); applicable California general-partnership liability; Bankruptcy Code claim procedures. Section 6502 supplies the collection period for a timely assessed tax.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Updike illustrates the distinction between the assessed obligation and persons answerable for it, subject to the actual statutory provisions. Separate assessment provisions for different liabilities cannot be generalized beyond their terms.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The Code distinguishes assessment from collection. State law may establish derivative responsibility without creating a separate federal tax for every liable person.
* **Open Point:** Whether the assessment-period language nevertheless requires a second partner-specific assessment before collecting the same timely assessed partnership tax.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Justiciability and personal partisan injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve justiciability and the governing constitutional standard on review of the dismissal. Personal standing, the sufficiency of amended allegations and ultimate liability remain distinct inquiries. No missing finding of deprivation, purpose or causation is assumed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether intentional political opportunity deprivation presents a justiciable claim under the First and Fourteenth Amendments; what personal political injury, governmental purpose and causal connection establish that claim; and whether partisan predominance or a projected delegation-wide seat majority without a statewide vote majority suffices by itself.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Recognize a constitutional claim when electoral boundaries materially deprive identifiable voters of an existing or demonstrably attainable opportunity for effective political organization, participation, nomination or election, substantially because officials seek to suppress political opposition and preserve governing power against electoral challenge. Apply four elements and a State-proved same-decision defense. This is an affirmative extension of independent-injury and political-association principles; neither Bandemer nor Cano already establishes this complete partisan-entrenchment rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad as to the constitutional rule, limited as to the present judgment. The rule protects effective political opportunity without requiring prior victory, inability to elect in every destination district, a prescribed historical range or a separate durability threshold. It does not constitutionalize ordinary partisan disappointment, district shape, every departure from traditional districting principles or projected seat distributions. Political affiliation, past electoral performance, incumbency, political balance and other legitimate districting objectives remain permissible considerations.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The First Amendment protects political expression and association; the Fourteenth Amendment protects equal participation in the political community. A State may consider politics in drawing districts, but may not deliberately use electoral boundaries to disable identifiable political opposition's effective opportunity and insulate governing officials from ordinary electoral accountability. The constitutional wrong is intentional material deprivation of an effective political opportunity through a concrete electoral operation. Partisan design alone is insufficient, and material deprivation does not require complete destruction of the opportunity. A plaintiff must establish four elements:

  **1. Identifiable political opportunity.** Identify an existing or demonstrably attainable opportunity for effective political organization, participation, nomination or election. An opportunity is a practical capacity to undertake the identified political activity, supported by evidence of the voters, association or coalition and the activity at issue. Prior electoral victory is not indispensable; an emerging opportunity may qualify. A party label, desire for additional seats, expected support for an incumbent or preferred statewide partisan distribution does not by itself identify the protected opportunity.

  **2. Material deprivation.** Show that the challenged boundaries materially impair that opportunity through a demonstrated electoral operation. Packing concentrates voters in a district in a way that impairs their effective opportunity elsewhere; cracking divides them among districts in a way that impairs their effective opportunity to act together. Displacement, division of an effective coalition or another proved districting mechanism may also cause the deprivation. These descriptions do not establish liability without proof of the resulting impairment. The impairment must be a substantial practical obstruction to the identified activity, rather than ordinary disappointment, inconvenience or a less favorable electoral prospect alone. District shape, partisan composition, predicted seat totals and departures from traditional criteria are evidence; none alone constitutes the injury. The plaintiff need not show complete destruction of the identified opportunity, that every possible political activity has been eliminated or that the affected voters could never elect their preferred candidate.

  **3. Prohibited entrenchment purpose.** Establish that the deprivation was substantially motivated by an objective to suppress identifiable political opposition's effective opportunity because of the affected voters' political association or participation, and thereby preserve governing power against electoral challenge. The objective must actually contribute to the decision, but need not be exclusive or predominant. Ordinary pursuit of partisan advantage is not by itself the prohibited purpose, and knowledge of partisan effects alone does not establish an objective to impose the protected opportunity's deprivation. Purpose is evaluated from the decision's context and evidence of what the officials sought to accomplish.

  **4. Causation.** Establish that the challenged boundaries materially caused the deprivation, rather than merely reflecting voter geography, ordinary electoral competition or independently lawful districting requirements. Identify the assignments or electoral operation responsible for the impairment. The boundaries need not be the only contributing cause, but a correlation between party preference and an unfavorable electoral result is insufficient.

  **Proof and same-decision defense.** Plaintiffs must prove each element by a preponderance of the evidence, meaning that it is more likely true than not. Once they establish the protected opportunity's deprivation, prohibited purpose and causal connection, the State bears the burden of proving by a preponderance that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited entrenchment objective. A genuine policy of partisan fairness or compliance with legitimate districting criteria may establish that defense if it independently accounts for the challenged result. An independently established and consistently applied fairness policy, or genuine population, geographic or statutory requirements, must actually account for materially the same lawful decision. Merely naming an additional political objective, offering a conceivable justification or stating that politics always enters districting does not defeat the claim. Once the four elements are proved and that defense fails, no additional balancing of partisan advantages excuses the violation.

  **When injury occurs.** The injury is complete when the plan imposes the proved deprivation. A plaintiff need not wait for a preferred candidate to lose a later election. Organization, participation and nomination are protected opportunities in their own right; a claim concerning one of those activities does not require proof of a lost general election.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The stated rule governs personal claims of intentional political opportunity deprivation in electoral districting. Subordination of neutral criteria to political aims, unusual boundaries and a projected seat supermajority without a statewide vote majority do not alone establish a violation of the First Amendment, the Fourteenth Amendment or Article I. There is no entitlement to proportional party representation, competitive elections in every district, a guaranteed nomination or electoral success. Independent constitutional racial-opportunity claims retain their own elements and defenses.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Standing requires personal exposure to the challenged electoral operation, a causal connection to the claimant's injury and relief capable of redressing it. Statewide vote-seat disparities alone establish neither personal injury nor a violation. Under the ordinary notice-pleading rules governing this case, the complaint must identify the protected opportunity and the districting operation alleged to impair it, with allegations supporting the claim's purpose and causation requirements; merits proof is not required at the pleading stage. The amendment ordered here allows plaintiffs to present their claims under the announced standard.

  At the merits stage, comparative maps, voting patterns, organizational activity, district boundaries, legislative evidence and electoral history may illuminate opportunity, impairment, purpose and causation. No single kind of evidence is mandatory or dispositive. A comparison must illuminate a real political opportunity and the challenged operation; a map producing the plaintiff's preferred partisan outcome does not establish injury simply because it can be drawn. Historical success may support an opportunity, but is not a prerequisite. No alternative map or prescribed number of comparison maps is mandatory. Examine the operative congressional plan and any legally permissible comparison without treating a prior malapportioned plan as a presumptively lawful baseline; Act 1's use under a stay does not cure its adjudicated population defect.

  If a violation is proved, identify the deprivation and the boundaries materially responsible for it. Give the State the first opportunity to adopt a lawful correction under a workable election timetable. Judicial relief, if required, must remove the established deprivation and extend no further than necessary, while respecting other governing districting requirements. It may not impose proportional partisan representation or guarantee a candidate, party or coalition a seat. A separate racial-opportunity claim must be assessed independently even when the same boundaries also support a partisan claim.
* **Transition:** Apply the announced rule to this live dispute and other open cases in which the claim is properly presented. Permit amendment here under the remand mandate, while retaining ordinary jurisdictional and procedural requirements. Announcing the rule does not reopen final judgments or invalidate completed elections.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lawful consideration of political interests does not confer an unrestricted power to disable political opposition. Equal participation and freedom of political association require a distinction between an unfavorable political arrangement and government-imposed deprivation of an identifiable effective opportunity. Requiring opportunity, material impairment, prohibited purpose and material causation supplies that distinction. The same-decision defense protects independently lawful choices without allowing an additional political explanation to conceal an established constitutional injury.

  Pennsylvania's asserted pursuit of Republican electoral advantage and the alleged statewide disparity between votes and seats do not, without more, invalidate its congressional plan. Susan Furey alleges that the redistricting reassigned her residence from former Congressional District 13 to reconfigured District 6, affecting her political association and representation. That allegation identifies a concrete setting in which the existence of an effective opportunity, its impairment, the decisionmakers' purpose and map causation can be examined. It is not itself proof of any element or a finding that the assignment is unconstitutional. The District Court dismissed the partisan claim without applying this standard; vacatur and permission to amend allow the required inquiry without declaring an unproved deprivation or ordering a replacement map.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Baker v. Carr establishes that constitutional districting claims are not categorically barred by the political-question doctrine. Davis v. Bandemer's justiciability holding is retained; this decision supplies the more precise substantive standard. Gaffney v. Cummings preserves room for legitimate political accommodation. Reynolds v. Sims supports meaningful, equal representation without guaranteeing proportional party seats, and White v. Regester directs attention to genuine access to political processes beyond formal eligibility.

  Page v. Bartels requires an actual protected political interest and a governmental operation materially impairing it; design and political disappointment are insufficient. Robertson v. Bartels prevents expected incumbent support or electoral preferences from becoming automatic constitutional injuries. Montiel v. Davis supplies the substantial-motivating-purpose principle and confirms that a partisan explanation does not automatically excuse an independent racial deprivation. Cano v. Davis distinguishes independent injury from design and recognizes that interference with organization, nomination, candidacy or participation need not await electoral defeat. Cano expressly left a standalone First Amendment claim outside its appeal. The present partisan-entrenchment rule extends those principles; it is not a holding already compelled by Cano.

  Brooks v. Barnes illustrates why nomination opportunity matters independently of ultimate general-election victory. Its Section 2 holding supplies an analogy, not a partisan-entrenchment test or an automatic incorporation of statutory racial-dilution elements. O'Hare Truck Service v. City of Northlake and Board of County Commissioners v. Umbehr show that a governmental penalty on political association can violate the First Amendment even without an entitlement to the governmental benefit at issue. That principle supports the present extension without making their distinct factual settings or doctrinal requirements identical to districting.

  Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp. supports evaluating governmental purpose through contextual evidence. Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle supplies the motivating-factor and State-proved same-decision framework. These authorities support the announced elements and burdens without turning every political consideration into a constitutional violation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The present record does not establish liability or the boundaries needed to cure a violation. Those questions remain for proceedings on remand. Preserve Cano's separate intentional racial-opportunity claim, with its own deprivation, purpose, causation and same-decision requirements: a State cannot defeat that claim merely by relabeling its racial objective as partisan strategy. The partisan rule neither displaces that independent protection nor makes racial and partisan claims interchangeable. Sections 2 and 5 of the Voting Rights Act retain their own statutory elements and remedies. Structural Entrenchment remains a separate proposed claim concerning control of an elected governing body despite an opposing voting majority, without a prohibited-purpose requirement. This opinion does not adopt that claim, and Pennsylvania's congressional delegation is not such a governing body. The entrenchment purpose within the four-element claim does not dispense with any element or decide that separate structural question.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Unless a five-Justice holding in an effective Vieth judgment adopts a different partisan standard or forecloses partisan claims, a claimant must prove four elements by a preponderance: (1) an identifiable existing or demonstrably attainable opportunity for effective political organization, participation, nomination or election; (2) material deprivation of that opportunity through a demonstrated districting operation; (3) a substantial motivating objective to suppress identifiable political opposition's effective opportunity because of the affected voters' political association or participation and thereby preserve governing power against electoral challenge; and (4) a material causal contribution from the challenged boundaries rather than voter geography, ordinary competition or independently lawful requirements. Ordinary partisan advantage alone does not establish the prohibited purpose. An emerging opportunity may qualify on concrete evidence without a prior victory.

A genuine, independently established and consistently applied fairness policy or lawful population, geographic or statutory requirement must actually account for that decision; a conceivable alternative justification is insufficient. If the defense fails, no additional balancing of partisan advantages excuses the violation. Personal standing requires the claimant's own injury, traceability and redressability and remains separate from proof of the merits. Map-caused injury is complete when the proved deprivation occurs; no completed-election waiting rule applies.

  **Governing control.** For a preserved legislative claim, propose an additional rule: district assignments may not insulate an existing governing party or coalition from a reliably demonstrated opposition vote majority across the recorded conditions through a material, avoidable effect of those assignments. This rule applies separately to an elected legislative chamber or other district-elected governing body, never to a State's congressional delegation. It requires no discriminatory-purpose showing.

  The claimant must prove by a preponderance: (1) an independently existing governing party or coalition; (2) recorded conditions in which a coherent opposition receives more than half the reliably measured votes for the whole body; (3) retention of a seat majority by the governing coalition in those conditions; (4) retention across the recorded opposition-majority conditions rather than at one isolated point; and (5) a material causal contribution from the challenged assignments beyond geographic concentration, turnout, candidates and lawful constraints. No fixed number of elections or cycles controls.

  **Measurement and comparison.** An absolute majority means more than half, not a plurality. A staggered body requires evidence encompassing its whole membership. Use votes actually cast, accounting for uncontested races through disclosed, tested evidence rather than assumed votes. The lawful comparison must be workable, satisfy governing legal requirements and rest on independent legal, institutional or practical considerations rather than a desired partisan result. Applied to the same recorded votes, it must support the inference that the challenged placements cause retained control; the court must state the inference's limits. A completed election under the challenged map is unnecessary if reliable evidence establishes the elements.

  **Lawful-constraints defense.** The State may prove by a preponderance that consistently applied lawful constraints made the retained-control result unavoidable. Ordinary partisan advantage, voter approval or commission authorship is insufficient. This defense concerns unavoidable lawful constraints, not the purpose-based same-decision defense governing the unified political-opportunity claim.

  **Standing and application.** The claimant's own assignment must contribute materially to the body-wide injury, with relief likely to redress it. Congressional claims cannot invoke this route. No partisan liability is found here. Preserved legislative claims proceed under the proposed governing-control rule only if the Court adopts it; the independent racial and §2 dispositions remain governed by their separate law and findings.
