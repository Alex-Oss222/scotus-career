## 8. Yarborough v. Gentry

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-10-20

* **Question Presented:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied the Sixth Amendment standard by rejecting an ineffective-assistance challenge to defense counsel's closing argument.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the federal district court's denial of habeas relief after California courts rejected the assistance claim.

* **Relief Requested:** The warden seeks reversal of the federal habeas judgment; Gentry seeks a new trial through a conditional writ.

* **Material Record:** Gentry admitted stabbing his girlfriend but claimed accident. Counsel challenged an eyewitness's inconsistent description, opposed reliance on Gentry's criminal history, and identified intent as the dispute. Counsel also used disparaging descriptions of his client and omitted some potentially favorable points. The claim concerns the actual summation, not an established absence of investigation.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The state merits rejection triggers §2254(d)(1). The constitutional rule must have been clearly established at the state adjudication, not supplied by a later case. An inadequate argument is not automatically complete abandonment.

### Issue 1: Closing argument and federal habeas limits

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor and Bell v. Cone require objective unreasonableness at the statutory gateway, with ordinary Strickland performance and prejudice remaining distinct. Bell v. Cone rejects presumed prejudice where substantive advocacy occurred. Lockyer v. Andrade confirms that §2254(d)(1) requires a contrary governing rule or an objectively unreasonable application, rather than independent error alone; clearly established substantive law is measured at the state merits adjudication without demanding a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record rather than isolated phrases or a correct formula alone.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Evaluate the supported defense as a whole, including informed tactical choices; a strategy label is not dispositive. Petitioner must establish deficient performance and reasonable-probability prejudice unless an actual Cronic category applies.

* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1).

* **Material Other Authority:** Strickland v. Washington and Herring v. New York supply the underlying performance and closing-argument principles.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Closing is protected advocacy. Neither perfect presentation nor every arguably useful point is required.

* **Open Point:** Whether this particular summation falls outside the range of reasonable representation and whether the state rejection exceeded the lawful range.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit treated the omitted arguments, derogatory language and counsel's treatment of credibility as deficient advocacy. The warden argues that counsel concentrated on intent and reasonable doubt, confronted damaging evidence, and made choices a reasonable advocate could make. Gentry argues that counsel abandoned affirmative reasons to acquit and improperly invited conviction if the jury disbelieved him. The complete argument and jury instructions matter; no particular phrase is conclusive. No independently different Solicitor General rule is verified.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A reversal can establish only that the state court permissibly rejected this supported tactical-performance claim, leaving genuinely distinct claims untouched. Affirmance would require a supported statutory gateway and both assistance elements, followed by a conditional new-trial writ. A remand may be necessary if the appellate court applied ordinary disagreement instead of §2254(d), but it cannot invent favorable strategy or prejudice facts.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Soft money and party connections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** How far Congress may regulate party and officeholder fundraising to protect valid federal contribution rules.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the preventive contribution and circumvention principles of Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb, Colorado II and Mariani to the demonstrated national-party system, preserving government-borne close fit and meaningful political association. Neither equalization of influence nor ordinary access alone supplies the required justification.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad across the integrated national-party channel and the federal-election activities of state and local parties because the record demonstrates how candidate-linked financing moves between them. A broader rule governing genuinely independent state political activity would lose the necessary connection. A receipt-by-receipt exemption for funds nominally designated nonfederal would leave the demonstrated national-party channel intact.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may dismantle a demonstrated system for circumventing lawful candidate-contribution restrictions through party fundraising when the government establishes an objectively grounded important corruption or circumvention interest and a close fit preserving meaningful association. The government bears that justification burden on the supported record; no completed bribe, completed local scandal, mandatory new local study or proof of legislators' subjective reliance on particular evidence is required. Relevant experience outside the jurisdiction may establish a preventive justification. Uphold §323(a)'s restrictions on national parties receiving and spending soft money, including receipts nominally designated for state or local elections, because the proved fundraising system is structurally integrated with federal candidate financing. Uphold §323(b)'s federal-election-activity limits, including appropriately allocated registration and turnout activity, with the statutory Levin-fund accommodation material to fit. Sustain §323(d)'s restrictions on party fundraising used to channel prohibited money through §501(c) and §527 organizations while preserving lawful hard-money donations and genuinely independent organizational activity. Sustain §§323(e) and (f) within their candidate, officeholder and federal-election connections. Preserve concrete challenges demonstrating disabling associational burdens. Party affiliation, ordinary meetings or access, lobbying, policy agreement, donor prominence and unequal influence alone establish neither corruption nor a general power to restrict political resources.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The complete national-party soft-money receipt and spending channel, including nominally nonfederal receipts; state and local party federal-election activities within §323(b); the nonprofit and political-organization channels covered by §323(d); and the candidate-linked solicitation and financing covered by §§323(e) and (f). Genuinely independent organizational activity and state politics outside those statutory connections receive no additional restriction from this holding.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Relevant outside experience may support preventive regulation when it bears on the identified mechanism; a local scandal or fresh local study is not invariably necessary. National-party designation of a receipt as nonfederal does not defeat the integrated-channel holding. A materially different application must still satisfy the government's objective-justification and close-fit burden and preserve meaningful association. Voluntary capped responsive public grants may be lawful under Daggett, but that permission supplies neither an entitlement to equal campaign resources nor authority to suppress competing private speech.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb supplies the central preventive principle: relevant experience can establish an objective justification without a completed local scandal, a mandatory new local study or proof of legislative subjective reliance, but the actual restriction must remain closely fitted and preserve meaningful association. Its recognition of tacit donor-party-candidate understandings and fundraising credits is directly relevant. The record here shows national-party arrangements through which large donors obtain candidate-connected benefits and furnish support unavailable as direct candidate contributions. Colorado II recognizes donor understandings, fundraising credits and allocation arrangements as evidence of circumvention through genuinely coordinated party financing; Mariani supports enforcement of actual source and channel limits. Those mechanisms justify controlling the national-party channel as a whole, including nominally nonfederal receipts, and preventing migration to covered state-party and outside-organizational channels. Levin funds and lawful hard-money transfers preserve activity while closing the demonstrated substitutes. Buckley and Shrink Missouri require actual justification and fit; political responsiveness, ordinary access and unequal influence cannot replace them. Daggett's approval of voluntary capped responsive grants identifies a permissible financing alternative, not a command that Congress establish one or a basis for suppressing private competitors.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb's objective preventive justification and meaningful-association limits as the central rule; apply Buckley, Shrink Missouri, Mariani and Colorado II to the proved fundraising and circumvention mechanisms. Preserve Colorado I's genuinely independent spending and Day's voluntary-association protection. Preserve Daggett's voluntary public-financing permission without turning it into an equalization power or funding mandate.

### Issue 2: Treasury funding of electioneering communications

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The presented facial challenges permit adjudication of the electoral-advocacy core and the governing independent-speech boundary. No particular future issue advertisement or nonprofit financing record is conclusively adjudicated. Reporting coverage, treasury-financing restrictions and constitutional exemptions remain separate questions.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §§203 and 204 may restrict corporate and union treasury financing of actual electoral advocacy while preserving genuine independent issue discussion and the functional protections of qualifying ideological nonprofit associations.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Sustain the valid electoral-advocacy core under surviving Austin and MCFL principles while applying Bartlett's genuine-independent-issue-advocacy boundary and Day's functional nonprofit protection. Do not convert Beaumont's direct-contribution holding into a rule equating independent expenditures with contributions.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad enough to sustain the presented facial operation and state a usable independent-speech boundary. A broader prohibition on every communication naming a candidate in the statutory window would disregard Bartlett and Day. A narrower rigid list of electoral words would fail to reach actual electoral campaigning that uses other language.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may require genuine electoral advocacy by covered corporations and unions to be financed through lawful segregated funds supported voluntarily rather than their treasuries, consistently with surviving precedent, but may not suppress genuine independent issue advocacy merely because a communication falls within the statutory electioneering definition. Sustain §§203 and 204 against the presented facial challenges to their valid electoral-advocacy core without declaring every covered application constitutional. The definition's candidate identification, broadcast, cable or satellite medium, 30-day primary or 60-day general-election period, and applicable electorate-targeting requirements provide objective statutory coverage; they do not conclusively establish constitutionally regulable electoral advocacy. Under Bartlett, examine the communication as a whole, including what it actually asks the audience to do; a reasonable nonelectoral interpretation remains constitutionally material. An approaching election, candidate identification, potential influence on voters or opposition to the candidate's legislative position cannot alone defeat a genuine-issue-advocacy claim. No rigid 'vote for' or 'vote against' vocabulary is required for actual electoral advocacy. The government must justify the electoral and financing connection. Under MCFL and Day, assess a nonprofit's actual organization, resources and financing: incidental commercial activity or imperfect formal compliance does not alone remove independent-expression protection, while material corporate treasury financing or use as a commercial conduit may change the result. The entity claiming the functional nonprofit protection must establish its actual qualifying characteristics. Preserve genuine press activity, individual independent expenditures and effective as-applied review.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Actual electoral advocacy financed through the covered treasury channels within the statutory time, medium and electorate boundaries. The holding preserves genuine independent issue discussion, functionally qualifying ideological nonprofits, genuine press activity and natural persons' independent political expenditures. Disclosure duties require their separate justification and do not establish power to prohibit the underlying message.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** A communication asking voters to replace a senator in November presents electoral advocacy; a communication asking the audience to contact that senator about an impending Medicare vote presents a materially different legislative appeal. Courts must consider the actual message, context, audience request and financing rather than decide either application from candidate naming or timing alone. A reasonable nonelectoral interpretation must receive constitutional consideration without automatically resolving every future dispute. Apply Day functionally: incidental commercial activity and formal defects are not conclusive, but actual material treasury financing and conduit use matter. Until a particular application is adjudicated, courts apply Bartlett's issue-advocacy boundary, MCFL and Day's functional association protections, and the government's required justification for the restriction.

* **Transition:** Apply these boundaries in the pending actions and prospective enforcement. The facial disposition neither reopens final judgments nor creates retrospective penalties through a newly enlarged construction; it does not extinguish a properly supported as-applied claim.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Austin remains effective for the valid treasury-financed electoral-advocacy core, but that permission does not eliminate the independent protections recognized in Bartlett, MCFL and Day. Bartlett rejects prohibition of genuine independent issue advocacy under an indefinite electoral-influence rationale and requires a sufficiently definite distinction between issue discussion and electoral campaigning without a mandatory word list. Objective dates and candidate references identify statutory coverage but can also describe a real legislative controversy, so expected electoral effects cannot replace examination of what the communication asks its audience to do. Day protected an ideological nonprofit's independent expenditure despite incidental commercial activity and imperfect formal compliance, making actual structure and financing decisive rather than corporate status alone. Material business financing or conduit use remains relevant; incorporation creates neither automatic prohibition nor universal exemption. Beaumont concerned direct candidate contributions, where candidate control and the actual source-and-voluntary-channel record justified the restriction. It supplies no general equation of independent expenditures with contributions. The valid electoral core and a meaningful genuine-issue-advocacy challenge can therefore coexist without deciding unpresented advertisements or funding arrangements.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply surviving Austin at the treasury-financed electoral-advocacy core; reaffirm North Carolina Right to Life v. Bartlett's genuine-issue-advocacy boundary and the functional association protection of MCFL and Day v. Holahan. Preserve Buckley and Colorado I on independent expenditures. Distinguish Beaumont and Stengel's direct-contribution holdings from a general restriction on independent expression.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of particular future issue advertisements and materially different nonprofit financing arrangements requires a concrete communication and record. The facial disposition grants no blanket advance exemption and forecloses no properly supported challenge under the stated boundaries.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the challenged wetlands and their discharge connection fall within the Act and federal commerce power.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established channels rule to the proved tributary connection. Preserve the distinct Home Builders externality requirements rather than infer their satisfaction from drainage alone.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The connection-based class needs a practical rule. A rule covering every local wet place lacks support; requiring navigability at the pollutant's point of origin would disregard demonstrated downstream effects.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Clean Water Act reaches these wetlands along the demonstrated tributary route into navigable waters, and Congress may prevent upstream discharges threatening those waters as protection of interstate channels. The government bears the ordinary civil burdens on statutory jurisdiction and violation and must support the interstate mechanism. A separate Home Builders externality ground additionally requires a substantial cross-border spillover that individual States cannot reasonably confine; physical drainage alone does not establish every part of that showing. The established tributary connection sustains this judgment under the channels ground without making the additional externality findings unnecessary by assumption.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The demonstrated connected system and adjacent wetlands; neither bird-use-only isolated-water jurisdiction nor a universal geographic buffer.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until a materially different water connection is decided, courts require proof of the actual statutory connection and apply the existing isolated-water limitation; they may not presume jurisdiction from environmental importance.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** SWANCC rejects the isolated-pond bird-use criterion while preserving Riverside Bayview's adjacent-wetland authority. The proved tributary chain into the navigable Wicomico supplies the concrete channels connection here. Home Builders separately permits regulation of a materially demonstrated substantial interstate spillover that individual States cannot reasonably contain. The watershed connection explains a possible transmission mechanism but does not alone establish the magnitude and uncontainability required for that additional ground. The channels ground is sufficient on the established record.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers is distinguished; United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes, United States v. Lopez and National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt are applied.

## 22. Maryland v. Pringle

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Maryland v. Pringle

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-809; 540 U.S. 366

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-12-15

* **Question Presented:** Whether discovery of packaged cocaine accessible within a small automobile, together with the other circumstances known to police, supplied probable cause to arrest the front-seat passenger when none of the three occupants acknowledged ownership.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Maryland’s Court of Appeals reversed the conviction, concluding that police lacked probable cause particularized to Pringle; the intermediate appellate court had affirmed.

* **Material Record:** Police stopped a car for speeding at about 3:16 a.m. The driver consented to a search. An officer found $763 in rolled cash in the glove compartment and five cocaine baggies behind the rear-seat armrest. Pringle occupied the front passenger seat. The three occupants denied knowledge or ownership; police arrested them. Pringle later waived Miranda rights and confessed that the cocaine was his. The later confession cannot supply the antecedent arrest’s justification.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The vehicle search’s validity is not the presented issue. The dispute concerns probable cause at arrest and the derivative suppression consequence, not whether guilt could be proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Distinguish collective physical access from evidence that singles out someone else.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Banks concerns warrant execution at a home, not antecedent probable cause to arrest a particular passenger.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Individualized probable cause in a shared vehicle

* **Controlling Law:** Ornelas v. United States requires independent review of probable cause with deference to supported historical facts and appropriate weight to grounded local inferences. Illinois v. Wardlow and United States v. Valentine require a supported connection to wrongdoing, not a shortcut based on location, association or innocent conduct alone.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Consider the total circumstances known when the seizure occurs. Probable cause is a practical fair probability of the particular person’s participation; it is not trial proof or a categorical inference against everyone present.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments; Maryland possession law supplies the conduct under investigation.

* **Material Other Authority:** Ybarra v. Illinois distinguishes mere presence among strangers in a public establishment; United States v. Di Re addresses circumstances pointing specifically to another participant.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The combined circumstances may support shared possession even when they do not identify exclusive ownership.

* **Open Point:** Whether this combination reasonably supports a common possession enterprise including Pringle.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The state high court regarded the rear-seat drugs and location of the cash as insufficiently connected to Pringle. Maryland, supported by the United States, argues that the private automobile, accessibility, packaging and shared circumstances permit an inference of common enterprise. Pringle argues that this would authorize arrest of innocent passengers whenever police find hidden contraband. Neither argument justifies treating silence itself as guilt; the relevant inquiry is the information police possessed about the drugs and the occupants together.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Reversal could recognize probable cause on this combined private-vehicle record while preserving the rule against guilt by association. Affirmance could require a stronger individualized link because the drugs were concealed away from Pringle; its reach would depend on whether common possession is a supported inference. A categorical all-passengers rule would exceed the record. If probable cause existed, this arrest supplies no ground for suppressing the confession, but independently preserved admissibility issues remain separate.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The shared-private-vehicle inference needs a defined basis. A rule for every passenger or all nearby people would abandon particularity. A requirement of exclusive ownership would confuse probable cause with trial proof and defeat supported joint-possession inferences.

The accessible packaged cocaine, the small private vehicle and the accompanying cash support that inference for Pringle without his later confession.

## 23. Castro v. United States

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Question Presented:** Whether an unwarned judicial recharacterization of a pro se Rule 33 motion as a first §2255 motion may make a later expressly filed §2255 motion second or successive; and whether the authorization-review bar prevents review of that classification.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed dismissal of Castro’s 1997 motion as an unauthorized second or successive §2255 motion.

* **Relief Requested:** Castro seeks first-motion treatment of the 1997 filing and adjudication under the ordinary applicable rules; the United States seeks affirmance and raises a jurisdictional objection.

* **Material Record:** Castro filed a pro se Rule 33 motion in 1994. The district court denied it, referring to both Rule 33 and §2255, without notifying him of recharacterization, warning of successive-filing consequences, or offering withdrawal or amendment. His pro se appeal did not contest that label. In 1997 he expressly invoked §2255 and raised additional ineffective-assistance claims; the later dismissal relied on the earlier conversion.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

### Issue 1: Review channel

* **Controlling Law:** Felker v. Turpin bars ordinary certiorari review of an actual circuit authorization grant or denial. Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal permits review of the distinct determination whether authorization is required; a court’s label is not dispositive.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the action reviewed and the precise statutory restriction before reaching the merits.

### Issue 2: Recharacterization and meaningful first review

* **Controlling Law:** Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal and Lonchar v. Thomas require the stated predicates for successive treatment and lawful dismissal; they do not create an equitable power to disregard enacted gates.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A procedural label with permanent consequences must be distinguished from a litigant’s informed election to invoke the remedy. No existing holding requires these specific warnings.

* **Other Governing Law:** Section 2255’s restrictions on second or successive motions; Rule 33’s distinct new-trial procedure; ordinary federal authority to construe pro se filings.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Courts may read a pro se submission for the relief it seeks, but existing law does not resolve when involuntary conversion consumes the first §2255 opportunity.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The lower court treated the 1994 merits ruling and unchallenged recharacterization as sufficient. Castro argues that conversion cannot silently forfeit his opportunity to present all collateral claims. The United States argues that the prior characterization is law of the case and that the circuit’s observation about successive requirements implicates the review bar. A warning must permit an informed decision both about amendment and about challenging the conversion; absence of an appeal does not by itself demonstrate that understanding. Competing positions.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may distinguish classification from authorization and require warnings before a converted motion counts for future successive restrictions. That would vacate the dismissal without deciding entitlement to collateral relief. Alternatively, treating any prior substantive §2255 adjudication as sufficient would preserve the dismissal but require an explanation of why the litigant’s chosen procedural vehicle is immaterial. A prohibition on all beneficial recharacterization is broader than necessary to resolve the adverse successive-filing consequence.

### Issue 1: Fair notice before consuming the first motion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the 1994 conversion can trigger the later successive restriction.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering channel distinction and propose a procedural protection for involuntary first-motion conversion.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring loss of a statutory opportunity warrants a uniform rule. Barring all recharacterization would prevent useful construction of pro se pleadings. A discretionary warning would leave the very forfeiture at issue uncontrolled.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court may not count a recharacterized pro se filing as the litigant’s first §2255 motion for later second-or-successive restrictions unless it first gives notice of the proposed conversion, warns of those restrictions, and provides a meaningful opportunity to withdraw or amend the filing. An unwarned conversion does not acquire that consequence merely because the litigant failed to appeal its label. The record establishes no qualifying warning here, so the 1997 motion is not successive by reason of the 1994 ruling.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Pro se filings converted into a first §2255 motion with adverse future consequences; not an expressly and knowingly filed first motion or a general exemption from statutory limitations.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those claims are adjudicated, courts apply ordinary first-motion §2255 requirements; the warning rule neither establishes ineffective assistance nor extends an otherwise applicable filing period.

* **Transition:** Apply the warning condition when deciding the present successive classification, including its reliance on the earlier converted filing. The 1994 merits disposition is not reopened; only its claimed preclusive classification consequence is removed.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Felker v. Turpin applies to actual authorization decisions; Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal confirms that classification can be reviewed without undoing an authorization judgment. Construing pleadings to provide relief cannot fairly extinguish a separate opportunity without the information necessary to choose. Law of the case is a rule of adjudicative practice, not a source of the missing informed election.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Felker v. Turpin and Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal; announce the warning rule as a new procedural holding rather than attribute it to either case.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying collateral claims and any independent timeliness or default defense require their own adjudication.
