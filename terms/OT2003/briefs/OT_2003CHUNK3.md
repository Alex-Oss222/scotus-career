## 24. In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** In re Trans World Airlines, Inc.

* **Citation or Docket:** 322 F.3d 283

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-12-15

* **Question Presented:** Whether §363(f) authorizes an asset sale free of successor liability for the identified employment-discrimination claims and travel-voucher settlement obligations, although those claims are not liens or other strictly in rem interests.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit affirmed the district court’s affirmance of the bankruptcy sale order barring the presented successor claims against American Airlines.

* **Relief Requested:** The EEOC and the voucher class seek preservation of successor liability; the airlines seek enforcement of the free-and-clear sale order.

* **Material Record:** American agreed to buy substantially all TWA assets for $742 million in a bankruptcy sale; no other qualifying bid emerged. The EEOC had pending discrimination charges, and a settled maternity-discrimination class held travel vouchers. Both objected and participated in the sale hearing. The sale order extinguished specified successor liability and enjoined its enforcement against American. The claims’ connection to the transferred enterprise and capacity for monetary satisfaction are disputed legal predicates. Record and order.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review assumes, without deciding, otherwise viable nonbankruptcy successor claims. The actual litigants had an opportunity to object; notice to unidentified future claimants is not established. The sale order’s broad language cannot be treated as an adjudication of every unknown claim or the purchaser’s own later misconduct. The limited-stay request is ancillary to the merits.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Property interests and monetary satisfaction

* **Controlling Law:** In re Dow Corning Corp. holds that §§105(a) and 1123(b)(6) do not authorize forced release of independent claims against nondebtors; equitable usefulness cannot replace a specific statutory source. Section 363(f)'s distinct authority over interests connected to transferred property remains to be construed here. Patterson v. Shumate requires application of the Code's operative text.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The proponent of free-and-clear treatment must establish an interest in the transferred property and at least one enacted §363(f) condition; economic desirability alone is insufficient.

* **Other Governing Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§101(5), 363(f), especially (f)(5), and 363(e); applicable employment statutes and the settlement establish the underlying obligations.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A lien is one statutory type of interest, not necessarily the only one; a general right against the debtor does not automatically become an interest in every asset.

* **Open Point:** Whether successor claims arising from the transferred operating enterprise and reducible to money fit the statute, and what remains outside the order.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit treated the claims as interests connected to the assets and capable of monetary satisfaction. The EEOC, representing the United States, and the class argue that employment rights are not interests in property and that extinguishing successor remedies sacrifices civil-rights enforcement; the class also stresses the noncash voucher promise. The airlines invoke the transfer connection, §363(f)(5), the priority system and the value preserved by a viable sale. The alternative contention that nonbankruptcy successor liability never existed was not necessary below and cannot be silently converted into a finding.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance may encompass the identified transferable-enterprise liabilities without extinguishing all unsecured claims or all future regulatory duties. A strict in-rem construction would preserve the successor claims and require a remedy consistent with the consummated transaction and applicable sale-finality law. A predicate-specific remand is possible if monetary satisfaction or the property connection is unproved; sale value cannot replace those statutory elements. No path authorizes a bankruptcy court to license future discrimination.

* **Record and Legal Uncertainty:** The broader notice record must be established before the sale order can bind persons whose notice is disputed.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment as to the identified EEOC and voucher-class successor claims.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the free-and-clear transfer and the injunction against these successor claims in place; preserve claims against the TWA estate and adjudication under the statutory distribution process. Dispose of the ancillary stay appeal consistently with the merits.

* **Remedial Reach:** The litigating EEOC claimants and the represented voucher class as bound by lawful process; this holding does not enlarge the order against unknown or unnotified persons.

* **Practical Relief:** American need not honor the specified obligations as successor solely because of this asset purchase. The bankruptcy court must continue claims administration and distribution under the Code; it may enforce the sale order only within the statutory and notice boundaries, without inventing a guaranteed recovery.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Defined successor claims in a free-and-clear sale

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the presented claims satisfy the Code’s property and monetary-satisfaction predicates.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose a text-based construction of an unresolved application, preserving separate statutory safeguards.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must explain these enterprise-connected claims rather than rest on airline rescue. A broader all-liabilities discharge would erase the property limitation and notice protections. A liens-only rule would read the statute’s separate treatment of liens as exhaustive without textual support.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 363(f) permits sale free of a successor liability that arises from the debtor’s use of the transferred operating assets and can be compelled into monetary satisfaction under applicable legal process, when the affected claimant receives the process required by law. The sale proponent bears the ordinary civil burden of establishing those statutory predicates. The presented discrimination claims and monetizable voucher obligations meet them; their estate claims remain governed by bankruptcy priority and allowance rules.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The identified pre-sale successor claims against American, not the purchaser’s independent post-sale violations, unrelated claims without a property connection, or unnotified persons whose rights were not lawfully adjudicated.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those distinct claims are decided, courts enforce ordinary notice, statutory sale predicates and independent liability rules; this order cannot be cited as a blanket immunity for the purchaser.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** In re Dow Corning Corp. rejects general equitable authority to extinguish independent nondebtor claims. This sale therefore requires §363(f)'s own predicates, not simply reorganization necessity. Section 363(f)(3) treats liens as a subset of interests, while subsection (5) supplies the monetary-satisfaction condition applied to these property-connected successor obligations. Preserving estate claims and priority supports the statutory design but does not itself authorize extinguishment. Adequate notice and any properly invoked §363(e) protection remain necessary; American's independent misconduct after the sale is outside the sale's release.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish In re Dow Corning Corp.'s independent nondebtor releases from the identified §363(f) property-connected successor claims; apply Patterson v. Shumate's textual method and preserve notice, priority and adequate-protection requirements.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Claims of persons without adequate notice, future injuries, and independent purchaser wrongdoing require facts or claims not before the Court.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Evidence and remedial classification

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors v. Peña establishes the common substantial-justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications. An important lawful objective and an evidence-supported substantial relation, including burdens, coverage and concrete comparably effective less racially disadvantageous alternatives, are required. It displaces the historical categorical strict-scrutiny formulation. City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters preserves the distinct sex-classification inquiry. Grutter v. Bollinger confirms the government's burden to justify an important lawful objective, an evidence-supported substantial relationship, actual competitive burdens and concrete practicable alternatives comparably effective with materially less racial disadvantage. Gratz rejects a corrective defense lacking that connection, without imposing prior adjudication or individual beneficiary injury as universal prerequisites. Those admissions applications reinforce, but do not decide, this procurement record.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government bears constitutional justification. Correcting demonstrated discrimination or barriers is a lawful objective; neither general societal disparity alone nor a prior adjudication of each beneficiary’s injury is necessary or sufficient. Engineering Contractors Association v. Metropolitan Dade County requires serious assessment of supported practical alternatives.

* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Denver’s 1990, 1996 and 1998 ordinances; 42 U.S.C. §1983 supplies the asserted civil remedy subject to its own predicates.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Reliable market evidence and linked private discrimination may establish the city’s actual remedial problem; no historical burden-shifting rule relieves Denver of ultimate justification.

* **Open Point:** Whether the established evidence supports the actual categories and relationship, and whether remaining fit objections were preserved.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court demanded forms of direct proof and rejected parts of the city’s statistical and anecdotal showing. Denver argues that those legal filters excluded evidence of barriers affecting its procurement market and that CWC waived the separate tailoring attack. CWC contests reliability, firm availability, causal inferences and use of private-market data. The United States appeared as amicus supporting the remedial program; no distinct verified remedial proposal is attributed. The Tenth Circuit applied historical heightened-scrutiny terminology and a challenger rebuttal framework; those formulations are not the governing burden.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance can rest on the supported remedial problem, substantial relationship and preserved law-of-the-case ruling under the Court's standards, without adopting historical strict scrutiny. Reversal requires an actual evidentiary or fit failure, not the absence of individual discrimination adjudications. A focused remand would be warranted for a genuinely unresolved material category or alternative, but a retrial merely to relabel an adequate record would serve no legal need. Invalidity would require relief against the program’s proved defect and separate proof for damages; validity defeats the presented liability theory.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Adarand’s common substantial-justification rule, not the lower court's constitutional vocabulary.

## 26. Center for National Security Studies v. U.S. Department of Justice

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Center for National Security Studies v. U.S. Department of Justice

* **Citation or Docket:** 331 F.3d 918

* **October Term:** 2003

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; merits submission for review of the identified judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-01-06

* **Question Presented:** Whether FOIA permits categorical withholding of information identifying persons detained in the post-September 11 investigation and their lawyers, and whether the First Amendment or common law independently requires access.

* **Question Granted:** The question presented above; review is confined to the preserved issues and judgment identified here.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit reversed an order requiring release of detainee and attorney names and otherwise sustained withholding; Judge Tatel dissented.

* **Relief Requested:** The requesting organizations seek disclosure and reversal of categorical withholding; DOJ seeks affirmance under FOIA exemptions and rejection of independent access claims.

* **Material Record:** The requests cover identities, lawyers, arrest and release dates, locations and reasons for detention. Records concern immigration detainees, persons charged criminally and material witnesses subject to court orders. DOJ invoked interference with enforcement, privacy, safety and other protections, relying on declarations about what an adversary could infer from combined information. The district court ordered names released while allowing other information to be withheld. Release or deportation of many detainees matters to whether an ongoing enforcement connection remains.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires an adequate exemption showing and review of segregability. Sealed judicial records, grand-jury material, agency records and publicly filed criminal charges are not interchangeable. An assertion that detainees could contact lawyers or disclose their own names does not establish actual access for every person or eliminate the statutory request.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conduct, state adjudication and judgment-finality dates retain their distinct legal significance where applicable.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2003. Any controlling decision effective before the case's event date applies according to its actual holding; a pending matter supplies no rule.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No earlier same-term holding is relied on unless identified below.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal issues independently and preserve the record-specific standard of review for factual findings; the Court may not supply missing findings.

### Issue 1: Exemptions and judicial assessment

* **Controlling Law:** United States Department of Justice v. Landano rejects an agency-wide presumption and permits supported, rebuttable categorical inferences under Exemption 7(D); it preserves the government’s burden, protective review and reasonably segregable disclosure. Its exact confidentiality holding does not itself decide Exemption 7(A).

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under FOIA the agency bears the burden of establishing a statutory exemption. Defined categories may suffice when their actual characteristics support the predicted harm; classification as national security is not itself an exemption.

* **Other Governing Law:** 5 U.S.C. §552, especially (a)(4)(B), (b)(3), (b)(7)(A), (C), and (F), and segregability; applicable court sealing orders and Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e).

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Predictive intelligence judgments deserve informed weight but must connect disclosure to the exemption’s statutory harm.

* **Open Point:** Whether the submitted blanket categories and declarations adequately establish interference with ongoing enforcement and protect only exempt information.

### Issue 2: Independent access

* **Controlling Law:** North Jersey Media Group v. Ashcroft recognizes qualified public access to adversarial removal hearings, subject to supported closure findings and consideration of narrower alternatives. United Reporting preserves the absence of a universal right to executive records while requiring scrutiny of speech-selected access. A hearing-access right and access to an executive investigative compilation are distinct.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the requested institution and record; judicial openness does not automatically establish a right to an executive investigative list.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment and the asserted common-law access doctrine, distinct from FOIA’s enacted disclosure rights.

* **Material Other Authority:** None additional material.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Government cannot evade applicable statutory disclosure by invoking absence of a general constitutional entitlement.

* **Open Point:** Whether any preserved access claim extends to these executive compilation records rather than particular judicial proceedings.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

DOJ argues that identities reveal investigative direction, permit coordination and intimidation, and create a mosaic useful to terrorists; privacy and safety independently support withholding. Requesters answer that largely immigration-based detentions and completed releases cannot all be presumed part of an ongoing terrorism proceeding, and seek meaningful segregability review. The majority below gave substantial weight to predictive declarations; the dissent disputed the sufficiency and breadth of the showing. A supported category can avoid person-by-person public explanations without making judicial review ceremonial. The common-law and First Amendment arguments must be addressed separately from statutory exemptions. Competing opinions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance could accept defined categories as adequate under Exemption 7(A), preserving other exemptions. Vacatur could require a concrete interference nexus and segregability, with protected submissions where needed. Immediate wholesale release would be unwarranted without resolving independent privacy, safety and sealing grounds. Rejecting a universal constitutional list-access right would not decide whether a particular removal or criminal hearing may be closed, and would not defeat FOIA relief.

* **Record and Legal Uncertainty:** Each withholding category requires the actual declarations and record; no detainee's investigative role, innocence or access to counsel is presumed.

### Issue 1: Reviewable statutory harm

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply FOIA’s government-borne burden and extend Landano’s supported-category method to the distinct statutory harm under Exemption 7(A).

Courts may protect sensitive supporting material, but must independently assess the nexus and release reasonably segregable nonexempt information. * **Reach Within the Question:** Requested agency records and properly defined categories, including identities and lawyer information where the necessary harm is actually established; not judicial authority to disregard a lawful sealing order.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those grounds are decided, courts apply each exemption’s own predicates, use justified protective review and segregate releasable material; no blanket release or blanket secrecy follows.

The same discipline gives effect to 7(A)’s different text without demanding disclosure of the very intelligence being protected. The seriousness of the investigation explains the potential harm but not why each category, including concluded detention records, necessarily reveals an active operation. Other exemptions may still justify particular withholding.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend United States Department of Justice v. Landano’s burden-and-category reasoning without importing its confidentiality element into 7(A).

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The existing record does not resolve all independent privacy, safety, grand-jury and sealing grounds.

### Issue 2: Access theories

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved issue is necessary to resolve the challenged judgment; the position rests on the material record described below.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Constitution independently requires this executive list.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering distinction between general access and discriminatory access conditions.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The requested executive compilation is the vehicle. A ruling on courtroom closure would reach different institutions and records. Avoiding the access question entirely would leave an independently asserted route unresolved.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The First Amendment does not create a general right to an executive investigative compilation merely because it identifies detained persons, and the common-law access claim presented here does not displace FOIA’s specific regime for these agency records. A speech-selected access restriction or a particular judicial proceeding remains governed by its own law.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The asserted general right to the requested DOJ compilation; no adjudication of a detainee’s access to counsel, judicial process or freedom to disclose personal information.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until those distinct claims are decided, courts apply the existing access and due-process rules for the institution and person involved.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** North Jersey Media Group requires a justified, appropriately limited closure of adversarial removal proceedings; a categorical national-security label is insufficient. That holding does not entitle a requester to an executive compilation of every detainee's identity. United Reporting separately addresses speech-selected access conditions, which are not established here. The hearing-access rule, individual detention rights and FOIA's record-specific duties each remain enforceable through their own legal channels.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply United Reporting to access classifications and distinguish North Jersey Media Group v. Ashcroft's qualified removal-hearing access without weakening its closure safeguards.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific judicial or removal-hearing closure and individual detention or counsel claims are outside this record.

The provider argues that converting its safety service into a listening device while disabling the promised services exceeds the statute. The Government treats minimum interference as a comparative command: the provider must use the least disruptive feasible method, even if substantial disruption is unavoidable. Compensation and inconvenience to the provider are analytically different from loss of the customer’s service.

## 39. United States v. Carnes

### Issue 1: Protection of a communications instrumentality

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lopez preserves federal authority over interstate instrumentalities. The instrumentality cases distinguish a direct network or instrumentality relationship from an object’s remote manufacturing history.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the instrumentality actually used and the statutory conduct directed at it. A local instance does not defeat protection of an interstate system, but a historical interstate component does not federalize unrelated conduct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §8; 18 U.S.C. §2511 and the applicable definitions of wire communication and interception.
* **Material Other Authority:** The telephone network’s interstate function is the asserted basis. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt addresses different noneconomic-conduct routes and does not make those routes exclusive.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Direct interference with a communications network can be regulated without proving that every intercepted conversation crosses a state boundary.
* **Open Point:** Application of that existing instrumentality principle to the actual statutory interception offense; no general jurisdiction over every recording is required.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Carnes argues that wholly intrastate calls and conduct lack a substantial interstate effect. The Government responds that the telephone network is an interstate instrumentality and unauthorized interception directly compromises it. The strongest limiting principle is actual interception of covered communications using that network, not mere interstate origin of a recorder. The lower court sustained federal power; independent sentencing and other criminal issues remain governed by their actual mandates.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the commerce objection if the charged conduct directly intercepts an interstate communications instrumentality. If the statutory offense reaches materially different conduct without the asserted network relationship, analyze that actual provision rather than add an element. No wholesale affirmance of unrelated sentencing components follows from rejection of the commerce ground.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The lawful rule covers covered intrastate calls carried by an interstate telephone system. It does not establish federal power over all face-to-face conversations or possession of an imported recorder.
