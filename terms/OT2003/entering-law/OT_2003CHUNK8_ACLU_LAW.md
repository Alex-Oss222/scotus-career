#### Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-511.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether the CDA's indecency and patently-offensive restrictions satisfy the First Amendment

**Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when uncertain and substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the Government fails to establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. On this Internet record, §223(a)(1)(B)'s indecency prohibition and §223(d)(1)'s patently-offensive transmission and display prohibitions, together with their corresponding facility-permitting offenses, fail that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Material application:** Uncertain criminal categories reach substantial lawful adult discussion; scienter does not supply a clear content boundary. The record's age-verification costs, anonymity loss and exclusion of lawful adults leave the statutory defenses inadequate, while user filtering and precise treatment of unprotected material provide materially less restrictive approaches.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Vagueness contributes to the First Amendment holding; no separate Fifth Amendment ruling is made. The decision establishes no uniform classification of all Internet services and does not invalidate every age screen or every future control.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sable, Butler and Bolger preserve adult speech despite child-protection interests. Ginsberg's confined commercial rule for material obscene for minors is distinguished. Pacifica retains its broadcast setting; ACT/Pacifica preserves independent protection of adult access. Turner's content-neutral carriage setting is distinguished. Denver Area's compulsory-segregation holding supplies an adult-access analogy, without generalizing its fractured permission judgments. Broadrick supplies substantial overbreadth.

##### What textual severance and clarified preliminary relief remain permissible

**Holding:** The discrete words imposing indecency liability in §223(a)(1)(B) are severable from its obscenity prohibition. The Court affirms the clarified preliminary injunction against that indecency branch and its corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2); the general severability provision does not authorize creating a new minor-only offense or inserting a value exception absent from the enacted text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No minor-only offense or serious-value exception is judicially added. The Court reserves a blanket prohibition for seventeen-year-olds, parental approval, extraterritorial reach and future effective nonexclusionary age assurance. Independently lawful obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful-contact laws retain their own predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified preliminary injunction is affirmed against indecency under §223(a)(1)(B) and corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2). Section (a)(2) remains enforceable for (a)(1)(A), (C), (D), (E), and obscenity or child pornography under (a)(1)(B). Section (d) has no separate textual obscenity branch and receives no such carve-out. The relief remains preliminary; no permanent decree or new monetary award is entered.

**Material judgment:** Transmission and corresponding facility relief: 8–1, O'Connor opposing only the minor-only transmission applications. Display relief and preservation of severable independent law: unanimous.




#### United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc., 529 U.S. 803 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1682.
**Decided:** May 22, 2000.

##### I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny

**Holding:** A governmental restriction selecting protected adult cable programming by its sexual content must satisfy strict scrutiny: it must be narrowly tailored to a compelling interest, with the Government bearing the ultimate burden of justifying its means. When a concrete, plausibly effective and less speech-restrictive arrangement is presented, the Government must establish why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Legislative predictions receive serious consideration, but neither scientific certainty, harm to every child nor a perfect alternative is required. A merely theoretical alternative is insufficient; actual adult burdens and supported implementation concerns matter.

##### II. The Government has not justified this adult burden against the noticed household alternative

**Holding:** Section 505 cannot impose its complete-blocking or sixteen-hour time-channeling burden on the protected programming at issue because the Government has not established that household-request blocking with adequate notice is comparatively inadequate enough to justify that burden. This is a developed-record failure of necessity, not a finding that adequate notice already occurred, that household blocking is flawless, or that every narrower child-protection measure is invalid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins. Scalia and Breyer dissent from this application.

**Material application:** The alternative is household-request blocking with adequate notice identifying the affected channels, understandable audio/video bleed, possible child exposure without parental knowledge, free blocking and the telephone request method. It includes repeated notice at reasonable intervals and special mailed notice to prior blocking recipients after the adult channel changes. Billing inserts, preview channels and advertisements elsewhere are examples, not cumulative compulsory media. No precise interval, installation deadline, repair protocol or automatic replacement rule is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The child-risk finding remains; low distributed-device figures during unproved notice establish neither informed refusal nor failed adequately publicized blocking. Delay, malfunction, channel changes and operator costs matter without becoming universal failure findings. No completed notice experiment, guaranteed technology, cost-free installation or digital conversion is assumed.

##### III. Affirmance preserves the injunction without creating a judicial notice program

**Holding:** The declaration and injunction against §505 are affirmed at the adjudicated scope, while §504's distinct requested-blocking command remains effective. The affirmance creates no Supreme Court notice order, statutory publicity command or new authority over nonparty operators, and leaves unreviewed collateral lower relief only at its actual scope and procedural status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 504, codified at §560, requires a cable operator, upon a subscriber's request and without charge to that subscriber, to fully scramble or block each channel's audio and video so that a nonsubscriber does not receive it. It has no independent indecency or sexually oriented channel trigger. Subsection (b) supplies the same scrambling definition. This command took effect February 8, 1996. Subscriber-selected blocking and §505's default/time restriction are distinct; compliance with one does not automatically satisfy the other. Free service to the subscriber does not mean zero operator equipment or installation cost. Section 504 itself contains no notice program.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The adjudication concerns protected nonobscene adult programming. Equal protection, vagueness, obscenity enforcement and evidence-supported narrower measures are not decided. The lower court’s announced contractual-notice direction is neither a proved implementation event nor a new Supreme Court notice-power holding; inadequate notice does not automatically restore §505.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the declaration and enforcement injunction at their adjudicated scope. Section 504 survives separately. No proof-repair remand, technical program, mandatory device, new hours, publicity order or nonparty obligation is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Action for Children's Television v. FCC / Pacifica Foundation v. FCC:** Applies independent review of necessity and the practical burden of a broad adult exclusion. Its broadcast-specific holding does not establish that every medium or every schedule is identical.
- **FCC v. Pacifica Foundation:** Preserves its confined contextual broadcast permission. It does not automatically sustain this cable restriction or withdraw protection from nonobscene adult programming.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC / Alliance for Community Media v. FCC:** Applies the distinction between private editorial permission and Congress's integral compulsory restriction. Its invalidation of that scheme neither condemns every household-request tool nor validates the present timing burden.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Applies protection of adult access and medium-specific scrutiny. It does not decide cable technology or create a finding that every parent receives effective notice.
- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC:** Preserves independent evidentiary review and the distinct intermediate standard for content-neutral carriage. Neither conclusive deference nor that intermediate test governs §505.
- **City of Erie v. Pap's A.M.:** Applies the distinction between communicative selection and genuinely general conduct regulation. The present content-directed command cannot receive incidental-burden treatment merely because it concerns distribution technology.




#### Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, 535 U.S. 564

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1293.
**Decided:** May 13, 2002.

##### Whether COPA's community-standards feature alone establishes facial invalidity

**Holding:** COPA's use of contemporary community standards does not, standing alone, establish facial invalidity: constitutional review must assess the complete offense, its limiting elements and defenses, and its actual burden on protected expression together. This rejection of automatic invalidity supplies neither a presumption that COPA survives nor a holding that varying local standards are constitutional in every application to the Web.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct votes. Breyer additionally joins the remand and continuing injunction, but his national-community construction does not join Part II. No national standard or broader separate theory controls.

**Material application:** Review must consider the commercial-coverage, whole-material, prurience, specified-content and serious-value elements together with each defense, provider exclusion and privacy condition. The statutory commercial-purpose label does not turn protected literature into advertising; lack of serious value for minors does not itself remove adult protection. The existing findings about geographic exclusion, verification expense, anonymity and adult access remain relevant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final constitutional approval of COPA, categorical validation of varying local standards or national-standard construction follows. After construction and examination of the full statutory operation, geographic variation may still be material to the complete challenge. Concrete, plausibly effective, less restrictive alternatives remain subject to the governmental justification burden; courts may interpret but not add missing offense restrictions.

**Material statutory scope:** COPA, 47 U.S.C. §231, reaches knowing commercial World Wide Web communications, made with knowledge of the material's character in interstate or foreign commerce, available to any person under seventeen and containing material harmful to minors. Commercial purpose requires a regular business course with a profit objective and knowing posting or solicitation of the material; actual profit and a sole or principal business are unnecessary. This statutory category does not make protected expression commercial advertising. Harmful material includes obscenity or material satisfying all three requirements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal or pandering judged by the average person applying contemporary community standards; patently offensive depictions, descriptions or representations of sexual acts or contacts, actual or simulated and including normal or perverted acts, or lewd displays of genitals or post-pubescent female breasts, with respect to minors; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for minors. The community-standards phrase appears expressly in the prurience clause.

Subsection (a) permits a fine up to $50,000, imprisonment up to six months, or both; intentional violations carry an additional fine up to $50,000 per violation, and the separate civil penalty is also up to $50,000 per violation. For the latter two, each day is a separate violation. Subsection (b)(1)–(3) excludes a telecommunications carrier to the extent it provides telecommunications service, an Internet-access-service provider in that capacity, and an information-location service in that capacity. Subsection (b)(4) separately protects intermediaries transmitting, storing, retrieving, hosting, formatting or translating another's communication without selecting or altering its content; deletion consistent with subsection (c) or §230 does not defeat that paragraph's protection. Subsection (c)(1) supplies a good-faith access-restriction defense through a credit card, debit account, adult access code or adult personal identification number, an age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable measures feasible with available technology. Subsection (c)(2) separately protects good-faith implementation or restriction activity against a cause of action only when that activity violates no law carrying a criminal or civil penalty. Subsection (d) separately requires protection against unauthorized access to collected information and prior written or electronic consent for disclosure, from the adult concerned or the parent or guardian of a person under seventeen. Disclosure is permitted when necessary to communicate, to conduct a legitimate related business activity, or under an authorizing court order.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical community-standards appellate judgment and remand for the remaining integrated constitutional inquiry. The preliminary injunction remains effective; the judgment authorizes no prosecution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Reno preserves adult Internet access and is distinguished as to COPA's different elements. Miller supplies the conjunctive obscenity framework; Pope keeps serious value independent of local approval. Hamling and Sable do not automatically decide the Web restriction on protected adult speech. Playboy preserves actual justification and consideration of concrete, plausibly effective alternatives without demanding perfect alternatives or a prescribed legislative-record form. Free Speech Coalition and Broadrick preserve substantial-overbreadth review; Free Speech Coalition also bars judicial insertion of absent limits.




#### United States v. American Library Association, Inc., 539 U.S. 194 — library filtering, lawful disabling and facial adult-access challenges

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-361.
**Decided:** June 23, 2003.

##### Permissible adult disabling and the distinct funding-program boundaries

**Holding:** The statutes permit disabling for bona fide research or other lawful purposes, which are alternative grounds; an adult may request an entire unfiltered session for lawful reading without identifying sites or explaining a purpose. E-rate's disabling clause expressly applies during adult use, whereas LSTA's clause contains no adult-use limitation. This is statutory permission, not a command that every request be granted or a factual finding that effective access exists at every library.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory permission is not a mandatory grant of every request or a finding of effective access at every library. The two program exceptions and compliance consequences remain separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Free Speech Coalition continues to protect nonobscene material made without actual-child exploitation; incorporation of child-pornography definitions does not restore invalidated protected coverage.

**Material application:** LSTA's identified computer-purchase and direct Internet-cost restriction applies to a covered library that does not receive the specified discounted services. E-rate's condition excludes libraries receiving discounted services solely for purposes other than Internet access, Internet service, or internal connections. The required technology covers Internet-access computers; obscenity and child pornography are the adult categories, with the additional harmful-to-minors category for users under seventeen. Harmful-to-minors material must satisfy all three statutory elements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal in nudity, sex, or excretion; a patently offensive depiction, description, or representation, with respect to suitability for minors, of actual or simulated sexual acts or contact, normal or perverted sexual acts, or lewd exhibition of genitals; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political, or scientific value as to minors. The child-pornography cross-reference operates subject to Free Speech Coalition's existing protection of nonobscene material made without actual-child exploitation; incorporation does not restore its invalidated protected coverage.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Under §9134(f)(5), when the Director has reason to believe a recipient is substantially failing to comply with the Internet-safety subsection, the Director may withhold further payments, seek compliance through a cease-and-desist complaint, or enter a compliance agreement. Those remedies are exclusive for that failure; the Director may not recover funds and must end withholding upon determining, by certification or other appropriate evidence, that the failure is cured. Under §9134(f)(6), invalidity of a provision leaves the remainder of that subsection unaffected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Under §254(h)(6)(F), knowing failure to comply with annual certification-submission guidelines makes a library ineligible for discounted services or funding in their place; submission of the missing certification restores discounted-service eligibility. Knowing failure to ensure that computer use complies with the certification requires reimbursement of all funds and discounts received under §254(h) for the certification's covered period. A library may cure that operational failure by bringing computer use into compliance; submission to the Commission of a certification or other appropriate evidence of cure restores discounted-service eligibility.

##### Effective adult access and the facial speech challenge

**Holding:** Congress may condition the specified E-rate and LSTA assistance on reasonable filtering measures designed to restrict obscenity, child pornography and material harmful to minors. A publicly operated library that accepts covered assistance and provides general Internet access to adult patrons may not, however, use the required filtering system to impose a continuing, unjustified prohibition on an adult's access to constitutionally protected material. An adult seeking lawful protected material must have an effective means of obtaining unfiltered access, through prompt whole-session disabling or an equally effective method, without being required to identify the subject of the reading, disclose a viewpoint or demonstrate an official-approved reason. Reasonable procedures addressing actual technical limitations, lawful supervision and ordinary resource constraints remain permissible; a nominal request process that routinely denies access, imposes material delay without adequate justification or requires disclosure of the content of protected reading does not satisfy the requirement. Because the statutory disabling provisions permit compliance, and the facial record does not establish that the conditions necessarily or substantially compel unlawful exclusion, neither program is facially invalid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins in this proposition and mandate, without a Marks synthesis of different broader explanations. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas join Part I and concur only in the judgment of reversal on their library-service ground; Stevens joins Part I and dissents from the reversal.

**Material application:** Part I's construction permits every covered library to provide the required access. The record establishes substantial overblocking and delay, staffing, privacy and deterrence concerns, but not that the conditions necessarily or substantially compel unlawful exclusion. Filtering may be the initial configuration; selection does not justify a continuing, unjustified bar on protected reading already available through the service the library has opened. A temporary malfunction followed by prompt repair differs from a continuing, discretionary prohibition on lawful reading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Constitution does not guarantee public provision of Internet access, require a library to acquire every information source, or forbid otherwise lawful rules concerning illegal material and resource administration. Neither statutory provision commands libraries to keep filtering for adults when disabling is necessary to provide lawful protected access. The Court neither assumes every request is honored nor treats one conceivable valid implementation as dispositive; an actual denial claim requires proof of the particular library's operation and injury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse facial invalidation and remove its injunction under both E-rate and LSTA, 8–1. An adult denied effective access through an actual library policy may seek prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against a legally responsible governmental defendant under an available enforcement cause of action; a proved violation requires prompt, effective access by lawful means rather than an automatic prohibition of all filtering or termination of the library's Internet service. Funding authorities may not treat lawful adult disabling as noncompliance; that funding holding appears under Constitutional Structure, United States v. American Library Association, No. 02-361, June 23, 2003.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Broadrick and Hicks supply the relative substantiality inquiry, distinct from an every-application requirement. Reno is applied to protect lawful adult Internet expression within the general-purpose service a library has opened. Denver is applied to distinguish genuine, effective adult access from a mechanism imposing substantial or unjustified burdens, and retains its distinction between compulsory delayed-access machinery and ordinary voluntary requests. Forbes and Finley are preserved for ordinary selective programming and funding decisions; they do not confer immunity for every government-imposed restriction on access to protected expression already available through an opened service. Rosenberger, Velazquez and Center for Reproductive Law preserve independent expression within their actual program settings.




#### United States v. American Library Association, Inc., 539 U.S. 194 — library funding and dependent unconstitutional-conditions claims

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-361.
**Decided:** June 23, 2003.

##### Effective adult access and the facial spending challenge

**Holding:** The covered funding conditions do not facially require unconstitutional conduct because the statutory disabling provisions permit effective protection of adult access to constitutionally protected material. Congress may not, however, condition covered assistance on a recipient's refusal to provide the adult-access protection required by the First Amendment. The federal funding authorities may not treat lawful adult disabling, authorized by the statute and constitutionally necessary in the covered circumstances, as a violation of the funding condition. A recipient that employs filtering in a constitutionally permissible manner may receive funds subject to ordinary eligibility and compliance requirements. An actual policy that unlawfully obstructs protected adult expression remains subject to appropriately fitted relief. The existence of a lawful funding program does not immunize a separate unconstitutional library practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas join only the judgment of reversal; Stevens dissents from it.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial spending ground along with the facial speech ground for both programs, 8–1, and remand for judgment rejecting the facial claims and removal of the injunction founded on them. An adult denied effective access through an actual library policy may seek as-applied prospective relief against actual denial, material delay or compelled disclosure. The statutory construction, the adult-access rule and the facial speech holding appear in First Amendment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dole’s bar on conditions inducing unconstitutional conduct remains intact and is applied: these conditions do not facially require such conduct because the disabling provisions permit compliance, but Congress may not condition covered assistance on a recipient's refusal to provide the adult-access protection the First Amendment requires.




## Standards and Tests selection: Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

### Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

**Governing question:** Facial review of indecency restrictions on a general-purpose communications network.

**Current rule:** A content-based criminal restriction fails the First Amendment when uncertain, substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the government does not establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. Knowledge that a minor is present, other available channels and scienter do not by themselves cure uncertain content categories or adult-access burdens. Age-verification defenses must afford workable protection on the actual record; cost, loss of anonymity and lawful adult exclusion remain material. A severability clause permits removal of a discrete unlawful textual branch but does not authorize judicial creation of a minor-only offense or an absent value exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Overbreadth, uncertainty and narrow fit: Reno v. ACLU, Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment; textual severance boundary: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (eight), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Reno v. ACLU, 1997-06-26.

**Open question:** Precisely drawn future controls with effective nonexclusionary age assurance, parental-approval questions, every Internet service's classification and extraterritorial scope remain open. The holding creates no independent Fifth Amendment rule and leaves obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful contact to their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union (1997-06-26) — First Amendment / Internet content restrictions.




## Standards and Tests selection: Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

### Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

**Governing question:** The justification required for government restrictions directed at the sexual content of protected adult cable speech.

**Current rule:** Strict scrutiny requires a narrowly tailored restriction serving a compelling interest; the Government bears the ultimate justification burden. Once a concrete, plausibly effective arrangement imposing less restraint on speech is presented, the Government must show why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden. Child protection and parental assistance do not by themselves establish necessity. Assessment concerns the actual medium, speech burden and supported implementation problems, with serious consideration but not conclusive deference to legislative predictions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.; No. 98-1682. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny, 2000-05-22; Ginsburg with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Neither scientific certainty nor a perfect alternative is required. A paper alternative alone does not establish practical effectiveness. The rule creates no statutory publicity duty, automatic restoration of an invalid restriction, universal blocking-device rule or prohibition of supported narrower regulation; obscenity remains distinct.

### Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union, No. 96-511

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Full affirmance | Entire opinion |
| Concurrence in the judgment in part and dissent in part | O'Connor | Author alone | Agrees with display and mixed-audience relief; would narrow transmission relief | Separate minor-only transmission and application-severability ground; no principal-opinion join |



#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part.** Butler, Sable and Bolger protect adult access; Ginsberg permits genuine restrictions confined to minors. The Internet presently lacks generally usable identity gates, so the display offense and mixed adult/minor conversations cannot be saved by treating the network as divided into adult and child zones. She agrees with the injunction to that extent and with preservation of independent obscenity enforcement.

She would retain the transmission provisions only where the initiating adult knows all recipients are minors, with corresponding facility liability confined to those underlying communications. She construes §223(d)(1)(A) to require knowledge of minority, relying on its wording and DeBartolo's constitutional-avoidance principle. Such a communication has no other adult audience whose access is lost. Brockett's application-severability principle and the statutory severability provision support preserving that application. Although the CDA's content categories are broader than Ginsberg's obscenity-for-minors rule, she finds no showing under Broadrick that the additional protected minor speech is substantial relative to the provisions' legitimate sweep. She does not deny minors all First Amendment protection or foreclose individual vagueness challenges. Her view relies on current technology, not predicted improvements. No other Justice joins her separate writing.

### United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc., No. 98-1682

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas throughout; Scalia and Breyer in Part I | Affirms the First Amendment invalidation and injunction | Part I: protected adult expression and demanding content-based scrutiny; Part II: comparative necessity on this record; Part III: confined affirmance and remedial limits |
| Dissent | Breyer | Scalia | Would reverse the First Amendment invalidation and enforcement injunction | Joins the Court's Part I framework, but finds the default-protection arrangement adequately justified and would remand for lawful disposition of preserved grounds not reached |



#### Separate Writings

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** The dissent joins Part I's protected-speech premise and strict-scrutiny framework but would reverse the First Amendment invalidation and enforcement injunction. It finds the defined default-protection scheme sufficiently tailored to the actual channel-bleed problem. Complete audio/video blocking remains an alternative to time channeling, the trigger concerns channels primarily devoted to the covered programming, and the remaining nighttime period preserves an important opportunity for adult access.

The dissent accepts the significant loss of daytime viewing and the concrete alternative with adequate recurring notice. Its disagreement concerns comparative protection: a request system depends on parental receipt, understanding and action, and it leaves implementation problems involving delay, malfunction and channel changes. In the dissent's assessment the supported difference between that activation-dependent system and default protection is sufficiently important to carry the Government's burden. The Court gives too little weight to children whose parents do not activate the alternative.

ACT/Pacifica's dissenting approach to justified channeling and Denver's distinct default-protection reasoning support that conclusion without importing Turner's intermediate scrutiny. Actual operator costs and technological limits also matter, but the break-even scenarios are not request forecasts and low distribution does not establish parental indifference. Neither universally failing devices, scientific certainty, perfect protection, cost-free installation nor inevitable digital conversion is assumed.

The dissent would remand for lawful disposition of properly preserved grounds not reached below, deciding equal protection and vagueness neither way and prescribing no interim relief. Section 504 would remain separate; reversal would create no statutory notice duty or technical-compliance finding. The dissent treats the challenged nonobscene programming as protected expression throughout its analysis.

### Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, No. 00-1293

#### Opinion Topology

Part II has seven direct joins. Breyer's separate construction supplies an additional judgment vote, not an eighth join in that rationale. No broader separate theory commands a majority.



#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring.** She joins the confined holding because the present showing does not identify sufficient otherwise protected material made unlawful solely through community variation once prurience and serious value are considered. Pope's objective-value inquiry and Sable's distinction between an adult ban and confined protection matter, but the Web's inability to select recipients prevents treating conventional mail and telephone distribution as conclusive. She affirmatively regards a national standard as constitutionally permissible and necessary for reasonable Internet obscenity regulation. Her judgment neither forecloses an adequately supported facial challenge nor denies later as-applied relief.

**Thomas, concurring, joined by Scalia.** The prurience and objective serious-value requirements materially narrow the relevant category compared with the CDA. Miller, Pope, Hamling and Sable support rejecting community variation as a sufficient objection to this statutory category; the existing showing does not establish substantial variation in the covered material. This broader explanation would reject the community-standards objection more fully than the Court's common ground. It does not adopt a national-standard amendment, resolve COPA's other constitutional defects, or treat statutory commercial purpose as permission to suppress protected adult literature. They join continued interim protection while the other injunction grounds are considered.

**Souter, concurring, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg.** The extent of protected expression cannot be measured until the statute's reach is determined. Commercial coverage, the relevant whole work or communication, the different ages within the under-seventeen category and venue consequences affect the relationship between legitimate regulation and protected expression. Reno, Playboy and Free Speech Coalition require attention to actual adult access, not an assumption that value for minors cures the adult burden. These Justices expressly preserve the community-standards challenge as part of the integrated inquiry: after construction, geographic variation may still prove decisive. Their join does not approve local standards as constitutionally harmless, depend on hypothetical geographic controls, or adopt a national standard.

**Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the statutory background and disposition but reads community standards to mean the national adult community. The ordinary phrase in context, its legislative explanation and avoidance of serious constitutional difficulty support that construction in his view. A local standard would give restrictive communities practical control over protected speech distributed nationally. Variation among juries applying one national standard is different from variation among the governing substantive standards themselves. This construction is necessary to his agreement, not a merely optional possibility. It does not resolve the other COPA objections, and he agrees that the injunction should continue.

**Stevens, dissenting.** He would affirm the preliminary injunction on the community-standards ground. His objections in Smith and Sable, together with Reno's adult-access rule, apply to the established inability of national Web speakers to avoid restrictive local audiences. Serious value for minors does not define all expression adults may constitutionally receive. Narrowing the commercial category or the whole-work unit may reduce absolute coverage without curing substantial overbreadth relative to legitimate applications. Verification burdens do not erase that injury. He rejects insertion of a national standard into language with an established community-based meaning. His position preserves the legitimate interest in protecting children and ordinary restrictions on unprotected material; it does not require invalidating every future Internet safeguard.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring.** Kennedy joins the opinion of the Court and writes to state the practical ground on which the facial question turns for him. Prompt whole-session disabling without explanation, which the Court requires, leaves no substantial adult-access burden. The record establishes overblocking and practical concerns but does not establish a significant burden on that lawful election sufficient for the facial decree. The difference from Denver's compulsory access machinery is material. Whether the election works is shown in the library where it is invoked: actual denial, material delay, disclosure requirements, or another substantial obstruction would call for review of the practice that produces it.

**Breyer, concurring.** The constitutional inquiry should examine this program's actual burden and means. Protection against unlawful images and minors' exposure are serious interests; imperfect filters impose burdens, but the lawful whole-session request matters to their extent. On this record the request burden does not produce disproportionate harm sufficient to invalidate the conditions facially. This contextual scrutiny is more demanding than ordinary rational-basis review and does not rest on rigid forum classification. Materially obstructive local practices remain separately reviewable.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices join Part I and the judgment. They consider the actual service Congress assists together with the lawful adult election. Libraries exercise selection in providing a usable resource, and Forbes and Finley protect genuine institutional selection within their respective programs. Filtering aimed at unlawful material and protection of minors, coupled with permitted explanation-free adult access, fits this assisted service sufficiently to defeat the facial challenge. That ground decides the case without a general constitutional prescription for adult disabling; concrete obstruction can be reviewed on the record of an actual practice. Their ground addresses this library design; funding terminology does not create immunity for material unconstitutional administration or penalties on independent expression.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Local librarians' selection differs from a nationwide condition compelling filters across an otherwise opened Internet resource. The default barrier burdens readers and authors whose lawful material the filters remove. Permitting a request does not eliminate the burden of asking or the discretion surrounding release. Reno, Denver, Velazquez, and Center for Reproductive Law require attention to that actual private-expression burden. Stevens would retain protection for adults and remand to fit the decree to the distinct statutory provisions and independently lawful minor protections.
