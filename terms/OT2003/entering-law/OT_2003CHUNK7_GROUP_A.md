## Standards and Tests selection: Retained family association and procreation during imprisonment

### Retained family association and procreation during imprisonment

**Governing question:** A restriction affects family association or an asserted no-contact method of conception compatible with confinement.

**Current rule:** Incarceration does not categorically extinguish family association compatible with confinement or a married prisoner’s claim to attempt conception through a privately arranged, no-contact specimen transfer. Apply Turner’s reasonable-relationship inquiry with substantial institutional deference: assess the restriction’s rational connection to a legitimate prison objective, actual alternatives for exercising the interest, the institutional effects of accommodation and ready alternatives. The inquiry does not require the least restrictive means. The actual proposal and justification require adjudication before relief; any prison-conditions injunction separately requires §3626 findings.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `Gerber-v-Hickman_merits_2003-03-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. A no-contact proposal requires the retained-right inquiry; II. Proof and a fitted prison remedy, 2003-03-25; seven-Justice O’Connor opinion; all nine join the conditional §3626 requirements.
- Compatible family association and Turner mechanics: Canonical Decision Record `Overton-v-Bazzetta_merits_2003-06-16.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Compatible family association and the governing standard, 2003-06-16; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
- Restoration eligibility and independent Eighth Amendment boundary: Canonical Decision Record `Overton-v-Bazzetta_merits_2003-06-16.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The three challenged regulatory designs; III. The separate Eighth Amendment claim, 2003-06-16; eight Associates join Part II; all nine join Part III.

**Present operation effective:** Overton, June 16, 2003, for family association; Gerber, March 25, 2003, for the no-contact conception claim.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary visitation loss alone does not establish objectively serious deprivation or substantial risk of serious harm under the independent Eighth Amendment inquiry. A two-year eligibility point for discretionary restoration is neither guaranteed restoration nor a constitutional safe harbor for every later duration.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.

Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

City of Hialeah — The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application, 1993-06-11. ## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions prospective relief

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires that federal law require the state-law violation, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

**Authority by component:**

- Enacted conditions, scope and all-relief transition: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §802, original 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1)–(2), (c)–(d), (g) and §802(b)(1), April 26, 1996.
- Existing-decree application and substantive findings: Lewis v. Casey — Holdings IV–VI, Holding IV, June 24, 1996; Kennedy Part IV for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins only the statutory disposition. 
- Bounded constitutional operation of termination, not a change to the present entry’s remedial conditions: Plyler v. Moore, Plyler v. Moore — II. The prospective-relief rule preserves judicial adjudication, June 23, 1997; see the termination entry’s component authority.

- Federal-law requirement replaces federal permission; effective on enactment and in pending cases: Prison Litigation Reform Act amendments — §123(a)(1)(A) and (b), amending §3626(a)(1)(B)(i), 1997-11-26.

**Present operation effective:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123, 1997-11-26, for the federal-requirement condition; the other statutory conditions and Lewis qualifications retain their original dates.

**Controlling construction:** Prospective relief is all relief other than compensatory monetary damages. A §3626 civil prison-conditions action excludes habeas proceedings challenging the fact or duration of confinement. A prison includes federal, state and local juvenile or adult criminal detention facilities. Consent decrees include relief based wholly or partly on consent or acquiescence and must comply with subsection (a). Private settlements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct; otherwise available state breach remedies survive. Section 3626 does not govern relief a state court enters solely on state-law claims.

**Effective transition:** Amended §3626 applies to prospective relief originally granted or approved before, on or after April 26, 1996. Existing findings may meet the necessary predicates at their actual scope; ritual wording, modest cost or reasonableness alone neither establishes nor defeats compliance. Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states Plyler’s June 23, 1997 resolution of the presented Article III and due-process objections to original §3626(b)(2), with the full §3626(b)(3) preservation condition. Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin apply the amended conditions within the bounds stated in Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays; no automatic-stay constitutional holding follows.

**Related current rules:** Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states the separate actual-motion triggers; Prisoner-release orders and prison crowding supplies additional release-order requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996). Plyler v. Moore (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prison access and prospective relief.

**Former-rule note:** Before November 26, 1997, §3626(a)(1)(B)(i) required federal permission rather than a federal requirement for the departure from state law. Section 123 applies the revised term from enactment, including pending cases.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b); because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, the presumption against statutory retroactivity does not avoid that constitutional question.

## Standards and Tests selection: Filing-time ownership and foreign-state instrumentality status

### Filing-time ownership and foreign-state instrumentality status

**Governing question:** When an entity claims Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act status and removal under the majority-ownership branch of 28 U.S.C. §1603(b)(2).

**Current rule:** Assess the asserted instrumentality status when the complaint is filed. A sovereign relationship that ended before suit does not supply the ownership-based status or §1441(d) removal privilege. Under the majority-ownership alternative, the foreign State or its political subdivision itself must own a majority of the entity’s shares or other ownership interest; ownership of an intermediary corporation’s shares does not by itself constitute ownership of the subsidiary’s shares. Sovereign control, benefit or purpose alone does not replace that ownership condition.

The entity must meet the separate-legal-person requirement and the other statutory conditions. Section 1603(b)(3) excludes a citizen of a United States State as defined in §1332(c) and (e), and an entity created under a third country’s law. The independent organ alternative retains its own operation; the direct-ownership rule does not dispose of an adequately supported claim under that branch.

**Authority by component:**

- Filing-time measurement: Canonical Decision Record `Dole-Food-Co-v-Patrickson_merits_2003-04-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Instrumentality status is measured at filing, 2003-04-22; all nine directly join.
- Direct majority ownership, independent organ branch and other statutory conditions: Canonical Decision Record `Dole-Food-Co-v-Patrickson_merits_2003-04-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The majority-ownership alternative requires direct sovereign ownership, 2003-04-22; Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg directly join.

**Present operation effective:** Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson, April 22, 2003.

**Exceptions and limits:** This classification does not replace Bancec’s separate liability-attribution rules or its supported agency and fraud-or-injustice exceptions. It establishes no universal conduct-date rule for other forms of immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA sponsor design, fiduciary implementation and genuine benefit payments

### ERISA sponsor design, fiduciary implementation and genuine benefit payments

**Governing question:** Whether plan amendment or release-conditioned retirement benefits establish a prohibited transaction.

**Current rule:** An employer designing and adopting pension-plan amendments acts as sponsor, not fiduciary. Actual implementation is a separate functional inquiry; party-in-interest status alone does not satisfy §406(a)(1)’s requirement that a fiduciary cause a transaction knowing or having reason to know that it is prohibited, subject to §408’s statutory exceptions. The asset-disposition branch does not acquire an extra discretion requirement. Payment of genuine enhanced retirement benefits under an otherwise lawful plan is not prohibited by §406(a)(1)(D) merely because required employment-related releases benefit the employer. This assumes fiduciary implementation for that classification and creates neither a §408 exemption nor a general net-benefit defense.

Employee contributions do not turn amendment design into fiduciary conduct or make participants co-sponsors. Genuine benefit payments within a continuing single plan do not inure to the employer under §403(c)(1), or become prohibited §406(a)(1)(D) transfers, merely because they reduce employer contributions or payroll costs. Surplus remains plan property. Section 406(b)(2)’s adverse-party prohibition is not triggered merely by sponsor adoption of an advantageous benefit design; independent fiduciary implementation and prohibited transactions retain their actual elements.

**Authority by component:**

- Spink — Part I, sponsor capacity and separate implementation, 1996-06-10; Ginsburg for all nine.
- Spink — Part II, genuine release-conditioned benefit payments, 1996-06-10; Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas (seven); Souter and Breyer reserve the question.

- Contributory-plan sponsor capacity, anti-inurement and genuine-payment boundaries: Canonical Decision Record `hughes_aircraft_co_v_jacobson_merits_1999-01-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Genuine payments within the continuing plan do not inure to the employer; II. Amendment design is distinct from fiduciary implementation; V. Incidental employer advantage does not make these benefit payments prohibited transactions, 1999-01-25; nine direct joins to sponsor capacity, eight to the independently stated anti-inurement and transaction grounds.

**Present operation effective:** Hughes Aircraft, 1999-01-25, for the contributory-plan, anti-inurement and §406(b)(2) components; Spink’s existing components remain effective from 1996-06-10.

**Exceptions and limits:** Amendment procedures, participation, funding, vesting, loyalty, anti-inurement and independently applicable §406(b) duties survive. No sham transfer, kickback, unlawful plan or unenforceable release is immunized. Particular release validity under the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act or other law remains open. Surplus does not become employer property.

**Related current rules:** “Corporate procedures for ERISA plan amendments” governs actual authorization and amendment procedure; “Discharge of pension funding obligations as an exchange” governs the distinct property-for-release transaction.

**Holdings navigation:** Lockheed Corp. v. Spink, 517 U.S. 882. [Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson — I. Genuine payments within the continuing plan do not inure to the employer](HOLDINGS.md#i-genuine-payments-within-the-continuing-plan-do-not-inure-to-the-employer-1); [Hughes Aircraft Co. v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Defined-benefit surplus and statutory termination

### Defined-benefit surplus and statutory termination

**Governing question:** Participants’ rights in an overfunded defined-benefit plan and the trigger for termination distribution.

**Current rule:** Participants retain promised and statutorily protected accrued benefits, including the employee-contribution floor, but do not presently own individual shares of all actuarial surplus. Mandatory-contribution protection under §§203(a)(1) and 204(c) uses accumulated contributions with the enacted interest treatment and an annual benefit beginning at normal retirement age, with actuarial equivalence for a different form; voluntary contributions retain §204(c)(4)’s distinct treatment. Lawful benefit payments reducing no protected benefit do not themselves establish forfeiture or cutback.

Closing a contributory benefit structure to new entrants while the plan and obligations continue does not itself trigger §4044 termination distribution. A common-law wasting-trust characterization cannot replace §§4041–4042’s statutory termination process. When termination distribution lawfully occurs, plan liabilities and statutory allocation priorities remain, including employee-derived residual protection; employer reversion additionally requires satisfied liabilities, consistency with law and plan authorization, subject to the enacted timing and employee-residual protections.

**Authority by component:**

- Accrued-benefit protection without individual surplus ownership and statutory nontermination: Canonical Decision Record `hughes_aircraft_co_v_jacobson_merits_1999-01-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. Accrued benefits are protected without individual ownership of all surplus; IV. Continuing benefit obligations do not trigger termination distribution, 1999-01-25; nine direct joins to the common holdings, eight to the statutory contribution and distribution explanations.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-01-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** No actual benefit amount, reversion entitlement or general rule for every partial termination follows. A fair-administration clause alone does not promise a later incentive to persons outside its lawful eligibility class or authorize judicial redesign; a due benefit, unequal administration among eligible participants or independent statutory restriction retains its own claim. Authority: the same Record, Holding VI, eight direct joins.

**Holdings navigation:** [Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson — III. Accrued benefits are protected without individual ownership of all surplus](HOLDINGS.md#iii-accrued-benefits-are-protected-without-individual-ownership-of-all-surplus); [Hughes Aircraft Co. v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Content-neutral permits for shared public space

### Content-neutral permits for shared public space

**Governing question:** Which procedural safeguards govern a genuinely neutral permit system allocating shared space and coordinating activity.

**Current rule:** A genuine content-neutral coordination system need not use Freedman's full censorship procedure, including government-initiated judicial proceedings and the censorship burden after every denial. It must nevertheless constrain discretion through objective management grounds, bound administrative decisionmaking and provide meaningful review. A neutral label does not suffice. Permission to excuse a listed disqualification is not itself facially unbounded discretion, but authorizes neither an invented ground nor viewpoint-selective treatment.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `thomas-v-chicago-park-district_merits_2002-01-15.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Neutral park coordination does not require the entire censorship procedure; II. The ordinance confines denial to objective management grounds; III. The actual deadlines, reasons and review support the facial judgment, 2002-01-15; Scalia's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Thomas v. Chicago Park District, 2002-01-15.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal judicial-decision deadline or automatic permit-issuance rule is adopted. The precise ordinance timetable and approved grounds remain case-specific applications; actual discriminatory administration, delay or unauthorized grounds require separate adjudication.

## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. A write-in prohibition is assessed within the complete access system by what the prohibition itself contributes to the asserted interests; the independent validity of surrounding filing, support and primary rules does not make it presumptively valid. When ordinary nomination routes close before voters can respond, the prohibition must leave an effective route for a genuine, legally eligible alternative candidate. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**
- Governing inherited framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden”, June 8, 1992; Stone, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter (five), applying Anderson and the earlier simulated Norman rule.
- Complete-system assessment and effective-route requirement: Burdick, “2. Hawaii’s complete write-in prohibition leaves no effective route for a genuine alternative candidate,” June 8, 1992; same five Justices.
- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the complete-system assessment, effective-route requirement and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute write-in right follows, and no particular route, counting mechanism or ballot form is required. Valid qualifications, narrow anti-raiding and sore-loser rules and materially different systems remain open. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

**Holdings navigation:** Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Three levels of calibrated review of ballot-access burdens

### Three levels of calibrated review of ballot-access burdens

**Governing question:** How a court places a burden on candidate, party or voter access to the ballot within Anderson's calibrated review, and the justification each level requires.

**Current rule:** Under Anderson–Burdick, the court weighs the character and magnitude of the actual burden against the State’s precise interests and the extent to which those interests require it, and explains where the burden falls on that single calibrated scale. A severe burden requires narrow tailoring to a compelling interest. A substantial burden requires an important objective and a demonstrated substantial relationship between that objective and the restriction’s actual reach; a burden may be substantial without proof that success is impossible, and a valid interest does not by itself establish a sufficient relationship. An ordinary burden imposed by reasonable, nondiscriminatory administration may be justified by important regulatory interests without proof of past disorder. The complete system remains the unit of analysis. A petition percentage sustained within one open system does not answer as a matter of law a pleaded claim that a later system’s combined operation imposes a substantial or severe burden; accepted allegations of that kind require factual development rather than dismissal.

**Authority by component:**

- Three levels, placement and the pleading-stage application: Canonical Decision Record `Cartwright-v-Barnes_merits_2003-02-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Anderson review has three levels, and these cumulative allegations require factual development, 2003-02-25; Souter’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. Thomas, joined by O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, would affirm the access dismissal without reaching the statement of levels.
- Underlying calibrated framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden,” June 8, 1992; the calibrated framework accepted by all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Cartwright, February 25, 2003; Burdick’s June 8, 1992 calibrated framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** The levels describe the structure of Anderson’s calibrated scale, not a new tier, and they leave Part I’s distinct Article I classification unchanged: a genuine support petition is not an added congressional qualification. No universal five-percent safe harbor follows; Jenness remains authority for the complete system it sustained, and the statewide one-percent rule was not independently decided. Party-specific failure to qualify is evidence, not proof of severity.

**Related current rules:** Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system; Effective alternative-candidate route after early closing of nomination; Meaningful official recognition of a qualified party's consenting nomination; Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions; Compulsory outsider selection of official party nominees.

**Holdings navigation:** [Cartwright v. Barnes, 304 F.3d 1138 — Georgia congressional petitions and cumulative access allegations](HOLDINGS.md#cartwright-v-barnes-304-f3d-1138-1).

#### Overton v. Bazzetta, 539 U.S. 126 — prison visitation and compatible family association

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-94.
**Decided:** June 16, 2003.

##### Family association compatible with confinement

**Holding:** Incarceration does not extinguish protected family association that is compatible with confinement. Restrictions on the retained interest are governed by Turner's reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives, considering the rational connection, actual alternative means, institutional impact of accommodation, and ready alternatives; the standard does not demand the least restrictive means.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No right to unrestricted visits or every preferred visitor follows. Prisoners must establish the burden and missing reasonable relationship; officials must identify legitimate neutral objectives and supported connections, with administrative latitude. Actual communication alternatives and institutional conditions matter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pell preserves compatible communication; Turner supplies the deferential operational inquiry, Shaw rejects a special heightened tier, and Gerber requires actual consideration rather than categorical extinction of the claimed compatible interest.

##### Facial validity of the child-visitor restrictions

**Holding:** The facial showing is insufficient to invalidate the limitation of under-eighteen visitors to the inmate's children, stepchildren, grandchildren, and siblings, the exclusion of a child after parental rights terminate, or the requirement of accompaniment by an adult immediate family member of the child or inmate or the child's legal guardian. These provisions bear a reasonable relationship to child protection and orderly supervision under Turner.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Material application:** Noncontact visits still require child protection and supervision. The record identifies no equally effective ready alternative at negligible institutional cost.

##### Facial validity of the former-prisoner restrictions

**Holding:** Requiring a former prisoner both to be immediate family and to obtain the warden's prior approval survives this facial Turner challenge because it reasonably addresses security and renewed criminal contacts while preserving an approved family exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

##### Facial validity of the repeated-substance-abuse visitation sanction

**Holding:** The record does not establish facial invalidity of suspending ordinary visits after repeated substance-abuse violations, preserving attorney and clergy visits, and allowing an application for discretionary restoration after two years. Deterrence, remaining communication, and institutional costs establish a sufficient Turner relationship on this facial showing; two years marks eligibility to apply, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor for every later duration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Letters and calls count according to their actual usefulness. A less severe sanction is not a ready alternative merely because it is less severe. Indefinite suspension, irrational restoration decisions and an actual absence of realistic contact require application of the governing rule to their own facts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial injunction and remand; two years is eligibility to request restoration, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor.

##### The independent facial Eighth Amendment challenge to visitation loss

**Holding:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm. The challenged regulatory design and this facial record fail that threshold under Wilson, Farmer, Helling, and Delaney, so the independent facial Eighth Amendment judgment cannot stand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Objective seriousness and culpability remain distinct. A proved severe or dangerous application remains reviewable; actual awareness may be proved circumstantially but obviousness does not compel it, and an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference. Preventive relief requires current serious danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wilson, Farmer, Helling and Delaney retain their objective-deprivation, subjective-fault and preventive-risk requirements; an association injury cannot substitute for those elements.

#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices control the complete rule. White's dissent, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, also accepts the calibrated framework without making its contrary application controlling.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** When a State closes its ordinary nomination routes before voters can respond, it must leave an effective route by which voters may support a genuine, legally eligible alternative candidate; a reasonable registered write-in procedure is one such route, and the State may require eligibility, the candidate's consent and declaration, and a reasonable registration date. Hawaii's complete prohibition leaves no such route within its reviewed system, and its interests in primary finality, sore-loser prevention, party raiding and uncontested primaries do not justify that full reach. The prohibition is invalid as applied to genuine eligible candidates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices support both the rationale and reversal. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule and its application and would affirm.

**Current force:** Controls write-in prohibitions that operate after ordinary nomination routes close, as applied to genuine, legally eligible alternative candidates.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. The small filing requirements favor Hawaii, but every ordinary route closes before the primary, and the all-office ballot choice and advancement conditions govern how an alternative reaches the deciding election. Burdick faced an actual single-candidate contest. Sore-loser prevention cannot explain a primary ban, anti-raiding interests do not justify excluding genuine support for a candidate on the selected primary ticket, and an uncontested-winner rule leaves no general-election ballot to protect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently invalidate any surrounding access rule or prescribe statewide filing, certification or counting machinery or a ballot form. Valid candidate qualifications, narrow anti-raiding and sore-loser restrictions, the design of a qualifying route and other States' materially different systems remain open; no absolute write-in right, guarantee of competitive races or entitlement to every late candidacy is created, and no new scrutiny tier is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand for prospective relief confined to votes for legally eligible, genuine alternative candidates. Hawaii chooses the qualifying route, and a reasonable registered write-in procedure suffices. This Court does not itself reinstate the district court's injunction; no special election, reopening of completed elections, or prescribed counting mechanism or ballot form issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, requires examining the prohibition's own contribution to the asserted interests; the independent validity of surrounding rules supplies no presumption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and sore-loser prevention but not this ban's full reach. *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, excuses proof of past disruption, not a showing of fit. *Rosario v. Rockefeller*, 410 U.S. 752, permits timely measures against raiding. *Lubin v. Panish*, 415 U.S. 709, is consistent: a write-in option is neither an automatic cure nor the only qualifying route. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls. White's dissent, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, expressly agrees on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate is protected by the effective-route holding separately stated above, and relief is confined to that interest. Political expression outside the ballot is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief, and relief on the electoral-choice claim does not extend to votes for fictional figures or protest messages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.

### Vieth v. Jubelirer; No. 02-1580; 241 F. Supp. 2d 478 — 2004-04-28

The decisive principle is the prohibition of intentional discriminatory treatment causing material practical injury. It adopts no scrutiny tier, free-standing competitiveness mandate, numerical electoral floor or balancing of a proved violation against partisan gains.

A personally injured voter may challenge an identified, existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity materially impaired through district assignments selected in substantial part to disadvantage the voter because of political affiliation or activity. The five-Justice rule requires the four elements, an independently justified lawful comparison and proof by a preponderance. The State then bears the same-decision defense by the same standard. Materiality requires a demonstrated, nontrivial reduction in effective ability to organize, nominate or elect under ordinary electoral conditions; a predicted vote-share change without reliable evidence of practical consequence does not independently establish it.

They also conclude that the majority substantially restricts intentional pursuit of partisan advantage without adequately reconciling that restriction with the political character of districting recognized by Gaffney.
